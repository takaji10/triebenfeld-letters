On 7 September 1768, before the municipal court of Pyzdry, the merchant Jan Tracholz of Witkowo made over to Stanisław Chełmski, Treasurer of Wschowa and owner of Łukom, the estate beside Trąbczyn, the claims he held against the late Paweł Prusimski of Trąbczyn: 1,053 imperials, 13 groszy and (it seems) 6 Pfennig for pine timber seized from him in the Trąbczyn forests, and 1,513 złoty for sawn goods and other outlays. On these claims Chełmski later sued Paweł's nephew Antoni Prusimski, Starost of Niszczewice and heir of Trąbczyn. <!-- context -->

### Contents

Tracholz, "sound in mind and body", declared that he ceded the two sums to Chełmski, who paid him for them <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app5321pyzdrygr75/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. The first sum, 1,053 imperials, 13 groszy and (it seems) 6 Pfennig, was for trees Tracholz had bought in the pine forests of Trąbczyn under a written contract with Paweł Prusimski; Prusimski, though he had once been paid in full under the contract, seized the trees by force and sold them to a merchant Simon of Szczecin, whose written statement (an "Auscuk", the German Auszug) attests the sale <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app5321pyzdrygr75/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. The second sum, 1,513 złoty, Tracholz had spent partly on making sawn goods, nine stacks of the kinds of timber called "Klapek" and "Rynek", which Paweł's full brother Antoni Prusimski likewise seized after the quarrel over the forests, and partly laid out for Paweł's benefit, as Tracholz's own register of the sums showed <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app5321pyzdrygr75/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. With the sums Tracholz handed over the right to them, the interest on them, and the legal proceedings he himself had begun against Paweł Prusimski's successors <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app5321pyzdrygr75/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. He signed with a "held hand": another hand guided his <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app5321pyzdrygr75/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

The Konin land court's decree of October 1777 in Chełmski's suit dates the transfer: Chełmski claims by a transfer acknowledged before the Pyzdry court on Wednesday, the eve of the Feast of the Nativity of the Virgin, 1768, that is 7 September <a href="{{ '/sources/app536046/' | relative_url }}">53/6/0/-/46</a>. The next sitting of the Pyzdry court, on Friday 9 September 1768, was held outside Pyzdry, because the enemy had burned the town on 11 June of that year. <!-- context -->

Antoni Prusimski, the nephew, protested in March 1778 against the sum Chełmski claimed from the transfer, calling it unlawful and saying it rested on a contract of 1744 for oak timber between his uncle Paweł and Tracholz; a later protest in the same Konin register calls Tracholz "olim", the late <a href="{{ '/sources/app53170koningr117/' | relative_url }}">53/17/0/-/Konin Gr.117</a>. A royal citation to the Crown Tribunal, the highest court of the Polish kingdom, entered in the Konin register of 1780–82, names the merchant Tracholz as the man who made the sum over to Chełmski <a href="{{ '/sources/app53170koningr118/' | relative_url }}">53/17/0/-/Konin Gr.118</a>. The Konin decree of 1777 and the protests of 1778 write the merchant's name Tracholz; the Pyzdry clerk wrote Traholtz. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The municipal court of Pyzdry took shape at the end of the fourteenth century. It judged the nobility in the serious criminal matters of the four articles of the municipal court (arson, attack on a nobleman's house, robbery on the public road, rape) and, in time, in other criminal matters and in suits over land and sums of money. Beside it stood the court's office, which kept the books; the office took entries of uncontested business every day, and the books had public faith: their entries counted as authentic. <!-- context -->
