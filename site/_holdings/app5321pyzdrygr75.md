On 7 September 1768, before the municipal court of the town of Pyzdry, the merchant Jan Tracholz of Witkowo made over to Stanisław Chełmski, Treasurer of Wschowa and owner of Łukom, the estate beside Trąbczyn, the claims he held against the late Paweł Prusimski, heir of Trąbczyn: 1,053 imperials, 13 groszy and (it seems) 6 Pfennig for pine timber Paweł Prusimski had seized from him in the Trąbczyn forests, 1,513 złoty for sawn goods and other outlays, and other sums set out in Tracholz's papers. On these claims Chełmski later sued Paweł's nephew Antoni Prusimski, Starost of Niszczewice, the heir of Trąbczyn after him. <!-- context -->

### Contents

Tracholz, "sound in mind and body", declared that he ceded his claims to Chełmski, who paid him for them <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app5321pyzdrygr75/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. The first sum, 1,053 imperials, 13 groszy and (it seems) 6 Pfennig, was for trees Tracholz had bought in the pine forests of Trąbczyn under a written contract with Paweł Prusimski; Paweł Prusimski, though he had once been paid in full under the contract, seized the trees by force and sold them to a merchant Simon of Szczecin, whose written statement (an "Auscuk", the German Auszug) attests the sale <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app5321pyzdrygr75/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. The second sum, 1,513 złoty, Tracholz had spent partly on making sawn goods, nine stacks of "Klapek", commonly called "Rynek", which Paweł's late full brother, the elder Antoni Prusimski, likewise seized after a dispute over the forests, and partly laid out for Paweł's benefit, as Tracholz's own register of the sums showed <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app5321pyzdrygr75/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. Further sums, set out in writings, registers and notes, went with these two <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app5321pyzdrygr75/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. With the claims Tracholz handed over the right to them, the interest on them, and the legal proceedings he himself had begun against Paweł Prusimski's successors <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app5321pyzdrygr75/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. He signed with a "held hand": another hand guided his <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app5321pyzdrygr75/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

The Konin land court's decree of October 1777 in Chełmski's suit dates the transfer: Chełmski claims by a transfer acknowledged before the Pyzdry court on Wednesday, the eve of the Feast of the Nativity of the Virgin, 1768, that is 7 September <a href="{{ '/sources/app536046/' | relative_url }}">53/6/0/-/46</a>. The next sitting of the Pyzdry court, on Friday 9 September 1768, was held outside Pyzdry, because the enemy had burned the town on 11 June of that year. <!-- context -->

Antoni Prusimski, the Starost, protested in March 1778 against the sum Chełmski claimed from the transfer, calling it unlawful and saying it rested on a contract of 1744 for oak timber between his uncle Paweł and Tracholz; the messenger's report of April 1778 on the royal citation that followed already calls the merchant "olim", the late Tracholz <a href="{{ '/sources/app53170koningr117/' | relative_url }}">53/17/0/-/Konin Gr.117</a>. A royal citation of 1781, entered in the Konin register of 1780–82, summons Antoni Prusimski and the heirs of Paweł's widow, Katarzyna Prusimska, to a hearing on the ground at Grab, the estate Katarzyna had held, which the Crown Tribunal, the highest court of the Polish kingdom, had ordered; in it Chełmski claims payment of the sum the merchant Tracholz had made over to him <a href="{{ '/sources/app53170koningr118/' | relative_url }}">53/17/0/-/Konin Gr.118</a>. The Konin decree of 1777 and the protest of 1778 write the merchant's name Tracholz; the Pyzdry clerk wrote Traholtz. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The municipal court of Pyzdry took shape at the end of the fourteenth century. It judged the nobility in the serious criminal matters of the four articles of the municipal court (arson, attack on a nobleman's house, robbery on the public road, rape) and, in time, in other criminal matters and in suits over land and sums of money. Beside it stood the court's office, which kept the books; the office took entries of uncontested business every day, and the books had public faith: their entries counted as authentic. <!-- context -->
