# APP 53/20/0/-/Poznań Gr.1181

The condescensio decree of 29 March 1784 <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app5320pozgr1181/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>: the sitting the castellany
offices of Greater Poland held on the grounds of Trąbczyn, at the manor
there, on the ultimate limitation (ultimariae limitatio) of the succession
that the sterile brothers Antoni and Paweł Prusimski left. One document on
fourteen pages of the relationes protocollon of the castellany court of
Poznań, entered under the register heading "4ta Martii"; the entry breaks
off mid-sentence at the foot of p. 167v, and nothing follows it in the
volume <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app5320pozgr1181/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

The parties were Antoni in Kolno Prusimski, starost of Niszczewice, knight of
the Order of St Stanisław, the nephew and heir of the sterile brothers,
against the heirs of the sisters' line: the Zakrzewski, Tomicki,
Taczanowski, Kołaczkowski and Tworowicz parties, the successors of the
three daughters of Krzysztof Prusimski and Teresa Golińska. The abbot and
convent of Ląd were cited besides, over the reproduction of the
quietation of 3,500 Hungarian ducats and of the jewels and silver of the
Prusimski house lodged in the convent's treasury <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app5320pozgr1181/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

The decree does three things. It records the sworn bringing of the
documents that the tribunal's decree of 1782 had commanded, completed at
Konin in December 1783, the oath taken by Ignacy Zakrzewski. It recites
the whole documentary history of the succession: the fraternal division of
1740 with its three schedas, the estates of Kolno and Kamionna at 250,000
florins Polish, Trąbczyn with its appurtenances, and the third scheda's
estates of Wierzbno and Brzezno, with the loads appended to each. The history runs from the wadion of 1700 and the sterile death of Ludwik Prusimski at Kamionna in
1708, through the dowry provisions and quietations of the three daughters, to the church
inscriptions of the maternal estates back to 1616. And it decides: the
clean maternal (Goliński) fortune of 98,379 florins groschen 5 Polish
divided among six capits, three brothers and three sisters, at 16,396
florins groschen 15 and 2½ solidi each. The clean paternal fortunes are adjudged to the starost as his uncles' heir, Paweł's scheda at 154,436
florins groschen 24½ Polish and his own at 154,936 florins groschen 24½,
each after the maternal portion's deduction. The widow Nierzychowska's
30,000, though quieted in 1740, and Puchalski's 10,000, presumed pacified, are not judged as borne over, so that nothing out of them can come to the
female sex. The Radoliński successors' claim of 43,000
florins Polish on Trąbczyn, its letter of 1736 disputed as not in
Andrzej Radoliński's hand, the cause remanded to Gniezno and undecided
since 1777, is left standing at the 18,000 enumerated, with the load removed,
pending the final decision in the competent forum <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app5320pozgr1181/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

The last page opens the question the decree answers only in part: what the
brothers alienated beyond the division. Katarzyna née Rozdrażewska, widow
of Paweł, and her 40,000 florins Polish inscribed in 1745 on Peczyska and
Dubno; the condescensio term at Grab of 3 June 1782, taken with Ludwik
Bogdański, chamberlain territorial of Kalisz, and Teresa née
Rozdrażewska; and at the naming of Ewa de Maniewska Potocka, widow of
Joachim Potocki, general lieutenant, the entry stops, mid-sentence <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app5320pozgr1181/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

The scans are the editor's own photographs of the fourteen pages (161 to
167v in the archive's pagination), one page per photograph, taken from the
volume of the relationes protocollon in the State Archive in Poznań. The
transcription is the editor's own, made from the photographs: a Latin text
of one continuous entry, typed page by page and, for p. 161, laid out in
the manuscript's own lines.

The editor's text was carried over as written. Claude had first completed
108 doubtful case endings by grammar. When the editor's fresh line-by-line
typing of p. 161 arrived the same day and kept the original doubts, the
completions were withdrawn, and the editor's text stands throughout. The
five readings the fresh typing settled (the December year 1783,
"videlicet" before the day numeral of the decree of October 1782, "Mensis"
before Junii, "oblatam porrecto", the break "camera-/riali") are the
editor's own readings, and everything else stands with its doubt mark as
they typed it.

The pages were cut from the photographs at the archive's pagination and
paired with the transcription; the holding is one document. Pp. 161v-167v
are presented by paragraph, each page's text beside that page's scan, as
the sibling registers of the same suit are; p. 161 is line by line, as the
editor typed it. The line division of the remaining pages is left open:
machine layouts tried for them put the line ends 2-4 words away from the
editor's own on p. 161, and were not used.

Some thirty doubtful readings remain, names, words and figures the
transcription marks with [?]. They are with the editor; where the editor
settles one, the text will be corrected.

The English was translated from the editor's transcription in a working
session under the same rules as the other holdings. The summaries in both
languages were written with it, and each of their statements was checked
against the document.
