In 1727 a royal messenger, Sebastian Szetlewicz of Szetlewek, reported to the land court at Kalisz that he had served a summons at the estate of Szetlew, leaving it on the table in the heated room of the house. A Szetlewicz family lived at Szetlewek in the 1720s, and the report is the first text to tell the two neighbouring villages, Szetlew and Szetlewek, apart. <!-- context -->

### The entry

Szetlewicz, "of the village of Szetlewko", declared that on the Thursday before the sitting he had taken a summons to the estate of Szetlew and left it on the table in the heated room, before a member of the household of the person summoned <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app534062/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. Neither the case nor the parties are named. The year is that of the court book, 1727. <!-- context -->

Just over fifty years later another messenger from the same village, Mathias Matuszkiewicz "de villa Szetlewek", served summonses in the boundary quarrel between Trąbczyn and Łukomia <a href="{{ '/sources/app53170koningr117/' | relative_url }}">53/17/0/-/Konin Gr.117</a>. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The land court at Kalisz was a court of the nobility. The starost or his deputy, as the king's lieutenant, had the leading part in it, with the judge, the sub-judge, the voivode, the chamberlain and the standard-bearer. The court travelled round the palatinate and held its sittings in the district; the terms at which it sat were called "roczki". It was competent in every suit in which a nobleman was the defendant, except those assigned to other courts, such as the dietine courts or the royal court, and except uncontested business. In 1792 its competence passed to a new land court, the "sąd ziemiański". <!-- context -->

A land court's messenger (woźny) was the officer who carried its citations and executed its orders. When the person cited was not met in person, the citation was left at his dwelling – here, on the table in the heated room, before a member of the household – and the messenger's sworn report of the service, his relatio, was entered in the court's book. <!-- context -->
