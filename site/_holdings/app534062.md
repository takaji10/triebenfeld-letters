One entry at the foot of page 222 of the relation and citation book of the land court at Kalisz for 1726 and 1727, kept in the State Archive in Poznań (Archiwum Państwowe w Poznaniu). It is the report, in Latin, of a court messenger that he served a citation at the village of Szetlew, and it is one document. It is the earliest holding in this edition from the land court at Kalisz. <!-- context -->

### The entry

The general royal messenger Sebastian Szetlewicz, of the village of Szetlewko, reports to the court that he had carried a citation to the estate of the village of Szetlew and to the residence of the party cited, and, on the Thursday next past, had laid and left it there on the table in the heated room, before the cited party's "Conte~" <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app534062/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. The entry gives neither the case nor the parties: the person served appears only as "the cited one". The report's opening formula does not fully parse as transcribed - the verb of reporting that the parallel formulas carry is not on the page as read - and the entry closes with the phrase "of which, the present report" <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app534062/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

### Why it is here

The holding was added for the messenger's name. A Szetlewicz family was living at Szetlewko - Szetlewek - in the 1720s; the name is formed from the place, as Szetlewek itself is formed from Szetlew. Just over fifty years later another messenger of the same village, Mathias Matuszkiewicz "de villa Szetlewek", reports in the Konin register in the boundary quarrel between Trąbczyn and Łukomia <a href="{{ '/sources/app53170koningr117/' | relative_url }}">53/17/0/-/Konin Gr.117</a>. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The volume belongs to the archive's fonds "Księgi sądu ziemskiego w Kaliszu", the books of the land court at Kalisz. According to the archive's description of the fonds, the land court at Kalisz was a court of the nobility. The starost or his deputy, as the king's lieutenant, had the leading part in it, with the judge, the sub-judge, the voivode, the chamberlain and the standard-bearer. The court travelled round the palatinate and held its sittings in the district; the terms at which it sat were called "roczki". It was competent in every suit in which a nobleman was the defendant, except those assigned to other courts, such as the dietine courts or the royal court, and except uncontested business. In 1792 its competence passed to a new land court, the "sąd ziemiański". The fonds holds resignations, inscriptions, reports, judgments, papers brought in for entry, registers of causes, powers of attorney, inquiries and registers. <!-- context -->

A land court's messenger (woźny) was the officer who carried its citations and executed its orders. When the person cited was not met in person, the citation was left at his dwelling - here, on the table in the heated room, before a member of the household - and the messenger's sworn report of the service, his relatio, was entered in the court's book. The same form, in the same Latin, fills the Konin and Kalisz registers of the 1770s that this edition carries from the years of the boundary dispute. <!-- context -->

### The readings that are not settled

The editor looked at the doubtful places on the scan at the pixel level; the download is 1340 pixels wide, so the letterforms do not settle beyond what the editor saw. The open readings, recorded in NEEDS_CONFIRMATION and in `review/app534062/unresolved.md`:

- "Conte~" (line 5), the person before whom the citation was left. The session's candidate is Contubernali, "household member" - the parallel formulas of Konin Gr. 117 fill this slot with a household person ("praesente Economo", "praesente virgine") - but the editor is not sure, and the word stands as written.
- "de Juris [F?]irma" (lines 5-6): no parallel anywhere in the corpus; the editor notes that Forma is as plausible as Firma. It stays as read.
- "eam" (line 2), written with a scribal mark above the final m; the editor guessed "eadem". The session's reading is "eam", the citation, for two reasons: it is the pronoun the sentence needs before the insertion's address ("[that he had carried] it to the estate of Szetlew") and it is picked up again by "eamque ibidem ... posuisse"; and "eadem", a nominative, has no grammatical place in the sentence. If the mark completes the word, "eamdem" (= eandem, the accusative) is the form the period writes. The word stands as read.
- "(inserat~)" (line 3, between the lines): the editor confirms the word is "inserat" with a scribal abbreviation - an insertion mark in the record, before the citation's address.
- The verb of reporting is missing: the parallel formulas read "recognovit se ... detulisse", and the transcription has no "recognovit". Whether the formula's opening stands higher on the page, or the sentence continues from the entry above, is for the editor's reading of the page.
