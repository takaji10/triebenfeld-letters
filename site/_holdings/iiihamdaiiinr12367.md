The Polish courts struck out the mortgage of 30,000 thalers that Weigel, a banker at Wrocław, had on the Trąbczyn estates for money lent to Prince Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen. From 1828 to 1832 the Prussian consul general at Warsaw, Schmidt, and Weigel himself pressed the government of the Kingdom of Poland to compensate him; the earlier course of the claim is in <a href="{{ '/sources/iiihamdaiiinr12366/' | relative_url }}">III. HA MdA, III. Nr. 12366</a>. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

Prince Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen (1746-1818), a Prussian general, received the Trąbczyn estates in 1796, after Prussia had confiscated them from the Starost Antoni Prusimski. He borrowed 30,000 thalers on them from Grotowski, and the mortgage passed to Weigel. In 1807 the estates were returned to Prusimski's daughter, Michalina Miączyńska. Between 1819 and 1826 the Polish courts held that the return had cancelled the debts raised in the meantime, and ordered Weigel's mortgage struck out of the register. Prussia protested at St Petersburg in 1827. <!-- context -->

Trąbczyn lay in the Kingdom of Poland, of which the Russian Emperor was king. His brother, Grand Duke Constantine, resided at Warsaw, and Baron Mohrenheim directed the Grand Duke's diplomatic chancellery. Prussia was represented at Warsaw by a consul general, Schmidt. On 29 November 1830 a rising against Russian rule began at Warsaw. It was put down in September 1831, and a provisional government under Engel administered the Kingdom until March 1832. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**Mohrenheim's answers.** On 7 February 1828 Mohrenheim answered Schmidt that he was not losing sight of the Weigel matter and would report what had been settled once the Grand Duke returned <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/iiihamdaiiinr12367/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. Later, in a private note, he told Schmidt that the council had heard his memorandum on the affair of "Weigel & Eisenhardt" and agreed unanimously that payment must be made; how was still to be decided <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/iiihamdaiiinr12367/2/' | relative_url }}">2</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/iiihamdaiiinr12367/3/' | relative_url }}">3</a>]</span>. On 20 February 1830 he reported that the Administrative Council had told the Grand Duke the previous August that the courts might have had no right to rule against Weigel, since the case could be settled only between the two governments. The Council had allowed the Commission of Finance and Treasury to put that question to the Council of State, and the Grand Duke had now asked it to hurry <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/iiihamdaiiinr12367/4/' | relative_url }}">4</a>]</span>.

**The minister of finance and Weigel, autumn 1830.** In September 1830 Schmidt was told that his notes on Weigel's claim had been laid before the minister of finance <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/iiihamdaiiinr12367/5/' | relative_url }}">5</a>]</span>. The minister, Prince Lubecki, wrote to Weigel himself on 22 September 1830. Weigel had asked for 63,100 thalers: 30,000 of capital, 31,800 of interest and 1300 of costs. Lubecki found the demand excessive and ordered the estates to be valued on the spot, so that the loss really suffered could be assessed <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/iiihamdaiiinr12367/6/' | relative_url }}">6</a>]</span>. Weigel replied from Wrocław on 14 October 1830 and sent the mortgage certificate and the court valuation of 1803. By the certificate the estates were worth 160000 Reichstaler and by the valuation 128917; the entries ranking before his came to 48166, so that on either figure his claim of 63100 was covered <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/iiihamdaiiinr12367/7/' | relative_url }}">7</a>]</span>. On 27 November 1830 Lubecki answered that the valuation dated from 1803, a time of inflated land prices, and that the claim must be reckoned by the present value of land, because the last judgment in Weigel's case was given in 1826 and only from then could he have sought execution. He would wait for the new valuation <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/iiihamdaiiinr12367/7/' | relative_url }}">7</a>]</span>.

**After the rising.** On 14 December 1831 the head of the finance commission wrote to Schmidt that he had ordered the commission to hasten the valuation of Trąbczyn and to examine how well Weigel's capital was secured on it. He added that the special authority which the Emperor had given Lubecki to treat with Weigel could serve no one else, and that he had taken steps to remove that obstacle <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/iiihamdaiiinr12367/8/' | relative_url }}">8</a>]</span>.>>>
