Trąbczyn's history begins in the charters of the Middle Ages: gifts to the Cistercian abbey of Ląd from 1173, the gift of Trąbczyn itself to Count Mikołaj in 1276, the town charter of Zagórów in 1407. The Kodeks Dyplomatyczny Wielkopolski, the printed edition of the medieval documents of Greater Poland, publishes those charters, and the entries here are those that speak of Trąbczyn and its neighbours. Each entry is given whole: the Kodeks's number, its editors' heading, summary and list of sources, the medieval text, and the editors' notes. The text is the electronic text of each volume in the [Wielkopolska Digital Library](https://www.wbc.poznan.pl/). <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The Cistercian abbey of Ląd, on the Warta between Konin and Pyzdry, was the great landowner of the district in the Middle Ages, and most of these documents concern it. Trąbczyn itself was held by knights, who took their name from it ("de Trampczino"), while the abbey held the villages around it and the tithe of its fields. The volumes 1 to 5 were edited in the nineteenth and early twentieth century, with their summaries in Latin and their notes in Polish; the volumes 6 to 11, from 1982, give heading and summary in Polish. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**The abbey of Ląd and its villages.** Duke Mieszko gives the abbey "little Vronchin", Wrąbczynek, in 1173 <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. Archbishop Fulko gives it the tithes of Zagórów, Skokum, Kopojno, Trąbczyn, Szetlew, Łukom and Wrąbczyn, in a document dated 1240 <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/2/' | relative_url }}">2</a>]</span>. The dukes confirm its estates in 1261 and 1280, among them Szetlew, Zagórów, Skokum and Oleśnica, and the sanctuary people whom the editors place at Świątniki <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/4/' | relative_url }}">4</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/6/' | relative_url }}">6</a>]</span>. In 1291 the abbey gains Kopojno by exchange <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/7/' | relative_url }}">7</a>]</span>. The judges of Kalisz declare in 1377 that Wrąbczynek and Adamierz have been the abbey's from of old <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/11/' | relative_url }}">11</a>]</span>, and in 1384 the judge of Kalisz gives it the investiture of "Vronczino", Wrąbczyn or Wrąbczynek <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/12/' | relative_url }}">12</a>]</span>. In 1399 the Archbishop of Gniezno adjudges the sheaf tithe of Trąbczyn to the abbey against Jan, the parish priest of Trąbczyn <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/13/' | relative_url }}">13</a>]</span>. In 1407 the King allows the abbey to found a town at Zagórów under the law of Magdeburg <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/14/' | relative_url }}">14</a>]</span>; the abbots then deal with its schultheiss and peasants <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/15/' | relative_url }}">15</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/18/' | relative_url }}">18</a>]</span>, and in 1414 the parish church of Zagórów is handed over to the abbey <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/16/' | relative_url }}">16</a>]</span>.

**Trąbczyn and its knights.** In 1276 Duke Bolesław gives the village of Trąbczyn, "Trupcino", to Count Mikołaj, treasurer of Ląd, after two earlier holders had died without heirs <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/5/' | relative_url }}">5</a>]</span>. In 1284 Przemysł II allows Count Mikołaj to locate his Trąbczyn, "Tropcssino", under German law, free of the Polish dues <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/23/' | relative_url }}">23</a>]</span>. In 1346 the brothers Jan and Przedpełk, heirs of Trąbczyn, and Andrzej of Trąbczyn with his kin lose their claim to Stropiszyn and Pępocin against the church of Gniezno <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/9/' | relative_url }}">9</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/10/' | relative_url }}">10</a>]</span>. Mikołaj and Andrzej of Trąbczyn witness a sale to the abbey at Gniezno in 1415 <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/17/' | relative_url }}">17</a>]</span>. In 1434, in the house of Mścichna, wife of Otto, heir in Trąbczyn, their son Wojciech, a canon of Kruszwica, makes his brother Jan of Trąbczyn his proctor <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/19/' | relative_url }}">19</a>]</span>: the only one of these documents written at Trąbczyn.

**Other hands.** Wisław son of Gerlach gives up Wrąbczyn in an exchange confirmed in 1292 <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/8/' | relative_url }}">8</a>]</span>. Wrąbczyn's own papers with the abbey are richer: in 1241 Duke Kazimierz of Kujawy and Ląd exchanges Głowiewo and Wrąbczyn, once the huntsmen's land, for the abbey's estate of Koszanów <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/22/' | relative_url }}">22</a>]</span>; Przemysł II gives Wrąbczyn to Henryk of Pyzdry to locate under German law in 1291 <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/24/' | relative_url }}">24</a>]</span>; Władysław grants Count Wojsław the same right over it in 1298 <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/25/' | relative_url }}">25</a>]</span>, and confirms the abbey's estates, Wrąbczyn with the lake of Seborowo among them, in 1324 <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/26/' | relative_url }}">26</a>]</span>. In 1357 the exchange of Zemka's third part of great Wrąbczyn for the abbey's Szczonowo is attested at Konin <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/27/' | relative_url }}">27</a>]</span>; Laurentius of Królikowo sells his third part to the abbey in 1362 <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/28/' | relative_url }}">28</a>]</span>, Stasco with his consort Iachna theirs, and Mikołaj the subdapifer the half of the lordship, in 1363 <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/29/' | relative_url }}">29</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/30/' | relative_url }}">30</a>]</span>; and in 1364 Dobrocha, heiress of Wrąbczyn, acknowledges that all her substance belongs to the abbot <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/31/' | relative_url }}">31</a>]</span>. In 1440 King Władysław III charges sums for Stanisław Czarny on Wrąbczynek, Adamierz and Borzykowo, which the editors call royal villages near Pyzdry <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/20/' | relative_url }}">20</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/21/' | relative_url }}">21</a>]</span>. Among the villages of the hospital of Gniezno, given to the canons of Miechów in 1243, is "Chemelino", which the editors identify as Imielno, the neighbour of Łukom <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/3/' | relative_url }}">3</a>]</span>.

The editors' notes are of their time and are kept as they are. Their note to the exchange of 1418 puts Wronczyn in the district of Poznań and could not say which Drzewce is meant, but the document places Great Wrąbczyn beside Zagórów, and it is tagged so <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/18/' | relative_url }}">18</a>]</span>. <!-- context -->

The editors' apparatus is in Latin and Polish in volumes 1 to 5 and in Polish in 6 to 11. Vertical strokes in some texts are the editors' marks of the line ends of the original, as words broken across them show ("signifi|camus"). <!-- context -->

### Estates and people

The estates tagged are the editor's seventeen around Trąbczyn and Zagórów: Zagórów, Skokum, Kopojno, Ląd, Szetlew, Szetlewek, Łukom, Imielno, Trąbczyn, Wrąbczyn, Wrąbczynek, Świątniki, Oleśnica, Łazy, Adamierz, Drzewce and Przybysław. Łazy and Szetlewek do not occur in these entries, and Przybysław only as the name of a man, the servant of the church of Zagórów in 1414 <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/kdw/16/' | relative_url }}">16</a>]</span>; none of the three is tagged. People are named in the index only where they are of Trąbczyn (the editor). <!-- context -->

### Translation and summaries

Claude translated each entry into English, the editors' parts as well as the document. Places are given in their modern Polish forms where the editors identify them, and the medieval spellings stand in the Latin. Claude wrote the summaries, and a separate pass checked every statement against the document; a statement the document does not support was cut or weakened.
