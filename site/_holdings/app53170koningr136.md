In July 1754 the widow Katarzyna Prusimska had the Trąbczyn estate inspected, and a court messenger reported the inspection to the municipal court at Konin. The manor farm's buildings were mostly old and some were falling down; the Olęder settlers of Nowa Wieś and the peasants who owed the estate labour in four villages were counted; and two noblemen found that the best timber of the estate's woods had been felled. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

Trąbczyn had belonged to Paweł Prusimski, chamberlain of the King, who died shortly before the summer of 1754. His widow Katarzyna, born Rozdrażewska, held the estate for life under her dower. A widow who took over an estate in this way had its condition recorded, so that she could not later be charged with what was already ruined or missing. Nine years later she was at law over potash from the same woods: <a href="{{ '/sources/app536045/' | relative_url }}">53/6/0/-/45</a>. <!-- context -->

The municipal court and office of Konin was the starost's court for the nobility of the district. Its office, open every day, registered the business they brought to it, sales, debts, the reports of the court's messengers and their suits, and what was entered in its books was accepted as proof. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**The manor farm.** The old manor house is bad from the ground up and cannot be repaired. A new one stands finished in the frame, roofed in part, with two small rooms not quite done. Then come the kitchen, four barns of which two are falling down, a large sheepfold, a small granary with rotted sills, the carriage house, pigsties, cowshed and stables, mostly old, and the distillery and the malthouse, with their pots, vats and barrels <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr136/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

**The settlers and the people.** At Nowa Wieś there are fifteen Olęder settlers; the sixteenth plot is empty, and some have built well while others have only huts dug into the ground. A count of the people who owe labour follows, village by village: in Trąbczyn four full farmers, each with eight of the lord's draught animals, five half-farmers, seven cottagers and eight women lodgers; fewer in Osiny, Łazy and Nowa Wieś. Some peasants neither work for the manor nor hold its cattle <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr136/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

**The woods.** Two noblemen who went round with the court messenger, Kazimierz Świerczewski and Józef Puchalski, found that in the pine woods of Trąbczyn that run from Osiny along the Drzewce boundary, the larger half of the better trees has been felled as far as Łomów. From there to the Biskupice boundary the finer pines have been picked out. Before and beyond Czarny Bród, above Szetlewek and towards Łazy, only the leavings stand, from which nothing can be chosen for building. It is the same round the glassworks, where the finest timber was, and with the oaks, except where the Olęder settlers have taken the timber on the plots long since marked out for them <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr136/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

**Who asked, and who went.** Katarzyna Prusimska was the widow of Paweł Prusimski, the King's chamberlain and heir of Trąbczyn, not long dead, and held her dower and a life interest in all his estates. At her request the court messenger Bartłomiej Szeptycki of Trąbczyn went with the two noblemen on 9 July 1754 to Trąbczyn, the villages of Osiny, Łazy and Nowa Wieś, the Olęder settlement, the glassworks and the woods, and declared what he had seen at the court office in Konin three days later <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr136/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

The description of the village houses, one by one, is not transcribed. <!-- context -->
