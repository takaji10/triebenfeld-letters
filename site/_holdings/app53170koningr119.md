Marking the boundary between Trąbczyn and Łukomia on the ground in 1782 did not end the quarrel between the two owners. Stanisław Ścibor Chełmski and Antoni Prusimski were back in court at once – over a wood cut along the new line, over an inroad at Łomowo, and over the men Prusimski was to produce before the court. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

In December 1782 a boundary officer raised forty-one mounds along the line the Crown Tribunal had drawn between Antoni Prusimski's Trąbczyn and Stanisław Ścibor Chełmski's Łukom: <a href="{{ '/sources/app53170koningr153/' | relative_url }}">53/17/0/-/Konin Gr.153</a>. Days later Prusimski's steward protested that Chełmski's people had cut 221 pines on the Trąbczyn side of it: <a href="{{ '/sources/app53170koningr118/' | relative_url }}">53/17/0/-/Konin Gr.118</a>. Three months later both sides were suing in the municipal court of Kalisz, the court of the Starost General of Greater Poland, which heard charges of violence. <!-- context -->
 A court messenger served each summons and then declared at the court office in Konin where and when he had left it. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**Chełmski's citations, March 1783.** By a citation of 10 March 1783 Chełmski and his steward Ignacy Jaroszewski charged Prusimski's steward Leon Maszewski with breaking into the pine wood that the Tribunal's line had left, with one Olęder settler, to Łukom, and with taking oxen, carts and axes from Chełmski's people. Named men of Trąbczyn and Nowa Wieś were put under arrest by it. Prusimski was to be punished too if he had given the order <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr119/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. A second citation of the same day charged Prusimski with an inroad "with men and dogs" into Łomowo, an estate belonging to Łukom <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr119/2/' | relative_url }}">2</a>]</span>.

**Counter-summonses.** Chełmski's messenger found that of two summonses of Prusimski's against Chełmski only one, of November 1782, had been declared at Konin <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr119/3/' | relative_url }}">3</a>]</span>. On 18 March 1783 Prusimski, Maszewski and Franciszek Liberacki summoned Chełmski in their turn <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr119/4/' | relative_url }}">4</a>]</span>, and in May Prusimski asked to be freed of a fine of fourteen marks and of the proceedings for it, which Chełmski had had entered against him <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr119/5/' | relative_url }}">5</a>]</span>; Chełmski summoned him again the same month <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr119/6/' | relative_url }}">6</a>]</span>.

**Prusimski's protest, October 1783.** Cited before the land court to produce certain men and to answer for an act of violence, Prusimski protested that, if those men did it, it was done without his knowledge or order; that he could produce only two of the men named, Gregory and Thomas; and that the arrest had not reached the others <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr119/7/' | relative_url }}">7</a>]</span>. The land court's decree in that cause, on the beating of an Olęder settler at Smoleniec, cites this protest: <a href="{{ '/sources/app536047/' | relative_url }}">53/6/0/-/47</a>.

**1784.** In September 1784 Chełmski summoned Prusimski before the Crown Tribunal; Maszewski, now commissioner of the Trąbczyn estates, found a week later that the summons had not been declared at Konin <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr119/8/' | relative_url }}">8</a>]</span>.
