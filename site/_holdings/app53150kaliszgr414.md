One document of three pages in a book of the municipal court of Kalisz for 1771, kept in the State Archive in Poznań (Archiwum Państwowe w Poznaniu). It is the delivery of possession by which the Lusnia wood and the Olęder settlers living in it were handed to Stanisław Ścibor Chełmski of Łukomia in November 1771. It is in Latin, with the act of delivery itself in Polish. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

Lusnia was a wood on the disputed ground between Trąbczyn and Łukom. Antoni Prusimski, heir of Trąbczyn and Starost of Niszczewice, treated it as his and had settled Olęder farmers in it. Stanisław Ścibor Chełmski of Łukomia claimed it as his from of old. Through the 1760s Chełmski obtained judgments against Prusimski, several of them by default, before a court sitting on the ground and before the Crown Tribunal. A party that had won in this way was put into possession by an officer who went to the place, read out the judgments and handed the property over before witnesses. That act is a delivery of possession (traditio). <!-- context -->

Four years later the tables were turned. The commission that fixed the boundary in 1775 found Lusnia to be Trąbczyn's, set this delivery of possession aside as unlawful, and ordered Chełmski to repay the rents he had taken from the twelve settlers and the profits of the inn: <a href="{{ '/sources/app53170koningr145/' | relative_url }}">53/17/0/-/Konin Gr.145</a>. <!-- context -->

The volume belongs to the archive's fonds "Księgi sądu i urzędu grodzkiego w Kaliszu", the books of the municipal court and office of Kalisz. According to the archive's description of the fonds, the municipal court took shape at the end of the fourteenth century. For the nobility it first heard criminal cases under the four "castle articles": arson, an attack on a nobleman's house, robbery on the public road, and rape. In time its competence changed and came to include the other criminal cases, and suits over land and over sums of money, and it took over appeals from the town courts. Beside the court grew the municipal court office, where the court books were kept. The office was open every day for the entry of uncontested business, and its books had public credit. The fonds holds resignations, inscriptions, reports, judgments, papers brought in for entry, registers of causes, loose papers, oaths, citations, judgments under Magdeburg law and powers of attorney. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**The occasion.** The act was done on the Monday after All Saints 1771, in the wood called Lusnia and at the house standing there. Present were the noblemen Cajetan Grabski and Antoni and Konstanty Fulwarski, father and son, Józef Jaroszewski, the steward of Trąbczyn, and the court messenger Joannes Bykierski of Węgierce, who proclaimed the act <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53150kaliszgr414/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

**The judgments recited.** The officer acts at the request of Stanisław Ścibor Chełmski, heir of Łukomia, as the party that has won at law. He recites the judgments: a first condemnation given on the disputed ground in 1761 and entered at Konin; a decree of the Crown Tribunal of 1766, given at Bydgoszcz, between Chełmski and Antoni Prusimski, Starost of Niszczewice, ordering first of all that Łukom and Trąbczyn be divided by a boundary. There follow a mandate of 1767 from the Tribunal at Poznań; a second Tribunal decree of 1768; and a third condemnation of 1770, entered at Kalisz, against Prusimski for contravening the decree <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53150kaliszgr414/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

**The delivery.** The act itself is in Polish. There is handed over to Chełmski the pine forest and wood called Lusnia, as belonging to Łukomia from of old, with the Olęder settlement founded in it not long before, which the settlers now call Olędry. First a house built for the sale of drink, with a new stable and no keeper; then the settlers, twelve of them, by name: Michał Kinos, Henryk Bedka, Jan and Krystyan Tulka, Bogusław Zelmer (written Celmer), Samuel Frinderberg, Bogusław Effthman, Krzysztofowa Kulawa (a widow or wife), Maciej Klinczpor, Brudzewski, Antoni Pisarz and Wawrzyniec Welner. No one contested the delivery. An inventory was drawn up, Chełmski was left in peaceful possession, and the settlers were ordered to obey him. The officer signs: Wawrzyniec Wstowski, sworn general commissioner of the provinces of Poznań and Kalisz <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53150kaliszgr414/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

**The entry.** On the Saturday before Saint Martin 1771 Antoni Serlatkowski brought the act to the municipal court office at Kalisz to be entered, and the original was sewn into the book <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53150kaliszgr414/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.
