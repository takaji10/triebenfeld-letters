In January 1782 the Crown Tribunal, the highest court of the Polish kingdom, decided the boundary between Trąbczyn and Łukomia. Its decree was carried out on the ground against Stanisław Ścibor Chełmski's resistance, while three other suits went on: Chełmski's claim on a debt he had bought from a townsman, Tracholz; the abbey of Ląd's defence against Prusimski's charge that it had colluded with Chełmski; and the claim of a Zakrzewski family and other descendants of Antoni Prusimski's aunts to a share of his uncles' estate. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

Since 1775 two boundary decrees had stood against each other: that of the parliament's commission, for Prusimski, and that of the commissioners Chełmski had brought, for Chełmski: <a href="{{ '/sources/app53170koningr145/' | relative_url }}">53/17/0/-/Konin Gr.145</a>, <a href="{{ '/sources/app53170koningr116/' | relative_url }}">53/17/0/-/Konin Gr.116</a>. In 1778 each side was still protesting against the other's: <a href="{{ '/sources/app53170koningr117/' | relative_url }}">53/17/0/-/Konin Gr.117</a>. The Crown Tribunal at Piotrków decided the cause "from the map", ruled on the two decrees, and sent an officer to raise the boundary mounds along a line it had drawn on that map. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**The boundary: the Tribunal's line on the ground, 1782.** In March 1782 a decree on the Olęder settlement belonging to Trąbczyn, given on the ground, was registered <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/11/' | relative_url }}">11</a>]</span>. In June the boundary chamberlain of Inowrocław, Jakub Górski, the officer who was to mark the line, protested: he had come, engaged by Prusimski, to carry out the Tribunal's decree of 23 January between Trąbczyn Minus or Nowa Wieś and Łukom and to raise the mounds along the line drawn on the map. Chełmski in person pushed away the man with the surveyor's chain, kicked the chain off the line and threatened, and Górski broke off to ask the army's War Department for soldiers to protect the work <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/17/' | relative_url }}">17</a>]</span>. At the same sitting, on 8 June, a field boundary decree of the sitting on the ground between Trąbczyn and Łukom, issued by the boundary chamberlain's office of Inowrocław, was registered; the register notes it without copying it <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/18/' | relative_url }}">18</a>]</span>. Chełmski answered on 15 June that an executive officer has to carry out a decree of the Tribunal and not interpret it, that Górski had gone beyond it and had wrongly described him as resisting, and that his act was null <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/20/' | relative_url }}">20</a>]</span>. Chełmski summoned Prusimski and Górski before the Tribunal <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/22/' | relative_url }}">22</a>]</span>; Prusimski summoned Chełmski, the chamberlain of the land of Dobrzyń and the convent of Ląd <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/23/' | relative_url }}">23</a>]</span>. In December the decree marking the new boundary, with its mounds, was registered, and with it the handing over of an Olęder settler of Trąbczyn, Martin Hunt <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/29/' | relative_url }}">29</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/30/' | relative_url }}">30</a>]</span>.

**Wood cut across the new line, December 1782.** Prusimski had Chełmski cited before the municipal court of Kalisz for acts of violence <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/26/' | relative_url }}">26</a>]</span>. On 7 December his messenger reported 221 building pines felled on the Trąbczyn side of the line, by his account on 28 November, by Chełmski's people <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/27/' | relative_url }}">27</a>]</span>, and the Trąbczyn steward Leon Maszewski protested in Polish, naming the men of Łomów and Łukom who had come with sledges and axes <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/28/' | relative_url }}">28</a>]</span>.

**The convent of Ląd.** Prusimski had charged the Cistercians of Ląd, owners of Drzewce, with colluding with Chełmski at the boundary commission. On 22 November 1781 their administrator, Father Tesselin Malechowski, protested in Polish that the convent had acknowledged the meeting point of the three grounds as Chełmski showed it for the reasons in the commission's decree, and offered an oath <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/8/' | relative_url }}">8</a>]</span>. In January 1782 the abbot, Benedykt Lubstowski, and the convent cited Prusimski before the Tribunal to be cleared of the charge, relying on the inspection of 1592 <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/10/' | relative_url }}">10</a>]</span>; a further citation followed in September <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/25/' | relative_url }}">25</a>]</span>.

**The Tracholz debt and Grab.** The claim that Chełmski had bought from the merchant Tracholz was now to be heard at a sitting on the ground at Grab, the estate of the late Katarzyna Prusimska. The land court judge Stefan Zielonacki, Chełmski, Prusimski and the Bogdańskis, husband and wife, she the heiress of Katarzyna Prusimska, were summoned to it for 3 June 1782, and four of Prusimski's subjects from Nowa Wieś were to be brought as witnesses <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/2/' | relative_url }}">2</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/9/' | relative_url }}">9</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/14/' | relative_url }}">14</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/16/' | relative_url }}">16</a>]</span>.

**The succession to Prusimski's uncles.** Prusimski took Kazimierz Zakrzewski and his sons, and the land court that had heard their claim, before the Tribunal in October 1781 and April 1782, and his agent Piotr Gosławski protested when the land court refused him a delay <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/4/' | relative_url }}">4</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/12/' | relative_url }}">12</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/13/' | relative_url }}">13</a>]</span>. Those who claimed a share were the children of Marianna, Wiktoria and Konstancja Prusimska, his aunts. Prusimski was the son of Stefan Prusimski and Wiktoria Malczewska, grandson of Krzysztof Prusimski and Teresa Golińska, and claimed to be sole heir of his uncles Antoni and Paweł, who died childless; he relied on a Tribunal decree of 1738 and argued that daughters whom their father had dowered could claim nothing from their brothers <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/24/' | relative_url }}">24</a>]</span>. This is the only document that names his parents and grandparents. <!-- context -->
