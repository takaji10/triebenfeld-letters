In late 1782 the boundary between Trąbczyn and Łukomia was finally marked on the ground, which settled in law a dispute that had lasted nearly two hundred years. The boundary chamberlain of Gniezno carried out the Crown Tribunal's judgment and raised forty-one mounds along the line, and an Olęder settler, Martin Hunt, whom both sides had claimed, was handed over to Antoni Prusimski. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The commission of 1775 had ended in two decrees: one by the parliament's commissioners, for Antoni Prusimski of Trąbczyn, and one by commissioners whom Stanisław Ścibor Chełmski of Łukomia had brought, for Chełmski: <a href="{{ '/sources/app53170koningr145/' | relative_url }}">53/17/0/-/Konin Gr.145</a>, <a href="{{ '/sources/app53170koningr116/' | relative_url }}">53/17/0/-/Konin Gr.116</a>. A law of 1776 sent causes left in that state to the Crown Tribunal. An attempt to carry it out in May and June 1782 failed, as the Konin register shows from the protests of both sides: <a href="{{ '/sources/app53170koningr118/' | relative_url }}">53/17/0/-/Konin Gr.118</a>. <!-- context -->

The line had "corner" mounds where the lands of Trąbczyn, Łukom and the abbey of Ląd's village of Drzewce met, "aciales" mounds at the other end, where Trąbczyn and Łukom met Biskupice, and "wall" mounds between the two. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**The Tribunal's decree of 17 October 1782.** In January 1782 the Tribunal had taken the two end points of the boundary as the map of 1775 showed them, confirming on that point the decree of Prusimski's commissioners, and had drawn a straight line between them on the map, signed by the Tribunal's President and Marshal. It had ordered wall mounds raised along the line and the mounds of Chełmski's commissioners levelled. It had also ordered that the Olęder settlers whom Chełmski had taken into possession be given back to Prusimski, all but one, Martin Hunt, whose house the map showed on Chełmski's side of the line <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr153/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

When the two boundary officers came to the ground in May, the sworn surveyor drew the line, and Hunt was not on Chełmski's side of it. Each side had engaged a boundary officer to carry out the decree; the officer Chełmski had engaged sent the cause back to the Tribunal. The officer Prusimski had engaged proposed to bend the line so that the site of an Olęder holding that had burnt down four years earlier would fall to Chełmski, and Chełmski refused that <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr153/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

The Tribunal now rules that the line stands as drawn. A sworn surveyor, it says, measures boundary lines and boundary signs; houses he marks only by their position, so the place of a house on the map proves nothing. The first decree is final under the law of 1776. What both officers had done was quashed. Within six weeks a sub-chamberlain or chamberlain, with a sworn surveyor, is to raise wall mounds along the straight line: what lies to the right belongs to Łukom, what lies to the left to Trąbczyn or Nowa Wieś. Hunt, since he does not sit on the right of the line, goes to Prusimski. Chełmski is to pay the sum already adjudged for the revenue he drew from the settlers. Both officers were punished: Prusimski's with a week in the tower of Radziejów castle and a money penalty, Chełmski's with a money penalty. The abbey of Ląd, which had entered a precautionary protest and withdrawn it, is freed from the penalty Prusimski claimed <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr153/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

**The execution, 28 November to 3 December 1782.** Franciszek Ksawery Jasiński, boundary chamberlain of the palatinate of Gniezno, opened his court at the three corner mounds on 28 November. Chełmski met the court on the forest road and asked that his appearance be recorded; he was told it would be if he came the next day, and he did not come. Prusimski produced the map with the signed line. The sworn surveyor, Benedykt Woronowski, a canon of Łęczyca, who had made the map, set the line out with poles, and the court compared it with the map. On 30 November and on 2 and 3 December the court raised forty-one wall mounds along the line, twenty perches apart, and drove thirty-one stakes of alder and oak where the line crosses the marsh called Stawisko. The prior of Ląd attended for the abbey; Chełmski appeared in person on 2 December. Each mound holds five stones laid as a cross, pieces of brick, broken glass and charcoal. The court then had the older mounds levelled <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/app53170koningr153/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.
