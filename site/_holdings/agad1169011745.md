After Prussia took the country around Konin in 1793, its officials set about counting what they had gained. This survey, made for the Prussian administration of South Prussia – the name Prussia gave its share of partitioned Poland – about 1797, counts the houses, churches and Christian and Jewish inhabitants of the villages around Konin, Trąbczyn and its Hollander settlement among them. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

Prussia formed the province of South Prussia from the Polish lands it took in the partitions of 1793 and 1795. The province was governed from Berlin by a department of the General Directory, the central authority for finance, war and the royal domains, and in the province itself by war and domain chambers at Poznań, Kalisz and Warsaw. The territory of each chamber was divided into districts, each under a district councillor (Landrat); Konin was one of the eleven districts of the Kalisz chamber. The new administration collected returns like this one on the places of the province. <!-- context -->

In 1796 the King gave the confiscated estate of Trąbczyn to Prince Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen (1746-1818). The survey counts the estate's villages as the Prussian administration found them in the first years of his ownership. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**The places.** The survey covers places around Konin from Kurow to Wierzbna, among them Ląd with its monastery, Lądek, Rzgow and Stare miasto; it does not name the district and is not dated <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/agad1169011745/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. Most are villages. The others are Hollander settlements ("Haul.", for Hauland), mills, demesne farms ("Vorw.", for Vorwerk), the monastery of Ląd and one inn <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/agad1169011745/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. Jews lived in eight of the places, never more than four in one <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/agad1169011745/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

**Trąbczyn and the places near it.** Trąbczyn, written "Trąpczyn", had 32 houses, a church and 165 inhabitants, 51 men and 114 women <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/agad1169011745/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. Its Hollander settlement was larger than the village: 34 houses and 271 inhabitants <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/agad1169011745/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. Two small places belonged to the estate: a Nowa wieś with 5 houses and 49 inhabitants and a Stara Huta with 2 houses and 9 inhabitants <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/agad1169011745/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. Szetlew had 11 houses and 84 inhabitants; Szetlewek 20 houses and 140 inhabitants, 4 of them Jews <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/agad1169011745/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. Łukom, the neighbour with which Trąbczyn disputed its boundary for two centuries, had 28 houses and 149 inhabitants, and its Hollander settlement 8 houses and 40 inhabitants <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/agad1169011745/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.
