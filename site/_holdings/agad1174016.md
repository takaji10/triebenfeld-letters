In July 1807, after Napoleon had defeated Prussia, the Governing Commission – the provisional Polish government he set up – decided to give the confiscated Prusimski estates back to the family. On 21 July 1807 it returned them, Trąbczyn among them, to Antoni Prusimski's daughter Michalina Dąbska, by extending to her a decree Napoleon had given in June in favour of Józef Wybicki, a member of the Commission whose own estates Prussia had also taken. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

Prussia confiscated the estates of the Starost Antoni Prusimski in 1796 and gave Trąbczyn and Kamionna with Kolno to Prince Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen (1746-1818), a Prussian general. In the war of 1806 and 1807 Napoleon drove Prussia out of the Polish provinces it had taken in the partitions. On 14 January 1807 he set up the Governing Commission, a provisional government of Poles under Stanisław Małachowski, which administered those provinces until 5 October 1807. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**The order for Wybicki, 12 July 1807.** Napoleon, as Emperor of the French and King of Italy, had decreed at Finckenstein on 5 June that Wybicki was to have back his estates of Manieczki, Przylepki, Boreczek and Esterpol in the department of Poznań, which the Prussian government had seized and given away, and had charged the provisional Polish government with carrying this out. The commission now sends a certified copy of the decree to its Director of Internal Affairs, who is to have the Poznań administrative chamber hand the estates over and put Wybicki in possession without delay <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/agad1174016/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

**The resolution for Dąbska, Niemojewski and Wichrowski, 21 July 1807.** The commission had followed Napoleon to Dresden, and there Dąbska asked for her father's estates. Napoleon referred her case to the commission, which now extends his decree for Wybicki to her and to two others whose hereditary estates Prussia had confiscated, General Niemojewski and Wichrowski, so that the estates return to their owners <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/agad1174016/2/' | relative_url }}">2</a>]</span>. The resolution names no estate and says nothing of the debts Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen had raised on them, and whether the return cancelled those debts was still argued over in the 1830s <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/agad1174016/2/' | relative_url }}">2</a>]</span>.
