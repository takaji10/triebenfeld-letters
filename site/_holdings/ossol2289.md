The copy, in a nineteenth-century hand, of a letter Tadeusz Kościuszko wrote in Polish from Paris on 3 September 1798. The copy stands in the manuscript collection "Listy, dokumenty i wiersze z lat 1697-1798" kept at the Ossolineum in Wrocław (B. Ossol. rkp. 2289 II), on page 145 of the codex, and it is one document. The collection is a gathering of copies made mostly by Stanisław Przyłęcki (1805-1866), the Lwów bibliographer and autograph collector whose manuscript legacy the Ossolineum bought in 1869. <!-- context -->

### The letter

Kościuszko answers an émigré correspondent who had written to him from Dresden in August of that year: he has read the expressions of goodwill "with the same courtesy as in those which reached me in America" <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/ossol2289/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span> - the letters an insurgent emigrant had sent him during his American years. He regrets that events did not let him see the writer in London, and would have liked to know him in person. When circumstances point out the time for regaining the national independence - which he does not yet see, and which hope alone makes sweet to long for - he will stand inseparable from every Pole of a truly Republican soul, and from all who fought with him against the enemies of the country; and in that time the ways of common defence and the nation's true happiness will be founded on the principles the writer's letter mentions. For now, he writes, care must be taken not to expose them, through premature steps while happier events are awaited, to the persecutions of the enemies. He asks the writer to keep the letter for himself, and promises to preserve his friendship <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/ossol2289/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

The dateline reads "z P[aryża]. d. 3 Września 98" - from Paris, 3 September 98, the expansion of the superscript abbreviation the editor's reading; Kościuszko had been in Paris since June 1798. <!-- context -->

### The addressee, and why it is here

The letter never names its addressee: "Obywatelu!" - Citizen! - is all the text offers. The identification rests on Pachoński's entry on Antoni Prusimski in the Polish Biographical Dictionary (PSB, 1982), whose list of sources cites exactly this manuscript, "B. Ossol.: rkp. 2289/II". The entry records that Prusimski, who had settled in Dresden at the beginning of 1798, welcomed Kościuszko's arrival in Paris in June with a letter of 13 August, and that the Naczelnik "answered him politely, expressing regret that he did not know him in person, only from correspondence" - every element the copy carries. Prusimski's letters of 18 September and 3 October 1797 had reached Kościuszko in America. If the identification stands, the copy preserves the one side of the exchange that nothing published carries: the PSB knew the reply existed, and quotes none of it. The attribution is the editor's working one and is kept under review; the page itself is silent. <!-- context -->

### The collection around it

The thirty scans read in the session of 1 November 2026 (leaves 0033-0062 of the codex) show what Przyłęcki gathered around the letter: the codex's own index of letters and lists of people; a July-1794 dossier of the uprising - letters to the Naczelnik, an officers' roster from the camp at Mokotów, the protocol of the Warsaw criminal deputation, and the opening of a patent signed by Kościuszko on 25 July 1794, whose continuation is missing from the scans; the file of the receipt and service attestation of the "Korpus Towarzystwa" of 1794; a name-day poem for Kościuszko of 1792; and a bishop's letter of 1796. None of those pages names Antoni Prusimski or anyone of his circle. Only the letter of page 145 is in the edition so far; the rest waits on the editor's rulings. <!-- context -->
