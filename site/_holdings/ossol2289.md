Tadeusz Kościuszko, the commander of the Polish uprising of 1794, wrote a letter from Paris on 3 September 1798 to a Polish émigré who is not named. It survives in a copy made by Stanisław Przyłęcki (1805-1866), the Lwów bibliographer and autograph collector whose manuscripts the Ossolineum bought in 1869, in his collection "Listy, dokumenty i wiersze z lat 1697-1798". <!-- context -->

### The letter

Kościuszko answers an émigré correspondent who had written to him from Dresden in August of that year: he has read the expressions of goodwill "with the same courtesy as in those which reached me in America from You" <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/ossol2289/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span> – the letters the same writer had sent him during his American years. He regrets that events did not let him see the writer in London, and would have liked to know him in person. When circumstances point out the time for regaining the national independence – which he does not yet see, and which hope alone makes sweet to long for – he will stand inseparable from every Pole of a truly Republican soul, and from all who fought with him against the enemies of the country; and in that time the ways of common defence and the nation's true happiness will be founded on the principles the writer's letter mentions. For now, he writes, care must be taken not to expose them, through premature steps while happier events are awaited, to the persecutions of the enemies. He asks the writer to keep the letter for himself, and promises to preserve his friendship <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/ossol2289/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

Kościuszko had been in Paris since June 1798. <!-- context -->

### The addressee

The letter is addressed only "Obywatelu!" – Citizen! <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/ossol2289/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. Pachoński's entry on Antoni Prusimski, the insurgent Starost of Niszczewice, in the Polish Biographical Dictionary (PSB, 1982) lists this manuscript among its sources and describes a polite reply from Kościuszko, the Naczelnik (commander-in-chief) of the uprising, that resembles this one. But the phrases it quotes from that reply are not in this copy, the entry does not say what it took from the manuscript, and nothing in the collection names Prusimski. The letter is therefore not taken to be his; only new evidence, such as the original or another copy, could show to whom it was written. <!-- context -->
