In 1796 and 1797 the Prussian crown gave Prince Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, a Prussian general, estates in South Prussia – Prussia's share of partitioned Poland – that had been confiscated from their Polish owners. The Prince's own papers show what he was given, how he was put in possession and his title registered, and what the land was worth. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

South Prussia was the province formed from Prussia's share of the second and third partitions of Poland. After the uprising of 1794 the crown confiscated the estates of landowners who had taken part in it, and in 1796 it took the estates of the church into state ownership. Estates of both kinds were granted to officers and officials. Ownership of noble land was recorded in mortgage books kept by the provincial governments, which sat as courts at Poznań and Kalisz. A new owner had to prove his title before it was entered; where an estate came from the crown, the Treasury first resigned its own title in a formal hearing. <!-- context -->

### Contents

#### The grant of 1796

The charter of 9 August 1796 grants Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen the town of Kamionna and the market town of Kolno in the district of Poznań, the villages of Trąbczyn, Szetlewek, Nowawies, Łazy, Old and New Laski, Osiny and the Trąbczyn Hauland in the district of Konin, and Brzyce in Kuyavia <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/2/' | relative_url }}">2</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/3/' | relative_url }}">3</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/4/' | relative_url }}">4</a>]</span>. The estates had been confiscated from Anton von Prusimski for his part in the uprising of 1794 <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/12/' | relative_url }}">12</a>]</span>. A letter of Count Hoym, the minister for the province, reports the King's decision, puts the yearly income of the estates at at least 4,000 Thaler, and recommends a manager <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/39/' | relative_url }}">39</a>]</span>.

#### The grants of 1797

A second charter, of 19 June 1797, grants sixteen villages formerly owned by the church, among them Pszczew, Zagórów, Kopojno, Drzewce and Wrąbczyn. The reason given was that Prusimski's estates had proved heavily indebted <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/5/' | relative_url }}">5</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/8/' | relative_url }}">8</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/9/' | relative_url }}">9</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/10/' | relative_url }}">10</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/17/' | relative_url }}">17</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/22/' | relative_url }}">22</a>]</span>. A declaration of 1 December 1797 adds the outlying parts of the estates of Pszczew, Zagórów and Wieniec <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/6/' | relative_url }}">6</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/7/' | relative_url }}">7</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/11/' | relative_url }}">11</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/18/' | relative_url }}">18</a>]</span>. Both grants are subject to a fixed annual payment to the crown and to the maintenance of the former ecclesiastical owners <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/5/' | relative_url }}">5</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/6/' | relative_url }}">6</a>]</span>.

#### Handover of the estates

The estates were at first handed over only on paper: those who held parts of them on pledge or lease stayed where they were. The King ordered the war and domains chambers at Poznań and Petrikau, which administered the crown's property in the province, to examine their claims and put the Prince's agents in possession <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/40/' | relative_url }}">40</a>]</span>. Hoym explained the delay, and the position of the clergy, who had been managing the former church estates for the Treasury <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/41/' | relative_url }}">41</a>]</span>.

#### Registration of title

Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen was registered as owner of Szetlewek, Kolno, Pszczew, Zagórów and Bątkowo, and the debts on them with him <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/2/' | relative_url }}">2</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/12/' | relative_url }}">12</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/15/' | relative_url }}">15</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/21/' | relative_url }}">21</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/25/' | relative_url }}">25</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/28/' | relative_url }}">28</a>]</span>: on Zagórów 250,000 Rthl owed to von Triebenfeld, parts of it passed on to other creditors <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/21/' | relative_url }}">21</a>]</span>, on Pszczew debts to Johann Wilhelm Glenck and others <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/25/' | relative_url }}">25</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/26/' | relative_url }}">26</a>]</span>.

Glenck, the Prince's building and economy councillor at Wrocław, saw to the registration. The governments at Kalisz and Poznań set and extended deadlines for proving the Prince's title, and Glenck pressed for the Treasury's resignation of the estates of Pszczew and Kuyavia <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/19/' | relative_url }}">19</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/20/' | relative_url }}">20</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/29/' | relative_url }}">29</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/30/' | relative_url }}">30</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/33/' | relative_url }}">33</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/34/' | relative_url }}">34</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/35/' | relative_url }}">35</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/36/' | relative_url }}">36</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/37/' | relative_url }}">37</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/38/' | relative_url }}">38</a>]</span>. The government at Kalisz wanted a certified copy of the judgment confiscating Prusimski's property, which Poznań could not give, the judgment having been given at Toruń <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/31/' | relative_url }}">31</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/42/' | relative_url }}">42</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/43/' | relative_url }}">43</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/44/' | relative_url }}">44</a>]</span>. Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen was also to swear the oath of homage for the granted estates <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/32/' | relative_url }}">32</a>]</span>.

#### Valuations

Kamionna and Kolno were valued, with a reckoning of what they would gain if part of their forest were cleared and settled, and a court valued Szetlewek at 34,803 Rthl gross <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/13/' | relative_url }}">13</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/14/' | relative_url }}">14</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/23/' | relative_url }}">23</a>]</span>.

Most of these papers are certified copies, and the date and place given for one are often those of its certification, not of the original. <!-- context -->
