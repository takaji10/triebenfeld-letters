In 1790 and 1791 the archbishop of Gniezno had every parish of the Konin deanery inspected. For the parish of Trąbczyn the visitors noted its priest, its church, its people village by village, its books and its endowments, and for the neighbouring parish of Zagórów they counted the people. It is the last account of the parish while the Prusimski family still owned Trąbczyn, two years before Prussia took the region. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

A visitation was the inspection of a parish on behalf of its bishop: the visitors asked who the priest was and how he came to the parish, which villages belonged to it and how many souls lived there, what the church held, who had the right to present its priest, and what income and documents it had. The Konin deanery then belonged to the archdiocese of Gniezno; the record is now kept at Włocławek. <!-- context -->

In 1791 the village of Trąbczyn and the villages of its parish belonged to Antoni Prusimski, Starost of Niszczewice, who was also the patron of the church and had presented its priest <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/adwl85/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

In 1793 Prussia took the region in the Second Partition of Poland, and in 1796 the King gave Prusimski's confiscated estate to the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**The parish of Trąbczyn.** The parish priest was the Reverend Józef Zurkowski, born in the diocese of Poznań in 1740 and ordained in 1767, whom the consistory of Gniezno, the archbishop's court for church matters, had instituted at Trąbczyn in 1785 on Antoni Prusimski's presentation. Money had been endowed to keep a vicar, an organist and a schoolmaster, but none of them was at the church. The parish took in Trąbczyn itself, Mariantów, Porąbki, a farm, the new and the old Olędry (the Hollander settlements), Łazy, Osiny and Nowa Wieś, all Prusimski's. It had 760 souls in 1791, Catholics and dissidents (Protestants); the dissidents lived almost all in the Olędry <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/adwl85/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

The church of St Martin was built in the form of a cross, with two wooden chapels, of St Martin and of St Aloysius, and had an organ, a sacristy, a library and a treasury; parts of it needed repair. The Trąmpczyński family, who held Trąbczyn before the Prusimskis, had built part of it, and "the Chamberlain's wife", Katarzyna Prusimska, born Rozdrażewska, had enlarged it, at a time no one could then say; it was consecrated in 1546. The patronage was lay and belonged to Antoni Prusimski. Of its five altars the high altar held a picture of the Mother of God of the Scapular <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/adwl85/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

The parish kept registers of baptisms and marriages from 1597, of burials from 1728 and of the scapular confraternity from 1662, and a description of the benefice of 1688. Books that an earlier visitor, the Reverend Dorposki, had found there had since been given to the creditors of a late parish priest, Paprocki, in payment of his debts <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/adwl85/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

The church's title deeds ran from 1636 to 1697. Sums of money had been charged on the estate of Trąbczyn in 1636 and 1637, the second by a Trąmpczyński, heir of the village, and on Kurów by Stefan Wardencki in 1668, on Strzałkowo in 1670 and on Przespolew, the interest to pay for Masses said at the church. The consistory had awarded the church its tithes in 1692, and in 1694 confirmed its right to an inn and named its school house, a wood and the clearing by it called Porąbki, where three cottagers lived. In 1697 Andrzej Trąmpczyński, heir of part of Trąbczyn, had given land to the church <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/adwl85/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>.

**The parish of Zagórów.** The souls of the neighbouring parish of Zagórów in 1791, counted in twenty-four places, from the town of Zagórów to the Hollander settlement of Holendry Wrąbczyn, among them Łukom, Drzewce, Oleśnica, Kopojno and Skokum: 2734 in all, dissidents living in four of the places only. The figures do not quite add up: the women counted are ten more than the total given for them, and the grand total is six short of the columns. At Myszakowko lived a woman of 110 <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/adwl85/2/' | relative_url }}">2</a>]</span>.
