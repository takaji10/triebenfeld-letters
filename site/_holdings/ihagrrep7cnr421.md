In June 1794, weeks before he joined the rising, the Starost Antoni Prusimski borrowed 12,000 ducats from his neighbour Stanisław Chełmski of Łukom and gave him a written undertaking that, if the money was not repaid by St John's Day 1795, Chełmski might enter Trąbczyn and its villages and hold them until he was paid. Prusimski fled, his property was confiscated, and Trąbczyn was given to the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen. In the autumn of 1799 Chełmski tried to recover the debt from the Fiscus, the Prussian state treasury, which held the confiscated property, and the King refused him. Prusimski's undertaking survives here in his own Polish. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

After the rising of 1794 the Prussian authorities in South Prussia sued its fled participants for the confiscation of their property, and in 1796 gave the confiscated estates to Prussian generals. An order of the King and an explanatory instruction of the ministry of justice, both of September 1796, laid down what the creditors of an insurgent could claim from the Fiscus; Chełmski and his lawyer argued over what they meant. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**Chełmski's petition, October 1799.** Chełmski tells the King that he lent Prusimski 12,000 ducats in gold in 1794 against a written undertaking, that Prusimski fled before the day of payment and was condemned to confiscation, and that he himself took possession of Trąbczyn and Nowawies but had to give them up to the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen when a higher court ruled against him. Referred to the Fiscus, he sued it; the Government at Kalisz first told him to prove that his claim was secured on the estates, then dismissed the suit as a merely personal claim. He argues that the cabinet order of 14 September 1796 makes the Fiscus answer for an insurgent's personal debts out of the confiscated property, and asks to be heard <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/ihagrrep7cnr421/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>]</span>. <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/ihagrrep7cnr421/2/' | relative_url }}">2</a>]</span> The King's answer of 21 October 1799: the Government's decision stands, since every creditor of an insurgent must understand that the costs of putting down the rising and the damage done far exceed the whole estate of such an insurgent <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/ihagrrep7cnr421/1/' | relative_url }}">1</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/ihagrrep7cnr421/3/' | relative_url }}">3</a>]</span>.

**Mondro's petition, December 1799.** Chełmski's lawyer, the criminal councillor Mondro of Kalisz, pressed the complaint again on 7 December 1799: the Government had confused the suit Chełmski first brought against the Prince, as owner of the Trąbczyn estates, with the suit against the Fiscus, and the ministry's own rescript of 1796 required a hearing where a party did not accept a dismissal <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/ihagrrep7cnr421/4/' | relative_url }}">4</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/ihagrrep7cnr421/5/' | relative_url }}">5</a>]</span>. On 23 December he was sent the King's answer of October <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/ihagrrep7cnr421/4/' | relative_url }}">4</a>, <a href="{{ '/documents/ihagrrep7cnr421/7/' | relative_url }}">7</a>]</span>.

**The suit and the bond.** Mondro enclosed a copy of the suit Chełmski had brought against Prusimski before the Government at Poznań, dated 19 March 1799 <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/ihagrrep7cnr421/6/' | relative_url }}">6</a>]</span>. It tells the loan of 16 June 1794, the witnesses Jakub Bieńkowski of Mierzewo and Antoni Przeradzki, Chełmski's entry on the estates when the money was not repaid, the Fiscus's suit to evict him of 7 December 1795, and the settlement that left him to sequester the estates and take his interest at 7 per cent from their income <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/ihagrrep7cnr421/6/' | relative_url }}">6</a>]</span>. With it is the copy of Prusimski's undertaking itself, signed at Łukom on 16 June 1794: if he did not repay on the eve of St John the Baptist 1795, Chełmski might enter Trąbczyn, Nowawies, Osiny, Łazy and all the Hollanders of the estate and hold them, without account, until the sum was paid <span class="cite">[<a href="{{ '/documents/ihagrrep7cnr421/6/' | relative_url }}">6</a>]</span>.
