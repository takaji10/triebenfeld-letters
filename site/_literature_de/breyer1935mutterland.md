<section class="lit-passage" id="p-2">
<h2 class="lit-loc"><a href="#p-2">S. 2</a></h2>
<div class="lit-orig" lang="de" markdown="1">

Der polnische Forscher H. Wiercieński sagt in einer dem Deutschtum in Kongresspolen gewidmeten Abhandlung über die deutsche Einwanderung folgendes:

> *"Die deutschen Siedlungen auf polnischem Boden sind ebenso alt wie Polen selbst. Dies bezeugen uns zahlreiche Namen von Ortschaften und Burgen, auch die Geschichte des Landes und die vieler polnischer Familien spricht davon. Die deutschen Siedler waren durch gemeinsame wirtschaftliche Interessen mit den einheimischen Wirtschaftskreisen verbunden. Durch Einheiraten verstärkte sich das gegenseitige Verhältnis. Der Wahlheimat erwuchsen hierdurch viele verdienstvolle Bürger, die des öfteren von dem Einerlei der Volksmasse abstachen und die für sich und ihre Nachkommenschaft Namen von historischer Bedeutung zu erwerben verstanden. Bis auf den heutigen Tag finden wir unter den hervorragenden Köpfen des Landes viele deutsche Namensträger*<sup class="lit-fn"><a href="#n-de-1">1</a></sup>*."*

Dauernd strömten deutsche Menschen im Laufe der Jahrhunderte mit größeren oder kleineren Unterbrechungen in die Lande jenseits der Oder. Dr. Lück hat in seinem epochemachenden Werk "Deutsche Aufbaukräfte in der Entwicklung Polens" diese deutsche Schicksalsfrage im Osten beleuchtet und uns des öfteren recht bittere Erkenntnisse vermittelt. Seine Forschung erstreckt sich über das gesamte polnische Reichsgebiet und über alle Jahrhunderte des staatlichen Seins der polnischen Nation. Meine Aufgabe ist räumlich und zeitlich nicht so weit gesteckt. Ich will zeigen, dass der Großteil der Deutschen, die im 17., 18. und 19. Jahrhundert nach Mittelpolen eingewandert sind, aus den angrenzenden Ostprovinzen Deutschlands kam, den alten und vor allem dem später durch die Teilungen erworbenen Westpreußen und Posen. Von dorther strömten die deutschen Bauern, Handwerker, Lehrer, Pastoren, Gelehrten und Künstler nach den kulturell unentwickelten Ländern des Ostens, um hier neue Wirkungsstätten zu finden und gute deutsche Arbeit zu leisten.

<span class="lit-note" id="n-de-1">1</span> "Ekonomista", Jahrg. 1908, Bd. II

</div>
<div class="lit-en" lang="en" markdown="1">

The Polish researcher H. Wiercieński, in a treatise on German immigration devoted to the Germans of Congress Poland, says the following:

> *"The German settlements on Polish soil are as old as Poland itself. Numerous names of places and castles bear witness to this; the history of the country, too, and that of many Polish families speaks of it. The German settlers were bound to the native economic circles by common economic interests. Marriage strengthened the mutual relationship. From this the adopted homeland gained many deserving citizens, who often stood out from the sameness of the mass of the people and who knew how to win names of historical importance for themselves and their descendants. To this day we find many bearers of German names among the outstanding minds of the country*<sup class="lit-fn"><a href="#n-en-1">1</a></sup>*."*

Over the centuries German people streamed continually, with greater or lesser interruptions, into the lands beyond the Oder. Dr. Lück, in his epoch-making work "Deutsche Aufbaukräfte in der Entwicklung Polens" (German constructive forces in the development of Poland), has shed light on this German question of fate in the East and has often given us quite bitter insights. His research covers the whole territory of the Polish state and all the centuries of the Polish nation's existence as a state. My task is not drawn so wide in space and time. I want to show that the greater part of the Germans who immigrated into central Poland in the 17th, 18th and 19th centuries came from the neighbouring eastern provinces of Germany, the old ones and above all West Prussia and Posen, acquired later through the partitions. From there German farmers, craftsmen, teachers, pastors, scholars and artists streamed to the culturally undeveloped lands of the East, to find new places to work here and to do good German work.

<span class="lit-note" id="n-en-1">1</span> "Ekonomista", year 1908, vol. II

</div>
</section>

<section class="lit-passage" id="p-3">
<h2 class="lit-loc"><a href="#p-3">S. 3</a></h2>
<div class="lit-orig" lang="de" markdown="1">

Vorarbeiten sind auf diesem Gebiet wenig vorhanden. Die Untersuchung muss daher auf den *Quellen* aufbauen. Als solche kommen in Frage:

> 1. die Kirchenbücher und Archive in den evangelischen Gemeinden des betreffenden Gebietes, in den deutschen Superintendenturen und im Konsistorium zu Warschau;

> 2. die Grundzüge der Mundart (Wenkersche Sätze);

> 3. Vergleich der Ruf- und Familiennamen;

> 4. Bauart der Häuser, Wirtschaftsweise;

> 5. Gesang- und Predigtbücher;

> 6. Volkskundliches Gut.

Mitunter lässt die Dorf- und Flurform auf die Stammesart und die Herkunft der Bewohner schließen. Neben diese allgemeinen Quellen treten besondere für einzelne Deutschtumsgruppen. Während für das Dorf die *Schulzenlade* in den meisten Fällen erstklassiges Material zur Frage nach der Herkunft liefert, treten in der Stadt an ihre Stelle die *Innungsladen* der deutschen Zünfte, auch vielfach im Familienbesitz befindliche alte Schriftstücke, Reisepässe, Zeugnisse und dergleichen.

Das Ausfragen der alten Leute hatte in den seltensten Fällen Erfolg. Bei der Landbevölkerung, die ja zumeist 200 bis 300 Jahre außerhalb der Grenzen Deutschlands im polnischen Umvolk lebt, ist die Erinnerung an die alte Heimat beinahe restlos geschwunden. Eine löbliche Ausnahme bilden in dieser Hinsicht die zur preußischen Zeit angesiedelten Schwaben, was zum Teil ihrer größeren Intelligenz zuzuschreiben ist.

Auf große Schwierigkeiten stieß mitunter das Kartieren der aufgefundenen Herkunftsorte, besonders der Kleindörfer und Weiler. In vielen Fällen halfen weder alte noch neue Ortsverzeichnisse. Vortreffliche Hilfe leisteten mitunter alte topographische Karten, aber auch neuere Karten in großem Maßstabe.

</div>
<div class="lit-en" lang="en" markdown="1">

Little preliminary work exists in this field. The inquiry must therefore be built on the *sources*. These come into question:

> 1. the church books and archives in the Lutheran congregations of the area in question, in the German superintendencies and in the Consistory at Warsaw;

> 2. the main features of the dialect (Wenker's sentences);

> 3. comparison of the first names and family names;

> 4. the way the houses are built, the way of farming;

> 5. hymn books and books of sermons;

> 6. folklore.

Sometimes the form of the village and of its fields allows a conclusion about the stock and the origin of the inhabitants. Beside these general sources there are special ones for particular groups of Germans. While for the village the *Schulzenlade* (the headman's chest of village papers) in most cases supplies first-class material on the question of origin, in the town its place is taken by the *Innungsladen* (the guild chests) of the German guilds, and often by old papers in the possession of families, passports, certificates and the like.

Questioning the old people succeeded only in the rarest cases. Among the country people, who have mostly lived for 200 to 300 years outside the borders of Germany among the surrounding Polish people (Umvolk), the memory of the old homeland has almost entirely vanished. A praiseworthy exception in this respect is the Swabians settled in the Prussian period, which is partly to be ascribed to their greater intelligence.

Mapping the places of origin found sometimes met great difficulties, especially for the small villages and hamlets. In many cases neither old nor new gazetteers helped. Old topographical maps sometimes gave excellent help, but so did newer maps on a large scale.

</div>
</section>

<h2 class="lit-loc"><a href="#p-4">S. 4</a></h2>
<div class="lit-orig" lang="de" markdown="1">

Im folgenden soll für drei ständische Gruppen des mittelpolnischen Deutschtums der Nachweis ihrer Herkunft geführt werden:

> 1. die ländlichen Siedler;

> 2. das deutsche Bürgertum und den deutschen Handwerkerstand;

> 3. die evangelische Geistlichkeit, die deutsche Lehrerschaft und die Vertreter der freien Berufe.

Die erste *ländliche Siedlung* auf mittelpolnischem Boden ist unserer gegenwärtigen Kenntnis nach das 1605 begründete Weichseldorf Słońsk - im Volksmund Schlonske<sup class="lit-fn"><a href="#n-de-2">2</a></sup> genannt - unweit des bekannten Soolbades Ciechocinek. Kurz nacheinander folgten dann weichselaufwärts eine ganze Reihe von Neugründungen, so Alt-Bogpomoż, Lentzen, Neu-Bogpomoż, Woluszewo. Der polnische Geschichtsforscher J. Baranowski sagt in seiner Abhandlung "Wsie holenderskie na ziemiach polskich":

> *"Auf diese Weise waren zu Beginn des 17. Jahrhunderts beide Ufer zwischen Thorn und Breslau [sic - gemeint ist wahrscheinlich Leslau/Włocławek - JD] mit holländischen Kolonien besät*<sup class="lit-fn"><a href="#n-de-3">3</a></sup>*."*

Die Siedler dieser Niederungsdörfer, die auch in den Kirchenbüchern des 17. Jahrhunderts der evangelischen Gemeinde zu Thorn als "Holländer" zu Lentzen, zu Wolluschewo usw. angesprochen werden, stammen zum überwiegenden Teil aus den Weichseldörfern um Thorn, Kulm, Graudenz und Schwetz. Schmidt sagt in seinem "Deutschtum des Landes Posen", dass bereits um 1600 unter- und oberhalb von Thorn, in den Dörfern Gurske, Nessau, Silno, Dybow zahlreiche lutherische "Holländer" saßen, da die streng bekenntnistreu eingestellte Thorner Stadtverwaltung Mennoniten als Siedler nicht gern sah. Die Thorner Niederungsdörfer waren zumeist Tochtersiedlungen der weichselabwärts gelegenen, um die Mitte des 16. Jahrhunderts entstandenen Holländereien. Die Insassen der Holländereien waren keinesfalls dem Stamme nach Holländer. In dieser Hinsicht stimmen wir der Ansicht von Schmidt zu, dass nur in den ersten Jahrzehnten an der Urbarmachung des Danziger Werders wirkliche Holländer beteiligt waren. Im Laufe der Zeit übernahmen auch

<span class="lit-note" id="n-de-3">3</span> "Przegląd Hist." Jhrg. 1915, Bd. 19, H. 1.

</div>
<div class="lit-en" lang="en" markdown="1">

In what follows, the origin of three groups of the Germans of central Poland, by their station, is to be proved:

> 1. the rural settlers;

> 2. the German burghers and the German craftsmen;

> 3. the Lutheran clergy, the German teachers and the members of the free professions.

The first *rural settlement* on the soil of central Poland is, to our present knowledge, the Vistula village of Słońsk, founded in 1605 and called Schlonske in the popular speech<sup class="lit-fn"><a href="#n-en-2">2</a></sup>, not far from the well-known salt-water spa of Ciechocinek. In quick succession a whole series of new foundations followed up the Vistula, such as Alt-Bogpomoż, Lentzen, Neu-Bogpomoż, Woluszewo. The Polish historian J. Baranowski says in his treatise "Wsie holenderskie na ziemiach polskich" (Dutch villages in the Polish lands):

> *"In this way at the beginning of the 17th century both banks between Thorn and Breslau [sic - probably meant is Leslau/Włocławek - JD] were sown with Dutch colonies*<sup class="lit-fn"><a href="#n-en-3">3</a></sup>*."*

The settlers of these lowland villages, who are also called "Holländer" at Lentzen, at Wolluschewo and so on in the 17th-century church books of the Lutheran congregation at Thorn, came for the greater part from the Vistula villages around Thorn, Kulm, Graudenz and Schwetz. Schmidt says in his "Deutschtum des Landes Posen" (The Germans of the Posen country) that as early as about 1600 numerous Lutheran "Holländer" were settled below and above Thorn, in the villages of Gurske, Nessau, Silno and Dybow, because the strictly orthodox town government of Thorn did not like Mennonites as settlers. The Thorn lowland villages were mostly daughter settlements of the Holländereien (the Dutch-law settlements) lower down the Vistula, which had arisen about the middle of the 16th century. The inmates of the Holländereien were by no means Dutch by stock. In this respect we agree with Schmidt's view that real Dutchmen took part only in the first decades of the clearing of the Danzig Werder. In the course of time the

German farmers of the neighbouring marsh areas also took over the Dutch way of farming. So the name "Holländer" passed to them in the legal and technical sense.

According to the Thorn church books, a small number of settlers from eastern Pomerania, from the neighbourhood of Christenberg, Schifelbein, Kolberg and Latzken, also lived in the new Vistula villages.

A comparison of the present *family names* with those of the old Thorn church books convinces us of the likeness of the names; a proof that the Vistula Germans have long been settled. Names such as Leuschner, Heise, Milke, Kersten, Gollnig, Moltzahn, Netzel, Kröning, Raths and so on bear witness to this. After 1700 the description "Holländer" disappears from the Thorn church books; it is replaced by farmers' names from Slonske, Woluschewo and so on.

About 1740 settlement in the lowland villages began to quicken. One after another numerous villages arose, up to the mouth of the river Wieprz, 100 km south of Warsaw. Of the origin of these settlers we receive reliable information from the church books of the Lutheran congregations of *Gombin, Iłow, Plozk* and *Neuhof*. Most came from the surplus population of the old settlements between Thorn and Wlozlawek. But settlers from the lowland villages *below* Thorn, Bromberg and Graudenz also took part in the clearing of the Vistula meadows; some even came from East Prussia.

Going through five years of the registers of the congregation of *Gombin*, founded in 1832, gave 45 statements of origin for the lowland villages. Of these West Prussia had 30, the old Vistula villages of central Poland 15; in detail, the neighbourhood of Thorn 12, Slonske 9, Graudenz 5, Schwetz 4, Bogpomoż and Dembe 3, Bromberg 2, and Fordon, Neuenburg, Straßburg, Riesenburg, Mewe, Mehlsack, Braunsberg and Driesen on the Warthe one family each.

The church books of the Lutheran parish of *Iłow*, founded in 1775, contain for the period 1779 to 1840 the following 80 places of origin, which we list going up the Vistula: Danzig Werder, Baldrum near Marienwerder, Neuenburg, Montau 1 settler each, Graudenz 4, the Kulm lowland 2, Bromberg, Flötenau 1 each; from Neidenburg, Amt Schoden, Bergbruch, Neuenort, Altretz 1 settler each, Altendorf (3), the neighbourhood of Thorn (11), Nischewke 19, Deutsch-Glinne 9, Slonske 9, Neu-Ciechocinek (2), Woluschewo and Rybit (1), Bogpomoż 5, Witoschiner Lengde, Liciszewo near Lipno 1 family each.

In the civil registers of the congregation at *Neuhof* (Nowydwór), which arose in 1780, we find for 1782 to 1806 the following places of origin: Danzig 1, Langenau 6, Bromberg 2, Straßburg in East Prussia 2, the neighbourhood of Rypin 5, the neighbourhood of Thorn 4, Slonske and Rybit 1 each, Bogpomoż and Osieker Lengden 2.

If we sum up the statements on the origin of the German farmers in the Vistula lowland, it follows that *95 in a hundred of the first settlers came from the Vistula country around Thorn and down the Vistula*. 5 in a hundred came from the Dobrzyń country (Dobriner Land), related by stock. The Vistula villages that arose after 1750 are mainly daughter settlements; they formed a widely branched, one might say, great family of one stock. Characteristic of the Vistula lowland, too, is the exclusive use of the *Thorn* and *Rogal* (Königsberg) *hymn books*. The Mennonites of our area, whose number has now greatly shrunk, came after 1760 mostly from the lowland south of Thorn.

The German country people of the *Dobrzyń* country, who began to immigrate about 1700, came from the neighbouring counties of West Prussia, and to a lesser part also of East Prussia. Part of the town population of Lipno and Rypin also came from the Netze district (Netzegau) and from the Reich.

The baptism, marriage and death registers of the congregation of *Rypin*, 1785 to 1854, give 127 statements of origin: of these Straßburg county 41 or 31 in a hundred, Graudenz county 11 or 8 in a hundred, Thorn county 8 statements, Briesen 7, Marienwerder and Kulm 4 each, Schwetz, Soldau, Bromberg, Osterode, Königsberg 3 each, Mohrungen, Mewe, Rosenberg in West Prussia, Pr.-Holland 2 each, Angerburg, Goldap, Darkehmen, Lyck, Gilgenburg, Kalminnen, Insterburg, Pr.-Eylau, Riesenburg, Johannisburg, Hammerstein, Wohlsin 1 statement each. The townspeople came from Konitz, Tuchel, Filehne, Nakel, Lobsens, Labischin, Schubin; from the Reich came craftsmen from Berlin and Hamburg, 2 each, and from Mühlhausen in Alsace, Stuttgart and Reichenbach in Silesia, 1 each.

Beside the clergyman, German elementary schoolteachers came into the newly arisen towns and villages, for the greater part from the neighbouring German provinces. Numerous school lists in the church archives bear witness to this.

In the old Polish time these settlers were already Germans abroad (Auslanddeutsche), who had lived for *centuries* in the western areas of Poland, had faithfully kept their nationality, and kept up schools and churches from their own means.

If the migration of the rural settlers went on quietly, unnoticed and evenly, that cannot be said of the immigration of the cloth-makers. The removal of 10,000 families to Congress Poland was a terrible bloodletting for the German border areas, from which they have hardly been able to recover. The empty houses in the former cloth-making towns had to be pulled down by order of the police, in Rawitsch for example some 55, as H. Barten reports in his work "Siedlung in Südwestposen" (Settlement in south-western Posen). But this by the way.

The guiding idea of our work is to establish as exactly as possible the make-up of the Germans by stock. Only this sure foundation makes possible, taking into account the very different colonising abilities, strengths and weaknesses of the individual stocks, a full understanding of the historical development and of the prospects of the Germans of central Poland.
