By the twentieth century the manor of Trąbczyn no longer belonged to the Miączyński
family, who had held it since 1807; it had been sold in 1892. Its remaining land was bought by a man named Jesse and called
Włodzimierow, and about 1911 it too was parcelled out, almost all of it to German
settlers ([Albert Breyer's notebook]({{ '/documents/app539680669/2/' | relative_url }})).
The [Olęder](/glossary/#haulaender) settlements of the estate — Łazińsk and Emilienheim
among them — were now villages of the commune of Trąbczyn in the Polish state.

Albert Breyer, a teacher at the German school in Sompolno and a historian of the German
settlements in Poland, went to Łazińsk and
Emilienheim in July 1936 and wrote down what the settlers told him: about their land and
their schools, who had gone to Volhynia, Russia, America or Brazil, how they spoke, and
how they marked their graves
([his notes]({{ '/sources/app539680669/' | relative_url }})). In a notebook of 1935
and 1936 he counted the farmers of Łazińsk — 87 in 1840, 146 by 1935
([his table]({{ '/documents/app539680716/7/' | relative_url }})) — and took notes on
Orlin and Józefów: their speech, their houses and customs, their poverty, and their
dealings with the Poles
([his notes]({{ '/sources/app539680716/' | relative_url }})).

He also copied the tables of 1868 that had made the settlers owners of their land (see
[the colonists' land and forest rights]({{ '/eras/wlodzimierz/' | relative_url }})). Three
years later the war began. In 1940 the German occupation renamed the commune of Trąbczyn
*Drommin*, an administrative district (Amtsbezirk) under a German official at Grabina; the name was confirmed in
1943 and lasted, like the occupation, to 1945.
