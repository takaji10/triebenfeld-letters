Land in this part of Europe changed hands by document — a boundary was a line of earth
mounds attested by a court, a gift was a charter with a seal on it — and Trąbczyn's
documents begin with gifts. In 1173 Duke Mieszko gives the hamlet of Wrąbczynek, by
Trąbczyn, to the Cistercian abbey he has founded at Ląd on the Warta, with its salt, its
lakes, its fishing and hunting, free of all Polish dues
([the gift]({{ '/documents/kdw/1/' | relative_url }})). In 1240 Archbishop Fulko of
Gniezno gives the same abbey the tithes of the chapels of Zagórów, Skokum, Kopojno,
Trąbczyn, Szetlew, Łukom and Wrąbczyn, "whether the villages are divided or moved or
called by another name"
([the tithes]({{ '/documents/kdw/2/' | relative_url }})). The clause was written for the
future, and it worked: a century and a half later the Archbishop's successor is still
adjudging Trąbczyn's sheaf tithe to the abbey, against the protests of Trąbczyn's own
parish priest ([the judgment of 1399]({{ '/documents/kdw/13/' | relative_url }})).

Trąbczyn itself was the duke's to give, and he had given it twice before it stuck: first
to Count Janko, then to a priest Mikołaj, both dead without heirs. In 1276 Duke Bolesław
gives it to Count Mikołaj, his treasurer at Ląd, and his heirs, free of the ducal dues
([the gift]({{ '/documents/kdw/5/' | relative_url }})) — the first owners of Trąbczyn
whose line the record follows. Their heirs appear in the lawsuits of the next two
centuries, and not always successfully: in 1346 the land judge at Pyzdry adjudges
Stropiszyn and Pępocin to the church of Gniezno against the brothers Jan and Przedpełk,
heirs of Trąbczyn, and puts them to perpetual silence
([the judgments]({{ '/documents/kdw/9/' | relative_url }})).

The abbey's own share of the country grew by purchase and exchange, and in 1407 it bore
its most lasting fruit: King Władysław allows the abbey to found a town at its village of
Zagórów under the law of Magdeburg, with a weekly market on Wednesdays
([the town privilege]({{ '/documents/kdw/14/' | relative_url }})). The town of Zagórów —
not Trąbczyn — is what the abbey's lordship left on the map.

None of this survives as archived paper: the Middle Ages of this country are known from
the printed edition of its charters, the Kodeks Dyplomatyczny Wielkopolski, and every
link above opens a charter's entry in the Kodeks. The era's documents
end in the 1430s; when the record of Trąbczyn resumes, in 1589, it resumes as a lawsuit.
