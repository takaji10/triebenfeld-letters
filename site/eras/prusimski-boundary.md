A boundary in this country was a physical thing before it was a line on paper: mounds of
earth thrown up along an agreed line, notches cut into trees, and witnesses old enough to
remember where both had stood. Trąbczyn and Łukom were two estates whose boundary had
never been fixed with care, and the argument about it begins in the records in June 1589,
when Albertus Trąmpczyński charges Thomas Łukomski before the land court at Konin with
building a pond at Łukom that flooded woodland and meadow of Trąbczyn, Nowa Wieś and
Osiny ([the order for an inspection]({{ '/documents/app536017/1/' | relative_url }})).

By 1644 the quarrel had changed shape. Jerzy Chełmski, now owner of Łukom, charges the
Trąmpczyński brothers of Trąbczyn with sending their subjects into his wood to cut some
eighty boundary marks into the trees — taking a large part of his ground, as he says —
and with felling about a hundred building trees; they do not appear, and the court
condemns them by default
([the judgments]({{ '/documents/app536036/1/' | relative_url }})). From then on the
dispute is about where the boundary runs, and everything else follows from that.

By the 1720s Trąbczyn belongs to the Prusimski family. The methods of the quarrel stay
physical: in 1728 the Chełmski brothers charge Krzysztof Prusimski and his son Antoni
with burning shingles and a hut on their ground, seizing four oxen in an armed raid and
carting off hay, and the court sends a sworn commissioner to examine old witnesses on
oath about who owns the disputed ground
([the decree]({{ '/documents/app536040/1/' | relative_url }})). In 1767 the new heir,
Antoni Prusimski, learns from his court messenger that an embankment raised by the people
of Łukom has backed the water up and flooded the [Olęder](/glossary/#haulaender)
settlers in the Trąbczyn woods, with their fields and meadows
([the reports]({{ '/documents/app53170koningr114/1/' | relative_url }})).

The wood called Lusnia — pine, with an Olęder settlement lately founded in it, a tavern
and twelve settlers — changed hands by force of decree in 1771: Stanisław Chełmski, who
had won before the Crown Tribunal, is put in possession of it, and the settlers are
ordered to obey him ([the delivery]({{ '/documents/app53150kaliszgr414/1/' | relative_url }})).
Six weeks later his own messenger reports the pond dam dug through and the fish let out,
loopholes cut in the tavern's walls and its windows shot to pieces — the work, he says,
of more than thirty Trąbczyn subjects in Prusimski's presence
([the reports]({{ '/documents/app53170koningr115/5/' | relative_url }})).

The Polish parliament tried to end the matter. The commission it appointed met in the
forest between the two estates in September 1775 and split: the commissioners under
Ludwik Dąmbski, voivode of Brześć Kujawski, found Antoni Prusimski's boundary line the
better, had mounds raised along it and sentenced Stanisław Chełmski to the tower; the
commissioners Chełmski had brought gave a decree of their own for his line
([the decree]({{ '/documents/app53170koningr145/1/' | relative_url }})). Each side
protested against the other's decree, and nothing was settled — while a second quarrel
opened on the money side, Chełmski having bought a merchant's claim against Antoni's late
uncle Paweł and sued on it
([the suit]({{ '/documents/app536046/2/' | relative_url }})).

A law of 1776 sent causes left with two contradictory decrees to the Crown Tribunal, and
its decision of 23 January 1782 is the dispute's turning point: the corner of Trąbczyn,
Łukom and Drzewce is fixed as Prusimski's commissioners had it, a straight line is drawn
between the two points on the map, and the Olęder settlers Chełmski had taken are ordered
back to Prusimski, all but one, Martin Hunt
([the Tribunal's decree]({{ '/documents/app53170koningr153/1/' | relative_url }})).
Carrying the line out took the rest of the year. Two boundary chamberlains, one engaged
by each side, disagreed; Chełmski in person pushed away the man with the surveyor's chain
and kicked it off the line, and the chamberlain on Prusimski's side stopped and asked the
War Department for soldiers
([the protests]({{ '/documents/app53170koningr118/17/' | relative_url }})). The Tribunal
quashed both chamberlains' acts, and the boundary chamberlain of Gniezno then did the
work: forty-one mounds thrown up along the line, thirty-one stakes driven into the marsh,
the older mounds of both sides levelled, and Martin Hunt delivered
([the decree]({{ '/documents/app53170koningr153/2/' | relative_url }})). Two centuries of
dispute were decided.

The decree of 1782 settled the boundary in law, but the quarrels between the two estates did not end with it. On the day the chamberlain's court opened, Chełmski's people felled
221 pines on Trąbczyn's side of the new line
([the protest]({{ '/documents/app53170koningr119/1/' | relative_url }})); suits and
counter-suits followed through 1783. The last entry of the court books, of June 1788, is
small and characteristic: Prusimski's forest keeper stops a peasant of Łomowo, one of
Chełmski's villages, carting a felled building tree out of the pine wood, and cart, oxen,
cow and mare are shown to the office at Konin and given back
([the entry]({{ '/documents/app53170koningr121/1/' | relative_url }})). Six years later
the estates were confiscated.

### The succession of the Prusimski brothers

A second quarrel of the same years ran inside the family. The brothers Antoni and Paweł
Prusimski died without children, and their whole substance, paternal and maternal, fell
to be divided as their father Krzysztof's division of 1740 had left it. The sisters'
heirs sued for their shares; the abbey of Ląd was cited over the jewels and silver of the
house lodged in its treasury; and the Radoliński claim of 43,000 florins Polish on
Trąbczyn, disputed on suspicion of forgery, the court left undecided. On 29 March 1784
the condescensio sat on the Trąbczyn grounds and gave its decree: the maternal fortune
divided among the six capits, the paternal substance adjudged to the brothers' nephew,
Antoni in Kolno Prusimski, [starost](/glossary/#starost) of Niszczewice
([the decree]({{ '/documents/app5320pozgr1181/1/' | relative_url }})).

### The parish

The visitation of the Konin deanery of 1790 and 1791 describes the parish the quarrel
circled: the church of St Martin, consecrated in 1546, built in part by the
Trąmpczyński family and enlarged by the widow Prusimska; a priest presented by the
heir; and 760 souls in the villages of the parish, the dissidents among them living
almost all in the Olęder settlements
([the visitation]({{ '/documents/adwl85/1/' | relative_url }})).

A fuller account of these years is still to be written.
