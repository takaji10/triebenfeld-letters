Michalina Miączyńska died in 1851, and the estate passed to her son, Count Włodzimierz
Miączyński. What he inherited was a settlement landscape with a flaw in its title: the
[Olęder](/glossary/#haulaender) colonies on the estate — Staro-Łazińska and
Nowo-Łazińska — held their farms on contracts the Hohenlohe years had made, and his
family had never signed anything.

The colonists sued him in 1859 over their rights of pasture and wood in his forest. His
answer, in October 1860, was a counterclaim against ninety-seven of them before the Civil
Tribunal at Kalisz: the court was asked to hold that the contract Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen
had made with fourteen colonists on 18 May 1805 did not bind him — because the return of
the estate in 1807 had cancelled what Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen had undertaken
([the printed summons]({{ '/documents/app539680783/1/' | relative_url }})). It is the
Supreme Tribunal's doctrine of 1826, the one that had freed his mother from the
Hohenlohe mortgages, now aimed the other way, at the colonists' rights. What the court
decided is not in the papers.

The law then did what the lawsuit had not. The Russian reform of 1864 made the peasants
of the Kingdom of Poland owners of the land they farmed; each village received a
liquidation table fixing its farmsteads, the land that went with them, and the capital
the government paid the former owner for the dues the peasants had owed. For the Łazińsk
colony the table, confirmed on 8 February 1868, lists ninety-one farmsteads, the school,
the cemetery and the common land — 1,691 [Morgen](/glossary/#morgen) 219 rods in all —
and a liquidation capital of 10,055 roubles 70 kopecks for the former owner
([the table]({{ '/documents/apl399806603/1/' | relative_url }})). Its copy of 1869 leaves
the settlers what remained of their old rights: to graze their cattle in the forest
"Studzianka" and gather fallen wood there twice a week, and no other
([the copy]({{ '/documents/apl399806603/2/' | relative_url }})).

Even that was fenced into numbers. In 1884 the fuel each farmstead might take from the
forest was fixed at 104 cloth-loads a year
([the inscription]({{ '/documents/apl399806603/3/' | relative_url }})). And in 1892 the
colonists gave the rights up altogether, for land: by a voluntary transaction confirmed
in 1894, their pasture and wood-gathering in "Studzianka" passed once and for all to the
owner of Trąbczyn, Countess Maria Wielopolska, for 393 Morgen 154 rods of manor land —
four Morgen to most farmsteads, six to seven of them
([the inscription of 1894]({{ '/documents/apl399806603/4/' | relative_url }})). A survey
of 1896 measured the whole allotment and each farmstead's share
([the statement]({{ '/documents/apl399806603/6/' | relative_url }})).

The historian Albert Breyer copied these tables in the 1930s, when the settlements were
still there to be visited; his copies stand with this era, and his visits with the next.
