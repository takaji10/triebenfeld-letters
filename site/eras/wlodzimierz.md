Michalina Miączyńska died in 1851, and the estate passed to her son, Count Włodzimierz
Miączyński. What he inherited was a settlement landscape with a flaw in its title: the
[Olęder]({{ '/glossary/#haulaender' | relative_url }}) colonies on the estate — Staro-Łazińska and
Nowo-Łazińska — held their farms on contracts made between 1805 and 1807 by Prince Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen,
to whom Prussia had given the estate before Michalina recovered it, and his family had
never signed them.

The colonists sued him in 1859 over their rights of pasture and wood in his forest. His
answer, in October 1860, was a counterclaim against ninety-seven of them before the Civil
Tribunal at Kalisz: the court was asked to hold that the contract Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen
had made with fourteen colonists on 18 May 1805 did not bind him — because the return of
the estate in 1807 had cancelled what Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen had undertaken
([the printed summons]({{ '/documents/app539680783/1/' | relative_url }})). It was the
argument by which the Supreme Tribunal had freed his mother of the Prince's mortgages in
1826, now turned against the colonists' rights. How the court decided is not known.

The Russian reform of 1864 made the peasants
of the Kingdom of Poland owners of the land they farmed; each village received a
liquidation table fixing its farmsteads, the land that went with them, and the capital
the government paid the former owner for the dues the peasants had owed. For the Łazińsk
colony the table, confirmed on 8 February 1868, lists ninety-one farmsteads, the school,
the cemetery and the common land — 1,691 [Morgen]({{ '/glossary/#morgen' | relative_url }}) 219 rods in all —
and a liquidation capital of 10,055 roubles 70 kopecks for the former owner
([the table]({{ '/documents/apl399806603/1/' | relative_url }})). Its copy of 1869 leaves
the settlers what remained of their old rights: to graze their cattle in the forest
"Studzianka" and gather fallen wood there twice a week, and no other
([the copy]({{ '/documents/apl399806603/2/' | relative_url }})).

Even those rights were then measured out. In 1884 the governorate's board fixed the fuel
each farmstead might take from the forest at 104 cloth-loads a year,
about ten two-horse cartloads,
([the inscription]({{ '/documents/apl399806603/3/' | relative_url }})). And in 1892 the
colonists gave the rights up altogether, for land: by an agreement confirmed
in 1894, their pasture and wood-gathering in "Studzianka" passed once and for all to the
new owner of Trąbczyn, Countess Maria Wielopolska, who had bought the estate from the
Miączyński heirs, for 393 Morgen 154 rods of manor land —
four Morgen to most farmsteads, six to seven of them
([the inscription of 1894]({{ '/documents/apl399806603/4/' | relative_url }})). A survey
of 1896 measured the colony's land and each farmstead's share
([the statement]({{ '/documents/apl399806603/6/' | relative_url }})).

The colonists were Lutherans of the parish of Zagórów. In 1882 its pastor, E. Erdmann, reported to the consistory in Warsaw, which governed the Lutheran church of the Kingdom, on the parish and its filial at Pyzdry: a brick church still without font, organ and bells, eight schools with about 240 children, eight cemeteries and 1,597 communicants ([the parish]({{ '/documents/agad14320139/1/' | relative_url }})); at Pyzdry no church of its own, five schools with about 120 children and 734 communicants ([the filial]({{ '/documents/agad14320139/2/' | relative_url }})).
