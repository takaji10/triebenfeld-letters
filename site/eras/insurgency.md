*One of the seven eras of the [Timeline]({{ '/timeline/' | relative_url }}).*

In 1793 Russia and Prussia took a second share of Poland's lands; Prussia made the country
around Konin, with Trąbczyn and Zagórów, part of its new province of
[South Prussia](/glossary/#south-prussia). In 1794 the Poles rose against Russia and
Prussia under Tadeusz Kościuszko, and Antoni Prusimski —
[starost](/glossary/#starost) of Niszczewice, owner of Trąbczyn, Kamionna and Kolno —
joined them. The rising failed, and in 1795 Russia, Prussia and Austria divided what was
left of Poland. The Prussian crown confiscated the estates of those who had taken part:
the Prussian authorities named Prusimski as one of the rising's leaders, and he fled
([the mortgage certificate]({{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/12/' | relative_url }})). In 1796
the King of Prussia gave Trąbczyn, Kamionna and Kolno to one of his generals, Prince
Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen.

Prusimski spent the years that followed abroad. In December 1796 the Prussian resident,
the King's diplomatic agent, at Venice reported that Prusimski had come to him ill and
broken by the death of his wife, and had asked for a certificate that he could not travel
([the resident's report]({{ '/documents/ihagrrep7cnr1414/2/' | relative_url }})). By
his daughter's later account, the certificate was meant to excuse him to the Prussian
authorities, who had summoned him to justify his leaving the country.

He was still abroad, and alive, in 1803. On 27 January of that year he and Xaver Jezierski,
a canon of Włocławek who was said to have worked with him in the rising in Kuyavia, the
region around Włocławek, petitioned the King from Dresden to be allowed home. Two
ministers, the Grand Chancellor Goldbeck and Count Haugwitz, reported on the case: the
rising in Kuyavia was said to have been agreed at his Smiłowice estates in August 1794,
an accusation never closely examined; his wife had died in Italy; his Trąbczyn estates had
gone to Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, his Pawłów estates to General von Sanitz and his Smiłowice
estates to General von Bischofswerder
([the report]({{ '/documents/ihagrrep7cnr3132/5/' | relative_url }})). On 10 May 1803 the
King let both men return, but they did not get their property back
([the cabinet order]({{ '/documents/ihagrrep7cnr3132/6/' | relative_url }})), and the
Prussian chamber at Poznań was to take their oath of allegiance to him.

In Paris in September 1798, in exile after the defeat, Kościuszko wrote to an émigré
correspondent who had written to him from Dresden in August, promising to stand with
every true republican when the time came to regain the country's independence
([the letter in copy]({{ '/documents/ossol2289/1/' | relative_url }})). It survives in a
copy made by the Lwów bibliographer Stanisław Przyłęcki.
