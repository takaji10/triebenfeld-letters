*One of the seven eras of the [Timeline]({{ '/timeline/' | relative_url }}).*

In 1794 the Poles rose against the partitioning powers, and Antoni Prusimski —
[starost](/glossary/#starost) of Niszczewice, owner of Trąbczyn, Kamionna and Kolno —
joined them. The rising failed, and the Prussian crown confiscated the estates of those
who had taken part: Prusimski was named by the Prussian authorities as one of its
leaders, and he fled
([the mortgage certificate that records it]({{ '/documents/oe1bu14525/12/' | relative_url }})).
Trąbczyn, Kamionna and Kolno, the estates that Prussia took from Prusimski, are the estates that the King of Prussia will give to one of his generals, Prince Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, two years later.

The state that took them was new. Prussia had made its separate peace at Basel in April
1795, and in October the three partitioning powers divided what remained of Poland;
Prussia annexed the province it called [South Prussia](/glossary/#south-prussia), and
Trąbczyn, Zagórów and their neighbours passed onto the map of the Prussian state.

Prusimski spent the years that followed abroad. In December 1796 the Prussian resident at
Venice reported that the dispossessed owner had come to him ill and broken by the death
of his wife, and had asked for a certificate that he could not travel
([the resident's dispatch]({{ '/documents/ihagrrep7cnr1414/2/' | relative_url }})). By
his daughter's later account, the certificate was meant to excuse him to the Prussian
authorities, who had summoned him to justify his leaving the country.

The era's one document so far is a letter of the man the rising is named for. In Paris in
September 1798, in exile after the defeat, Tadeusz Kościuszko wrote to an émigré
correspondent who had written to him from Dresden in August
([the letter in copy]({{ '/documents/ossol2289/1/' | relative_url }})). It survives in
the copy collection "Listy, dokumenty i wiersze z lat 1697–1798" made mostly by the Lwów
bibliographer Stanisław Przyłęcki and bought by the Ossolineum with his manuscript legacy
in 1869. The collection holds more; this era is one document deep, and the rest of its
papers are still to be transcribed.
