<div class="lit-summary" markdown="1">

A collective volume of some 330 printed pages, issued by the Municipal Office in Zagórów in 2024, edited by Mirosław Słowiński and Katarzyna Połom, with the book concept and the introduction by Słowiński, an editorial committee under Łukasz Parus, Dr Zbigniew Chodyła as scientific consultant, and the German-language material translated by a team of seven (Jacek Kin the greater part). The cover and one inner picture show the clock face given in 1927 to the tower of the Zagórów Evangelical church by Luiza and Ferdynand Krüger of Olchowo. The book grew out of the search for the region's Protestants that the Zagórów House of Culture announced in 2020; the money for the printing came from the town's budget, the starosta's office, an internet collection of 9,910 zł and the benefit concert "Muzyka i Sacrum" of 23 September 2023 in the Evangelical church (3,200 zł). It is a *Silva rerum* of the German-Evangelical community of the Zagórów land, in three parts, and closes with a German summary of the whole (the "Kurzfassung", translated by Kin) and the list of the donors.

### Part I: History and Memory

**Bogdan Grzonkowski, "The Protestants of the Zagórów Land (16th to 21st Centuries)"** (text prepared for print by Katarzyna Połom). The survey the edition's Protestant ground hangs on: the Reformation reaches the Ląd monastery's lands in 1520-1525 - the monastery itself seized by it, its abbot's correspondence with Wittenberg, the Zagórów coat of arms read as the Luther Rose, the monks driven to Henryków in Silesia in 1553 - and the Counter-Reformation under Abbot Jan Wysocki ends it; the Cossack emissary rising of 1651 among the Ląd peasants. Then the eighteenth century: the Hauländer settlement from 1746, when the owner of Trąbczyn, Paweł z Kolna Prusimski, permits the founding of the Łazińsk colony and of Trąbczyńska Huta in the forest; after 1804 the ring of colonies around Zagórów named with their German-era names - Olchowo (Sophiental), Mały Las (Ludwigslust), Tarszewo (Friedlichsfeld), Michalinów Oleśnicki (Emilienheim), Anielewo (Ingelfingen), Chruściki (Adelhof), Augustynów (Augustinhof), Zalesie. A table of 1835 counts 3,245 German Protestant colonists in twenty localities of the nine gminas around Zagórów - in the gmina of Trąbczyn alone 898: Łazińsk 610, Michalinów Trąbczyński 185, Trąbczyńska Huta 103. The oldest cantorate was Łazińsk's (a new house of prayer for 300 built 1838); others at Olchowo, both Michalinóws, Anielewo and Tarszewo. The parish's beginnings: pastoral care from Września, Grodziec, Konin and Żychlin; the failed church-building plans on Gottlieb Weigel's estate at Kopojno (1829-1836); the branch congregation of 1843; the heiress of Trąbczyn, Michalina Mączyńska, offering in 1846 to support a church at Łazińsk with grain and fuel - and the Consistory's refusal after the protests of the faithful of Koszuty, Młodojewo and Kosewo; the purchase of 54 Konińska Street in 1850; independence at Christmas 1856 with the first pastor Wilhelm Winkler; the church built 1878-1882 under pastor Karol Edward Erdmann (foundation stone 8 May 1878, the building committee's names given, 13,131 roubles 78 kopecks, consecrated at Advent 1882), the organ of Schlag and Sons of Świdnica (1883), two bells (1884), Erdmann's marble tablet; the hurricane of 1890; the parsonage-cantorate-school of 1913-1917; the interwar parish under pastor Aleksander Gross from 1923, the clock of 1927 endowed by the Krügers for their son lost in 1920, bishop Bursche's visitations, the fiftieth anniversary of 1933, the rectory of 1938. The war: the propaganda war within the Evangelical Church after 1936, the lynchings of 1939 (the cantor Adam drowned at Pyzdry; three Protestants murdered at Szymanowice), the Volksliste, the closures; the murder in the forest near Grabina, in revenge for the "Barka" action of 1918, of the POW men on the night of 22 November 1939 - named here: Karol Rychliński, Franciszek Pluciński, Jan Kmieciak, Feliks Molski, Antoni Płócienniczak, Stanisław Budaszewski, with four others not of the POW. After the war: the lynchings (the Winterfeld family at Olchowo), the expulsions and deportations, pastor Gross dying in November 1945 in the camp infirmary at Finolne near Stalino, the church looted (the organ gone), the real estate returned step by step (the Fundusz Mienia Kościelnego at Wierchy to 1997; Edwin Rydl's petition; the delegate Bernard Otello and the diocesan senior Ryszard Trenkler), the first post-war service on 18 July 1954 under Karol Świtalski; the cantor's house a kindergarten, then the library and community centre; the 1968 reorganisation making Zagórów a branch of the Konin parish (Świtalski, Rudolf Gajda, Henryk Schneider, and from 1976 Andrzej Mendrok, the renovator of the 1980s); the gatherings from 1992 (the tablet to pastor Gross unveiled), the Evangelical Senior Home dedicated 16 June 1995 and closed on the death of its last resident Edward Szwarc (6 April 2011 - the year 2002 given for its seven residents elsewhere in the volume), the August services and the partnership with the Detmold parish (its choir under Johann Pöld; the joint CD of 2001; the carillon of 2008), the ecumenical prayers of 18 August 2018 on the three disused cemeteries of Michalinów Trąbczyński, Łazińsk Pierwszy and Łazińsk Huta with the plaques Erich Kuhn founded, and the services of 2021-2023. Grzonkowski's manuscript and iconographic sources are named (notes and materials of Aleksandra Koronkowska and Rev. Andrzej Mendrok; the typescript of Günter Breede, Deutsche Siedlungen in der Umgebung von Zagórów, 1993; Kneifel's parishes of the Kalisz diocese of 1937).

**Przemysław Kowalski, "Hauländer settlement and Prussian colonization in the vicinity of Zagórów in the years 1746-1806"** - the scholarly study of the settlement the edition's colonies belong to. The Puszcza Pyzdrska before the eighteenth century and its ring of old towns and villages; the essence of Hauländer (Olęder) law - personal freedom, perpetual lease after the okupne, solidary liability of the commune, elected sołtys, own village court, freedom of trade and religion, own schools and houses of prayer; its Dutch-Mennonite origin and its spread in Poland 1547-1864. In 1746 the Trąbczyn estate's owners, the spouses Paweł Prusiński and Katarzyna née Rozdrażewska, located the first two villages - the Trąbczyńskie Luźne Stare Olędry (today Łazińsk Pierwszy and Drugi) and the Trąbczyńskie Luśnie Olędry - in the forest belt from Stawisko to Czarny Bród; then the Trąbczyńskie Luźne Nowe Olędry, later Łukomskie Olędry (1754-1767), settled by Lutherans and Catholics together; the Łukomia estate's villages of 1746-1789 (Bukowe, Imielno, Huta Łukomska - then Zagórowskie Olędry, Myszakówek); Łazy Olędry founded by the widow Katarzyna née Rozdrażewska Prusińska with Polish peasants freed from corvée; and Koszelewska Łąka (1794-1800) settled from Western Pomerania. The census of 1789-1791: 503 inhabitants in the eight settlements, 256 Catholics and 247 Evangelicals. Then the Prussian colonization after 1793: the confiscation of the Ląd monastery's estates and the starosties, the compensation of 50 and 25 per cent, and in 1796 the new owner, the commander Prince Fryderyk Ludwik Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, whose domains of 250 łans carried 100 łans of settlement; the eighteen families of 1805 and the subleasing of further forest to settlers and hired labourers; the eight new settlements of the Zagórów commune with their contract terms (30 thalers a year per cleared Magdeburg łan after four free years, 21 morgi for school, sołtys and cemetery), the settlers from Brandenburg and Pomerania, and the villages of Święcia and the colony of Wrąbczyn. The comparison of the two forms of settlement: the Hauländer, coming from nearby, self-governing and self-financing, succeeded where the state-supported colonists from Württemberg often failed. After 1807 (the Duchy of Warsaw) the promised assistance became rent-and-tax holidays, and no new colonization was carried out in the Zagórów commune. The study's bibliography names Breyer's two studies of 1935 and 1938, Chodyła's books of 2006 and 2015, Kneifel, Rusiński, Wąsicki's article of 1953 and Woźniak's monograph of 2013.

**Rev. Waldemar Wunsz, "The Evangelical-Augsburg Church in the interwar period (1918-1939)"** - the church's national history as background to Zagórów: the Reformation's reach and the Counter-Reformation's cut, the settlement influx from the late eighteenth century, the two tendencies among the settlers' descendants (the villages' traditionalism, the towns' Polonization), the war of 1914-1918 and the Polish state; bishop Juliusz Bursche's *suum cuique* principle of 1906 and its erosion, the church order of 25 November 1936, the church elections of 1937 and the German pastors' opposition under Pastor Eduard Kneifel (a native of Władysławów near Konin), the commissarial seniors (in the Kalisz diocese Edward Wende in Adolf Ulbrich of Grodziec's stead), the Faculty of Evangelical Theology at Warsaw from 1922, the military chaplaincy under Ryszard Paszko; the Kalisz diocese's 60,000 faithful, mostly German-speaking; the Polish-language services at Konin (Pastor Karl Henkl), Turek, Kalisz and Koło; the occupation: the Volksliste, the church reduced to a private association, the cantors' ban of 1942, the murders (Major Adolf Laube and General Ryszard Paszko among the Katyn victims); and the counterweight to Bruno Mielke: **Eryk Stark of Łazińsk**, deported to forced labour in the Reich, who fled across the Alps, joined the Polish Army in Italy as a sapper rifleman, went through Monte Cassino and stayed in Britain - the Łazińsk man on the other side of the war. The Zagórów pastor Gross's wartime apologia is recorded ("Forgive me, forgive me for what the Germans are doing…").

**Katarzyna Połom, "Cantors and kantorents, or education the Lutheran way"** - the cantor's office from its biblical and medieval roots to the kantoratschule, and then the schools of the Zagórów parish: the 1864 decree's three categories; the regulation of 17 December 1936 quoted on the cantor's duties; and the list of the parish's kantorate schools at the outbreak of the war, the volume's most compact evidence for the edition's ground: Olchowo (cantor and teacher Ferdinand Homt from 1924), Tarszewo (Adolf Münch), Michalinów in the gmina of Oleśnica (Ludwig Schulz), Michalinów in the gmina of Trąbczyn (the farmer Paul Schulz), **Łazińsk (cantor and teacher Gustav Adolf Mielke, from 1925)**, Anielewo (cantor and teacher Bruno Mielke). The visitation of superintendent Teichmann of 22 August 1877 counts one elementary school (in Michalinów, some sixty children) and seven cantors' schools with 230 pupils. The Łazińsk school: the largest kantorat of the parish, around 160 children, its first building of 1838-1839 in brick and bog iron ore, the twenty morgi and 218 rods the village gave its teachers; the last teacher Gustav Adolf Mielke headmaster "from 1932" by the school chronicle's record (the introduction and the German-era annex date his works from 1925 and his removal from 1936 - the volume does not settle its own dates); the new school of three classrooms and an office built by the community's efforts under Mielke's direction - the school chronicle, the 1942 article and the annex date it to 1933, the introduction's "a hundred years later" is the volume's own reading; Mielke stripped of the headship for "overzealousness in cultivating Germandom" and transferred to Galicia, back after 1939, teaching until his call-up after Stalingrad; the school's decline under his sister Leokadia and two teachers from Thuringia, 153 children in the war years. The other schools: Anielewo (60-70 children under Bruno Mielke, who shortly before the war also served at Maślaki near Wilczyn), Olchowo (over 40 children), Michalinów Oleśnicki (about 60 pupils under Julius Berendt), Michalinów Trąbczyński (nearly 70 pupils, teacher Friedrich Klingbeil from 1923), the Zagórów school (from 1850 at 54 Konińska Street; from 1940 the Deutsche Volksschule im Hinterberg under Mr Gulke), and the public school of Wrąbczynkowskie Holendry, kept through the chronicle of Olga Ritterówna: the cantorate settlement of 10 morgi 71 rods, the cantors' names from Michał Hertrog (1842) to Adolf Münch (1909), the five-class Polish school of 1931 with its 313 pupils and four teachers, the German division under Juliusz Bauer, the account of Gustaw Kuske of Lądek on the Polish-German school to 1939, and the school's wartime end.

**Damian Kruczkowski, "The Beutlers: Two Centuries in the History of a Family"** - the family history of a Hauländer clan around Konin (Bilczew, Klonowa, Święcia, Dąbrowica), told from Gertruda Schwemmer née Beutler (1918-2018) and her daughter Lili Vogt: the progenitor Andreas Beutler (b. 1778) and the generations of teachers, farmers, a pharmacist and a butcher; the fates of the Dąbrowica branch - the brothers fighting on both sides of the war, one killed in Austria in 1945, another murdered with his brother-in-law at Małe Cieśle on 25 January 1945 (the same crime the introduction tells of the Beutler and Byczyński families), the widow Gertruda's wartime marriage in the Zagórów Evangelical church, her arrest in 1945, the wanderings to Skulsk and the late move to Berlin. The family is off the edition's ground; its chapter is named here as the book's, not worked into the edition.

**Mirosław Słowiński, "Letters of a Wehrmacht soldier discovered in the wall of the cantor's house"** - the study of the Łazińsk cache in its first form, the seed of the separate book *Przeżyć tę jedną zimę* (2023), which the edition carries in detail: the Łazińsk community's two hundred years, the first brick building of 1839 and the new school of "a hundred years later" directed from 1925 by the cantor Gustav Adolf Mielke, his Nazi propaganda work, his forced departure of 1936 and return with the Wehrmacht; the expropriations of April 1945, the teachers' flats, Bogdan Karaszewski's discovery of 1995 (the letters with Wehrmacht field-post stamps, the baptism book of the kantorat of 1912-1918 kept by the cantor E. Cellmer, altar cloths, tools, and the thirty-one books headed by Bade's biography of Goebbels), and the handover of October 2020 to the House of Culture. The cache's contents are counted: 63 letters, 43 postcards and 18 documents of 1936-1939 - among them the permit of the starosta of Konin of 26 September 1939 for Gustav Adolf Mielke to move freely about the Konin powiat - with the field-post address of the war years, "Łazińsk, post office Dromin (Trabczyn) via Konin Wartheland". The letters themselves run from 14 February 1940 to 14 May 1944: the family of Samuel Julius Mielke (the father's two letters of 1942, ending "Jesus works miracles. Heil Hitler!"), the cantor Gustav Adolf (his loneliness, his marriage-seeking, his letter of 1 May 1942 choosing no Saviour "from the Germanic people... But it pleased God so", his advice to the sister in December 1944 on managing the Polish girl assigned to the farm as a slave), the sisters, and Bruno Edwin Mielke - born 8 September 1911 at Białołęka, the State German Teachers' Seminary at Łódź 1932, teacher and cantor at Anielewo, then Maślaki and Konary, called up in 1942 to the 2nd Gebirgs Division, the Eismeerfront at Petsamo/Pieczenga (the bridge at Parkkino, the front stabilized to September 1944), the Volksliste named before his promotion of 1 December 1942, the corporal's promotion of 20 April 1944, the field post numbers 39 801, 31 059 and 48 341, the dream of the theological faculty at Vienna, the marriage-seeking conducted by correspondence, the forged "Reichsstelle für Familienzuwachs" document planted by the Home Army (reproduced in Part III), and the last letter of 14 May 1944 in Norway - after which no trace of him. The chapter's pages carry the political-religious commentary the 2023 book expanded: nazism as a political religion, the Deutsche Christen, the biblical texts under the declarations of loyalty.

**Katarzyna Połom, "The Doctor's wife: the splendour and fall of Janina Breede"** - the portrait of the Zagórów doctor's wife, Janina Breede née Stawicka (b. 1899, daughter of the town's mayor of 1919-1921), the elegant first lady of the pre-war town, Sokół group leader, and from September 1939 a signatory of the Volksliste married to the German doctor Hugo Breede (d. winter 1941); her wartime years, her flight from the Red Army in January 1945, her return and her thirty years of poverty and half-madness in the town's attics until her sudden death in August 1975, buried in haste in the Evangelical section; her son Georg's visit of 1993. The chapter rests on the recollections of ten Zagórów inhabitants, recorded 2020-2022.

### Part II: Memories and Reflections

Nine memoirs and interviews: Professor Lech Słowiński's childhood Zagórów (the cowherd "Mr Matuszak", the bishop's welcome of the 1930s, the gravedigger Kazimierz); **Erich Kuhn's conversation with Wiesława Kowalska** - born at Łazińsk in 1932, the iron-ore house of his family, the school at Huta and Łazińsk, the German resettlement of his family to Łazy in 1940 and the Polish family moved into theirs, the war's taking of his two brothers (one killed in the Carpathians in 1944, the other taken by the Russians the day after his return and never heard of again), the father's death in a Soviet camp, the mother's of typhus in 1949, the expulsion of December 1945, the departure to Germany in 1950, the cabinetmaker's life at Detmold, the return to Poland with Solidarność's aid transports (the federal medal of 2017), the memorial oak planted on the site of the family home at Łazińsk Drugi in October 2015, and the film *Ludzie z żelaznych domów* (People from the Iron Houses) made by the Kowalskis of Pyzdry, whose title gives the section its watchword: where there is a border, there is also a road; Eugenia Plitt née Schmechel's memoirs written down by Günter Hennings (the Schmechel house of 1778 in bog iron, the farm of twenty hectares, the war's distant thunder, the father taken on 20 February 1945 and dead near Moscow on 10 April, the expropriation, the farewell of 11 December 1945 from Grabina by narrow-gauge railway and goods wagon to Demmin); Günter Hennings's account of the first visit to his parents' homeland at Pentecost 1981, through Poznań, Zagórów and Łazińsk to Trąbczyn, where his parents had worked and lived with a Polish family after losing their own home; Rev. Katarzyna Kowalska's recollections of the Evangelical Senior Home's last years (the residents of the 2000s, their care, the television serial, the home's end in 2011 and the guesthouse "Gęsi Puch" of 2021 in the former cantor's house); Damian Kruczkowski's Brudler family history (a blacksmith's line from Strzyżewo; Ferdinand Brudler of Łazińsk and Anielewo, three marriages, dead June 1933); the interview with the novelist Zdzisław Kulawinek on his book about the three nations of eastern Wielkopolska; the history of Ida Disterheft (born at Łazy in 1913, of a Protestant family of the Zagórów municipality, no kin of the occupation mayor whose name she bore, a farm servant all her life, dead 1988 at the Ślesin care home, exhumed into the Konin ossuary, and called back out of oblivion by the author's search); the story of "Mrs Dolkowa" - Genowefa Dalke née Zeigner (b. 1911 at Linden in Wolfenbüttel), the German-speaking teacher of the Lądek school under the occupation who taught German and, it is said, carried notes to the partisans, a lifeguard's widow, dead 2007 as a resident of the Zagórów Senior Home; and the oral-history fragments compiled by Katarzyna Połom - weddings at the German church and in Olchowo recalled by Eugenia Wysokowska, the Weber family's profession of faith by work, Alicja Beutler's letter on her father Alfred (POW, the Legions, the "Medal for Lida"), Tadeusz Molski's memory of the murdered father and of the decent Beutlers, the monstrance hidden under the Diesterhefts' floor, the gendarme Rydel's and the sexton Krygier's quiet help, the Home Army billet saved by Rydel's call-off of the search, Marta Janas's family documents and the cemetery care at Wrąbczynkowskie Holendry, Professor Lech Słowiński's Dr Breede, and the flooded Olchowo of the 1960s.

### Part III: Testimonies - the German-language sources

**Damian Kruczkowski, "Materials from German-language sources"**, opens with the German-era note on Łazińsk from *Der Heimatbote* 8/81-3 (the Kartoteka Wartheland) - the account the edition knows from the 1942 feature's companion: Łazińsk a village of the former Konin powiat in the gmina of Drommin (Trąbczyn), some 170 German families to 1945; the year 1718, the fourteen families from Silesia under their village headman, the forest received from the owner of the Trąbczyn estate, the village grown "aus grüner Wurzel"; the families Kuhn, Merk, Schönfisch and Haupt; the cemetery's first grave of 1727; the school and house of prayer, Łazińsk with its 150 inhabitants the largest kantorat of the later gmina of Zagórów (Hinterberg); to 1933 teaching and services in the old building, then the new one under teacher and cantor [Gustav] Mielke; the war years, 153 children, teachers from Thuringia; the end of 1945. The volume's statistics follow: the village's official names and administrations from 1815 to 1945, the census of 1921 (Łazińska Kolonia, gmina Trąbczyn: 900 inhabitants, of them 571 of German nationality and 757 Evangelical, 144 Polish and 138 Catholic, 5 Jewish), and today's two villages, Łazińsk Pierwszy (159 inhabitants) and Łazińsk Drugi (82).

**Günter Hennings compiles "The Parish of Zagórów"** - the parish history in the German tradition's shape: the filial of 1843, the independence of 1856, the pastors' line (Winkler; "Eugen Biermann" 1860-1865; Karl Eduard Erdmann 1876-1878, the church's builder, its foundation stone of 8 May 1878 and cost of over 13,000 roubles, the consecration at Advent 1882, the organ of 1883 and the bells of 1884, his tablet; "Bernard Erdmann" 1898-1903; the war administrator Haack; Gross 1923-1945), the new bell and the Krügers' clock of 1927, the kantortes' property recovered through the courts in 1935, the parish house of 1938 (40,000 złotys), Gross's deportation and death at Finolny, the post-war care (Świtalski; Mendrok from about 1980; the sexton Ewald Riedel at eighty). Some forms differ from the Polish study's (Biermann for Biedermann; Bernard for Karol Edward Erdmann's later namesake) - the volume does not reconcile them.

**The settlement-history chronology of the Zagórów-Trąbczyn region** - the table of dates from 1145 to 1968, translated by Jacek Kin from the website of the Gliege family (familie-gliege.de): the monastery of Ląd "founded by German Cistercians in 1145" and the monks' flight of 1553; the town's founding of 1445; 1718 and the fourteen families from Silesia (Kuhn, Merk, Schönfisch, Haupt); 1727 the first cemetery; **1746 Paul von Kolno-Prusinski, owner of the Trabczyn estate, concluding the hereditary lease with the German-Evangelical settlers** and granting the wooded land to the south, the church records to 1838 kept at Trąbczyn, Grodziec, Konin, Września and Żychlin; 1793-1807 the five estates of the Zagórów area (Trombczyn, Nowa-Wieś, Oswiery, Łazy and Szetlewek) confiscated and given to the prince zu Hohenlohe-Öhringen (Ingelfingen), who sold half to Karl Gottlieb Weigel of Kopojno; 1807-1815 the Duchy of Warsaw and the famines of 1808, 1811 and 1812; 1815 the Congress of Vienna's division; 1842 the filial; **1846 "Michelina Miaczynska" the owner of the Trabczyn estate**; 1856 the independent parish; 1863 the Polish Count A. Taczanowski acquiring the estates around Zagórów after the uprising; 1864 the emancipation; 1878 the foundation stone and the building committee's names; 1898 the house of prayer in Michalinów burned; 1913 the parsonage's foundation stone and its committee; 1920 Versailles; **1933 the new school and house of prayer in Łazińsk "under the direction of the teacher Adolf Mielke"** (the table's form of the name); 1937 the German schools Polonized; 1939 the Reichsgau Wartheland and Mielke teaching again, his sister and two teachers from Thuringia, 153 children; 1943 the place-name ordinance; 1945; 1968 Zagórów a filial of the Konin parish. The table is the website's own account of the region's history; where it touches the edition's ground its dates stand beside the edition's documents, which the edition follows.

**The German place names of Zagórów and Konin county, 1804-1891 and 1938-1945** - the two-column list (Trabczyn = Drommin, Zagorow = Hinterberg, Michalinów in the gmina of Oleśnica = Emilienheim, Anielewo = Ingelfingen, Olchowo = Sophienthal, Tarszewo = Friederichseld, Chruściki = Adelshof, Łazy's region, Konin = Kuhnau, Pyzdry = Peisern, Słupca = Grenzhausen and the rest), from the Gliege website and Breyer's study of the Warthebruch of 1938 - the same Breyer the edition carries.

**The protocol of the meeting of the communal/parish assembly of the Cantors' Council of Łazińsk of 26 November 1928** - the resolution to build the small church (the new school-house and place of prayer), the levy of 7 złotych a day and 10 zł per citizen, the nine-man building committee (among them the teacher Gustaw Mielke, August Grunwald the chairman, Gustav Schuch the deputy), and the page of the community's signatures, "many of the first names and surnames very illegible" - translated by Michał Rek.

**The list of the inhabitants of Michalinów Trąbczyński, Łazińsk and Huta Trąbczyńska** as of 1945, compiled by Leonard Kuske of Syke with Regina Schiewe's help: the households of the three villages named family by family - in Michalinów Trąbczyński the Sidows, the Zaches, the Schulzes, the Zellmers, the Giebels, the Glieges, the Weidners, the Kucks, the Starks, the Winterfelds and the three Polish families (Wypychowski, Marciniak, Urbaniak) among them; in Łazińsk the Zellmers, the Hirsches, the Kraniches, the Schönfisches and the Köhlers; in Huta Trąbczyńska the Schönfisches, the Haupts, the Schmechels, the Zellmers, the Rittkamners, the Kunkels' grocery and Gustav Herrmann. The village of Michalinów Trąbczyński is distinguished from its namesake Michalinów Oleśnicki (Emilienheim), the two having belonged to two different Catholic parishes - the reason for the double names.

**"With the Sokół Falcons in Zagórów"** - the excerpt from *Orędownik Wrzesiński* no. 97 of 22 August 1931, p. 3, on the Sokół gathering in the People's House and the local Evangelicals' goodwill to it (the microscopist Teodor Milicz named a patriot).

**"Reproduction Officer"** - the forged document found in the Łazińsk kantorate, reproduced in full with its translation: the "Reichsstelle für Familienzuwachs" of Berlin, 15 May 1943, file I.A.4711, appointing its recipient a reproducer "to service 9 women and 18 girls" for the birth statistics, his wife without the right of divorce, promotion to "reproduction officer" with the Order for Copulation first class with red ribbon, the extra food rations - signed "Hüpfauf"; the work of the Home Army's intelligence and propaganda service, planted among the Germans (the neighbourhood knew that Gustav Adolf Mielke had long been looking for a wife).

### The appendix

**Damian Kruczkowski, "FRYDHOF is not just a cemetery"** - the history of the Wielkopolskie Stowarzyszenie na rzecz Ratowania Pamięci FRYDHOF (entered in the register on 13 January 2017; Urszula Karolczak president, Kruczkowski deputy, Waldemar Wunsz on the board), the two volunteer groups' union and its work on the Evangelical cemeteries of the former Konin voivodeship - more than 200 of them: the clearings of 2016-2018, the three cemeteries of Michalinów Trąbczyński, Łazińsk Pierwszy and Łazińsk Huta with the stones and plaques of 2018 founded by Erich Kuhn, the Zagórów councillor Jan Kloc's years of work, the association's publications and lectures, and its work beyond the region (Stolec, Gostków, Święcie, the Jewish cemetery at Rychwał).

**"A Broken Connecting Link"** - the volume's own German Kurzfassung by Mirosław Słowiński, translated by Jacek Kin with Günter Hennings' assistance: the argument of the introduction in brief (the three communities, the incomplete picture, the work of 2020-2023, the concert and the donors) and the German table of the volume's contents.

### Where the edition's documents say otherwise

The volume is a witness of the edition's nineteenth and twentieth centuries, not a document of the estates' history; the edition follows its documents. Its three accounts of the founding of Łazińsk - the introduction's, the annex's German-era note and the chronology table's - repeat the year 1718 and the fourteen families from Silesia, the Ostdeutscher Beobachter's figure of 1942, which stands against the edition's records and is open (NEEDS_CONFIRMATION, "The newspapers"): the book is a third witness to the date, not a settlement of it. Its forms of the founders of 1746 differ among themselves - the introduction and the Kurzfassung name Katarzyna and Paweł Prusimscy (in the book's spelling "Prusińscy"), Kowalski's study names "Paweł Prusiński i Katarzyna née Rozdrażewska", the annex's contemporary note (from Wikipedia) "Andrzej i Paweł" - against Chodyła's Paweł and Katarzyna Prusimscy, the edition's documents first. Its date for the cantor Gustav Adolf Mielke's works at Łazińsk: 1925 in the introduction, the letters chapter and Breyer's notebook of 1936 (APP 53/968/0/-/669, "Mielke seit 1925"), but "from 1932" in the school chronicle's record Połom quotes; and his removal from Łazińsk: 1932 by the chronicle's record, 1936 by the introduction, "four years from 1933" by the 1942 article - the volume does not settle its own dates (NEEDS_CONFIRMATION, "Słowiński (2024)"). Its new school's date: 1933 in the annex and the chronology table (the table's "teacher Adolf Mielke" the table's form of Gustav Adolf's name) against the introduction's "a hundred years later". The volume's own accounts differ where they touch: the Senior Home's dedication of 16 June 1995 (Grzonkowski) against 16 June 1996 (the home's chronicle); the parish history's pastor names against the Polish study's. The chronology table's early history (the monastery of 1145, the flight of 1553) and its account of the confiscation of five estates and the Weigel purchase stand against the edition's documents, which are first.
