# PREFACE

### *To the Benevolent Reader.*

Among the various branches of knowledge that appear most necessary for common human use, I have judged jurisprudence to be of the greatest utility, in particular in our Kingdom of Poland; and since, in order to acquire a thorough command of it, a great many volumes of laws must be studied, I thought it worthwhile to present to you, Benevolent Reader, this work which I have compiled in summary form over many years with great labour. I wished to call it the *Rule of the Field Boundary Process*, not because I might wish to instruct in this matter the Illustrious Right Honourable Sub-Chamberlains (*Succamerarii*), chosen from among thousands and proven and approved in all branches of learning, nor their Right Honourable Boundary Chamberlains, since I am more than fully aware that they have a perfect command of both the laws and the limits of estates — but so that the studious youth of jurisprudence might have a readier means by which to arrive more quickly at a mastery of this branch of knowledge. For one can plainly see that our young men spend several years in academies of jurisprudence, yet return to their own homes with little or no competence in this kind of knowledge, and involve themselves in the clamour of the law — not without great inconvenience to the judges — in the land courts, municipal courts, and Tribunal courts alike. I myself was not unlike this,

[p. iii]

when in the first flower of my youth I attended cases in the municipal and land courts; and for this reason, when by God's ordering I reached maturity, wishing to remedy this ill, I set down — with great effort, yet with willing spirit — a rule, with distinctions as to the delimitation of *Royal, Ecclesiastical, and Terrestrial* estates, both in the borderlands of *Ruthenia, Prussia, and the Duchy of Masovia*, and in the other palatinates and lands joined to the *Kingdom of Poland*, according to the intent of the laws and constitutions of the *Kingdom*, so that henceforth any man may without doubtful legal clamour take his stand concerning his own estates. There do indeed exist several books already treating of this matter, but what you will find elsewhere scattered in various places, you will find here gathered together in a single compendium. Therefore receive with a grateful spirit this modest work of mine, and guard it once received from the malicious criticism of a Zoilus. For although I have gathered together everything that I collected from various reading and from my own experience, and have set it forth for the public benefit, yet I am compelled to confess with Cicero that I *pour out into another all that I know, and learn nothing new in order to teach*. If however you should detect any errors, I would wish you not to decline to correct them. Farewell, and make the best possible use of this work.

### *On the Legal Process in Boundary Cases.*

In boundary cases, according to the ancient institution of the public laws, the legal process is fourfold. The first is the original terrestrial, that is, the process of the land law and the land court. The second is the final executory field process of the Sub-Chamberlain. The third is the arbitral compromissorial process. The fourth is determined by amicable means through an amicable settlement. The terrestrial process is the origin and preparatory stage of the future field process, which most often arises from a case of fact, which is the mother of the boundary case, and is pursued in one of two ways: either in contumacy, or through contested proceedings.

By contumacy: when the respondent, after a penalty of contumacy has been obtained against him at the first session (for actions of this kind are not of the first peremptory session), fails to appear at the second session of the second citation, he is condemned by the plaintiff before the land court in the gain of the plaintiff's right of path, and immediately ^[*After the gain of the right of path has been obtained, a referral to the field.*]

[p. 2]

a referral of the case is made from the land court to the Sub-Chamberlain's field office of that palatinate or land where the estates are situated, with the plaintiff's right of path ready and gained, for the erection of the boundary mounds of the case.

At the session of the field hearing appointed by the Right Honourable Sub-Chamberlain to the parties by his summons letters, and once the service return thereof has been acknowledged by the court messenger in the proper district, and after the gain has been obtained in the land court or the Crown Tribunal: the party defeated in law shall not be admitted to contest the matter in the field before the Sub-Chamberlain's court; adhering to the statute of the Kingdom, and indeed notwithstanding any documentary evidence or more apparent signs that the party defeated in law might present at that time, the Right Honourable Sub-Chamberlain shall adjudge the boundary path to the plaintiff, and the defeated party must follow it; and once the mounds, both corner mounds and wall mounds, have been erected, the Sub-Chamberlain shall order the party victorious in law to swear an oath with six noble witnesses at the final mound, and shall declare that it shall have the force of perpetual validity.

It is to be noted here. Although the boundary case, after the gain of the right of path has been obtained in the land court, carries on its face — by virtue of the summons letters or summons letters of the Right Honourable Sub-Chamberlain issued for the session of the field hearing of his office, at the instance of the plaintiff's party victorious in law, for the disputed place — a satisfactory session; on the foundation of positive law and

[p. 3]

the statute of the Kingdom, providing that the party defeated in law shall not be admitted to contest in the field, nor be given any opportunity to justify the matter of the boundary case — nevertheless, according to the later constitution of the year 1588, under the title *On the Relaxation and Annulment of Banishment*, contained in this book, the person under banishment may avail himself of the benefit of the law through a session issued for the purpose of quashing the ruling against the party victorious in law, suspending the execution of the land court decree in respect of the erection of the mounds, and prove that it was wrongly obtained, or escape on grounds of ignorance of the process. In such a case, therefore, the Sub-Chamberlain shall stay the execution and the erection of mounds, and shall refer the case, for the cognisance and resolution of the obtained and conducted process, to the Crown Tribunal of that province where the estates are situated. Or to ^[*Where a session for quashing obtains, on account of a wrongly obtained process or ignorance thereof, a referral to the Tribunal must follow.*] that court where the process was conducted and obtained.

### *The Original Terrestrial Process.*

First, when the plaintiff wishes to delimit his estate with a neighbouring estate, and to resolve the differences that have arisen in respect of the boundaries, he is obliged, in the legal form described below, to summon by formal citation to the land court both the principal party — from whose side and estate the plaintiff seeks the separation and delimitation of his own estates — and also the collateral heirs: the one as the starting point of the boundary path, as the point from which it begins, and the other as the determining point of the boundary path, as the end point, that is, the party whose boundary wall the boundaries are directed toward.

[p. 4]

### *First Land Court Citation against the Principal Party.*

Augustus the Fourth, by the Grace of God King of Poland, etc. We command you, the Honourable [name], heir of the estates of the village of [name] (if there are several co-heirs, they are cited together), in your person and as regards all your estates generally, that you appear personally and peremptorily before Our Land Court at the next and immediately following land court sessions of [name] [name] to be held henceforth, at the instance of the Instigator of that same Our court, and of his informant, the Honourable [name], heir of the estates of the village of [name], plaintiff, who, adhering to his protestation made against you before the authentic records of the Kingdom, cites you for the delimitation of the said hereditary estates of himself the plaintiff — to see and hear the right of path adjudged to him the plaintiff by the sentence of Our court, and to see you bound and compelled to the restitution of revenues from the plots and fields wrongfully appropriated to himself, upon a prior rendering of account, and to the compensation of damages caused thereby, and to have penalties decreed against you for the acts of violence perpetrated, for the purpose of having such penalties decreed and extended against you, you are cited. Be present at the session to comply, and to respond judicially to the foregoing matters, these and others to be set forth to you at a future session. Given etc. etc. (If the co-heirs are minors, it shall be written in the plural, with the assistance of the guardians duly incumbent by law.)

[p. 5]

### *First Uniform Citation against Both Collateral Heirs.*

The royal title is written from the above, down to the words: and appear peremptorily, at the instance of [name], plaintiff, who cites or rather summons you, and this for the following reason: That you demonstrate your corner point or your own particular corner point, meeting the plaintiff's boundary wall, who wishes to distinguish and delimit his estates and inheritance from the estates of [name], and that you attempt the same delimitation. Be therefore present at the session to comply, and to respond judicially to the foregoing matters. *Given etc. etc.*

Upon these first citations, issued both against the principal party and against the collateral heirs, a penalty of contumacy must be obtained in the land court. Or, with the consent of the parties, the matter can be done even at the first session (for no injury is done to one who consents): a referral to the field to the Right Honourable Sub-Chamberlain for the making of the boundaries.

### *Second Land Court Citation against the Principal Party and Collateral Heirs.*

The above is written, with this addition: who cites you for the second time, after the penalty of contumacy has been obtained. *Summons etc. etc.*

Upon this second citation of the peremptory session, if the respondent, either in person or through his registered attorney (a registered power of attorney is moreover required by law in this case as

[p. 6]

a matter of hereditary right) has not appeared in the land court, he is condemned by the plaintiff in that same court in the gain of the right of path; through which condemnation the plaintiff gains the right of path, and with the right of path ready is referred to the field law, to continue the further process according to the usage of the field law of boundaries.

### *Gain of the Right of Path.*

And by virtue of the gain, the present land court, adhering to the common law and the contents of the citation, in contumacy of the respondent, adjudges the right of path in boundary proceedings, or the boundary path, to the plaintiff's party; and refers the present case, for the making of the boundaries or limits, and for the further prosecution thereof in law, to the Sub-Chamberlain's office.

And not only the principal party himself, but also the collateral heirs who fail to appear either in person or through their registered attorney, are condemned in a similar gain of the right of path.

When, however, both the plaintiff's party and the principal respondent's party appear, as well as the collateral heirs, either in person or through their attorneys, at that second session before the land court, a postponement is granted and conceded to the respondents (if they seek it judicially, as in a case of hereditary right) for the documents necessary to the case, until the next sessions. It is likewise within the discretion of the respondent's party, if it does not wish to avail itself of this postponement, to consent to the plaintiff regarding a decree of referral to the office

[p. 7]

of the Right Honourable Sub-Chamberlain, for the purpose of testing in the field the matter of the disputed place; the form of which referral is as follows:

### *Decree of Referral to the Field.*

The present land court of [name], resolving the citation of the plaintiff's party issued to its court, and having heard the contested submissions of the parties — since the plaintiff's party acts in the present session not only in respect of the acts of violence committed, but also for the delimitation of the estates of [name] — therefore, adhering to the constitution of the year 1611, and suspending the resolution of the case of fact, refers the present case, in respect of the boundaries, for final adjudication, together with the collateral heirs entering appearance, to the field, to the Right Honourable Sub-Chamberlain of the district of [name], or his office; upon summons letters to be issued by the Right Honourable Sub-Chamberlain to both the respondent's party and the collateral heirs; before which Right Honourable Sub-Chamberlain or his office the parties shall be obliged to appear and to prosecute the case, under the penalty of banishment to be proclaimed at that session by virtue of the present decree.

### *Form of the Summons Letters to the Field pursuant to a Land Court Decree.*

[name] [name], Sub-Chamberlain of the Palatinate of [name], We command you, the Honourable [name], heir of the estates of the village of [name], in your person and as regards all your estates (if there are several co-heirs cited, it shall be written in the plural: you) generally, by

[p. 8]

Royal authority and Our own, in which capacity We exercise Sub-Chamberlain's authority in this matter, that you appear personally and peremptorily before Us, or before Our Sub-Chamberlain's Field Boundary Office, at the place of the differences between the hereditary estates of the village of [name] of the undersigned plaintiff on the one part, and your likewise hereditary estates of the village of [name] on the other part, at such place namely as the said plaintiff shall appoint for Us or Our office, on the Monday next after the feast of [name], next and immediately to come, at the instance of the Honourable [name], heir of the said estates of [name], plaintiff, who, adhering to the decree of the Land Court of [name], issued on the [day] of [name] in the year [name], between himself the plaintiff on the one part and you on the other part, cites or rather summons you for the delimitation of the estates and the erection of mounds where there are none, and for the renewal of the old ones where they are found, for the purpose of testing the matter in that case; therefore you are cited to see and hear the right of path adjudged to him the plaintiff by the sentence of Our court as the more probable, and to be admitted to swear an oath at the final mound according to the intent of the statute. Be therefore present at the session to comply, and to respond judicially to the foregoing matters. *Given etc.*

The court messenger shall acknowledge the service return of these summons letters before the proper court where the estates are situated.

### *Form of the Summons Letters to the Collateral Heirs.*

The same title of the Right Honourable Sub-Chamberlain, the name

[p. 9]

and surname of the respondent and of the plaintiff, the session, and the place, shall be written from the above down to this point: who, adhering to the decree of the Land Court of [name], issued on the [day] of [name] in the present year, between himself the plaintiff on the one part and the Honourable [name], heir of the estates of [name], on the other part, whereby the referral to the field to the Right Honourable Sub-Chamberlain for the delimitation of the said estates was established, summons you, and this for the following reason: that you demonstrate your corner point, or your own particular corner point, meeting the boundary wall of the *aforesaid* estates, situated at the convergence of three estates, and acknowledge the same to the plaintiff, and that you attempt the same session. For which purposes you are cited or summoned. Be therefore present at the session to comply, and to respond judicially to the foregoing matters. *Given etc.*

*When, through contested proceedings, the parties litigating between themselves advance the case in the Sub-Chamberlain's field court, this rule is to be observed in all respects, providing for the security of the decree.*

### *On Accessory Matters.*

Although the satisfactory session for entering upon the field hearing of the Right Honourable Sub-Chamberlain for the delimitation of estates, pursuant to the intent of the decree, whether of the land court or of the Tribunal, does fall due — nevertheless any litigating party may avail itself of lawful exceptions, insisting upon the law, and invalidating that same session of the field hearing,

[p. 10]

for the security of the judgment.

**1st.** The appearance must be sufficiently registered, both by the principal acting parties and by the collateral heirs; if any party presents itself through an attorney, it must produce a sufficient power of attorney.

**2nd.** If there are several co-heirs of the estates and only one heir of his own share raises the question of boundaries, and the others do not enter into the sessions issued or into the land court or Tribunal decree, without whose appearance the boundary case cannot be decided, a referral must follow, back to the same court from which it issued, for the enjoining of a summons to the other co-heirs.

**3rd.** If the summons of all the collateral heirs, pursuant to the intent of the decree, has not been interposed for the session of the field hearing, then the case must be referred back to that court for the decreeing of penalties of contravention; but if the collateral heirs appear without a summons and submit themselves to the Sub-Chamberlain's court, then the Sub-Chamberlain, having decreed the penalties of contravention upon the contravening party upon whom the summons had been enjoined and entrusted, shall proceed to the delimitation.

**4th.** If at the first session of the field hearing a summoned collateral heir fails to appear, then upon him there must follow a proclamation of the penalty of banishment interposed by decree, and with the citation suspended for the conviction of the absent party; and once the proclamation has been obtained, through the path

[p. 11]

of execution, a referral shall follow from the executory municipal court office to that court from which the case arrived.

**5th.** If the collateral position of royal estates obtains at a corner point or particular corner point of those same estates that wish to delimit themselves, such a delimitation cannot follow without obtaining a royal commission from His Sacred Royal Majesty, even if the corner mound or particular corner mound of the royal estates is visible; and if it has been enjoined by decree to obtain a royal commission from His Sacred Royal Majesty, and the plaintiff has not procured it, or has not engaged the commissioners in the full number required by law, then from that point, with the decision of the boundary case suspended, a referral must follow to that court from which the case came, for the decreeing of penalties of contravention of that decree.

Finally, if the court messenger is absent for the proclamation of the act and for the founding of the jurisdiction of the field hearing office — without whose presence and the founded jurisdiction the judge is deemed a private person — then in such a case the session of the field hearing lapses.

### *What Postponements Are Granted in Boundary Cases.*

^[*Five postponements are granted in the field to heirs of terrestrial estates; to holders of royal estates, however, no postponement is granted.*]

*1st*, of illness; *2nd*, the principal postponement of up to eight weeks, once only; *3rd*, suspension inasmuch as a senator during a sitting of the Kingdom's Diet, or a deputy during the exercise of his function, or a judge deputed to the Crown Tribunal; *4th*, postponement

[p. 12]

of soldier's exemption, for soldiers quartered in camp; *5th*, deliberation granted once to the Sub-Chamberlain's court.

When the accessory matters have thus been resolved, after the registered appearance, the court shall proceed, by its sentence proclaimed to the parties, to the review of the boundary path, first of the plaintiff's party and then of the respondent's party; and it shall consider carefully and diligently the places and positions, the mounds if they are credible, and the boundary markers demonstrated by the parties from the starting point, through the intermediate points, to the end point, and proved by the documents deposited in the boundary path, noting faithfully from which quarter of the sun it begins and toward which it is directed, whether in a straight line or not, or obliquely, setting aside partiality, together with the protests and counter-protests declared by the parties, and shall inscribe these into the decree; and it shall likewise inscribe the documents deposited by the parties in the court, with the act and the day of session.

After the review of the boundary paths, before all else, regarding the corner point, if it is invisible and confirmed by no documents, ^[*The corner point and the particular corner point are established first.*] the court shall conduct sworn inquiries, and each party — not only the principal acting parties but also each collateral heir for its own part — shall call witnesses; when these have been completed, the court shall proceed to the other particular corner point and perform the like, and shall thereafter descend to the reading of the sworn inquiries and shall decide finally. Having decided the corner point and the particular corner point and proclaimed the sentence to the parties, and having erected the corner mounds and particular corner mounds, it shall form the boundary wall as straight as possible from the starting point, through the intermediate points, to the

[p. 13]

end point — as straight as possible, for the entire force of boundaries rests in the straight line — and shall mark it with mounds, with a description of the places, trees, and all the markers found in the boundary path. Where, however, a natural boundary separates the estates, or a boundary road, the straight line is not observed.

If, however, there are boggy and inaccessible places where passage through them is impossible, or dense forests of great breadth and length, then between such estates ^[*A sworn surveyor is necessary for boggy and inaccessible places.*] a sworn surveyor shall be required to form the straight line artificially from the starting point to the end point, in the presence of the Right Honourable Sub-Chamberlain.

When, moreover, the boundary wall of two estates reaches a river and its bank, and on the other side ^[*The corner point in the vicinity of a river.*] there is a third estate, the Sub-Chamberlain's court must take note of the corner mounds of that third estate, in what place they stand, and toward them, or from the opposite side, it shall form the corner point and erect two mounds on this side of the river where the estates are situated, not crossing the river, and on the other bank a third, on the same estates meeting it collaterally on that bank where they are situated — not, however, all three estates on one bank, because the heir would thereby have usurped both banks of the river for himself, if a mound were situated for him by crossing the river. The laws of the Kingdom contained in this book, under the heading Sub-Chamberlain, ^[*The ancient natural course of the river is to be considered and assigned as the boundary.*] will inform you of the remaining rules regarding rivers and the diversion of rivers and the ancient course to which one ought to have regard.

[p. 14]

Now, if a river by its natural course diverts itself from one bank to the other, causing loss to one and gain to another, by means of what is called ^[*Odsypisko.*] *odsypisko*, each heir must be content with his own bank. [T.N. 1]

It often happens that the heir of one estate, ^[*On collusion between the parties.*] raising a boundary question against his neighbour, having entered into collusion with the collateral heir, acknowledging a corner point mutually to one another in a particular place, to the prejudice of a third estate — and this occurs most commonly when the mounds are not visible and the parties are wanting in proofs, or the position of the place where the corner point should be established is open to interpretation and explanation — in such case the Sub-Chamberlain's court must take firm notice of the injury to the third estate, and review the boundary wall of the collateral heir marked with mounds, not formally, but only for its own information, as to whether or not the straight line meets at this place where it acknowledges the corner point to the neighbouring estate; and if collusion is alleged against the collateral heir, he must then clear himself by oath, on the ground that he proceeded with no collusion with the party. If, however, the collateral heir shall prove the extension of the boundary wall in a straight line or by his own mounds to the place which he acknowledges to the party, then the collusion objected was unwarranted and the sworn discharge is already less necessary.

[p. 15]

### *On the Boundaries of Estates that Have Never Been Delimited.*

A boundary question also arises between estates that have never been delimited, having formerly been of one lordship and inheritance. In such a case the Sub-Chamberlain shall consider the starting point (if one can exist) from the corner point and form the boundary wall in a straight line to the end point; if that is lacking, it shall be lawful for the Sub-Chamberlain to reach, with a new boundary wall and newly erected mounds, the neighbouring boundary wall, not crossing it in the slightest, and to form a triangle — but this must be done in the presence of the heir of that boundary wall which he is joining, upon a prior summons of the same; and such boundaries are concluded without an oath. If, however, one party shall gain the boundary path in its entirety, then the party shall at that time establish by oath the thing gained, according to the intent of the statute, with six witnesses of the same rank.

### *As Regards the Ecclesiastical Estates of the Most Illustrious Bishops or Cathedral Chapters, with Royal and Terrestrial Estates.*

All the formalities pertaining to a boundary case having been completed beforehand, if the cathedral chapter wins the boundary path, then two canons deputed from the chapter to this case with the consent of the Most Illustrious Bishop are required to confirm it, with six

[p. 16]

noble witnesses at the final corner mound. If, however, the Most Illustrious Bishop wishes to delimit his episcopal estates, whether as plaintiff or as respondent, with any neighbouring nobleman or with the leaseholder of royal estates, and if he wins the boundary path, then not the bishop but two canons deputed from the chapter are required to confirm the won boundary path with six noble witnesses.

It is to be observed that the Most Illustrious Bishop cannot on his own raise a question in respect of boundaries, except together with his cathedral chapter, and likewise respond as respondent.

If, however, the leaseholder of royal estates, together with the community (without whose appearance the case cannot be decided), wins the boundary path, then not the leaseholder but six subjects from the community shall be required to confirm the same at the final mound according to the intent of the law.

### *As Regards the Estates of His Most Serene Highness the Archbishop of Gniezno, First Prince and Primate of the Kingdom.*

He may advance the right of path through his commissioners, as he is accustomed to advance it before every court and office of the Kingdom, yet with the assistance of the Most Illustrious Cathedral Chapter and of two canons appointed to the boundary case.

### *As Regards Conventual Estates.*

[p. 17]

If the convent wins the boundary path, then the Prior of that convent shall be required to confirm it with six noble witnesses, and not with religious.

By the same method, if a delimitation and confirmation of the boundary path of abbatial estates follows, if the abbot together with his convent wins the boundary path.

### *Estates Situated on the Borders of the Kingdom.*

If a noble heir of terrestrial estates is situated in the vicinity of royal estates and suffers injury from the leaseholder of those estates, and the boundary wall of both estates, terrestrial and royal alike, meets the boundaries of a foreign province, and he wishes to delimit his estates from the royal estates and from the foreign province, then the nobleman shall be obliged first to procure commissioners for the delimitation of those estates at the General Diet of the Kingdom, so that commissioners may be appointed by constitution, and thereafter from the royal court of that province the Commonwealth of the Kingdom must obtain commissioners for the purpose of attempting those boundaries.

### *Furthermore, What is the Corner Point and the Particular Corner Point? They are to be understood as follows.*

The corner point belongs to that estate which takes for itself the beginning of the boundary wall, and the collateral angle. The particular corner point, however, ^[*At the convergence of 3 or 4 estates.*] and the corner mound of the particular kind is so called for that estate, because it determines the boundary wall

[p. 18]

of estates in the vicinity of the corner mounds of the other estate; ^[*The mound of that estate which begins the boundary wall is called the corner mound; that which determines the end of the boundary wall is the particular corner mound.*] and so in both corner points: one is the corner point which begins the boundary wall, and the other is the particular corner point which determines its own estate and cannot advance further. *The intermediate point* is understood thus: if, in its boundary path, the leading party demonstrates to the court a natural boundary, or at minimum five mounds, or three, or ancient boundary markers in the boundary wall, or likewise a boundary road assigned from antiquity as the boundary. This too must be observed when the boundary path is directed not from the corner mound itself but from the midpoint between mounds, and terminates at the midpoint of the particular corner point.

The Sub-Chamberlain's cord must be of a dimension of a circle ^[*The length of the Sub-Chamberlain's cord, for measuring the mounds.*] for the erection of corner mounds and particular corner mounds, otherwise known as *węgielniki*, of a length of five ordinary cubits; for wall mounds, of a length of two and a half: from the midpoint of the place assigned for the mound, the Sub-Chamberlain shall mark out the circle. It is necessary to describe how far each mound is from the next mound, and toward which quarter of the sun the boundary wall extends.

### *Final Observation on the Positions of Places.*

Since boundary cases and acts are of a demonstrative kind, and can only be known and decided from the thing presented to the eyes, ^[*An accurate description of the boundary path and the erection of the mounds is made.*] the circumstances, positions, and situations of places must therefore be carefully observed

[p. 19]

and specifically and expressly entered into the records, so that in future the circumscription and delineation contained in the documents may correspond to the signs that have been marked, and so that the heirs may also, all the more easily, as the marks fade with age over fifty years — in the course of which especially the renewal of boundaries and investigation of boundaries is accustomed to be made — restore those same boundaries by mutual consent in a shorter time, after the lapse of any decade, in the presence of the Sub-Chamberlain's office, and renew the mounds broken down, trodden flat, and reduced to nothing, after the manner of the prior act. And any heir of estates, if he observes this, will leave peaceful tranquillity to himself and his successors. And first, because boundary paths are concluded in various ways — from the limits and starting point to the limits and end point — the following are to be generally observed: ^[*What is to be observed in all matters in a boundary case.*] east, west, south, north, or the eastern, western, southern, northern (also called the boreal and aquiline) quarter; for one proceeds, or is led, from the east, summer or winter, toward the west, from the south toward the north, and conversely, as shall seem good and pleasing first to the plaintiff as leading party; or also between the solar quarters, the southern and western, eastern and northern, or conversely; therefore from where the beginning commences and in what direction the steps are taken must be specifically noted. Finally, a delineation must be made of those same places before the eyes,

[p. 20]

of the cultivated and uncultivated, the barren, sandy, marshy, muddy, boggy, and wooded — such as forests, groves, heathlands, thickets, briar patches, shrublands, oak woods, copses, thorn bushes, wastelands, and trees of various kinds, in which boundary markers in the form of a cross may be cut beside the mounds themselves lying adjacent (though this perishes with the passage of time, and is destroyed by the hand of man through the cutting down of the tree, wilfully or in ignorance). Likewise hills, headlands, valleys, hollows, slopes, ^[*In the boundary path and the erection of mounds an accurate description is made so that posterity may the more readily investigate the boundaries.*] knolls, barrows, and pits. Then there are to be observed and accurately noted in the records, if they occur or intervene in the boundary tract: streams, rivulets, brooks, rivers, waterways, watercourses; springs, torrents, lakes, ponds, cisterns, marshes, wellsprings, channels, runnels, fords, mud, mire, banks, shores, margins, the edge of a mere. Moreover roads, incidental, royal, paved, public, both those running straight and in a line and those running crosswise, leading, conducting, or going from village to village, from town to town, and conversely; crossroads, three-way junctions, divergence of roads, footpaths among them. Boundary stones, rocks, boulders, markers, mounds, crags, peaks, summits, ridgelines, heights, tops, roots of hills. Finally, the joining of lands, palatinates, territories, towns,

[p. 21]

villages, mills, ironworks, works of iron, glass, paper, and all others of every kind and description — estates, that is; and these and other features of the same kind, positions and situations of places, circumstances, adjacencies, and connexions must be taken note of and specified and expressed precisely in the boundary act. Furthermore, the erection of boundary markers and metal markers is of two kinds: either by corner mounds on both sides of the boundary wall, three, or according to the convergence of estates and boundary walls even four, which are also called particular corner mounds and corner mounds, erected and built in a triangle or quadrangle in a rounded mound shape from earth; and by several wall mounds between the estates of both parties, erected and built from earth by the same method, or marked out in the open country by a ditch; of which mounds ^[*The straight line is to be observed.*] the straight line, the orderly and just spacing of each from the next, the number — how many paces each is from the next — together with the metal markers placed in the mounds, and with the abovementioned definition of the places, shall be described in the boundary act.

It is further to be noted that, in the completion of boundaries, ^[*The natural boundary is to be observed in the absence of boundary markers.*] rivers, waterways, brooks, and torrents, their channels, courses, and beds, are of the greatest observance as natural boundaries and limits, and are assigned to estates as a perpetual boundary, in the absence of boundary markers.

[p. 22]

If a river or stream naturally and spontaneously diverts itself, the ancient channel is to be treated as the boundary; ^[*The ancient course of a river, for the delimitation of estates and inheritances.*] but if it is through the deliberate effort of a man, then the new channel or course is to be taken as the boundary, while the old channel entire is to belong to the estate in which it is situated.

Moreover, parcels of an estate or ^[*It is lawful to reclaim avulsed portions, notwithstanding prescription.*] quantities of land, fields, meadows, forests, and plots avulsed in any manner from their principal part — that is to say, from the estate and possession — and occupied, may by virtue of an act of general boundary restoration be reincorporated into their principal part or head; nor shall the allegation of any prescription stand in the way, provided the plaintiff confirms the same parcel within his boundary path by oath.

In actions and cases of boundaries no prescription runs; actions of boundaries are not extinguished by prescription and discharge; and it is likewise open to the plaintiff, at such time as he wishes and is able, to bring an action for boundaries; and the adjudication of boundaries by the ordinary judge is always open, just, and equitable.

In boundary cases, for winning ^[*Three things are required to establish the corner point in law.*] and legally establishing the right of path, three things are of primary necessity: documentary evidence, from which the truth is drawn; real mounds and clear and credible markers; and the acknowledgement of both boundary walls by the collateral heirs. By which real acknowledgement the corner point is established, the parties disputing the same among themselves.

[p. 23]

The penalty for the destruction of mounds between estates ^[*The penalty for the destruction of mounds between terrestrial estates.*] on both sides terrestrial: for each corner or particular corner mound, adhering to the statute of the Kingdom, three marks; for each wall mound, six *scuta*, reckoning the *scuta* at two groschen — with the summoning of the field Sub-Chamberlain's office for the restoration at the expense of the destroyer himself. (In the present day, however, larger penalties are extended according to the precedents of the decrees of the Crown Tribunal, having regard to the greater value of money.) ^[*A similar penalty against those who erect mounds without authority.*] Similarly against those who erect mounds without authority and out of turn, with the formal demolition and removal of the unlawfully erected mounds as a penalty.

The penalty, however, against leaseholders of royal estates ^[*The penalty between royal and terrestrial estates.*] who destroy the boundaries between royal and terrestrial estates: one hundred marks, with restoration at the expense of the destroyer through the engagement of commissioners. But against royal subjects who demolish boundaries: fifty marks, with the restoration thereof at the expense of the leaseholder of the royal estates, according to the intent of the constitution of the year 1578.

The penalty against a commissioner absent from the act of the commission, ^[*A penalty of 100 marks for a commissioner who is absent.*] after having signed the summons letters, is one hundred marks — saving the lawful impediment of illness.

### *Boundaries Determined by the Moderation of the Right Honourable Sub-Chamberlain, the Parties' Boundary Paths Having Been Rejected, with the Oath Barred.*

When it shall happen that the Right Honourable Sub-Chamberlain rejects by his sentence the boundary path of both parties — both plaintiff and respondent — as proved by no real documents, mounds, boundary markers, or sworn inquiry, but only from the thing presented to the eyes, the position of the place, the straight line considered, and the natural boundary — by the moderation of his court and in accordance with his conscience, he shall assign the boundary path to the parties with the oath barred, so that perjuries may not occur, and shall determine the boundaries.

When also at the session of the field hearing pursuant ^[*Referral to the Tribunal in case of disagreement.*] to a Tribunal decree, two Right Honourable Sub-Chamberlains, or their officials, shall be in disagreement in their sentences as to the adjudication of the boundary path, then before the erection of the mounds they shall refer the case back to the Crown Tribunal court for the cognisance and resolution of their sentences.

## PRESCRIPTION
### *The prescription of possession is to be noted, and boundaries are to be shown.*

^[*Herburt, fol. 333.*] By custom and ancient law it was observed: that the seller of an estate was bound to protect the buyer for three ^[*The buyer of a village prescribes the right to bring an action after the lapse of three years and three months.*]

[p. 25]

years and three months from any claim by intervention and discharge. But because it often happens that the neighbours of the sold estate, whose boundaries likewise touch upon it, postpone troubling the buyer within the said three years and three months — until the seller and intercessor escapes through the prescription of the sale; after which has elapsed, they do not scruple first to disturb the buyer over the boundaries, even though they were present at the sale — for this reason we wish and enact: that when any estate ^[*Prescription of three years in respect of boundaries with the new possessor of the estates.*] is shown to the buyer by the seller, the neighbours of that estate shall be summoned by the buyer with the court messenger, to be present at that showing. If they shall have been unwilling to come, summoned thus by the court messenger, and within three years and three months shall not have raised a question of boundaries against the possessor, then the possessor shall be admitted to proof of his purchase with witnesses, and shall be assisted by the prescription of so long a time.

## DELIBERATION.
### *The judge deliberates once, and the parties contest once.*

^[*Herburt, fol. 139.*] We enact: that not more than once shall a land judge, a municipal judge, or a Sub-Chamberlain deliberate; and likewise a Captain and Vice-Captain, likewise once, for two weeks in a case of the municipal court office, shall deliberate until the next municipal sessions: under a penalty of three

[p. 26]

marks of money to be paid to the prevailing party; for which penalty the Captain shall be obliged to do justice to the party, either before Us or before the land court, or otherwise wherever it shall please the party to pursue that penalty. After which deliberation of the judge, captain, or his office, no party shall be able to improve or supplement its contested claim; it shall only hear the decree arising from that contested matter upon which deliberation was had. But because provision has been made in the new statute that, before the proof of the sentence — that is, before the decree — a party may improve its contested claim or case: this is to be understood as follows: that this same improvement of the contested matter must be made once, at the peremptory session, that is, at the time when the judge is already receiving the matter for deliberation, or for examination; for if a party were required to improve its case several times, and the judge were likewise required to deliberate upon the same several times, then a great delay of justice would arise and be permitted.

## FOREWARNING
### *To the Reader Who Would Stir up Litigation.*

To all and each of those who litigate, I wish sincerely that they would be pleased to proceed by the same method — the shorter, amicable way of settlement — and to resolve the boundary differences among themselves, avoiding

[p. 80]

the immense costs of litigation, and subjections both in the Crown Tribunal courts and at the field hearing session, over a long series of years; for it may be said that a boundary case — and most of all between powerful parties — is immortal.

He who has the intention of delimiting his estates and inheritance from a neighbouring inheritance, let him first learn to be at home what he ought to be abroad before the contestation of the litigation. And since no one is judge in his own cause, let him consult those versed in the law in boundary cases, and learned men; let him review the documents and weigh them carefully and diligently, whether they correspond with the position and situation of the places in the boundary path which he wishes to claim for himself. Let him place no trust at all in the reports and allegations of his subjects, because they very often deceive; but his own eyes and the documents are more to be believed. For the allegation of subjects is flattering and servile, which seduces and incites to litigation: they allege one thing in private to please their master, and show another in court around the sworn inquiry, or give testimony out of ignorance; and from this come perjuries with offence to the Divine Majesty. I have known many who, relying on the allegation of their subjects and not having well considered the grounds of the matter to be pursued, lost their case. And even if some perchance won the boundary path, they nevertheless perished stripped of their substance — handing over their estate to creditors, and leaving to their successors only the chests of documents of a ruined fortune and exhausted funds.

[p. 81]

Let a man take the measure of the costs and the fruit of the thing to be won. The outcome of war and of law is uncertain; their beginnings are always pleasing, their progress hard, their outcome doubtful — and every combatant loses in the end who does not begin from the end.

And mark this: that if you by force and power shall seize from your neighbour to the east, the collateral heir may wrest from you from the west.

And above all things they are to be avoided — even with just oaths, and the solemn formula weighing heavily upon him — that parcel of estate won and gained by oath is very precious; I advise no one to buy it so dearly; it is better to conclude the matter by the shorter path of friendship, without hazard to one's conscience.

A point of honour also incites various men — and great men above all — to litigation; they use every means for the sake of a point of honour to win the matter and a favourable judgment. If it falls out justly for them, good; but if they win unjustly, it weighs heavily upon their conscience, and they are obliged to make restitution before the forum of conscience.

Consider therefore, Reader, these reflections, and take note of them if you wish to live in peace — which I wish you with a clear conscience, having seen so many examples played out in the lives of various persons.

## ON ROYAL COMMISSIONS,
### *Or the Delimitation of Terrestrial Estates with Royal Estates.*

[p. 82]

Before the form and model of a commissarial act — in respect of the boundaries between the estates of His Sacred Royal Majesty on the one part and the hereditary terrestrial estates of the nobility on the other, erected or to be erected — is set forth, the difference, in particular how far royal commissions differ from the termination or delimitation of estates on both sides terrestrial, explained above, must first be considered; which may readily be gathered from the notes below.

And first, that for a future commissarial act, the referral of the land court to the field — which in the process described above, between estates on both sides terrestrial, is laid as its foundation — is not necessary; indeed, instead of the referral, a royal commission or instrument of commission must be obtained from His Sacred Royal Majesty, from the Chancery of the Kingdom, directed to certain ^[*Five commissioners are to be present together with the Sub-Chamberlain.*] principal persons — two of the dignitary order, two of the terrestrial officials, and a fifth of the Sub-Chamberlain or Boundary Chamberlain of whatever palatinate or land they may most readily be engaged — (who, on account of the absence of one or two, are customarily named in greater number) for the purpose of the delimitation of estates and the erection of mounds; which commission may also be issued outside the borders of the Kingdom, together

[p. 83]

with a mandate to the leaseholders of the royal estates and to the collateral heirs.

And thereafter, in place of a summons, the summons letters ^[*The summons letters and mandate must precede the session by two weeks.*] of the lord commissioners, both to the Captain and to the collateral heir, for a certain fixed session and place, shall be issued and served by the court messenger and two noblemen two weeks before the session, together with the royal mandates; the service return of the same, once served, the same court messenger must acknowledge before the records of his own district; and the commissarial act itself must be concluded within the space of one year and six weeks.

Furthermore, that in the court and act of a commission, by virtue of the laws and constitutions of the Kingdom contained in this book, no postponement is granted, on the ground that it is an act of a single occasion; except that if the matter cannot be concluded in one day, it may be transferred to the next immediately following day and continued and executed on the morrow.

Moreover, no appeal from that commissarial court ^[*In the commissarial act no postponement is granted. No appeal is granted.*] and its decree lies to any higher court of the Kingdom, on the ground that it is an absolute court and has, by virtue of the laws of the Kingdom (its conscience alone obligated and burdened thereto), the absolute faculty and power of judging and distinguishing estates from royal ones.

Furthermore, as regards the form of this court: that ^[*The royal subjects are required to confirm the mounds, not the leaseholder.*] in respect of the right of path and the won boundary path, not the leaseholder of the royal estates but the peasant settlers of those same

[p. 84]

estates, that is the village subjects, are required to confirm by oath the won boundary path regarding the true and just conduct and erection of the mounds at the very determination of the boundaries — namely six subjects from the community, in the oath formula prescribed by the statute of the Kingdom.

If, however, the heir of terrestrial estates wins the boundary path, then not his subjects but the nobleman himself, together with six witnesses of equal rank, shall be required to confirm the won boundary path in the oath formula prescribed by the statute of the Kingdom.

Moreover, when both parties are wanting in documentary proof ^[*The commissioners make the boundaries in the absence of boundary markers.*] and visible boundary markers, or only doubtful ones are standing, then in such a case, both parties' boundary paths having been rejected, the commissarial court shall determine the aforesaid boundaries from the thing presented to the eyes and by the moderation of its court, on equitable and just grounds, the oath barred to the parties.

^[*Prosecutorial letters against the commissarial act are void and of no effect.*] Furthermore, any citations and summonses to any court of the Kingdom whatsoever — whether on account of a motion or appeal not admitted, whether in accessory matters or in the final sentence, or for the purpose of quashing or relieving the commissarial act, issued on whatever legal pretext and ground — are void and of no effect, once the commissarial act has been determined in full and complete form, in the customary legal form and with all prior solemnities necessary for that purpose.

Furthermore, the penalty for the destruction of mounds

[p. 85]

erected by the commissioners is far greater than for terrestrial ones, with restoration at the expense of the leaseholder of the royal estates, through the same number of commissioners and the Sub-Chamberlain's office designated by His Sacred Royal Majesty.

Finally, ^[*Between royal and terrestrial estates no amicable settlement is granted.*] whereas differences occasioned by the boundaries and limits between estates on both sides terrestrial may be resolved and settled by amicable composition, those between royal and terrestrial estates cannot be concluded and resolved by the same method and by amicable means, for the reason that they are estates of the Commonwealth, which cannot, without the consent of all the orders of the Kingdom, be alienated, nor in any part diminished, severed, or made to suffer detriment.

### *On Royal Commissions and Commissioners, for the Delimitation of Royal and Terrestrial Estates.*

Formerly two dignitaries and a Sub-Chamberlain were given for commissions on boundaries.

### *But henceforth five commissioners shall be appointed.*

^[*Herburt, fol. 95.*] Likewise, since injuries and oppressions, and sometimes even acts of killing, were committed between Our registered and unregistered estates and those of Our neighbouring subjects; and since many difficulties arose in the delimitation of the boundaries, with Our Captains or leaseholders oppressing Our neighbouring subjects under cover of Our royal name and Our estates, or obstructing the delimitation of the boundaries — therefore, from the virtue and royal benevolence innate in Us, for the sake of all justice and peace for Our subjects in this matter, We promise: that to all Our subjects of whatever rank and condition, seeking boundaries from the side of Our estates, We and Our successors, according to the ancient statutes, for the discernment of equity, shall appoint and shall cause to be appointed the commissioners of that land and district in which the estates to be delimited are situated,

[p. 117]

namely two dignitaries and two officials, together with the Boundary Chamberlain thereof.

### *The commissioners, having received an oath from the elderly witnesses on both sides, shall make the delimitation.*

^[*Herburt, ibid.*] Which commissioners, after they have gone out, must summon the elderly witnesses from both sides; from whom, having received an oath, they shall proceed to the delimitation according to their recognition and testimony. If, however, those same elderly witnesses are in disagreement, then the commissioners according to their consciences, reconciling the disagreements of the elderly witnesses according to their own private judgment, shall pursue and conclude the delimitation itself. Where, moreover, the commissioners themselves should disagree, We will that the defined authority and power to conclude shall rest with the sentence of those who are in agreement by a majority of voices — the opposition of Our officials, namely the Captain or leaseholder, notwithstanding.

### *If commissioners are lacking in the proper district.*

^[*Herburt, ibid.*] If, however, there were few dignitaries in that land or palatinate in which the boundaries are to be made who could be designated for those boundaries, or if they happened to be absent — in that case, from a neighbouring palatinate, or even from a more distant one,

[p. 118]

### *The Captains shall defend the royal estates until the delimitation.*

^[*Herburt, fol. 49.*] We enact: that henceforth the Prefects of Our castles and cities shall not obtain commissions for the fixing of boundaries, but noblemen alone; ^[*A nobleman who obstructs a commission shall be liable.*] yet so that nevertheless it shall be lawful for Our Prefects or Captains (as has been received by ancient custom and is provided by statute) to defend Our estates until the declaration of the boundaries.

### *The Captain or leaseholder of the royal estates shall go out to the delimitation of the estates, and shall provide for the commissioners if he holds free estates.*

^[*Herburt, fol. 96.*] We shall command Our Captain or leaseholder of Our estates to go out and descend together with those same commissioners to delimit those same estates, and to provide for the commissioners at his expense — in particular with respect to those estates which We hold free and without encumbrance.

### *The session of the commission for delimiting the estates: two weeks.*

^[*Herburt, ibid.*] The commissarial summons together with the mandate

[p. 119]

to the Captain must be served by the court messenger and two noblemen two weeks before the delimitation, so as to certify the Captain both as to the time and as to the place.

Moreover, Our aforesaid commissioners, two weeks before they convene together for the making of the boundaries, shall inform Our Captain or leaseholder ^[*The mandate shall be served in the presence of two noblemen.*] of the time and place of their convening through the court messenger and two noblemen, and also through their own letters and Our mandate.

### *On the honour and security of the commissioners.*

^[*Herburt, ibid.*] If any commoner, in the matter and court of the boundaries, ^[*A violator of the commissarial act shall be referred to the Captain of that place for due penalties.*] or on any other occasion, should inveigh against the commissioners in words and dishonour them — such a person shall be punished with imprisonment, or otherwise with a sitting of half a year. Nobility, however, for such excesses shall be punished according to the statute.

## ON REVIEWS
### *Between Estates on Both Sides Royal, and on Reviewers Assigned by His Sacred Royal Majesty.*

Reviews, in place of commissions, between estates on both sides royal but of different possession and leasehold of Captains or leaseholders, are customary when a difference and dispute over a plot arises between leaseholders. In that situation, by decree of His Sacred Royal Majesty, certain reviewers are given and deputed for the settling of differences — not indeed from the senatorial order and terrestrial officials, but selected from the royal court from among the secretaries of His Sacred Royal Majesty, or such other

[p. 136]

principal and suitable persons as both parties shall have obtained from His Sacred Royal Majesty; with the Sub-Chamberlain or Boundary Chamberlain of that land or palatinate, or in his absence in that palatinate where the estates are situated, another, even from a more distant one, added to them.

Which reviewers have authority equal to that of commissioners in deciding plot disputes and the distinction of leaseholdings one from the other by boundaries — except that they do not have the absolute faculty of making a final decision, there being open to the parties hereafter from their final sentence, to His Sacred Royal Majesty after the court, a free appeal — after the manner of the commissarial act, customarily made for commissions between royal and terrestrial estates. Collateral heirs are required at the review act, and their acknowledgement, if their boundary walls meet the corner mounds of the royal estates; and likewise the production and sworn inquiry of elderly subjects from both leaseholdings.

Most of all in that act, the peaceful and long-standing possession of plots held by the one side peaceably and continuously against the other prevails. Which is inquired of by elderly witnesses bound by oath and proved by sworn inquiry. For since the estates are on both sides of royal right, nothing falls away from them even if it has accrued to one party, nor can they suffer detriment — except for the villagers and their leaseholders from whose side a portion of the plot avulsed has been adjudged

[p. 137]

to the other party; the cognisance and adjudication of which matter falls to the revisory court. The reviewers, both parties' boundary paths having been examined and reviewed, with due regard to the acknowledgement of the collateral heirs, the metal markers and real mounds having been examined, but above all a careful investigation having been made of continuous use and lordship, and of the testimony of elderly witnesses — shall adjudge the right of path to that party which has proved peaceful possession, demonstrated boundary markers, and relies on the acknowledgement of the collateral heirs; and shall moderate the matter and business of the boundaries of the acknowledged boundary path — both corner and particular corner mounds, and wall mounds — the oath being barred to the leaseholders themselves, as is customary in such matters of ambiguous and doubtful boundary paths of both parties, by their revisory sentence. From which, if an appeal shall have been made, and at the session arising from the appeal His Sacred Royal Majesty shall have approved the reviewers' sentence, those boundaries are always valid and acquire the force of perpetual firmness. As regards acts of violence, if any occurred, the forum is assigned in the court of His Sacred Royal Majesty after the court as for leaseholders of the royal leasehold.

### *Exemptions for soldiers and others sent on embassies.*

^[*Constitution 1638, fol. 1638.*] Exemptions for soldiers in camp, or on a war footing or expedition, or

[p. 138]

in the service of the Commonwealth — We provide that all courts shall accept the same *in personalibus* only in those cases that the ancient constitutions have described and circumscribed, upon the attestation of the Hetmans, or the colonels acting in place of the Hetmans. And likewise for those who shall be sent on an embassy — whether by Our Hetmans in the affairs of the Commonwealth, or by Us ourselves, likewise in the service of Ours and the Commonwealth's, *ad exteros Principes & ad externas Respublicas*.

### *Soldiers' exemptions.*

^[*Constitution 1673, fol. 105, vol. 5.*] We reassume the prior constitutions on exemptions in soldiers' cases, which the Tribunals and other courts are to observe in the cases in which they are required to proceed according to law, according to the ancient law.

### *Exemptions.*

^[*Constitution 1676, fol. 363, vol. 5.*] The exemptions of Our Right Honourable Hetmans, as they are permitted by the ancient law — so that they may have their force in every court *in personalibus* only: We declare. If, however, condemnations should stand against soldiers' persons remaining in camp *in Personalibus Terminis* before any present cap-tribunal courts anywhere: such condemnations likewise, which are contrary to law, We annul by the authority of the present convention, the principal matter restored *in integrum*.

[p. 139]

Since to soldiers performing military service ^[*Postponement of soldier's exemption.*] and in actual service of the Commonwealth, the postponement of soldier's exemption has been circumscribed and granted by so many constitutions in every court and tribunal of the Kingdom — both the plaintiff and the respondent may therefore avail themselves of this benefit of the law in boundary cases before the field Sub-Chamberlain's court (with prior reproduction of the attestation before that court), since personal appearance is required for the winning of the boundary path. This postponement must also be granted to the collateral heir when the corner point or particular corner point is not visible and is subject to dispute.

### *On the Office and Power of the Sub-Chamberlains.*

^[*Herburt, fol. 478.*] First of all, the Sub-Chamberlain in the matter of delimitation shall consider the natural boundary — namely a stream or river flowing between, or a new or ancient channel otherwise called *Rzeczyłko*, delimiting the estates.

Frequent disputes between Our subjects over boundaries in distinguishing their estates give rise to serious questions, from which expenses increase and those in disagreement are sometimes subjected to excessive labour. And since rivers and river channels for the most part ^[*The Sub-Chamberlain shall consider the natural boundary.*] flow and are held between estates, possessions, and villages — if any villages or estates meet one another

[p. 140]

by the mediation of any river or stream, and between those same estates there are no legally made boundaries: then every such estate shall hold its own bank of that river or stream, extended to that estate; and that same river or stream shall always be held as the boundary. Which river or stream, if even abandoning its original ^[*The ancient course of a river is assigned as the boundary.*] channel it diverts its course or flow through other places, without the agency of man but by its own natural force — nonetheless the original channel of that same river, where the flow of water previously was, shall be the true boundary of those estates. In which ancient river or stream, otherwise the channel of the ancient river, the people inhabiting either of those villages or estates may lawfully carry out their fishing, as they did when the water had its original or ancient course in that same ancient channel. But if a river or stream of this kind were diverted by the industry of man from its ancient course and channel, so as to flow into another new channel — then that new channel shall delimit the estates; and shall yield likewise as that ancient channel was accustomed to yield: one bank to one estate, the other to the other which it washes. In which case, when the diversion of the river is by the industry of man, one of those same estates — namely that in which the ancient channel remains — shall have full and entire ownership of the channel and banks of the ancient river or stream.

^[*Herburt, fol. 479.*] Moreover, the Sub-Chamberlain, under his honour and conscience,

[p. 141]

admits the party who shows more notable boundary markers to prove his case; but where the markers are lacking on both sides, proof shall be offered to the plaintiff with witnesses.

As regards the boundaries of Our nobility, We have thought fit to ^[*The party who shows more probable markers shall be admitted to confirmation.*] provide as follows: that if it should happen that any litigant for boundaries should show or produce the mounds called *kopce* — at least three — or other notable markers in the open country, commonly *wiązdy*; and in forests, markers in the manner of a cross, as is the custom — then Our Sub-Chamberlain, discerning the one who has shown better and more notable markers or mounds, shall under his honour and conscience be required and bound to send him to prove his case. Where, however, both parties lack notable markers between the estates — then the plaintiff shall be admitted to prove the boundaries with witnesses as the law requires.

### *The Sub-Chamberlain shall not transpose the session of the parties, unless they consent or a cause exists.*

^[*Herburt, ibid.*] Since Sub-Chamberlains or their Boundary Chamberlains for the most part transposed the delimitations of boundaries, either out of favour or on account of the necessity of one party, the other being unwilling — and from this, through negligence, favourable delays were committed in the delimitation of boundaries — We, providing for this error, have decreed and decree to be perpetually observed: that from now on, the Sub-Chamberlains of all lands of the entire Kingdom shall not transpose boundary sessions of their own accord, against the order of the law,

[p. 142]

under a penalty of fourteen marks to be exacted by Us from whoever shall do otherwise — except where the consent of the parties shall concur, or a legitimate cause shall intervene.

A party condemned in the land court ^[*Herburt, fol. 477.*] is not admitted to contest in the field; indeed, notwithstanding any documentary evidence whatsoever, or more apparent signs, if the defendant should produce any at that time, it is nonetheless to stand with the plaintiff's conduct and the oath with witnesses.

At that same field session, it shall not be permitted to the cited party to raise any contest before the Sub-Chamberlain or his office regarding the right of path — which he has already lost in the land court — but the plaintiff shall lead the office according to his own will and conscience; and shall make the boundaries by raising mounds and making other metal markers, notwithstanding any opposition whatsoever of the cited party, nor any documentary evidence or more apparent signs, if any he should produce at that time. ^[*The plaintiff shall confirm as seventh.*] When these things are done, beside the final and particular corner mound, the plaintiff shall swear with six witnesses of equal rank regarding the true and just delimitation: that he has taken nothing of the land and estate from the cited party, and alienated nothing from him.

Furthermore, if the condemned party should not comply even in the field, ^[*Herburt, fol. 478.*] the same applies: where the plaintiff shall have sworn the oath with witnesses, the Sub-Chamberlain decrees that the boundaries shall endure in perpetuity.

[p. 143]

If at the field session the respondent should fail to appear and be in contumacy: the Sub-Chamberlain or his office shall nonetheless be required to follow the plaintiff and make the boundaries according to his leading. Nonetheless beside the final and particular corner mound, the plaintiff shall swear with witnesses, notwithstanding the absence of the cited party, in the presence only of the Sub-Chamberlain's office. Which boundaries, made as aforesaid and confirmed by the oath of the plaintiff, the Sub-Chamberlain or his office shall decree to endure and be kept in perpetuity.

## MINORS.
### *If Co-Heirs Cited in a Boundary Case Were Minors.*

^[*Herburt, fol. 283.*] If there were several participants or co-parties of one and the same estate, cited by some other person for the making of boundaries; and among those cited parties there were some co-heirs who were minors, likewise cited — ^[*Minors are required to respond in a boundary case.*] then proceedings shall be taken against such parties, both by the land court and by the Sub-Chamberlain's office, notwithstanding their minority. To which delimitation the court and Sub-Chamberlain's office shall proceed. Nevertheless the Sub-Chamberlain or his office shall reserve for them an action for their interest against the plaintiff of the boundaries; ^[*The reserved action of the minor is saved.*] namely when they shall attain the years of discretion — then it shall be lawful for them to proceed at law against those

[p. 144]

by whom they should consider themselves to have been prejudiced; yet they shall proceed only for their own portion and interest.

### *When Minors Reach the Years of Discretion, They May Proceed for Injuries Suffered; Prescription Not Obstructing Them.*

^[*Herburt, ibid.*] Wishing to succour and provide for children, especially those in need, We enact: that if an injury shall have been done to any persons by someone following the death of their father — on coming to the years of discretion, they may proceed at law against those who committed the injury, and the exception of prescription cannot be opposed to them, unless the lawful age be then fully completed in them. And if within the due time they shall have neglected to proceed for the injuries once done to them, then the exception of prescription stands against them on account of the negligence alleged.

### *Collateral Heirs Must Be Summoned.*

^[*Herburt, fol. 475.*] Against neighbours or collateral heirs, who if together with the cited party must necessarily be cited both to the land court and to the field court, the following procedure must be followed, so that they may not obstruct the delimitation in the field.

If, however, there were other noblemen having neighbouring

[p. 145]

estates, whose boundary walls were adjacent and contiguous to the estates between which such boundaries were to be made, and who considered themselves to have an interest by virtue of their contiguity in those same boundaries — such neighbouring collateral heirs shall likewise be summoned by the plaintiff for one session, together with the principal party, before the land court; and likewise as with the principal party, so too shall the plaintiff proceed with them, so that they may not obstruct the boundaries at the field session.

## MOUNDS.
### *The Penalty and Satisfaction for Destroyed Mounds.*

^[*Herburt, fol. 447.*] If anyone shall have destroyed or violently annihilated — in part or in whole — any mounds erected, or other metal markers: such a violator shall pay to the party proceeding against him three marks of current money for every particular corner mound. For other mounds destroyed and all metal markers destroyed and annihilated, he shall pay six *skots*. And the same violator shall nonetheless be required to bring the Sub-Chamberlain's office to the place of the violated boundaries at his own expense, and to restore and newly erect those same boundaries destroyed by him — which he shall be required to do, after notification by the court messenger and noblemen on behalf of the party that suffered such injury, within eight weeks: under a penalty of fourteen marks succumbing to the party and the same to the court. Which penalty having been paid, in

[p. 146]

the next eight weeks he shall be required to restore the boundaries as aforesaid; and as often as he shall refuse and be unwilling to restore those boundaries — so many times shall he forfeit the aforesaid fourteen marks to the party and the same to the court, in each successive eight weeks.

### *What Penalty Is Paid by One Who Erects Mounds Without Authority, and How He Gives Satisfaction.*

^[*Herburt, ibid.*] If anyone on another's soil or estate, without the Sub-Chamberlain and his office, shall of his own accord, voluntarily, and rashly erect and make boundary markers — such a person shall likewise pay three marks for every particular corner mound, and six *skots* for others (as above). And nonetheless all the mounds and metal markers so unlawfully made shall be formally destroyed, removed, and annihilated by the Sub-Chamberlain's office: so that in future no prejudice may come to the injured party from any trace of them — which shall be done under a penalty of fourteen marks to the party and the same to the court. And proceedings shall similarly be taken against such a person, in each eight weeks: as against the violent destroyer of boundaries as described above.

### *What Postponements Are Admitted to the Respondent in the Field.*

^[*Herburt, fol. 478.*] The cited party may, however, defer such a field session

[p. 147]

by true illness, and likewise by a principal postponement, and not otherwise.

### *On the Sub-Chamberlain in Masovia.*

^[*Masovian Exceptions, Constitution 1576, fol. 947, vol. 2.*] The land Sub-Chamberlain, according to the ancient custom observed in the Palatinate of Masovia, shall himself and through his Boundary Chamberlain conduct the site inspection and how it shall be conducted, and similarly the cases concerning boundaries, and shall bring them before the land court. And so that the contests which the parties shall raise before him or his Boundary Chamberlain may also be raised and presented before the court in his presence, and likewise the title deeds that the parties have between themselves be shown. And the court, according to the ancient custom and his testimony, and from the contests of the parties and their presentations or title deeds, as it shall understand according to the common law — shall have the power to adjudge both the right to proceed and the proof. And this has been preserved from of old, because the court shall make a legal decree from the contest, and the land notary shall write down the contest sufficiently; the common law therefore has not and could not permit the Sub-Chamberlain or his Boundary Chamberlain to have records. Appeal, however, shall be free to the party according to the ancient custom.

### *On the Making of Boundaries.*

^[*Ibid.*] When one nobleman brings an action against another nobleman, so that between those estates of theirs adjacent to one another — where the boundaries were

[p. 148]

not fixed but only use and possession of the parties — he should begin proceedings in court: then the court shall adjudge and send the appropriate land officials; and whichever party shows better and more notable markers before them, and through elderly witnesses and witnesses would prove its peaceful possession of that estate — for how long and through what places it held its estate — and those officials should accept this: then there boundaries shall be made and new mounds erected, overriding the objection of the other party. And if on both sides they should see or accept no boundary markers: then the respondent shall have the right to proceed, and through what places he shall go with witnesses and shall swear, not transgressing the first boundary of his entry or passage — then mounds or boundary markers shall be made according to the ancient custom of the land.

### *On the Renewal of Boundaries.*

^[*Herburt, fol. 948.*] When one person summons another to renew boundaries with him and erect mounds through the old boundary markers between their estates — which markers and ancient boundary wall or mounds he would name in the summons and undertake to confirm by oath — such a person shall by law have the priority of presentation and proof through elderly witnesses from those same markers. Which, if the land official accepts them and the other party denies them: the plaintiff with witnesses shall prove by oath that those markers are old and true boundary ones between those estates,

[p. 149]

or the ancient boundary wall, or between the boundary road and the old mounds; according to which markers he shall present his passage and use; and so following those markers through which he passes and swears, there boundaries shall be made and mounds erected. Which witnesses the plaintiff shall name and enter before the land court at the nearest sitting after the judgment, in the manner with an oath formula; and at the place of the differences with those witnesses of his passage from corner to corner through the places and boundary markers, as mentioned above — he shall be required to swear at the sitting appointed to the parties by the official before that same official. Which act in its entirety the aforesaid official shall be required to declare before the land court of the mounds and boundary markers made or circled, and to enter them in the land records. And where one party should have a privilege, or any letters, or any deeds concerning the boundaries or certain boundary places — namely certain boundary features — that party shall have priority in presenting and supporting those boundaries and places, according to its title deeds and ancient observance. And this — if from that time on it should not depart from such laws or title deeds through newly made boundaries, and from the making of boundaries and the erection or circling of mounds — then both parties, both the respondent and the plaintiff, shall pay the Sub-Chamberlain the fee of *puskopy*, that is thirty groschen, and the Starosta another thirty groschen of Polish currency; and to the court messenger six

[p. 150]

groschen from those same boundaries they are required to pay. And where mounds have been erected or boundaries made between the parties by mutual will: then both parties shall be required to give that same office only thirty groschen and no more; and to the court messenger three groschen.

### *On the Authority of Officials at a Site Inspection in Masovia.*

^[*Masovian Exceptions 1576, fol. 937, vol. 2.*] When a Boundary Chamberlain or court messenger fixes a certain day for the parties for the conduct of a site inspection at the estate in accordance with the summons, and the fixing of that day has been recorded in the land or municipal records — if any party should not attend at that appointed day of the named inspection, nor appear before that official at the place and session of the inspection: such a party shall, from such a removal of that aforesaid official made before the court at the next sitting, be condemned and adjudged to the loss of the matter concerning which that summons was brought and the inspection was to take place. And if the official himself should fail to come on that appointed and recorded day for the conduct of that inspection, *intercedente legali impedimento*: then another day for the conduct of that inspection, the same Sub-Chamberlain's office shall fix at the next land sittings and shall be required to come to it — since no one should or may lose through the office. And if one party should drive away the other by open threats or any violence from the inspection,

[p. 151]

or should mock it with any dishonourable or quarrelsome words: against such a party that office has full power to adjudge *penas in Statuto Regni descriptas*, which adjudication it shall bring before the court, and the court on sufficient proof shall confirm the same according to the testimony of that official; and that obstructor or violent hinderer of the inspection shall be required to respond to a summons according to law, without any further inspection, without any postponement. For which inspection the person who took a site inspection need give no more to the Boundary Chamberlain or court messenger than three groschen to both, and they too shall not demand more.

### *The Election of the Land Court and the Sub-Chamberlain's Court, and the Sub-Chamberlain's Oath.*

^[*Constitution 1588, fol. 1211, vol. 2.*] The election for the election of officials of the land court — after the departure or death of any of them — according to the ancient custom, the Palatine, and in his absence the senior Castellan of that palatinate or land, shall fix at the latest within six weeks at the customary place. And if the Palatine should not fix that sitting within six weeks, then the Castellan shall be required to do so. In the same manner shall the election of the land Sub-Chamberlain proceed, who is also required to be sworn from that time — excepting the present holders, from the date of this constitution. And

[p. 152]

such elections We safeguard with the same security as the Tribunal judges. And the Sub-Chamberlain shall swear as follows: ^[*The oath formula of the Right Honourable Sub-Chamberlain.*] I, [name], swear to the Lord GOD that I will judge justly, according to the Lord GOD, the written law, and the justice of the parties' contests, listen carefully to those contests and receive them; likewise consider the boundary markers and their probabilities, and faithfully describe them; and likewise keep my records and those of my predecessors faithfully, and enter matters in them; and at which court I shall have no regard for any person — neither friend nor enemy, rich nor poor, stranger nor kinsman — showing no favour or disfavour to anyone in that my court; and taking no gift from anyone with respect to that court. So help me Lord God, and this Holy Gospel.

### *On the Relaxation and Annulment of Banishment.*

^[*Same year, fol. 1220, vol. 2.*] If anyone being under banishment should reconcile himself with the opposing party and receive from that party itself a release from that banishment: then such a person shall be given a relaxation of banishment from Our Chancery, which he shall obtain at the latest within twelve weeks after the release; and during that time he shall have standing before every court, and may register any recognitions. And since all cases and actions are customarily obstructed by the party defeated in law by various means: therefore We provide that at every instance — even *Bannitionis in ipso puncto* — the party defeated in law shall be

[p. 153]

required to allege ^[*A wrongly obtained process is to be proved.*] *male obtentum* without bringing a separate action therefor. And in such a measure a free appeal to the Tribunal shall be permitted. And when the Tribunal shall recognise that process to be good and shall have imposed banishment on the party defeated in law: no annulment shall be given to such a person any longer. Nevertheless after such banishment, the party defeated in law shall have twelve weeks' free time for a settlement *cum jure vincente*; and may make deeds for the settlement of that case, may dispose of and sell its estates, and satisfy creditors *authoritate præsentis Constitutionis mediante* — otherwise if it is not willing, then the Starosta shall already be required to carry out execution against that person under banishment. And if the banishment was obtained *in contumaciam*: then at the summons of that Starosta and the opposing party by the person under banishment before the Tribunal, he may annul that banishment by his oath, even if he has no annulment *jure victus*. And where from anyone in any case banishment has been lifted by *per definitivam sententiam*: there no relaxation from Our Chancery shall be needed. Likewise where banishment has been imposed on anyone *in Contumaciam* in any case: immediately ^[*A discharge is granted on account of ignorance of the process.*] upon swearing *super ignorantiam illius Bannitionis*, he shall have standing *in illo Judicio*, and thereafter may remove that banishment from himself in the manner described above.

[p. 154]

### *On Sub-Chamberlain's Surrogacies.*

^[*Constitution 1601, fol. 1503, vol. 2.*] Since it is provided by law that Sub-Chamberlains shall sub-delegate surrogates to their places in their own cases — those surrogates, however, must be for that one act only; and it is necessary that they be sworn, as the Sub-Chamberlains are; and it is difficult to persuade persons of good judgment and honourable reputation to this. We therefore enact: that each Sub-Chamberlain, when he has his own case, shall be required to bring out the nearest Sub-Chamberlain or his Boundary Chamberlain, or a sworn judicial official of his land or district, for such acts; who as a sworn official shall conduct the boundary act, and having subscribed himself upon his summons letters, shall be required under the penalty enacted for commissioners to arrive at the appointed time. This is to be understood for future times.

### *Ecclesiastical Persons in the Crown Holding Hereditary Estates.*

^[*Constitution 1607, fol. 1603, vol. 2.*] Since some ecclesiastical persons are accustomed to acquire terrestrial hereditary estates for themselves and their relatives and successors: We therefore declare that from such paternal estates and those serving them and their relatives and successors in whatever manner — and not given and founded for the Church — ecclesiastical persons shall be required to perform the military expedition, and shall be subject to all

[p. 155]

other burdens like the secular estate. Likewise, with respect to all injuries arising from the plots and estates mentioned: they shall be required to respond to the injured party as is appropriate in the land court and municipal court; and likewise when by way of appeals and remissions such cases shall fall to the Crown Tribunal, they shall be adjudicated by all the secular judges and by six ecclesiastical persons delegated to all cases by the ecclesiastical estate according to the constitution, from the side of the hereditary estates. However, if an ecclesiastical person should be found among the co-parties in such a case: such cases shall be adjudicated by the secular court alone. Likewise cases concerning such estates as began to be adjudicated *cum Personis sæcularibus* and thereafter *lite pendente* fell to ecclesiastical persons, whether by succession or upon the acquisition of a right — the secular court shall adjudicate in the manner described below, *ex regestro causarum sæcularium, fine rejectione*: excepting those estates which have been or shall be attached to the Church *per commutationem* or by the sale of ecclesiastical estates, which are subject to the ancient prerogatives of the Church. The *onera publica æquivalentia* remain upon the commuted and sold estates. Other cases — both ecclesiastical and secular — *in sæculari* shall be conducted according to the constitution of 1578. And in boundary cases, those parish priests who have no subjects

[p. 156]

and no separate farms, but are founded only on tithes and annuities, shall not be joined together with the heirs, but such cases shall belong to the heirs alone.

### *Which Cases of Ecclesiastical Persons Fall Under the Terrestrial Law.*

^[*Herburt, fol. 452.*] Ecclesiastical persons, for estates or injuries to estates subject to the common law, shall proceed by the terrestrial law. The injuries are in respect of boundaries, peasants, fugitives, killing, and wounding — otherwise according to the statutes of King John Albert.

### *Judicial Cases Touching upon Land, Personal and Simple, and Postponements of True Illness and for the Principal.*

^[*Constitution 1611, fol. 78, vol. 3.*] In judicial cases that concern land and are called personal and simple, no postponements shall be permitted — except that if ^[*A postponement of illness is granted.*] an oath in such and other cases falls upon someone and is ordered to a party, then through the court messenger of that land, a resident with full authority, a postponement of true illness shall be permitted, both to the plaintiff and to the respondent, until the next sittings. At which sittings, from the preserved session, the party to whom the postponement shall be granted shall

[p. 157]

swear as a third, that it was truly ill; and after the oath it shall be admitted to the case.

A postponement shall also be given for the principal ^[*Ibid., fol. 79.*] likewise through ^[*A postponement for the principal.*] a possessory deputy sent with full authority, if personal sessions fall in different courts — that is, if an oath has arisen for someone, he is required to discharge it first in the case that involves the greater sum, and that one shall be postponed as the lesser to the next sittings; and thereafter he shall prove this to the one to whom the postponement was given by the signatory letters of that court, that he truly had a case under oath there, and a proceeding from which that case arose — so that the plaintiff may not be mocked and damaged in vain *sub amissione causæ*.

Adding to this: if in injuries and land differences ^[*Ibid., fol. 79.*] that arise between neighbours ^[*A boundary case cannot be decided without cognisance of it.*] there are no boundaries made, and one summons another either to escape injuries or wishing himself peace — then the boundary case shall first be adjudicated, and then the injuries considered. However, without delay the boundaries are to be continued; otherwise, if the party shall not pursue them, the actions brought for injuries and losses shall have free recourse to the law, without any opposition or legal impediment.

### *Security of the Lord Senators and Land Deputies.*

^[*Constitution 1659, fol. 591, vol. 4.*] Reassuming in full the constitutions of the most recent Sejms concerning the security of the lord senators, Crown officials, commissioners, deputies to foreign lords, and land deputies, and their wards — by the authority of the present convention We declare that all decrees and condemnations that shall have stood against any of the lord senators and land deputies elected to the Sejm, whether by contest or in contumacy, at whatever election sitting, in whatever court of the greater or lesser tribunal, and that shall be standing until the relation sittings — all such decrees and condemnations We annul. After the relation sitting, however, within two weeks each of the lord senators and deputies shall be required to conduct himself *in quocunque Judicio* at the instance of any person. And so that there may be no doubt about this in the future, by the authority of the present convention We declare this law to be a perpetual law. Which shall also serve for deputies to foreign lords, residents, or commissioners *post expeditam functionem*.

### *On the Security of the Right Honourable Senators and Honourable Land Deputies, both of the Crown and of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania.*

^[*Constitution 1661, fol. 721, vol. 4.*] Inclining ourselves to the constitution of the year 1659 on the security of the Right Honourable Senators and Honourable

[p. 159]

Land Deputies — all condemnations, and any courts and *actus quosvis Juridicos* obtained during the present and future Sejms up to the relation sitting, both before the Tribunal and *in quibusvis subselliis in præjudicium & aggravationem* of both the Right Honourable Senators and the Honourable Land Deputies and their spouses, *etiam in causis dotis & inscriptionum* — We annul and destroy, wishing that the tribunals of all courts shall in everything conform to this law. That same security shall also cover the Inspectors of Our estates.

### *Security of the Lord Senators and Land Deputies of the Crown and of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania.*

^[*Constitution 1667, fol. 927, vol. 4.*] Reassuming the constitutions of the years 1659 and 1661 on the security of the lord senators and land deputies, and all those who are secured by the mentioned constitutions, written *pro perpetua lege* — We declare that they shall not be required to swear to the legality of their presence at the Sejm and of their function before the Tribunals; but Our Right Honourable Senators, deputies *ad Exteros*, and commissioners shall, by the attestation of whichever Right Honourable Marshal is at the Sejm; and the Honourable Land Deputies by the attestation

[p. 160]

of the Honourable Marshal of the Deputies at the Sejm, upon showing their presence, shall be required to conduct themselves *super suspensionem Causæ ex Regestro incidentis simpliciter*. Any sworn proofs *super suspensionem ex occasione prorogationis causæ* contrary to the prescription of the law, ordered in whatever tribunal of the Kingdom and of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, as contrary to the law, We repeal and annul. Which constitution shall also extend to those condemnations and decrees issued and obtained during the preceding and present Sejm. That same constitution shall cover those who from the dependency of the present Sejm shall be elected at the relation sittings to the Treasury Tribunal.

### *Security of the Right Honourable Senators and Honourable Land Deputies.*

^[*Constitution 1678, fol. 565, vol. 5.*] Since against the express laws written concerning the Right Honourable Senators and Honourable Deputies before the Crown Tribunals there have been excesses — in that some in the exercise of their function have been burdened by decrees, and some by condemnations — therefore, reassuming all constitutions, and in particular those of the year 1659 and of the year 1661, likewise of the year 1667: all such decrees and condemnations — and in particular those obtained against the Honourable Władysław and his son Jan Dobrski and other persons in that same case — We annul; and We enact by the authority of the present convention that municipal courts

[p. 161]

shall not dare to execute such condemnations *sub privatione Officij*; and those executions carried out during the Sejm We declare *pro nullis & irritis, salvo negotio principali*. Safeguarding this for the future: should anyone dare to obtain a decree or condemnation against the Right Honourable Senators and Land Deputies *sub tempus per legem exemptum*, such person shall be required to *succumbere pænæ mille Marcarum Polonicalium* ^[*Penalty of one thousand marks.*] to be vindicated from the register of the lord senators and deputies.

### *Soldiers Exempt from Courts.*

^[*Constitution 1609, fol. 1681, vol. 2.*] Guarding against those dangers that might fall upon the Crown and Our adjacent domains from various sides, and which could not be repelled without soldiers — We therefore, for all those who on any side, wherever the need of the Commonwealth indicates, shall have been directed by the Crown Hetmans and of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania — reassuming in full the constitution of the reign of King Stefan in the year 1578, We exempt them from all courts in general, *exceptis Causis ad Inscriptiones, Expulsionis, Violentiæ & Rei judicatæ*.

### *Exemptions.*

^[*Constitution 1620, fol. 373, vol. 3.*] Captains of cavalry and those who at that time shall be

[p. 162]

in Our service and that of the Commonwealth are to have exemptions from all laws, going according to the constitution of the year 1590 made on exemptions — excepting *recentia crimina* from the date of the Sejm. Which constitution shall also serve the Grand Duchy of Lithuania.

### *A Vault for the Sub-Chamberlain's Records in Sandomierz; On the Records and the Boundary Notary of the Palatinate of Sandomierz.*

^[*Constitution 1631, fol. 698, vol. 3.*] Considering the security of the boundary records of the inhabitants of the Palatinate of Sandomierz, so that they may not be kept in private houses as has been the case up to now — for this reason We designate a vault in Our castle of Sandomierz for the keeping of the Sandomierz boundary records; which vault the Right Honourable Starosta of that place shall show. And so that access for everyone needing extracts from those records may be the easier — the Honourable Sub-Chamberlain of Sandomierz shall be required to provide from his Sub-Chamberlain's office a notary resident in that same palatinate. Which notary, before he takes those boundary records into his administration, shall be required to swear an oath at the land sittings of Sandomierz in these words: I, [name], swear to Almighty GOD, One in the Holy Trinity, that I shall faithfully enter into the boundary records the boundary acts presented

[p. 163]

to me for inscription by the Sub-Chamberlain's office — changing nothing, adding nothing — as well as the contests of the parties; having no regard in this matter to anyone, whether poor or rich, friend or enemy. And all the records given over to me by the Honourable Sub-Chamberlain of Sandomierz or his office, I shall keep faithfully. So help me Lord GOD, and the Passion of Christ the Lord. Every Boundary Chamberlain of that palatinate shall therefore, after the conduct of the boundaries, be required to send the act of those same boundaries, properly written up, into the hands of the said Sub-Chamberlain's notary, *sub amissione Officij sui*, within the course of six weeks, and to enter it in the records of his district. And since the Honourable Krzysztof of Tęczyn Ossoliński, the present Sub-Chamberlain of Sandomierz, *ex nunc* releases the revenue from the Sandomierz customs for the provision of that Sub-Chamberlain's notary — We therefore approve this for that same notary for all time. In this same manner the Palatinate of Cracow receives the act and the Sub-Chamberlain's notary. And since the Palatinate of Sandomierz shall at the next sitting, which shall be fixed and conducted immediately after the Sejm in that same palatinate, designate a certain tax for the transcription of the land records of the Palatinate of Sandomierz, and shall there draw up a more sufficient ordinance concerning this matter,

[p. 164]

which We approve by the present constitution — therefore, providing in this for the needs of the noble estate of the Palatinate of Sandomierz, since access to the land records is not always open, We order that all boundary acts and divisions in general from each district shall be separately extracted *specificè in unum Volumen* and thereafter all together from all the districts delivered to the records of the Honourable Sub-Chamberlain of Sandomierz as boundary records, and kept in proper order by the said Sub-Chamberlain's notary together with the others. And so that those boundary acts and divisions from each district may be properly extracted and delivered to the Sub-Chamberlain's office: the Honourable Land Notary of the Palatinate of Sandomierz shall be required to see to this with diligence *sub quadrupla pæna contra negligentes Officiales sancita & sub amissione Officij sui*. And in the future all acts of perpetuities, as mentioned above, shall no longer be submitted to the land or municipal records, but to the Sub-Chamberlain's records of that palatinate.

### *Equation of the Palatinates in the Sub-Chamberlain's Offices.*

^[*Constitution 1677, fol. 464, vol. 5.*] Equating, as in an equal Commonwealth, the prerogatives of the Sub-Chamberlain's office between the Sieradz ^[**Equation of Sandomierz with Sieradz.**] and Sandomierz palatinates — We will that in Sieradz, as in Sandomierz, there shall be

[p. 165]

as many Boundary Chamberlains in the palatinate as there are districts; and likewise a Sub-Chamberlain's notary — in which matter the constitution serving the Palatinate of Sandomierz, which the Palatinate of Cracow also adopted in the year 1631, under the title *Vault for Sub-Chamberlain's Records*, shall in the future serve the Palatinate of Sieradz.

### *Delimitation of the Inhabitants of the Palatinates of Ruthenia, Bełz, Podolia, and Volhynia Among Themselves.*

^[*Constitution 1601, fol. 1505, vol. 2.*] We enact that the inhabitants of the palatinates of Ruthenia, Bełz, Podolia, and Volhynia — between whose hereditary estates the matter concerns not jurisdiction or forum with respect to the estates, but only the delimitation itself and the settlement of the plots — since such differences requiring settlement only in the matter of use itself, having no controversy over jurisdiction and forum, require delimitation: therefore, without waiting for a principal commission according to the ancient laws and constitutions, an inhabitant of any of those palatinates who requires boundaries from the adjacent inhabitants of another palatinate of the above-named palatinates shall bring out both Sub-Chamberlain's offices — both that of his own district and that of the district from which the differences arise — as well as the standard-bearer and the military judge of both districts. Which Sub-Chamberlains,

[p. 166]

standard-bearers, and military judges shall be required to subscribe themselves upon the summons of the injured party, and to appear at the appointed time of the summons under a fine of one hundred marks — for which each shall be required to answer before the Tribunal among the official cases, if he should be unwilling to subscribe, or having subscribed should fail to appear, excepting the cause of true illness. Upon such a summons and notification the respondent shall be required to appear before the plaintiff's party and lawfully dispute over the ownership of the plot and the differences; and the absence of one or two of the mentioned officials shall not obstruct this — indeed, those who assemble at that time shall be required to conduct that act. There, for the weightier proofs, an oath shall be adjudged to whichever party by the Sub-Chamberlains or their offices, together with the other dignitaries — that is, the standard-bearers and military judges — yet preserving in this a free appeal to the Tribunal from such a decree, if any party should consider itself injured by such a decree. And if those two offices should be in disagreement among themselves and should issue different sentences or decrees: then to both parties, from either side, from the decree of whichever court and office, there shall be a free appeal to the Tribunal; the recognition of those decrees being in that palatinate which shall fall due first — that is, either Volhynian, or Kievan, or Bracław — after the conduct of such

[p. 167]

a case. And the Sub-Chamberlains, standard-bearers, military judges, and that office shall, for the effective conclusion of such decrees, send their sufficient sentences, with the sufficient contests and presentations explained, to the Tribunal — for the Poles in Polish writing, and for the Volhynians in Ruthenian writing. For which such session the parties shall be required to appear in an equal retinue — that is, of thirty horses only, counting among them servants and friends, without any firearms, only with customary weapons — that is, with sabre, sword, cutlass, and rapier — under a fine of five hundred marks: for which the respondent shall give account and shall have a complicated session before the Tribunal among the official cases. And where there should be an adjacency of an estate of His Royal Majesty leased by anyone: then by the customary method, according to the law, such differences shall be delimited by commissioners.

### *Delimitation of the Palatinates of Ruthenia, Bełz, and Volhynia.*

^[*Constitution 1647, fol. 124, vol. 4.*] Reassuming the constitution of the year 1601 and declaring it, on the delimitation of the palatinates of Ruthenia, Bełz, Volhynia, and Podolia — We will and enact by the force of this Sejm: that for such a division of plots and differences between those three palatinates — Ruthenian, Bełz, and

[p. 168]

Volhynian — and their districts and lands, those needing boundary proceedings may bring out together with the Sub-Chamberlains or their offices not only the standard-bearers and the land office but also other land officials together with the Sub-Chamberlains or their offices — that is, stewards of the table, cup-bearers, under-stewards, military judges, sword-bearers, treasurers — provided they are from whatever district or land of those palatinates; then sufficiently and fully such a delimitation may be conducted, provided there are not fewer than three from one side and likewise from the other side. Adding to this: when the summons from the plaintiff's commissioners reaches the cited party and shall have been acknowledged in the municipal court *ubi bona Citati ratione quorum res est consistunt*, the cited party shall be required *pro tempore & loco* assigned by the summons of the plaintiff's commissioners to bring out his own commissioners from his own side; and for him an equal number shall likewise be sufficient as for the plaintiff. And if by the appointed time from the plaintiff's commissioners the cited party shall not have brought out his commissioners, and either shall have gone out himself or shall not have gone out (in which only true illness, *juxta Statutum in Termino sequenti comprobanda*, can excuse him) — then the commissioners brought out by the plaintiff shall be able to delimit on their own and erect the mounds; and this We enact to be ratified and firm. Referring in that delimitation first to documentary evidence, and *in defectu* to the testimony of elderly witnesses, and according to the statute,

[p. 169]

natural markers and possibilities, without prejudicing the right of path if either party should have it; and if even that should be lacking and both parties should have doubtful documents — they shall be able to divide the disputed plot in half, so that every avenue to differences may be closed. And when such a commission stands, it can never be disturbed.

Since boundary cases and their decision are accustomed to take ^[*Constitution 1768, under the title: Union of the Crown Tribunal in Boundary Cases, fol. 220.*] the most time in the Tribunals, and justice is frequently made to suffer through inadequate and poor delineation of maps produced before the court — therefore We enact *lege præsenti* that such cases shall necessarily have their first instance before the land court, which court *non intrando in cognitionem* of that case, nor prescribing any rule, shall send it to the plot *cum præfixione Termini Condescensoralis Granicialis & optione conducendi Geometræ iurati*; and there the Sub-Chamberlain's office — whether the Sub-Chamberlain himself (who all shall be sworn by the formula in the constitution of 1588) or his Boundary Chamberlains — admitting no appeal *in præliminariis causæ*, with prior summons of the collateral heirs, having regard to the documents and boundary markers or the natural boundary, and *in defectu* of those obvious ones, with prior sworn inquiry and testimony of elderly witnesses, shall adjudicate that case *finaliter*.

The map, if it shall be necessary, formed on the plot, shall be signed; and the case of such a court *per apellationem*

[p. 170]

from a final sentence, or in a case not admitted, by grievance, shall then go to the Tribunal. Which Tribunal, if from any legitimate considerations it should not be able to approve the Sub-Chamberlain's decree at the table, shall then refer the matter to a second field hearing on the plot, and shall designate another Sub-Chamberlain and not a Boundary Chamberlain; and if at such a second field hearing conducted on the plot the first boundary decree should be approved, then that decision shall *transire in rem plene iudicatam*; and We will that the party wishing to disturb such decrees be punished *pænis convulsionis Decretorum*.

Cases not having *delineationem Mappæ & exequitationem Ductuum*, even if begun before the Tribunal, shall be adjudicated first and foremost before the land court *cum decernenda Condescensione ad Campum*. The party raising a boundary question concerning a plot ^[*Acts of violence are prohibited in disputed places under penalties.*] possessed by a neighbour shall in no way violently take possession of the plots or forests claimed, nor commit any damage, *sub pæna siedzenia Wieży czterech Niedziel & mille marcarum ex Regestro Terminorum Tactorum vindicandarum & responsione* for such damage and violence, *etiam ante decisionem Causæ Granicialis extendendæ*. And the party possessing the plot or forest in dispute shall upon its adjudication to the other party be required to surrender it *una cum calculo seu bonificatione*.

[p. 171]

Boundary Chamberlains shall be possessioned residents ^[*Sub-Chamberlains must be residents of the palatinate.*] in the palatinates or land, *sub amissione Officij*; and in the Palatinates of Kiev, Volhynia, Podolia, Bracław, and Bełz, on account of the remoteness of the districts, We permit the Honourable Sub-Chamberlain to have three Boundary Chamberlains in each district.

And since the Convocation Sejm drew up a penalty for officials ^[*On official reports at field hearings, fol. 219.*] who write dissenting opinions at field hearings, and the rigour of that penalty *metum incutere* seems to the judges — wishing sometimes to avoid an unjust sentence and the risk of responsibility in the promotion by *Activiorem Partem* of the case before the Tribunal — We therefore, clarifying the Convocation constitution, will it to be understood as follows: that if from indifferent proofs and evidence such cases conducted by *per aliter sentientes Officiales* should be written in disagreement, and before the Tribunal from those two sentences one *ex potiori iustitia* should prove to be more just — then in that case the sentence of the other official shall not carry *pænalitatem* for the one writing the dissent; but the one writing the dissent shall be subject to punishment only in the case where his sentence should prove manifestly favourable *contra Documenta* or *aliter obloquentibus Inquisitionibus*, or contrary to the Rule of the Tribunal decree.

Furthermore, if those same officials having agreed *in resolutione Jurium*, specifically *in puncto*

[p. 172]

of ordering the session and of satisfaction, they should be required to write dissenting opinions — then in that case also they shall be subject to the penalty *per Constitutionem* 1764 *imposite & per Tribunal irrogandæ*. The dissenting sentence, however, shall be null.

**Translator's Notes**

[T.N. 1] *Buxus* in classical Latin denotes the box tree (*Buxus sempervirens*); *Cis* (spelled *Ciss* here) in Polish denotes the yew (*Taxus baccata*) — a different species. Paciorkowski groups them as a single entry. Both are evergreen trees of similar dense-wooded character; the English rendering "box / yew tree" follows the source's grouping without resolving the botanical discrepancy.

[T.N. 2] *Terebintus* in classical Latin is the terebinth (*Pistacia terebinthus*), a Mediterranean tree not native to Poland. Paciorkowski equates it with Polish *Modrzew* (larch, *Larix decidua*), which is the ecologically appropriate Polish equivalent. The translation "larch" follows the Polish gloss as the operationally intended meaning in this context.

| *Succamerarius* | Sub-Chamberlain | The *Podkomorzy*; the principal judicial officer of field boundary proceedings. |
| *Camerarius Granicialis* | Boundary Chamberlain | Deputy to the Sub-Chamberlain; often conducting proceedings in his stead. |
| *Ductus* | boundary path | The path walked, demonstrated, and staked by a party during field proceedings. *Lucravit Ductum*: "won the boundary path." |
| *Actoratus Ductûs* | plaintiff's right of path | The procedural right to lead the first boundary path, awarded by the land court. |
| *Lucrum Actoratûs* | gain of the right of path | See *Actoratus Ductûs*. |
| *Sipatio* / *sipavit* / *sipatus* | erection (of a mound) | The formal act of building and staking a boundary mound. *Sipavit Scopulum*: "erected a mound." |
| *Scopulus angularis* / *acialis* | corner mound | Mound at the meeting point of three or four estates. Equivalent to *kopiec narożny*. |
| *Scopulus parietalis* | wall mound | Mound along the line of a boundary wall between two corner mounds. Equivalent to *kopiec ścienny*. |
| *Acialitas* / *Angularitas* | corner point | The meeting point of three (or four) estate boundary walls at a corner mound. Used interchangeably in the source. |
| *Paries* | boundary wall | The line of boundary mounds between two corner points. Not a physical wall; the "wall" is the line of demarcation. |
| *Terminus a Quo* | starting point | The point from which the boundary path begins. |
| *Terminus per Quem* | intermediate point | Waypoints demonstrated along the boundary path. |
| *Terminus ad Quem* | end point | The terminal corner where the boundary path concludes. |
| *Condescensio* | field session | The Sub-Chamberlain's or commissioners' descent to the field; a formal judicial site hearing. |
| *Remissa* | referral | The order sending a case from the land court to field proceedings, or from field proceedings to a higher court. |
| *Borra* / *bór* | pine forest | Dense pine forest used as a landscape feature in boundary proceedings. |
| *Pæna Bannitionis* | penalty of banishment | Formal outlawry penalty enforceable on the estate. |
| *Innotescentiales* | summons letters | The formal letters by which the Sub-Chamberlain or commissioners summoned parties to the field session. |
| *Ministerialis* | court messenger | The sworn court official who served documents and formally proclaimed sessions open. |
| *Relatio* | service return | The formal endorsed return confirming a document had been served. |
| *Referat* / *referatio* | opening (of a mound) | The judicial act of digging open a mound to inspect its metal markers. *In quo referato*: "in which, when opened." |
| *Signa metallica* / *Signa Granicialia* | metal markers | Materials (charcoal, brick, iron slag, glass, millet in a sealed bottle) placed inside a mound as permanent identifying marks. |
| *Dimoscorium ferreum* | iron slag | One of the standard metal markers placed inside mounds. *Dimoscorium vitreum*: glass slag. |
| *Inquisitio* | sworn inquiry | The formal examination of elderly witnesses on oath as to the historical position of boundaries. |
| *Deliberatio* | adjournment for deliberation | The Sub-Chamberlain's formal decision to reserve judgment; limited by statute to one occasion per case. |
| *Dilatio majoris* | principal postponement | One of the five statutory adjournment types; granted for eight weeks. |
| *Dilatio infirmitatis* | postponement of illness | Adjournment on grounds of genuine illness, proven by oath. |
| *Exemptus militaris* | soldier's exemption | Adjournment type available to those in active military service. |
| *Condictamen* | collusion | A prejudicial agreement between the principal party and a collateral heir, to the detriment of a third estate. |
| *Collateralis* | collateral heir | An owner of an adjacent estate whose corner point adjoins the disputed boundary line; formally summoned as a third party. |
| *Commissio* / *Commissarij* | royal commission / royal commissioners | The special commission issued by the King for boundaries between royal and noble estates. |
| *Capitaneus* | Captain | The holder of a royal estate in the capacity of Captain. Distinct from *Tenutarius*. |
| *Tenutarius* | leaseholder | The holder of a royal estate in the capacity of a leaseholder. Distinct from *Capitaneus*. |
| *Prosecutoriales* | prosecutorial letters | Citations issued to obstruct or unwind a commissarial act; declared void. |
| *Complanatio* | amicable settlement | The voluntary agreement between parties resolving boundary differences without a decree. |
| *Arbitri* / *Superarbiter* | arbitrators / chief arbitrator | Persons registered by the parties in the compromissorial procedure; the Sub-Chamberlain acts as chief arbitrator. |
| *Decretum* | decree | The formal decision of the Sub-Chamberlain or commissioners. |
| *Motio* | motion | A formal challenge or appeal motion. *Movit ad Tribunal*: "moved to the Tribunal." |
| *Avulsio* | avulsion | Land forcibly separated from its principal estate and wrongfully incorporated into another. |
| *Præscriptio* | prescription | Limitation period; in boundary cases, does not run against injured heirs. |
| *Rectilinium* | straight line | The legal requirement that boundary walls run in a straight line between corner points. |
| *Rotham* / *Rotha* | oath formula | The specific wording of the oath sworn at the final corner mound. *Rothisante Ministeriali*: "with the court messenger reading the formula." |
| *Instigator* | Instigator | The Crown's official prosecutor; retained as a title throughout. |
| *Judicium Terrestris* / *sąd ziemski* | land court | The principal first-instance court for hereditary matters; originating forum for boundary cases. |
| *Officium Castrense* / *sąd grodzki* | municipal court office | The subordinate court responsible for execution and enrolment of documents. |
| *Tribunal Regni* | Crown Tribunal | The supreme appellate court of the Kingdom, sitting at Piotrków (*Petricoviæ*). |
| *Odsypisko* | *odsypisko* | Polish term for land accreted by the natural action of a river on one bank at the expense of the other. Retained in italics; no established English equivalent. |
| *Upust* | *upust* | Polish term for a water sluice or mill-gate controlling the release of water from a mill pond or mere. Retained in italics; Paciorkowski himself glosses it as vernacular. |
| *Herburt* | Herburt | Jan Herburt's *Statuta i przywileje koronne*, the principal Polish statutory compilation cited throughout. Given with folio references. |
