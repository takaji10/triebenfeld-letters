[…] In this state of things the Nuns acted imprudently, for the town of Zagórów and the estates belonging to it had been encumbered by the previous owner, Prince Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, with debts to the amount of 2,000,000 Polish gulden, and Ignacy Trzciński bought them in 1812 with the debts resting on them, which, as arising still from 1805 and transferred from the Prussian mortgage, took the first place in the mortgage of the new regulation. On the town of Zagórów itself, for which Trzciński gave 78,000 Polish gulden, there were debts of 300,000 Polish gulden. Nothing strange, then, that in such circumstances Trzciński could not hold on to the Zagórów estates, which on 1 September 1827 Karol Bogumił Weygel, a banker of Wrocław, bought at public auction for 1,000,000 Polish gulden, whereby of the 120,000 Polish gulden he had mortgaged on them he saved only 56,879 Polish gulden, and went down with the rest, and after him all the other mortgage creditors.

Thus the sum of 40,000 Polish gulden was lost to the Cistercian Nuns, together with the interest owing from 1806, of which minister Sobolewski informed the Prussian government in a letter dated from Warsaw on 23 October 1828[^2].

[^2]: A. p. p. Kl. Ol. b, 7.
