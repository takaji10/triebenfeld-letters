# Janocki (1779): notes

Jan Daniel Janocki, *Ianociana, sive clarorum atque illustrium Poloniae
auctorum Maecenatumque memoriae miscellae*, tomus II (Varsaviae et Lipsiae:
apud Michaelem Groellium, Sereniss. Pol. Regis Commissarium et Bibliopolam,
sub signo Poetarum, 1779 — the title page's "A. R. S. MDCCLXXIX", *anno
reparatae salutis*). Janocki (1712-1781) was librarian to Prince Józef
Aleksander Jabłonowski and keeper of the early Polish manuscripts in the
Załuski Library; the entry's own mention of Jabłonowski pointing to the
*Museo Polono* fits him. Volume II carries the "Fragmenta" (the lexicon's
entries L to the end); the entry on Lochowski is item LXXVII, printed
pp. 159-161.

## The text

- By paragraph, as the print sets them; the long s read as s; the æ ligatures
  kept where the print shows them (REGULÆ, CRACOVIÆ, POSNANIÆ, sæculi), "ae"
  left separate where it prints so (aetate, Haeredum, praefixa, Praemissa).
- Running head ("FRAGMENTA. 159") cropped off the supplied first page; the
  page number 160 at the head of the supplied second page left out. The
  catchword "Librum" at the foot of p. 159 left out (it is the first word of
  p. 160, which the second page supplies). No signature marks.
- The item number "LXXVII." stands at the head of the supplied first page; it
  is kept, as the entry's own number.
- Names in spaced capitals kept in capitals. The format marks: "12" =
  duodecimo, "8o" = octavo, "4." = quarto, all as printed.
- "Opocznensis"/"Opoczn." read uniformly from the print's
  "Opoczenſis"/"Opoczn." (the castle court of Opoczno); the English follows
  the edition's rendering of the castle court as the municipal court
  (paciorkowski1776's English).
- The 1779 print's orthography kept: "diflimitanda" (as in Paciorkowski's own
  title), "Sandomiriensi", "pernecessarium", "Miedzyborze" (see Open).

## The English

Claude's, paragraph for paragraph. Item titles translated in caps after the
Latin's layout; the quoted Regula title translated as Janocki prints it. One
translator's note, [T.N. 1]: Janocki quotes the first edition of 1749, the
edition's copy is the second of 1776 — "Editio secunda ab ipso authore aucta"
— whose title page calls the book Paciorkowski's own. Janocki's dates for the
two editions are 1749 and 1760. No German translation (an accepted gap, as for
the Polish works).

## Doubtful readings

- **"Miedzyborze"** (the place of Lochowski's subscription to item IV, the
  last line of p. 160): the print is worn; the Google copy's OCR reads
  "Miedzy- ioreae", the Jagiellonian copy's "Miedzyborege". Read as
  "Miedzyborze" (a Polish place name in -borze); the year "MDCXLIIII" (1644)
  and "xi mensis Ianuarii" (11 January) are sure. NEEDS_CONFIRMATION.
- **"1737"** (the heirs of Caesarius Junior's edition of the Regulæ): the
  Google copy and the page image read 1737; the Jagiellonian copy's OCR reads
  1727. Kept 1737.
- **The Acta Eruditorum reference**: "MENSIS MAII, MDCCII. p. 197 - 199"
  (May 1702, pp. 197-199); the Google copy's OCR blurs the page numbers, the
  Jagiellonian copy agrees.

## What it says (and what it conflicts with)

- The heart of it, p. 160: Lochowski's *Processus iuris limitanei, Regni
  Poloniæ* (Cracow 1641) was "bis curavit recudendum" — twice printed again —
  in Paciorkowski's own age by Marcin Paciorkowski, who "oppresso LOCHOWSKII
  auctoris veri nomine, temere atque insolenter opus sibi ipsi tribuit"
  (suppressing the true author's name, rashly and insolently claimed the work
  for himself), under the *Regula processus granicialis* title, with the
  editions of 1749 (Częstochowa) and 1760 (Lublin).
- Against the 1776 title page in the edition: it calls the book Paciorkowski's
  ("Authore Generoso MARTINO PACIORKOWSKI", "Editio secunda ab ipso authore
  aucta"). Janocki says the opposite. The edition keeps Janocki's attribution
  in the citation (the editor's request) and the difference stands recorded
  here.
- The other works it lists for Lochowski (Regulæ iuris 1636-1737, De
  correctura iurium 1641, Comitiorum 1644) name Trąbczyn nowhere; no document
  of the edition is touched.
