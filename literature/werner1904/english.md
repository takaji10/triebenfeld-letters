201. Wreschen. (Diocese of Posen I.)

Soon after the beginning of the eighteenth century Evangelicals seem to have lived in the town of Wreschen (Polish Września or Wrzesno). In 1758 they obtained from the lord of the estate, Matthias Lodzia Poniński, the leave to hold religious meetings and to found a school. The Crown Treasurer Prince Adam Poniński renewed this leave in 1760 and furthered, to raise the town by German settlers, the forming of an evangelical local congregation. By a privilege of 1 May 1778 he gave the Evangelicals an orangery house standing in his castle garden for a church, two buildings connected with it for the parson's and the cantor's dwelling, and granted one Culm Hufe of land as parsonage land. The Consistory in Lissa approved the forming of a separate parish in Wreschen in 1779, and the congregation chose Marggraff as its first pastor. The very extensive parish originally included Schwarzenau as well (see there). When a separate parish arose there, Marggraff, it seems, was moved by disagreements with the lords of the estate to go there, and the congregation was then supplied by assistant pastors. The parochial relations were first fully ordered in the time of South Prussia. A district rich in German Hauland lands, with the towns of Peisern and Zagorowo, was lost to the parish in 1817, when those places came to Russia.

In 1822 the church, which had become very dilapidated, was rebuilt from the ground up, for which the lord of the estate Poniński gave generous help; it had massive enclosing walls and a massive tower in the lower storeys. For its further furnishing the Gustav Adolf Verein granted help of 500 Mark in 1883. A new church nevertheless had to be

[p. 423]

built in the year 1895. It was dedicated on 27 September of the same year by the General Superintendent D. Hesekiel. It cost 67500 Mark. Towards the building sum contributed: the Evangelical Ober-Kirchenrat 3000 Mark, the Gustav-Adolf-Frauenverein in Bremen 1000 Mark, the Provincial Synod from the provincial distress collection of the year 1893 1000 Mark, Gustav-Adolf-Vereine 14000 Mark, besides which a lottery brought 1158.05 Mark.

On 9 November 1879 the congregation kept its centenary jubilee, on which occasion the pastor Schramm published "Nachrichten über die evangelische Kirche in Wreschen" (Notices on the evangelical church in Wreschen).

After the parishes of Miloslaw (see there) and Neßla-Hauland (see there) were branched off, the parish comprises the following localities: Wreschen, Bardo, Biależyce, Biechowo, Bieganowo, Bierzglin, Bierzglinek, Chocicza, Chwalibogowo, Gozdowo, Grzymisławice, Gr. and Kl. Guttowy, Raczanowo, Nehringswalde, Neudorf, Neuvorwerk, Oblaczkowo, Ossowo, Ostrów, Gr. and Kl. Piary, Slomowo, Sokołowo, Soleczno, Zajezierze, Zawodzie, Zerniki, Zieliniec, Gorazdowo, Piastowo, Zafutowo, Tischdorf, Stempocin, Lobenau, Opatówko, Targowa Góra, Racławki, Amilfarowo, Mala Góra, Buchwald, Miecysławowo, Młodziewice, Sokolnik, Galenzewo, Dambrowo, Szamarzewo, with 1821 parishioners. The filial Wilhelmsau belonging to it, set up on 1 June 1892, to which nine other localities belong, counts 336 Evangelicals. In Wilhelmsau stands a Bethaus erected by the Royal Settlement Commission, which was dedicated on 3 June 1892 by the General Superintendent D. Hesekiel.

The pastorate has been held by:

1. Martin August Marggraff, formerly rector in Schmiegel, came here in 1779 and went in 1783 to Schwarzenau (see there).

2. Jonathan Benjamin Krause, born in Liegnitz, was sent here by the Consistory in 1783 as general substitute and died here in 1787.

3. Christian Gottfried Rohrmann, born in Fraustadt, died here as general substitute in 1795.

4. Samuel Gottlieb Schrödt, born in Wollstein, was rector in Schwerzen and was called here as pastor in 1795. He died on 15 November 1818.

5. Johann Gottlob Tiesler, born at Storchnest, became second master and later rector in Rawitsch and was called here in 1819. He died on 18 April 1835.

6. Friedrich Wilhelm Gotthelf Vogel, born at Primkenau in the government district of Liegnitz, entered his office here in 1835. The days of terror which April 1848 brought the

[p. 424]

German inhabitants of Wreschen forced him to flee with his family, and they brought death to two of his children then lying ill. His early death also seems to have been a consequence of the sufferings he had gone through. He died on 24 December 1849, 41 years old.

7. Rudolf Oswald Heise, born at Reinswalde near Sorau, entered the pastorate in 1850 and laid it down of his own will in 1856.

8. Heinrich August Schiffmann, born in Rügenwalde in Pomerania, became parish curator in 1857 and pastor in 1858. He left his office in 1873.

9. Friedrich Wilhelm Gustav Schramm, born in Jastrow in West Prussia, became assistant preacher in Bromberg (see there) in 1865, parish curator in Ciele (see there) in 1867 and was called here as pastor in 1873. He went in 1883 to Friedeberg in the Neumark as deacon at the town church there.

10. Otto Bock, formerly pastor in Piesske (see there), entered the pastorate in 1884.

On 1 May 1894 an assistant preacher was sent here in the person of the hitherto assistant curate Emil Gellert. When he was called in the same year still to Gogolin, Traugott Schent took his place. The latter was called in 1895 to be pastor of Lindenwald, whereupon Martin Sänger was sent here.
