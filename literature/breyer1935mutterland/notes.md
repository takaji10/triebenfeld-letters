# Breyer (1935b): notes

## How it came in (2026-10-19)

From the editor's folder "Books_2": "Albert Breyer - [1935] Ostdeutschland
als Mutterland der deutschen Siedlungen in Mittelpolen.pdf", Jutta
Dennerlein's digital reissue (UpstreamVistula.org, version 1.0, October
2006) of Breyer's lecture, first printed in the *Deutsche Monatshefte in
Polen*, Jahrgang 2 (12), Heft 1/2, July/August 1935. The work is cited by the
reissue's pages (2 to 15).

The whole text is taken in: unlike the other two Breyer reissues it has no
foreword by Dennerlein (its text begins on PDF page 2), so nothing is left
out and no `--skip` is given.

    python pipeline/literature/upstream_pdf.py "<Books_2>/Albert Breyer - [1935] Ostdeutschland als Mutterland der deutschen Siedlungen in Mittelpolen.pdf" breyer1935mutterland

The quotations and the numbered lists of pp. 3 and 4 are set as indented
paragraphs in the PDF and come out as quotations (`>`), as the script reads
every indented paragraph.

The English (`english.md`) is Claude's (2026-10-19). "v. H." (vom Hundert)
is rendered "in a hundred", as Breyer writes it; German terms of the time
stand in brackets (Schulzenlade, Innungsladen, Umvolk, Volkstum, völkisch,
Auslanddeutsche). Place names are left as Breyer spells them, German or
Polish; only "Kalisz country" and "Dobrzyń country" are given in the modern
form, with Breyer's name in brackets the first time.

## What is Dennerlein's, not Breyer's

One addition, in the quotation from Baranowski on p. 4: "[sic - gemeint ist
wahrscheinlich Leslau/Włocławek - JD]". Kept, as the source has it; it is not
Breyer's.

## Slips in the source, kept

"Wartebruch" (p. 7 and p. 8, for Warthebruch), "ein beweis" (p. 5),
"4,Bogpomoż" (p. 5, the space missing), "Goßgrundbesitz" (p. 10, for
Großgrundbesitz), "Brausnsberg" (p. 10), "Grodzierc" (p. 13, for Grodziec),
"Rawisch" (p. 14, for Rawitsch, which the same page also has), "evangl."
(p. 13). The English renders the sense and shows the slip in square
brackets where a reader comparing the two would look for it.

## Points for the reader

- **Zagórów (p. 8): the count does not add up.** Breyer gives 18 place names
  for the marriage and death registers of Zagórów, 1843 to 1847, but the
  figures he lists come to 13; the "western Warthe marshes" stand without a
  figure. The other four parishes on that page add up. Whether a figure was
  lost in print or in the reissue cannot be told without the print of 1935.
- **Kalisz (p. 11): the same.** Of the 298 couples in Sinell's register, the
  four groups Breyer lists come to 278, and his percentages fit 278 better
  than 298.
  His summary for the Kalisz country (p. 9) comes to 97 in a hundred.
- **What the pages give the edition.** Zagórów is the town of the estate;
  Grodziec (p. 8, and its first pastor Bergemann, p. 13) is the place named
  in the title of Breyer's notebook "V. Grodziec Juli 1936" (APP 53/968/0/-/669, document 2);
  Stawiszyn (pp. 8, 13) and Władysławów (pp. 8, 9, 13) are the neighbouring
  Lutheran parishes. The counts are Breyer's, not the church books'; a
  document of the edition is to be held against the books, not against him.
- **The language of 1935.** Breyer wrote for the German minority in Poland.
  He speaks of "stocks" (Stämme) and their "strengths and weaknesses"
  (p. 15), of the "national (völkisch) power of resistance" of the farmers
  (p. 9), and ascribes the Swabians' memory to "their greater intelligence"
  (p. 3). The account on the work's page says so.
