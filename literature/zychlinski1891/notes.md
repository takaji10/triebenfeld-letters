# Żychliński (1891): notes

## How it came in (2026-10-19)

From the editor's folder "Books_2": "Miączyński_Złota księga szlachty polskiej.
R. 13.pdf", four photographed openings, pp. 218-225 of the volume, under the
running head "MIĄCZYŃSCY". The PDF's text layer is a machine reading too
garbled to use; the pages were rendered at 240 dpi with PyMuPDF, cut into
strips, and read in session, and `original.md` was written by hand.

The editor masked the top of p. 219 and the whole of p. 218 in the PDF. The
text begins where the copy is open: "Hr. Stanisław Adam (XI pok.)" on p. 219.
Two things above that point are left out on purpose:
- the last line of the masked paragraph, on p. 219: "...owskiéj córki:
  Dorota i Honorata, zgasłe w młodosci, i syn Aleksander, bezżennie" (the
  daughters Dorota and Honorata, who died young, and a son Aleksander,
  unmarried, of some earlier Miączyński), which belongs to the masked text;
- the footnote at the foot of p. 219, whose mark stands in the masked text:
  "Jéj portret znajduje się w posiadaniu hr. Władysława Miączyńskiego w
  Trąbczynie. Przyp. Autora." (Her portrait is in the possession of Count
  Władysław Miączyński at Trąbczyn. Author's note.) Whose portrait cannot be
  told from what is visible.

The page number 219 is not visible on the masked page; it follows from 218
and 220.

The year, 1891, is reckoned from the series (rocznik I appeared in 1879, one
volume a year), not read off a title page; the editor may correct it. The
text agrees: it reports Zbigniew Morawski's death in June 1889, while a
footnote on p. 225 says the author's news of Witold's sons goes only to 1885.

## How the text is given

- **As printed.** The old spelling is kept ("jéj", "Kaliskiém", "ztąd",
  "męztwo", "rólniczą", "stósunku", "dla tego"), and so are the punctuation
  and the book's quotation marks („ ... “). Where the accent on an e was not
  certain at the resolution of the photographs, the book's own habit was
  followed (é in "-éj" and "-ém").
- **Spaced type as bold, italics as italics.** The book prints names in
  letter-spaced type ("A l e k s a n d e r") and titles and some quoted
  passages in italic. Spaced type is given in **bold**, italic in *italics*,
  in the original and the English, so the two can be told apart. The same is
  done in Żychliński (1885).
- **Words broken at the line end** are joined. A word broken across two
  pages ("poprze-" / "dnio", pp. 220-221; "Mo-" / "rawskie", pp. 223-224) is
  given whole on the first page.
- **Footnotes** stand at the foot of their page, as `[^1]`; each page numbers
  its notes from 1, as the book does. The note on p. 221 is set before the
  page mark of p. 222, so it falls between "herbu" and "Koss", the end of a
  sentence that runs over the page.
- **The numbered sons** ("1. Hr. Aleksander" to "4. Hr. Witold") are written
  "1\." in the files, so that the site does not turn them into a numbered
  list and renumber them; the number is the book's.
- **The order of the day of 27 November 1809 and the Prince's letter of 1812**
  are set line by line, as the book sets them.

## Slips in the print, kept

- "Stérauy" (p. 220): for "Stérany", worn out.
- "Poniatowki" (p. 220), in the signature of the letter of 1812: for
  Poniatowski.
- "dżienny" (p. 220), "Rozkaz dżienny": for "dzienny". The mark over the z may
  be a speck on the page; it is given as it looks.
- "Prasimskich" (p. 221): for Prusimskich.
- "nagrobkinm" (p. 221, footnote): for "nagrobkiem", tombstone; "i tamże
  dnia 5 września tamże" has "tamże" twice.
- "ktory" (p. 221): without the accent.
- "Gesselschaft für Erdkunde" (p. 222): for Gesellschaft.
- "obrał w posiadanie" (p. 222): very likely for "objął", took possession; the
  English gives the sense.
- "drewniane" (p. 223): for "drewnianego", the little wooden church.
- "watpić" (p. 223): for "wątpić", to doubt.
- "wojsk wojsk" (p. 223): the word printed twice.
- "dzisdzinie" (p. 224): for "dziedzinie", field.
- "Slepowron" (p. 224) beside "Ślepowron" in the same sentence.
- "art 56" (p. 224, footnote): without the full stop.

The English shows the slip in square brackets where a reader comparing the
two would look for it.

## The English

Claude's (2026-10-19). "pok." (generation) and the titles are handled as in
Żychliński (1885). "Hr." is "Count" or "Countess"; "ś. p." (świętej pamięci)
is "the late"; "m. m." (morgów magdeburskich) "Magdeburg morgen"; "S. I. R."
and "Ś. Państwa Rzymskiego" the Holy Roman Empire; "W. Ks. Poznańskie" the
Grand Duchy of Posen; "Stare Ziemstwo" the Old Landschaft (the credit society
of the Posen estates); "Zb. Pr." the Code of Laws (Svod zakonov). The order
of the day and the Prince's letter are translated in full, keeping the
book's italics. Place names are in today's Polish form.

## Points for the reader, held against the edition

1. **Michalina's second marriage: August 1809.** "zaślubił ją w sierpniu
   1809 roku" (p. 221), with the marriage settlement and its witnesses. This
   agrees with the PSB (Pachoński 1982, "from 1809") and not with the
   edition's `prusimska` and `miaczynski`, which say "about 1814". It also
   fits the order of the day of 27 November 1809, which calls Stanisław a
   captain, and Triebenfeld's fear of his influence in 1815. Asked in
   `docs/NEEDS_CONFIRMATION.md`; the bios are not changed without the
   editor's word.
2. **Maryanna Dąmbska, Michalina's mother, died at Padua on 2 September
   1797,** aged 38, and was buried in the church of St Anthony (p. 221,
   footnote). The PSB has her go with the Starost to Venice at the end of
   1794 and gives no year of death; the edition's documents have her dead by
   1800 (Michalina's suit for her "late mother's" Brzyce estates). **But the
   year is very likely wrong:** the dispatch of the Prussian resident at
   Venice, Count Cattaneo, of 14 December 1796 (I. HA GR, Rep. 7 C, Nr. 1414,
   document 2) has Prusimski come to him "affecté presqu'à la stupidité pour
   la mort de son epouse", dulled almost to stupor by his wife's death. If
   both speak of the same death, she died before December 1796 (2 September
   1796?). Found 2026-10-19 while the links were checked; the link
   `zychlinski1891-maryanna` says so on the site, and the point is asked in
   `docs/NEEDS_CONFIRMATION.md`.
3. **Who held Trąbczyn, 1809-1879.** By Żychliński, Michalina made all her
   estates over to Stanisław in the marriage settlement of 1809; before his
   death in 1845 Stanisław gave the Trąbczyn estates and Szetlewek "in the form
   of a sale" to his third son Mieczysław; and Włodzimierz bought Trąbczyn
   from Mieczysław after he came back from exile (pp. 221, 222). The edition
   (`reference/eras.yml`, era `wlodzimierz`; the bios of `prusimska` and
   `miaczynski-wlodzimierz`) has Michalina hold the estate until her death in
   1851 and Włodzimierz after her. The two may both be partly right (Michalina
   living at Trąbczyn as a widow, Mieczysław the owner in law), but they do
   not say the same thing. Asked in `docs/NEEDS_CONFIRMATION.md`; nothing is
   changed.
4. **Włodzimierz died on 18 December 1879** (p. 222); the editor's ruling of
   2026-10-15 gives 20 December 1879 (`reference/people_bios.yml`). One of
   the two is a slip, or 20 December is the funeral or the notice. Asked in
   `docs/NEEDS_CONFIRMATION.md`; the editor's date stands.
5. **Władysław, heir of Trąbczyn "by his uncle Włodzimierz"** (p. 222), and
   Włodzimierz's guardianship of him (p. 223), agree with the editor's ruling
   of 2026-10-15 and with Żychliński (1885) ("her grandson").
6. **Michalina died on 19 January 1851 at Trąbczyn** and was buried beside
   her husband (p. 221): the same date as the editor's.
7. **Stanisław died in Berlin on 22 April 1845** and was buried in the
   family's burial chapel at Trąbczyn; Aleksander (1869), Marya née Słubicka
   (1865) and Włodzimierz (1879) were buried there too. The funeral notice
   places Włodzimierz's grave in a chapel on the cemetery, a verst from the
   wooden church, which his gift was to rebuild in brick.
