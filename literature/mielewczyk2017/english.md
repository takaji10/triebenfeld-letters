# Changes in rural space in the second half of the 19th and early 20th centuries as exemplified by selected maps from the collections of the National Museum of Agriculture and Agro-Food Industry in Szreniawa

**Wojciech Mielewczyk**

The National Museum of Agriculture and Agricultural-Food Industry, Szreniawa

E-mail: w.mielewczyk@muzeum-szreniawa.pl

**Summary of the contents:** From the mid-19th century until the mid-20th century fundamental socio-economic changes took place in the villages of the Polish lands. They were bound up above all with the processes of the enfranchisement of the peasants, but also with the drainage works carried out, with the consolidation of agricultural lands, and with the clearing of forests for new crops and the founding of new settlements. These actions caused quick and frequent changes in the rural space.

The changes taking place in the countryside found their reflection and their documentation on the large-scale maps drawn at the time, of which many are held in the National Museum of Agriculture in Szreniawa. The maps show the changes in the use of the land and in the buildings, the new colonies being created, and the courses of the drainage ditches. These changes were presented graphically, also with the use of a suitable palette of colours. Another way of marking the changes was to place suitable annotations in chosen parts of the map. In many cases the changes are entered by hand in pencil. The maps presented are a precious source of the historical changes in the study of the rural space in the 19th and 20th centuries.

**Key words:** large-scale maps, village, rural space, land reform, historical source

# 1. The changes in the countryside in the 19th century

# 1.1. The Prussian partition

With regard to the rural areas, the 19th century and the first half of the 20th were rich in events resulting in changes in the space of the village in the Polish lands. They were bound up above all with the agrarian reforms introduced by the authorities of the partitioning states. The greatest transformations took place in the villages of the Prussian partition.

As a result of the enfranchisement and regulation reforms carried out in the years 1823–1865 land was given in all to 88,918 peasant holdings

[p. 42]

covering a total area of 371,829.3 ha. The separation carried out, that is the separating of the peasants' lands from the manorial ones, covered an area of about 2.5 million hectares[^1]. As its result 793 villages were moved to new places. At the same time an action of abolishing the communities was carried out, among which were counted the pasture and forest servitudes. They covered 64,386 holdings[^2]. As a result of the works connected with the separation of lands and the enfranchisement of the peasants the old layouts of the villages vanished, and manorial settlements and loose row colonies appeared. A complete rebuilding touched a quarter of all the villages of Wielkopolska[^3].

The second period of changes in the countryside of the Prussian partition fell at the end of the 19th and the beginning of the 20th century. In Wielkopolska and Pomerania there then developed on a broad scale the parcellation-and-settlement action begun by the anti-Polish activity of the Prussian Settlement Commission (Ansiedlung Kommission), set up in the year 1886. The Commission acquired in all over 460 thousand ha of land, on which it created 341 new villages with close to 22 thousand holdings, handed over to colonists from the depths of Germany[^4]. Countering the Prussian colonisation policy, Polish institutions and parcellation companies created over 11 thousand new holdings[^5]. The Polish–Prussian struggle for the land, carried on until the First World War, contributed to great changes in the rural landscape, enriching it above all with many new buildings and access roads to the holdings created.

The changes taking place in the countryside found their reflection and documentation on the large-scale maps drawn at the time. A representative collection of them is held in the Scientific Archive of the National Museum of Agriculture and Agricultural-Food Industry in Szreniawa[^6]. An example of a Prussian map from this collection, with the new divisions of the existing parcels entered on it, is the cadastral map of the village of Przecław (today the gmina of Szamotuły, the powiat of Szamotuły, the voivodeship of Wielkopolska) of the year 1894 (fig. 1).

In the second half of the 19th century drainage works intensified. To their intensification contributed the law on water companies passed in 1879, allowing the creation of melioration companies, whose activity the state credited. # 1.2. The Russian partition

In the Russian partition the enfranchisement of the peasants came later and took a different course than in the Prussian one. The enfranchisement ukase of the tsarist authorities issued on 2 March 1864, "On the ordering of the peasants", gave the peasants as their property the land they used. This grant was not joined with the merging of the arable plots belonging to the individual holdings, the abolishing of the servitudes and the separation of the peasants' lands from the manorial ones. An example of a map with the lands of the enfranchised peasants marked on it is the map of the estates of Trąbczyn (today the gmina of Zagórów, the powiat of Słupca, the voivodeship of Wielkopolska) (fig. 4)[^8].

*Fig. 4. A fragment of the map of the estates of Trąbczyn of 1861 at the scale of 1:15 000 with the field of the enfranchised peasants marked. Reduced. Shelfmark AN 489/5*

In the face of the great fragmentation of the fields belonging to the individual peasant holdings, in the second half of the 19th century in the territory of the Kingdom of Poland there began to develop a consolidation from below, carried out by those interested themselves. By the outbreak of the First World War it covered an area of about 2.5 million ha.

Only in 1911 did a governmental consolidation begin, carried out by the offices for peasant affairs[^9]. Despite the intensification of this action the majority of the peasant holdings was still strongly fragmented. Yet in the places where the lands were merged the characteristic chessboard of fields vanished.

In parallel, still in the age of the partitions, there was at work the process of the parcellation of the lands of the great landed property, carried out from below by the interested parties themselves. It was caused mainly by economic factors. The parcellation of lands both among landless peasants and among those wishing to enlarge their holdings brought significant changes in the space of the village: on the parcelled areas balks appeared, roads were led to the new parcels, often planted with trees, dwelling houses and farm buildings were put up on the parcels. At times orchards were laid out by the houses. An example of a map on which the parcelled area is marked is the map of the estates of Zabłocie (today the gmina of Zagórów, the powiat of Słupca, the voivodeship of Wielkopolska) of the year 1889 (fig. 5). There an annotation was entered of the parcellation of the marked field with the help of the Peasants' Bank.

# 2. The changes in the countryside in the interwar period

After the regaining of independence, among the most important matters requiring regulation in the countryside were: the carrying-out of the land reform, that is the parcellation of the great landed property and the granting of land to landless and smallholding peasants, and also the liquidation of the chessboard of lands and the carrying-out of melioration.

In the years of the Second Republic 2,654 thousand ha of land were parcelled in all, that is about 7% of all the agricultural lands in Poland. Thus the surface of the lands held in the possession of the great landed property diminished by more or less a third; the majority of these areas passed into peasant hands[^11].

[^11]: W. Roszkowski, Rolnictwo i leśnictwo [Agriculture and forestry], in: Problemy gospodarcze Drugiej Rzeczypospolitej [The economic problems of the Second Republic], collective work, Warszawa 1989, p. 143.

[p. 52]

By the outbreak of the war 153.6 thousand new agricultural holdings were created, and 503.0 thousand were enlarged[^12]. The new peasant homesteads, put up for the most part in dispersed building, changed significantly the space of many Polish villages. The melioration carried out at the same time changed 548.7 thousand ha of waterlogged meadows into arable fields; the total length of the melioration ditches and of the regulated rivers came to 19.0 thousand km[^13].

At the moment of the rebirth of the Polish state the general area requiring consolidation was estimated at about 10–12 million ha, of which only 300 thousand ha fell to the lands of the former Prussian partition (above all the southern powiats of the Poznań voivodeship)[^14]. The greatest problem was the liquidation of the chessboard of the peasants' fields in the territory of central, eastern and southern Poland. The approximate area of lands requiring merging amounted in 1921 to about 57% of the whole surface of small property. The number of settlements holding lands in chessboard was about 1.5 million, of which to the western voivodeships fell a mere 46 thousand holdings[^15]. In the central voivodeships there were about 570 thousand holdings requiring consolidation, in the southern about 550 thousand, and in the eastern — 347 thousand.[^16]

The act on the consolidation of lands was passed in the year 1923[^17]. Under it the plots distributed in chessboard were subject to merging, but the holdings holding lands excessively narrowed were also to be regulated. The act introduced the right of excluding from consolidation the lands under buildings; for the moving of the buildings every owner had to give consent. On the other hand in the case of the consolidation of the lands of dwarf holdings the parcellation of the estate neighbouring on the village had to be carried out, to the end of making the holdings full-sized.

Of the about 12 million ha of lands requiring consolidation in the interwar period 5,423 thousand ha were merged, belonging to over 920 thousand holdings, chiefly in the central voivodeships[^18]. The consolidation of the peasants' plots carried out was bound not only with the liquidation of the mid-field balks, but also with the change of the course of the access roads to the fields, and not seldom also with the removals of the peasants' holdings. The economic effects of the consolidation works were significant; as a result of the liquidation of the balks there came an increase of the area under cultivation of 3–9%.

# 3. Conclusion

From the mid-19th century until the mid-20th century fundamental socio-economic changes took place in the villages of the Polish lands. They were bound up above all with the processes of the enfranchisement of the peasants, but also with the drainage works carried out, with the consolidation of agricultural lands, and with the clearing of forests for new crops and the founding of new settlements. These actions caused quick and frequent changes in the rural space.

A map, as a picture of the surface of the Earth, presents chosen objects and phenomena in a defined geographical space, and above all in a defined time. Yet owing to the unceasing changes the maps, above all the large-scale ones, presenting most exactly the state of the natural and the cultural environment, quickly become out of date. This caused, on the one hand, the need to draw new maps often, but on the other hand, to save time, annotations or corrections were entered on maps already existing. This way of updating the maps of villages was already in use in the second half of the 19th century, and examples of it can be found on the maps discussed here. Yet in the case of maps drawn by hand the updates entered cannot always be told apart from the original record: in the drawing of maps various styles of hand were used, and in the added notes no date was placed. In this situation it is hard to fix the time of the changes that took place in the rural space. To obtain reliable information in this matter it would be necessary to reach for other sources: the acts of the granting of lands, the annotations in the land registers and the acts belonging to them, and the cadastral documents. Nevertheless these maps remain a rich historical source for the history of the individual villages, often, for the lack of other documents, the only one.

# Changes the villages space in the second half of the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries on selected maps from the National Museum of Agriculture and Agricultural-Food Industry in Szreniawa

**Wojciech Mielewczyk**

The National Museum of Agriculture and Agricultural-Food Industry, Szreniawa

E-mail: w.mielewczyk@muzeum-szreniawa.pl

**Summary:** From the mid-nineteenth century up to half of the twentieth century in polish villages became fundamental socio-economic changes. They were connected mainly with the process of enfranchisement of peasants, parceling out of large estates, as well as with the making melioration of agricultural land, the regrouping of fields and grubbing of forests for new fields, installing of new farms and colonies. As a result of these actions followed quick and significant changes in the rural space.

These processes are shown on great-scale maps of villages, many of which are located in the National Museum of Agriculture in Szreniawa. The maps presents the changes in the land and building the village, creating new colonies of settlement, as well as course of drainage ditches. These changes were presented graphically, also by using the appropriate color palette. Another way of marking changes was to place the appropriate annotations written on certain parts of the map . In many cases, changes are handwritten pencil drawing.

The presented maps make up to valuable historical source to research changes in the rural space in nineteenth and twentieth century's.

**Keywords:** large-scale maps, village, village space, land reform, historical source
