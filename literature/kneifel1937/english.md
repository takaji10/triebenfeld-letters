## I. The Kalisch Diocese.

A comprehensive survey of its historical course.

The Reformation movement in Germany found a strong echo also in Poland, favored by particular preconditions. Within a few decades the land stood in religious ferment: in the struggle of the leading circles for or against the new doctrine of faith. The conditions of that time, with their abundance of creative ideas and manifold influences, brought it about that Protestantism in Poland first found its characteristic form in three separate evangelical churches. In Greater Poland, where the center of gravity of the Lutheran church lay, divine service according to the Augsburg Confession was held in German at 110 places and in Polish at 32. Reformed services were held here, according to more recent research, at 80 places. "The figure cited for Greater Poland includes within itself also the Brethren's congregations, which had entered into a union with the Calvinists at the synod of Koźminek in 1555"[^p7-1] In the Kalisch land, in the Warthe lowland and in Kujawien — on the territory of the present Kalisch Evangelical-Augsburg diocese — there existed in the 16th century about 25 Reformed and Brethren's congregations, whose number, however, diminished more and more in the course of time. From the beginning of the 17th century the Reformed in Kujawien lost one church after another to the Catholics, last of all in 1615 the church at Radziejów with its school and seminary. In consequence the Reformed senior Mikołajewski resolved to join the Brethren in order to strengthen the seven Reformed congregations still preserved. In December 1627 the final union was accomplished at the synod of Ostroróg, whereby the Reformed confession in Greater Poland once more lost influence[^p7-2]

Built on a narrow congregational base, worn down by internal struggles, harassed by the Roman Catholic church, Protestantism in Poland moved toward its decline. In the Catholic Counter-Reformation there grew up against it an opponent whose attacks it proved unable to withstand in the long run[^p8-3] Until the granting of freedom of religion in our land (1768) there remained in Greater Poland only seven Reformed congregations, very much weakened in their existence, one of them in the Warthe lowland and another in the Kalisch land — at Żychlin (near Konin) and Wola Tłumakowska[^p8-4]

A vigorous strengthening and promotion of Protestantism in Poland came only in modern times through the German colonization. In the Kalisch land it began as early as about 1750. Numerous larger and smaller settlements arose here, as also in the Warthe lowland and in Kujawien[^p8-5] As an urban place of immigration Władysławów (Rosterschütz) is to be named for 1727. In the Prussian period seven large Swabian villages were founded: Neu-Renneberg (Wygorzele, formerly the parish of Dąbie, now Łęczyca), Schwedelbach, Rosental, Wilhelmstal, Friedrichstal, Lilienfeld, Czarnocice. In South Prussia, in the towns of Kalisch, Dąbie, Kowal, Przedecz, Chodecz and others, German officials, craftsmen, and clothmakers settled. In the period from 1815 to 1863 rural and urban settlement activity made further progress. In the Kalisch land the founding of Wojciechowo took place in 1845, after that those of Michalinów, Łagiewniki, Sołomina. In Kujawien the colonies were laid out: 1840 Morzyczyn, 1842 Władysławów, 1850 Marcinkowo, 1855 Szarłatowo, 1859 Mieczysławowo, 1866 Besiekiery. Of far greater economic and church significance was the settlement of the towns[^p8-6]

The ecclesiastical gathering of the evangelical population settled in the villages and towns often met with considerable difficulties. At first it was chiefly economic reasons that stood in its way. The processes of settlement and of the putting down of roots by the colonists extended for the most part over one or two decades. How difficult these beginnings were the archives bear witness again and again. Thus Pastor Grimm-Stawiszyn writes, for example, of his congregation members that they "dwell in huts of turf." These huts of earth were precisely emergency forms of settler life, which played itself out in merciless narrowness and hardness[^p9-7] Only after the German peasants, in laborious years-long work of clearing, had wrested from the often meager soil bread for themselves and their families could they set about building up a cultural and religious life of their own. Already at the laying out of a colony it was the custom to set aside a plot of land for school and house of prayer. After the distressful time of putting down roots had been overcome, simple school and prayer houses, mostly thatched with straw, were erected, and a teacher and cantor was called (often a landless farmer or a craftsman). "In most places, especially in the voivodeship of Kalisch, the evangelical schools came into being in this way: when one, two, or three evangelical families settled in a place remote from all evangelical churches, they chose out from among themselves someone who would instruct their children and hold edification hours for them on Sundays . . ."[^p9-7a] Through the union of the school with the house of prayer there arose everywhere in central Poland, and later also in Volhynia and other territories, that cantorate system so characteristic of the Evangelical-Augsburg church in Poland, which in all probability represents an intermediate form between the Swabian "Stunde" and the "pre-state" type of school and is an original creation of our colonist people.

The German settlements were at first typical islands of language and religion in the midst of an environment of another kind. As such they sought, in order to maintain their faith and hand it on to coming generations, to draw close to one another, and thereby marked themselves off religiously from their surroundings. This endeavor, supported by Roman Catholic lords of the manor out of economic considerations, found clear expression in the founding of congregations. In the period 1776–1799 the parishes came into being: Władysławów, Stawiszyn, Kalisch, Grodziec and Babiak; in the 19th century: the parish congregations Kowal-Chodecz, Dąbie, Prażuchy, Łask, Sobiesęki, Wieluń, Przedecz, Konin, Zduńska Wola, Turek, Sompolno, Zagórów, as well as the filials Maślaki, Józefów and Lissewo-Pyzdry; in the 20th century: the congregations Koło, Izbica, the filial Ludwikowo, Kozy and Sieradz.

Superintendent von Modl was, although not entirely free of rationalistic influences, an active and energetic administrator of the diocese entrusted to him[^p13-14] The visitations of the congregations lay very near his heart. Thus he visited, for example, in the year 1829 nine parishes, in which he always took care to promote the religious-ecclesiastical life in them and to convey appropriate impulses. As he himself attested, at the visitations he wished to ascertain how the pastors "sought to make themselves worthy" as preachers, liturgists, catechists, shepherds of souls "in the vineyard of the Lord," whether the "cura animarum" was their chief task. ## 3. Kalisch.

Kalisch is the oldest town in Poland concerning which we possess historical records from the earliest time. Thus already in the 1st century A.D. the Roman historian Caius Plinius the Younger mentions in his "Historia Mundi" the settlement "Calisia". Likewise the Greek Claudius Ptolomaios of Alexandria names it in the 2nd century A.D. The old trade route, the so-called Bernsteinstrasse, led from the south to the Baltic Sea by way of Kalisch.

Among the Polish chroniclers the first report concerning Kalisch comes in the year 1108. Since then it looks back on a richly eventful past. It was the scene of armed conflicts, capital of the Kalisch territorial principality, an important trading place, a focal point of religious and cultural endeavors.

The environs of Kalisch show German settlers already in early times. Around 1280–1300 localities are founded here "under German law" (Breitenfeld, Rajsko); in 1597 the village of Olędrowo. Before 1260 Kalisch was furnished with German law. In 1264 Rithwinus was Schulze and locator here. In Kalisch itself Reformed, Lutherans, Bohemian Brethren and Socinians were represented, of whom apparently only the first had a congregational organization and a preacher here (Paul Paliura). At that time there distinguished himself in Kalisch as an excellent educator (from 1588 at Lubartów) and speaker, as a man of rich knowledge and author of numerous writings (among others "Schola Levartoriana Restituta", printed 1593), the Socinian Albertus Calisius, a decided opponent of the Jesuits. These set themselves the goal of the complete extermination of Protestantism in the Kalisch land.

Introduced at Kalisch in 1581 by the Gnesen archbishop Stanislaus Karnkowski, they went eagerly to work. Toward the end of the 16th century the Jesuits took over the direction of the so-called "Academy Colony" and transformed it into a five-class school; about 1591 they founded an ecclesiastical seminary, in 1595 a "Collegium Nobilium" for the noble youth of the Kalisch land; about 1603 they acquired the printing establishment of Johann Wolrab and soon afterward issued the "Roman Catechism" in the Polish language[^p56-3] The religious center of their counter-reformatory activity was the college built for them by Karnkowski together with the Jesuit church (with three naves and a beautiful baroque altar). This house of God, consecrated on September 8, 1596, was erected according to the plan of the Milanese Jesuit architect Gian Maria Bernardone (†1605)[^p56-4] "In the year of the completion of the church building Sigismund III came in person to Kalisch and inspected in every detail the new foundation of the shepherd, thereby giving expression to his favor toward the archbishop, whom he loved for his sense of justice and honored for his merits"[^p56-5]

The Protestants in Kalisch were in the long run unable to withstand the onslaught of the Jesuits. After Sigismund Grudziński (1572–1653), the mighty voivode of Kalisch, had become Catholic shortly before his death, the evangelical-Reformed congregation in Kalisch came to an end.

The evangelical cemetery in Kalisch, dating from the 17th (or indeed already the 16th) century, lies on an eminence which the common people formerly called the "Lutheran hill." On September 26, 1790 this cemetery was handed back to the Kalisch Evangelicals and fenced in by them in the following year. This proves that Evangelicals were likewise settled here at that time[^p57-6] They had their religious acts performed for the most part by the pastors of the Stawiszyn congregation.

Through the second partition of Poland (1793) Kalisch fell to Prussia. Into the newly acquired town came German-evangelical military men, officials, craftsmen and merchants. In the environs of Kalisch, in the "Dember Holland," evangelical farmers settled, whose "Schulhalter" (school-holders) were in 1796 Christian David Paetzolt, in 1805 Johann Gottlieb Schultz. In the year 1795 the number of those who had immigrated into Kalisch, together with the Evangelicals already settled here before, was so considerable that they petitioned the town administration to call a preacher for them. They also submitted to the government and to the consistory in Posen the request to establish in Kalisch "a church system of its own and to appoint a preacher, who was at the same time to fill the office of school-rector." Their efforts had complete success. On September 3, 1795 the "concession for the establishment of an evangelical church system of its own at Kalisch for the evangelical congregation there" was granted. Still in the course of that year Pastor Karl Michael Christian Sienell assumed the pastorate of the newly organized congregation. In a rented hall on the Old Ring, in the so-called cobblers' stalls ("jatki szewskie"), he held divine service for two years. Thanks to the intervention of the Prussian government Pope Pius VI, on the basis of the brief of August 27, 1797, made over to the Kalisch Evangelicals the Jesuit church standing empty since 1773 "cum pleno jure patronatus". After the creation of the province of "South Prussia" Kalisch became in 1798 the seat of the "South Prussian evangelical consistory," of which Consistorial Councilor Karl Friedrich Herzberg became a member and at the same time preacher at the Kalisch evangelical church. He died on November 10, 1812 "of consumption and lung disease at the age of 43 years . . ."[^p58-9] Pastor Sienell served the parish until his death, which took place on October 12, 1820[^p58-10] He reached an age of 45 years (died "of lung disease").

In the year 1824 the bishop of Kalisch-Kujawien, Koźmian, came before the public with the proposal of handing over to the evangelical congregation at Kalisch the Franciscan church, but taking back into possession the former Jesuit church and opening in it an ecclesiastical seminary. This plan, which had already begun to take effect, met with so decided a resistance on the part of the Catholic population of Kalisch and its environs that Bishop Koźmian had to let it fall. To the petition of the town dwellers Emperor Alexander I replied, among other things: ". . . I praise the zeal of the inhabitants of Kalisch that they wished to protect the church with their prayers . . . I praise the unconcern of the Protestants that they were not hasty about the taking away (of the church) . . . We therefore determine that the church shall remain with the same monastic connection."

The founding of an evangelical school at Kalisch received the greatest attention from the church council. In the years 1798 to 1810 there existed here an evangelical school whose rector was Pastor Karl Gottlieb Bartsch; then it came to an end. For years the church council strove in vain for the reopening of the school. It turned, among others, also to State Councilor Radoszewski, the president of the Kalisch voivodeship commission. The latter showed himself well disposed, but was of the opinion that the evangelical school to be opened must have suitable premises. Therefore Radoszewski commissioned the voivodeship master builder F. Reinstein to work out a building plan together with a cost estimate for the schoolhouse to be erected. This was done. Yet the church council drew back from this undertaking and acquired instead, in the year 1826, a spacious house on the Piekarska-Str. with rooms for the school as well as apartments for the pastor, the vicar, the teacher, the sexton and the bell-ringer. This house was remodeled and enlarged in 1844. On February 24, 1834 the Kalisch superintendency reported to the consistory that an evangelical elementary school had been founded at Kalisch. If in 1846 two teachers (Kaselitz and Schroeter) were already at work in it, after a decade there were already four teachers (Schroeter, Rasch, Ecke and Foerster) active here. In the year 1857 the teacher and cantor Schroeter suggested the formation of an evangelical singing choir at Kalisch. Soon a number of song-loving town burghers joined the choir (among others Robert Franken, Johann Blüml, Einhorn, R. Wüstehube, Bertelmann sen., Thiele, Bothner).

On November 20, 1858 the Kalisch Evangelicals Dr. Adolf Sturm, Stanislaus Kossecki, Johann Niedomański, the notary Emiljan Ordon and K. Dabenbuth turned to the consistory with the request

For four decades Pastor Modi (†April 22, 1860) was shepherd of souls of the Kalisch parish, for which, as also for the development of the Kalisch diocese, of which he had been superintendent, he rendered great service.

After Pastor Modl's death the neighboring pastors Orive-Grodziec, Jarnecki-Prażuchy, Teichmann-Turek and Fiedler-Sobiesęki administered the vacant congregation, whose administration was later entrusted to the local vicar Ernst Wilhelm Bursche. From 11 candidates who applied for the pastorate the church council chose only 3, namely the pastors Stockmann-Radom, Tydelski-Włocławek and Benni-Tomaschow. On October 8, 1860 the congregation elected Franz Johann Wladilaus Stockmann as its pastor. Until 1869 he exercised his office here; from 1861 he was superintendent of the Kalisch diocese, from 1865 member of the Warsaw evangelical-Augsburg consistory. On January 15, 1869 Pastor Stockmann died a tragic death in Warsaw and was buried in the Lutheran cemetery there[^p60-11]

After a short vacancy the parish elected on March 14, 1870 as its shepherd of souls Pastor Hermann Immanuel Friedrich Benni of Tomaschow, who however already in the next year was called to the University of Warsaw as lecturer in the English language. Thereupon Pastor Johann Adam Haberkant of Dąbie entered the service of the congregation in 1872. In the years 1877 and 1882 he renovated the church, for which in 1887 the Kalisch artist Bertelmann painted the beautiful altar piece "The Ascension of Christ." Since there were several secondary schools in the town, Pastor Haberkant diligently concerned himself with the evangelical instruction in religion in these schools, until then treated rather stepmotherly.

On July 16, 1904 Pastor Haberkant celebrated his 50-year jubilee of office, in which the pastors Krempin-Prażuchy, Serini-Stawiszyn, Wojak-Brzeziny, Buse-Grodziec, Winkler-Wieluń and Semadeni-Żychlin (Reformed) took part. After almost 33 years of activity in the Kalisch congregation Pastor Haberkant departed this life on September 12, 1905 and was buried in the Warsaw evangelical-Lutheran cemetery.

In the World War 1914/18 Kalisch, as then a border town, suffered severely. Already in the first days of August 1914 the German troops (the 155th regiment, Major Preusker) bombarded the town, which was in part sorely devastated. As the reason there was given the shooting of German soldiers by civilians. The partial destruction of the town also involved many evangelical congregation members in suffering. After the close of the war Kalisch slowly recovered, rising like a phoenix from the ruins to new bloom. Through the shifting of the border, however, it lost its former interstate economic significance.

## 4. Grodziec.

In the year 1772 the German-evangelical villages of Borowiec, Konary, Grunde and Wielołęka were founded on the estates of Grodziec. The owner of Biskupice settled colonists in Orlina (Adlerholland) in 1784; in the same year Peter von Sokoła-Gałczyński, the lord of the manor of Gadow, settled German peasants in Gadow Holland. In 1787 F. Stadnicki, the starost of Adelnau, laid out the Holländerei Białobłoty. In the records the villages are designated "gminy holendrów" [gminas of the Holländer]. The pastors of Władysławów visited all these localities1). On July 12, 13 and 14, 1796 the representatives of the villages named concluded with Count Stadnicki, in the presence of the judicial commission of the county of Pyzdry (Peisern) assembled on the manor farm of Grodziec, a contract concerning the establishment of the evangelical parish congregation of Grodziec2). According to the agreement reached the count pledged himself to present all building sites on which the church, the parsonage, the cantor's house, the farm buildings and the like were to be erected; he declared himself likewise ready to assign to the Evangelicals a place for a cemetery ("by the road to the glassworks"). Further, he held out the prospect of his energetic help in the erection of ecclesiastical buildings, in that he promised to furnish all requisite building material — timber, brick, lime, glass — "so that the congregation, promoted by this benefaction, might the sooner complete the building of the house of God and of all churchly edifices." Stadnicki gave expression to his good will also in this, that he presented for the pastor 2 Morgen of meadow and 1 Morgen of garden land, likewise for the cantor 1 Morgen of garden land, as well as an annual allowance

For himself and his descendants he reserved the right of patronage, especially in the filling of the pastorate, yet permitted the holding of trial sermons before the congregation, which "might represent to him its opinion of the qualifications of the candidate." As first pastor of the congregation of Grodziec the church patron and the representatives of the localities elected unanimously the candidate of theology David Bergemann of Fraustadt. This act of foundation was confirmed on January 9, 1797 by the South Prussian government at Posen.

Hard were the beginnings. The building of the church, begun in 1797, was completed only in 1803. The house of God was not large, only 14 ells high, built in the manner of a "preußische Mauer" (Prussian wall), that is, of timber with bricks laid in between. The parsonage, a simple wooden building (erected 1800), consisted of three small living rooms and was thatched with straw. The cantor's house and all the farm buildings (of timber with straw roofs) Pastor Bergemann erected likewise in the year 1800. His activity in the congregation lasted 10 years. On August 11, 1807 he died unexpectedly "of bodily pains" at the age of 41 years. His earthly remains were buried on August 13 of that year before the altar in the old church by Pastor Friedrich Heinrich Wilhelm Ernst Jonathan Grimm of Stawiszyn4).

The congregation, now shepherdless, called as its shepherd of souls Pastor Johann Christian Gottlieb Rissmann, a native of Glogau, who exercised his office here until 1815. He then went to Ostrowo5). Into the period of his activity fall the grave events of the year 1812, which did not remain without influence upon the religious life of the little congregations.

The religious life of the parish suffered for a time under sectarian agitation. In the year 1823 enthusiasts appeared who called themselves "Erweckte" (the awakened), held assemblies in private houses and interpreted the Bible according to their own judgment. A particular veneration was accorded a girl of 16 years, who in a state of ecstasy presumed to know about life after death. The sect, however, did not spread. After a few years the "Erweckten" rejected their false doctrines and returned to the bosom of the evangelical church.

The Polish-Russian war of 1830/31 involved the congregation in suffering. It was not granted to Pastor Stegmann to serve his parishioners in this hard time, for already on January 29, 1831 he departed this life at the age of 69 years. He was buried in the evangelical forest cemetery at Grodziec.

The parish, vacant from 1831 to 1832, then received in the person of Pastor Joseph Spleszyński a new shepherd of souls. In his work he directed his attention chiefly to the building of a new church, whose necessity he recognized, the more so as the old house of God was quite dilapidated and almost unusable. On September 1, 1834 a meeting took place in the matter of the church building, at which, besides the local pastor, Sup. Modl of Kalisch also took part, as well as the church elders: Gottfried Kurtz of Grodziec, August Matheke of Alt-Grądy, Georg Heintz of Borowiec, Gottlieb Janott of Konary, Gottfried Reich of Wielołęka, Gottlieb Drewitsch of Neu-Grądy, Johann Rentz of Białobłoty and Christoph Polsfuth of Klein-Orlin. There appeared further, at this exceedingly important deliberation, the village headmen of the congregation: Michael Lempke of Borowiec, Andreas Fengler of Konary, Gottfried Gützel of Wielołęka and Gottfried Splistösser of Neu-Grądy. Those assembled resolved to build a new church with seating room for 800 persons. Plan and cost estimate were prepared by the voivodeship building councilor Reinstein at Kalisch. For the examination of the cost estimate Pastor Spleczyński held on October 5 of that year a second consultation with the church elders, the cantorate elders and the village headmen, at which the covering of the church with roof tiles (not with sheet zinc, as had at first been intended) and the procurement of the building material were negotiated. At the instigation of the Kalisch voivodeship there was drawn up on November 18, 1834, in the presence of Sup. Modl, building councilor Reinstein and the church council of Grodziec, a protocol concerning the complete unusability of the old house of God and the pressing necessity of the church building. Shortly thereafter the cost estimate of 47,209 zt.

7 groschen was definitively worked out, and on June 7, 1836 the congregation pledged itself to raise the sum of 24,214 zlotys 29.5 groschen in three installments. Namely, the first installment was to be payable after the laying of the foundation stone, the second after the erection of the shell of the building, and the third after the completion of the church. The remaining sum of 22,994 zt. 7 groschen was to be borne by the lordship of Grodziec according to the act of foundation. Yet it could not fulfill its obligation, in that the government took it into receivership and at the same time notified the evangelical congregation on December 28, 1836 that the affiliated parishioners themselves must bear all the costs of building, although it too was ready to grant an aid. This letter greatly discouraged the local pastor and the congregation. The new building of the church seemed, in consequence of this position taken by the authorities, not capable of execution. For still greater sacrifices could not be imposed upon the poor parish. Therefore the building matter rested. In the year 1839 Pastor Spleszyński left Grodziec and assumed the administration of the evangelical-Reformed congregation of Warsaw6).

During the vacancy the parish was administered by Pastor Karl Friedrich Orive of Stawiszyn, who, after the election had taken place, was confirmed by the government on July 24, 1841 as local pastor. The new shepherd of souls was deeply concerned for the welfare of his parish, in which above all the church building lay at his heart. The documents of the parish archive bear witness to this. On April 13, 1841 he induced the congregation to vote a higher building contribution and to apportion the cost estimate in such a way that farmers with one hide each should pay 87 zlotys, farm hands and inhabitants (tenants) 6 zt., maidservants and female inhabitants 4 zlotys. On the other hand the members of the cantorate of Obory, which lies far from the parish town, had to pay of the hide only 58 zlotys, farm hands and inhabitants only 4 zlotys, maidservants and female inhabitants 3 zlotys. This favor was granted them also for the reason that they had built a house of prayer at their own cost. By this apportionment of the cost estimate it was hoped to raise a building sum of 37,410 zlotys 22 groschen, namely after deduction of all the amounts which for various reasons could not be collected (niedobory). The deficit of 9798 zlotys 20 groschen was to be granted as assistance by the government, which was petitioned to that end.

The church building took on ever more tangible shape. On August 14, 1843 the county building office at Konin resolved to carry out the building by administrative procedure, the more so as no building contractor showed himself inclined to undertake it. Alexander Pawłowski, the estate steward of Grodziec, began in 1846, as plenipotentiary of the count, the building of the house of God. It was erected, against the wish of many congregation members, on a hill of sand. The solemn laying of the foundation stone of the church took place in the presence of Sup. Modl on August 9, 1846. Soon the shell of the building (up to the roof) was finished. Then there came unexpectedly an interruption of the work, partly because the lordship would not fulfill its obligations, partly also because the energetic estate steward Pawłowski had left Grodziec. Pastor Orive, notwithstanding all difficulties, did not remain idle. He negotiated with the county building office, with Count Bieliński, took steps with Sup. Modl-Kalisch and with the consistory, in order only to push the building forward.

Meanwhile the old dilapidated church threatened to collapse. Therefore the authorities saw themselves compelled to order the sealing of the church. This took place on October 24, 1850. By this measure the holding of divine services met with difficulties, for the uncompleted new house of God could not yet be used. In consequence the church council of Grodziec petitioned the county office at Konin to have the old church repaired so far until the new one should be built. The county office, however, did not consent to this. It is to be assumed that the divine services took place in the open air for lack of a suitable room. At Whitsuntide 1852 the congregation celebrated its first divine service in the church, not yet fitted out within. It did so reluctantly, in the fear that the further building might thereby be hindered. Sup. Modl sought to dispel these misgivings. In the letter of May 26, 1852 he encouraged "his dear parish children at Grodziec" not to despair but to trust in the success of the work begun

[p. 72]

with faith. "Be firmly convinced — he wrote — that the holding of services of worship in the new church will in no wise hinder the building of the same. Your old superintendent, under whose eyes a part of you has grown up and who himself has reached the age of gray hairs, will do everything that your church may be completed and adorned with a tower." These comforting, fatherly words instilled into many new courage and new cheerfulness. In spite of all — the building stagnated. The roof of the church was covered only by half in makeshift fashion, the tower not built, the interior of the house of God not finished. Nevertheless the zeal of the church council did not flag. On May 22, 1852 it concluded a contract with the organ builder and cabinetmaker Karl Köhler of Kalisch, according to which the latter pledged himself to rebuild the organ and set it up in the new church; besides, he was to deliver pulpit, altar, baptismal font and altar rail. At the same time the church board ordered of him also benches, hymn boards and the like. All the work was executed in 1853.

Years passed, but the completion of the house of God was delayed ever more. The ministry of the interior decreed indeed on May 12, 1859 that Count Bieliński was obligated to complete the church building according to the original plan and cost estimate. Soon afterward Count Bieliński died († 1860), which was quite unfavorable for the further building. The widowed Countess Virginia von Bielińska, to whom the church council of Grodziec now turned again and again in the building matter, held out in 1865 the prospect of an assistance of 5000 zlotys, without, however, redeeming her promise. The Kalisch superintendency therefore saw no other way out than to call upon the help of the government. The latter, by its measures, moved the countess to make good the obligations assumed by the lordship. On September 22, 1866 the building of the church, which had lasted 20 years, was finished7).

On August 2, 1860, together with the old house of God (St. Peter's and Paul's church), the dilapidated cantor's building also was torn down. The plan and cost estimate for the erection of a parsonage and cantor's house the government confirmed on March 9, 1863. In the spring of the following year the building of the parsonage was begun by the architect Reich of Pleschen and completed in 1868. It cost 2971 rubles 33 kopecks, the consistory granting 750 rubles.

The duties of the sexton were performed in the congregation at first by two so-called "Kirchenväter" (church fathers) chosen for the purpose. As they had, indeed, too much to do, they transferred this office about 1850 to a man named Gottfried Blesing, who thus was the first sexton of Grodziec.

On May 16, 1872 the lordship changed owners: Count Mieczysław v. Kwilecki acquired Grodziec. Through the village bailiff he gave the cantor notice of his dwelling and gave expression through his further conduct to his aversion to the evangelical parish. The allowance accruing to the congregation on the basis of the erection charter of January 9, 1797 was lost to it, because it was not secured by mortgage (lawsuit 1884). The close relation, though not always free of friction, which for decades had existed between the dominium of Grodziec and the evangelical parish, dissolved.

In exemplary fidelity and devotion Pastor Orive administered Grodziec. The welfare of the congregation determined his course of action always, even when he personally had to suffer under it. His outward situation was shadowed by the care for an existence worthy of a human being. Especially heavy the years 1865–1876 must have been for him, for he complains that the parish owes him for some time past his salary, and that the dominium does not give him the allowance. The material need sharpened for him to such a degree that beside his pastoral work he still filled the office of a clerk in the civil commune of Grodziec. On August 3, 1876 he died at the age of 72 years.

After his death the parish was administered by Pastor Erdmann of Zagórów, who then from 1878 to 1892 was pastor of Grodziec. In 1883 he obtained from the firm Schlag u. Söhne in Schweidnitz an organ for 2357 rubles. — It is related that Pastor Erdmann was suddenly taken ill in the pulpit during the sermon, so that he had to be carried out of the church. In the parsonage garden he is said to have died († October 17, 1892).

The administration of Grodziec was entrusted by the consistory to Pastor Joseph Rosenberg of Stawiszyn, who visited it until 1894. From 1894 to 1898 (April) Pastor August Loth labored here, at present first pastor of the Warsaw evangelical-Lutheran congregation. According to the annual report of 1895 there dwelt at that time within the bounds of the parish 20 Reformed persons, whom the pastor of Żychlin cared for spiritually. On April 13, 1898 Pastor Loth petitioned the consistory to relieve him of the direction of the Grodziec pastorate and to appoint him admini-

strator of Rawa and of the filial Błędów. The consistory complied with this request and at the same time declared Grodziec vacant. Since, however, no candidates presented themselves, it again entrusted the administration of Grodziec (until September 1899) to Pastor Rosenberg of Stawiszyn. For the vacant pastorate Pastor Arnold Hoffmann of Rożyszcze applied, whose candidacy the consistory, however, declined to confirm, because he did not belong to the body of pastors of the Warsaw consistorial district. Meanwhile Pastor Rosenberg removed to Konstantynow, and administrator of Grodziec became, from September 10, 1899, Pastor Karl Henkel of Konin. On October 29, 1900 the congregation again received a shepherd of souls of its own, Pastor Rudolf Buse.

Proceeding from the recognition that the work of the cantors is exceedingly important for the congregation, Pastor Buse instituted cantors' conferences, which enjoyed great popularity. From July 8, 1910 he labored also as superintendent of the Kalisch diocese. In the World War, arrested by the Cossacks in 1914, he was banished to Russia8). After severe suffering he departed this life on January 3, 1917 at Saratov, where on January 5 of that year he was buried by his predecessor in the Grodziec pastorate, Pastor August Loth.

The care of Grodziec was assumed by Pastor Sachs of Turek. In June 1915 Pastor Otto Krenz came here, who officiated until 1919. After Pastor Sachs had once more administered the congregation, the pastorate was assumed on May 1, 1922 by Pastor Adolf Gustav Ulbrich.

As the farm buildings had burned down, he built new ones in 1923. In the years 1924/25 he surrounded the church square with a beautiful brick fence, for which among other things 481.50 dollars flowed in from America from former congregation members. On the front side of the parsonage Pastor Ulbrich erected likewise, from voluntary gifts, a fence. In the year 1924 the house of prayer at Orlina-Wielka burned down. It was then rebuilt, enlarged and provided with a gallery. Scarcely was it erected when the school inspectorate of the gmina Rzgów sought to appropriate it. The congregation set itself against this. There came a protracted lawsuit, which was decided first in the supreme administrative court in favor of the evangelical parish.

The religious-moral state of the parish is satisfactory. Church attendance is good, Bibles and devotional books are everywhere present, family prayers are held in many households.

## 12. Konin.

The county town of Konin on the Warthe is a foundation under German law (13th century). As vogt Wilhelm is named here in 1365. In 1331 Konin was destroyed by the Knights of the Cross. During the war against the German Order of Knights King Wladislaus Jagiełło sojourned several months at Konin in 1433. In the wide environs of the town, back of Gosławice, there stretch two lakes: the Gostawicer and the Pontnower lake. Above the two bodies of water lies the village of Pontnow1). The road, which formerly separated the two lakes and formed, as it were, a bridge between them, is the so-called Bernsteinstrasse, which led from old Rome by way of Kalisch to the Baltic Sea. The movement of the Reformation in Poland sent its waves also into the Konin region. In the year 1590 (exact records only from 1630) there came about, in Żychlin 3 km from Konin, the formation of an evangelical-Reformed congregation called into being by the noble family Żychliński, which has preserved itself to the present day. The beginnings of the evangelical-Lutheran church system in the Konin district go back into the first decade of the 19th century. About this time efforts were afoot to gather all the Lutherans here into one congregation, the little town of Slesin, 20 km from Konin, having been designated as parish town. Already since 1790 Slesin, with the preaching places Butter Hol. (Maślaki), Szyszyner Hol. and Grabina, was a filial of Witkowo and was cared for by Pastor Johann Gottlieb Bothe2). In the year 1810 there was active at Slesin, without being authorized thereto, the rector and candidate of theology Radke, who held divine services in the evangelical localities and performed acts of office. In consequence Pastor Bothe petitioned the prefect of the Posen department on July 24, 1810 that he might forbid Radke all further officiating and settle the question of his own accord,

A serious time broke in upon the congregation of Konin after the death of its first pastor. The Russian-Polish war of 1830/31, the cholera, the waning of the religious-ecclesiastical sense and other unwelcome phenomena threatened the existence of the newly founded parish. For a time the sect of the "Zaftianer," which had many adherents in the country, was of special disadvantage to the congregational life. These sectaries held for a sign of "true conversion" when "the Spirit compelled the sinner to lamentation and crying out." The sect gained ground so long as it was combated with outward means. In the year 1837 Pastor Justus Hermann Otto Hinz, who came from Wilhelmsberg in East Prussia, became shepherd of souls at Konin. In the villages houses of prayer and cantorate schools arose, whose number constantly increased. Thus there were in 1839 — 16 and in 1866 already 21 village schools united with houses of prayer. In Konin itself the evangelical school could be opened only in 1849. The obstacles that had to be removed here seemed at first insurmountable. After Pastor Kegler's death the office of cantor and teacher at Konin lapsed. The evangelical town dwellers had to pay their school contributions to the Catholic teachers. The Kalisch voivodeship commission decreed indeed that in the school at Konin visited by evangelical and Catholic children one of the two teachers must be of evangelical confession. The carrying out of this ordinance, however, the Catholics knew how to hinder for a longer time. Then Pastor Hinz addressed a petition to the viceroy Paskiewicz, who sent a commission of investigation to Konin and thereupon brought about the opening of the evangelical school in the school building erected jointly by Catholics and Evangelicals.

In the year 1840 Pastor Hinz built the present brick parsonage. In 1843 he founded the filial Maślaki, which he visited 12 times a year. Thanks to the energetic help of the evangelical town burgher A. Schuppe, who presented to the congregation a site on the highroad Konin–Koło as a place of burial, the evangelical cemetery at Konin could be laid out in the year 1846. In 1849 the three Lutheran cantorates of Święcia, Borowo and Brzezińskie Holendry, which before had been included in the Reformed congregation of Żychlin, were attached to Konin. In the years 1853/54 the building of a new church on the site bought in 1820 was begun and completed. In the year 1859 an organ was procured for 1287 rubles and in 1872 a bell tower built. Further, steel bells (of the weight of 60 puds) were procured for 452 thalers.

On June 10, 1876 and June 4, 1880 Gen.-Sup. Bishop Everth visited the Konin parish. He was very well satisfied with the state and the direction of the church system and thanked the "worthy pastor, whom he commended to the divine assistance of grace for the further faithful conduct of his office." After 45 years of blessed service, on August 28, 1882, Pastor Justus Hermann Otto Hinz departed this life. The congregation of Konin preserved a faithful remembrance of its shepherd of souls.

[p. 168]

For the vacant pastorate, administered by the pastors Edmund Schultz-Prażuchy and Kasimir Lembke-Dąbie, there applied the Reformed pastor Karl Henkel of Żychlin. The evangelical-Augsburg consistory at Warsaw refused, on grounds of confession, to confirm his candidacy. Notwithstanding this position of the consistory, the Konin church council championed Pastor Henkel all the more, as it knew that herein it represented the opinion of the congregation. And in truth, the whole congregation of Konin desired Pastor Henkel as its shepherd of souls. As local pastor of Żychlin (from 1869 to 1884) he enjoyed so good a reputation that the name "Pastor Henkel" everywhere in the Konin region stood for that of a faithful, considerate and kindly shepherd of souls. The consistory, perforce, laid aside all its misgivings, after Pastor Henkel had pledged himself to hold and to represent the confession of the church in which he wished to labor. Elected shepherd of souls of Konin on April 30, 1884, he entered upon his new pastorate already on July 27 of that year. 11 days later (on August 7) Gen.-Sup. Bishop Everth visited the parish.

Quietly, without much ado, Pastor Henkel went at the outer and inner development of the congregation. In order to serve it better, he induced the affiliated parishioners to buy land, in order that with his own team he might visit the sick and perform other journeys of office. On July 7, 1894 the congregation acquired from Karl Fibich 15 Morgen of land (10 Morgen of arable land and 5 Morgen of meadow) for 1982 rubles 50 kopecks. Further, at the instance of the local pastor, on the plot which the evangelical town burghers Paul Michel and Daniel Stark presented to the church, there was erected a house with rooms for the evangelical school, for the teacher and cantor, the sexton and the gravedigger. Soon thereafter there was built on to this house a "home for the aged for poor Evangelicals of the town of Konin," whose consecration took place on July 8, 1905. In the next year the evangelical school was then transferred into the congregational house and consecrated likewise by Pastor Henkel.

On the high holidays the house of God built in the year 1854 was much too small and in need of enlargement. With the consent of the congregation members a tower was built on to the church (laying of the foundation stone on June 26, 1901). Soon thereafter, however, it appeared that the house of God must be remodeled and also surrounded with a solid fence. The two congregational meetings (on February 9, 1902 and March 11, 1906) determined the funds for this. The costs of the remodeling of the church amounted to 8306 rubles 42 kopecks. The tower building itself cost 11,302 rubles 78 kopecks.

After the completion of these works there arose the necessity of providing for the interior fitting out and a worthy furnishing of the house of God. Therefore the local pastor held a congregational meeting on February 14, 1909, at which he reported upon the works still to be executed

[p. 169]

and presented the cost estimate, which was also approved. In all the costs of the interior fitting out, the renovation and the furnishing of the church (altar, pulpit, benches and the like) amounted to 10,022 rubles 30 kopecks.12) The total costs of the tower building, the remodeling and interior fitting out, the renovation and adornment of the house of God amounted to 29,631 rubles 50 kopecks.

On May 3, 1915 Pastor Karl Henkel died of the consequences of a long throat ailment. His death was sincerely mourned both by his congregation members and by those of other faith. In the evangelical cemetery at Konin he found his last rest.

The temporary administration was assumed by Pastor Sachs of Turek and after him by the pastor Schnorr from the Reich, who laid down the pastorate in 1917 in order again to care for his home congregation of Feuchtwangen in Bavaria. He was followed by Pastor Adolf Löffler from 1917 to 1927.

To Pastor Löffler there fell the hard task of protecting the evangelical population of the country from the excesses of the local authorities. It was especially the cantorates that he had to defend. While the German-evangelical country population gave him its full confidence, the Polish-speaking Evangelicals of Konin, together with the local authorities, stood opposed to him.13) This cleavage led to unpleasantness and conditioned also Pastor Löffler's transfer to Chodecz.

After Pastor Krempin-Koło had temporarily cared for the congregation of Konin and Pastor Kreutz-Sompolno for the filial Maślaki, the consistory appointed on April 1, 1928 Pastor Robert Gustav Badke administrator (Verweser) of the parish and filial congregation. He unfolded his activity: "1. in the direction of the completion of the restoration of the church begun in Pastor Henkel's time and 2. in the direction of the work of building up the congregation by the deepening of pastoral care"14). In the year 1929 the church and the parsonage were renovated, the organ put in repair, for which about 12,000 zlotys were expended. The electric lighting plant in the church was presented by August Walter of Konin (it cost 4000 zlotys). In the parish as also in the filial Maślaki Pastor Badke instituted in the year 1928 mission festivals, gathered the confirmed youth, founded singing and brass choirs, held cantors' conferences in 1929 and 1930. ## a) Maślaki.

The lord of the manor Bogucki of Dębowiec concluded on December 9, 1779 with German-evangelical colonists a contract concerning the purchase of the colony of Maślaki (Butterholland)1). For the school he set aside 15 Morgen of land, which, however, since the founding of a school did not immediately follow, the settlers distributed among themselves. The later protracted efforts of the consistory to recover this land for the purpose named were without success. In 1799 the Evangelicals built here a schoolhouse. The school garden comprised 216 square rods, the churchyard 49 square rods. In place of the old house of prayer, which in the year 1800 fell a victim to a conflagration, the evangelical farmers erected in the following year a new wooden house of prayer — 17 ells long, 14 broad and 8 ells high2).

Maślaki was also "among the 70 villages" which held to Slesin as their religious center. The pastors of Witkowo, then of Slesin, Babiak and afterward of Konin cared for this region. But after Pastor Drechsler had left Slesin in 1815, this place lost its former local-ecclesiastical significance, and the evangelical house of prayer there was taken from the evangelical population again3). From the year 1838 efforts made themselves felt to gather the evangelical settlements about Maślaki into a filial congregation, in order thereby to give them again an ecclesiastical support. As early as 1888 the filial considered erecting a new solid church at an expenditure of 6568 rubles 17.5 kopecks. The building plan, together with the cost estimate, was submitted to the Russian authorities for confirmation. But since this project was not approved by the authorities, the filial congregation was compelled to repair and enclose the old defective house of prayer, to lay a cement floor within, and even to put a wooden tower on the "church," as the plain house of prayer is here designated. Nevertheless the house of prayer remained much too small and cramped, so that the building of a new church was here felt to be the most pressing task. Pastor Henkel of Konin indeed supported the plan of the church building, but wished at first that the house of God should be built "not at Maślaki on the border of the filial, but in the market town of Kleczew, which forms the center of the filial congregation"4). In the meantime years went by. When Gen.-Sup. Bursche visited Maślaki in 1908, he emphasized the indispensable necessity of the church building. The members of the filial were in principle agreed with this. What divided them into two parties was the opposed conception concerning the place of building of the new house of God. While some advocated Maślaki, the others desired that the new church be erected at Kleczew. The filial assembly of June 29, 1910 indeed voted 15,000 rubles for the building, which were to be paid in three annual installments, yet the differences of opinion continued with unabated sharpness. The consistory definitively appointed Maślaki as the place of building, whereupon on April 10, 1913 the ministry of the interior granted the permission for the church building at Maślaki for the sum of 17,392 rubles 41 kopecks. The disunity in the filial, as also the outbreak of the World War in 1914, hindered the execution of this work. To the present day the question of the church building has unhappily not been settled here.

On April 29, 1912 the cantor Christoph Möller died, who had served the cantorate of Bolesławowo uninterruptedly for 58 years. "For 58 years he has proclaimed to us and to our forefathers the precious Word of God, taught the youth and called back to God many a hardened heart. His sermon readings and his singing penetrated into all hearts, and Möller was often envied by his colleagues because of his voice and his gift"5). On September 22, 1904 he was privileged to celebrate his 50-year jubilee as cantor.

## 17. Zagórów.

In the year 1145 the German Cistercian monks founded the monastery Ląd on the Warthe (Lond, Lande). The first 25 abbots were of German origin1). Only with the year 1553 did the German monastery pass into Polish direction. The first Polish abbot was Jan Wysocki. After his entry upon office the German monks fled with the records of the monastery of Ląd to the monastery Heinrichau in Silesia.

To the colonizing activity of the monastery there go back, among others, the foundations of the towns: Lądek (Landeck) 1269, Pyzdry (Peisern) 1257, Zagórów 1445. In order to connect the last-named town with the nearby monastery, a dam 4 km long was thrown up.

The immigration of German-evangelical colonists into the Zagórów region can be documented from the year 1746. At that time Paul von Kolno-Prusiński, owner of the estate of Trąbczyn, concluded with German peasants a hereditary lease contract, by virtue of which he left to them for clearing woodland on which they founded the village of Łazińsk. It is the oldest settlement in the commune and at present also the largest cantorate, with over 150 evangelical farmers. In Łazińsk itself there are 115 of them, besides 15 farmers of the Huta Trąbczyńska, 10 from Trąbczyn B, 5 from Trąbczyn C and about 12 farmers from Trąbczyn D. The present school at Łazińsk was built in 1838/39, the bricks being replaced by the so-called "Sumpferz" or "Wiesenerz" (bog ore or meadow ore), a building material of importance in this region.

Until 1817 the pastors of Wreschen visited the Evangelicals in and about Zagórów, then those of the neighboring congregations of Grodziec, Konin and Żychlin (Reformed). In the year 1829 efforts made themselves felt to found an evangelical-Lutheran parish in the Zagórów region and to call a preacher. Karl Gottlieb Weigel, the lord of the manor of Kopojno, intended at that time to build on his possessions an evangelical church as well as the other needed buildings and to grant "for the clergyman to be appointed there an yearly quantum of 40 quarters of rye, 10 quarters of barley and 25 quarters of oats, as well as later additionally 6 barrels of beer . . ." The protocol taken on the manor farm at Kopojno on December 7, 1829, as also Weigel's two declarations of November 15, 1833 and July 8, 1834, made the expectation of the impending founding of a congregation appear justified, for which the Evangelicals from the Zagórów environs pleaded as warmly. On March 22, 1836 Weigel wrote, among other things, to Sup. Modl at Kalisch: ". . . Long since, and truly from purely Christian intentions, for the furtherance of religion and morality among the numerous evangelical colonists of my estates and their neighborhood . ., it was my most earnest wish, . . . to see the building of an evangelical church upon my territory really brought to fulfillment. The unhappy events in Poland have also held up this undertaking . . .

Weigel's death († 1836 at Breslau) worked out unfavorably in this, that his son and heir (Alexander Weigel) was finally unwilling to make any endowments whatever for the congregation to be founded. Nevertheless the matter did not rest. Still in 1836 men were elected into the "congregational board" (provisional church council): Matthäus Luther, Ludwig Sperling, Johann Kelsch, Johann Gottlieb Kittel, Karl Heit. These petitioned Sup. Modl to assist them in the church building at Zagórów and in the establishment of the parish. Sup. Modl let them know that he would energetically support their endeavors with the competent ecclesiastical and secular authorities. From the petition of the congregational board it appears that now the town of Zagórów was chosen as parish seat.

According to the "Project of the establishment of the evangelical congregation at Zagórów" transmitted in 1836 to the Konin district commissioner

the Protestant population here numbered 2937 souls. The fund for the church building and the parish buildings amounted to 1727 zlotys. For the maintenance of the pastor there were appointed: 253 zlotys 28 groschen, 40 quarters of rye, 25 quarters of oats, 10 quarters of barley and 6 barrels of beer.

Meanwhile the choice of Zagórów as parish seat did not meet with the undivided approval of the evangelical country population. Thus on February 9, 1837 "in the name of the Evangelicals of Lissewo" Christian Wolf, Gottlieb Weber and Christian Kannwischer declared that they did not intend to take part in the building of the church at Zagórów, since they had a school (that is, also a house of prayer), "which the king has built for us," and were content with the administration of Grodziec. The same they repeated on July 28, 1840. There joined themselves to them the Evangelicals of Adelshof (Chruściki), who wrote on February 10, 1837 that they did not wish to take part in the church building, the more so as they "still have their dear evangelical preacher at Grójec (Grodziec)". Further, on February 9, 1837, in the name of the "Alt-Tomnitzer Hauländer," Martin Kannwischer, Gottfried Schnitzer and Gottfried Schultz announced that they had resolved not to attach themselves to the church at Zagórów. Likewise the Evangelicals of Peisern (Pyzdry) and the environs declared, at the meeting in the town hall there on June 21, 1838, that for the reason following they did not wish to be attached to Zagórów, because they wished to found a separate evangelical-Lutheran congregation at Peisern. Likewise the "Ciemierower Hauländer" refused to be attached to Zagórów (declaration of July 28, 1840). It was chiefly localities of the present filial of Lissewo-Pyzdry that stood opposed, for local and economic reasons, to the congregational organization to be created at Zagórów.

In the letter to Sup. Modl of August 30, 1839 the Warsaw general consistory gave expression to its opinion that "in consequence of insufficient means an evangelical congregation in the Zagórów region cannot at present be founded". Although the circumstances compelled abstention from an independent parochial foundation at Zagórów, yet the Evangelicals resolved, at the meeting of July 29, 1840, to establish first a filial at Zagórów and to elect the first church council. Into the church board there were elected: Matthäus Luther, Johann Abramowski, Johann Friedrich Rasch, Johann Eichhorst, Ludwig Neumann and Gottlieb Schnitzer. On January 24, 1841 Pastor Scholtz-Żychlin offered, in his letter to Sup. Modl, to care for the Zagórów Evangelicals, whom he had already visited for 5 years before. Therefore Sup. Modl proposed to the general consistory that Pastor Scholtz be entrusted with the visiting and further development of the filial. The general consistory, however, entrusted on June 28 of this year Pastor Orive of Grodziec with the administration of Zagórów.

[p. 251]

The register of the villages to be included in the filial, prepared on July 31, 1841 by the burgomaster of the town of Zagórów, which showed a number of 3924 souls, was confirmed on October 29, 1842 by the governmental commission for internal, spiritual affairs and public instruction. The high number of souls of the settlers dwelling round about induced the general consistory to call Sup. Modl's attention to the fact that at Zagórów in reality not a filial but a parish congregation ought to be organized. Yet the filial should exist only so long, "until the congregation can show sufficient means for the erection of a parish".

The filial had at first no house of prayer, so that the divine services were held in a rented hall. This displeased a number of villages which already had houses of prayer of their own and therefore stood in a better position than Zagórów. On November 23, 1845 Łazińsk, Michalinów (gm. Trąbczyn), Stara Huta, Emilienheim (Michalinów gm. Oleśnica) petitioned to remove the filial seat from Zagórów to Łazińsk. They grounded this on the fact that at Łazińsk there was a brick house of prayer which "could accommodate over 300 persons". In the month of January 1846 the Evangelicals of Anielewo expressed their readiness to attach themselves to Łazińsk, in case the filial should be transferred thither. Of importance also was the declaration of the owner of Trąbczyn, Michalina Miączyńska, who informed Sup. Modl on February 10, 1846 that she would be inclined to give grain and firewood "in proportion to the size of the congregation," if Łazińsk had a church and a pastor. In consequence Sup. Modl came to Łazińsk on July 6, 1846 and established, after careful examination of the situation, that the evangelical colonists there desired to have the center of the filial and in that case would be ready to rebuild the house of prayer at Łazińsk. Sup. Modl indeed supported their efforts, yet the general consistory did not concur in his opinion, because the Evangelicals of Koszuty, Młodojewo and Kossewo had raised objection to the transfer of the filial from Zagórów to Łazińsk.

In the year 1850 the filial bought a half-timbered house in Zagórów at the Koninerstr. No. 54, one half of which was used as prayer hall, the other as school room and cantor's dwelling. Only until Christmas 1856 was Zagórów a filial of Grodziec, whose shepherd of souls Karl Friedrich Orive administered the new congregation uninterruptedly. From Christmas 1856 Pastor Wilhelm Winkler officiated as first local pastor of the parish, now become independent.

In 1857 the congregation acquired in Zagórów at the Koninerstr. No. 51 and 52 two little houses, of which the one served as parsonage, the other as kitchen of the parsonage. In 1859 the parsonage was correspondingly fitted up.

[p. 252]

The raising of Zagórów to a parish congregation did not, however, please the lord of the manor Weigel of Kopojno. Therefore he caused, through his plenipotentiary Wilhelm Jaenike, on January 10, 1858, the protocolary declaration to be given that he was ready to present 12 Morgen of parsonage land and bricks for the church building, if the center of the congregation were removed from Zagórów to Emilienheim (Michalinów Kopoiński). On February 28, 1862 the Warsaw consistory decided that, in consideration of the local situation and the conditions of traffic, only Zagórów could come into question as parish seat, and that here also the church must be built. Although the Evangelicals of Trąbczyn directed to the consistory on September 26, 1862 the petition to determine Emilienheim as the parish center, yet this had no practical consequences.

In 1860 Pastor Winkler left the congregation, after heavy dissensions with the church council, which rested upon unsachlichen accusations; into his service there now entered Pastor Eugen Biedermann. "The parish was small, the salary of the pastor very small, and the little house appointed for him as dwelling was so low that the threshold had to be taken up in order to carry into it (at the entry of the pastor) a wardrobe. Besides, the dwelling was cold and damp, which drew after it a grave illness of his wife, and this moved the young pastor in the year 1865 to removal to Pułtusk"2). Pastor Biedermann was indeed concerned to erect a church at Zagórów, toward which he also took the first steps, but soon there broke out in the congregation disputes concerning the place of building, which he was unable to compose. This also influenced his resolve to leave the parish. After his departure Zagórów was administered for a decade by Pastor Haeßner of Stawiszyn. In the year 1876 Pastor Karl Eduard Erdmann came here, who however after only two years assumed the neighboring pastorate of Grodziec, from which he continued to administer his former congregation.

Since the Zagórów prayer hall had room for only about 80 persons and was so low "that the pastor, standing before the altar, reached with his head to the beams," the building of a church was felt as the most pressing concern of the parish. Already in 1863 the consistory turned to Count Taczanowski, the owner of the estates and of the town of Zagórów, with the request to grant an assistance for the church building and for the maintenance of the pastor. Of significance, however, was first the congregational meeting of July 27, 1872, which passed the resolution for the church building. From that time this question took a slow but steady development. On April 8, 1877 the ministry of the interior approved the building and granted for this purpose 3000 rubles.

On August 22, 1877 Pastor Teichmann, superintendent of the Kalisch diocese, visited Zagórów. In the visitation protocol he noted that the parish possessed only 1 elementary school with 60 children at Michalinów and 7 cantorate schools with approximately 230 pupils. He mentioned also that in the congregation the Neu-Strelitz catechism was to be introduced. Of the parish buildings, which Sup. The foundation stone of the building of the evangelical church at Zagórów was laid on May 8, 1878 by the local pastor Karl Eduard Erdmann. For the building the congregation members were to raise within 4 years a sum of 8000 rubles, while 3000 rubles, as already mentioned, were granted by the government. Members of the building committee were: Alexander Arndt, Wilhelm Schmechel, Wilhelm Münch, Wilhelm Kuck, Martin Ziegeldorf and Emanuel Munk; of the church council (elected on July 5, 1882): Christian Schultz, Karl Pfeifer, Heinrich Zelmer, Alexander Arndt and Johann Schütz. The technical supervision of the building was in the hands of the county building inspector Przanowski of Słupca. All the work at the building was undertaken by the master mason Gustav Jursch of Orlina Wielka for the fee of 3000 rubles. The town of Zagórów presented for the church building 110 trunks of building timber from the town forest. The building of the house of God cost 13,131 rubles 78 kopecks. In the late autumn of 1882 the church was already erected. On July 1, 1883 the church council ordered of the firm Schlag und Söhne in Schweidnitz an organ. The congregational meeting of June 29, 1884 resolved also to tax itself 25 kopecks per Morgen for the purchase of two bells. The consecration of the new church took place, according to oral tradition (the records report nothing more definite concerning it), on the first Advent Sunday 1882. In the beginning of October 1883 there took place the protocolary handing over of the church to the congregation.

Until his death († 1892) Pastor Karl Eduard Erdmann-Grodziec cared for the Zagórów congregation, which, mindful of his merits for the parish (the building of the church), honored his memory by the placing of a corresponding marble tablet in the house of God.

The administration of Zagórów the consistory then entrusted to Pastor Henkel of Konin, whereupon from the middle of July 1898 until the beginning of November 1903 Pastor Bernhard Erdmann, formerly vicar at Zgierz, labored here as settled shepherd of souls.

In the year 1890 the sheet-metal roof of the church and of the tower was torn off by the storm wind. The church council expended 760 rubles for the new roofing. Since the parsonage was too small and also otherwise did not answer the needs, the congregational meeting of October 3, 1893 resolved to buy from the married couple Ulatowski, for 2600 rubles, a house which lay opposite the church, as

[p. 254]

parsonage. On January 18, 1896 the supreme permission for this was granted. In 1898 the house of prayer at Michalinów burned down. In the same year it was resolved to build a new house of prayer at Chruściki (repaired 1918).

For a long time the church council had carried the thought of erecting a new cantor's house, the more so as the old one no longer answered its purpose. In consequence it petitioned the consistory on October 15, 1902 to permit it to sell the old cantor's house. The parochial assembly of December 13, 1907 therefore resolved to erect at Zagórów a new cantor's house and at the same time also a new parsonage beside the church. Pastor Buse of Grodziec, the then administrator of Zagórów, succeeded in convincing the congregation that through the building of the parsonage it would again have the possibility of obtaining a shepherd of souls of its own. According to the estimate of the year 1908 the building of the parsonage was to cost 7972 rubles 75 kopecks, that of the cantor's house (school room, dwellings for cantor and sexton) 6596 rubles 5 kopecks. The ministry of the interior confirmed on September 26, 1912 the plans and cost estimates. On March 7, 1913 the congregational meeting resolved, for the purpose of enlarging the building site for the parsonage, to buy from Johann Milner a small plot of land for 311 rubles. The parsonage (opposite the church), unsuitable because of its dampness, was sold in 1913 to the congregation member August Driesner for 3000 rubles. On October 16, 1913 the foundation stone of the parsonage building was laid. For this building the affiliated parishioners pledged themselves to pay a contribution of 1 ruble per Morgen, in all about 6000 rubles, besides 1000 rubles from the building fund; the remainder was to be covered by voluntary sacrifices. In the same year the erection of the cantor's and school house was begun. The building committee comprised: August Driesner, Wilhelm Neumann, Paul Rentz, Ferdinand Schulz, Friedrich Schütz, Christian Münch, August Kintzel, Julius Schneider, August Giebel, Daniel Gliege, Eduard Kuhn and Heinrich Kinast. In 1914 the World War broke out, which abruptly interrupted the works already begun. Only in 1916 could the cantor's house be continued and in 1917 completed. On June 4, 1917 the congregation bought yet from Milner two plots lying between the evangelical church and the cantor's house.

In the year 1923 Pastor Alexander Groß entered upon the pastorate of Zagórów. In place of the bell requisitioned in 1918 by the German occupation authorities a new bell was procured in 1923. In the years 1926/27 the remodeling of the organ was accomplished at an expenditure of 6000 zlotys (in 1910 the organ was repaired). In 1927 there was erected on the parsonage premises a farm building. In 1927 the married couple Ferdinand and Luise Krüger of Olchowo presented, in memory of their son Rudolf Krüger, missing in the Polish-Bolshevist war (1920), the present tower clock, which cost 5000 zlotys

[p. 255]

with its works. From September 1 to 11, 1927 Pastor Groß, to whom the religious work lies very near his heart, held in the Zagórów congregation as also in the filial of Lissewo-Pyzdry an evangelization week, in which, besides Pastor Groß, the pastors Löffler-Gąbin, Paul Otto — Christian Community Lodz, and the preacher Mark of Leonberg (Lwówek) near Gąbin labored.

In the year 1931 the building of the parsonage was taken up again. At the end of 1934 the shell of the building, including the roofing, was finished. It is presumed that it will be completed in 1937. The whole building costs of the parsonage will probably amount to 35–40,000 zlotys. In October 1933 the congregation celebrated the 50-year jubilee of the consecration of the church.

In 1934 the congregation (together with the filial of Lissewo-Pyzdry) entered upon the path of litigation concerning the legal award of the cantorate land and of the buildings standing upon it at Łazińsk (20 Morgen), Ciemierów (13 Morgen) and Wrąbczynkowska Kolonia (10 Morgen). The two last-named villages lie in the filial of Lissewo-Pyzdry. The lawsuit concerning Ciemierów the congregation won, while the judicial decision in the two other lawsuits is still awaited.

The parish of Zagórów, which counts 3500 souls, is an avowed colonist congregation. The affiliated parishioners live for the most part close together; those dwelling farthest have 10 km to the parish church.

The parish was visited: on August 10, 1884 by Bishop Everth, on May 10, 1903 by Gen.-Sup. G. Manitius, on May 23, 1909 and on July 6 and 7, 1925 by Gen.-Sup. Bursche.

## a) The filial of Lissewo-Pyzdry.

The town of Pyzdry (Peisern), which arose in 1257, is a foundation of the Cistercian monastery of Ląd on the Warthe. In the 14th century (about 1370) it belonged to the six towns whose elected councils "formed the highest municipal court for the province of Great Poland, without appeal to Halle and Magdeburg"1). According to the "inspection" of the year 1564 there were here alone 182 tax-paying craftsmen and "33 butchers". In the years 1790/94 Peisern counted over 3000 inhabitants.

Evangelical life in Peisern and its environs is demonstrable only from the Prussian period. For the German population numerously represented here it was intended to organize a congregation, for which on June 6, 1806, 100 Reichsthaler were appointed. Since 1798 there existed at Peisern an evangelical burgher and garrison school in a rented house. The teacher received 60 thalers salary for the instruction of the garrison children from the Royal war treasury and 10 thalers school money from the burgher children. The school was visited by 34 pupils2). The Prussian authorities also concerned themselves with the erection of schools in the country. Thus on September 3, 1799 the intendant Kolbe "of the intendancy of Peysern at Ciążyń" made proposals concerning the erection of new rural schools at Ostrowite (Ostrowska Kolonia), Wola Łaszczowa, Młodojewo, Lissewo, Kąty, Ciążyń, Gozdowo and Rataj. "The timber for it — he wrote — can be granted free. The inhabitants would . . . find themselves very ready, not only to accomplish the hauling of the timber," but also "to procure from their own means the materials, such as stones, lime and straw, for the roofing." At Lissewo a school and prayer house was in fact built, to which the colonists appealed again and again in the following decades. In 1797 there existed already also at Wierzchy a

Therefore they petitioned him for a teacher and cantor, for "otherwise in the end most of the Evangelicals (at Peisern) would go over to the Catholic church". Still in the same year (1845) the Evangelicals of Lissewo and Ciemierów came forward with the request to make Lissewo, where a house of prayer for approximately 100 persons was present, the center of the filial. Likewise in 1845 the colonists of Wierzchy desired the transfer of the seat of the filial to their village. Taking account of all these wishes and endeavors, Sup. Modl reported to the consistory on February 6, 1850 that, in case of the transfer of the filial from Peisern to Lissewo, the localities Piotrowo, Koszuty and Młodojewo could under no circumstances belong thither, because the distance was far too great. Besides, through this change the evangelical population of Peisern would be exposed to the danger of a stronger Catholization. Accordingly the consistory left the filial further at Peisern, which in turn called forth a counteraction on the part of the discontented cantorates. On February 7, 1851 the cemetery boards of Lissewo, Wierzchy, Ciemierów and Koszelewska Łąka petitioned the consistory to abolish the filial of Peisern and to establish the congregation of Zagórów. The consistory refused the dissolution of the filial, which since then bears the designation "Lissewo-Pyzdry"6).

In the year 1850 it was intended to erect a wooden church at Peisern, for which the former burgomaster Strużewski (later city president of Kalisch) promised to give a site and timber from the town forest. His successor, however (burgomaster Szydłowski), did not show himself well disposed toward the Evangelicals, so that the church building did not come about. In consequence the Peisern Evangelicals had also further to hold their divine services in rented halls (1847 in the "first side street at the market"; the room here was "12 ells long and 15 broad," and "in it there were 10 benches, each seating 10 persons"; in 1860 rented from Karl Balk; in 1864 from Jastrzębski). Only in 1909 did the Evangelicals of Peisern receive the supreme permission to buy a house destined for purposes of worship (from the married couple Kuske for 1500 rubles) at the Neuer Markt No. 101. In this house are the prayer hall and the cantor's dwelling.

The house of prayer at Wierzchy, which lies on the evangelical cemetery there, dates from the year 1803. In spite of the many renovations it is very dilapidated. Already in 1906 there was the intention here to build a new house of prayer. The building site was to be bought from the married couple Martin and Auguste Schwartz. Even the supreme permission for the building of the house of prayer was granted. Nevertheless no hand was laid to the work.

Before the World War of 1914/18 the religious life in the filial was very weakly developed. Much contributed to this the circumstance that the Evangelicals here dwelt for the most part near the former German-Russian frontier, whereby many fell into temptation to occupy themselves with frontier smuggling. Money easily earned in dishonest ways led many to drunkenness and other vices. After the shifting of the frontier a certain improvement in religious-moral respect has set in.

The cantor's dwelling at Peisern was for many years officially under seizure. The filial members thereupon, with the help of the consistory, instituted a lawsuit, which they also won. They thereupon renovated prayer hall and dwelling and called to be their cantor Gustav Adam, who is also teacher in the general elementary school with 7 classes at Peisern.

The Evangelicals of Ostrowska Kolonia, belonging to the cantorate of Wierzchy, are attached to the congregation of Grodziec, where they also pay their church contribution and enter all acts of the civil register.

The cantorate members of Wrąbczynek, a part of the cantorate congregation of Wrąbczynkowska Kolonia, have been included in the parish of Zagórów.

The filial of Lissewo-Pyzdry has no civil register office and therefore also no filial cantors. Concerning the place where the civil registers are to be kept, the filial members can still come to no agreement. If the Evangelicals of Peisern wish that the civil register office should be there, then in turn the fellow believers of the country demand that Ciemierów be determined as the place of the civil register office, because here

allegedly lies the center of the filial. In consequence of these differences of opinion, which since the founding of the filial congregation have shown themselves in various ways and last to the present day, the records of Lissewo-Pyzdry and those of the congregation of Zagórów are kept jointly. Every cantorate has, as also Peisern itself, its own cantor, whom it also pays.

The pastor of Zagórów visits the filial 12 times a year. The church contribution the filial members pay to the pastor of Zagórów, because no special filial budget is in existence.

STATISTICS.

Year of foundation: 1842.

Souls. In the year 1820 there dwelt at Peisern 76 Lutheran and 4 Reformed families; in the Peiserner Hauland 4 families, at Lissewo 22 families; in Zamość-Hauland 19 families, in the Wrąbczyner Hauland 19 families, in all 144 families. In 1841 there were counted in Peisern and in the country 156 families with in all 832 souls. Of these there were in the town of Peisern 50 evangelical families with 181 souls. In 1935 there dwell in the filial congregation approximately 240 families with about 1300 souls. In Peisern itself there are toward 50 evangelical families, who are partly laborers, partly also craftsmen or town farmers.

Pastors on the spot there have not been hitherto. The filial is administratively united with Zagórów.

Filial cantors and sextons there have likewise not been hitherto.

Numerical development of the filial.

The filial of Lissewo-Pyzdry and the parish congregation of Zagórów have a common civil register office. In the year 1896 there were baptized in the filial 52 children; 48 persons died. The Holy Supper was received by 738 filial members, of them 370 men and 368 women (among them also 4 home communions). In 1864 there were 709 communicants, 300 men and 409 women; confirmands 41, of them 11 boys and 30 girls. 1918: 222 male and 322 female communicants, in all 541 (also among them 1 home communion). 1933: the number of communicants counted jointly with Zagórów. 1934: 41 confirmands.
