SITTING 51.

On 13 February.

Present His Excellencies President Małachowski, Gutakowski, Stanisław Potocki, Wybicki, Działyński, Bieliński, Sobolewski.

After deliberating the queries of the Administrative Chamber of the Department of Poznań, that is, in what manner the January instalment

[p. 44]

of 24 grosze and the March instalment of the chimney tax are to be paid — and after reading in the matter the opinion of the late Director of the Treasury Małachowski — it was decided by a resolution in actis that this whole question, as likewise that of the following instalments of the tax of 24 grosze and the chimney tax, should, once the project has been drawn up, be referred again to His Excellency the Director of the Interior as Deputy for the Treasury.

Likewise the memorial of Michalina née Prusimska Dąbska was referred for an opinion to His Excellency the Director of Justice, with the recommendation that, with regard to the confiscation of the estates of Citizens by the past Government, he submit a project of general rules in this matter.

The lists of candidates for the Administrative Chamber of Warsaw, submitted by the 4 Directors, were read. Likewise the list submitted by the Director of Justice of Justices of the Peace for the Departments of Płock, Kalisz, Poznań and Warsaw, of Justices of the Land and of the Court of Appeal of the Department of Warsaw, was read. The Director of Justice was instructed to submit another list, with two candidates placed upon each seat.

His Excellency the President was asked to give a reply to Governor Gouvion on his letter recommending the Messieurs Jędrzejewicz, with the assurance that the Prince Director of War would be instructed to do full justice to the Governor's intercession, as far as circumstances allow.

The sitting was announced for after dinner.

Stanisław Małachowski, President.

SITTING 58.

On 18 February, after dinner.

Present His Excellencies President Małachowski, Gutakowski, Wybicki, Działyński, Bieliński, Sobolewski.

The memorials of Mr. Podgurski, asking that it be permitted him to deliver the requisitioned forage not to Łowicz but to Kalisz or Częstochowa; of Wojciech Maryański, asking for an office; and of the Magistrate of Mszczonów, asking leave to establish a magazine of forage and cattle in the town of Mszczonów — were referred to His Excellency the Director of the Interior; the project of Amtmanowicz Kossowski concerning the tithes was ordered to be laid aside ad acta.

Upon receiving from His Excellency the Director of Justice, on the memorial of Michalina née Prusimska Dąbska sent to him, his opinion that the petition she brought, with regard to the return of the estates confiscated by the past government, is a matter

[p. 55]

of a purely political character, and, as flowing from the will of the Absolute Monarch, where the laws are silent on such cases, cannot go the ordinary way of the courts — the Commission decided to send a note to His Most Serene Majesty the Emperor and King, asking the repeal of those confiscations and the return of the estates to their former rightful owners. There went out with it a recommendation to His Excellency the Director of the Interior, that he order the Administrative Chambers to keep diligent watch upon the state of those estates, and to guard that they not be ruined by their present possessors. The Commission ordered that an extract of this article of the Journal, with a copy of the recommendation to the Director of the Interior communicated, be given to Madame Dąbska in answer to her memorial.

The list of Justices of the Peace and of candidates for the Courts of Appeal, submitted by His Excellency the Director of Justice, and the form of the patents for the Justices of the Peace, were taken into deliberation.

Upon the motion of His Excellency the Director of the Interior, the Commission fixed by a resolution in actis a pension for Mr. Dentzel, Commandant of Warsaw, of 15 Thalers a day.

Stanisław Małachowski, President.

SITTING 210. Extraordinary.

On 21 July, at Dresden.

Present His Excellencies President Małachowski, Gutakowski, Stanisław Potocki, Wybicki, Działyński, Bieliński, Sobolewski.

Whereas the affair of Madame Michalina née Prusimska Dąbska, demanding the return of her hereditary estates confiscated under the past government and given to another, has been sent back to the Commission by H.M. the Emperor; taking example from the justice dealt out by H.M. the Emperor to His Excellency Wybicki by the Decree of 5 June given at Finckenstein; and wishing likewise, in a demand that is just, to keep strict justice for everyone — the Commission extends the decree of H.M. the Emperor serving His Excellency Wybicki both to the case of Madame Michalina Dąbska and to those of General Niemojewski and of Mr. Wichrowski, wishing that the hereditary estates of the persons named above, confiscated by the past Prussian government, return to their true owners.

Inclining to the petition of Mr. Batowski, on the motion of His Highness the Prince Director of War, present at this sitting, it was permitted

[p. 304]

to the same Mr. Batowski to wear the uniform of the National Guard and to use the insignia attached to the rank of Colonel.

Stanisław Małachowski, President.

No. 3. 15 July 1807. (Presented 22 July 1807 at the extraordinary sitting in Dresden).

To the Governing Commission. The sitting, made up of 4 Directors: the Prince came for a brief moment to announce that he was leaving for Dresden; he presented General Kamieniecki in his place; the Directorium declared that it has no power to admit anyone into its body without the express will of the Government; he referred himself to the Resolution of the Governing Commission of the 11th of the month, and, having taken the papers sent from the Directorium to the Government, departed. General Kamieniecki was not there, except for part of the sitting.

The yesterday's protocol was read.

The Plenipotentiary Kuczyński arrived and declared orally what the original sent to the Governing Commission tells of the unhappy state of the citizens, who at the summons of the Governing Commission had given everything to the army, and who have now passed back under the power of the Russians.

On Praga there were working at the entrenchments about 1,150 men, and besides them 60 carpenters.

In the lazarets there are found 811 sick, and those coming back to health.

The state of the Treasury in both cash boxes 200,992 gr. 16.

But at once the outlay on the army 156,666, on the Modlin entrenchments 8,000, for the wood a debt to the lazaret of 7,000 by the arrangement, to add the small outlays — there remains in the cash box 13/m.

The report of the state of the National Guard of Warsaw: 2,479 men.

The Director of the Interior brought in orally and showed the original letters, in which the purchase of oxen is requisitioned for the French army and the lazarets. The Directorium declared that it will give the authorisation, provided he submit it in writing and attach a project.

Mr. Daru has requisitioned that magazines of food be established in Warsaw; the French authorities were asked, through Mr. the Director of the Interior, what funds there are to be for this?

[p. 322]

Various particular petitions resolved in brief hand.

The public discontent at the uncertainty of the existence of the Name of Poland has passed also into the army, which is struck by the impression that under Augustów it is drawn together for the single purpose of being disarmed. The just fear was that this spreading should not draw further evil consequences after it; the Prince Director assured that there is nothing in the army to fear by way of unrest, provided only it be paid; this was the reason for the motion of the Prince Director, that the Directorium, having certain news that the money lies in Kalisz and in Poznań, charged the Treasury Administration to negotiate with Mr. Kiissel, Senior of the Confraternity of merchants, that for the army the sum be paid out here and at once forwarded to the army, 500/m, and there assigned to those same merchants. The negotiation can cost no more than the transport of the money would cost, and the army, once paid, will be the calmer.

If by this means we can soothe the alarmed army, so that it be neither disarmed, nor dispersed, nor made to yield to anyone — for all this passes through their heads — I do not know how one will soothe the people, who have suffered so much, and who now stand garrison and labour at the entrenchments; and the thought that they will not be Poles increases the despair ever greater; there is nothing to promise them, nothing to delude them with, and we do not know by what, at the least, they might be calmed.

Very many are leaving for Dresden; Dąmbska, Kownacka, Węgrzecki, Bórakowski, with two principal men of the city (one of them is Paschalis), say that they mean to make that city anzeatyczna, free. Horodyński has left, Gliszczyński, Czarnecki; Riviera means to go, Załuski, Wołoski, Nakwaski; of many I cannot give notice, yet I expect that, like me the Director of Police, the Government must be informed of them daily, for the Government ought to know of all who arrive there, in case it should be asked by those parts.

To ease the dispatching in the chancellery I have given one of the Secretaries of the Directorate, and the sitting is put off to Friday.

The letter of His Excellency the Governor was the reason for calling the extraordinary sitting at six in the evening. Four Directors were present at it, for the Director of War had gone away.

The Governor reports that peace between the Emperor of the French and the Emperor of Moscow was concluded on the 8th of the month and ratified on the 9th; he asked that it be made public in Warsaw, and the Directorium at once ordered it. No signs of rejoicing were asked for, and the thing was received in gloomy silence.

At the sitting, those gathered wished to know how they ought to conduct themselves when Mr. Kamieniecki comes to the sitting, whether as Deputy Director he may take a seat? To this end there was read the Commissorium given to the General Directorium on the 10th of the month, in which the Governing Commission names expressly His Excellency Dembowski as having the right of a seat in the Directorium, as presiding in the Treasury Administration; besides that we looked into the establishment of that same Administration, in which it is set down that His Excellency Dembowski by his office is to sit in the Directorium

[p. 323]

; clear then was the will of the Government in this. We read next the resolution of the Governing Commission permitting the Prince Director of War his departure, given on the 11th of the month, and then, the day after, the commissorium given to the Directorium; yet we found a great difference: there Mr. Dembowski is expressly named to sit, here Mr. Kamieniecki is the Deputy of the Director of War, but with two officers added; and three may not be admitted into the Directorium. We resolved then to await in this the speedy and express will of the Governing Commission, whether Mr. Kamieniecki is to sit among us as Deputy of the Director of War, or not; for we will by no means allow ourselves beyond the power granted, and the public cause does not suffer by it, for a full complement is always prescribed.

Łubieński.

I ask for the most gracious approval; it will be enough, it seems to me, to write at the side: approved.
