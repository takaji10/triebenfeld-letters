Two special situations required a separate decision: when an old settlement received Olęder rights, and when single-homestead settlements were called olędry. In the first case, despite the absence of a new location in the material sense, there arose, in the legal sense, a new Olęder settlement, and from then on it must be assigned to such unconditionally. Single-homestead settlements, however — whose number is, moreover, small 1 and which are usually treated unclearly in the sources — do not belong to the Olęder settlements. For here the coming into being of the conditions deciding their classing among the Olęder settlement is not possible. These are usually villages located on the principles of German law, and they acquired the name of olędry in some sources because they arose in the early modern period together with the Olęders 1, either in the surroundings of Olęder settlements, or else they were settled by settlers coming from those settlements.
