ANNEX II — Sig.: AW. Kliny Ol., pow. Poznań-Wschód, CG. 3, Jol. 46 5I. -

Charter for the leaseholders of the Kicin estate complex

Actum feria quarta post Dominicam, ramas Palmarum die scilicet 1l-ma mensis Aprilis Anno 1781. [Done on the fourth day after Palm Sunday, namely on the eleventh day of the month of April.]

The copy of the law of the village of Czerwonak, of the Czerwonak Olęders, of the Olęders on the Kliny and of those dwelling at the Hamer mill, and of the rent-payers at the Mchowo mill, written by the hand of His Worthiness the Reverend Mr Józef de Bończa Miaskowski, Provost of Poznań Cathedral; given in writing to that whole village community and confirmed with his armorial seal in red wax, of which law and of the articles expressed in it the tenor is as follows:

Yielding to the petitions of our faithful subjects paying rent in the Kicin estate complex, and in particular of the leaseholders of the village of Czerwonak, thirteen in number, likewise of the Czerwonak Olęders, eleven in number, as also of the Olęders on the Kliny, ten in number, likewise of the leaseholders at our Hamer mill, three in number, and of those rent-payers settled on our lands at the Miechowo mill, four in number — that to the former, in particular to the village of Czerwonak and to some of the Czerwonak Olęders, laws had been granted by our predecessors, that is: the Reverend Mr Paweł Łodzi Poniński, suffragan and Provost of Poznań Cathedral; likewise His Serene Highness Prince Cardinal Lipski, formerly Provost of Poznań Cathedral and later Bishop of Kraków; the Reverend Mr Wawrzyniec z Wybrarowa Świniarski, Canon of Poznań Cathedral and for that time administrator of the provost's estates; the Reverend Mr Konstanty Moszyński, Bishop of Inflanty [Livonia] and Provost of Poznań Cathedral; the Reverend Mr Józef Tadeusz z Kirska Kierski, Bishop of Boliń and suffragan, Provost of Poznań Cathedral — while to the latter, in particular to the Olęders on the Kliny at the Hamer and at the Miechowo mill, after the expiry of the years of freedom granted by the Reverend Mr Antoni Onufry z Okęcia Okęcki, then Bishop of Poznań and Chancellor of the Crown, formerly Provost of Poznań Cathedral, a share in that same

[p. 146]


law had been bestowed — to all whom it may concern we make known that, having before our eyes the law granted by our predecessors to some of the leaseholders of the Kicin estate complex, we have, in accordance with this, for all the others mentioned above, after measuring out their hubas, granted and written out the law.

First

Establishment of the village office and its duties. Having assembled on the feast of the Three Kings, the communities of the Kicin estate complex, in the village of Czerwonak and at the former sołtys's, are to elect from among the farmers of that village, the most long-standing rent-payers, a sołtys, whose term of office is to last for [printed: from] three years; and if he should prove remiss in anything or wanting in vigilance, and in particular should transgress this present law, and just complaints against him should come from the communities to the manor, then, with the permission received from the manor, they may proceed within the three years to the election of another sołtys. On the same feast the communities are also to elect two ławniks in that village, one from the Czerwonak Olęders, the other from the Kliny Olęders; one of them must at least know Polish and German [printed: 'po niemieckich', recte: 'po niemiecku']; and if within those three years one of these officials dies or sells up, the elder of those remaining is to summon the community at any time to elect another. The duty of these two ławniks will be to come to Czerwonak to the sołtys on every court day and then, together with the sołtys, to hear the complaints that a neighbour may have against another from whichever community, and to punish the guilty party according to the articles of the law written out below. At the same time it will also belong to this office to see to the public roads, the dams and the ditches, to give one another notice in good time of any meadow tax or dues, likewise to collect the taxes from the communities and to pay them over to whom they are due, to take the receipts for what has been paid and to keep them in the chest of the community treasury.

If, however, some new case should call them out beyond those court days — whether by order of the manor or by summons of the sołtys — the ławniks must appear at once and deal with the matter for which they have been summoned. If this village office (into which it should diligently inquire) sees that any of the rent-payers is slothful in the management of his holding or is letting his buildings go to ruin, then, after a triple [warning], it is to assess his huba and his buildings and see to finding a buyer.

Second

Establishment of the community chest.

It is to this end that people join together, so as to be of use to one another; unforeseen events reduce even the mightiest men to nothing and

[p. 147]


to poverty; it is therefore the most just of things that the communities should have their own secure treasury, in which in genuine misfortune they might find help. Accordingly every one of the rent-payers, of all the communities in general, will be bound to pay without fail into the community chest, on every feast of St Wojciech, into the hands of a ławnik or of the sołtys, one grzywna, that is one złoty and fifteen copper groszes; in case of non-payment of that grzywna he will incur the penalty of paying two grzywnas. This chest is to remain with the sołtys under two different locks, for which the ławniks are to have the two keys, but no one may open it without the other and without the sołtys. If, however, one of them should fall ill at the time and not be at home when the chest needs to be opened, the key is then to be entrusted to the senior farmer; and on every feast of St Wojciech, when the communities are called together, this chest is to be counted over, and it is never to be lent to anyone without the summons of at least ten farmers and without an evident [cause]; and even then for no longer than half a year, except in case of fire, for which money may be borrowed from the chest for up to three years, and after those years repaid without interest.

Penalties for offenders

If any insubordination against the authority of the manor, or of the sołtys or of the ławniks, should appear in any of the rent-payers, or he transgresses even the smallest point of this ordinance, then, once this is proven to him, he is to pay three grzywnas into the community chest, that is four and a half złotys. If a neighbour does damage to a neighbour in the field or in the barn, or causes an uproar, a quarrel or plain abuse of the house, or in an inn — God forbid — a beating (this is to be understood as applying to outsiders as well), and the matter is brought before the village court, then the wrong done to the injured party is to be made good as soon as it can be assessed; as in cases of quarrels and abuse, that is three grzywnas for the injured party, and the offender is to give one and a half to the chest. If on a Sunday or on a holy day ordered for public observance in the Kingdom of Poland any of the rent-payers presumes to go to town to sell or to buy, and as a Catholic fails through laziness to be found in his parish church at Kicin, he is to be fined two grzywnas to the community chest. If anyone — God preserve him — is careless with fire in his house, as by carrying a burning splinter about the yard or the buildings, or using a candle without a lantern, or not keeping a chimney or ladders, or failing to sweep out the soot on a Saturday or a Friday, or drying splinters at, and yet not properly on, the household oven, he is to be fined two grzywnas to the chest; and if from such an occasion a fire of any kind should break out, the injured party is to pursue the wrongdoer for damages on his estate. Should God permit an unlucky fire at any of the farmers', then anyone who is lazy about helping to save the property and does not come with water incurs a fine of three grzywnas; 10*

[p. 148]


but whoever comes first to the rescue with water, or in any other unhappy accident comes to the aid of life or limb, is to be paid six grzywnas by the community chest without delay as recompense, and the village court is to see to this most solemnly. None of the rent-payers remaining under this present law in the Kicin estate complex is to contract a debt upon his huba and buildings from anyone without first declaring it in the village court and receiving written permission from the manor; this permission he must in turn lay before the village court, and only then, if the court judges that there is genuine need of it, is it to accept it; and if the court sees that there is no just reason for contracting the debt, or that the borrower is already heavily indebted, the manorial permission is to be carried back to the manor and the reasons for non-acceptance laid before it, which the manor is to accept as just; otherwise the lender will have no claim against the debtor. Every one of the rent-payers is to take care that all his children, or at least one of them, especially the sons, can read, write and count; whoever neglects this is to pay three grzywnas to the chest.

|

Any leaseholder in the Kicin estate complex who intends to sell up must first notify the village court, and it the manor; only after express permission for this has been received, and after the seller has deposited thirty złotys and the buyer the same sum, will he be free to leave his holding — but not before the seller and the buyer alike have each paid three Polish złotys into the community chest.

The dues of the leaseholders of the Kicin estate complex.

Every leaseholder (except those who may hold separate charters) of the Kicin estate complex is to pay into the lord's treasury on every feast of St Martin fifty Polish złotys, 50 zł, counting thirty copper groszes to the złoty, and two wierts of oats; the wiertel is to hold sixteen garnce of the new measure. From the other lordly dues — hens, sheep, spinning quotas, nuts, mushrooms, watch duty — they are freed for ever. Excepted from this common point applying to the other leaseholders are the three leaseholders at the Hamer, who are to pay rent, without the other lordly dues and even without oats, sixty-six Polish złotys. Every one of the leaseholders of the Kicin estate complex, unless he sits under a separate charter, is to go out ploughing three times a year, that is for the root crops, for the fallow and for the winter sowing, and on each occasion he is to plough eight rods, i.e. 8; the rod is to hold seven and a half ells of the new measure. They are also to do nine days' hand labour, that is three in the garden, three with the sickle and three with the scythe. From all the other labour dues, such as the carting of manure from the provost's curia and the carting of wood there, they are freed; in return they are to receive each year six sądki [small casks] of beer of the new measure — three at Czerwonak with their Olęders, two at Kliny, one at the Hamer and the Janikowo Olęders.

[p. 149]


The carting of grain, over a distance of at most six miles, they are to perform once a year; provided, however, that no more than four korcy of the new measure, that is eight wierts, may ever be loaded on to one waggon. If the manor has no need of this, then instead of the cartage over six miles he is to do a day's ploughing and break up and plough eight rods. Each of the leaseholders is to perform one road cartage of at most six miles, and if this is not needed, he is to go out ploughing for one day, eight rods. The Czerwonak Olęders are freed from the grain cartage and from the rod-ploughing of the cartage duty; in return they are to go out ploughing two days a year, eight rods each. For the brewing of beer at the Kicin brewhouse they are to take turns, prepare and cart the wood for it, and carry out the malt and bring it back. All manner of help in building — that is, in bringing timber, stone, brick, lime, clay and sand, in raising buildings and in daubing — they owe in turn. Church dues, as well as taxes and instalments of whatever kind, now or in future, each is to hand over to the sołtys, and he is to deliver them where they are due.

They are to keep the ditches (so that the water has its fall), the dams by the mills and the public roads in the best possible condition and repair them when called upon to do so by the sołtys. As to the laying waste of the Kicin forests, they shall not only not cut there themselves (apart from on their own morgs), but the seizure of what they detect being felled shall be their duty.

So that everyone may know and understand all this, when the community has assembled twice a year at the village court, that is on St Martin's day and on St Wojciech's day, at the sołtys's at Czerwonak, it is to be read out aloud and freely.

Since it is the honour of our leaseholders of the Kicin estate complex that is at stake, the last point concerns theft, namely that if there should appear the least sign not only of actual theft but of complicity with a thief, such a person, as an enemy of the common good, is to be expelled without delay; his estate having been sold, the injured party is to be compensated as the case shows, ten grzywnas are to be paid into the community chest, and the remainder given to the culprit.

So that these our present provisions may carry the greater weight, we append them with our own hand together with the impression of the seal. Given at Kicin on the second day of February of the year one thousand seven hundred and eighty-one.

Józef Bończa Miaskowski, Provost of Poznań Cathedral, mpp.

I certify the corrections made above, so that they may not fall under any suspicion that they were made by my own hand. Datum ut supra [as above].

Miaskowski Kat. Poznański mpp.

ANNEX III

Wilkierz for the Kotusz Olęders. Sig.: Kotusz A. 1 (spelling corrected).

[fol. 5] The laws of Kotusz, from the lords.

Given [1] and confirmed to the Olęders. I give notice and make known to each and every one, and to whomever it is needful to know this: whereas I have sold my hereditary lands named Kotusz to good honest people, and to their children and descendants, for a sum, and have set them the said lands at a fixed yearly rent, yet these inhabitants live without order and without obedience to the laws; therefore, so that in the times to come there may be among them order, justice, peace, concord and obedience, I have judged it well to give and appoint to them the law and articles, fines and penalties described below, which both parties alike are bound to keep and observe as strictly as possible on pain of their own penalty. For the sake of better order the neighbours shall come together and elect for themselves a sołtys and ławniks for the year, and whomsoever they choose I shall always confirm. They are to be such persons from the community as will look after the good of the whole village and of their common property; they are also to judge and settle whatever disputes may arise, according to this law and my direction, strictly to uphold it, to collect and take the fines from anyone who offends against this law, and to render an account of them to the neighbours. I order that the said fines be paid into the official chest, which chest is to be kept at the sołtys's. The ławniks are to have a key to this chest. They are not to spend the fine money on gatherings of any kind, but are to apply the fine money to the good order and benefit of the village, as for instance the dams, the ditches, the causeways and whatever may be useful to the community; this is to be done with the knowledge of the neighbours and of the whole community. The laws and articles set out below are to be read to the whole community twice each year, so that no one can excuse himself out of ignorance when he offends against the law. These are the articles:

1. Whenever the sołtys commands or forbids anything, they are to obey.

If anyone, however, stays at home and does not carry out the obedience owed to his sołtys, he is to be fined five groszes.

[p. 152]


2. If a neighbour addresses the sołtys or his ławniks in discourteous or abusive words, or threatens them with a weapon, and when he comes to court refuses to listen, he is to be fined two good grzywnas without any [exception]. If he then behaves worse still, or threatens blows, he is to be sentenced by their judgement to a heavier penalty.

3. But if anyone boldly and violently attacks the sołtys or his ławniks, and having given rein to his anger [fol. 6] refuses to obey, or will not submit, then whoever beats or wounds him in that event, by day or by night, goes free and incurs no penalty, nor is any court needed.

4. Whoever is sent by the sołtys on official business, and anyone presumes to abuse or beat him, shall be fined two good grzywnas.

5. When the sołtys holds court with the ławniks, no woman is to be present, unless she herself has some complaint of her own, or her husband is not at home. Otherwise a woman who offends in this is to pay a fine of five groszes.

6. I permit that each year they are to pay the sołtys thirty groszes from every huba for his trouble and toil, and his ławniks fifteen groszes each year. Whoever opposes this is to be punished in duplo [twice over].

7. If the sołtys and the ławniks make any journey on the community's business and for the good of their estate, the costs are to be taken from the hubas according to the account.

8. The sołtys and the ławniks are to watch carefully over the good of the whole village and community, to take care of the fences, dams, ditches and sluices, and wherever harm might come to the community, to block, repair and put it right. If the sołtys and the ławniks are negligent in this, they are to be punished at the discretion of the whole community.

9. When there is any purchase of grain or of cattle, and a sealing drink (litkup) has already been drunk at the purchase, the party who does not keep the bargain is to give a barrel of beer as a penalty.

10. God forbid that a building should catch fire and burn down through a thunderbolt or through the doing of wicked men; the neighbours are then to help him out of Christian charity — each huba contributing, at 100 grzywnas, the 15th grosz, according to the assessment of the damage — and they are to help him with timber, reed and carting.

11. God forbid that a fire from carelessness should break out in the village, in one of the houses: any neighbour who will not come to the rescue and to the extinguishing is to be [fined] three good grzywnas.

12. Whoever at a fire takes someone else's axe, hatchet, weborek [?], hoe or any other utensil belonging to another, is to bring those things to the sołtys and deposit them with him, so that they may be returned to their owner, on pain of a fine of one good grzywna.

[p. 153]


13. No one is to presume to go and challenge another before his porch or his door. Whoever does this incurs a fine of three good grzywnas.

14. If anyone presumes to waylay another on the road, in the field or in the village, and this can be proved by two reputable witnesses, he is to be punished according to the assessment of the seriousness of the matter. [fol. 7]

15. If one neighbour reviles another, or women abuse one another with unseemly words, to the damage of their honour, and there are two steady witnesses, they are to be fined two good grzywnas; the unseemly words [are to] be proved, or honour made good, at the discretion of the court.

16. One neighbour is to help another with manure and water in the building and fitting out of his farmyard, when this is needed. Whoever will not do this is liable to a fine of one good grzywna.

17. If a neighbour opens an enclosure or a fence and does not close it again, so that any damage results, he is to make good that damage and pay a fine of one grzywna.

18. If anyone drives across another's sown field, he is to bear a fine of one grzywna and make good the damage.

19. If the sołtys orders the pastures to be cleaned, the boundaries to be finished, the outflow of the waters to be cleaned and sluiced, and whatever ditches may be useful to the village, whoever does not obey this order and does not do his part is liable to a fine of two grzywnas and must complete the said work within a week, on pain of a double penalty.

20. One neighbour is to keep and maintain the boundaries of another, whether ditches or fences, as the place requires. Whoever does not do this, if damage results, is to make good and pay for the damage; and whatever complaint there may be, ten groszes always fall to the sołtys.

21. The proper boundary is to be as follows: a ditch is to be half a rod or a laska wide, and two ells deep. A fence or enclosure is to be two ells high, so that a half-year-old calf cannot climb over it. Moreover, if there is in the village a horse or horned cattle that spoil and jump the fences, this is to be prevented, or such cattle disposed of. There are to be thirty rządek or rods of farmyard, with the dwelling, the garden and the stables for the cattle, pigs, hens, geese and ducks, well provided.

22. If anyone's horse, ox or cow is stolen, the neighbours are to take pains that the theft is disclosed and found out.

[p. 154]


23. No one is to presume to refuse and take away labourers or hired men who have been engaged, without the consent of the man for whom they work, unless they are already released after payment. Whoever presumes to do this will be liable to a fine of one good grzywna. 4fol. 8]

24. Whoever presumes to refuse a neighbour a farmhand or a maidservant, or to buy them off, shall be fined one grzywna, and is to send the farmhand or the maid back to serve their former master.

25. If anyone impounds a neighbour's cattle to its harm, he is to water and guard it, so that it does not suffer or die. Whoever is the cause of this is to bear the damage.

26. When anyone's cattle is impounded, the sołtys has notice given to the owner of the cattle; when he leaves his cattle in the pound, he is to pay for the first night five groszes a head, for the second night ten groszes a head, and so on up to the fifth night, always doubling. If after the fifth night the cattle is not collected and redeemed, it is to be driven to the lord's farmyard.

27. No one is to presume to drive a neighbour's cattle into his own house; it is to be handed over at once to the sołtys or a ławnik, on pain of a fine of one grzywna.

28. The sołtys is entitled to a third part of the redemption money for impounded cattle.

Whoever has no money to redeem the cattle is to find those who will stand surety for him, and leave a twofold pledge, to the sołtys's satisfaction.

29. If anyone, when his cattle is impounded and driven to the sołtys, opposes this and wants to take the cattle back by force, he is to be fined two good grzywnas.

30. If anyone beats impounded cattle or throws things at it, so that the animal suffers harm by it, he is to make good the damage and is to be fined three good grzywnas.

31. No one is to milk impounded cattle, or make use of impounded horses, or ride on them. Whoever contravenes this is to be fined one grzywna.

32. When someone finds another's cattle in his corn or in his meadow, he is to have the ławniks inspect and assess the damage; if he will not have this done, he is to be content with the customary redemption money, namely a grosz a head of cattle. For the inspection he is to pay the ławniks twelve imperial groszes, which they are to take on every occasion.

33. If the sołtys orders the pigs to be hobbled (klockować), and anyone neglects this, so that damage results to a neighbour, he is to take [recte: give] 5 groszes a pig for the impounding.

[p. 155]


[fol. 9] 34. Geese and ducks, when they stray in and do damage to a neighbour, may be killed and sent back home to their owner.

35. If anyone during a court session hisses at another in discourteous words or plays a trick on him, he is to pay a fine of five groszes; if he threatens blows, he is to go into obedience and pay 10 groszes. And he is not to be released from obedience any sooner until he has come to an agreement with his neighbour and apologised.

36. The wiertel, the quarter and the beer measure are to be measured out fairly according to the measures of the neighbouring towns, so that no one suffers wrong. Whoever acts against this and cheats his neighbour is to be punished at the discretion of the sołtys and the ławniks.

37. No one is to presume — and this I do not allow — to take a smallholder (zagrodnik) or a cottager, that is a lodger, into his own house, or settle him on his land, without the knowledge of the whole neighbourhood, on pain of a barrel of beer.

38. If there is need, for the better good of the village, to make a dam with a sluice, or ditches for draining off the waters, or to provide the fields with dams, the neighbours are to contribute to it according to their hubas; I on my side promise to bring in the neighbouring villages to help. Whoever opposes this is to be punished at the discretion of the sołtys and the ławniks.

39. The sołtys and the ławniks are obliged every two weeks, on a Tuesday, to hold court for the neighbours, hear complaints, settle disputes and collect the fines as diligently as possible; only, one party is to give notice and instruction to the other a day before the court sitting; and for an outsider justice is to be done at any time, on payment of the official fee.

40. Before the court sitting the complainant is to deposit twelve imperial groszes, of which four imperial groszes belong to the sołtys.

41. And this I reserve to myself: that the sołtys is to keep good watch that criminal cases and fines belonging to the lord's court are not concealed. These are always to be brought before me, and such matters must be reported to me. As for the other cases, I entrust them to the sołtys for the better judgement of the village; for wounding and beating [with] marks, eight imperial groszes are always to be paid to the sołtys, besides the amount due to his ławniks.

42. If someone complains before the sołtys and his ławniks and wishes to take his case to the lord, he must be allowed to do so, and this is [fol. 10] ordered; but if anyone wants to appeal presumptuously and insolently, he is to be fined one grzywna.

43. If one neighbour wishes to sell another an estate or property of any kind, he must first notify the sołtys

[p. 156]


and not sell it secretly, so that no one leaves debts behind him. Whether it is

under the lord or under the community, the buyer is always to give the neighbours a barrel of beer when he buys. And his own kinsmen, who are always the nearest to the purchase, are also to give the neighbours a barrel of beer. — 44. If anyone wishes to buy another person's huba or anyone's estate, he must first give due notice at the sołtys's, with a barrel of beer; if there is a neighbour who can buy that huba, and such a person is found [in] the village, he is to have precedence in the purchase of this huba and be admitted to the purchase; but the neighbour who borders on the huba being sold has the nearer right of purchase. If there are kinsmen from that huba, they are the nearest to receive the huba being sold, and they are not to be barred from it in any way. Whoever opposes this is to pay to the lord a fine of ten good grzywnas, and to the neighbours, for this presumption, a barrel of beer by way of penalty.

I therefore earnestly command the sołtys and the ławniks to hold firmly to the said law and its penalties and to keep them, on pain of severe penalty from the manor. This statute and constitution is to be kept and carried out as diligently as possible; I permit no one to act against these laws. For surer assurance and confirmation I seal with my own natural seal knowingly and sign. Given in my hereditary estate of Żerków.
