4. Communal self-government. a) Organisation. The broadly developed communal self-government constituted, as we have mentioned, an essential feature of Olęder settlement. In principle every settlement formed a separate unit of self-government. In exceptional cases two or even three settlements could have self-governing organs in common.[^50-2] The constitution of communal self-government in the Olęder settlements presented itself differently from that in the remaining villages. This concerned above all the organs of self-government. These organs consisted of an elected sołtys[^50-3] and ławniks. Their term of office lasted one year. Quite exceptionally in the Radoszkowskie Olęders the sołtys, and in the Uścikowskie Olęders the whole village bench, was elected for life, while the common sołtys of the Czerwonackie, Janikowskie[^50-4] and Kliny Olęders was elected for a three-year period. The choice both of the sołtys and of the ławniks was made by the assembly of all the farmers of the community. Only in the Karpickie Olęders did the choice of the ławniks belong to the sołtys. The ławniks were most often 2 in number,[^50-5] more rarely 3,[^50-6] exceptionally 4. In the same composition the substitutes were appointed. As a rule the choice of the organs of self-government fell at Whitsuntide, at times, however, the term of office ended at St Martin's (11 November) 5, or St John the Baptist (24 June) 8. After the expiry of the period of freedom the communal authorities were bound to report to the manor the names and surnames of all the settlers.[^51-6] To them also belonged the duty of watching over the boundaries between[^51-5] the lands of the settlement and the neighbouring lands.

The communal judicature comprised above all cases of offences against the provisions of the wilkierz and the administrative orders of the communal organs, further all other cases (quarrels, brawls, thefts, boundary disputes) apart from matters of greater importance reserved to the lord. To the competence of the village court belonged also cases of offences against the provisions of the location charter.1 It is possible that the village court also performed the functions of an arbitration court. According to the charter for Borui the village judges "will look most diligently to order, security[^53-1] and tranquillity, indeed to success, order and justice, and will apply all diligence and earnestness[^53-2] of their own to it". The punishment applied by the court was the fine, and in exceptional cases arrest 3. From the judgments of the village court the condemned always had an appeal[^53-3] to the manor. So that the means of appeal should not be abused, the wilkierze provided that "whoever appeals out of spite and needlessly, groundlessly troubling the lord, shall be punished double".[^53-4] While in the sphere of administration the sołtys usually had wider competences than the remaining communal organs, in the panel of judges every member had equal rights.[^53-5] An accused member of the court was judged by the remaining sworn members in the same way as any other inhabitant of the settlement. The wilkierze defined the judicial procedure in detail. In Góździn the sittings of the court were to take place every 2 weeks, in the Jastrzębnickie Olęders every 4 weeks, on a Sunday. The members of the community were bound to appear at every summons of the court, to give testimony and to respect unconditionally all the other judgments and orders issued by the court. Special police provisions were designed to secure the safety and dignity of the court during the hearings. Women as a rule had no procedural capacity, nor could they be present at the place where the court sat. Exceptionally they could appear in court when their own affairs were being decided and their personal appearance was necessary. Also by way of exception they could substitute for their husbands in urgent matters, in the event of their absence or grave illness. But if they presumed to hurl insults before the court, a severe punishment awaited them. For their administrative and judicial acts the communal organs received a certain remuneration, usually from the community, exceptionally from the lord. This remuneration comprised certain fixed annual fees (from the farm 2[^54-2], or from the unit of land[^54-3]), further fees for the individual complaints (Klugegeld).[^54-4] The costs of the journeys of the sołtys or the ławniks on the community's business were borne by all the farmers proportionally. In the Stare Oberskie Olęders the lord exempted each sołtys for the time being from all labour dues 5.[^54-5] c. The communal treasury, insurance against fire. The revenues from the administrative fees and judicial fines formed the communal chest. The receipts were kept in a special box kept by the sołtys, of which the ławniks held the key,[^54-6] or, which happened rarely, the lord.[^54-7] Before the expiry of the term of office each sołtys with the ławniks made up the annual accounts, either before the whole community or at the manor. The revenues from the judicial fines usually fell in their entirety to the lord;[^54-8] at times, however, the lord reserved to himself only a part of the revenues,[^54-9] or else applied them to some definite purpose, e.g. the maintenance of the ditches within the settlement.[^54-10] It is possible that in the communal chest there were also kept, where they existed, funds for the maintenance of the communal herdsman, the night watchman, the teacher or the lector. The chest revenues could also be applied to replenishing the so-called fire chest. Such a chest was founded on the lord's order, among others, in Ol. Radoszkowskie.[^54-11]
