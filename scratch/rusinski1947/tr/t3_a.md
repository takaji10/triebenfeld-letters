B. The religious composition of the Olęder population

We possess no source statistics from which the religious composition of the population of the Olęder settlements could be reconstructed; certain general conclusions can be drawn only on the basis of the loose, chance mentions and data found in the sources. The question of the religious affiliation of the Olęders had already been discussed by German authors; a few, contrary to the obvious, tried to maintain that the whole Olęder population was Evangelical,1 but the majority admitted,[^27-1] that a certain percentage of the Olęders belonged to the Roman Catholic confession. The question therefore reduces to establishing what part of the population of the Olęder settlements was made up of Catholic settlers. The sources show that the population of the settlements founded in clerical estates on the territory of the Poznań voivodeship was always Catholic. Important conclusions concerning the religious relations of the Olęder settlements are suggested by the social stratification of the population of the settlements. As follows from Table IV, about 36% of the population consisted of landless people, who for settling in the settlement needed neither an individually expressed consent of the lord nor material means for redeeming themselves and establishing a household. On the other hand, on[^28-2] the basis of settler registers sometimes inserted in charters, and of inspections of one and the same settlement carried out at different times, it can be established that the settlers did not bring with them the needed contingent of hired labour forces, but hired them as the farm grew and the demand for labour increased. It is most probable that the Olęders sought such labour forces in the nearest neighbourhood. This met with no obstacles of a legal character, owing to the fact that in the villages of Wielkopolska there existed a number of labourers who were not subject (the free, contract workers), whom the Tol. even groups[^28-3] into the categories: "the free, occupied with work" and "beggars". Hence it must be accepted that, especially in settlements located among Polish villages, the poorer, landless population was Polish and Catholic. C. The nationality of the Olęder population

Determining the ethnic affiliation of the Olęders presents very great difficulties. Direct source information in this respect is almost entirely lacking.[^29-2] For contemporaries this question had no significance whatever in view of the complete equality of rights enjoyed by both nationalities. Indirect indications are supplied to us by the surnames of settlers given from time to time.

The national character of the Olęder settlement on the territory of the former Poznań voivodeship was not uniform throughout the whole duration of the colonization. It cannot admit of any doubt that the first settlers were authentic Hollenders.[^29-3] They were settled in the oldest settlement on the territory that interests us, namely in the Olęders of Ujście (Olędrowie), and in the four oldest settlements of the Wieleń estate: Folsztyn, Herbardowo, Marianowo and Nowe Dwory.
