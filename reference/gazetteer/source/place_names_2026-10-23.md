**Adamierz**—Village south of Wrąbczynkowskie Holendry. First mentioned as *Nadamirza* in a 1377 document where Palatine Przecław of Kalisz and others protested to Louis I of Hungary that Wrąbczynek with the adjoining village of Adamierz belonged to Ląd Abbey.[^1] Mentioned as *Nadomirz* in a 1440 document where Władysław III bequeathed Stanisław the Black 500 grzywny from Adamierz and other villages.[^2] Known in metrical records as *Yadomierzh’ (Ядомиржъ)* during the Russian period (ca. 1868-1915).

*Adelhof/Adelów, Kolonia*—See **Chruściki**.

*Adolfsberg, Kolonia*—See **Zalesie**.

**Anielewo**—Village southwest of Łukom. Originally named *Kolonia Ingelfingen* and founded as a German colony by Friedrich Ludwig, Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen and Öhringen (1746-1818) who named it after his family name. Renamed to Anielewo in 1891 as part of efforts by the Polish authorities to remove traces of previous Prussian occupation.

*Augustinhof, Kolonia*—See **Augustynów**.

**Augustynów**—Village south of Zagórów. Originally named *Kolonia Augustinhof* and founded as a German colony by Friedrich Ludwig, Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen and Öhringen (1746-1818) who named it after one of his children. Augustinhof was established on March 21, 1806, when the Trąbczyn Patrimonial Court issued an agreement between Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen and eighteen German colonists to settle on 100 *Hüfen* (766 ha, or 7.7 km2) of woodland on the Drzewce estate.[^3] Renamed to Augustynów in 1891 as part of efforts by the Polish authorities to remove traces of previous Prussian occupation. *Hol. Augustinow* (Reymann 1834).

**Bartosz**—Stream or canal running between Osiny and Łukom. The same waterway may have been noted in a 1775 court dispute between the owners of the Trąbczyn and Łukom estates, where the flow of water had been blocked by the people of Łukom and had caused flooding on land belonging to Trąbczyn.[^4] Breyer noted a stream running through the Łazińsk settlement which extended to Pyzdry via Łukom and Bukowe, and stated that it was called *Graben* (“stream” in English) by the local people.[^5]

*Beźsług—*Tavern which existed north of Łazińsk Pierwszy. Possibly depicted as an unlabeled tavern east of Trąbczyńskie Stare Olędry (Gilly 1802); *Beźsług* (Richter 1843).

*Bieganszczyzna, Osada*—Settlement which existed east of Wrąbczynkowskie Holendry. A forestkeeper’s house (Pl. *leśniczówka*) was located nearby in the forest next to the settlement. Only one map noted these features (Richter 1843).

*Biskupice*—Tavern which existed south of Trąbczyńskie Stare Olędry, at the meeting point of the Trąbczyn, Biskupice, and Łukom estates. Appeared (erroneously?) north of the southern part of Trąbczyńskie Stare Olędry (*Biskupski*, Perthées 1798); *Biskupicer Kr\[ug\]* (Gilly 1802); labelled as a tavern and field belonging to Biskupice \[estate\] (Pambo 1803); *Biskupie* (Chrzanowski 1858).

**Budy**—Hamlet north of and belonging to Grądzeń. *Budy do Huty* (Łukom 1766); *Budy Łukomskie* (Hollring 1804); *Lukamske Budy* (Richter 1843); as part of Huta Łukomska (Słupca 1957).

**Bukowe**—Village east of Łukom. The name is an adjective for beech trees (*Buk*). According to Chodyła, *Bukowo*/*Bukowe* (which may have existed previously as a medieval-era village) or *Olędry Łomowskie* was established on the initiative of the owner of the Łukom/Łomów estates before 1789,[^6] either through a change in its legal status or by clearing land which had been long-since abandoned.[^7] It is illustrated as an Olęder settlement on a sketch of Zagórów parish from circa 1790 by Karól Perthées (Fig. 74\).[^8] *Bukower Hol.* (Gilly 1802).

*Chełmskie Olędry*—See *Lusnie Olędry*.

**Chruściki**—Village east of Kopojno. Originally named *Kolonia Adelhof* and founded as a German colony by Friedrich Ludwig, Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen and Öhringen (1746-1818) who named it after one of his children. Renamed to Chruściki in 1891 as part of efforts by the Polish authorities to remove traces of previous Prussian occupation.

*Cygany*—Eight mounds which existed on the southwest edge of the Trąbczyn estate. The mounds were mentioned in a 1775 court dispute between the owners of the Trąbczyn and Łukom estates as being built illegally and secretly made at night to influence the demarcation of the border between the two estates.[^9] Only one map noted this feature (Królików 1800, see Fig. 56). 

**Czarna Struga**—River flowing into the Warta River and running approximately from north of Zagórów, east to Chruściki, and then south alongside Mariantów, Stanisławów, Szetlewek, and continuing south to Grodziec. A hammer mill (Ger. *Frischfeuer*) existed along the Czarna Struga river south of Mariantów and was depicted on Gilly 1802\.

**Czarnybród**—Village south of Łazińsk Drugi in Grodziec Gmina. Czarnybród was once an Olęder settlement which likely belonged to the Biskupice estate. Notes on the Grodziec Catholic parish taken by geographer Karól Perthées mentioned a possible alternative name of Czarnybród as *Stary Młyn*.[^10] *Czarnybrod Hol.* (Gilly 1802); *Kol. Czarnybrod* (Richter 1843).

**Czerwonka**—Hamlet south of and belonging to Łazy. The Geographical Dictionary of the Kingdom of Poland in 1892 listed Grabina and Czerwonka as the same place (pl. “*Grabina al\[bo\] Czerwonka*”),[^11] though the two places are in separate locations on modern-day maps. Appeared as a folwark (Kozłowski 1861).

**Długa Górka**—Isolated dwellings south of and belonging to Wrąbczyn. Possibly appeared as *Siodmagorka* (Gilly 1802); *Osada Dlugagórka* (Richter 1843).

*Drommin*—See **Trąbczyn**.

**Drzewce**—Village south of Zagórów. First mentioned in a privilege document from Ląd Abbey in 1362.[^12] Drzewce (*Drzewycz*) was mentioned as an alias of *Nouo Sagorowo* (Pl. *Zagórowo Nowe*) in 1411, when the monastery’s abbott Gerhard exchanged a scullery in Drzewce with a scullery in Kownaty.[^13] Possibly mentioned in a 1418 document concerning the abbey’s trading of Wrąbczyn to Laurencius Yelonouicz in exchange for Drzewce (*Drzewcza*), although the source expressed uncertainty as to which Drzewce was being referenced. The document stated that Drzewce had been purchased by Yelonouicz from the monastery’s previous abbot Heinrich.[^14] Breyer noted that the estate was parceled around 1900 and that German settlers claimed some of the land.[^15] *Drziwza* (von Pfau 1770).

*Drzęzgi*—Water mill which existed south of Maryantów. First mentioned in a 1630 marriage record from Trąbczyn Catholic parish metrical records.[^16] Only one map noted this feature (Reymann 1834).

*Dziadolina*—Locale which existed south of Łomów. First mentioned as a forest in a 1775 border commission report which stated that *Trąbczyńskie Stare Olędry* was established on part of the Dziadulina forest on the Trąbczyn estate.[^17] Listed as a settlement (Pl. *osada*) in the Geographical Dictionary of the Kingdom of Poland in 1881[^18] and shown on Trójwiorstówki 1914 as *G\[uta?\] Diadolina*. *Dziadowy* (Gilly 1802); as a forest called *Dziadulina* (Kozłowski 1861).

*Emilienheim/Emilienhain, Kolonia*—See **Michalinów Oleśnicki**.

*Friedrichsfeld, Kolonia*—See **Tarszewo**.

*Garbin*—West of Stanisławów. Listed as a village in the Geographical Dictionary of the Kingdom of Poland in 1881.[^19] Only one map noted this feature, as a field (Kozłowski 1861).

*Glinianki*—Isolated dwellings northwest of Grabina. Only one map noted this feature (Pełzucki 1896).

**Grabina**—Isolated dwellings north of and belonging to Łazińsk Pierwszy. The Geographical Dictionary of the Kingdom of Poland in 1892 listed Grabina and Czerwonka as the same place (pl. “*Grabina al\[bo\] Czerwonka*”),[^20] though the two places are in separate locations on modern-day maps. *Osada Grabina* (Richter 1843).

**Grądzeń**—Village south of Łukom. Mentioned as *Pustkowie Grądziem* in a 1461 privilege document from Ląd Abbey, which was sold by the German Bochneth family to the monastery for the sum of 400 Cologne marks.[^21] *Pustkowie Grądziem* included three farms in Łukom, the forests of *Grądziem*, *Chmielniki*, and *Łome* (the latter forest which extended up to the road known as *Rosaszewska* that ran from Łukom and Łomow to the inns known as *Karczemne Gory* and *Karczemne Ługi*), the Pyzdry crag/cliff, and the meadow above Imielno.[^22] *Grązien* (Łukom 1766); *Pustkowie Grądzin* (Perthées 1798); *Grondzyn* (Gilly 1802); *Haulanderei Grądzyn* as part of the Kopojno estate (Hollring 1804); with a customs office as *Grazin* (Richter 1843); *Hol. Grądzeń* (Chrzanowski 1858).

**Grądzeń-Las**—Hamlet south of and belonging to Grądzeń. *Las* is “forest” in Polish and likely refers to the name of the forest outside Grądzeń. First appeared on Messtischblatt 1940\.

*Hagrowo*—Unknown place. Mentioned in a population table of Zagórów Catholic parish from 1790-91.[^23]

*Hinterberg*—See **Zagórów**.

**Huta Trąbczyńska**—Hamlet west of and belonging to Łazińsk Drugi. First mentioned in a 1736 marriage record from Trąbczyn Catholic parish metrical records.[^24] According to a modern-day sign at the Lutheran cemetery, there was a glass factory here in the 18th and 19th centuries. Huta was sometimes referenced as an Olędry in the same metrical records in the early 1820s, and also sometimes as *Stara Huta*. *H.\[olendry\] Stara Huta* (Richter 1843); *Stara Huta* (Chrzanowski 1858); *Trąbczynska-Huta* (Messtischblatt 1940). Also known as *Huta Łazińska* and *Huta Trąbczyńska*.

**Huta Łukomska**—Village south of Łukom. Mentioned as *Huta* in a 1761 compromise decree from Ląd Abbey concerning the borders between the estates of Łukom and Drzewce.[^25] According to Chodyła, Huta Łukomska (then known as *Stara Huta*) was established as a settlement before 1789 (likely before 1766, as it was included on Łukom 1766\) on the initiative of the owner of the Łukom/Łomów estates as part of a village of mixed legal and organisational status that included Myszakówek and *Zagórowskie Olędry*.[^26] Occasionally referenced without the *Łukomska* name, making it difficult to distinguish in metrical records between Huta Trąbczyńska. *Huta* (Łukom 1766); *Stara Huta* (Gilly 1802); *Huta Lakomska* (Richter 1843); *Stara Huta* (Chrzanowski 1858).

*Imielanka*—Settlement which existed north of Łukom. Only one map noted this feature (Richter 1843). Possibly *Kol. Imielno* (Messtischblatt 1940).

**Imielno**—Village north of Łukom. First mentioned as *Chemelino* in a 1243 document where Przemysł I, Duke of Greater Poland donated a hospital (almshouse) in Gniezno to “the rule of the Holy Sepulchre of the house of Miechov.”[^27] The hospital owned several villages, including Imielno. Mentioned in a 1461 privilege document for *Pustkowie Grądziem*.[^28] According to Chodyła, Imielno was re-established as *Imielno Olędry* on the initiative of the owner of the Łukom/Łomów estates before 1789,[^29] either through a change in its legal status or by clearing the land which had been long-abandoned.[^30] This appears likely as Łukom 1766 showed this place as *Dezerta Iemielno* (*dezerta* is Polish for an abandoned place). *Iemielna* (Richter 1843).

*Imielno Olędry*—See **Imielno**. 

*Ingelfingen, Kolonia*—See **Anielewo**.

*Jeziorko*—Folwark which existed southwest of Zagórów. Today part of the southwest outskirts of Zagórów. Only one map noted this feature (Richter 1843).

**Kirchol**—Isolated dwellings south of and belonging to Kościołków. First appeared on Messtischblatt 1940\.

*Koczogowa*—Water mill which existed east of Myszaków. Possibly located at Łukom, which was recorded as having a mill (Gilly 1802\) and was mentioned in a 1775 court document concerning a property dispute between the Trąbczyn and Łukom estates.[^31] Only one map noted this feature (von Pfau 1770). 

**Kopojno**—Village east of Zagórów. First mentioned as *Kopanino* in a 1240 document where Archbishop Fulco of Gniezno conferred the tithes from seven villages to Ląd Abbey.[^32] Mentioned in a 1291 privilege document from Ląd Abbey which stated that Count Sendivogius traded Kopojno (*Copaino*) to the abbey in exchange for Główiew, Sławsk, and Chocz.[^33] *Kopaino* (von Pfau 1770); *Kopanie* (Perthées 1798); with a windmill (Gilly 1802); *Kopiec* (Chrzanowski 1858); with a manor house (Wreschen 1901).

**Kopojno-Parcele**—Hamlet south of and belonging to Kopojno. Likely appeared in the later half of the 20th century (not present on Słupca 1957).

**Kościołków**—Village south of Wrąbczyn. *Koscialkowo* (Gilly 1802); *Kosciółki* (Reymann 1834); *Kościołków* (Zagórów 1841).

**Koszelewska Łąka**—Village southwest of Łukom. Founded between 1794 and 1800 as the last village established in the Zagórów Evangelical-Augsburg parish under Olęder law.[^34] An inn was noted here (Weydner 1804). Remains of a Lutheran cemetery still exist but are poorly preserved. *Kozialewskalonka* (Gilly 1802); *Hol. Koszelewska Ląka* (Richter 1843); *Kozialąka* (Chrzanowski 1858).

*Kowaliska*—Location unknown. Listed as an area or subdivision of the Trąbczyn estate by The Geographical Dictionary of the Kingdom of Poland in 1892.[^35]

**Królestwo**—Hamlet south of and belonging to Łomów. Likely appeared in the later half of the 20th century (not present on Słupca 1957).

*Kuzoi*—Unknown place which existed south of Myszaków. Only one map noted this feature (von Pfau 1770).

*Laski*—Folwark which existed west of Trąbczyn. Only one map noted this feature (Kozłowski 1861).

**Łazińsk Drugi**—See *Łazińskie Olędry.*

*Łazińskie Olędry—*Olęder settlement which existed south of Łazy. Created after the amalgamation of Trąbczyńskie Stare Olędry and Trąbczyńskie Nowe Olędry. First mentioned in Trąbczyn Catholic parish metrical records in 1822\. At some point in the later half of the 20th century, Łazińskie Olędry was separated into the villages of Łazińsk Pierwszy (the area which contained Trąbczyńskie Stare Olędry) and Łazińsk Drugi (the area which contained Trąbczyńskie Nowe Olędry). *Lazinskie Hol.* (Reymann 1834); *Hol. Lazinskie* (location erroneously switched with *Michalinów Olędry*) (Richter 1843); *Łazinskie \[Kolonie\]* (Kozłowski 1861); *Łazińska Kolonja* (Messtischblatt 1940 and Fig. 75).

**Łazińsk Pierwszy**—See *Łazińskie Olędry*.

**Łazy**—Village south of Trąbczyn. According to Breyer, *Łazy* referred to a cleared marshy area (Ger. *Brücher*).[^36] Mentioned at least as early as 1597 in metrical records from Trąbczyn Catholic parish.[^37] In 1791, Łazy was inhabited by 48 people of Catholic faith.[^38] Chodyła claimed that the village of Łazy changed its legal status at some point to an Olędry[^39] on account of a Prussian register of houses and population from 1797 which listed the settlement as “*Łazy Haul\[endry\]*” with 42 inhabitants.[^40] *Lase* (von Pfau 1770); *Lasy* (Trąbczyn 1790).

**Łomów**—Village south of Łukom. First mentioned as *Łomowo* in a 1461 privilege document where Ląd Abbey received Łomów for *Pustkowie Grądziem*.[^41] *Łomaw* (Łukom 1766); *Lumowo* (von Pfau 1770); *Lumow* (Perthées 1798); with a windmill (Gilly 1802); *Łamowo* (Reymann 1834).

*Łomówskie Olędry*—See **Bukowe**.

*Ludwigslust, Kolonia*—See **Mały Las**.

**Łukom**—Village southwest of Zagórów. First mentioned as *Lukome* in a 1240 document where Archbishop Fulco of Gniezno conferred the tithes from seven villages to Ląd Abbey.[^42] Łukom was also an estate owned by the Ścibor-Chełmski family roughly from the 15th to 19th centuries. In 1884 it was 204 *włóka* (5,850 morgen or 33 km2) in size and included the settlements of Łukom, Łukomska Huta, Bukowe, Imielno, Małgorzatów, Myszaków, and Adamierz.[^43] *Lukom* (Łukom 1766); with a chapel and water mill (Gilly 1802); with a tavern (Reymann 1834); with a manor house (Richter 1843). Also see *Koczogowa*.

*Łukomskie Olędry*—Olęder settlement which existed west of Łukom. Not to be confused with the short-lived Lusnie/Łukomskie Olędry which existed on the southwest corner of the Trąbczyn estate from 1771-1775 (see *Lusnie Olędry*). First appeared on Perthées 1798; *Lukomer Holendry* (Gilly 1802).

*Lusnie Olędry*—Olęder settlement which existed in the southwest corner of the Trąbczyn estate in the Lusnie forest from 1771 to 1775. A 1775 border commission report detailed a dispute between the estates of Trąbczyn and Łukom, which involved an inn and twelve Olęder farms that were illegally taken in 1771 by Stanisław Chełmski, owner of the Łukom estate. While the farms had originally been part of Trąbczyńskie Stare Olędry, over the next five years the area became known as *Lusnie Olędry*, *Łukomskie Olędry*, and *Chełmskie Olędry*. In 1775, Chełmski was forced to return the land to Antoni Prusimski, then owner of the Trąbczyn estate. According to the border commission report, Trąbczyńskie Stare Olędry was founded in the forests of *Miąsykąt*, *Dziadulina*, and *Lusnie*.[^44] The location of Lusnie Olędry was not recorded on any known maps, with the exception of a map which was drawn for the 1775 border commission by “Reverend Benedict Woronowski, Doctor of Theology and Philosophy, and current Professor of Philosophy at the Academy of Poznań,”[^45] which has not survived.

*Łutow—*Possibly a site of windmills which existed south of Łukom. Only one map noted this feature (Richter 1843, Fig. 76).

*Maly*—Settlement that existed west of Anielewo (Ingelfingen). Likely part of Anielewo. Only one map noted this feature (Reymann 1834).

**Małgorzatów**—Hamlet east of and belonging to Myszaków. First appeared on Richter 1843\.

**Mały Las**—Hamlet south of and belonging to Olchowo. The German colony *Kolonia Ludwigslust* was likely integrated into the smaller Mały Las settlement immediately south in 1891 as part of efforts by the Polish authorities to remove traces of previous Prussian occupation. A hand-drawn map from the 1990s by descendants/former residents of Olchowo listed them as two separate places (Map 26, Tab. 26). First appeared on Messtischblatt 1940\. The settlement map found in the Lutheran church in Zagórów included a list of settlers, along with the text: “These are the names of the families who lived here as the last generation. The numbers are randomly assigned and are not identical to the official house numbers.”

*Maradz—*Forest southwest of Stanisławów. Appeared as a forest (Kozłowski 1861\) and as a settlement (Messtischblatt 1940).

*Maradz Olędry—*Olęder settlement which existed southwest of Stanisławów. First mentioned in a 1808 birth record from Trąbczyn Catholic parish metrical records.[^46]

**Mariantów**—Village east of Łazy. A wrought iron workshop (Ger. *Eisenhammer*) and a watermill were located in the same vicinity on Gilly 1802\.

*Miąsykąt*—Forest located in the southwest corner of the Trąbczyn estate. Exact location unknown. Mentioned in a 1775 border commission report which detailed a boundary dispute between the estates of Trąbczyn and Łukom. According to the source, Trąbczyńskie Stare Olędry was founded in the forests of *Miąsykąt*, *Dziadulina*, and *Lusnie*.[^47]

*Michalinów Olędry*—See **Michalinów Trąbczyński**.

**Michalinów Oleśnicki**—Village southwest of Trąbczyn. Originally named *Emilienheim* and founded as a German Olęder colony by Friedrich Ludwig, Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen and Öhringen (1746-1818) who named it after one of his children. Renamed to Michalinów Oleśnicki (as it was part of Oleśnica Gmina) in 1891 as part of efforts by the Polish authorities to remove traces of previous Prussian occupation.  *Hol. Emilienhain* (Reymann 1834).

**Michalinów Trąbczyński**—Village southwest of Łazy. Michalinów Trąbczyński was first mentioned as *Cholędry Michalinowo* in a 1809 birth record from Trąbczyn Catholic parish metrical records.[^48] Possibly named after Michalina Prusimska (1778-1851), who was owner of the Trąbczyn estate in the first half of the 19th century. In the 20th century, Michalinów Olędry became known as Michalinów Trąbczyński. *Michalinowo* (Colberg 1827); *Hol. Michalinowo* (with its location erroneously switched with Łazińskie Olędry on Richter 1843 and Slupetsk 1800); *\[Kolonia\]* *Michalinow* (Kozłowski 1861); *Michalinów* (Messtischblatt 1940).

**Myszaków**—Village east of Myszakówek. According to Chodyła, Myszaków and Myszakówek were simultaneously established before 1789 by the owner of the Łukom estate.[^49] *Miszokowo* (von Pfau 1770); with a windmill (Wreschen 1901).

**Myszakówek**—Village east of Łukom. According to Chodyła, Myszakówek was established as an Olędry[^50] before 1789 (however, it does not appear on Łukom 1766\) on the initiative of the owner of the Łukom/Łomów estates as part of a village of mixed legal and organisational status that included *Stara Huta* and *Zagórowskie Olędry*.[^51] First appeared on Perthées 1798; as *Myszakowko* (Gilly 1802).

*Noga—*Forest northwest and southeast of Huta. Listed as a forest settlement (Pl. *osada leśna*) with one house and five inhabitants in the Geographical Dictionary of the Kingdom of Poland in 1881.[^52] Only one map noted this feature (Kozłowski 1861).

**Nowa Wieś**—Village west of Trąbczyn. Mentioned as early as 1597 in Catholic metrical records from Trąbczyn parish.[^53] According to a court document from 1775, Nowa Wieś was also known as *Trąbczyn Mały*.[^54]

**Olchowo**—Village northeast of Zagórów. Originally named *Kolonia Sophienthal* and founded as a German Olęder colony by Friedrich Ludwig, Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen and Öhringen (1746-1818) who named it after one of his children. Renamed to Olchowo in 1891 as part of efforts by the Polish authorities to remove traces of previous Prussian occupation. *Hol. Sophienthal* (Reymann 1834).

**Oleśnica**—Village east of Zagórów. First mentioned as *Oleszyno* in a 1261 document where Prince Bolesław the Pious confirmed the earlier 1145 founding privilege for a monastery in Ląd made by Mieszko III and donated additional villages including Oleśnica.[^55] First appeared with a windmill (Gilly 1802); with a manor house (Richter 1843); renamed to *Erlenbruch* during Nazi Germany occupation (Hohensalza 1942).

**Oleśnica-Folwark**—Hamlet southeast of and belonging to Oleśnica. Likely the site of a previous folwark. Likely appeared in the later half of the 20th century (not present on Słupca 1957).

**Osiny**—Village west of Łazy. First mentioned in a 1606 marriage record from Trąbczyn Catholic parish metrical records as *Osynÿ*.[^56] Mentioned as a mill in a 1784 court document which challenged the inheritance of the Trąbczyn estate and other assets by Antoni Prusimski (1744-1806).[^57] *Oszin* (von Pfau 1770), *Ossiny* (Perthées 1798).

*Ostrowiec*—Forest near Włodzimirów. Mentioned in a 1775 border commission report which detailed a boundary dispute between the estates of Trąbczyn and Łukom.[^58] Noted as far east as Koszelewska Łąka on Weydner 1804 as “*Gegend der Ostrowiec”* (Area of Ostrowiec), but it is unclear if this was referring to the forest.

**Podbiel**—Village east of Trąbczyn. In 1775, Podbiel and the nearby village of Szetlew were mentioned as being owned by Jan Nepomucen Zakrzewski, the son of Marianna Prusimska who was sister to Paweł and Antoni Prusimski, owners of the Trąbczyn estate in the 18th century.[^59] Depicted as a folwark on Wreschen 1901\.

**Porąbki**—Hamlet east of and belonging to Łazy. First mentioned in a 1776 birth record from Trąbczyn Catholic parish metrical records.[^60]

*Posada*—Location unknown. Listed as an area or subdivision of the Trąbczyn estate by The Geographical Dictionary of the Kingdom of Poland in 1892.[^61]

*Potaszniki*—Location unknown. Mentioned in Trąbczyn Catholic parish metrical records in the latter half of the 18th century. In one case, a record from 1768 referred to this place as “Potaszniki v\[el\] Huta” in Latin (“Potaszniki or Huta” in English).[^62] Possibly named after a local noble(?) family with the same last name (*Potasznicki*) as seen in metrical records.

**Przybysław**—Hamlet south of and belonging to Nowa Wieś. First mentioned in an 1813 birth record from Trąbczyn Catholic parish metrical records.[^63] Appeared as a folwark on Richter 1843; similarly on Pełzucki 1901\.

*Przycierz—*Folwark which existed east of Trąbczyn. Only one map noted this feature (Kozłowski 1861).

**Pyzdry**—Town in Września Gmina west of Zagórów. Mentioned as early as 1232 when the army of Duke Henry I the Bearded entered Greater Poland. The Lisewo-Pyzdry branch of the newly-formed Zagórów Evangelical-Augsburg Lutheran parish was established here in 1842\.

*Rosciszewo*—Folwark which existed south of Łomów in the forest on the Łukom estate. Likely named after the Rościszewski family. Around 1740, Jakub Rościszewski married Helena Zielonacka who was the owner of the Łukom estate after the death of her spouse Józef Ścibor-Chełmski.[^64] *Rosciszew* (Łukom 1766); *Rostrziczewo* (von Pfau 1770); as part of the Łukom estate (Królików 1800); *Vorwerk Rosciszew* (Pambo 1803); *Roscisewo* (Richter 1843).

**Rydlowa Góra**—Earthen mound north of Zagórów. Once a historic settlement built around the 9th/10th century. Appeared on Messtischblatt 1940\.

**Ryjew**—Hamlet southeast of and belonging to Grądzeń. *Ryiewo* (Reymann 1834).

*Sadornicz—*Unknown place which existed between Wrąbczynek and Myszaków. Only one map noted this feature (von Pfau 1770).

*Siergiejewo*—See **Wincentowo**.

*Siodmagorka*—See **Długa Górka**.

*Sklanna Huta*—Settlement which existed west of Łomów, possibly in the same location as Budy. *Huta* was a common Polish name for a place where iron ore was found. Only one map noted this feature (Gilly 1802).

**Skokum**—Village east of Zagórów. First mentioned as *Skokome* in a 1240 document where Archbishop Fulco of Gniezno conferred the tithes from seven villages to Ląd Abbey.[^65] Mentioned in a 1261 document where Prince Bolesław the Pious confirmed the earlier 1145 founding privilege for a monastery at Ląd made by Mieszko III and donated additional villages including Skokum.[^66]

*Skokamskie Olędry*—Olęder settlement which existed north of Skokum. First appeared on Reymann 1834\. *Skokamskie* *Olędry* did not appear on Richter 1843 and may have been amalgamated into Kolonia Sophienthal or Kolonia Ludwigslust.

**Smoleniec**—Hamlet east of and belonging to Koszelewska Łąka. May have been a place where tar was made (Pl. *smolarnia*).[^67] *Smoliniec* (Perthées 1798).

*Sophienthal, Kolonia*—See **Olchowo**.

**Stanisławów**—Village west of Szetlewek. Named after Stanisław Miączyński (1780-1845), whose wife Michalina Prusimska (1778-1851) was owner of the Trąbczyn estate. Appeared as a folwark (Wreschen 1901); *Stanisławów* (Messtischblatt 1940).  

*Stara Huta*—See **Huta Łukomska**.

*Stara Huta Olędry*—See **Huta**.

**Stawisko**—Isolated dwellings southeast of and belonging to Łomów. A significant swamp crossing existed west from here called *Brod Kaliski* and was shown on a map of the Łukom estate from 1766 (Łukom 1766). Appeared as a folwark (Richter 1843). In Polish, a *stawisko* is described as a large mud pond, or an area formerly occupied by a pond.[^68] The low-lying, marshy area north of Stawisko was being used for peat cuttings as late as the 1960s (Army Map Service 1962).

**Studzianka**—Village south of Stawisko. Mentioned as the location of the corner mounds between the estates of Trąbczyn, Łukom, and Biskupice in a 1775 border commission report detailing a dispute between the owners of the Trąbczyn and Łukom estates.[^69] *Studzienka* (Łukom 1766).

**Szetlew**—Village southeast of Łazy. First mentioned as *Szetlevo* in a 1240 document where Archbishop Fulco of Gniezno conferred the tithes from seven villages to Ląd Abbey.[^70] Mentioned in a 1261 document as “*Szethowo cum cocis”* (Latin for “Szetlewo with cooks,” also mentioned as such in 1280[^71]) where Prince Bolesław the Pious confirmed the earlier 1145 founding privilege for a monastery at Ląd made by Mieszko III and donated additional villages.[^72] In 1589, Nicolaus Otto-Trąmpczyński, the owner of Szetlew, was sued by other members of the Otto-Trąmpczyński family for destroying their nearby mill by diverting its watercourse and blocking the road to the mill.[^73] The most recent version of the mill was built circa 1910-1912 by W. Hauke and ceased operations in 1979.[^74] In 1775, Szetlew and the nearby village of Podbiel were mentioned as being owned by Jan Nepomucen Zakrzewski, the son of Marianna Prusimska who was sister to Paweł and Antoni Prusimski, owners of the Trąbczyn estate in the 18th century.[^75] *Szetlewo* (Perthées 1798).

**Szetlewek**—Village east of Stanisławów. Szetlewek, or *Szetlewo Kątne*[^76] was first pawned (used as a collateral for a loan) to Jan Goliński. Later in 1671, the property was fully sold to Goliński by the heirs of former owner Martin Strzałkowski. It eventually descended by hereditary succession to Krzysztof Prusimski and his wife Teresa Łoś-Golińska, as well as Łoś-Golińska’s mother Konstancja Krusińska and her husband Marcin Katowski.[^77] Mentioned as *Szetlewko* in a 1685 marriage record from Trąbczyn Catholic parish metrical records.[^78] Sebastjan Szetlewicz from Szetlewko was mentioned in Kalisz municipal court records from 1727.[^79] *Szetlewka* (von Pfau 1770); *Szelkowek* (Trąbczyn 1790); *Szedlewek* (Gilly 1802).

*Tarczewo*—Folwark which existed south of Kopojno. Only one map noted this feature (Reymann 1834).

**Tarszewo**—Village northeast of Kopojno. Originally named *Kolonia Friedrichsfeld* and founded as a German colony by Friedrich Ludwig, Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen and Öhringen (1746-1818) who named it after one of his children. Renamed to Tarszewo in 1891 as part of efforts by the Polish authorities to remove traces of previous Prussian occupation.

*Tarszew*—Water mill which existed south of Kopojno. First mentioned in a 1742 birth record from Trąbczyn Catholic parish metrical records.[^80] *Kolonia Friedrichsfeld* took the name Tarszewo in 1891 as part of efforts by the Polish authorities to remove traces of previous Prussian occupation. First appeared on Trąbczyn 1790; *Tarszew M.* (Gilly 1802); *Osada Tarszew Ml. \[Młyn\]* (Richter 1843).

**Trąbczyn**—Village southeast of Zagórów. First mentioned as *Trampczino* in a 1240 document where Archbishop Fulco of Gniezno conferred the tithes from seven villages to Ląd Abbey.[^81] Trąbczyn (*Trupcino*) was given to Count Nicholas, treasurer of Ląd in 1276 by Duke Bolesław V the Chaste after the former owner Count Ianchoni, son of Simon, died without an heir.[^82] Nicholas was granted permission to lease Trąbczyn (*Tropcssino*) by Przemysł II in 1284.[^83] Trąbczyn was owned by the brothers Johannis and Przedpełk, and Andreas in 1346.[^84] The village was also mentioned in 1399 (as *Trambczyno*) when the Archbishop of Gniezno ruled that the tithing of a portion of crops from Trąbczyn were to be given to Ląd Abbey, instead of to the village’s rector Johannes.[^85] The Geographical Dictionary of the Kingdom of Poland in 1892 stated that Trąbczyn had a wooden parish church, an elementary school, a post office, distilleries, an oil mill, a brickyard, and a windmill.[^86] In the early 20th century, Trąbczyn was surrounded by a folwark with the same name as the town (Pełzucki 1901). Renamed to *Drommin* during Nazi Germany occupation (Hohensalza 1942\) and was part of *Amtsbezirk (District) Drommin*.[^87] *Trąmbczino* (Blaeu 1665); *Trompzin* (von Pfau 1770); *Trąpczin* (Perthées 1798); with a windmill and chapel/church (Gilly 1802).

**Trąbczyn B**—Hamlet west of and belonging to Michalinów Trąbczyński. Appeared in Zagórów Evangelical-Augsburg Lutheran parish metrical records around 1920.[^88] Likely created after the parcelling of Trąbczyn estate property in the late 19th century.

*Trąbczyn C*—Settlement with an unknown location. Appeared in Zagórów Evangelical-Augsburg Lutheran parish metrical records from 1919.[^89] Likely created after the parcelling of Trąbczyn estate property in the late 19th century.

**Trąbczyn D**—Hamlet southwest of Łazińsk Pierwszy. Appeared in Zagórów Evangelical-Augsburg Lutheran parish metrical records around 1920.[^90] Likely created after the parcelling of Trąbczyn estate property in the late 19th century. Breyer noted that Trąbczyn D was forest land that was initially part of the Trąbczyn estate, which was bought by a Jewish person and then sold to Germans 5-6 years before World War I. Breyer cited the *Bocheński, Kinast, Winterfeld, Retzlaff, Schwarz, Lipelt*, and *Schmechel* families living here around 1936.[^91]

*Trąbczyn F*—Locale which bordered the southern part of Trąbczyn. Likely created after the parcelling of Trąbczyn estate property in the late 19th century. Only one map noted this feature (Pełzucki 1901).

*Trąbczyn G*—Locale which existed northeast of Trąbczyn. Likely created after the parcelling of Trąbczyn estate property in the late 19th century. Only one map noted this feature (Pełzucki 1901).

**Trąbczyn Dworski**—Village northwest of Trąbczyn. Originally the location of a manor house. First mentioned in a 1795 birth record from Trąbczyn Catholic parish metrical records as *Trąmbczyn Dwor*.[^92] *\[Trąbczyn\] Dw. \[Dwór\]* (Richter 1843).

*Trąbczyn Mały*—See **Nowa Wieś**.

*Trąbczyńskie Nowe Olędry*—Olęder settlement which existed north of Czarnybród, located at Łazińsk Drugi. Possibly established in 1776 by Antoni Prusimski following the resolution of a dispute with Stanisław Chełmski, the owner of the Łukom estate concerning Trąbczyn’s southern forests. Chełmski lost a court case in 1775 and was forced to return the western portion of the Trąbczyn estate to Prusimski, including twelve Olęders who were living there. First mentioned as *Nowe Olędry* in Trąbczyn Catholic parish metrical records in 1776.[^93] This date coincides with the resolution of the dispute with Chełmski and may indicate that Prusimski proceeded to continue to develop the Trąbczyn estate, having been assured of his continuing ownership. Trąbczyńskie Nowe Olędry was merged with Trąbczyńskie Stare Olędry to create Łazińskie Olędry, which first appeared in Trąbczyn Catholic parish metrical records in 1822\. *Neue \[Trombczyner Holendry\]* (Gilly 1802).

*Trąbczyńskie Stare Olędry*—Olęder settlement which existed southwest of Trąbczyn, located at Łazińsk Pierwszy. Established as the first Olęder settlement on the Trąbczyn estate in 1746 by Paweł Prusimski and his wife Katarzyna Rozdrażewska on the forests of Miąsykąt, Dziadulina, and Lusnie in the southwest part of the Trąbczyn estate.[^94] Trąbczyńskie Stare Olędry was merged with Trąbczyńskie Nowe Olędry to create Łazińskie Olędry, which first appeared in Trąbczyn Catholic parish metrical records in 1822\. First appeared as an unlabeled *Hollendry* (von Pfau 1770); *Holendry Lusnia Stara* (Trąbczyn 1790); *Holendry Stare Trąbczynskie* (Perthées 1798); *Alte Trombczyner Hol\[endry\]* (Gilly 1802).

*Trzcianne*—Field which existed north of Osiny. Only one map noted this feature (Kozłowski 1861).

**Warta River**—River running east to west along the northern border of Zagórów Gmina.

*Wielki-Bór Obręb*—Historical forest precinct which existed southwest of Łazińsk Pierwszy. The precinct included the districts (Pl. *okręg*) of *Studienka,* and *Wypalanki*. Only one map noted this feature (Kozłowski 1861).

**Wincentowo**—Village northwest of Łazy. Previously known as *Siergiejewo*, and listed as part of Osiny and containing five households in the Geographical Dictionary of the Kingdom of Poland in 1889.[^95] The same source also stated that both Siergiejewo and a folwark with the same name were established recently at the time of writing. The name change may have occurred in the early 20th century after the decline of Russian influence on culture and language in Poland. Only one map noted this feature as *Siergiejewo* (Wojskowy Instytut Geograficzny 1933). First appeared as *Wincentowo* on Messtischblatt 1940\.

**Włodzimirów**—Village south of Michalinów Oleśnicki. Named after Count Włodzimierz Miączyński (1811-1879), owner of the Trąbczyn estate. According to Breyer, Włodzimirów was established on German-owned land.[^96] First mentioned in an 1826 Trąbczyn Catholic parish metrical record.[^97] Appeared as a folwark (Richter 1843); *Kolonia Włodzimirów* (Pełzucki 1901). 

*Włodzimirow Obręb*—Historical forest precinct which existed east of Włodzimirów. The precinct included the districts (*okręg*) of *Grabina* (north of Grabina), *Garbinck* (northwest of Stanisławów), and *Osiny* (southwest of Osiny). Only one map noted this feature (Kozłowski 1861).

**Wrąbczyn**—Village west of Zagórów. First mentioned as *Wronczino* in a 1240 document where Archbishop Fulco of Gniezno conferred the tithes from seven villages to Ląd Abbey.[^98] Wrąbczyn came under possession of the abbey in 1241, when Prince Casimir I of Kuyavia traded the village (“which once belonged to the huntsmen”) to Ląd Abbey in exchange for Koszanów (Fig. 78\).[^99] In 1287, Wrąbczyn (*Wroncino*) and Bieganin were traded by Wislai, son of Gerlacho, to Woislai, son of Goslai in exchange for the village of Niałek.[^100] It was later leased to Henry of Pyzdry by Przemysł II in 1291 and amalgamated with the village of Sal.[^101] Wrąbczyn was also leased to Count Wojsław in 1298.[^102] Władysław I Łokietek, noting “the excessive devastation and destruction we brought upon the house of Lendz of the Cistercian Order when we were with our army in Poland,” granted Ląd Abbey a water mill near Pyzdry on the Warta River in 1324 and also confirmed the abbey’s hereditary rights to Wrąbczyn (*Wronchyno*) “with the lake called *Seborowo*” (today a swamp north of Wrąbczyn) and other villages.[^103] The lake Seborowo was also mentioned as *Prziborowo* in a 1377 document, where it was stated as belonging to Wrąbczynek.[^104] In 1357, Ląd Abbey traded the village of Szczonowo to Zemka, spouse of Albert in exchange for one third of Wrąbczyn (*Magnum Wroenschino*).[^105] Parts of the village were also sold on two other occasions to Ląd Abbey, and various heirs recognised the monastery as the rightful owner between 1362-1364.[^106] The ownership of Wrąbczyn was disputed in 1384; despite Petrko of Lang’s claim, judge Nicolaus of Kalisz confirmed Ląd Abbey as the rightful owner.[^107] Wrąbczyn and its meadows called Obroczna and Dokolowka were again traded by Ląd Abbey in 1418 to Laurencius Yelonouicz in exchange for Drzewce (*Drzewcza*), although the source expressed uncertainty as to which Drzewce was being referenced.[^108] *Wromszin* (von Pfau 1770); with a windmill (Gilly 1802); with a manor house (Richter 1843).

**Wrąbczyn Górski**—Village northwest of Wrąbczyn. First appeared on Słupca 1957\.

**Wrąbczynek**—Village northwest of Wrąbczyn. First mentioned as *Parvum Vronchin* (Latin for “Small Wrąbczyn”)  in an 1173 document where Mieszko I granted the village to Ląd Abbey which included “salt, lakes, fishing, hunting, and other uses.”[^109] Wrąbczynek was traded in 1362 by Ląd Abbey to Casimir III the Great in exchange for part of the forest called *Czirnetz* near Kazimierz.[^110] Mentioned as *Wraczina* in a 1440 document where Władysław III bequeathed Stanisław the Black 500 grzywny from Wrąbczynek and other villages.[^111] Re-established as a settlement around 1770 by Szczepan Turna, starost of Pietrzyków.[^112] *Wromsziniek* (von Pfau 1770); with a windmill (Wreschen 1901).

**Wrąbczynkowskie Holendry**—Village west of Wrąbczyn. Established by Szczepan Turna, starost of Pietrzyków in 1781 on overgrown land with the aim of bringing the starosty (an administrative district governed by a royal official called a starosta) and the village of Wrąbczyn into good condition.[^113] In 1886, Wrąbczynkowskie Holendry consisted of 32 households and belonged to the Pietrzyków estate which also included the villages of Pietrzyków and Białobrzeg.[^114] *Holendry Wrąbczyneckie* (Perthées 1798).

*Wygoda*—Tavern which existed north of Łukom. Only one map noted this feature (Richter 1843).

**Wymysłów**—Isolated dwellings north of Zagórów. *Stary Przewoz Wymysław* (Perthées 1798\) (may have been an old river crossing point); as a tavern *Wimislaw* (Zagorowo 1799); with a tavern and folwark (Gilly 1802).

**Zagórów**—Town east of Pyzdry. First mentioned as *Zagorovo* in a 1240 document where Archbishop Fulco of Gniezno conferred the tithes from seven villages to Ląd Abbey.[^115] Mentioned in a 1261 document where Prince Bolesław the Pious confirmed the earlier 1145 founding privilege for a monastery at Ląd made by Mieszko III and donated additional villages including Zagórów.[^116] In 1407, Władysław II Jagiełło gave permission to Ląd Abbey to establish a city in the town of Zagórów and transfer it from Polish law to Magdeburg/Teutonic law. The city was made exempt from any jurisdiction of existing castellans, judges, and other authorities, which was taken over by city authorities. The right to hold a weekly open market was also granted.[^117] The parochial church in Zagórów was absorbed by Ląd Abbey in 1414 in agreement with Paul, the rector of the church. In return the rector was invited to join the monastery and was provided with clothing and a salary.[^118] Appeared with two windmills on Gilly 1802; with a customs office (Richter 1843); with four windmills (Reymann 1834); renamed to *Hinterberg* during Nazi Germany occupation (Hohensalza 1942).

*Zagórowskie Olędry*—Olęder settlement which existed north of Imielno. According to Chodyła, Zagórowskie Olędry was established before 1789 on the initiative of the owner of the Łukom/Łomów estates as part of a village of mixed legal and organisational status that included *Stara Huta* and Myszakówek.[^119] Only one map noted this feature (Gilly 1802).

**Zalesie**—Village northwest of Nowa Wieś. Originally named *Kolonia Adolfsberg* and founded as a German colony by Friedrich Ludwig, Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen and Öhringen (1746-1818) who named it after one of his children. Renamed to Zalesie in 1891 as part of efforts by the Polish authorities to remove traces of previous Prussian occupation.

*Zwierzyniec*—Folwark which existed east of Szetlewek. Only one map noted this feature (Kozłowski 1861).
