# Chronological Document Register — Trąbczyn / Łukomia Boundary Dispute

*Assembled from the Document Outline sections of the English translations in the project corpus. Each entry gives the archival reference, the date of the events recorded, the court, the parties, and the outline of the document's content, followed by a note of its principal cross-references to other corpus documents.*

## 1. 1589 — Flooding Damage Case

**File:** `Inscriptiones__relationes__decreta__inducta__1589__53_6_0_-_17__-_English_Translation.md`
**Archive ref.:** 53/6/0/-/17, f. 27
**Court:** Konin Land Court
**Date of events:** 1589
**Parties:** Nobilis Albertus Trąmpczyński Otha (plaintiff) vs Nobilis Thomas Łukomski (defendant)

**Content:** A citation for the second time after the penalty of contumacy. Łukomski had constructed a pond (with other neighbours), retaining water on the estate of Łukom, which flooded Trąbczyn, Nowa Wieś, and Osiny — killing standing timber of productive oak, beech, and ash, and destroying bee-trees.

**Cross-references:** The *stawisko* and its flooding effects on Trąbczyn ground recur in documents 6, 7, 8, and 10. The Trąbczyn woodlands of Osiny appear in documents 4 and 10.

## 2. 1641–1644 — Timber Felling and Boundary Notches in Łukom Woodland

**File:** `Decreta__inducta__1641-1644__53_6_0_-_36__-_English_Translation.md`
**Archive ref.:** 53/6/0/-/36, ff. 641v–642
**Court:** Konin Land Court
**Date of events:** 1641–1644
**Parties:** Honourable Jerzy Chełmski (plaintiff) vs Honourables Marcin, Wojciech, Władysław, and Andrzej Otto-Trąmpczyński brothers, and Anna of Słończyc their mother (defendants)

**Document Outline:**

- **Entry 1 — Trąmpczyński Concession** [f. 641v]: Decree of concession in favour of Chełmski against the Otto-Trąmpczyński brothers and their mother Anna, concerning eighty unlawful tree notches in the woodland of Łukom made after the manner of boundary marks; contumacy finding; return of service by the Royal Messenger.
- **Entry 2 — Similar [Action] Against the Same** [f. 642]: A parallel decree in the same matter concerning the unlawful felling of timber in the woodlands and forests of Łukom; damages assessed at three hundred Polish marks; remainder as per Entry 1.

**Cross-references:** The Chełmski family as heirs of Łukomia and the Trąmpczyński/Prusimski family as heirs of Trąbczyn are the principal parties in all subsequent documents. The theme of unlawful felling in the Łukom woodlands recurs in documents 3, 4, and 10. Jerzy Chełmski's successors are Franciszek and Józef Ścibor Chełmscy (document 3) and then Stanisław Ścibor Chełmski (documents 8–11).

## 3. 1728 — Decree of Location: Armed Invasions and Estate Dispute

**File:** `Decreta__inducta__1722-1780__53_6_0_-_40__-_English_Translation.md`
**Archive ref.:** 53/6/0/-/40, ff. 368, 372–373v
**Court:** Konin Particular Land Court
**Date of events:** 1727–1728 (adjourned from 1727; decided on the Monday after *Septuagesima* 1728)
**Parties:** Honourables Franciszek and Józef Ścibor Chełmscy, heirs of Łukomia (plaintiffs) vs Honourable Krzysztof Prusimski (principal defendant) and Antoni Prusimski his son, and various retainers and subjects of Trąbczyn (co-defendants)

**Content:** The court hears multiple grievances: arson of shingles and a dwelling; seizure of four plough-oxen; a violent armed invasion of Łukomia lands with seizure of hay and conveyance to Trąbczyn; and other transgressions. The court issues a **Decree of Location**, ordering a commissarial descent to the disputed estate: the parties are to convey the Commissioner Albertus Biskupski (chosen by mutual consent) and two arbitrators per side to the disputed estate on the Monday after the Sunday *Misericordiae*, to inquire into ownership and transgressions, conduct sworn inquiries through aged witnesses, and adjudicate all claims according to justice.

**Cross-references:** Krzysztof Prusimski and his son Antoni are the predecessors of the Antoni Prusimski who appears in documents 6–10. The Decree of Location is the earliest commissarial order in the corpus; the 1776 commission (document 10) is the eventual outcome of this line of proceedings. The glassworks (*Huta*) mentioned in this document recurs in document 4.

## 4. 1754 — Judicial Site Inspection of the Trąbczyn Estate

**File:** `Relationes-oblatae__protocollon__1754__53_17_0_-_Konin_Gr_136__-_English_Translation.md`
**Archive ref.:** Konin Gr. 136, ff. 121–123
**Court:** Konin Starost's Municipal Court (registration)
**Date of events:** 9–12 July 1754
**Parties:** At the instance of Katarzyna Prusimska, widow of Paweł Prusimski, life-tenant and dower-holder of the Trąmpczyn estate

**Document Outline:**

- **Section 1 — Building Survey** [ff. 121–121v]: A building-by-building inventory of the manorial and farm structures on the Trąbczyn estate and its adjacent villages — Nowa Wieś, Osiny, Łazy, and the *olędry* — dated 9 July 1754.
- **Section 2 — Tenant Enumeration** [f. 122v]: A listing of *dependent people* in Trąbczyn and adjacent villages (Osiny, Łazy, Nowa Wieś) by category: *full farmers*, *half-farmers*, *cottagers*, and *lodgers*.
- **Section 3 — Forest Testimony** [f. 123]: A signed witness statement by two noblemen, together with the Court Summoner Bartłomiej, reporting the extent of felling and selective logging in the Trąbczyn pine forests, dated 9 July 1754.
- **Section 4 — Registration Record** [f. 123 cont.]: A Latin *relatio* registering the present inspection in the Konin Starost's Municipal Court, 12 July 1754, at the instance of Katarzyna Prusimska, surviving widow and life-tenant of the Trąmpczyn estate.

**Cross-references:** The Court Summoner Bartłomiej of this document is the same Bartłomiej Szepczyński / Szeptycki who appears in documents 6, 7, 10, and 11 — a continuous presence across the entire period 1754–1782. The *Huta* (glassworks) consuming timber recurs from document 3. The forest testimony on selective logging directly informs the 1776 commission's treatment of the Trąbczyn woodlands (document 10).

## 5. c. 1762–1765 — Potash Dispute; Suspension Pending Boundary Demarcation

**File:** `Decreta__protocollon__1750-1765__53_6_0_-_45__-_English_Translation.md`
**Archive ref.:** 53/6/0/-/45, ff. 653v–654
**Court:** Konin Land Court (particular)
**Date of events:** Potash incident: 1761; this decree: c. 1762–1765 (exact session date not given; before the first Tribunal condescension decree of 1766)
**Parties:** Right Honourable Catharina de Rozdrazewska Prusimska, widow (plaintiff); Honourable Joseph Puchalski (plaintiff); Right Honourable Stanisław Ścibor Chełmski and Honourable Czajkowski (defendants/plaintiffs)

**Content:** A dispute over *potash* produced in 1761 on the contested boundary ground between Trąbczyn and Łukom. Chełmski intercepted potash being conveyed to Gdańsk near Pyzdry, placed it under arrest at a Pyzdry inn, but the potash-burners subsequently removed and conveyed it to Gdańsk. The court, finding that the boundary between the estates is still pending and undecided at the Crown Tribunal, **suspends resolution** of the potash claim until the estate boundary case is determined by demarcation, and orders the sworn inquiries to be extracted for the parties.

**Cross-references:** The Crown Tribunal case referenced here produces its first condescension decree in 1766 (cited in document 9). Stanisław Ścibor Chełmski is the same heir who appears as principal party in documents 8–10. The Pyzdry area recurs as a geographical reference throughout the corpus.

## 6. April 1767 — Embankment Raised at the Stawisko, Flooding Trąmpczyn Olędry

**File:** `Relationes__protocollon__1765-1767__53_17_0_-_Konin_Gr_114___297-298_-_English_Translation.md`
**Archive ref.:** Konin Gr. 114, ff. 297–297v
**Court:** Konin Municipal Court
**Date of events:** Holy Saturday (c. April 1767); *relatio* filed Wednesday after Easter 1767
**Parties:** At the instance of Right Honourable Antoni Prusimski of Kolno, heir of Trąmpczyn; report by Honest Bartłomiej Szepczyński, Royal Court Summoner, with noble witnesses Stanisław Lipiński and Józef Rudkowski

**Document Outline:**

- **Section 1 — Relatio** [ff. 297–297v]: A formal report recording a judicial site inspection of a newly raised *embankment* at the end of the *stawisko*, which had caused flooding across the Trąmpczyn estates, alleged to have been built by the people of the neighbouring estate of Łukom on the mandate of its heir.

**Cross-references:** The same embankment is inspected again in document 7 (August 1767). Bartłomiej Szepczyński carries this same role in documents 4, 7, 10, and 11. Józef Rudkowski appears as witness again in document 7. The embankment is demolished by Prusimski's men in document 8, and the *stawisko* features as a boundary landmark in documents 10 and 11.

## 7. August 1767 — Boundary Site Inspection of the Embankment in Trąmpczyn Woodland

**File:** `Relationes__protocollon__1765-1767__53_17_0_-_Konin_Gr_114___334-334v_-_English_Translation.md`
**Archive ref.:** Konin Gr. 114, ff. 334–334v
**Court:** Konin Municipal Court
**Date of events:** Friday after the Feast of Saint James the Apostle (c. late July 1767); *relatio* filed Thursday, Feast of the Transfiguration (6 August 1767)
**Parties:** At the instance of Right Honourable Antoni Prusimski of Kolno, heir of Trąmpczyn; report by Honest Bartłomiej Szepczyński, Royal Court Summoner, General, with noble witnesses Józef Gołembiewski and [Józef] Rudkowski

**Document Outline:**

- **Section 1 — Relatio** [ff. 334–334v]: A formal report recording a boundary judicial site inspection of an *embankment* raised in the woodland on Trąmpczyn ground near the boundary of the Łukomia estates, which was damming the pond and causing flooding of the grounds and meadows of four of the Trąmpczyn *olędry*. The closing notes that the same embankment, raised some years earlier by the heirs of Łukomia, had now been repaired.

**Cross-references:** Companion document to 6, recording a second inspection of the same embankment in the same year. The note that the embankment was "raised some years earlier" connects back to the 1589 pond dispute (document 1). Demolished by Prusimski's men in document 8 (December 1771). Bartłomiej Szepczyński and Józef Rudkowski both appear in document 6 as well.

## 8. 1771–1773 — Violence at the Lusnia Pine Forest and Olędry

**File:** `Relationes__protocollon__1768-1774__53_17_0_-_Konin_Gr_115__-_English_Translation.md`
**Archive ref.:** Konin Gr. 115, ff. 185v–245
**Court:** Konin Municipal Court
**Date of events:** December 1771 – January 1773
**Parties:** Right Honourable Stanisław Ścibor Chełmski (heir of Łukomia) vs Right Honourable Antoni Prusimski of Kolno, Starost of Niszczewice (heir of Trąmpczyn)

**Document Outline:**

- **Section 1 — *Oblata* of the Delivery of Possession of the Lusnia Pine Forest with the *Olędry*** [f. 185v]: A cross-reference entry noting the enrollment of the formal *delivery of possession* of the Lusnia pine forest and *olędry*, issued on behalf of Chełmski as heir of Łukomia.
- **Section 2 — Presentation of Weapons** [f. 186v]: Krzepczyński, on commission from Chełmski, presents to the court weapons seized from Chełmski's men during a violent incursion at the Lusnia pine forest on the immediately preceding Monday: four swords, one unsheathed blade, four spears, three long firearms, one brass-fitted firearm.
- **Section 3 — Judicial Site Inspection of the Demolition of the Embankment at Łukom** [f. 186v]: Filed on the same day. Witnesses saw the *embankment* dug up, fourteen structural components cut with axes, and the water and fish let out; at the innkeeper's cottage, eight loopholes cut in the walls and three windows shot through. Asserted to have been done by more than thirty Trąmpczyn subjects in Prusimski's presence and on his command.
- **Section 4 — Judicial Site Inspection of the Violated Tavern in Lusnia** [f. 226]: Filed Tuesday after Saint Andrew 1772. Three windows and four doors with iron hinges removed from the Lusnia tavern, taken to the Trąmpczyn estate by Lipiński, Radzewski, and others by Prusimski's mandate.
- **Section 5 — Protest (*Manifestatio*)** [f. 245]: Chełmski lodges a formal protest against Prusimski and his servants, on grounds that Prusimski permitted his steward Lipiński and chamber attendant Marzęcki to attack the tavern and *olędry* despite Chełmski's previously acknowledged possession by right of a prior *manifest*.
- **Section 6 — Judicial Site Inspection of the Lusnia *Olędry*** [f. 245]: Bikierski files a further *relatio*; records that the stove, three windows, four doors — newly repaired by Chełmski — were now chopped and cut out; chimney demolished; drinking vessels smashed. Committed 29 January (current year) by Lipiński, Marzęcki, and others.

**Cross-references:** The *delivery of possession* referenced in Section 1 is the formal instrument recorded in document 9. The embankment demolished in Section 3 is the same structure inspected in documents 6 and 7. The *manifest* cited in Section 5 is the one referenced throughout the 1766–1770 Tribunal proceedings cited in document 9. The 1776 commission (document 10) annuls the delivery of possession, finds the violent incursions proven, and awards default judgements against Chełmski in connection with subsequent contraventions.

## 9. November 1771 — Formal Delivery of Possession of the Lusnia Pine Forest and Olędry

**File:** `Relationes__protocollon__1771__53_15_0_-_Kalisz_Gr_414__-_English_Translation.md`
**Archive ref.:** Kalisz Gr. 414, ff. 402–403
**Court:** Conducted in the Lusnia pine forest; enrolled at the Kalisz Municipal Court
**Date of events:** Monday after the Feast of All Saints 1771 (delivery); Saturday before the Feast of Saint Martin 1771 (enrollment)
**Parties:** At the instance of Right Honourable Stanisław Ścibor Chełmski, heir of Łukomia; conducted by General Sworn Commissioner Wawrzyniec Wstowski; enrolled by Honourable Antoni Serlatkowski

**Document Outline:**

- **Section 1 — Delivery of Possession of the Lusnia Pine Forest and *Olędry*** [f. 402]: The formal *delivery of possession* of the Lusnia pine forest and woodland, and twelve named *olędry* with the tavern building and stabling, to Chełmski, based on five preceding decrees: (1) condemnation of Monday after Saint Bartholomew 1761; (2) Bydgoszcz Tribunal condescension decree of Tuesday before Saint Giles 1766 — ordering demarcation of the estates of Łukom and Trąpczyn; (3) Poznań Tribunal decree of Thursday after the Exaltation of the Holy Cross 1767; (4) Bydgoszcz Tribunal condescension decree of Monday after the Visitation of the Virgin Mary 1768; (5) condescension session of Monday after the Sunday *Cantate* 1770. The twelve *olędry* are named individually. The commissioner declares obedience to be rendered to Chełmski.
- **Section 2 — Enrollment at the Kalisz Municipal Court** [f. 403]: Filed Saturday before Saint Martin 1771; the original delivery document sewn into the court protocol.

**Cross-references:** This is the formal instrument of which an *oblata* is noted in document 8, Section 1. The five preceding decrees cited here provide the legal chain underlying the entire 1766–1771 phase of the dispute. Prusimski's own steward Józef Jaroszewski was present as a witness at the delivery, which the 1776 commission (document 10) subsequently finds to have been conducted unlawfully — without a writ of execution — and accordingly annuls.

## 10. September 1775 / September 1776 — Commissorial Boundary Decree (Principal Document)

**File:** `Relationes-oblatae__protocollon__1776__53_17_0_-_Konin_Gr_145__-_English_Translation.md`
**Archive ref.:** Konin Gr. 145, ff. 654–715
**Court:** Commissorial court constituted under the Warsaw Constitution of 1774; *oblata* submitted to the Brest Kuyavia Starost's Municipal Court on 23 September 1776
**Date of events:** Commission sits 13 September 1775 (decree date); oblata 23 September 1776
**Parties:** Honourable Antoni Prusimski, Starost of Niszczewice (heir of Trąbczyn Mały / Nowa Wieś); Honourable Stanisław Chełmski (heir of Łukomia); Reverend Convent of Ląd (holder of Drzewce)

**Document Outline:**

- **Oblata Frame** [f. 655]: Brest Kuyavia Municipal Court dateline and opening formula; submission of the Commissorial Boundary Decree for enrollment by Simon Szaunski.
- **Section 1: Commissorial Boundary Decree** [ff. 655–658]: The decree in full: the disputed boundary roads; the commission panel; the Warsaw Constitution authorising the commission; the presiding oath; the signatory block.
  - *Warsaw Constitution* [f. 656v] — *Oath* [f. 656v] — *Signatory Block* [f. 657]
- **Section 2: Proceedings of the Commission: Opening, Scribal Pen Dispute, Failed Reconciliation, Resumption of Proceedings** [ff. 658–660]: The commission's opening session; adjournment on account of the feast day; the dispute over the scribal pen (*pióro*) and the consequent splitting of the court; two days of attempted reconciliation; resumption of judicial proceedings in the field.
- **Section 3: Citations, Appearances, and Document Production** [ff. 660v–661v]: Service of citations; appearances of the parties; production and formal submission of documents by both sides.
- **Section 4 — The Boundary Line: Prusimski's Case** [ff. 661v–680]: Prusimski walks out his boundary line in full; documentary evidence; the Chełmski corner mound and its alleged encroachment on Trąbczyn ground; the woodlands Ostrowce, Miąsykąt, Dziadulina, and Lusnia; formal protests.
  - **The Boundary Line Continued: Mounds, *Stawisko*, and Cygany** [ff. 667v–680]: Continuation of Prusimski's boundary line to the south; the *stawisko*; the eight Cygany mounds and forty-one blazes; documentary evidence and sworn inquiries; Studzianka and the tavern hills; the terminal point.
- **Section 5 — Review of Chełmski's Boundary Line; Trąbczyn–Biskupice Boundary; Sworn Inquiry; Resolution on the Starting Point** [ff. 680–690]: The court's review of Chełmski's boundary line; the Trąbczyn–Biskupice boundary; the sworn inquiry conducted by the court; resolution and adjudication of the starting point.
- **Section 6 — Court's Resolution on the Full Boundary Line; Mound-Heaping; Trąbczyn–Łukomia; Oath** [ff. 690–702]: The court's full resolution on Prusimski's boundary line; the mound-heaping record for the Trąbczyn–Łukomia boundary; the commissioners' oath.
- **Section 7 — Trąbczyn–Biskupice Boundary; Mound-Heaping; Damages; Annulment of the Delivery of Possession; Default Judgement; Signatory Block** [ff. 702–715]: The mound-heaping record for the Trąbczyn–Biskupice boundary; award of damages; annulment of the 1771 delivery of possession; default judgements against Chełmski; the commissioners' signatory block and closing formula.

**Cross-references:** This document explicitly draws on and adjudicates the legal history from documents 1–9. It annuls document 9 (the 1771 delivery of possession), finds it unlawfully conducted. It references the sworn inquiry of 1728 concerning the *stawisko* (document 3), and the *wizja* records of 1767 (documents 6 and 7). The *stawisko* inspected in documents 6 and 7 and demolished in document 8 appears as a boundary landmark here. The forest testimony of document 4 informs its treatment of the Trąbczyn woodlands. The Tribunal decrees cited in document 9 are assessed and adjudicated.

## 11. September 1780 — Decree on the Contravention at Grab and the Smoleniec Violence Inquisition

**File:** `Decreta__protocollon__1781-1791__53_6_0_-_47__-_English_Translation.md`
**Archive ref.:** 53/6/0/-/47, ff. 239v, 240, 236v (Case No. 26)
**Court:** Konin Particular Land Court
**Date of events:** Contravention arising from the condescension at Grab on Monday after the Feast of the Nativity of the Blessed Virgin Mary 1780 (11 September 1780); decree and inquisition recorded as Case No. 26 in this volume (1781–1791)
**Parties:** Right Honourable Stanisław Chełmski, Treasurer of the Land of Wschowa (Plaintiff, represented by the Honourable Józef Stablewski) vs Illustrious Right Honourable Antoni of Kolno Prusimski, Starost of Niszczewice (Defendant, represented by the Honourable Wojciech [surname uncertain])

**Document Outline:**

- **Section 1 — Decree on the Contravention** [f. 239v]: Arising from the objected Condescension at Grab of 11 September 1780, Chełmski had obtained and published a finding of contravention of a Tribunal decree against Prusimski. The Court holds Prusimski bound to produce a representative; Prusimski submits to the Court's discretion. The Court establishes Prusimski's right of standing, orders him to pay penalties (split between the objecting party and the Court), and — since the cause also concerns alleged acts of violence — commits the matter to a formal inquisition, ordering both parties to draw up sworn Inquisitions.
- **Section 2 — Adjournment, Oaths, and the Inquisition's Findings: the Smoleniec Incident** [f. 240]: The Court adjourns until the Inquisitions are read; both parties swear the oath as to the uncorrupted character of the testimony and submit their Inquisitions. The findings describe men of Prusimski's household, having strayed from the Trąpczyn estate, arriving at the deserted tract of Smoleniec on Chełmski's Łukom land. There they assault the *Laboriosus* Jan Kłonica, a Hollander, after he tells them the land belongs to Chełmski — striking him with a whip and a broken pine stick and threatening to kill him, so that he flees into an alder grove. The men then seize and beat a servant at Kłonica's house and take him to the Tavern of Tomice, beating him continuously en route; he is released the next day. A witness complains to Prusimski in person the same day; Prusimski promises to look into it. The Inquisitions find the acts violent but do not establish whether Prusimski knew of or ordered them.
- **Section 3 — The Court's Final Decree** [f. 236v]: The Inquisitions name the perpetrators for the first time as Józef (the Coachman) and Walenty (the Under-coachman), and report they cannot be found on the Trąpczyn estate. Since Prusimski cannot produce them and states they were not arrested, he lodges a Manifest offering a sworn clearance. The Court orders him to clear himself within six weeks on the grounds that he did not allow Chełmski's side to arrest the two men, under penalty of banishment. The Inquisitions themselves are to remain in the local chancellery, to be handed to the parties on prior acquittance.

**Cross-references:** The date of the Grab condescension (Monday after the Nativity of the BVM, 1780 = 11 September 1780) matches "die undecima mensis 7bris anno 1780... in fundo bonorum Grab" cited in document 13 (Gr.153) as the basis of the contravention for which Prusimski was ordered to pay fourfold fourteen Polish marks (document 13, §2c) — this document is very likely the Konin land court's own record of that same contravention finding. Jan Kłonica is a newly identified named Hollander, alongside Martin Hunt (document 13, §6). The Tavern of Tomice connects to the place-name "Tomie" appearing in document 10 (Gr.145). The eventual outcome of the bannitio ordered against Prusimski here (re: Józef and Walenty) is not otherwise recorded in the corpus — see the Gap Map.

## 12. October 1781 – December 1782 — Tribunal Escalation: Succession Dispute, the Grab Condescension, Gorski's Manifest, and the December 1782 Timber Inspection

**File:** `Relationes__protocollon__1780-1782__53_17_0_-_Konin_Gr_118___English_.md`
**Archive ref.:** Konin Gr. 118, selected entries ff. 172v–674v
**Court:** Konin Municipal Court
**Date of events:** October 1781 – December 1782
**Parties:** Most Honourable Antoni Ostroróg Prusimski, Starost of Niszczewice, Knight of the Order of Saint Stanisław (heir of Trąbczyn and others) vs Right Honourable Stanisław Ścibor Chełmski, Treasurer of Wschowa (heir of Łukomia, Łomowa, and others); also involving the Zakrzewski family (Kazimierz the father and his sons Nepomucen, Ignacy, and Ludwik), the Ląd Convent (Abbot Benedykt Lubstowski; Administrator Tesselin Malechowski), and Jakub Pomian Gorski, Boundary Surveyor of Inowrocław

**Document Outline:**

- **Section A — Tribunal Citations and the Ląd Convent's Acknowledgement** [ff. 172v–396v, Oct–Nov 1781]: A series of Tribunal citation reports between Prusimski, Chełmski, and the Zakrzewski family (including an unrelated Brdów dispute involving Castellan Gurowski). Father Malechowski, Administrator of the Ląd Convent, lodges a manifest acknowledging the trijunction corner marker of Łukomia, Trąbczyn, and Drzewce as found by the Commissorial Court, denying any collusion with Chełmski.
- **Section B — The Grab Condescension and the Zakrzewski Succession Dispute** [ff. 398–519, Nov 1781–Jun 1782]: Reports for the Term of the Condescension in the settlement of the estates of Grab (cf. document 11). The Ląd Convent is cited to the Tribunal (4 January 1782) regarding the trijunction and the 1592 Survey. A Condescensorial Decree concerning the Olęder settlement belonging to Trąbczyn is enrolled (18 March 1782). Prusimski's plenipotentiary Piotr Gosławski lodges manifests against the Kalisz Land Court's handling of the Zakrzewski succession dispute, citing an attestation from the Tribunal Marshal Kęszycki.
- **Section C — Gorski's Manifest and the Field Obstruction** [ff. 523–530v, May–Aug 1782]: Jakub Pomian Gorski, Boundary Surveyor of Inowrocław, lodges a manifest describing how, while executing the post-commissorial Tribunal decree of 23 January 1782 at the field condescension for Trąbczyn Minor/Nowa Wieś, Chełmski personally and rebelliously obstructed the survey — pushing away the chain-bearer and refusing to permit the Geometer to continue — forcing Gorski's office to suspend proceedings and seek military assistance. A judicial site inspection of the location at the Trąbczyn corner mound is separately enrolled. Chełmski (15 June 1782) lodges a counter-manifest against Gorski alleging the Surveyor exceeded his commission and acted unlawfully regarding the *olędry* adjudicated between the parties.
- **Section D — The Genealogical Citation of September 1782** [ff. 585v–622v, Aug–Sept 1782]: Requisitions and Tribunal citations culminate in an extensive citation (21 September 1782) reciting Prusimski's full ancestry — son of the late Stefan Prusimski and Wiktoria Malczewska, grandson of the late Krzysztof Prusimski and Teresa Golińska — and naming the contested successions: the Zakrzewski line (through Marianna Prusimska, first married to Józef Tomicki, second to Kazimierz Zakrzewski), the Tworowicz line (through Wiktoria Prusimska and Michał Tworowicz), and the Kołaczkowski line (through Konstancja Prusimska and Jan Kowalski). The citation invokes the Tribunal Decree of 1738 and its 1760 confirmation.
- **Section E — The December 1782 Timber Inspection and Enrollment Cross-References** [ff. 671v–674v, Sept–Dec 1782]: A citation concerning penalties for attempted violence with arrest of persons. On 7 December 1782, Bartłomiej Szepczyński reports a judicial site inspection of 221 construction-grade pines felled on Prusimski's side of the boundary line, cut down by Chełmski's people on 28 November "during the Act of the Condescension of the two sessions." Leon Maszewski, Estate Manager of Trąbczyn, lodges a manifest against Chełmski and his steward Jaroszewski for the same felling and for related violent incursions, naming a long list of Łukom/Łomowo subjects involved. Two enrollment notices follow: the Condescensorial Boundary Decree between Trąbczyn and Łukom with description of the boundary mounds, and the Delivery of the *olęder* Martin Hunt by Commissioner Józef Łukaszewicz — both "as stands in the submitted documents."

**Cross-references:** The two enrollment notices at f. 674v are cross-references to material recorded in full in document 13 (Gr.153): the Condescensorial Boundary Decree with mound description (§§3–4) and the Martin Hunt *traditio* (§6). The 7 December 1782 inspection of 221 felled pines (f. 673) describes the felling of 28 November 1782 "during the Act of the Condescension of the two sessions" — the same field execution recorded in document 13 §4 (30 November – 3 December 1782). The Term of the Condescension in the estates of Grab (ff. 398, 495v, 519) is the proceeding whose underlying 1780 contravention is recorded in document 11. The genealogical citation (ff. 622v–623) is the fullest statement in the corpus of Antoni Prusimski's ancestry and the contested successions after his uncles Antoni and Paweł Prusimski, who died without issue. Leon Maszewski (Estate Manager) and Bartłomiej Szepczyński continue from earlier documents; both recur in document 14 (Gr.119).

## 13. October–December 1782 — Crown Tribunal Decree and Field Execution

**File:** `Relationes-oblatae__protocollon__1782__53_17_0_-_Konin_Gr_153__-_English_Translation.md`
**Archive ref.:** Konin Gr. 153, ff. 1391–1404
**Court:** Crown Tribunal of Piotrków (decree); field execution by the Boundary Surveyor of the Gniezno Voivodeship
**Date of events:** Tribunal decree 17 October 1782; field execution 28 November – 3 December 1782
**Parties:** Most Honourable Antoni Prusimski, Starost of Niszczewice (plaintiff); Right Honourable Stanisław Chełmski, Treasurer of Wschowa (cited and plaintiff); Honourable Jakub Gerski, Boundary Surveyor of Inowrocław; Honourable Alojzy Orławski, Boundary Surveyor of the Dobrzyń Land; Most Reverend Lubstowski, Abbot, and Reverend Ląd Convent

**Document Outline:**

- **Section 1 — Opening Record** [ff. 1391–1391v]: Opening formula establishing jurisdiction, date, and location at the three corner mounds; participants; Court Summoner Bartłomiej Szepczyński.
- **Section 2 — Embedded Tribunal Decree: Piotrków, 17 October 1782** [ff. 1391v–1399v]: The Crown Tribunal decree in full: the parties' claims; the tribunal's narrative of the boundary dispute; its resolution on the wall mounds, the *olęder* Martin Hunt, and associated penalties. Signatory block.
- **Section 3 — Bridge Clause and Commencement of Field Execution** [ff. 1399v–1401]: The condescensorial record: arrival at the estate; resumption of jurisdiction; production of the map; the geometer forms the line.
- **Section 4 — Mound-Erection Record** [ff. 1401–1402v]: Erection of wall mounds and stakes along the boundary line across three days (30 November – 3 December 1782); demolition of prior mounds.
- **Section 5 — Registration and Presentation of the Condescensorial Decree** [f. 1403v]: Registration of the present act at the Konin Municipal Court; the condescensorial decree presented by Leon Maszewski.
- **Section 6 — Condescensorial Decree: Possession of the *Olęder* Martin Hunt** [f. 1404]: Record of proceedings at the Trąmpczyn *olędry*: transfer of the *olęder* Martin Hunt into the possession of Prusimski.

**Cross-references:** The corner mounds at which this document opens are those erected by the 1776 commission (document 10) and approved by the post-commissarial Piotrków Tribunal decree of 23 January 1782 (cited in Section 1). Bartłomiej Szepczyński, who first appeared in document 4 (1754), continues into document 14 (Gr.119); he does not appear for the last time here. The *stawisko* appears as a terrain feature in the mound-erection record (Section 4). The 1775 commission decree (document 10) is the legal foundation for all field operations here. Section 1's reference to the contravention of 11 September 1780 "in fundo bonorum Grab" is the same contravention adjudicated in document 11 (53/6/0/-/47, Case No. 26). The mound-erection record (Section 4) and the Martin Hunt *traditio* (Section 6) are each separately cross-referenced by an enrollment notice in document 12 (Gr.118, f. 674v); the 221-pine inspection in document 12 (f. 673) describes the felling that occurred during this document's field execution (Section 4).

## 14. March 1783 – September 1784 — Forest-Boundary Invasion Citations and the Maszewski Manifest

**File:** `Relationes__protocollon__1783-1784__53_17_0_-_Konin_Gr_119___English_.md`
**Archive ref.:** Konin Gr. 119, ff. 62, 62v–63, 73, 128, 131, 304v, 685
**Court:** Konin Municipal Court
**Date of events:** March 1783 – September 1784
**Parties:** Right Honourable (Ignacy) Stanisław Chełmski, Treasurer of Wschowa (heir of Łukom, Łomowa, and others), with steward Ignacy Jaroszewski, vs Illustrious Right Honourable Antoni Prusimski, Starost of Niszczewice (heir of Trąmpczyn and others), with steward/Commissioner Leon Maszewski and co-plaintiff Franciszek Liberacki

**Document Outline:**

- **Section 1 — Two Kalisz Citations of 10 March 1783** [ff. 62–63]: Stanisław Stachowski and Jan Bekierski, on Chełmski's behalf, serve two citations. The first cites Prusimski and his steward Leon Maszewski for an alleged invasion of the pine forest along the boundary line established by the post-commissorial decree, with interception of Chełmski's wagons and the arrest of named forest keepers and coachmen (Thomas Fryzyer, Stanisław Lokaj, Jan Wochnicki, Simon Jachowski, and the coachmen Stanisław, Józef, and Grzegorz, with the court summoner Jan Mathias). The second cites Prusimski alone for an alleged armed hunting-party invasion of Łomowo.
- **Section 2 — Requisitions and Document-Production Citations** [f. 73]: Stachowski's requisition, reviewing the Protocol of *Relationes* from 26 November 1782 to date, finds only one prior *relatio* (29 November 1782) and certifies the non-existence of any further return. Szepczyński separately reports citations served on Chełmski for documents to be produced at the Kalisz Municipal Court.
- **Section 3 — The Fourteen-Mark Penalty Citation** [f. 128, 22 May 1783]: Szepczyński reports a citation served on Chełmski seeking the quashing of the fourteen-Polish-mark penalty paid by Chełmski to the Tribunal Instigator's exchequer.
- **Section 4 — Further Document-Production Citation** [f. 131]: Stachowski reports citations served on Prusimski for substantive documents to be produced.
- **Section 5 — Prusimski's Manifest on the "Stationing" Accusation** [f. 304v]: Prusimski responds to Chełmski's accusation regarding the "stationing" of men, declaring that any violence by his men, if proven, was without his knowledge or mandate; that he can produce only two of the named men (Gregorius and Thomas); and that he is ready for inquisitions and will seek penalties for undue harassment.
- **Section 6 — Maszewski's Requisition of September 1784** [f. 685, 15 September 1784]: Leon Maszewski, "Commissioner of the Trąmpczyn estate district," confirms before the Konin Municipal Court the *relatio* of a Tribunal citation served on Chełmski's behalf at Trąmpczyn.

**Cross-references:** This continues the Tribunal-level escalation of document 12 (Gr.118). The fourteen-Polish-mark penalty (Section 3) is the same penalty referenced in document 13 §2c (Prusimski ordered to pay fourfold fourteen Polish marks to Chełmski and the court). The forest-boundary invasion citations (Section 1) concern the same pine-forest boundary line established by the 1775/76 commission (document 10) and executed in document 13 §4. Bartłomiej Szepczyński and Leon Maszewski both continue from document 12; Maszewski's title here ("Commissioner of the Trąmpczyn estate district") marks a step up from "Estate Manager" in document 12.

## 15. March 1785 – October 1786 — Wound Report (Kinos) and Tribunal Citation on the Tracholz Pine-Woodland Contract

**File:** `Relationes__protocollon__1785-1786__53_17_0_-_Konin_Gr_120___English_.md`
**Archive ref.:** Konin Gr. 120, f. 63; ff. 623v–624
**Court:** Konin Municipal Court
**Date of events:** 14 March 1785 (wound report); 2 October 1786 (Tribunal citation)
**Parties:** *Laboriosus* Andreas Kinos of Trąmpczyn (complainant) vs Right Honourable Martinus Sarzyński of Obory (alleged assailant); and Right Honourable Stanisław Chełmski, Treasurer of Wschowa (Plaintiff) vs Illustrious Right Honourable Antoni Prusimski of Kolno, Starost of Niszczewice (Cited)

**Document Outline:**

- **Section 1 — Wound Report of Andreas Kinos** [f. 63, 14 March 1785]: *Laboriosus* Andreas Kinos of Trąmpczyn files an *obductio vulnerum*, describing injuries to both eyes, his face, and his right shoulder, alleging he was beaten with fists and a stick by the Right Honourable Martinus Sarzyński on the public road near Obory while travelling to Pleszew the preceding Saturday.
- **Section 2 — Tribunal Citation on the Tracholz Contract** [ff. 623v–624, 2 October 1786]: *Providus* Sebastianus Biegański of Słask serves a Crown Tribunal citation on Prusimski at Trąmpczyn, on Chełmski's behalf, concerning the contract for the sale of pine woodland made with the Honourable Jan Trachultz (Tracholz). The citation recites the procedural history — remissions between the Condescensorial, Plenary, and Tribunal Courts, and the suspended Kalisz Land Court decrees at Konin — and seeks satisfaction for the Plaintiff.

**Cross-references:** Andreas Kinos of Trąmpczyn is a newly named Olęder/settler figure in the corpus. The Tracholz/Trachultz pine-woodland contract (Section 2) is the same financial claim discussed at length in the 1778 manifests of Konin Gr.117 (ff. 54v–55v, 71–71v), which trace it to a 1744 oak-grove contract — Gr.117 is not currently a numbered document in this register.

## 16. June 1788 — Presentation of Seized Livestock: the Gryndel Timber-Poaching Incident

**File:** `Relationes__protocollon__1787-1788__53_17_0_-_Konin_Gr_121___English_.md`
**Archive ref.:** Konin Gr. 121, ff. 563–563v
**Court:** Konin Municipal Court
**Date of events:** 9 June 1788
**Parties:** Most Honourable Antoni in Kolno Prusimski, Starost of Niszczewice, Knight of the Order of Saint Stanislaus (heir of Trąbczyn), represented by the Honourable Ludovicus Żurawski, vs the peasant Martin Gryndel of Łomowo (subject of the Right Honourable Scibor Chełmski, Treasurer of Wschowa, heir of Łukom)

**Document Outline:**

- **Section 1 — Presentation of Livestock and Cart** [ff. 563–563v]: Ludovicus Żurawski presents to the Konin Municipal Court Chancellery a cart, two oxen, a cow, and a chestnut mare, seized from the peasant Martin Gryndel of Łomowo. Gryndel had been intercepted by the Guardian of the Forests of Trąbczyn while riding into the Trąbczyn pine forest and already transporting a cut, building-grade tree toward his home at Łomowo. The animals and cart are left at the Chancellery pending extradition back to Gryndel.

**Cross-references:** This is the latest-dated entry in the corpus (1788) and the last recorded incident of cross-boundary timber-cutting in the Trąbczyn pine forest — a pattern recurring since document 3 (1728) and document 12 (the 221-pine inspection of December 1782). Martin Gryndel of Łomowo is a newly named figure.
