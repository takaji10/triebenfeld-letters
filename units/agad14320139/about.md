Every year the parishes of the Evangelical-Augsburg (Lutheran) Church in the Kingdom of Poland answered a set of questions from the church's consistory in Warsaw. The two reports here are the answers that Pastor E. Erdmann of Zagórów gave for 1882, one for the parish of Zagórów and one for its filial congregation of Lissewo-Pyzdry. Pastor Erdmann's answers describe the churches, schools, cemeteries and congregations of the German colonists around Trąbczyn. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The Evangelical-Augsburg Church in the Kingdom of Poland, under the Russian emperor, was governed by a consistory at Warsaw, divided since 1849 into dioceses under superintendents, with a General Superintendent at their head. Kneifel's history of the church lists the parishes of the Kalisch diocese, among them "Zagörow with the filial Lissewo-Pyzdry", and names Ignatius Karl Wilhelm Boerner as superintendent of the Płock diocese (Kneifel 1964, p. 123). <!-- context -->

Each year the parishes answered the consistory's questions on their church buildings, schools, church life, morals, confirmations and collections. The German colonists on the Trąbczyn and Zagórów estates, whose schools and prayer houses appear in the reports, are the settlers of the Olęder colonies: [[unit:app539680669]]. <!-- context -->

### Contents

Both reports answer an order of the consistory of 30 October, No. 267, and follow the same headings: church buildings (I, a), church life (I, b), remarkable events (II), confirmands (III), candidates in the congregation (IV) and collections (V) [[1, 2]]. The second report repeats the first almost word for word in its later sections.

**Document 1, the parish of Zagórów.** The congregation has a brick-built church that still lacks a baptismal font, organ and bells; the pastor's house and the cantor's house are very small and low [[1]]. Besides the religion school at Zagórów there are seven more schools with prayer houses, one of them an elementary school at Michalinowo; the eight schools have about 240 children [[1]]. The congregation has eight cemeteries [[1]]. The communicants are given as 726 men and 871 women, 1,597 in all [[1]]. The mission collection of 10 roubles 71 kopecks was passed on to Superintendent Pastor Manitius at Warsaw [[1]]. The report names no Baptists, Irvingians or other sects, no special vices and no conversions to other confessions, no remarkable events, 67 confirmands (36 boys and 31 girls) and no candidates in the congregation [[1]]. For other purposes 4 roubles 61 kopecks was collected for grammar-school pupils intending to study theology and 2 roubles 36 kopecks for the widows' and orphans' fund, sent to Superintendent von Boerner [[1]].

**Document 2, the filial Lissewo-Pyzdry.** The filial has neither a church nor a sexton's house; divine service at Pyzdry is held in a rented room [[2]]. It has five schools with prayer houses, with about 120 children, and five cemeteries, fenced and with crosses [[2]]. The communicants are 326 men and 408 women, 734 in all, 131 more than in 1881 [[2]]. The Evangelicals of Pyzdry would like a school of their own but are too poor, the town counting only nine families [[2]]; the colonies have their own teachers and prayer houses [[2]]. The mission collection was 3 roubles 40 kopecks; there were 23 confirmands (13 boys and 10 girls) [[2]]. For students of theology 1 rouble 76 kopecks was collected [[2]].
