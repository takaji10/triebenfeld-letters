The file of the liquidation table of the village "Колонія Лазинская", the Łazińsk colony in the commune of Trąbczyn, kept in the State Archive in Łódź (Archiwum Państwowe w Łodzi) in the records of the Kalisz Governorate Board for Peasant Affairs (Urząd Gubernialny Kaliski do Spraw Włościańskich). It holds the table approved in 1868, the copy made of it in 1869 for the governorate government, and the inscriptions and statement that later changed it, to 1901. Albert Breyer copied the same table into his notebook in the 1930s, from a copy dated at Słupca on 29 June 1868, [[unit:app539680716]]. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The Russian ukases of 19 February (2 March) 1864 made the peasants of the Kingdom of Poland owners of the land they farmed. For each village a liquidation table recorded the farmsteads, their land, the dues the government would compensate, and the capital owed to the former owner; the peasants kept their servitudes, rights of pasture and wood in the manor forest, which were later bought out or exchanged for land. The Kalisz commission for peasant affairs, from 1870 the governorate board, drew up and kept these tables and entered later changes in them. The Łazińsk colony was one of the two Olęder colonies founded on the Trąbczyn estate, whose settlers had gone to law with Count Włodzimierz Miączyński over the same forest in 1859 and 1860, [[unit:app539680783]]. <!-- context -->

### Contents

The table lists 91 farmsteads of the colony, each with its land in Morgen and rods, and then the farmstead of the village school, the common cemetery, a plot of common land, the village head's plot, one more farmstead and the land under the roads: 1,691 Morgen 219 rods in all, of which 9 Morgen 224 rods unusable. The dues the government compensates come to 603 roubles 34⅕ kopecks. On 8 February 1868 the Central Commission for Peasant Affairs confirmed the table and fixed the liquidation capital for the former owner at 10,055 roubles 70 kopecks [[1]].

The copy, made at Kalisz on 10 September 1869 to be kept at the governorate government, has the same entries in another hand. Beside the first entries it states the servitudes: all the peasants of the Łazińsk Hollanders may graze the cattle they have in the forest "Studzianka" and gather fallen wood there twice a week, and have no other rights [[2]].

In March 1884 an inscription in the copy, under a resolution of the governorate board of 25 February 1882, fixed the fuel each farmstead might take from the forest at 104 cloth-loads a year, equal to 10½ two-horse carts, and allowed the farmsteads to graze 408 head of horned cattle there [[3]]. By a voluntary transaction of 14 June 1892, recorded in an inscription of 12 October 1894, the peasants gave up their rights of pasture and wood-gathering in the forest to the owner of Trąbczyn, Countess Maria Wielopolska, and received 393 Morgen 154 rods of manor land [[4]]. A survey of 1896, recorded in an inscription of 29 November 1897, put the whole allotment at 1,068 desiatins 1,272 sazhens [[5]], and a statement gives each farmstead's land after it, under buildings and gardens, arable, meadow, pasture and unusable [[6]].

### Form and language

Russian, in the spelling of before 1918. The table is a printed form filled in by hand, with columns for the land of each farmstead and for the dues; the copy is another hand on the same form; the inscriptions of 1894 and 1897 are certified copies on separate sheets. The tables are given as tables. Where the writing runs across an opening, the two pages are shown as one. <!-- context -->
