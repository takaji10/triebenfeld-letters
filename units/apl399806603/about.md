The colonists of Łazińsk, the old Olęder colony on the Trąbczyn estate, became the owners of their land under a liquidation table approved in 1868. The table, and the changes officials added to it until 1901, follow the colony's land and its rights in the manor's forest for more than thirty years, to the exchange of the colonists' forest rights for 393 Morgen of manor land in 1892 to 1894. Albert Breyer copied the same table into his notebook in the 1930s, [[unit:app539680716]]. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The Russian ukases of 19 February (2 March) 1864 made the peasants of the Kingdom of Poland owners of the land they farmed. For each village a liquidation table recorded the farmsteads and their land, the labour and rents the peasants had owed the landowner, for which the government now compensated him, and the capital he was to receive; the peasants kept their servitudes, rights of pasture and wood in the manor forest, which were later bought out or exchanged for land. The Kalisz commission for peasant affairs, later the governorate board, drew up and kept these tables and entered later changes in them. The Łazińsk colony was one of the two Olęder colonies founded on the Trąbczyn estate, whose settlers had gone to law with Count Włodzimierz Miączyński over the same forest in 1859 and 1860, [[unit:app539680783]]. <!-- context -->

### Contents

The colony had 91 farmsteads, a farmstead for its school, a common cemetery, a plot of common land and the village head's plot: 1,691 Morgen 219 rods in all, roads included. The dues its farmsteads had owed the owner, which the government now took over, came to 603 roubles 34⅕ kopecks a year. On 8 February 1868 the Central Commission for Peasant Affairs confirmed the table and fixed the liquidation capital for the former owner at 10,055 roubles 70 kopecks [[1]].

A copy made at Kalisz in 1869 for the governorate's government sets out the colonists' servitudes: all the peasants of the Łazińsk Hollanders may graze the cattle they have in the forest "Studzianka" and gather fallen wood there twice a week, and have no other rights [[2]].

In March 1884, under a resolution of the governorate board of 25 February 1882, officials fixed the fuel each farmstead might take from the forest at 104 cloth-loads a year, equal to 10½ two-horse carts, and allowed the farmsteads to graze 408 head of horned cattle there [[3]]. By an agreement of 14 June 1892, registered on 12 October 1894, the peasants gave up their rights of pasture and wood-gathering in the forest to the owner of Trąbczyn, Countess Maria Wielopolska, and received 393 Morgen 154 rods of manor land [[4]]. A survey of 1896 put the colony's whole land at 1,068 desiatins 1,272 sazhens, and set out each farmstead's buildings and gardens, arable, meadow and pasture [[5, 6]].
