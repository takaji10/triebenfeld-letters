Ten entries on nine pages of a register of the castle court of Konin for the years 1768 to 1774, kept in the State Archive in Poznań (Archiwum Państwowe w Poznaniu). They follow the quarrel between Antoni Prusimski of Trąbczyn and Stanisław Ścibor Chełmski of Łukomia from the citations of 1768 to the fighting over the Lusnia wood in 1771 to 1773. They are in Latin, two of them with a passage in Polish, and are ten documents. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The Lusnia wood lay on the ground that Trąbczyn and Łukom both claimed, and Prusimski had settled Olęder farmers in it, with an inn. In November 1771 Chełmski, on the strength of judgments by default, had himself put into possession of the wood and its twelve settlers: [[unit:app53150kaliszgr414]]. Prusimski did not accept it. For more than a year each side used force on the ground and then sent a court messenger to see the damage and report it at Konin. The commission of 1775 heard sworn witnesses on these same events, found that Chełmski had raided the inn with armed men, and gave the wood to Prusimski: [[unit:app53170koningr145]]. The reports here are one-sided by their nature: each says what one party asked a messenger to see. <!-- context -->

The volume belongs to the archive's fonds "Księgi sądu i urzędu grodzkiego w Koninie", the books of the castle court and castle office of Konin. This book is a rough register: each day the office sat has a line beginning "Actum in Conin", and under it short entries, each headed with the name of the party and the kind of entry. Many are one-line notes that a paper has been brought in and lies among the papers produced. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**Citations, 1768.** A note of January 1768 records Stanisław Chełmski's citation of Antoni Prusimski and of the successors of the late Katarzyna Prusimska before the land court [[1]]. On 17 May 1768 two notes record citations before the Crown Tribunal at Bydgoszcz for the fixing of the boundary: Prusimski's against the Chełmskis, the Żychliński heirs, Ignacy Gałecki, Starost of Bydgoszcz, and the abbot and convent of Ląd; and Chełmski's against the abbot and convent of Ląd, Prusimski and Gałecki [[2]]. A note of the same month records that Prusimski brought in an extract from the castle court at Bydgoszcz with his approbation of a term for quashing a condemnation [[3]].

**The delivery of Lusnia, December 1771.** On 2 December 1771 the delivery of possession of the Lusnia wood with the Olęder settlers was brought in for Chełmski [[4]].

**The first clash, December 1771.** On 20 December Chełmski's messenger, Nicolaus Krzepczyński of Myszaków, laid weapons before the office: five sabres, four spears, three long guns and a gun mounted with brass, loaded. Chełmski, he said, had taken them on the Monday before in the Lusnia wood, when Prusimski broke in by force and armed men besieged the innkeeper's house [[5]]. The same day he reported what he had seen there with two noblemen: the embankment at the pond-ground dug through, its weir hacked to pieces and the water let out with the fish, twenty fresh-cut clubs left lying; and in the inn eight loopholes cut in the walls and three windows shot out. He laid the breaking of the embankment to more than thirty subjects of Trąbczyn, in Prusimski's presence, and the shooting to Prusimski's men at his order [[6]].

**The inn stripped, November 1772.** On 1 December 1772 another messenger, Joannes Bikierski of Węgierce, reported for Chełmski that on the Saturday before three windows and four doors had been taken out of the inn at night by Prusimski's steward Lipiński and others, at Prusimski's order, and carried off to Trąbczyn with a table and a barrel of beer [[7]].

**Prusimski's count of felled trees, February 1773.** On 26 February 1773 the Trąbczyn messenger Bartłomiej Szepczyński reported for Prusimski: fifty-one stumps of building pine in the wood beyond Lusnia, carted off by Chełmski's subjects, and thirty-two alder stumps on the meadows called Kąty, cut by men of the neighbouring estate of Podbiele [[8]].

**The inn wrecked, January 1773.** In March 1773 Chełmski entered a protest over his own signature: Prusimski, knowing that the settlers of Lusnia were adjudged to Chełmski, had let his steward Lipiński and his chamber servant Marzęcki ride upon the inn and the settlers [[9]]. Bikierski reported the damage: stove, three windows and four doors, newly put in by Chełmski, hacked out, the chimney ruined, the vessels smashed, in the night of 29 January [[10]].

### Form and language

Each entry is a few lines among many others on its page; the pages are shown whole and the other entries are not transcribed. The Latin is heavily abbreviated and the transcription writes it out. Two of the notes of 1768 are transcribed only as far as they could be read, and "…" marks what is left out. Chełmski's signature stands under his protest, and twice more at the foot of the page with the entries of 20 December 1771. Three entries stand under a sitting whose dated heading is on a page that was not photographed; they are dated to the month from the headings before and after them. <!-- context -->
