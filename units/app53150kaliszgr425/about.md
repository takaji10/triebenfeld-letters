The boundary cause between Antoni Prusimski of Trąbczyn and the Chełmskis of Łukomia did not end with the commission's decree of 1775, and commissioners were still meeting over it in 1776. On 28 September 1776 five commissioners, kept away by the parliament at Warsaw and the Tribunal at Piotrków, put their sitting on the ground of Łukom off to January 1777. None of the five had signed the commission's decree. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The Polish parliament appointed a commission in 1774 to end the boundary dispute between Trąbczyn and Łukomia. It opened on the ground on 13 September 1775. In its first days the commissioners fell out over who should keep the record of the proceedings, and split into two groups, each sitting on its own. The group that sat under the president, Ludwik Dąmbski, walked the boundary, ruled for Prusimski, and put the rest of its business off to 21 May 1776; its decree was entered in the court records at Brześć Kujawski on 23 September 1776 with six signatures: [[unit:app53170koningr145]]. <!-- context -->

The five commissioners of this adjournment are not among those six. One of them, Tomasz Radoński, is the boundary surveyor whom the other group had made its clerk in 1775. Five days after the decree was registered at Brześć Kujawski, they treat the cause as still open before them. <!-- context -->

The municipal court took shape at the end of the fourteenth century. For the nobility it first heard criminal cases under the four "castle articles": arson, an attack on a nobleman's house, robbery on the public road, and rape. In time its competence changed and came to include the other criminal cases, and suits over land and over sums of money, and it took over appeals from the town courts. Beside the court grew the municipal court office, where the court books were kept. The office was open every day for the entry of uncontested business, and what was entered in its books was accepted as proof. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**The adjournment.** The five were Stefan Leszczyc Zielonacki, deputy judge of the Kalisz land court; Maciej Leszczyc Mierzewski; Tomasz Radoński, boundary surveyor of Kalisz; Stanisław Jabłkowski; and Stanisław Otto-Trąmpczyński. Appointed by the parliament of the Commonwealth to judge the cause between Prusimski, Starost of Niszczewice, heir of Trąbczyn, and Stanisław Ścibor Chełmski, heir of Łukomia, they declare that they cannot give a final judgment at the sitting now due, the date the commission had fixed in its decree of the year before: the parliament is sitting at Warsaw and the Crown Tribunal, the kingdom's highest court, at Piotrków, and they have business at the one or the other. They put the meeting off to the first Monday after Epiphany 1777, and the parties are to appear then on the ground of Łukomia [[1]].
