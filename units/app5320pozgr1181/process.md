# APP 53/20/0/-/Poznań Gr.1181 — how this holding was prepared

The scans are the editor's own photographs of the fourteen pages (161 to
167v in the archive's pagination), one page per photograph, taken from the
volume of the relationes protocollon in the State Archive in Poznań. The
transcription is the editor's own, made from the photographs: a Latin text
of one continuous entry, typed page by page and, for p. 161, laid out in
the manuscript's own lines.

The editor's text was carried over as written. The session that
brought the holding in first applied 108 small completions of its own (the
doubtful case endings, completed by grammar); when the editor's fresh
line-by-line typing of p. 161 arrived the same day keeping the original
doubts, the completions were withdrawn and the editor's word taken
throughout. The five readings the fresh pass settled (the December year
1783, "videlicet" before the day numeral of the decree of October 1782,
"Mensis" before Junii, "oblatam porrecto", the break "camera-/riali") are
the editor's own readings, and everything else stands with its doubt mark
as they typed it.

The pages were cut from the photographs at the archive's pagination (page
161 = 0161_a, page 161v = 0161_b, and so on), paired with the transcription
by the declared pairing, and read into the corpus as one document. Pp.
161v-167v are presented by paragraph, each page's text beside that page's
scan, as the sibling registers of the same suit are; p. 161 is line by
line, as the editor typed it. Lineation of the remaining pages is left
open: the machine layouts the session tried measured 2-4 words off the
editor's own line ends on p. 161 and were not built.

The doubtful readings that remain (some thirty names, words and figures
the transcription marks with [?]) are collected on a spot sheet published
at takaji10.github.io/triebenfeld-spot/sheets/app5320pozgr1181/ for the
editor to answer away from the desktop; the sheet's answers, when they
come, are ruled into the text and recorded in the holding's notes.

The English translation was written in the working session, from the
editor's transcription, under the rules the translation pipeline gives its
translator; the summaries in both languages and the claim check were made
in the same session, and no paid model run was spent on the holding.
