Six pages from a copy-book of the privileges of the Cistercian abbey of Ląd, kept in the State Archive in Poznań (Archiwum Państwowe w Poznaniu) as unit 53/1455/0/2/21, in the fond of the abbey, "Klasztor cystersów w Lądzie". The archive calls the volume "Series privilegiorum monasterii Landensis transcripta ex libro privilegiorum", a series of the privileges of the monastery of Ląd copied from the book of privileges. The pages are 13, 14, 15, 25, 26 and 66 of the volume, by its pencil pagination, the pages the editor chose; they hold six documents. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The abbey of Ląd, on the Warta between Konin and Pyzdry, was founded for Cistercians from Altenberg and endowed by Duke Mieszko the Old between 1145 and 1174; grants by knights added to the foundation later (the archive's description of the fond, after the introduction to its inventory by Z. Wojciechowska). The fond holds deeds of property and foundation, the abbots' papers, privileges, a budget, a list of the abbey's estates and an inventory. <!-- context -->

The abbey was the neighbour of Trąbczyn. Its villages and woods ran from Zagórów and Wrąbczyn to Drzewce, and the medieval documents are mostly its charters: [[unit:kdw]]. The copy-book was written in the eighteenth century; these pages are in one hand. The copyist gives each privilege a heading and a number in the margin, and copies the dating clause and the witnesses; where an entry was entered in a court's books, he copies that note too. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**No. 14, the boundary of Makownica, 1399.** Mikołaj, heir of Venacia and general judge of the land of Kalisz, had resigned his estate of Makownica to the monastery of Ląd before King Władysław, who confirmed it. At Ląd, on the Sunday within the octave of Corpus Christi 1399, he has its bounds marked with mounds, "kopce" in the vernacular, from the boundaries of Malenin to those of Ruchocin, gives the abbey half of the thicket of Kobielnica, and leaves the monastery all his goods there, bees, cattle, sheep and mares, after his death [[1]].

**No. 15, Łukom and the forest of Grądzyń, 1461.** At Pyzdry, on the Monday after the Exaltation of the Holy Cross 1461, Piotr of Szamotuły, castellan of Poznań and general captain of Greater Poland, attests a sale. Three brothers Wochneth, one of them parish priest at Koscielec, and their three nephews Bochneth resign to Abbot Cornelius and the convent of Ląd, with King Kazimierz's consent, three whole mansi with gardens in the village of Łukom in the parish of Zagórów. With them go the forest of Grądzyń, the forests of Chmielniki and Lome as far as the road called Rosiszewska, which runs from Łukom and Lomowo to the hill of Karczemne gory, the marshes of Karczemne Lugi and a field above Imielno. The price is 400 marks of broad groschen, 48 groschen to the mark [[2]]. In the margin a note adds "and with the forest of Lomy, from the boundaries" [[2]].

**No. 16, the king's confirmation, 1447.** The copy after it, the copyist's "Confirmatio Gradziem Regis Casimiri", shows how the abbey had come by the land. At Gniezno, on the Tuesday before St Lawrence, in a year written over and read as 1447, King Kazimierz confirms the contract by which Abbot Maciej bought the same three mansi in Łukomia and the forest of Grądzyń from the brothers Mikołaj, Jan and Warsz of Koscielec and their stepson. He gives his reasons: the monastery lacks forests with which it might be restored, and it should be well provided when he visits [[3]]. Mikołaj and Jan are also the names of two of the Wochneth brothers of 1461, and the third brother's name, which the copy of no. 15 writes in two ways, neither of them clear, may be Warsz [[2, 3]].

**No. 25, the exchange of 1362.** At Pyzdry, on the Tuesday after the octave of the Epiphany 1362, King Kazimierz takes the abbey's village of Little Wronczyn near Pyzdry and its mill below Pyzdry castle, and gives it in exchange sixty Franconian mansi of his forest Czirnetz in the district of Konin, measured by his messengers and bounded by mounds. The abbey may found villages there under Polish or German law, free of royal dues as its other estates are; the king keeps the hunting of deer and boar in what stays forest. The copyist's heading adds that the village of Drzewce was afterwards founded in that forest [[4]]. No. 26 follows: the king's grant of twenty years' freedom to the peasants settled in the forest, here written Czernetz, and their release from the levies sep and naraz, from the dues in pigs, cows and sheep, and from carting. It breaks off at the foot of page 26; the rest is on page 27, which is not included [[5]].

**No. 46, page 66: the mounds between Łukom, Zagórów and Drzewce.** The entry begins on page 63: by its heading, a piece of ground of the estate of Łukom by the boundary of Drzewce was ceded to the convent of Ląd and marked out by the decree of a compromissory court, which was entered at Kalisz castle. Page 66 counts the eighth to the nineteenth mound, with their distances in rods, along the roads from Huta to Grądzyń, Rosiszewo and Lomowo, from Łukom to Lomowo and from Tomice to Zagórów and Łukom. Two hills that the convent claimed as old mounds were opened and found empty. The next day, the Friday after Misericordia Sunday, the court renewed the mounds between the estate of Łukom, the town of Zagórów and Drzewce, before the burgomaster, the wójt, the notary and others, whose names run on to the next page [[6]].

### Form and language

Latin, copied in the eighteenth century in one clear hand, with the copyist's spelling and abbreviations; Polish words stand in it as the originals had them ("kopce", "niwa", "sep", "naraz", "powos"). Each privilege has a heading in larger letters and its number beside it; the copyist marks the start of each with an "x" in the margin and ends each page with a catchword. Later hands have written page numbers in pencil and red crayon at the top of the pages, and the years of the privileges in pencil in the margin. <!-- context -->
