### Images

The editor photographed the volume in the archive, the cover and its pages, one page to a photograph, with a camera that saved raw files. They chose five pages for the edition: 13, 14, 25, 26 and 66, by the archive's pagination in pencil at the top outer corner of each page. Which photograph is which page was read off those numbers. Each page was taken from the full-size picture inside the raw file, turned upright and cut to the page. Pages 13 and 25 begin with the end of a privilege copied on the page before; at the editor's word the picture and the text begin below it, at the heading of the first privilege that starts on the page. Page 66 lies wholly inside a long entry and is given whole.

### Transcription

There was no earlier transcription. An AI model (Claude) read the pages from the photographs, enlarged, in the working session. The text keeps the copyist's spelling, writes out his common abbreviations, as the edition does with Latin, and leaves names and adjectives he cuts short with a stop as he wrote them. Words he struck out are not transcribed. The note in the margin of page 14 is transcribed and set apart. Page numbers, the years written later in pencil in the margins, and the copyist's marks are not transcribed. Seventeen words and names are read with doubt and marked so.

### Dating

Each document is a copy and takes the date of what it copies. Documents 1, 2 and 4 are dated by their own dating clauses, given by feasts, and the day of the month is counted from them. Documents 3 and 5 are beginnings that stop before their dates, and are left undated. Document 6 names only "the Friday after Misericordia Sunday"; the year, 1761, is in the heading of the entry on page 63, which is not in the edition.

### Summary and translation

The summaries were written in German from the Latin and translated into English; each of their 70 statements was then checked against the text, and nothing in them rests on a word read with doubt. The English translation was made in the same session. Christian names are given in their Polish form; surnames and the names of places the edition has not identified stand as written.
