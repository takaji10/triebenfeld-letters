In 1783, the year after the boundary between Trąbczyn and Łukom was marked, men of Antoni Prusimski's household beat an Olęder settler of Stanisław Chełmski's and a servant at Smoleniec. The decree of the land court at Konin on the assault finds only Prusimski's coachman and under-coachman accused, and puts Prusimski to an oath that he cannot produce them. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The boundary between Prusimski's Trąbczyn and Chełmski's Łukom had been marked on the ground in November and December 1782, after the Crown Tribunal's judgment: [[unit:app53170koningr153]]. This decree shows the two neighbours at law again within a year, now over a beating. The Olęder settlers were free tenant farmers whom both estates had planted in the woods along the old boundary; who they belonged to had been part of the boundary dispute. <!-- context -->

In 1780 Chełmski had obtained a judgment by default against Prusimski at a sitting of the court on the ground at Grab: [[unit:app53170koningr117]], [[unit:app53170koningr118]]. A party under such a judgment had to show that he was entitled to be heard at all before he could answer a new suit. An "arrest" in these suits was an order, served with a summons on a lord, to keep named subjects of his ready to be produced in court. <!-- context -->

The land court at Konin was a court of the nobility. The starost or his deputy, as the king's lieutenant, had the leading part in it, with the judge, the sub-judge, the voivode, the chamberlain and the standard-bearer. The court travelled round the palatinate and held its sittings in the district; the terms at which it sat were called "roczki". It was competent in every suit in which a nobleman was the defendant, except those assigned to other courts, such as the dietine courts or the royal court, and except uncontested business. In 1792 its competence passed to a new land court, the "sąd ziemiański". <!-- context -->

### Contents

**Standing.** At the land court's sitting at Konin in October 1783 Chełmski held the judgment of 1780 against Prusimski. Prusimski could not show his right to be heard and left it to the court, which fined him, payable at once on pain of banishment, and then let him answer [[1]].

**The inquiry.** Because the cause concerned acts of violence, both parties had witnesses examined on oath [[1]].

**What the court finds.** Men serving Prusimski's household came from Trąbczyn, off their road, to the deserted settlement of Smoleniec, which belongs to Chełmski's Łukom. They asked the Olęder settler Jan Kłonica whose village it was. He answered that these settlers belonged to Chełmski. Prusimski's under-coachman called him a liar and struck him with a whip, the coachman beat him with a pine stick until it broke, and the under-coachman shouted that he should be killed. Kłonica fled into an alder grove. The men went to his house, took a servant from it by force and beat him, on the way to the tavern of Tomice as well; the servant came home the next day. A witness complained that day to Prusimski himself, who passed along the same road and promised to settle the matter at home [[1]].

**The decree.** The acts were done on Chełmski's ground. The inquiry does not show whether Prusimski knew of them or ordered them, and only the coachman Józef and the under-coachman Walenty are accused; neither is at Trąbczyn any longer. Prusimski says he cannot produce them and offers an oath. The court ordered him to swear within six weeks at the court office in Konin that Chełmski's order to keep the two men ready had not reached them, and that he had no means of producing them, on pain of banishment [[1]].
