One entry on three pages of a book of decrees of the land court at Konin for the years 1781 to 1791, kept in the State Archive in Poznań (Archiwum Państwowe w Poznaniu). It is a decree of the sitting of autumn 1783 on an assault by men of Antoni Prusimski's household on a settler of Stanisław Chełmski's. It is in Latin and is one document. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The boundary between Prusimski's Trąbczyn and Chełmski's Łukom had been marked on the ground in December 1782, after the Crown Tribunal's judgment: [[unit:app53170koningr153]]. This decree shows the two neighbours at law again within a year, now over a beating. The Olęder settlers were free tenant farmers whom both estates had planted in the woods along the old boundary; who they belonged to had been part of the boundary dispute. <!-- context -->

The decree opens with an older matter. In 1780 Chełmski had obtained a condemnation against Prusimski at a sitting on the ground at Grab, in the suit on a debt he had bought from the merchant Tracholz: [[unit:app53170koningr117]], [[unit:app53170koningr118]]. A party under such a condemnation had to show that he was entitled to be heard at all before he could answer a new suit. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**Standing.** Chełmski, plaintiff, through Józef Stablewski, raises the condemnation of 1780 against Prusimski, who is cited. Prusimski does not show his right to be heard and submits to the court's discretion. The court lays a money penalty on him, payable at once on pain of banishment, and admits him to the cause [[1]].

**The inquiry.** Because the cause concerns acts of violence, the court orders both parties to examine witnesses on oath, with written questions exchanged beforehand. Both swear that their witnesses are not suborned, and both hand in their records [[1]].

**What the court finds.** Men serving Prusimski's household came from Trąbczyn, off their road, to the deserted settlement of Smoleniec, which belongs to Chełmski's Łukom. They asked the Olęder settler Jan Kłonica whose village it was. He answered that these settlers belonged to Chełmski. Prusimski's under-coachman called him a liar and struck him with a whip, the coachman beat him with a pine stick until it broke, and the under-coachman shouted that he should be killed. Kłonica fled into an alder grove. The men went to his house, took a servant from it by force and beat him, on the way to the tavern of Tomice as well; the servant came home the next day. A witness complained that day to Prusimski himself, who passed along the same road and promised to settle the matter at home [[1]].

**The decree.** The acts were done on Chełmski's ground. The inquiry does not show whether Prusimski knew of them or ordered them, and only the coachman Józef and the under-coachman Walenty are accused; neither is at Trąbczyn any longer. Prusimski says he cannot produce them and offers an oath. The court orders him to swear within six weeks before the castle office at Konin that he did not forestall their arrest, which Chełmski had sought, and that he has no means of producing them, on pain of banishment. The records of the inquiry stay in the chancery [[1]].

### Form and language

The entry is no. 26 of its sitting. It fills leaf 239 verso and leaf 240; for want of room the clerk wrote its end on an earlier leaf, 236 verso, under entry no. 22, and marked both places. The first page is in a hurried hand that abbreviates heavily; from the findings on, a second and clearer hand writes. The images are frames of a black and white microfilm. <!-- context -->

The entry has no date of its own. The heading of the sitting, which the editor transcribed from a page that is not among the images, reads: "Actum in Judiciis Terrestribus Palatinatus Calissiensis Districualibus Feria Secunda post Festum Sanctae Haedvigis Viduae Electae videlicet Die vigesima mensis Octobris Anno Domini Millesimo Septingentesimo Octuagesimo Tertia in Conin Celebratis". The entry stands twenty-six leaves after that heading. <!-- context -->
