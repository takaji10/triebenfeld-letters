### Scans

The folder has three frames of a microfilm of this book, each showing two facing pages. Two carry the first entry. They were cut at the fold, and three of their four halves are shown, in the order in which the entry is read. The third frame has the second entry on both its pages, and both are shown.

### Transcription

The editor transcribed the entry and the heading of its sitting and supplied them by paragraph, with the Latin written out.

The second entry, no. 43 of the sitting of May 1784, the editor had photographed and not transcribed. At the editor's word an AI model (Claude) transcribed it from the film, enlarged, and translated it in the editor's terms. A few of its words are marked as doubtful.

### Check against the scans

An AI model (Claude) read the whole entry against the microfilm and corrected 17 readings. On the first page a line had been skipped, between two occurrences of the words "et quoniam": the court finds Prusimski bound to show his right to be heard, and he does not show it. What Chełmski raises against him is a "condemnation" at Grab, not a sitting on the ground. The witnesses are examined on written questions "exchanged between the parties"; the word that had been left unread after that is "the accused", whose production the court puts off. The servant who came home is "the aforesaid" servant, not one who had been released. Prusimski is to swear "from now and from this act" within six weeks. The clerk's two directions that join the parts of the entry were added. Each change is logged with its reason.

### Division and dating

Each of the two entries is a document. Neither has a date of its own. The second is dated to the year 1784 from the heading of its sitting, 3 May 1784, which is likewise on a page not among the images. The first is dated to the year from the heading of its sitting, 20 October 1783, which stands twenty-six leaves earlier on a page that is not among the images, and the document says so.

### Translation

The English is the editor's own translation and was not made again. It was changed in ten places where the text had been corrected. The editor's translator's notes, which discussed readings now settled, are left out.

The summary was written in German from the Latin and translated into English. Each of its eleven statements was then checked against the document. All held. The summary of the second entry has seven statements, checked the same way; all held.
