In 1644, more than fifty years after the suit over the pond at Łukom, the owners of Łukom were again at law with the Trąmpczyński family, who then owned Trąbczyn. At the land court's sitting at Konin, Jerzy Chełmski of Łukom obtained two judgments by default against the four Trąmpczyński brothers and their widowed mother: one for notches cut into his trees like boundary marks, the other for timber felled in his wood. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

In the seventeenth century Trąbczyn belonged to the family that took its name from it, the Trąmpczyńskis, who bore the by-name Otto. The neighbouring estate of Łukom, or Łukomia, belonged to the Chełmski family. The boundary between the two estates ran through woodland and was disputed for two centuries; it was fixed by the commission of 1775, whose decree is [[unit:app53170koningr145]]. That decree cites judgments of these same years over notches cut into trees and mounds newly raised. <!-- context -->

A boundary was marked by mounds of earth and by notches cut into trees. To cut such notches in a neighbour's wood was to claim the ground as far as they reached. Where a boundary ran was decided by the sub-chamberlain (podkomorzy), a royal official who held his court on the disputed ground itself. <!-- context -->

A plaintiff before a land court had his opponent cited by one of the court's sworn messengers, who laid the citation at the opponent's manor. If the opponent, called aloud at the first term, did not appear, the plaintiff obtained judgment against him by default, "in the penalty of contumacy". <!-- context -->

The land court at Konin was a court of the nobility. The starost or his deputy, as the king's lieutenant, had the leading part in it, with the judge, the sub-judge, the voivode, the chamberlain and the standard-bearer. The court travelled round the palatinate and held its sittings in the district; the terms at which it sat were called "roczki". It was competent in every suit in which a nobleman was the defendant, except those assigned to other courts, such as the dietine courts or the royal court, and except uncontested business. In 1792 its competence passed to a new land court, the "sąd ziemiański". <!-- context -->

### Contents

**The notches.** Jerzy Chełmski, plaintiff, had cited the four brothers Marcin, Wojciech, Władysław and Andrzej Trąmpczyński, sons of the late Stanisław, and Stanisław's widow Anna of Słończyce, who held her dower and a life interest in what he left; any of the brothers under age were cited with their guardians. He charged that they had sent their subjects into his own woodland of the village of Łukom and had about eighty notches cut into trees in the manner of boundary marks, meaning to add a great part of his ground to their own. He reckoned the harm, leaving the ground itself aside, at a thousand Polish marks and as much again in damages, and asked that the marks be annulled once the sub-chamberlain had come to judge the ground. The defendants, summoned at their manor at Trąbczyn, did not appear, and the court gave judgment against them by default [[1]].

**The timber.** In a second suit Chełmski charged the same defendants with having lately felled about a hundred trees fit for building, chiefly pines, and about three hundred cartloads of firewood in the woods of Łukom, and with having used them as they pleased. He reckoned the harm at three hundred Polish marks, and again they were judged by default [[2]].

The land court gave both judgments at its sitting at Konin on 6 June 1644. <!-- context -->
