### Scans

The archive's images show the front cover and seventeen openings of the notebook. The openings were cut at the fold, so that each of its 35 pages, the inside covers included, has its own image.

### Transcription

There was no transcription. An AI model (Claude) read the notebook from the scans, line by line, each opening in enlarged strips and the faint inside covers with the contrast raised. Words not read are marked "[?]"; words read with doubt carry "?" inside square brackets; where only some letters were read, those letters are given. There were 88 such marks, many of them on the two faint inside covers. The editor then read the most uncertain of them on the scans, 39 in all on two sheets that show each beside a picture of its line, and settled 21, many of them differently from the first reading; 70 marks remain, most of them on the inside covers and in the log of photographs. On a third sheet the editor read the place Swięcia twice more where the first reading had Smięcia without doubt. A word broken at the end of a line ends in "¬". Breyer's dividers between notes, a short line with a small circle, are kept. Words he struck out are left out, as everywhere in this edition; his sketches are not transcribed, and can be seen on the scans. The editor has not checked the rest of the reading.

The editor's own English notes, kept beside the scans, render parts of another of Breyer's notebooks in the same archival unit, and were not used.

### Division and dating

The notebook is one document, dated by its cover, July 1936. One page of the photograph log at the back is headed July 1935.

### Translation

The English was written by the AI model from the reading, note by note. It keeps Breyer's notes as notes, gives quoted field names and sayings in German with an English gloss, and uses the edition's spellings for places such as Trąbczyn, Pyzdry and Wrocław.

### Summary

The summary was written in German from the reading and translated into English. Each of its 16 statements was then checked against the document, and two were weakened: the notebook carries the village mayor's stamp but does not say who put it there, and the note of 170 pupils with one teacher does not name the school. Nothing in the summary rests on a word read with doubt.
