Albert Breyer (1889-1939), a teacher at the German school in Sompolno and a historian of the German settlements of central Poland, visited Łazińsk, Emilienheim and their neighbours in July 1936 and wrote down what the settlers told him of their land, the dividing up of the manor, their schools and teachers, emigration, field names, speech, houses and graves – the last account of these communities before the Second World War. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

Łazińsk and Emilienheim were Protestant German settlements on the land of the former Trąbczyn estate, in the commune of Trąbczyn. Two Olęder colonies had been founded on the estate, first called Trąbczyńskie Stare Olędry and Trąbczyńskie Nowe Olędry and later Łazińskie Stare Olędry and Łazińskie Nowe Olędry; their settlers' dispute with the owner of the estate over pasture and wood in his forest is the subject of the summons of 1860, [[unit:app539680783]]. Breyer went from village to village collecting what the settlers remembered and photographing their houses, schools and graves, for his studies of the German settlements. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**The land.** The colony of Łazińsk, in the commune of Trąbczyn, had 2,040 Morgen and 250 farmers. About 1880, Breyer was told, the colonists had received land from the Trąbczyn estate in exchange for their old rights of pasture and wood in its forest; it was registered with their old land at the notary's and was heavily taxed [[1]]. Włodzimierów had been parcelled out about 25 years before from the estate, whose owner was a Count Miączyński, almost all of it to Germans; the remaining manor of some 60 Morgen went to one Jesse, from Białobłoty. Forest land of the estate, bought by a Jew and sold on to Germans five or six years before the First World War, became the new settlement "Trompczyn D." [[1, 2]]. Michalinów was divided into house plots, strips and meadows, with land for the village mayor and the school; Emilienheim had 337 Morgen in all [[1]].

**The schools.** Mielke had taught in Łazińsk since 1925; before him Eduard Zelmer of Łazińsk had taught for 34 years and Rasch for 50, still teaching at 80. On the first day of lessons there were 186 children in the school, and for ten years one teacher had 170 pupils. The army complained to the Ministry of Education that the recruits from the commune of Trąbczyn could not speak Polish [[1]]. Emilienheim had been made part of the village district of Drzewce, being eleven people short of the 200 it needed to stand on its own, and the mayor there did not know the German people [[1]].

**Where they went.** Very many people from Łazińsk had gone to Russia some 65 years before, a month's walk in March, to Volhynia around Novograd-Volynsk and Żytomierz; some came back shortly before the First World War. Others went to America, and one woman to Brazil [[1, 2]]. Under the Prussian Colonisation Commission, which settled Germans on land in the province of Posen, many had moved there and got good, drained land; at Ostfelde near Września the Germans from Łazińsk were later driven off by the Poles for a few groszy [[2]].

**Their life.** Breyer noted whom they married, which hymn books they used, field names, sayings, the slow, drawn-out speech of the "Brücher" of the marsh villages, houses of clay and timber, Pomeranian barns, and the wooden grave boards in Łazińsk and Emilienheim, which he sketched [[1]]. He wrote down too the speech of Obory, the teachers and sextons from 1895 to 1928, and the years 1744, 1782 and 1790 against Züllichau, Old Kazimierka and Józefów, the last "from the Chocz estate", whose owner was Lipski [[2]].
