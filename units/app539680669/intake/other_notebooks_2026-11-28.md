## IMG_5189: a list of 1838 (the year on the facing page)

The notebook is not the one of July 1936 in the edition: its right page is headed "Schulzenlade
Konary" (the headman's chest at Konary), copied entries of 1790; below a rule, a list, each line
a person with status, place, origin and a number (an age, all three readers):

    Bruntsch, okup. Tromp. Hol., Benssen[?], Pr. 58
    Brüllow, gorzel. w Gosławicach, ur. Berlin[?] 24
    Reich, okup. Wielołęka[?], pod Driesen, Pr. 46
    Stroese[?], " [ditto], Zistritz[?], Pr. 39
    Elsner, owcz.[?] w Zagiern.[?], ur. Pacanowice[?], Pr. 29
    Wendt[?], dekan w Kaliszu[?], ..., X.P. 29
    Fürst[?], okup. ..., Miatków[?], X.P. 26
    Ramin[?], okup. Sofiental, ur. Salderhorst[?], Pr. 24

- "okup. Tromp. Hol." = okupnik (holder) in the Trąbczyn Olędry; "gorzel. w Gosławicach" =
  distiller at Gosławice; "Pr." = Prussia, "X.P." = the Grand Duchy of Posen.
- **A couple?** The editor read the first two lines as a marriage of 1838. One reader and Claude
  see two separate entries: each line has its own surname, occupation, place and age, and the
  second is a distiller (a man's trade); the list's other lines (a dean at Kalisz among them) do
  not pair as grooms and brides either. What the list records (marriages, arrivals, a census) the
  page does not say. For the editor.
- The birthplace of Brüllow: "Berlin" (Claude, one reader) or "Berene/Bernau" (the other).

  one of the Prince's colonies of 1805) of Peter Abraham Piper, 29, of Schoeneberg, a schoolmaster,
  and Wilhelmine Schultz, the headman's daughter; "die Erlaubnis hierzu" - the permission for it -
  was given by Honrichs, the Prince of Hohenlohe's commissary at Trąbczyn, in 1808.
- Why it matters: the edition's Honrichs (`honrichs` in reference/people.yml) acting for the Prince
  at Trąbczyn in 1808, after the Governing Commission returned the estates in July 1807; the
  Hohenlohe era's account already has the patrimonial court issuing settlers their papers in
  January 1808 (docs/HOHENLOHE_ERA_PLAN.md, section V). The editor leaned "not relevant" on the
  thought that it was his marriage.
