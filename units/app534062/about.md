In 1727 the general royal messenger Sebastian Szetlewicz of Szetlewek reported to the land court at Kalisz that he had served a citation at the estate of Szetlew, leaving it on the table in the heated room of the house. The messenger's report records the Szetlewicz family, who lived at Szetlewek in the 1720s, and it is the first text in the collection that tells the villages of Szetlew and Szetlewek apart. <!-- context -->

### The entry

The general royal messenger Sebastian Szetlewicz, of the village of Szetlewko, reports to the court that he had carried a citation to the estate of the village of Szetlew and to the residence of the party cited. He adds that on the Thursday next past he had laid and left it there on the table in the heated room, before the cited party's "Conte~" [[1]]. The entry gives neither the case nor the parties: the person served appears only as "the cited one". The report's opening formula does not fully parse as transcribed – the verb of reporting that the parallel formulas carry is not on the page as read – and the entry closes with the phrase "of which, the present report" [[1]].

The entry has no date of its own: only "Feria Quinta proxima praeterita", the Thursday next past, relative to the sitting. The year 1727 is assigned from the volume's title; the sitting's date is to be read from the preceding pages of the book. <!-- context -->

### Why it is here

The holding was added for the messenger's name. A Szetlewicz family was living at Szetlewko – Szetlewek – in the 1720s; Szetlewicz is a surname, not a place name. Just over fifty years later another messenger of the same village, Mathias Matuszkiewicz "de villa Szetlewek", reports in the Konin register in the boundary quarrel between Trąbczyn and Łukomia [[unit:app53170koningr117]]. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The volume belongs to the archive's fonds "Księgi sądu ziemskiego w Kaliszu", the books of the land court at Kalisz. According to the archive's description of the fonds, the land court at Kalisz was a court of the nobility. The starost or his deputy, as the king's lieutenant, had the leading part in it, with the judge, the sub-judge, the voivode, the chamberlain and the standard-bearer. The court travelled round the palatinate and held its sittings in the district; the terms at which it sat were called "roczki". It was competent in every suit in which a nobleman was the defendant, except those assigned to other courts, such as the dietine courts or the royal court, and except uncontested business. In 1792 its competence passed to a new land court, the "sąd ziemiański". The fonds holds resignations, inscriptions, reports, judgments, papers brought in for entry, registers of causes, powers of attorney, inquiries and registers. <!-- context -->

A land court's messenger (woźny) was the officer who carried its citations and executed its orders. When the person cited was not met in person, the citation was left at his dwelling – here, on the table in the heated room, before a member of the household – and the messenger's sworn report of the service, his relatio, was entered in the court's book. The same form, in the same Latin, fills the Konin and Kalisz registers of the 1770s from the years of the boundary dispute. <!-- context -->
