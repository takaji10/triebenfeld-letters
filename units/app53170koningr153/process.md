### Scans

The folder has fourteen images from the archive: one single page and thirteen openings of two facing pages. Each opening was cut at the fold. Twenty-six pages are shown whole; one blank page is left out.

### Transcription

The editor transcribed both acts and supplied them by paragraph, with the Latin written out.

### Check against the scans

This holding had a light check, not a full one. An AI model (Claude) read eight of the twenty-six pages word for word against the scans, and parts of seven more, and corrected 67 readings. Most of the Tribunal's decree was not read through and stands as the editor has it.

Three abbreviations had been written out wrongly throughout, and were corrected in every place on the ground of those that were looked at. The surveyor and the court messenger are "sworn" ("jurato"), where the transcription had "jurisdiction". The sittings on the ground are "condescensio", where it had "conditio", a condition. And the boundary chamberlain of Inowrocław is Gorski, not Gerski. A word the editor had marked as unread, for what is done with the mounds, is "sipatio", their raising.

Other corrections that change the sense: what Chełmski had objected against Prusimski from the sitting at Grab was a "condemnation", not a field inspection; Chełmski alone appeared in person before the Tribunal; the court messenger proclaimed the chamberlain's court "by his voice"; the Marshal of the Tribunal is "castellan" of Gniezno; and in the act for Martin Hunt Prusimski "was present in person", Hunt was delivered "into actual possession", and "obedience" to Prusimski was laid on him. Each change is logged with its reason.

### Division and dating

The chamberlain's decree, with the Tribunal's decree inside it and the note of its entry at Konin, is one document, dated by its opening line to 28 November 1782; it sat until 3 December. The delivery of Martin Hunt is an act of another officer and is a document of its own, of the same day.

### Translation

The English is the editor's own translation and was not made again. It was changed in 25 places where the text had been corrected, and the three words corrected throughout were changed with it. Twenty-five of the editor's forty-two notes are kept, in square brackets; seventeen that discuss readings now corrected are left out. Where the Latin was not read through, the English stands as the editor wrote it.

### Summaries

The summaries were written in German from the Latin and translated into English. Each of their 22 statements was then checked against its document. All held.
