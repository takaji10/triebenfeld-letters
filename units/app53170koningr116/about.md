In 1775 a boundary commission sat on the disputed ground between Trąbczyn and Łukomia to fix the line once and for all. The register of the municipal court at Konin records the notices of its coming, the protests the commissioners, Antoni Prusimski and Stanisław Chełmski made while it sat, and the searches each side made afterwards for a decree that could not be found at Konin. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The Polish parliament appointed a commission to fix the boundary between Antoni Prusimski's Trąbczyn and Stanisław Ścibor Chełmski's Łukomia. It opened on the ground on 13 September 1775 under Ludwik Dąmbski, voivode of Brześć Kujawski, and ruled for Prusimski: [[unit:app53170koningr145]]. Chełmski did not accept the result. He had brought commissioners of his own, who made a decision of their own on the disputed ground, and both sides then came to the municipal court office at Konin, first to protest and later to ask whether a decree had been entered there. <!-- context -->

The municipal court and office of Konin was the starost's court for the nobility of the district. Its office, open every day, registered the business they brought to it, sales, debts, the reports of the court's messengers and their suits, and what was entered in its books was accepted as proof. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**The commission announced, August 1775.** Prusimski registered a copy of the act of parliament that ordered the commission [[1]]. On 16 August 1775 the notices of the commission were registered for him, with his summonses to the heirs and holders of Łukom, Podbiele and Szetlew and to the abbey of Ląd, and Chełmski registered his summonses to the heirs of Trąbczyn, Drzewce and Biskupice [[2]].

**Protests while the commission sat, September and October 1775.** Five of the commissioners sitting on the ground, the voivode Ludwik Dąmbski as president, Brzeziński, Starost of Inowrocław, Dąmbski, standard-bearer of Brześć Kujawski, Kwilecki and Umiński, came in person to protest against Chełmski and "the commissioners brought in on his part", who had made a decision on the disputed ground without them [[3]]. Prusimski protested against Chełmski, against the Gliszczyński family of the neighbouring estate of Tomice and others, for obstructing the boundary proceedings [[4]]. Piotr Rajewicz declared that he had taken no part in them [[5]]. On 3 October 1775 an inspection of the boundary line was announced for Prusimski [[6]], and Chełmski protested in his turn against Prusimski and against the commission's decision [[7]].

**Searches for a decree, from 1776.** On 16 February 1776 Prusimski's men, Antoni Lipiński and Nicolaus Ostrowski, with the Trąbczyn court messenger Bartłomiej Szepczyński, asked whether a decree given against Prusimski in his absence ("in contumaciam") on the disputed ground had been registered at Konin; the office certified that it had not [[8]]. That decree, given against him, cannot be the commission's, which ruled for him; it was presumably the decision of Chełmski's commissioners against which the five had protested. Chełmski twice announced searches of his own, for the commission's decree and for a decree "given between the two estates", but they were never carried out [[9, 10]], and a protest Prusimski announced against Chełmski was never lodged [[11]]. In October 1777 Chełmski came in person and asked for the decree given between the heirs of Nowa Wieś, Łazy, Osiny, Trąbczyn, Łukom and other villages and signed by the commissioners on Prusimski's side; it had not been registered at Konin, and the office certified so [[12]].

The land court, citing Chełmski's search of October 1777, then ordered Prusimski to bring the commission's decree to Konin, where its text now stands: [[unit:app536046]]. <!-- context -->
