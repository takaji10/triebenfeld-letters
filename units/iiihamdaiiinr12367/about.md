Fourteen scans from a file of the Prussian Ministry of Foreign Affairs, kept in the Geheimes Staatsarchiv Preußischer Kulturbesitz, Berlin. The file is the second volume on the claim of Michalina Miączyńska, born Prusimska, and continues [[unit:iiihamdaiiinr12366]]; its cover gives its span as January 1828 to the end of November 1833. The pieces scanned run from February 1828 to March 1832 and concern one matter: the compensation of Weigel, a banker at Wrocław, whose mortgage on the Trąbczyn estates in central Poland the Polish courts had struck out. They are eleven documents on 15 pages: eight in French, two in Polish and one in German. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

Prince Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen (1746-1818), a Prussian general, received the Trąbczyn estates in 1796, after Prussia had confiscated them from the Starost Antoni Prusimski. He borrowed 30,000 thalers on them from Grotowski, and the mortgage passed to Weigel. In 1807 the estates were returned to Prusimski's daughter, Michalina Miączyńska. Between 1819 and 1826 the Polish courts held that the return had cancelled the debts raised in the meantime, and ordered Weigel's mortgage struck out of the register. Prussia protested at St Petersburg in 1827. <!-- context -->

Trąbczyn lay in the Kingdom of Poland, of which the Russian Emperor was king. His brother, Grand Duke Constantine, resided at Warsaw, and Baron Mohrenheim directed the Grand Duke's diplomatic chancellery. Prussia was represented at Warsaw by a consul general, Schmidt. On 29 November 1830 a rising against Russian rule began at Warsaw. It was put down in September 1831, and a provisional government under Engel administered the Kingdom until March 1832. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**Mohrenheim's answers.** On 7 February 1828 Mohrenheim answered Schmidt that he was not losing sight of the Weigel matter and would report what had been settled once the Grand Duke returned [[1]]. Two short private notes from him to Schmidt are not dated. In the first he writes that he read his memorandum on the affair of "Weigel & Eisenhardt" to the council the day before, that the council recognised unanimously that payment must be made, and that the manner of payment was still to be deliberated [[2]]. In the second he returns something Schmidt had lent him to read and promises to push the matter [[3]]. On 20 February 1830 he reported that the Administrative Council had told the Grand Duke the previous August that the competence of the courts which had ruled against Weigel was open to question, because the case could be resolved only by diplomatic means. The Council had authorised the Commission of Finance and Treasury to declare a conflict of jurisdiction and to bring it before the Council of State, and the Grand Duke had now asked the Council to hasten the conclusion [[4]].

**The minister of finance and Weigel, autumn 1830.** A letter to Schmidt of 29 September 1830, whose signature has not been read, states that his notes on the suit between Miączyńska and her creditors, and on Weigel's claim against the treasury of the Kingdom, had been laid before the minister of finance and had warned the Polish administration in time [[5]]. The minister, Prince Lubecki, wrote to Weigel himself on 22 September 1830. Weigel had asked for 63,100 thalers: 30,000 of capital, 31,800 of interest and 1300 of costs. Lubecki found the demand excessive and ordered the estates to be valued on the spot, so that the loss really suffered could be assessed [[6]]. Weigel replied from Wrocław on 14 October 1830 and sent the mortgage certificate and the court valuation of 1803. By the certificate the estates were worth 160000 Reichstaler and by the valuation 128917; the entries ranking before his came to 48166, so that on either figure his claim of 63100 was covered [[7]]. On 27 November 1830 Lubecki answered that the valuation dated from 1803, a time of inflated land prices, and that the claim must be reckoned by the present value of land, because the last judgment in Weigel's case was given in 1826 and only from then could he have sought execution. He would wait for the new valuation [[8]].

**After the rising.** On 14 December 1831 the head of the finance commission wrote to Schmidt that he had ordered the commission to hasten the valuation of Trąbczyn and to examine how well Weigel's capital was secured on it. He added that the special authority which the Emperor had given Lubecki to treat with Weigel could serve no one else, and that he had taken steps to remove that obstacle [[9]]. The letter is signed "R. Fuhrmann" on the scan. On 13 March 1832 Engel informed Schmidt that Józef Lubowidzki, president of the Bank of Poland, had been charged with carrying out the convention of 29 May 1830 between the two governments and was also authorised to settle the questions arising from Weigel's claim [[10]]. On 20 March 1832 Fuhrmann reported that the Commission of Finance and Treasury had on 29 February charged Drake, a commissioner in the department of state domains, with valuing the Trąbczyn estates, and that the work would take some time [[11]].

The scans end with that letter. They do not show whether Weigel was paid. <!-- context -->

### Form and language

The letters to Schmidt are the originals he received, with the letterhead of the Kingdom's foreign affairs or the signature and address of the sender; Engel's letter is a copy. The three pieces exchanged between Lubecki and Weigel are copies. Beside each of Lubecki's Polish letters stands a German translation of the time, written in a second column; it is not transcribed here. <!-- context -->
