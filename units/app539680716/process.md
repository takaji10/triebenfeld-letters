### Transcription

There was no transcription. An AI model (Claude) read the notebook and the sheet from the photographs, line by line, each page in three enlarged strips. Words not read are marked "[?]"; words read with doubt carry "?" inside square brackets; where only some letters were read, those letters are given. There were 68 such marks. The editor then read 17 of them, the ones a name, a place, a heading or the sense of a note rests on, on a sheet that shows each beside a picture of its line, and settled or changed 13 of them; on a second sheet they read 18 more and settled or changed 11; 46 marks remain. A word broken at the end of a line ends in "¬". Breyer's dividers between notes, a short line with a small circle, are kept. Words he struck out are left out, as everywhere in this edition. The Russian keeps its old spelling (ѣ, і, ъ). The editor has not checked the rest of the reading.

### Division and dating

The holding is divided by content into thirteen documents, at the editor's choice: the sheet of 1858; each of the three liquidation tables; Breyer's copies of the servitudes of Łazińsk and of Orliny and his extracts on Emilienheim of 1870 and 1892; his counts of farmers and land; his table of farmers and Morgen; his list of the farmers of Orlin; and his notes of 1935 and 1936. A page that holds the end of one document and the start of the next stands under both. Breyer's copies stand apart from his own notes because a copy takes the date and the era of what it copies, not of the notebook (the editor, 2026-10-14). So the sheet of 1858, the tables of 1868, the extracts dated by their first entry, 26 November 1870, and the copies of the servitudes belong to the years of Włodzimierz Miączyński; the copy of the Łazińsk servitudes is dated to 1868, the year of the table it follows, and the table and servitudes of Orliny have no date. Breyer's own notes and figures belong to the twentieth century: the notes of 1935 and 1936 carry the month, the others are dated to 1936, the year in which he used the notebook, and the list of Orlin to July 1935, between his notes of that month and those of 1936.

### Tables

Six documents are given as tables: the sheet of 1858, the three liquidation tables, the table of farmers and Morgen, and the list of Orlin. Each entry's number stands first and its name second, as in every table of the edition, although on the sheet of 1858 the name comes first. Where the copyist wrote only the last figure of a number, it is given in full; the copyist's dash after a name is left out; a heading repeated at the head of a page is given once. In the Łazińsk table Breyer wrote in pencil, between the lines, who held each holding in 1875: his pencil stands in the name cell after a dash.

### Translation

The English was written by the AI model from the reading. In the tables only the names and the words are translated, and every figure stands in the same cell as in the original. Russian names are transliterated letter by letter, without the hard sign at the end of a word, so a German name written in Russian appears in its Russian form (Shults, Merzh); German and Polish names written in Latin letters are kept as written. Morgen and Hufen are kept; "пр." and "прут." are rods.

### Summary

The summaries were written in German from the reading and translated into English. Each of their 86 statements was then checked against the document, and 16 were weakened or cut: the first draft said of the liquidation tables what is known only from elsewhere, counted rows wrongly where numbers are skipped or doubled, and gave names in German forms that the English does not use. On one page the total Breyer wrote, 1,157.69 Morgen, is 6 Morgen more than his figures add up to; the photograph shows the figures as read, and the summary says so. Nothing in the summaries rests on a word read with doubt.
