### Scans

The folder has fifteen photographs of this book, each showing two facing pages. Each was cut at the fold. Eighteen of the thirty halves carry an entry that is transcribed and are shown whole; twelve are left out.

### Transcription

The editor transcribed the entries that concern Trąbczyn and supplied them by paragraph, with the Latin written out. The long entries are a first reading and mark more than two hundred words as doubtful.

### Check against the scans

This holding had a light check, not a full one. An AI model (Claude) read against the scans every heading and date line, the names of the judges, and five of the eighteen pages word for word: the report of 7 March 1778, the report on the boundary pine, the report of January 1779 and Nosalski's protest. It corrected 50 readings. The long Latin of seven documents was not read through and stands as first read.

Those that change the sense: Prusimski is cited to "produce" his subjects at the sitting on the ground, not to hold them; the judges are Walknowski and Mikorski, and a line the transcription had passed over is restored in the first citation to the Tribunal; the boundary mound, not a second estate, bears the name written above the line; the men of Rzgów were taken "as they fled" with the pines; Nosalski's people were carting trees "on sledges", and he means to have Prusimski's two men "shut out from giving testimony". One word was corrected throughout, sixteen times: "Patrui", the uncle's, for "Patrii" or "Patris", the father's. It was looked at on one page, where it is written "Patruj", and corrected elsewhere on that ground. Chełmski's signature under his two protests was added. Each change is logged with its reason.

### Division and dating

Each entry is a document. The note of Prusimski's first protest and the text it points to on the facing leaf are one. Four documents are dated by the line "Actum in Conin" directly above them and three by the heading of their sitting elsewhere on the opening. Six have no dated heading on a photographed page: four are dated to the month from the headings before and after them, two to the year. Each says so.

### Translation

The English is the editor's own translation and was not made again. It was changed in 23 places where the text had been corrected, and "father" was made "uncle" in fifteen. Where the Latin was not read through, the English stands as the editor wrote it.

### Summaries

The summaries were written in German from the Latin and Polish and translated into English. Each of their 54 statements was then checked against its document and rests on words that are not marked as doubtful. All held.
