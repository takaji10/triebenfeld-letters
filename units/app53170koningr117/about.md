The boundary decree of 1775 did not make peace between Antoni Prusimski of Trąbczyn and Stanisław Ścibor Chełmski of Łukomia. In 1778 and 1779 Chełmski sued Prusimski on a debt he had bought from a townsman, Jan Tracholz; Prusimski appealed to the Crown Tribunal, the highest court of the Polish kingdom; Chełmski protested against the boundary decree; and trees were cut and stolen at the boundaries of Trąbczyn. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The commission of 1775 had given the disputed ground to Prusimski: [[unit:app53170koningr145]]. In October 1777 the land court of Kalisz, sitting at Konin, ordered him to bring that decree to the Konin records: [[unit:app536046]]. At the same sitting, on 20 October 1777, it ordered, in another suit of Chełmski's against him, a sitting of the court on the ground at Trąbczyn, a "condescensio": a sitting held on the estate in dispute by the court or by a judge it deputes. <!-- context -->

Tracholz had a claim from a contract for oak timber made in 1744 with Paweł Prusimski, Antoni's uncle, and made it over to Chełmski. Prusimski answered that the papers that would show who owed whom lay with the heirs of his uncle's widow, Katarzyna, born Rozdrażewska, from whom he was still suing to recover them, and that no court could judge the claim without them. The widow is the Katarzyna Prusimska of the inspection of 1754 and the potash suit of 1763: [[unit:app53170koningr136]], [[unit:app536045]]. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**The sitting on the ground, March 1778.** On 7 March 1778 Chełmski's messenger reported that he had served a royal citation on Prusimski for the sitting fixed on the ground of Trąbczyn for the Monday after Invocavit Sunday; Prusimski was to produce there named subjects of his, one of them the shepherd of Nowa Wieś [[1]]. Prusimski protested against Chełmski and the judges of the land court: the claim bought from Tracholz was unlawful, the papers were with the heirs of his uncle's widow, and the land court had earlier ruled that Tracholz's claim be heard together with those heirs, which its new decree contradicted [[3]]. On 30 March his messengers reported that they had served a citation before the Crown Tribunal at Piotrków on Chełmski and on the judge Franciszek Wierusz Walknowski, the sub-judge Stefan Zielonacki and the notary Xawery Mikorski [[2]].

**After the sitting, April 1778.** On 15 April Prusimski protested again, now also against Zielonacki as judge at the sitting on the ground: Zielonacki had, at Chełmski's instance, sent the cause back to the full land court, although that court stood cited before the Tribunal [[7]]. A second citation before the Tribunal was served on Chełmski and the same officers of the court; it asks that, if the papers show a debt of Tracholz to the uncle, Chełmski be made to pay it [[8]]. Chełmski for his part had Prusimski cited before the land court, to have the sum made over by Tracholz adjudged to him with interest and costs [[9]]. Zielonacki's decree sending the cause back was registered [[10]].

**Chełmski against the boundary decree, April 1778.** In two protests Chełmski set his own boundary decree against the one given for Prusimski. His had been given in 1775 by the commissioners he had brought in, on the strength of an inspection of the boundary made in 1592, and he had sworn to it himself at the last boundary mound. He calls the latter a private act, written after the boundary had been drawn and the mounds raised, which Prusimski had neither brought to the records of his own province nor carried out [[4]]. The second protest says that Prusimski's commissioners left the inspection of the line, the raising of the mounds and the taking of the oath to Albert Czarnecki, boundary chamberlain of Kalisz, whom Prusimski had engaged; that Czarnecki had the decree written some weeks after the commissioners had gone; and that Prusimski's fellow-swearers were unfit [[5]].

**Trees.** A messenger reported for Prusimski that a pine several decades old, marked with three crosses, on a mound dividing Trąbczyn from Biskupie, had been cut down and taken at night by Bogusławski, heir of a part of Kurów [[6]]. On 29 January 1779 the same messenger reported twelve stumps of freshly cut pines on Trąbczyn's own ground and more beyond and between the mounds, cut by subjects of Rzgów, and laid before the office twenty axes taken from them as they fled [[11]]. Marcin Nosalski, heir of Rzgów, protested: his people had been carting trees on sledges from Chełmski's woods when, on Tomasz Joachimowicz's orders, the two stopped them on the public road, beat them and took their axes; he means to have those men shut out from giving testimony [[12]]. Prusimski in turn protested against the Nosalskis [[13]].

The commissioners' decree that Chełmski praises in his two protests is the "unilateral act" against which five of the parliament's commissioners protested in 1775, and for which Prusimski's men searched at Konin in 1776: [[unit:app53170koningr116]]. <!-- context -->

Antoni Prusimski calls Paweł Prusimski his "patruus", his father's brother; no other holding says how the two were related. <!-- context -->
