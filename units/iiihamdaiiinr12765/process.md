### Scans

The archive photographed the file as 46 openings, two facing pages at a time. Nine blank openings were left out. Every other opening was cut at its fold, and only a side with writing on it became a page of the edition: 48 pages and a cover. The editor placed each fold line.

### Transcription

The German and French text was transcribed by an AI system and supplied by the editor, one file for each scan. It was not made by a trained reader of old handwriting.

### Division into documents

Each letter, draft or note is one document, thirty in all. A reply drafted on an incoming letter counts as a document of its own. Shorter marks made by the office that received a letter, such as who it was passed to or what should be answered, stay with that letter and are shown at its end under a label, "Written by the receiving office". Dates are taken from each document's own dateline.

### Corrections

An AI model (Claude) went through the transcription and proposed corrections. A word was changed only where something other than the model's sense of the sentence supported the change: a second copy of the same text elsewhere in the file, a fixed formula of the chancery, or a misspelling one letter away from the only word that fits. 193 words were corrected this way, and each change is logged with its reason. The editor checked a random twenty against the scans and found all twenty right, then ruled on the doubtful ones by looking at the page. A passage too garbled to settle was left as it stands. Nine drafts are still rough and are marked as such.

### Paraphs

Officials initialled drafts instead of signing them, and the transcription read these marks badly. Every one was cropped from the scans and laid side by side. They sort into four hands, which the editor confirmed as Hardenberg, Stägemann, Hoffmann and Balan, and the names are filled out in square brackets. An earlier reading of one mark as "Hoym" was dropped: a minister of that name had died in 1807.

### Addition from the scan

In the letter of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen to Stein the transcription had left out a clause giving the sum he paid to free the estates from debt. It was read on the scan and added at the editor's wish, and the letter's own arithmetic confirms the figure [[6]].

### Reading and summaries

The model read each document whole and recorded who writes to whom, what is said, how legible it is and which words it doubts. German summaries were written from that reading and then checked by a second, separate run of the model, which was allowed only to cut or weaken a statement the text does not bear out. It changed eight summaries. The English summaries are translations of the checked German ones.

### Translation

The model translated each document with its reading notes and doubtful words in front of it. An automatic check then confirms that every figure, every name and every mark of doubt in the German has survived into the English. A few titles and names were then corrected by hand in the English. A translation can only be as good as the transcription under it: where a draft is badly read, its English is uncertain too.

### Open points

Some words remain unread and are listed for the editor. A few clauses and office marks that are on the scans but not in the transcription have not been added. No themes have been assigned to these documents.
