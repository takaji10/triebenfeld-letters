The file was kept by the third section of the Prussian Ministry of Foreign Affairs from 1814 to 1820. It concerns the claim of Prince Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen (1746-1818), a Prussian general, to be compensated for the Trąbczyn estates in the former province of South Prussia, which were taken from him in 1807. It contains thirty documents: incoming petitions and reports, and the ministry's drafts of its replies and instructions. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

In 1796 the King of Prussia granted Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen a group of estates in South Prussia, the province formed from Prussia's share of the partitions of Poland. The estates had been confiscated from the Starost Prusimski after the uprising of 1794 [[4, 14]]. They comprised Trąbczyn with several villages in the district of Konin, and Kamionna and Kolno further west [[4]]. Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen sold Kamionna and Kolno to Leixner, a bank director; the file gives the year of the sale both as 1804 and as 1805 [[4, 14]].

Prussia ceded the province by the Peace of Tilsit in 1807, and the Duchy of Warsaw was established in it. Fourteen weeks after the peace a Polish administrative commission took possession of all the estates without a court judgment and transferred them to Prusimski's daughter [[4]]. She is named in the file as Miączyńska and as Dąbska, after two husbands [[4, 19]]. Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen brought an action, and the tribunal at Kalisz declared that it had no authority to set aside the commission's order [[4]]. After the Congress of Vienna the district of Konin belonged to the Kingdom of Poland under the Russian Emperor, to whom the claim was thereafter addressed [[8]].

### Contents

#### Petitions of 1814 and 1815

Peter Friedrich von Triebenfeld, the general agent of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, petitioned the State Chancellor, Hardenberg, in Paris in May 1814 and in Vienna in October 1814 and March 1815 [[1, 3, 4]]. The petition of March 1815 states the case in full and asks for the return of the Trąbczyn estates with nine years' revenue, 36,500 Rthl [[4]]. Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen wrote in April 1815 to Baron vom Stein and in May 1815 to Hardenberg. He gives his loss as 325,400 écus in the first letter and as more than 261,000 Rthl in the second, and in the second asks to be given the crown estate of Sokolnik in compensation [[6, 8]]. The replies defer the matter until the settlement of Polish affairs and refer to a negotiation on the compensation of all holders of granted estates in South Prussia [[2, 3, 5, 7, 9]].

#### Report of Zerboni di Sposetti, 1815

Hardenberg requested a report from Zerboni di Sposetti, the senior Prussian official at Poznań [[10]]. The report of 2 November 1815 rejects the calculations submitted for Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, states that he had allowed Trąbczyn to deteriorate and had encumbered it with debt, and concludes that Prussia could do no more than ask the Russian government to decide the claim [[14]]. A marginal opinion of April 1816 records that Triebenfeld had died [[14]].

#### Intercession at St Petersburg, 1816

In April 1816 the ministry instructed the Prussian envoy at St Petersburg, Major General von Schöler, to bring the request to the Emperor's notice in general terms and without pressing it, compensation being a matter of grace [[15, 17]]. Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen was informed of the instruction [[16]]. Schöler reported on 23 November 1816 that the Russian ministry had refused, and the ministry sent Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen a copy of the Russian note [[25, 26]].

#### Petition of the widow von Brehmer, 1816

The widow of Colonel von Brehmer petitioned as heir of her brother, Major von Seehausen, who in 1803 had lent Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen 5,000 Rthl secured on Szetlewek, a village of the Trąbczyn estates; neither capital nor interest had been paid [[19]]. A marginal opinion on her petition holds that any compensation granted by Russia should go to the creditors registered on the estates [[19]]. The ministry directed her to sue Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen before his own court and informed Schöler of the creditors' position [[20, 21, 22]]. She then submitted a judgment of 1806 in her favour; the ministry replied that it could take no further step [[23, 24]].

#### Claim of the heirs, 1820

After the death of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen his heirs had their claim recommended to the Russian government by Duke Eugen of Württemberg, citing two Prussian generals who had been compensated [[27]]. The Russian envoy at Berlin, Alopeus, replied on 31 January 1820 that the claim had been refused in 1816 and that the Emperor could not act on the recommendation [[27]]. The ministry took no further step and referred the matter to the Minister of the Interior, von Schuckmann [[29]].

#### Request of Count Bethusy, 1819 to 1820

The same Russian note refuses an unrelated request. Count Ernst Bethusy, a landowner in Silesia and in the Kingdom of Poland, had asked for free movement between his properties under article 18 of the Treaty of Vienna. The Polish government held that the article applied only to properties divided by the new border [[27, 28]]. Both Prussian ministries considered the request inadmissible [[29, 30]].

### Form and language

Twelve documents are drafts of outgoing letters, bearing the paraphs of the officials who approved them [[2, 5, 7, 9, 10, 16, 17, 21, 22, 24, 26, 29]]. Three drafts are written on the incoming letter to which they reply [[2, 9, 10]]. One reply to Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen exists as a cancelled draft and its fair copy, which was not sent [[11, 12]]. The paraphs are those of Hardenberg, the Councillor of State Stägemann, and the officials Hoffmann and Balan; the edition expands them in square brackets [[5, 22, 29]]. The documents are in German, except five in French [[6, 7, 13, 27, 28]]. The drafts are written in a cursive chancery hand, and several are transcribed imperfectly; this is noted on the page of each document concerned. <!-- context -->
