In 1806 Prince Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, through his agent Peter Friedrich von Triebenfeld, let a parcel of the Trąbczyn forest to the settler Christoph Erbet in hereditary lease. The court file holds the lease and the proceedings of 1811 before the peace court at Konin, in the Duchy of Warsaw, to recover the costs. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen held the Trąbczyn estates in South Prussia by a royal grant of 1796. As lord of the estates he maintained a patrimonial court, which sat at Mariantów and recorded contracts made with the settlers. His general agent, Peter Friedrich von Triebenfeld, let parcels of forest and waste land in hereditary lease: the lessee paid a sum on entry and a fixed annual rent, and held the land heritably. After 1807 the district belonged to the Duchy of Warsaw, and its courts worked in Polish. <!-- context -->

### Contents

The record of the court, dated 31 March 1806, sets out the contract. Triebenfeld, as authorised agent of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, grants Christoph Erbet of the Trąbczyn Hauland one Hufe, Magdeburg measure, in the Trąbczyn forest for 100 Rthl entry money and an annual rent of 30 Rthl [[1]]. The first three years are free of rent for the quite-unimproved acre, and the first payment, of 15 Rthl, falls due at Christmas 1809 [[1]]. The court's order of 23 May 1806 directs that the contract be issued [[1]].

Filed with the contract are the general power of attorney given to Triebenfeld on 19 February 1805, in a certified copy, and the issued contract with its covering notes to both parties [[1]]. A letter of 1811, in Polish, records that an official, Hawich, handed the file to the peace court of the Konin district and asked that costs of 9 Rthl 18 dgl be recovered from Erbet [[1]].
