After the reform of 1864 made the peasants of the Kingdom of Poland owners of the land they farmed, officials drew up a liquidation table for every estate, fixing the land each farmstead received and the rights it kept in the landowner's forest. At Trąbczyn those decisions were fought over: the owner, Count Włodzimierz Miączyński, appealed against what the commissioners granted his peasants and a neighbour, and the colonists of Łazińsk petitioned for their old rights in his forest. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The ukases of 19 February (2 March) 1864 made the peasants of the Kingdom of Poland owners of the land they farmed, and kept for them their servitudes, the rights of pasture, wood and litter in the manor's land. Commissioners for peasant affairs, each over a district, settled who was entitled to what; appeals went to the commission for the governorate at Kalisz, and above it to the Constituent Committee in Warsaw, the body that carried out the reform, and its Central Commission. The liquidation table of each village recorded the result; the table of the Łazińsk colony, approved in 1868, is in [[unit:apl399806603]]. Daniel and Wilhelm Hohm, among the Łazińsk petitioners, are probably the colonists that table writes in Russian as Daniel and Wilhelm Holm. <!-- context -->

### Contents

In 1864 and 1865 the peasants of Trąbczyn, Nowawies and Marianton and Count Miączyński petitioned the commission, and the Commissioner of the 5th District gave his decisions on 13/25 May 1865; the commission corresponded with the military chiefs of the Konin and Kalisz districts. From 1867 to 1870 the Central Commission sent its orders and the commissioners and the Słupca district commission their reports [[1]].

A plan of the village of Trąbczyn of 15 May 1865 shows the peasants' and the manor's gardens and buildings [[2]].

On 23 July 1865 Miączyński asked the commission to annul the decision of a commissioner, Baron von Rosen, granting Krystian Lippeldt, of a neighbouring village, pasture for 2 horses and 7 head of horned cattle on the folwark Włodzimirów, at three hollows called "smugi" on the boundary of Emilienhejm. Lippeldt had grazed there by written leave, under a contract with the owner that ended on 1 November 1863 and a new agreement of 1 May 1864, which Lippeldt himself had given up; the owner held that a stranger who had not used the pasture at the time of the ukase had no right to it [[4]]. On 10/22 August 1865 he appealed against the decision of the Commissioner of the 5th District of 13/25 May 1865, which ordered six farmsteads of Nowawies, of 5 Morgen each, returned to the peasants who had held them. He wrote that those peasants had left of their own will before 1856, that he had settled new peasants on land from the folwark, and that the old land was now under the folwark's crop rotation [[3]].

The colonists of Łazińsk wrote to the Constituent Committee on 6/18 December 1867 for the recognition of their servitudes of pasture in the Trąbczyn forest, litter and stumps, and asked for an impartial official [[5]]. On 10/22 August 1868 they wrote again: under Prince Hohenlohe's privilege of 1805 they might graze in the Studzienka forest section, rake litter, and use 6 Morgen of clay land; the owner had taken these from them since 1838, and had brought false witnesses before Commissioner Władimirow [[6]].

The index gives these papers at its numbers 30, 59, 75, 102 and 112; the editor photographed them from the file [[1]]. The resolution the commission wrote in the margin of the petition of 1865, in Russian, is only partly read [[3]].
