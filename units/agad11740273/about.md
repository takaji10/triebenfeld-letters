Four papers from the files of the Governing Commission (Komisja Rządząca), kept in the Central Archives of Historical Records (Archiwum Główne Akt Dawnych) in Warsaw. The archive calls the file "Papers of the Governing Commission during its stay at Dresden". The four pieces published here were written at Dresden between 20 and 23 July 1807 and concern one matter: the return of the confiscated estates of the Starost Antoni Prusimski, Trąbczyn among them, to his daughter Michalina Dąbska. They are three petitions of hers and the commission's draft resolution, on five pages; three are in Polish and one in French. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

Prussia confiscated the estates of the Starost Antoni Prusimski in 1796 and gave Trąbczyn and Kamionna with Kolno to Prince Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen (1746-1818), a Prussian general. In the war of 1806 and 1807 Napoleon drove Prussia out of the Polish provinces it had taken in the partitions. On 14 January 1807 he set up the Governing Commission, a provisional government of Poles under Stanisław Małachowski. It administered those provinces until 5 October 1807, when the government of the new Duchy of Warsaw took office. In July 1807, after the Peace of Tilsit, the commission was at Dresden, where Napoleon gave the Duchy its constitution. <!-- context -->

Józef Wybicki, a member of the commission, had also lost estates to Prussia. Napoleon returned them to him by a decree given at Finckenstein on 5 June 1807, and the commission ordered the decree carried out on 12 July: [[unit:agad1174016]]. Michalina Dąbska took that decree as her precedent. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**The petitions of 20 and 21 July.** On 20 July 1807 Dąbska wrote to the commission in Polish. She had twice asked for the return of the estates confiscated from her father for the benefit of the Prussian generals she names as Prince Hohenlohe, Bischoffswerder and Zanitz: Trąbczyn in the department of Kalisz, and Kamionna, Kolno, Pawłowo, Pawłówko and a storehouse at a place written "Wrocławek" in the department of Poznań. She had had no answer. She asked the commission to intercede for her, so that she might obtain the judgment Wybicki had obtained from the Emperor [[2]]. On 21 July she petitioned Napoleon himself, in French and in the same terms [[3]]. The commission's note on that petition records that it was presented at an extraordinary session at Dresden on 21 July 1807 by the Prince Director of War, on the Emperor's order [[3]]. The note does not name the director; the commission's director of war was Prince Józef Poniatowski.

**The resolution of 21 July.** The commission's draft resolution is dated Dresden, 21 July 1807. It states that Dąbska's case had been referred to the commission by the Emperor. It takes as its example the justice done to Wybicki by the decree given at Finckenstein, and extends that judgment to her case and to those of General Niemojewski and Wichrowski: the hereditary estates of these persons, confiscated by the former Prussian government, are to return to their true owners [[1]]. The draft names no estate. It is corrected in several places and carries the paraph of the president, Małachowski [[1]]. The fair copy is the entry in the commission's register: [[unit:agad1174016]].

**The petition of 23 July.** Two days later Dąbska wrote to the commission again. The Emperor's decree for Wybicki had been taken as the principle and pattern for the commission's decree of 21 July. She asked that the commission have its resolution carried out as the decree for Wybicki had been, by directing the Directorate of Internal Affairs to order the administrative chambers to put her in possession of the estates [[4]]. The commission noted the petition as presented on 23 July 1807 and filed it [[4]].

The papers published here do not include the order that followed. Trąbczyn was handed to Dąbska's agent in October 1807: [[unit:oe1bu9454]]. <!-- context -->

### Form and language

The three petitions are the originals, each signed "Michalina z Prusimskich Dambska" or, in French, "Micheline Dambska née Prusimska". The commission's office noted on each the day it was presented; these notes stand at the foot of the page here, though they are written at its head. The petition to the Emperor is addressed on the back to Małachowski. The resolution is the commission's own corrected draft. The outer edge of that leaf is torn, and two words lost there are supplied in square brackets. <!-- context -->
