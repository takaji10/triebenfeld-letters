### Scans

The editor supplied five images from the file. Each shows two facing pages, of which one belongs to this matter; that page was cut out of the image and the other left aside. The edition has five pages. The pieces are numbered here by the image they are on. The leaves themselves carry pencil numbers (5, 7 and 8 for the first three pieces), which the editor's transcription uses.

### Transcription

The editor supplied the transcription as one text, written paragraph by paragraph and not line by line. It was divided to match the pages. The address of the petition to the Emperor is written on the back of its leaf and stands on a page of its own.

### Division into documents

Each petition and the draft resolution is one document, four in all, in the order in which they are bound. Dates are taken from each document's own dateline.

### What the commission's office wrote

On each petition the commission's office noted the day it was presented. These notes are written at the head of the leaf. In the edition they stand at the foot of the page under the label "Written by the receiving office", so that the petition reads first.

### Corrections

An AI model (Claude) compared the whole transcription with the scans and corrected it where the page is plain. 25 passages were corrected and two short lines added. Most restore the writers' spelling and abbreviations, which the transcription had modernised or spelled out. Three change the sense. The note on the petition to the Emperor says that it was presented by the Prince Director of War on the Emperor's order. In the draft resolution a word that is struck out on the page was removed, and the abbreviation for "the Most Serene Emperor" was read correctly. The president's paraph, which the transcription had left out, was added under the draft and under that note. Each change is logged with its reason.

### Summaries

The summaries were written in German from a reading of each document against its scan, and translated into English. Each statement in them was then checked against its document, under the same rules as the separate check that the summaries of other holdings received: a summary may say only what the document says. All of them held.

### Translation

The documents were translated into English in a working session under the same rules as the other holdings: every figure, name and mark of doubt carried across, names in the edition's settled forms where it has settled them and otherwise as the page spells them, and passages that do not read left visibly doubtful rather than smoothed. The three Polish documents were translated from the Polish and the petition to the Emperor from the French. The forms of address are given in English (the Most Illustrious, Mr, Mrs); the word in the closing formula of the petition of 23 July that is not a Polish word as it stands is left in Polish. The same automatic check as elsewhere then confirmed that every figure and every mark of doubt survives into the English.

### Still to do

The English has not yet been read against the scans. One word in the closing formula of the petition of 23 July is not securely read. Words the writers struck out are not transcribed, by the editor's rule; at the end of the draft resolution a whole clause is struck out in this way.
