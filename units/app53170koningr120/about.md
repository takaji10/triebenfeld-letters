The quarrel between Antoni Prusimski of Trąbczyn and Stanisław Ścibor Chełmski of Łukomia went on into the later 1780s. In one of the two entries chosen from the Konin register of 1785 and 1786 a subject of the Trąbczyn estate shows the court the injuries of a beating on the road; in the other Chełmski presses on with the suit over the debt he had bought from the merchant Tracholz, begun nine years before. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

Chełmski had bought from the merchant Jan Tracholz a claim that went back to a contract of 1744 for oak timber with Paweł Prusimski, Antoni's uncle, and had pursued it since 1777 through the land court, sittings on the ground and the Crown Tribunal: [[unit:app53170koningr117]], [[unit:app53170koningr118]]. Prusimski had always answered that the papers were not in his hands. The second entry here shows the suit still alive nine years on, and Chełmski now saying that the contract itself had stayed with Prusimski. <!-- context -->

A person who had been beaten could come to the municipal court office and have his injuries seen and written down; the entry served as proof in a later suit. Such an entry is headed "obduxit". <!-- context -->

### Contents

**The beating of Andrzej Kinos.** On 14 March 1785 Andrzej Kinos of Trąbczyn showed the office a bloodshot and swollen right eye, a bloodshot left eye, a swollen face and a swollen right shoulder, and stated that Marcin Sarzyński had beaten him with fists and a stick the Saturday before, barring his way on the public road at Obory as he went to Pleszew [[1]].

**Chełmski's citation of October 1786.** A court messenger reported that he had served on Prusimski a citation before the Crown Tribunal, dated at Piotrków on 2 October 1786. Chełmski asks that Prusimski be bound under oath to produce the contract made with Tracholz for the sale of an oak wood, which had stayed with him. He asks that he be punished for having had the cause sent from the court of the sitting on the ground to the full land court and on to the Tribunal. He asks, too, that the land court's decrees fixing the sitting on the ground be carried out [[2]].

**Dating.** The second entry has no dated heading on the photographed pages and is dated from the citation it reports. <!-- context -->
