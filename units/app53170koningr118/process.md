### Scans

The folder has thirty-two photographs of this book, each showing two facing pages. Each was cut at the fold. Thirty-seven of the sixty-four halves carry an entry that is transcribed and are shown whole; twenty-seven are left out.

### Transcription

The editor transcribed the entries that concern Trąbczyn and supplied them by paragraph, with the Latin written out. One note on a photographed page was not in the editor's text; an AI model (Claude) transcribed it from the scan, four lines in a clear hand. One entry in the editor's text, a note of a decree for the town of Brdów, does not concern Trąbczyn and is left out.

### Check against the scans

This holding had a light check, not a full one. Claude read against the scans every heading and date line, the twenty short entries word for word, the opening and close of the longer ones, and the signatures, and corrected 42 readings. The long Latin of five documents and the Polish of one were not read through and stand as first read.

Those that change the sense: the first note is a report "to the Tribunal", and its plaintiff Zielonacki is "judge", not the court; Gałecki and Skórzewski are "the former and the present" heirs of Biskupice; a citation is to the "condescension", the sitting on the ground, at Grab; the abbot is Ludwik Benedykt Lubstowski; the witnesses of a note of August 1782 are Reczkowski, Świętosław Bogusławski and the messenger Matuszkiewicz; and the boundary decree of December 1782 was "given", not entered "in the protocol". Signatures were added under six entries. Each change is logged with its reason.

### Division and dating

Each entry is a document. Ten are dated by the line "Actum in Conin" directly above them and three by the heading of their sitting elsewhere on the opening. Fifteen have no dated heading on a photographed page: six of them could be dated to the month and nine only to the year, from the headings before and after them. One heading names the Saturday before Michaelmas and the 29th, which was the Sunday; it is dated to the Saturday, the 28th. Each says so.

### Translation

The English is the editor's own translation and was not made again. It was changed in thirteen places where the text had been corrected; six lines that had come into the file from a chat window were taken out; and the note that was newly transcribed was translated in the editor's terms. Where the Latin was not read through, the English stands as the editor wrote it.

### Summaries

The summaries were written in German from the Latin and Polish and translated into English. Each of their 69 statements was then checked against its document and rests on words that are not marked as doubtful. All held.
