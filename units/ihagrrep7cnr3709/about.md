Michalina Prusimska, the daughter of Antoni Prusimski, was still a minor when she went to law against Prince Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen over the estates of Brzyce in Kuyavia, which had come to her from her mother but had been confiscated with her father's lands. From 1800 to 1802 she complained to the King of the delays, years before the Duchy of Warsaw gave all the estates back to her. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

In 1796 the King of Prussia granted Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen estates in South Prussia that had been confiscated from the Starost Antoni Prusimski after the uprising of 1794. Among them was Brzyce in Kujawy. Michalina Prusimska was his daughter. In South Prussia a "Regierung" was the province's high court, and its councillors also sat on the board that supervised guardianships. In 1807, after Prussia had ceded the province, a Polish commission transferred all the estates to her. <!-- context -->

### The lawsuit

Prusimska, through the guardian the court had appointed her, sued Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen before the Government at Poznań for the recovery of the Brzyce estates [[3]]. She claimed them as her mother's property [[1]]. Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen argued that her father, who was still alive, had held a lifetime right in his late wife's property, and that this right had passed to the fisc with the rest of his confiscated property [[3]]. In January 1802 Prusimska wrote that the suit had by then lasted four years [[10]].

### Contents

He returned them on 1 July with the order to finish preparing the case, which had been delayed by indulgence towards the Prince's agent, to try a settlement, and failing that to give judgment quickly [[2]].>>>

 On 7 December the Grand Chancellor ordered that the Government at Warsaw should judge the case, and the Kammergericht, the high court in Berlin, any appeal [[4, 5, 6]].

**The Prince asks for other judges, November and December 1800.** On 30 November 1800 Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen asked the Grand Chancellor to take the judgment away from Poznań, because the guardianship board that conducted the suit for the plaintiff was made up of the same councillors who would judge it [[3]]. In a covering letter he added that a settlement proposed by the court's deputy made him doubt the court's impartiality [[3]]. On 7 December the Grand Chancellor ordered that the Government at Warsaw should judge in the first instance and the Kammergericht in Berlin on appeal [[4]], had rescripts to that effect drafted for the three courts [[5]], and told the Prince [[6]].

**The order comes too late.** The Government at Poznań reported on 18 December that it had already given judgment on 9 December, as the King's order of July had required speed [[7]]. The order to Warsaw was withdrawn, and the Prince was told that the appeal would still go to the Kammergericht [[8, 9]]. How the court decided is not known.

**Prusimska's petition, January 1802.** From Dresden, on 22 January 1802, Prusimska asked the King in French to hasten the appeal. The minister of justice, von der Reck, had her told on 29 January that she could expect the appeal judgment to be published [[10]].
