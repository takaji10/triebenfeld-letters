A file of the Prussian central administration for South Prussia, kept in the Geheimes Staatsarchiv Preußischer Kulturbesitz, Berlin. It concerns the lawsuit of Michalina Prusimska, a minor, against Prince Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen (1746-1818) for the Brzyce estates, and runs from 18 June 1800 to 29 January 1802. It holds fifteen documents on 19 pages, in German, with one petition in French. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

In 1796 the King of Prussia granted Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen estates in South Prussia that had been confiscated from the Starost Antoni Prusimski after the uprising of 1794. Among them was Brzyce in Kujawy. Michalina Prusimska was his daughter. In South Prussia a "Regierung" was the province's high court, and its councillors also sat on the board that supervised guardianships. In 1807, after Prussia had ceded the province, a Polish commission transferred all the estates to her. <!-- context -->

### The lawsuit

Prusimska, through her guardian, sued Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen before the Government at Poznań for the recovery of the Brzyce estates [[5]]. The King's order of June 1800 describes the case as one over her maternal property [[2]]. Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen argued that her father, who was still alive, had held a lifetime right in his late wife's property, and that this right had passed to the fisc with the rest of his confiscated property [[6]]. In January 1802 Prusimska wrote that the suit had by then lasted four years [[15]].

### Contents

**The King calls in the files, June and July 1800.** On a complaint from Prusimska the King ordered the Government at Poznań, on 14 June 1800, to send him the files of the case [[2]]. He returned them on 1 July with the order to finish preparing the case, which had been delayed by indulgence towards the Prince's agent, to try a settlement, and failing that to give judgment quickly [[4]]. The Government sent copies of both orders to Berlin with short reports [[1, 3]].

**The Prince asks for other judges, November and December 1800.** On 30 November 1800 Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen asked the Grand Chancellor to take the judgment away from Poznań, because the guardianship board that conducted the suit for the plaintiff was made up of the same councillors who would judge it [[5]]. In a covering letter he added that a settlement proposed by the court's deputy made him doubt the court's impartiality [[6]]. On 7 December the Grand Chancellor ordered that the Government at Warsaw should judge in the first instance and the Kammergericht in Berlin on appeal [[7]], had rescripts to that effect drafted for the three courts [[8, 9, 10]], and told the Prince [[11]].

**The order comes too late.** The Government at Poznań reported on 18 December that it had already given judgment on 9 December, as the King's order of July had required speed [[12]]. The rescript to Warsaw was withdrawn [[13]] and the Prince informed that the appeal would still go to the Kammergericht [[14]]. The judgment itself is not in the file, and the file does not say how the court decided.

**Prusimska's petition, January 1802.** From Dresden, on 22 January 1802, Prusimska asked the King in French to hasten the appeal. The Cabinet passed the petition to Minister von der Reck, who had her told on 29 January that she could expect the appeal judgment to be published [[15]].
