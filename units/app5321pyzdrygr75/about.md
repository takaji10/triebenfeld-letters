On 7 September 1768, as another document of the edition dates it, at the municipal court of Pyzdry, the merchant Jan Tracholz of Witkowo acknowledged that he made over to Stanisław Chełmski, the neighbour of the Prusimski family, the claims he held against the late Paweł Prusimski of Trąbczyn: 1,053 imperials, 13 groschen and (it seems) 6 pfennigs for pine timber seized from him in the Trąbczyn forests, and 1,513 florins for sawn goods and other outlays. The entry is the deed that Chełmski's later suit against Antoni Prusimski rests on. <!-- context -->

### The entry

Tracholz, "sound in mind and body", declared that he ceded the two sums to Chełmski, who paid him for them [[1]]. The first, 1,053 imperials, 13 groschen and (it seems) 6 pfennigs, was for trees he had bought in the pine forests of Trąbczyn under a written contract with Paweł Prusimski; Prusimski, though he had once been satisfied under the contract, seized the trees by force and sold them to a merchant Simon of Szczecin, whose written statement (an "Auscuk") attests the sale [[1]]. The second, 1,513 florins, Tracholz had spent partly on making sawn goods, nine stacks of "Klapek" called "Rynek", which Paweł's full brother Antoni Prusimski likewise seized after the quarrel over the forests, and partly laid out for Paweł's benefit, as his own register of the sums shows [[1]]. With the sums he handed over the right to them, the interest on them, and the legal proceedings he himself had begun against the Prusimski successors [[1]]. He signed with a "held hand", another hand guiding his [[1]].

The entry has no date of its own on its pages. The Konin land court's decree of October 1777 in Chełmski's suit gives it: Chełmski claims by a transfer acknowledged before the Pyzdry court on Wednesday, the eve of the Feast of the Nativity of the Virgin, 1768, that is 7 September [[unit:app536046]]. The entry stands just above the heading of the next sitting, held on Friday 9 September 1768 outside the town, because the enemy had burned it on 11 June of that year. <!-- context -->

### Tracholz and the quarrel

Antoni Prusimski, in his protest of March 1778, called the sum Chełmski claimed from the transfer illegal and said it rested on a contract of 1744 for oak timber between his uncle Paweł and Tracholz; a later protest in the same register calls Tracholz "olim", the late [[unit:app53170koningr117]]. A royal citation to the Crown Tribunal, entered in the Konin register of 1780-82, names the merchant Tracholz as the man who made the sum over to Chełmski [[unit:app53170koningr118]]. The entry here is the transfer itself, the act the later documents refer to. The Konin entry of 1777 and the protest of 1778 write the name Tracholz; the Pyzdry clerk writes Traholtz. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The municipal court of Pyzdry took shape at the end of the fourteenth century. It judged the nobility in the serious criminal matters of the four articles of the municipal court (arson, attack on a nobleman's house, robbery on the public road, rape) and, in time, in other criminal matters and in suits over land and sums of money. Beside it stood the court's office, which kept the books; the office took entries of uncontested business every day, and the books had public faith. In this volume, Inscriptiones, such entries are numbered one after the other; this is no. 39. <!-- context -->

In 1768 the court's own town had burned. The heading under this entry says the sitting of Friday 9 September was held outside the town because the enemy had set fire to the whole of the town of Pyzdry and its churches on 11 June of that year. <!-- context -->
