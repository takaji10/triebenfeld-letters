On 9 June 1788 a servant of Antoni Prusimski brought before the court office the cart and animals of a peasant of Stanisław Ścibor Chełmski's, caught carting off a felled tree from the Trąbczyn pine wood – the quarrel between Trąbczyn and Łukomia still alive six years after the boundary was marked. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The boundary between Trąbczyn and Łukom had been marked on the ground in December 1782: [[unit:app53170koningr153]]. In the years before, each side had brought the other's axes and weapons before this office as proof of trespass: [[unit:app53170koningr115]], [[unit:app53170koningr117]]. This entry shows the same practice six years after the boundary was settled. The wood is now described as belonging to Trąbczyn "and never in any dispute". <!-- context -->

Things taken from a trespasser were shown to the municipal court office, described in the register, and given back to their owner against a receipt; the entry then served as proof of the trespass. <!-- context -->

### Contents

Ludwik Żurawski, in Prusimski's service, presents a cart with unshod front wheels and iron-shod rear wheels, two oxen, one pale and one mottled, a brindled cow and a chestnut mare, the oxen and cow in yokes and the mare in traces. They belong to Marcin Gryndel, a peasant of Łomowo, an estate of Chełmski's that belongs to Łukom. Gryndel had driven into the pine wood of Trąbczyn and was carting home a felled tree fit for building when the Trąbczyn forest keeper stopped him. The team was driven to Konin and stood before the chancery. Żurawski leaves it there to be given back to Gryndel, who asks for it at once; the receipt was entered the same day [[1]].

The clerk describes the cart and the animals in Latin and repeats each description in Polish after "als" or "alias", so that they could be identified. <!-- context -->
