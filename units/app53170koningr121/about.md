On 9 June 1788 a servant of Antoni Prusimski brought before the court office the cart and animals of a peasant of Stanisław Ścibor Chełmski's, caught carting off a felled tree from the Trąbczyn pine wood – the quarrel between Trąbczyn and Łukomia still alive six years after the boundary was marked. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The boundary between Trąbczyn and Łukom had been marked on the ground in December 1782: [[unit:app53170koningr153]]. Things taken from a trespasser were shown to the court office at Konin, described in its register as proof of the trespass, and given back to their owner against a receipt; in the years of the quarrel each side had brought the other's axes and weapons there: [[unit:app53170koningr115]], [[unit:app53170koningr117]]. The wood was now said to belong to Trąbczyn "and never in any dispute". <!-- context -->

### Contents

Ludwik Żurawski, in Prusimski's service, presented a cart, two oxen, a cow and a mare. They belonged to Marcin Gryndel, a peasant of Łomowo, an estate of Chełmski's that belongs to Łukom. Gryndel had driven into the pine wood of Trąbczyn and was carting home a felled tree fit for building when the Trąbczyn forest keeper stopped him. The team was driven to Konin, and was given back to Gryndel the same day against his receipt [[1]].
