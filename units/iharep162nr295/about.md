In January 1805 Peter Friedrich von Triebenfeld, the agent of Prince Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, borrowed 50,000 Reichsthaler from the Prussian army's Invalids' Fund, pledging part of what the Prince owed him on the Zagórów estates. The papers that created the debt show what was already charged on the estate of Zagórów. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

Prince Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen (1746-1818), a Prussian general, owned the lordship of Zagórów in South Prussia, the part of Poland that Prussia had taken in 1793. His agent there was the War and Forest Councillor Peter Friedrich von Triebenfeld. The Prince's debts grew faster than the rents of the estates. In June 1804 he gave Triebenfeld a bond for 250,000 Reichsthaler and had it registered on the Zagórów estates; Triebenfeld could pledge parts of it to lenders. In January 1805 the Invalids' Fund, the pension fund of the Prussian army, administered by a department of the army's Supreme War College, lent 50,000 Reichsthaler on this security. The letters of Triebenfeld show how the loan was arranged and that he was later pursued for its interest, since he had signed for it himself ([[unit:oe1bu9454]]). <!-- context -->

### Contents

**The bond, 28 January 1805.** At Berlin, Triebenfeld acknowledged a loan of 50,000 Thaler from the Invalids' Fund, paid out to him in bonds of the Seehandlung, the Prussian state bank, at 5 per cent interest, repayable on six months' notice from either side. As security he pledged all he had and, in particular, the Prince's bond to him of 1 June 1804 for 250,000 Thaler, registered on the town of Zagórów, Drzewce, Kopojno, Świątniki, Skokum, Oleśnica, Wrąbczyn and Grądzyń. If he did not pay, the fund was to have 50,000 Thaler of that bond, ahead of the 200,000 that remained his [[1]].

**Registered, February and March 1805.** Triebenfeld acknowledged the bond again before a councillor of the Government at Kalisz, the provincial court, on 27 February, and on 13 March the 50,000 Thaler were entered for the Invalids' Fund in the mortgage register of the Konin district on Zagórów, Drzewce and Kopojno [[2]].

**What stood on Zagórów, 13 March 1805.** The Prince held the town by King Friedrich Wilhelm II's grant of 19 June 1797. Charged on it were a yearly due of 88 Reichsthaler 5 Groschen 9 Pfennig to the Kalisz chamber; the Treasury's right, registered in January 1803, to timber and firewood from the estate's forests, a right that had come to it with the church estates it had confiscated; and the rights of Johann Giese, Johann Bagans and Friedrich Gietzinger under their hereditary lease of Oleśnica of February 1804, to wood, to four days' digging of pine stumps and to sheep grazing on at least 330 Morgen. Ahead of Triebenfeld stood a claim of 32,300 Reichsthaler of the Provincial Elder von Lichnowski at Brzeg, already registered on Trąbczyn; then came Triebenfeld's 250,000, of which the Invalids' Fund now held the first 50,000 [[3]].
