Trąbczyn's history begins in the charters of the Middle Ages: gifts to the Cistercian abbey of Ląd from 1173, the gift of Trąbczyn itself to Count Mikołaj in 1276, the town charter of Zagórów in 1407. They are printed in the Kodeks Dyplomatyczny Wielkopolski, the edition of the medieval documents of Greater Poland, with its editors' notes. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The Cistercian abbey of Ląd, on the Warta between Konin and Pyzdry, was the great landowner of the district in the Middle Ages, and most of these documents concern it. Trąbczyn itself was held by knights, who took their name from it ("de Trampczino"), while the abbey held the villages around it and the tithe of its fields. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**The abbey of Ląd and its villages.** Duke Mieszko gives the abbey "little Vronchin", Wrąbczynek, in 1173 [[1]]. Archbishop Fulko gives it the tithes of Zagórów, Skokum, Kopojno, Trąbczyn, Szetlew, Łukom and Wrąbczyn, in a document dated 1240 [[2]]. The dukes confirm its estates in 1261 and 1280, among them Szetlew, Zagórów, Skokum and Oleśnica, and the abbey's "sanctuary people", peasants bound to serve its church, whom the editors place at Świątniki [[4, 6]]. In 1291 the abbey gains Kopojno by exchange [[7]]. The judges of Kalisz declare in 1377 that Wrąbczynek and Adamierz have been the abbey's from of old [[11]], and in 1384 the judge of Kalisz gives it the investiture of "Vronczino", Wrąbczyn or Wrąbczynek [[12]]. In 1399 the Archbishop of Gniezno adjudges the sheaf tithe of Trąbczyn to the abbey against Jan, the parish priest of Trąbczyn [[13]]. In 1407 the King allows the abbey to found a town at Zagórów under the law of Magdeburg [[14]]; the abbots then deal with its schultheiss, the hereditary head of the town, and its peasants [[15, 18]], and in 1414 the parish church of Zagórów is handed over to the abbey [[16]].

**Trąbczyn and its knights.** In 1276 Duke Bolesław gives the village of Trąbczyn, "Trupcino", to Count Mikołaj, treasurer of Ląd, after two earlier holders had died without heirs [[5]]. In 1284 Przemysł II allows Count Mikołaj to locate his Trąbczyn, "Tropcssino", under German law, free of the Polish dues [[23]]. In 1346 the brothers Jan and Przedpełk, heirs of Trąbczyn, and Andrzej of Trąbczyn with his kin lose their claim to Stropiszyn and Pępocin against the church of Gniezno [[9, 10]]. Mikołaj and Andrzej of Trąbczyn witness a sale to the abbey at Gniezno in 1415 [[17]]. In 1434, in the house of Mścichna, wife of Otto, heir in Trąbczyn, their son Wojciech, a canon of Kruszwica, makes his brother Jan of Trąbczyn his proctor [[19]]: the only one of these documents written at Trąbczyn.

**Other hands.** Wisław son of Gerlach gives up Wrąbczyn in an exchange confirmed in 1292 [[8]]. Wrąbczyn's own papers with the abbey are richer: in 1241 Duke Kazimierz of Kujawy and Ląd exchanges Głowiewo and Wrąbczyn, once the huntsmen's land, for the abbey's estate of Koszanów [[22]]; Przemysł II gives Wrąbczyn to Henryk of Pyzdry to locate under German law in 1291 [[24]]; Władysław grants Count Wojsław the same right over it in 1298 [[25]], and confirms the abbey's estates, Wrąbczyn with the lake of Seborowo among them, in 1324 [[26]]. In 1357 the exchange of Zemka's third part of great Wrąbczyn for the abbey's Szczonowo is attested at Konin [[27]]; Laurentius of Królikowo sells his third part to the abbey in 1362 [[28]], Stasco with his consort Iachna theirs, and Mikołaj the subdapifer the half of the lordship, in 1363 [[29, 30]]; and in 1364 Dobrocha, heiress of Wrąbczyn, acknowledges that all her substance belongs to the abbot [[31]]. In 1440 King Władysław III charges sums for Stanisław Czarny on Wrąbczynek, Adamierz and Borzykowo, which the editors call royal villages near Pyzdry [[20, 21]]. Among the villages of the hospital of Gniezno, given to the canons of Miechów in 1243, is "Chemelino", which the editors identify as Imielno, the neighbour of Łukom [[3]].
