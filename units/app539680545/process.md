### Scans

The archive's eleven images show the folder that holds the sheets and the ten sheets themselves, one photographed page each, landscape. The photograph of the folder is not a page and is left out; the ten sheets are copied into the holding as its pages, with generous margins, uncut.

### Transcription

The five list sheets were read by an AI model in enlarged bands of the images, every doubtful reading marked, and the readings then put to the editor on one sheet with a picture of each region beside its draft: thirty rows of the sheet were answered, and the editor's readings replaced most of the doubtful ones (the heading, "Nachtigall - Słowiński", the seventeen translation pairs, the whole end of the fifty-three list). Two rows were left unanswered and are recorded in the holding's notes. The three prose sheets are the editor's own transcription, kept as typed, with Breyer's spellings and slips untouched; the words the pencil defeated are recorded in the holding's list of unresolved readings. The tenth sheet is shown with the holding and has no transcription.

### Division and dating

Seven documents on the ten pages: the four lists and the excerpt of 1741 each stand apart, the fifty-three list runs over two sheets, and the two prose essays are one document each, the second spanning three sheets, its last shown but not transcribed. No sheet is dated; every document is an undated sheet of Breyer's notes. The excerpt of 1741 is Breyer's own excerpt of a printed work, not a copy of a document, and keeps the holding's era with it.

### Translation

The English was written in the working session. In the lists every name stays as written, each list in its own spelling, and only the headings and the common words are translated. In the prose, an evident slip of the pencil is rendered by its evident word; a stretch that cannot be read with confidence is bracketed and left, never guessed, and the record of those words is in the holding's list of unresolved readings.

### Summary

The summaries were written in the working session in both languages. Each of their thirty-three statements was then checked against the document; two were corrected in the draft, one for a line the first version had passed over ("Gem. Zagórów"), one for a claim about where the schools lie that the page does not make. No statement rests on a word read with doubt except where the summary carries the mark too.
