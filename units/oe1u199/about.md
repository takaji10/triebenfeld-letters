In his will of 7 October 1805 Prince Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen bound his estates in Silesia and South Prussia into a family entail, so that they would pass undivided to one heir. Friedrich Wilhelm III, King of Prussia, confirmed the entail and its order of succession on 19 December 1805, two years before the Prince lost the estates in South Prussia. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen owned estates and ironworks in Silesia and, by a royal grant of 1796, estates in South Prussia, the province formed from Prussia's share of the partitions of Poland. An entail bound the named properties to a fixed order of succession: they passed undivided to one heir and could not be sold or divided. An entail needed the King's confirmation to bind the Prince's heirs. <!-- context -->

### Contents

The will placed under entail the houses of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen in Wrocław and the park at Szczytniki; the estate of Bytków; the lordship of Koszęcin with Boronów, Olszyna, Harbultowitz and Cieszowa; and the South Prussian lordships of Zagórów, Trąbczyn, Pszczew, Wieniec and Bątkowo [[1]]. If the Prince's male line died out, the estates were to be divided among his daughters: Auguste, the third, was to have the South Prussian estates, Adelheid the Silesian ironworks, and Emilie Koszęcin [[1]]. The King confirmed the will with reservations of his own rights [[1]].
