The copy, in a nineteenth-century hand, of a letter Tadeusz Kościuszko wrote in Polish from Paris on 3 September 1798. The copy stands in the manuscript collection "Listy, dokumenty i wiersze z lat 1697-1798" kept at the Ossolineum in Wrocław (B. Ossol. rkp. 2289 II), on page 145 of the codex, and it is one document. The collection is a gathering of copies made mostly by Stanisław Przyłęcki (1805-1866), the Lwów bibliographer and autograph collector whose manuscript legacy the Ossolineum bought in 1869. <!-- context -->

### The letter

Kościuszko answers an émigré correspondent who had written to him from Dresden in August of that year: he has read the expressions of goodwill "with the same courtesy as in those which reached me in America" [[1]] – the letters an insurgent emigrant had sent him during his American years. He regrets that events did not let him see the writer in London, and would have liked to know him in person. When circumstances point out the time for regaining the national independence – which he does not yet see, and which hope alone makes sweet to long for – he will stand inseparable from every Pole of a truly Republican soul, and from all who fought with him against the enemies of the country; and in that time the ways of common defence and the nation's true happiness will be founded on the principles the writer's letter mentions. For now, he writes, care must be taken not to expose them, through premature steps while happier events are awaited, to the persecutions of the enemies. He asks the writer to keep the letter for himself, and promises to preserve his friendship [[1]].

The dateline reads "z P[aryża]. d. 3 Września 98" – from Paris, 3 September 98, the expansion of the superscript abbreviation the editor's reading; Kościuszko had been in Paris since June 1798. <!-- context -->

### The addressee

The letter never names its addressee: "Obywatelu!" – Citizen! – is all the text offers [[1]].

An identification was proposed with Antoni Prusimski, the insurgent Starost of Niszczewice, through Pachoński's entry on him in the Polish Biographical Dictionary (PSB, 1982), whose list of sources cites this manuscript, "B. Ossol.: rkp. 2289/II", and whose description of the Naczelnik's polite reply matches the copy in shape – the answered letter written from Dresden in August, the regret at knowing the writer only from correspondence. **The editor has ruled the connection out for now** (2026-11-01) **until something establishes it**. What stands against taking it as read: <!-- context -->

- the entry's citation of the manuscript stands in its general source list, and that list is the whole of what the entry gives about any of its sources: a PSB entry carries no per-claim footnote apparatus, and the file the edition holds is the entire article. What Pachoński took from this codex, and for which claim, is therefore not recoverable from what he printed;
- the two phrases the entry quotes for the exchange («Lud i Wolność to dwa filary…», «nie zna go osobiście») appear in the copy in neither wording – if he had quoted this copy, a match would be expected;
- nothing else connects the letter to Prusimski: the codex's own index of letters (leaves 0033-0034) names no addressee for it (the editor's reading), and the letter itself names nobody. <!-- context -->

The evidence is exhausted in hand. The library's record for the codex is the catalogue entry the editor shared (the joint catalogue of the Ossolineum and the region's libraries), which describes the contents no further than its title; the codex's index names nobody; the PSB entry's source list is everything Pachoński gives. The identification is therefore set aside not pending a look but for lack of anything further to look at: only new evidence – the original of the letter surfacing, a second copy elsewhere, or new scholarship – could reopen it. Until then the letter is kept as what its page shows: Kościuszko's reply to an unnamed émigré correspondent. <!-- context -->

### The collection around it

The thirty scans read in the session of 1 November 2026 (leaves 0033-0062 of the codex) show what Przyłęcki gathered around the letter: the codex's own index of letters and lists of people; a July-1794 dossier of the uprising – letters to the Naczelnik, an officers' roster from the camp at Mokotów, the protocol of the Warsaw criminal deputation, and the opening of a patent signed by Kościuszko on 25 July 1794, whose continuation is missing from the scans; the file of the receipt and service attestation of the "Korpus Towarzystwa" of 1794; a name-day poem for Kościuszko of 1792; and a bishop's letter of 1796. None of those pages names Antoni Prusimski or anyone of his circle. Only the letter of page 145 is in the edition so far; the rest waits on the editor's rulings. <!-- context -->
