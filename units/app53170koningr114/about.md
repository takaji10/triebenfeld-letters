Two entries on four pages of a book of reports of the castle court of Konin for the years 1765 to 1767, kept in the State Archive in Poznań (Archiwum Państwowe w Poznaniu). Both are reports of a court messenger, made in 1767 for Antoni Prusimski, on an embankment that flooded the Olęder settlers of Trąbczyn. They are in Latin, with the thing seen described in Polish, and are two documents. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

By 1767 Trąbczyn belonged to Antoni Prusimski, later Starost of Niszczewice. In its woods, towards the boundary with Łukom, he and his predecessors had settled Olęder farmers. On that boundary lay the pond-ground (stawisko), the bed of a former pond, which both estates claimed. An embankment across its outlet held the water back onto whoever lay upstream. The commission of 1775 judged the pond-ground to be Trąbczyn's and awarded Prusimski damages for the flooding: [[unit:app53170koningr145]]. <!-- context -->

The volume belongs to the archive's fonds "Księgi sądu i urzędu grodzkiego w Koninie", the books of the castle court and castle office of Konin. A report (relatio) was the commonest kind of entry in these books: a sworn court messenger came before the office and declared what he had seen or done, and the declaration was written down. A man who meant to go to law over damage had it seen and reported in this way first, so that the state of things on a given day stood in a public record. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**April 1767.** On 22 April the court messenger Bartłomiej Szepczyński of Trąbczyn declared at Konin that on Holy Saturday, at the request of Antoni Prusimski of Kolno, heir of Trąbczyn, he had gone onto the estate with two noblemen, Stanisław Lipiński and Józef Rudkowski. He saw an embankment lately raised at the end of the pond-ground. Because of it the water that runs from the pond-ground through the Trąbczyn lands had backed up and flooded the Olęder settlers living in the Trąbczyn woods, with their manured fields and their meadows. He stated that people of the neighbouring estate of Łukom had done this on the order of its heir [[1]].

**August 1767.** On 6 August he reported again. On the Friday after Saint James, with the noblemen Józef Gołembiewski and Rudkowski, he had seen the embankment in the wood on the pond-ground, by the boundary of the Łukom lands but raised on Trąbczyn ground. The water dammed in the pond spilled onto the fields and meadows of the Trąbczyn Olęder settlers, so that the meadows of four of them lay under water and could not be mown. He and the noblemen declared that the embankment had been raised some years before by the heirs of Łukomia and had now been repaired [[2]].

### Form and language

The book is a register of rough entries, many to a page. The first report stands at the foot of leaf 297 and the top of leaf 297 verso, the second at the foot of leaf 334 and the top of leaf 334 verso. The other entries on those pages are not transcribed. The frame of each report is abbreviated Latin, which the transcription writes out; what the messenger saw is in Polish, in the spelling of the clerk. <!-- context -->
