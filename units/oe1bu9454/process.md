### Scans

The archive supplied 549 images, each showing one or two sides of a letter. They were cropped to single pages, and a folded sheet photographed open was cut at the fold. The edition has 870 pages. Each page was matched to its place in the transcription, and the matches were checked by hand.

### Transcription

The text was transcribed from German cursive handwriting by an AI system. The present transcription is a second machine reading, made in September 2026, in which the editor corrected names and layout by hand. Its typical errors are a single misread letter and, more seriously, a real word substituted for the right one without any mark of doubt. The editor later transcribed seventeen passages written sideways in the margins, which the machine had missed; these are shown on their page under a label.

### Division into documents and dating

The documents keep the archive's numbers. Where one number holds a letter with enclosures, each enclosure is a document of its own under the same number with a letter added. Four numbers are missing from the file and are shown as missing. Senders, recipients and places were taken from signatures, salutations and datelines; where a letter gives none, they were settled from its content, and the reason is recorded. Dates not written on a letter were supplied by the editor or inferred from neighbouring letters, and the page of each document states which.

### Corrections

An AI model (Claude) proposed corrections to the transcription in several passes. The rule, set with the editor, was that a word is changed only where an independent witness supports the change and the new word is close in its letters to the old one. Accepted witnesses were a second copy of the same letter, the same word written correctly elsewhere in the letter, a word or name attested many times in the file, and fixed formulas of address and closing. Names, figures, abbreviations and passages in French, Polish or Latin were not changed on the model's judgement. A word that is plainly wrong but has no such witness was left as transcribed and listed.

The editor checked samples of each batch against the scans. In five spot checks, between 18 of 19 and 24 of 24 sampled changes were confirmed. Each miss led to a stricter rule: for example, a broken word is no longer completed from a common phrase. Recurring misreadings were corrected throughout once the editor had ruled on them. The editor also read many doubtful names, abbreviations and line endings on the scans.

Personal names and titles were brought to one spelling in the text on the editor's ruling. Place names stand in the German form the letters use; the lists and the English translation give the Polish name. Abbreviated titles and initials are filled out in square brackets where the context settles them.

A trial in which the model read two letters afresh from the scans was judged less accurate than the existing transcription and was not continued. Thirteen letters remain largely uncorrected and are marked as rough.

### Reading and summaries

The model read each letter whole, with the letters before and after it in date order, and recorded what is said and which words it doubts. This reading produced 250 further proposed corrections, of which 180 were applied. German summaries were written from the German text, each statement tied to lines of the letter. A second, separate run of the model then checked every statement against the letter and was allowed only to cut or weaken; it changed 132 summaries. The English summaries are translations of the checked German ones.

### Translation

The model translated each letter with its reading notes in front of it. An automatic check confirms that every figure, every name and every mark of doubt in the German has survived into the English, and that two pairs of words the transcription often confuses are flagged wherever they occur. The register of creditors is translated as a table. A translation can only be as good as the transcription under it.

### Open points

About 260 doubtful readings remain marked in the text, most of them names and figures. A small number of abbreviations and names could not be read by the editor on the scans and stand as transcribed.
