A boundary decree had to be entered in the court books before it could be enforced. In November 1776 the municipal court office at Kalisz, searched at the request of Antoni Leszczyc Mierzewski, certified that its books held no entry of the boundary decree of 1775 between Trąbczyn and Łukom – one of several searches that the two sides made for that decree in 1776 and 1777. <!-- context -->

### Historical background

The decree of the commission that fixed the boundary between Trąbczyn and Łukomia was entered in the court records at Brześć Kujawski on 23 September 1776 and from there in the book of the municipal court of Konin: [[unit:app53170koningr145]]. A judgment gained its public standing from such an entry, and a party who meant to contest one had reason to establish where it had been entered and where not. A municipal court office would search its books on request and certify what it found. <!-- context -->

Antoni Leszczyc Mierzewski, who asked for the search, bears the same name and arms as Maciej Leszczyc Mierzewski, one of the five commissioners who, two months earlier, had adjourned the cause as still open: [[unit:app53150kaliszgr425]]. <!-- context -->

The municipal court took shape at the end of the fourteenth century. For the nobility it first heard criminal cases under the four "castle articles": arson, an attack on a nobleman's house, robbery on the public road, and rape. In time its competence changed and came to include the other criminal cases, and suits over land and over sums of money, and it took over appeals from the town courts. Beside the court grew the municipal court office, where the court books were kept. The office was open every day for the entry of uncontested business, and what was entered in its books was accepted as proof. <!-- context -->

### Contents

**The question.** On 23 November 1776 Antoni Leszczyc Mierzewski asked the court office at Kalisz whether the decree fixing the boundary between Łukom, Trąbczyn and other villages had been brought to it to be registered. It was the decree given by the commissioners whom the parliament of the Commonwealth had appointed at the request of Antoni Prusimski of Kolno, Starost of Niszczewice, heir of Trąbczyn [[1]].

**The answer.** The office searched what had been brought in since 17 October 1776 and certified that no such decree had been registered with it [[1]].
