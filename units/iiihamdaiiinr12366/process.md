### Scans

The archive photographed the file as 25 images: the cover, nine single leaves and fifteen openings of two facing pages. Every opening has writing on both sides and was cut at its fold into two pages, at a line the editor placed or approved. One opening was photographed twice, because a slip of paper bound into the fold hides part of the left page in one image and part of the right page in the other; the left page is taken from the first image and the right page from the second. The edition has 37 pages and the cover.

### Transcription

The editor supplied the transcription as one text, written paragraph by paragraph and not line by line. It was divided to match the pages: where a paragraph runs over a page turn it is cut there. A date or an address that the transcription set at the head of a document, but the writer put at its end, stands on the page where it is written. The line on the cover that gives the volume and its dates was added by the editor.

### Division into documents

Each letter, report, draft or judgment is one document, six in all. Dates are taken from each document's own dateline. The note from St Petersburg is dated in both calendars, and the western date is used.

### Corrections

An AI model (Claude) compared the whole transcription with the scans and corrected it where the page is plain and the corrected form is a word that fits its sentence. In the four French documents written in clear copyists' hands, 72 single words were corrected, nearly all of them typing slips. In the ministry's draft of 11 May 1819, written in a hurried hand, 21 passages were corrected, and in the Polish judgment 64. One word that the transcription had dropped, a "ne" in the Warsaw report, was added from the scan at the editor's wish. Each change is logged with its reason. The writers' own spelling was kept, and the Polish keeps the spelling of its time. The editor then ruled on thirteen doubtful words against the scans; three that could not be read stand as transcribed.

### Notes in the margin

The pencil notes in the margins of the Warsaw report were first read by the editor, who also named the passage each stands beside. The model read them again on enlarged images of the scan and changed several words. Each note stands at the foot of its page under the label "Written by the receiving office"; the French words in square brackets before it are the passage it refers to and were added by the edition. The editor then settled the doubtful words on the scan.

### Summaries

The summaries were written in German from a reading of each document against its scan, and translated into English. They have not yet had the separate check of each statement that the summaries of some other holdings received.

### Still to do

The documents have not yet been translated into English.
