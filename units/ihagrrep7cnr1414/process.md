### Scans

The archive supplied seven scans. The editor chose four: the others are the modern folder and blank leaves. One scan is a single page and is shown whole. Three are openings whose left side is blank; each was cut to the written side, and on one of them to the slip that carries the writing.

### Transcription

The editor transcribed the four pages line by line, one file for each scan. A word broken at the end of a line is marked and shown joined in the reading text.

### Division into documents

Each letter is one document, three in all, numbered in the order the pages lie in the file, which is not the order of their dates. The slip with the councillor's direction of 8 January 1797 is counted with the extract it was written about and stands first in that document, as it lies in the file.

What the department wrote on a paper it received (the day of receipt, the journal number, the routing to an official, the order to file it) is set after the text of that page under a label. The draft is the department's own paper, and its marks stay where they are.

### Corrections

An AI model (Claude) compared the whole transcription with the scans and corrected it where the scan is plain. 20 passages were changed, each logged with its reason. Half are typing slips and single letters. A word the transcription had left out of the French extract ("affatigué") was added. The others concern the hurried office hands and the signatures. The councillor who signs the directions is Raumer: his name is written out in Latin script at the head of the extract, and the transcription had read his three signatures as Rammer, Maunes and Maurer. The line a larger hand added to the draft names the Grand Chancellor von Goldbeck as a third addressee. Of the two ministers' signatures under the draft the second reads Haugwitz; the first is probably Alvensleben and is given with a mark of doubt.

### Summaries

The summaries were written in German from a reading of each document against its scan, and translated into English. Each statement in them was then checked against its document, under the same rules as the separate check that the summaries of other holdings received: a summary may say only what the document says. Of 19 statements two said more than the text and were weakened.

### Translation

The documents were translated into English in a working session under the same rules as the other holdings: every figure, name and mark of doubt carried across, names in the edition's settled forms where it has settled them and otherwise as the page spells them, and passages that do not read left visibly doubtful rather than smoothed. The extract was translated from the French. The registry marks are given in English; two abbreviations that the page does not expand are left as they stand. The same automatic check as elsewhere then confirmed that every figure and every mark of doubt survives into the English.
