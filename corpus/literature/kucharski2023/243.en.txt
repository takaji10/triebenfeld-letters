# 4. The judicial process

Let us look then at how the process before a land court of the age of the Noble Commonwealth looked, on the example of a criminal process. In that time a clear division into a criminal and a civil process had not yet taken shape, and so the procedural elements interweave for both types of the proceedings. A criminal case began with the bringing of a complaint by the wronged party himself, whom with time the instygator supported before the court. Then the court chancellery draws up the document called the summons, which was to be served on the settled nobility by the usher in writing, 14 days before the term of the hearing, while to the landless nobility it was announced orally. That important document is issued by the land scribe at the court, furnished with the royal and the land seal, in the Latin language and from the mid-16th century in the Polish language. The serving of the summons was attested by the deposition of the usher in the court book, which was held as the fact of an effective service. A frequent practice, however, was the ignoring of the summonses. The noble Arnold of Kościerzyna sued Mikołaj, the castellan of Bydgoszcz, before the land court at Nakło in a process "over the fled subjects", which was a rather frequent subject of the disputes between the nobility. "The court summoned lord Mikołaj, who did not stand at all, holding in contempt the summonses brought by the usher: 'the same usher, giving his account in the face of the court, attested publicly that he called the first, the second, the third and a fourth time over that lord Libijski of Złotów to stand and explain, which lord Mikołaj neither himself, nor through a substitute, stood forth and made no effort to explain'"[^32].
