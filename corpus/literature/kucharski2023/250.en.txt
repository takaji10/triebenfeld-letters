The land judiciary in the 17th and the 18th century lived through a crisis because of the estate specificity of the participants appearing in the proceedings carried on before them. In that period the municipal courts play a greater role. Only in the year 1765 does the revival of the activity of the land courts begin, which compete effectively with the municipal courts[^43]. Stanisław Staszic criticised the doubleness of the courts with reason: "The municipal and the land courts are needless, so long as these have an equal power of the judging of the same matters and their rulings an equal force. If the Commonwealth does not distinguish the matters of the kind proper to each of these courts, it will act wisely and destroy the disinclinations — the quarrels between the land and the castle — when instead of these two courts it establishes one court permanent"[^44]. The reforms of the state carried out at the end of the 18th century abolished the division of the judicial organs. The Four-Year Sejm liquidated the land, the municipal, the podkomorski courts, introducing in their place the uniform noble courts, made up of 10 judges appointed for 4-year terms and adjudicating through the whole year[^45]. In this way the chapter in the history of the Polish judiciary closed — the period of the noble administration of justice.
