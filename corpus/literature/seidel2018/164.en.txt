This left the great gap between the Bohemian [...] and our positions open to the French. The second book worth reading bears the title: 'Portrait of the Social Conditions in the Kingdom of Prussia up to 14 October 1806, by the author of the new Leviathan.' Berlin and Leipzig, 1808. The work contains much that is well considered. Already in the preface the author declares himself against those who have set it in their heads that a state exists only through agriculture. He is convinced that the true strength of a state rests not on physical foundations but on the [assent] of its citizens, and that this assent cannot be spread too widely. There follows a description of a new order of estates. Although I do not agree with the author in many particulars, I must nevertheless confess that in most of them he says a great deal that is true, well considered, and excellent [...] the author certainly deserves to be read. As for the fact that Colonel von Massenbach has had his defense — that he is not the author of the notorious *Gallery of Prussian Characters* [that is, as already noted, Friedrich Buchholz] — inserted in the *Minerva* [another journal], this will probably already be known to Your Serene Highness. I regret with all my heart that he has fallen into this disadvantageous reputation, and I wish that his vindication might convert the very great number of his enemies. It is incomprehensible to me how he has made so many adversaries [sic] for himself among the military, when otherwise everything was in his favor. In the March issue of the political journal, Lieutenant-General von Blücher has, in a satirical manner, had a call to the author inserted in the *Lichtstrahlen*, which accuses him of having brought about the disaster at Prenzlau through his failure to obey the orders of Your Serene Highness.
