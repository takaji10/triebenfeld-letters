## Preface

The book originally came into being as a declaration of the author's love for the town of Ingelfingen in Hohenlohe, where he spent large parts of his early youth. There, Prince Friedrich Ludwig still plays an important role in the consciousness of the population today; he is constantly present through the castle and the Mariannenvorstadt (Marianne's Suburb) that he planned. This serves as an explanation for the starting point of this account.

A complete biography of the Prince and his family requires that the wider circumstances of his life also be grasped. These are Hohenlohe with its ancestral seat of Ingelfingen, the Kingdom of Prussia, and the emerging Kingdom of Württemberg; it is without doubt an exciting period of German history in which Friedrich Ludwig lived. On the one hand it is the "age of Goethe," on the other the French Revolution is unfolding, and Napoleon, through his military campaigns, defines an entire epoch. Goethe lived from 1749 to 1832, Napoleon from 1760 to 1821; our Prince Friedrich Ludwig from 1746 to 1818 — they are contemporaries. All three also had something to do with one another. Friedrich Ludwig, although initially merely Hereditary Prince of a very small dominion (Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen), took a lively part above all as a Prussian military commander in the upheavals of this period.

FL has gone down in history chiefly for the following reasons: he was a high-ranking soldier in Prussia's service, a (co-)loser at Jena and Auerstedt in 1806, and then a Prince who resigned after his Hohenlohe dominion was appropriated by the Duchy of Württemberg, elevated to a kingdom. Finally, he attempted to become an entrepreneur in Upper Silesia.
