# Seidel (2018), p. 176
# translation by Claude

What is to be noted here is the clear distance from Cosmar, who will later also relinquish his duties for the family. But what of Hardenberg (1750–1822)? In his various capacities for the Prussian state, first in Ansbach-Bayreuth and then in the negotiations for the Peace of Basel, he had stood in close, and presumably also friendly, contact with FL. He evidently maintained the relationship with Marianne, or with her mother, when in 1799 Marianne needed a new home (and did not go to Oppurg, as had indeed first been considered, see p. 79), but instead, presumably in 1803, moved into the manor of Bellin with her new partner/husband Count Osten-Sacken.

Hardenberg was its previous owner (since 1781; for 1802/03, that is a very brief period immediately before the change of hands, a v. Lepel is also listed)! At the time of purchase there must have been financial arrangements from which he now wished to withdraw. In 1815/16 he negotiated in Vienna for Prussia. It remains unclear why he now pressed Marianne to redeem the 43,000 reichsthalers. She, in any case, was not delighted about it ("money-lender"). With the son Adolph's willingness to help (his name is spelled alternately with "ph" or "f"), she apparently found a solution. – Until now one might have thought that financial troubles were confined to FL, but no! Marianne herself had, on the neighboring estates of her second husband in Mecklenburg, debts of 96,200 thalers in the year 1809, toward the settlement of which she intended to apply the annual 4,000 thalers from the Transact.[^135]

Another letter:

> *Berlin, 13 April 1817/Beloved August/After my long silence my heart longs once again to behold the features of your hand. I wrote you two letters, which remained unanswered, which perhaps you did not receive. – Let us therefore forget the past and grant my present request, and let me soon come into possession of a fine page of your hand in which I may find the assurance of your approval
