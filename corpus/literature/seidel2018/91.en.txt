# Seidel (2018), p. 91
# translation by Claude

"votre carriere" (your career) in mind, which he — unsurprisingly — envisioned as a military career. To this end it made sense to keep his son up to date on this subject, to let him know who was now serving where, in what capacity. An immense number of names came up, some of which are only partly familiar to us today: Haugwitz, Strachwitz, Treuenfels, Kockeritz, Hardenberg, Hatzfeld, Wolzogen, Wedell, Wartensleben, Zitzewitz, Tauenzien, Reitzenstein — FL knew them all.

As General and Governor of Breslau, he traveled a great deal: to Berlin and Potsdam, the center of Prussian power and administration; he thought it worth mentioning that he had lodged at Sanssouci palace. Bielefeld, Magdeburg, Erfurt, Bautzen, Chemnitz, Zwickau, Glogau — he made his inspection tours everywhere. In the south there were, in addition, Hof, Fürth, and other places in the (Prussian) Margraviate of Bayreuth (including Ansbach and Erlangen).

As a father he also took an interest in his son's studies at the University of Erlangen. He asked about the subjects Latin, mathematics, philosophy, and law. Regarding the last of these he also names a professor: Gründler (see p. 203f.). Whether August had told him this name, or whether FL had heard of him himself, is unclear — Gründler was a professor specializing in Prussian law. Elsewhere[^67] English, Latin, optics, physics, and philosophy are named as his subjects. — It remains open whether August told FL about his fellow student Eugen, Prince of Württemberg. FL may have known of him, for there had already been contacts between the two houses before.[^68] FL, for his part, repeatedly mentioned the Margravine (Caroline of Bayreuth), who resided at Erlangen Castle and was much appreciated by August as a hostess. Since, as noted, most of these letters are composed in French, she appears as "Madame le Margrawe."
