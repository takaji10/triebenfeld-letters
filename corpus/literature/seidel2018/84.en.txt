> *confidential lines apply to no one. I ask, therefore, that they not be placed among the papers that might fall into the hands of any third party outside this small circle./It is now twelve years since I left Prince Hohenlohe — in my view I had to take this step in order to save my children's fortune. Already on several occasions, to save the Prince, I had been **compelled** to sign, against my mother's wishes, sums of enormous amount, and yet the mass of his debts grew again, like a rooster's crop. Had I remained in relations with him for these twelve years, then — especially in the most recent period, in which every household, even one economically managed, was in any case falling into financial straits — the dissipation of my fortune would **certainly** have followed./I renounced, then, the splendor of my rank, I renounced the advantage of my station — but at the same time I also renounced the fame and the greatest happiness of my life, in that I tore myself from the midst of my children, and fled into a foreign land, exposed to the vengeance of the world, to the momentary displeasure of my mother, so beloved above all things, so deeply revered and worthy — who at that time was less informed of the various causes of my step than she later became. In the first surge of her displeasure she demanded of me — perhaps stirred by other impulses — a great many sacrifices, all of which I willingly brought her for her sake. I myself am astonished now, when I read through the Transact of 19 November 1799 and the reversals and declarations that followed it, which I was made to sign within a short span of time, at the demands that were made of me.
