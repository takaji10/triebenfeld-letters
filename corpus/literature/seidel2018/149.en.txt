# Seidel (2018), p. 149
# translation by Claude

> *In money matters he was like a child who does not know the value of money. As often as he received any, existing debts were always paid off, which is why he did not lack credit for a long time. Even after the catastrophe of 1806 he was for a considerable time unable to accustom himself to the necessary restraint.[^118]*

And yet he knew and made use of methods of financial management still common today: savings-and-credit models like those of building societies, the issuing of shares to raise additional capital, the taking out of mortgages on real estate. Looking back, it is astonishing that he had declined the offer of the certainly not inconsiderable sum of money from Frankfurt (see p. 51). Might this have gone against his professional self-conception as a military commander?

### 14.2 Debts, Credits, Mortgages ...

It is, alas, so: Friedrich von Cölln was right. Finance was not FL's great strength. He (or rather his General Plenipotentiary Triebenfeld) left behind an extensive correspondence with his creditors, from 1784 until FL's death.[^119] Early on, FL already had to ask for help or borrow money. In 1790 the Duke of Courland lent him 300,000 thalers; in 1798 the Schlabrendorf couple lent 169,000.[^120] He then asked the Duke of Courland to become godfather to Alexander (see p. 72).

In addition to Bytków (in the year 1801), FL acquired in 1804 the great domain of Koschentin (with Boronow, a neighboring locality) in Upper Silesia, which also remained the property of his descendants. But it had been expensive — 925,000 thalers! He first had to raise this money — and it is very likely that this extreme sum was a quite essential reason for his continuing financial misery. The Princess Sacken had acquired Landsberg, neighboring Koschentin, shortly before drawing up her will. It went, like Koschentin, later to her grandson Adolph. —
