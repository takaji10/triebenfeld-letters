# Seidel (2018), p. 138
# translation by Claude

For 30 January 1828, the birthday celebration of Grand Duchess Louise Auguste, to which he was invited, Glenck asked Goethe for an occasional poem, which he also received (it was not, however, to be given out of hand, as Goethe told a publicist – yet it did appear in 1830 in a Musen-Almanach). The birthday greetings, with a dedication to the Duchess, to which Glenck presented, among other things, freshly mined salt from Stotternheim in a precious glass vessel, have been preserved:

> *The first products of the Stotternheim saline, accompanied by a poetic dialogue between gnomes, Geognosy, and Technology, presented on 30 January 1828 with a comforting Glückauf! C. Glenck, Director of Salines, most humbly.*

In Goethe's little work, a gnome speaks out against the exploitation of treasures hidden in the earth, but can do nothing to prevent it and joins the festive company. "Geognosy" (i.e., the science of the earth's structure, properly the doctrine of the structure and composition of the solid crust of the earth; here personified) regards the successful borehole as the outcome of a firm belief in the universally identical laws of seam formation. "Technology" boasts of having, with the aid of physics and geometry, first developed the means for exploiting the salt deposit.

The supposition has been advanced that Goethe, impressed by Glenck's work, "made use of" these conversations in *Faust II*.[^106] There, in the first act, "Imperial Palace," the subject is economy and mineral wealth, mostly however the precious metals found there, though brine as the raw material for salt is also mentioned. Glenck, for his part, also understood a great deal about mining. And gnomes likewise appear in *Faust II*.
