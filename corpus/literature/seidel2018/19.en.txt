# Seidel (2018), p. 19
# translation by Claude

Among the children were the daughter Sophie Christiane; Friedrich Karl, Austrian Field Marshal; Georg, Prussian Major-General; and Friedrich Ludwig himself. From another letter of the old Prince of Hohenlohe:

> *In the coming week I expect your dear wife with her dear children, which I look forward to with childlike joy and can scarcely await. Would to God that you too were among them, then my pleasure would be complete.*[^8]

The course of events brought it about that in 1805 Friedrich Ludwig had to give up the Ingelfingen residence in order to carry on the House of Hohenlohe-Öhringen there. He then resigned as early as 1806, and his son Friedrich August succeeded him there as Prince August II (1784–1853). Another son, Prince Adolf zu Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen (1797–1873), appears initially to have taken charge of Ingelfingen; his interests, however, lay elsewhere: as a Prussian general and member of the Council of State, he rose to become Minister-President of Prussia. — The castle and castle garden in Ingelfingen (and much else besides) now belonged to whichever Prince of Hohenlohe-Öhringen held the title. A branch office of the administration remained in Ingelfingen until the middle of the nineteenth century.
