As a descendant of Heinrich August and as a general in the service of the imperial army, he had little to do with the other Hohenlohe houses. Until 1802 the above-mentioned arrangement held. After that Caroline received no further payments and, incomprehensibly, did nothing until 1806.

1806 is a singularly unfavorable moment for the petition. Since the summer of 1806, the Prince of Hohenlohe-Öhringen (and simultaneously of Ingelfingen) is FL's son August, himself now a subject of the King of Württemberg. FL will only take up residence, quite at the end of the year, as a prisoner of the French, entirely without authority, at the castle in Öhringen. The King begins to align the administration of his newly acquired territory of Hohenlohe with that of Württemberg. The Ingelfingen Chamber will shortly be merely an outpost of Öhringen.

In Öhringen as well as in Ingelfingen, an administrative process arises in response to the petition to the King, confirming that Caroline had received a cash payment for years; the vouchers are found. Why nothing more was paid out after 1802 remains unclear. The statement of position for Prince August reads:

> *May Your Most Serene Highness graciously be pleased to observe from the enclosed documents what Caroline Siller of Ingelfingen has represented to His Royal Majesty at Stuttgart concerning a pension of 200 Hohenlohe florins annually, and what the local Court Chamber has, as is its duty, noted thereon by way of explanation.
