# Seidel (2018), p. 60
# translation by Claude

> *from me, I look forward to seeing you again soon. Emily's [the sister four years younger] Unfortunately I heard should also now shortly be lifted [i.e., cured], since the cause of it has been recognized. To Mr. Kahlert I myself give thanks today for the care he has had for you, which you will certainly recognize with gratitude as I do. Farewell, dear son, with a faithful heart loves you your faithful father [signature of Friedrich Ludwig]./Postscript: embrace dear Mama and Aunt for me. To the latter I give thanks most humbly for her two letters, which I received here yesterday together, through Schulenburg.*

To be noted here is the concern for the health of the boy, about which he had previously received less good news. But from whom? There must therefore also have been some other channel of correspondence. The generosity of the grandmother, Princess Sacken, was to be maintained by her throughout her life. Something similar held for the contact with the tutor Kahlert, which lasted until FL's death, thus long beyond his period as tutor.

The further letter to August bears the date 26 March 1799:

> *My dear son. I have just now received your last letter and am very grateful to you for it. It pleases me that your health is good again and that the little feverish attacks have had no consequences. I trust that you will contribute your own share to the maintenance of your health. Give Mama and Aunt many regards from me, and I am pleased that the former is quite well, and the latter is again feeling better. Embrace all your siblings in my name. To the Countess [Massancourt], to Mr. Kahlert, Bally, Glenck many compliments; to Miss Stetten and Count Schulenburg I wish from my heart good and speedy recovery. If you see Mr. von Triebenfeld, give him much [...] from me. For the Hohenlohe lands I have for some time been very worried, since*
