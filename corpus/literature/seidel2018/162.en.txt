# translation by Claude

There follow reports on military persons who would have been known to FL, for instance his former aide-de-camp von Blumenstein, who had received the post of vice-commandant.

Then Kahlert turned to "literary products." Two periodicals with the titles "Feuerbrände" ("Firebrands") and "Die Zeiten" ("The Times") were causing "a great stir, because they mostly contain articles in which men of distinction are attacked." The editor of the "Feuerbrände" was von Coellen (whom we already know as Friedrich von Cölln), who wrote, according to his own account, "to draw the King's attention [to certain matters]." In another journal, the "attacked persons had had defences printed." Concerning von Massenbach a biographical sketch had appeared, intended "to put his merits in their proper light":

> *Another recently published journal, called 'Löscheimer' ('Fire-buckets'), which has the intention of extinguishing the ill impressions that the aforementioned periodicals may have produced, makes it its duty to convey to us laudable anecdotes of the Prussian military, but the Duke of Brunswick is entirely disparaged. In the author's opinion he alone is to blame for the whole calamity; he was said to have been nothing but a mere drill-master who had not kept pace with the spirit of the age, but, proud of his imagined self-conceit, had rejected all better counsel for no reason other than that it did not originate with him. All the reproaches accordingly made against the other commanders of the army would be unjust and fall solely and exclusively upon the Duke.
