Christian came as a five-year-old child from Schmalkalden, where his father held the post of Chief Forester, to his mother at Massenbach. He received his first schooling in Ludwigsburg. Later he attended the Karlsschule (Charles's Academy) in Stuttgart, the country's foremost educational institution for the nobility and persons of the court. There he met Friedrich Schiller, one year his junior, who was a pupil, and later a student of medicine, there from 1773 to 1780. He kept in touch with him, and the two occasionally exchanged letters. Massenbach finished school in 1778. This education gave him a thorough knowledge of antiquity, of mathematics, but also of military science, geography, and history; he was able to put these subjects to excellent use in his wide-ranging activity as both officer and writer.

He began his military career in 1778 with the Württemberg Guard. When the Duke of Württemberg decided to appoint a further professor to the military faculty of the Karlsschule, he chose Massenbach, who from February 1782 taught there as professor of mathematics, tactics, and strategy. Massenbach, however, wanted something else. He sent Frederick II a translation of a French work "On the Trajectory of the Ball [...]" (the work dealt with the flight path of a fired projectile). The King thanked him for it and offered Massenbach the chance to come to Potsdam. Massenbach then transferred to the army of the Prussian King Frederick II (just as FL had also done). This transfer took place against the Duke's will. In 1783 he served on the Prussian General Quartermaster's Staff, took part in the 1787 campaign against Holland, and suffered a severe wound to the hand.
