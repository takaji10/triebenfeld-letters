After the conclusion of peace at Rastatt in 1798, he had been keen to enlarge "his" Hohenlohe decisively, through the lands of Schöntal Abbey, the Teutonic Order's territory with Mergentheim, parts of the Palatinate, and of the Archbishopric of Mainz (both territories in which Hohenlohe had once held possessions in earlier times). He even, as mentioned, entertained hopes of being put forward as Duke of a newly created land of "Franconia" within the framework of the reorganization of the Empire. His emissary, or agent, in this matter was the Privy Councillor Mayer, who went as far as Karlsruhe in order to be closer at hand in Rastatt. Yet nothing came of it in writing, apart from promises; "Berlin" did nothing here. He was not a fixed quantity in the great powers' marathon of negotiations; not then, and no longer now either.

FL had not been in military service as a commander since shortly after the defeat of 1806. Precisely in this field, however, there was, at least briefly, once more serious consideration given to him:

Interestingly, in 1813, after he had already lived in Upper Silesia for a good many years, he was inwardly prepared to be called upon once again. The King had already, in 1807, entrusted Scharnhorst with reorganizing the army. What was now at stake was the King's project of creating an entirely new military order. Blücher was judged by other military men to be too old for this task, and FL was urged to inform the King of his readiness. He did so on 12 March 1813 in a long letter, whose respectful, indeed almost submissive tone strikes me as so characteristic that it shall be reproduced here word for word in its essential parts:[^89]
