*with the F.P. Hofrichter, so that it now seems to me this affair is somewhat delayed. I hoped to see more clearly in the matter once Mr. Kunorsky and Forest Master [Mi] have returned, since Mr. Hofrichter will then have met with them and with Metallurgical Councillor König. This affair concerning Landsberg is to be finally settled on the spot, in the presence of your brother Adolph. Korb, who is acquainted with all of this, I advised to remove the misunderstanding with Metallurgical Councillor König and to accept his communication so modestly that no harmful jealousy [Eifersucht] should arise from it. I found it only necessary to note all this to you, as it serves your guidance [...] from your brother [...] the most earnest regards to you and your amiable consort, whose recovery gave us heartfelt joy. I hope these lines will find you both at Friedrichsruhe, where a stay at this time of year should still be quite pleasant. Farewell, dear son, continue to grant me your love and trust [...] in return for my affection and high regard.*  
*Your faithful father*

This letter—admittedly somewhat clumsy and perhaps too long for some readers (dictated in the bathtub!)—omits only a single subject: the existing heavy burden of debt, which will be addressed later in connection with Triebenfeld's duties. Everything else that comes up must be seen as a document of admirable activity. The 68-year-old was in poor health, but he concerned himself, comparably to Faust in *Faust II*, with the development, renovation, and expansion of the entire territory, with the forest and its use, with roads, with agricultural installations, with mills, and not least he took joyful part in the development of coal and ore mining (the aforementioned Korb was the manager there).
