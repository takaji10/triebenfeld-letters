# Seidel (2018), p. 172
# translation by Claude

> reason than that he too, through closer knowledge of his character, might appear to him in a light — that would unite us all more closely — that my husband wished this was natural, and that for me, every day, indeed almost every hour, urged its accomplishment, you know — as well as my own uncomfortable position in the matter — I regarded the relationship in which August stands too clearly to wish to make it still more difficult for him — on the other hand, mine toward my husband was no less pressing; to calm him and dissuade him from the idea, I told him what is really true, what you yourself have often repeated to me — how delicate and complicated August's position is at present in every respect toward his father — and how difficult and burdensome it must also become for both, in consequence, through the testament — wherefore I held it my duty, on my part, to avoid everything that might occasion misunderstandings, bitterness, and the Prince's displeasure toward my son. — Sacken recognized this with deep feeling, with tearful eyes — and pressed me no further — You must swear to me likewise so [...] — that he did not quite understand this, or interpreted it otherwise, I did not suspect — for I was glad to cut short this discussion, and did not indeed grant it — how little, with the purest of intentions, could I have suspected that I would bring such grief upon myself. Once more, dear Cosmar! The love of my son is a treasure that must be preserved to me undimmed, if I am not to lose everything along with it — this explanation cannot be a matter of indifference to him either. I am indescribably despondent — this has affected me inhumanly — and because he is with the King — these are the reasons that today deprive me of the pleasure of seeing him — instead I expect from you tomorrow some reassurance, and from him, that he may manage to give me the evening, since I depart on Friday./Marianne Sacken*
