## 1 Introduction to Hohenlohe and Ingelfingen

### 1.1 The Town of Ingelfingen[^1]

Ingelfingen, the birthplace of Friedrich Ludwig, had belonged to the House of Hohenlohe for around 400 years, from 1409 to 1806. The place itself, however, is considerably older. Ingolf was the name of the clan leader of the Alemanni; "Ingiluingen" is the early form of the place-name. The first documentary mention dates from 1080; in 1302 the place is referred to as an "oppidum", and town and market rights were granted in 1328 and 1334 respectively. What emerged was a town with a town wall and three town gates; the town's coat of arms bears an abbot's staff, which relates to the local church of St. Nicholas and to Comburg Abbey near Schwäbisch Hall.

Lichteneck Castle served as the residence of the ruling authority or of its local administrators (bailiffs, or, somewhat confusingly, also called "Keller"). An information panel, on which some details about the castle are recorded today, attests that Ingelfingen was a wine-growing town — and still is.

Lichteneck was built in 1251 by Kraft von Krautheim-Boxberg. He was the brother-in-law of the progenitor of the House of Hohenlohe, which held many possessions here. The surrounding vineyards (so viticulture already 800 years ago!) belonged in part to the Benedictine abbey of Comburg near Schwäbisch Hall. The castle was pledged as security several times. The bailiffs (referred to as Schultheiss, Keller, or Amtmann — in earlier times exclusively noblemen) resided here. The destruction of the castle, about which little is known, must have taken place between 1390 and 1460.

For administrative purposes, buildings were erected in the town, among them the "Alte Amtskellerei" (Old Bailiff's Office), now better known as the "Altes Schloss" (Old Castle), then the "Schwarzer Hof" (Black Court) next to the castle, and finally the "Amtshaus" (Bailiff's House) on Bühlhofer Straße.
