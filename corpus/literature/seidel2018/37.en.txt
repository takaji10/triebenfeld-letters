Complete exemption from corvée labor and military billeting. The costs of construction were advanced by the princely house or the building fund; on moving in, a quarter of the sum had to be paid, the remainder in annual installments over three years. The building funds were administered by the Government Councillor and Ingelfingen bailiff (Amtmann) Taurinus. Interested parties could also take over half-finished buildings and complete them with their own money, which apparently occurred chiefly toward the end of the project's overall twenty-four years.

The overall planning of the many new buildings here and in the actual suburb lay in the hands of the Director of Buildings and Salines, Court Councillor J.G. Glenk, engaged by Friedrich Ludwig in 1781, surely in close coordination with FL himself, who at this time was still Erbprinz. However, as an officer in Prussian service he was often not on site.

The various house types in the suburb, including the larger houses for officials or the ruler, were reviewed by FL. He himself designed one type of house entrance: an entryway reached by a few steps, from which apartment entrances led into the interior of the house to the right and to the left. These are thus semi-detached houses.

The first construction phase of the overall project began in 1782, after the Prince's proclamation, with the planning of 19 dwellings in five buildings and a projected cost estimate of 16,000 florins. The building material had to be brought in: the stones came from quarries within the Ingelfingen district itself; the timber, by contrast, was hauled in from Schrozberg. In 1785 it became apparent that the initial plan had been too small. Previously the Steubertor (the former eastern town gate, on the town side of the pharmacy) had already been demolished and a new gate structure erected about 400 meters to the east — this was now torn down again in order to carry the expansion further.
