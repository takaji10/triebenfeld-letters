> *his entire estate as collateral, but also, besides this, a bond. Namely one made out to him, von Triebenfeld, by the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, dated Breslau, 1 June 1804, for the sum of 250,000 [...], and entered, pledged, and conditionally assigned upon the Serene Highness's Zagorower estates, granting the Fund, in respect of its loan, priority over the remaining 200,000 [...] still outstanding to him under the latter bond. Of the aforementioned loan of 50,000 [...], the said [...] Triebenfeld has remained in arrears of interest at 5% since 1 Nov. 1807 until the end of July 1811, that is, for 3 ¾ years, in the amount of 9,375 [...]. All means of obtaining payment of this arrears of interest from him have proved fruitless, and the General Invalids' Fund has therefore seen itself obliged to avail itself of the rights accruing to it [...] against von Triebenfeld as well as against the Zagorower estates pledged to it by His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe, arising from the above-mentioned instrument of debt and pledge, in respect of this loan together with the interest accruing thereon in arrears, and to this end it has judicially empowered the Royal Saxon and Duchy of Warsaw Tribunal Advocate [Mitschka] of Kalisch, upon transmission of the original documents concerning that loan, to bring suit in its name before the competent Duchy of Warsaw Tribunal, for the present against the possessor of the Zagorower estates, for the arrears of interest outstanding on the capital of the 50,000, and, should it be necessary, to seek the sequestration of these estates.
