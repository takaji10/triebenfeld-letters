"Mama" states later in the divorce record that she left "during an absence of her husband." No exact date for this step can be found in the documents. It probably took place in the summer of 1799, since the so-called Transact, an extensive agreement on the separation that had to satisfy legal requirements, was finished in November. As late as the end of March FL was writing a letter to his son, meaning the mother must still have been together with the children at that time.

The theme of health and the exhortation to good conduct belong from the outset to the repertoire of such letters, as we shall see from those he sent to the fourteen-year-old August:

Of the letters, the first dates from early 1798, from Ansbach (where FL had his headquarters for a time), and probably went to Breslau; the second, of 26 March 1799, went from Öhringen to Berlin or perhaps to Breslau.[^39] He dictated both to his aide-de-camp von Stetten; from FL's own hand comes a brief postscript in each case. Von Stetten has, not only here, a legible hand, but occasionally an idiosyncratic orthography and word order, which I have, as with the other letters, largely retained. — In the letter from Ansbach (1798), the subject is first of all news of the improvement in the boy's health:

> *My dear son. However much the news I had received of your health had until now worried me, all the more it pleases me now to be assured, both by Mr. Kahlert and by yourself, of your complete recovery. Without becoming soft on that account, do nevertheless take care of your health; it is all the more necessary since I hear that you are growing strongly, and nature requires additional strength for this development. That you ride diligently pleases me, it will be beneficial to your health. That GrosMama gives you proofs of her gracious benevolence on every occasion pleases me; seek always to deserve [sic] this disposition through diligence and good conduct. Greet all your siblings*
