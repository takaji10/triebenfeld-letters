# Seidel (2018), p. 69
# translation by Claude

Could it be that FL's strength lay not in battles but in the leading of armies; in the display of military strength, not its deployment; in negotiation, as at Frankfurt and in achieving Hohenlohe's neutrality? Or was it that his dreams, which he could make credible as his intentions, were able to convince many people? Did he possess persuasive power and organizational talent? His positions of leadership cannot have been conferred on him without reason.

And even when he disagreed with orders or particular courses of action — for example, those of the Duke of Brunswick or even orders of the King (as happened repeatedly in 1805/06) — he behaved correctly. This was probably not always easy for him. For he once formulated (1805), quite as a complaint, that he was

> *systematically kept in complete uncertainty regarding all the major measures for the protection of the Prussian state and regarding knowledge of the theaters of war. We are dealing with an enemy whom I do not love, but whom I must respect, because he understands war. I neither wish nor can appear, in the eyes of my contemporaries and of posterity, as a general who presumes to wage war without any fixed points, who does not understand the art of incorporating fortresses into his operations, of giving his army strength and his operations solidity. It is a great error that the strategic importance of Erfurt has not long since been taken to heart.*[^47]
