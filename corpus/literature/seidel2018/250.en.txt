# Seidel (2018), p. 250
# translation by Claude

## Literature and Sources

The Hohenlohe Central Archive at Neuenstein (HZAN) preserves an immense treasury of documents on the subject of this book, one that a single individual can scarcely exhaust. During the numerous visits there to the reading room, it quickly became clear that the many letters would be an essential source, and that priorities would have to be set and a selection made. I have accordingly dispensed with piles of letters that the Prince's descendants wrote to one another, and placed the emphasis on the correspondence in which the Prince himself (or his wife) was sender or addressee. Standing above the individual sources, so to speak, is the now roughly 150-year-old work by Adolf Fischer (1871), which was also drawn on for many brief items of information without direct citation. The book is the standard work on the ruling families of the House of Hohenlohe. In keeping with the time and the state of knowledge then prevailing, the author placed particular emphasis on matters military history in the broadest sense (which I have likewise made use of, though not in as much detail). His book was reissued in 1991 by the Historical Society for Württemberg-Franconia at the Hohenlohe Printing and Publishing House, Gerabronn-Crailsheim. Beyond this, the following secondary literature was consulted:

- Albrecht, J. (ed.): *Archiv für Hohenlohische Landesgeschichte*; vol. 2. Öhringen 1870.
- Bartl, Heinrich: *Die Saline und das Solebad zu Stotternheim 1826-1949. Ein Verdienst des Salinisten Carl Christian Friedrich Glenck.* Erfurt 2008.
- Borst, Arno: *Valmy 1792 – Ein historisches Ereignis?* In: Der Deutschunterricht 26, issue 76 (1974). pp. 88-104.
- Breyer, Albert: *Die deutschen ländlichen Siedlungen des mittelpolnischen Warthebruchs.* Deutsche Wiss. Zeitschr. für Polen, issue 34 (1938). Digital reissue, 2010.
