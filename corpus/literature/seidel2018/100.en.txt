## 10 Statements by Contemporaries on the Prince and the Events of His Time

To gain further insight into FL's personality, we now turn to a number of assessments by contemporaries and companions. These often, though not exclusively, concern the struggles of Prussia and Saxony against Napoleon, with the greatest significance attaching to the Battle of Jena and Auerstedt and the capitulation of the remaining troops under FL's command at Prenzlau shortly thereafter. — The authors drawn upon here as reporters write in particular on questions of his character, often only a few years after the events, and with quite varying degrees of concern for accuracy.

### 10.1 Friedrich von Cölln (1767-1820)

Friedrich von Cölln was a jurist and Prussian state official who, among other things, served after the Polish partitions as a War Councillor and administrator in Posen and in South Prussian districts from 1793 to 1803. After a checkered career, he ultimately worked as a writer.

Relevant here is his work written in the form of letters from Stettin: "Confidential Letters on the Internal Conditions of the Prussian Court since the Death of Frederick II,"[^73] which appeared as early as 1807. Incidentally, he also wrote defamatory tracts. He knew "him (FL) and his circumstances well." "He has never avoided the appearance of wrongdoing, that is true." "Hohenloh was esteemed and favored by Frederick, and this brought him renown. He has broad vision, fire, energy, talent, knowledge, and character. [...]" We know from Kahlert (chap. 16) that von Cölln knew the estate at Scheitnig. Kahlert had shown him, when he was once a guest there, the garden layout and the park. Whether this was before or after Marianne's departure is unfortunately unknown.
