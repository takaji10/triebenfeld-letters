# translation by Claude

The next illustration shows, from another document — also a letter dictated to a scribe — FL's signature and an additional note in his own hand:

*[illustration: facsimile of a handwritten letter closing, dated Berlin 19 May 1800, with FL's signature and a handwritten postscript]*

Here he has signed "treuer Vatter" (faithful father); other formulations are "Dich treu liebender Vatter" (your faithfully loving father) or, in French, "Votre tendre et fidele Pere" (tendre: loving; fidele: faithful).

For the years 1798/99 to 1805, in which FL had no military operations to conduct — there were no armed conflicts — we nonetheless learn a good deal from the large number of letters he wrote in this private context (they are not reproduced here in their complete original text).

His domestic center was the Governor's House in Breslau. The summer house at Alt-Scheitnig (spelled "Scheidnich" in the letter) was rebuilt and extended during these years. The painter Schillinger from Öhringen had just finished his work. One room is referred to, in writing to August, as "your room." It was mainly there, and in the large garden that went with it, that he received guests — and would continue to do so only for a short time longer.
