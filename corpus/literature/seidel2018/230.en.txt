### 23.3 Friedrich Ludwig's "Human" Side

Was FL a caring person, a good father (and husband)? What do historical records offer for such questions about the life of a person who lived more than 200 years ago? At a time when there were still binding rules for how people dealt with one another, above all rules that made distinctions of rank clear in correspondence. As Prince and military commander, FL stood very high in the social hierarchy.

Assessments of his "interpersonal" conduct in his professional world as a soldier are accessible on the basis of individual sources: it was, for instance, decidedly courageous of him to wrest the captured young Prussian officer away from his own soldiers, thereby saving his life. That von Zastrow was grateful to him for this, and later, in his own elevated position, helped him as a reward for this good deed, is without doubt a fine testimony to the conduct of both men. FL was also later very popular and respected among his soldiers — by then Prussian ones, sometimes Saxon ones as well — and also among his aides-de-camp; with many of them a personal relationship persisted even after their careers had ended. Whenever improper incidents occurred within his area of responsibility, he strove, even before the King, to whom he was obliged to report them, to secure fair treatment — as once from Frankfurt, where he pleaded for leniency (this letter to the King survives in Frankfurt as a copy. The presumably numerous military reports to the King probably passed, after the sorting of the estate, into the Prussian archive). When von Marwitz was assigned to him as aide-de-camp without FL's knowledge or involvement, FL, at his request, explained after a few days his conspicuous reserve on precisely these grounds ("I did not choose you"). What then developed was a relationship of mutual respect, as von Marwitz himself described in his book.[^168]
