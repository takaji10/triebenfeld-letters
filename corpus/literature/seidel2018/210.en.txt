## 22 Excursus VI: Christian August Ludwig von Massenbach

Preliminary note: Christian von Massenbach has often been named in the main part of this book as a person closely connected to FL in his military career. Even after the defeat at Jena and Auerstedt in 1806, he remained in contact with him as an author. Unlike FL, however, he was an intellectual and a rebellious spirit. This makes him particularly interesting, as a kind of contrasting figure, so to speak.

### 22.1 Origin and Career

The von Massenbach family was an Imperial-immediate (reichsunmittelbar) knightly lineage. Since the beginning of the 16th century there had been a Prussian branch and a South German branch; both branches remained in constant contact and "helped each other out" whenever one of the two threatened to die out for lack of an heir.

Christian was the middle of three sons of the South German branch. All three served either in the military or in courtly administration; Christian in Prussia, the other two in Württemberg. The elder, Friedrich Reinhard Wilhelm, belonged to the knightly council of the Kraichgau knightly canton and, from 1809, was Württemberg Chamberlain and head of the War College; the younger, Dorotheus Friedrich Eberhard Ferdinand, was first Privy War Councillor in Berlin, then, from 1811, Württemberg State Councillor, and from 1817 Director of the Württemberg Superior Chamber of Accounts.

The family's ancestral seat, with its castle, was in Württemberg, in the village of Massenbach near Heilbronn.
