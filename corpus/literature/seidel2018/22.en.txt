# Seidel (2018), p. 22
# translation by Claude

- Adolf, born on 29 January 1797 in Breslau, died in 1873; married in 1819 to Luise zu Hohenlohe-Langenburg.
- Alexander, born on 3 July 1798 in Alt-Scheitnig (Breslau), died in 1829.

The wedding of Friedrich Ludwig zu Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen to Amalie Charlotte Marianne Luise Christiane, Countess of Hoym zu Droyßig, took place on 8 April 1782 at the castle in Glaina, Thuringia. As early as 9 October 1781, extensive contracts concerning the financial side of the union had been concluded in Leipzig. — The firstborn, August, became FL's successor. All three daughters married in a manner befitting their rank.

After the separation from FL in 1799 (see p. 77f.), Marianne entered into a union with Friedrich, Count of Osten-Sacken, a relative of her stepfather, and had two daughters with him: Angelica, born 1802, and Auguste, born 1804. These daughters, too, married in a manner befitting their rank in Mecklenburg at the ages of 18 and 20 respectively. — The formal judicial divorce of the princely couple did not take place until 1804. A so-called Transact of 19 November 1799, which contained arrangements between mother and daughter, plays a major role here.

From the marriage contracts:[^10] Of primary importance are first the proceeds from the inheritance of her father, Count Hoym, which was administered by Marianne's mother. Without going into detail here, suffice it to say that mother and daughter each received about 8,000 thalers annually from it. In the marriage contract between Marianne and Friedrich of 1781, no concessions exceeding the usual measure were made to the husband regarding his share. The Hereditary Prince undertook to leave his mother-in-law a free hand in the administration of the dominions, and "not to burden the same with debts, so that they may be preserved together for the flourishing of both future lines of descent." Of those 8,000 thalers of annual income, the Prince received 4,600 into his own hands, and left 3,400 to his wife as so-called pin money. He likewise promised to claim for himself only a quarter of any future income accruing to her by inheritance, but to leave the remaining three-quarters at her free disposal.
