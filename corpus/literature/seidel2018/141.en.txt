One example is the locality of Betsche (where FL, too, later held property):[^107] Betsche was, until 1793, the property of the Bishopric of Posen, which forbade any settlement of non-Catholics there (there were 522 inhabitants). Then a rapid development set in: by 1843 the population numbered 1,329, and alongside the Catholics there were now also 179 Protestants and 173 Jews. With Prussian thoroughness, documentation on the new state territories was available as early as 1798: K.J. Heubner's "Historical-Statistical-Geographical Description of South Prussia and New East Prussia, or of the Royal Prussian Seizures from Poland in the Years 1798 and 1795".[^108] In this work all localities are recorded, and one finds the locations of the estates referred to when Friedrich von Cölln wrote of the squandering of estates.

There were regulations for the colonists: no service duties at court, only monetary interest payments. The purchase contract stipulated: "Hereditary leaseholders and their descendants shall forever remain wholly free of all hereditary bondage, and shall likewise render the manorial lordship not the least court service nor deliver any produce in kind." Interest was to be paid after the expiry of four rent-free years. Some of these arrangements are strikingly reminiscent of the provisions made for the settlement of craftsmen in Ingelfingen!

In Berlin, the War and Forestry Councillor Triebenfeld was to direct and oversee allocation practice through a General Directorate established by the King. Administration of the new territorial parts was subordinate to Minister Georg von Hoym. A "Founders' Consistory" consisted of him, General Bischofswerder, Triebenfeld, and the "Royal Chamber Servant" Ritz.

Mainly noble military men were interested in acquiring estates. Many of them, as younger siblings of the firstborn heirs, will have had no career prospects other than military service (and administration); owning an estate of one's own would secure a new livelihood, and speculative profits (and tax evasion) might also be possible.
