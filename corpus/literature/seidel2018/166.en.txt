# Seidel (2018), p. 166
# translation by Claude

> Several very wealthy people have, through French protection, managed [...] to free themselves from it, and the burden now falls with all the greater weight upon the middle class.

In a further letter Kahlert came to speak of von Cölln:

> On all sides people are now busy bringing to light the most odious matters and slandering those who stood, whether in the military or [...], at the head of affairs. One of the most zealous contributors to such pasquinades is the former War Councillor von Coelln, known to Your Serene Highness. I remember very well with what courtesy His Highness received this man at Scheiting [probably Scheidnig, the summer residence near Breslau], and instructed me to show him the finest part of the garden — and now this wretched scribbler has taken the liberty, in the third part of his *Confidential Letters*, of having odious remarks about His Highness's military measures printed as well.

What follows is an account of the deserved punishment of von Coelln, who is also once called "Collini", then further of the action of von Held (see p. 142), and finally he mentioned a writing in which "Triebenfeld in particular is treated in the most disparaging manner, and on this occasion much that is odious is also said about his relationship with Your Serene Highness." There follows again a report on the reception of the capitulation of Prenzlau, Blücher's conduct, and also on the painful death of the Duke of Brunswick, who had been mortally wounded in the Battle of Jena-Auerstedt. — All this FL was, in the opinion of the letter-writer Kahlert, to learn while in Öhringen. But there are also other topics, above all after FL had arrived at Slaventzitz in Silesia.
