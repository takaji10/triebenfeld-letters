# Seidel (2018), p. 130
# translation by Claude

Father and son succeeded in recruiting Johann Georg Glenk of Schwäbisch Hall, who was employed there at the great saline ("Baugegenschreiber", a building and accounts clerk). Glenk's wish to be granted leave from Hall in order to take over the direction of the Weißbach saline was rejected by the councils of the Imperial City. He changed employment nonetheless and entered Ingelfingen service in early 1781 as Court Councillor (Hofrat). His annual salary of 600 florins was quite considerable, and he had, besides, a number of additional benefits (by comparison: the annual salary of the best-paid clergyman in Ingelfingen, the Court Preacher, amounted to 120 florins, to which were added a further 24 florins for wine as well as free provisions in kind). Supervision of the salt works lay with FL. — It was this same Glenck who gave concrete architectural form to the Hereditary Prince's plans for the settlement of craftsmen in Ingelfingen — the later Mariannenvorstadt — and who saw the project through until his death in 1801 (see p. 34f.).

Glenck worked on the rehabilitation of the salines. In 1787 he even began boring a further shaft at Niedernhall, which was given the name Friedrich Ludwig Brunnen. Truly profitable, however, they did not become in this period.

This deficiency was also rooted in the great quantity of wood required and its price. The surrounding forests — for instance up above at Hermersberg Castle — were already suffering. With coal as a heating material of higher calorific value, an improvement might perhaps have been achieved, and this was evident to FL as well. He himself had some acquaintance with the matter from his own possessions in Upper Silesia, where coal mining and ore mining were carried on. And Glenck had become familiar with coal deposits and mines there on study journeys.

Glenck had some understanding of geology and mineralogy. In test borings, it was believed that a coal seam had indeed been found. In order to be able to begin mining, capital was now also needed. FL called, in a printed promotional tract, for the formation of a company; it states there, among other things: "Friedrich Ludwig, Hereditary Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, grants to Court Councillor Glenck the establishment of coal mines in the lands of Hohenlohe."
