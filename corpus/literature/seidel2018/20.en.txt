## 2 The Prince's Career

### 2.1 Early Youth in Ingelfingen and Öhringen

Friedrich Ludwig was born on 31 January 1746 in Ingelfingen as the eldest son of Heinrich August von Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen. His mother Wilhelmine was a Princess of Hohenlohe-Neuenstein-Öhringen; in the choice of partners the family had kept to its own circle — as so often in Hohenlohe. It is also worth noting that his godfathers were the Margrave of Ansbach and Bayreuth and King George of England.

The boy grew up at first in the Ingelfingen residence with an "Informator" (tutor), Conradi. When the latter accepted a parish post in Rupertshofen (belonging to another Hohenlohe court) in 1755, the child was brought to the court at Öhringen, where the prince's own son had already died in 1755. There he was instructed together with his cousin Count Castell-Rüdinghausen by the tutor Johann Christoph Riegel; a French "language master" was also employed (he later of course wrote and spoke French, and many of his letters, in keeping with the custom of the time, are composed in French, including ones within the family, such as those between his wife and himself). The Court Painter Schillinger and his assistant Wagner also took an "active part" in portions of the instruction. He then also attended the Gymnasium in Öhringen. — Particularly worth noting, in view of his later persistent financial troubles, is that the boy Friedrich Ludwig was required to keep his own accounts of daily expenditure out of his pocket money; a reprimand for lack of punctuality in this regard is documented.[^9] Unfortunately this educational measure had no success — financial troubles accompanied him throughout his life.
