The number of catastrophes was great enough: the end of his marriage; the debts; the military defeats without any prospect of rehabilitation; the certainty of no longer being needed; the quasi-banishment from Hohenlohe. This did not mean, however, that he had nothing to do in Silesia. Slaventzitz, Koschentin, and Landsberg were the estates there that still needed to be developed and that were managed by his staff. In addition there were the previously mentioned acquisitions in South Prussia, which in retrospect must clearly be classed as highly risky, and for which — along with their final financing, which was never successfully achieved — he relied on his General Plenipotentiary von Triebenfeld.

### 9.4 Interim Summary I

Friedrich Ludwig made his career in the military, where his name was "eminently well known"; he could look back on a 40-year career with many honors, and had been richly furnished with decorations. He took the liberty of enumerating much of this in his Regierungserklärung of 1796 (p. 64). In Ingelfingen he was most certainly open to advancement and improvement of economic conditions, and he also did something toward it. He was evidently loved by the citizens there and is described as affable. They were all no doubt proud of the Mariannenstraße, surely his most enduring achievement for the residential town. That, too, had cost a great deal of money. The building fund, for which Amtmann Taurinus was responsible, was often empty, even though the buildings were, for the most part, only advance-financed and then sold to their users.
