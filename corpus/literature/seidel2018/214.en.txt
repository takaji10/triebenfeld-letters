Massenbach has, at least for 1806, a poor reputation among many contemporaries and near-contemporary writers. As an intellectual, he reflected far more on the overall political situation of Europe than would ever be known of FL. FL remained wholly bound by his obligation to his liege lord, the Prussian King, and it was thus his task to protect the Empire from the enemy, France. Whatever progressive impulse for the future of Europe emanated from revolutionary France (not from Napoleon's wars) does not appear to have engaged him. In the longer text already mentioned, which he wrote in 1805 on the political situation in Europe to the publicist Böttiger, FL muses almost entirely on questions of power and military alliances.[^155] Massenbach, after the end of the military hostilities, was to conduct himself as one committed to so-called progress — and would then have to pay for it.

### 22.3 Massenbach as a Political Author: Writings I

His personal range of knowledge was very broad: antiquity, mathematics, cartography, military affairs; and this is also evident in his activity as a writer. Between 1782 and 1802 fifteen articles and books had already appeared. After that, in rapid succession, came:

- Marc Aurel und Sully, 1806.
- Lichtstrahlen. Beiträge zur Geschichte der Jahre 1805, 1806, 1807. Eine Zeitschrift in freien Heften von einer Gesellschaft wahrheitsliebender Militärpersonen, Zivilbeamten, Gelehrter. Heft 1-5. Hamburg und Leipzig, 1807/08.
- Rückerinnerungen an große Männer, 1808.
- Vollständiger Bericht von den Operationen der Königlich Preußischen Armee, welche im Feldzug von 1806 unter den Befehlen des Fürsten von Hohenlohe. Hamburg und Leipzig: Historisch-Politisch-Militärisches Institut, 1808.
