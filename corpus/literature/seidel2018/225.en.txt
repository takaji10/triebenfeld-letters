## 23 Who Was Friedrich Ludwig, Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen?

### 23.1 Friedrich Ludwig and the Kings of Prussia, Frederick William II and III

FL, although born into a very small Hohenlohe dominion, had already risen as Hereditary Prince, in his military capacity within Prussian state service, to become one of the leading generals. The military, and its officers in particular, was bound to the King, who was the absolute ruler and holder of supreme command. His decisions were binding. From his soldiers and his commanders he expected unconditional obedience. FL never called these arrangements into question. The King — beginning with Frederick II, then Frederick William II, and, from 1797, Frederick William III — always remained for him the supreme authority; loyalty, and obedience too, was an absolute requirement.

On matters of high (foreign) policy he rarely voiced his own thoughts; his views of society were marked by skepticism.

The duties of a senior officer in times without armed conflict can include diplomatic missions. FL undertook such missions after the conclusion of the Peace of Basel — see p. 50f. He was, moreover, responsible for the internal constitution of the troops — training and the recruitment of prospective officers, for example. On these matters there survive a few letters to the King, though most of the correspondence will have gone to the Prussian military archive. They concerned individual officers whose reassignment was under discussion, who had been proposed for promotion, and similar matters.
