# Seidel (2018), p. 49
# translation by Claude

At any rate, when he was once summoned by him in September of the fateful year 1806, Frederick William III put him up in the rooms of Frederick II in the palace. FL would proudly report this on several occasions. Von Marwitz, FL's former aide-de-camp, writes in his memoirs that these were "now" occupied by the Crown Princess, and further: "Everyone crowded around the man who had sacrificed his princely holdings in order to follow the call of honor and the wishes of the Prussian Army."[^31]

FL had previously been Governor of Ansbach-Bayreuth, and from 1791 of Breslau. Silesia had become the center of his life; see p. 64f. on this.

He took part in the War of the First Coalition against France. At the so-called Cannonade of Valmy (13 September 1792), where the commander-in-chief, the Duke of Brunswick, had decided upon a retreat, he was not directly involved. He had been given the task of forming an advance guard with a cavalry troop and holding a corresponding French formation in check, driving it back if possible. He succeeded in this, but the Duke of Brunswick had made the above-mentioned decision with the main army. FL won numerous smaller battles in the course of this campaign. In particular, he defeated the French at Kaiserslautern, although he actually had no clear orders for a battle. From then on he was regarded as "the Victor of Kaiserslautern." Details of this campaign, which Fischer (1871) describes precisely, are otherwise without significance. Goethe took part in it as companion to Duke Charles Augustus and later wrote "The Campaign in France"[^32] about it.

From May 1794, FL was Inspector-General of the Lower Silesian Infantry in Breslau, then again head of an infantry regiment. In 1795 he received the superior inspection over the Prussian troops in the Franconian Margraviates and took up headquarters in Ansbach.
