# Seidel (2018), p. 108
# translation by Claude

via Stralsund and Malmö to Königsberg — reported the following remark by the King there, as related by von Marwitz: "Sadly true enough that some did not do their duty, - but the Prince Hohenlohe, so distinguished a gentleman — I cannot comprehend how he came to that capitulation!"

From this it may be inferred that "Prenzlau" had impressed itself deeply upon the King's memory. FL was never again to be free of it. With this capitulation, and after a stubborn negotiation with the commander of the superior French forces, he had acted rightly, and had thereby saved the lives of many soldiers. It had, however, been preceded by a misjudgment of the situation on the part of von Massenbach, one that soon became known and that Massenbach himself did not gloss over even in a writing of his own. FL, for his part, had taken the responsibility upon himself before the King.[^79] — The relationship between FL and von Massenbach will be further discussed in Chapter 22.

And one further point ought to be clarified: what does Marwitz mean by the French "flatteries"? It is documented that the French, as the leading force behind the "Confederation of the Rhine", were interested in winning over as many "states" as possible for the alliance of South German territorial rulers. Hohenlohe did not belong to the Confederation of the Rhine. As a means to this end — the creation of a larger confederation of states under Napoleon's influence — the forging of dynastic ties also served this purpose, as in the marriage of Napoleon's brother Jerome to Catherine of Württemberg and that of Stephanie de Beauharnais to Charles Louis of Baden. And there is a document,[^80] according to which FL's daughters Adelheid and Louise Amalie were drawn into such considerations, with prospective marriage candidates being named! That, however, had already taken place before the defeat and the captivity.

On 13 July 1806 the "Act of the Confederation of the Rhine" had been signed in Paris. FL, who ten years before had secretly hoped for a Duchy of Franconia, had now refused — despite being offered a large territorial lordship — to join in with his Hohenlohe domain
