A biography of the deceased is not the subject. Anyone who — the author admits — upon first reading the two extensive texts had expectations regarding the great turning point in the Princess's life, the separation from FL and the children, then still small, will find not a single word on the matter!

In the sermon of 1 June, the pastor, after paying homage to "our most serene, most gracious Prince and Lord," who "as the eldest of the children mourns a dearly beloved mother," turns briefly to the deceased, giving her name, date of birth, the early loss of her father, her mother's second marriage to Prince Sacken, and her own marriage to the then Most Serene Lord Hereditary Prince Friedrich Ludwig zu Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, and the children of that marriage (that is, only those from this marriage). Nothing more is said of her life, of anything personal. There follow examples from biblical stories concerning death and maternal love; then back again to Prince August and his loss of his mother. God, death, rank and birth, and faith are of course also discussed; theologically demanding, yet (one may say so) remaining within a conventional framework. Of the second marriage and the two further daughters, not a word.

The further "Memorial Address on the Death of the Late Departed Mother of His Serene Highness the Reigning Prince and Lord of Hohenlohe-Öhringen-Slaventzitz, delivered on 4 June at Laskowitz" was no less devout toward the Prince. Nevertheless, the text engages intensively with the "mighty of every tongue," with the advantages of their exalted station, with "excess and dissolution," "outrages and misdeeds"! It sets against them the God-fearing and charitable, righteous, exemplary, and virtuous souls.
