## 21 Information on the Male Descendants

### 21.1 The Son and Successor August II

The firstborn (born 1784 in Breslau and died 1853 in Slaventzitz) studied in Erlangen from November 1802 to April 1805. Von Stetten, the father's former aide-de-camp, accompanied him there, having previously secured generous accommodation befitting his rank (three rooms, an alcove; stabling for four horses, servants, "attendance," for 125 florins payable semi-annually). The costs of the period of study were covered by his grandmother Princess Sacken, with an allowance of 3,000 thalers annually. The military sciences were not offered as a subject of study in Erlangen, and to his father's regret, August was not much interested in that in any case.[^147] It must have been the legal sciences, for in a letter from FL to August, exactly one professor in Erlangen is named, Karl August Gründler, whose field of teaching (with an emphasis on Prussian law) was jurisprudence. He (born 1770) had been called from Halle to Erlangen in 1797. In a commemorative volume of the university from the mid-nineteenth century, all the professors are listed,[^148] and there is also information about the students, along with their year of matriculation and subject of study;[^149] not, unfortunately, about all of them, for prefixed to the general register are the names of a few princes, that is, sons from especially high-ranking houses, and these are given without indication of their subject of study (is this perhaps an early form of data protection?). Alongside August one finds there, with exactly the same date of matriculation, Eugen Prince of Württemberg (which can hardly have been a coincidence).
