# Seidel (2018), p. 74
# translation by Claude

Dittersdorf provided the musical entertainment with a concert. One finds this in the annals of the Prussian monarchy, "Das Leben der Königin von Preußen Luise Auguste Wilhelmine Amalie"[^54] as well as in K. F. Klöden's "Lebens- und Regierungsgeschichte Friedrich Wilhelms des Dritten, Königs von Preußen"[^55]. For the "review" in the year 1800:

> *In the evening the Prince of Hohenlohe gave them a brilliant festivity in his park at Scheitnig, in which the public too could take part. A free masked ball was held in the park, and it was illuminated in the most tasteful manner, with many surprising features.*[^56]

In an extensive work, "Wanderungen durch Breslau und dessen Umgebungen nebst weiteren Ausflügen", 1836, its author Julius Krebs writes:

> *Breslau must give thanks to the Prince of Hohenlohe, who, beyond his means, supplied what the commune, given the circumstances of the time, could not yet provide — that he laid out this park with its lofty, beautiful leafy halls, and, full of humanity, granted admission to everyone.*[^57]

Mentioned are an equestrian statue of Frederick II, a column of Trajan and reproductions of antique figures, a temple at the end of the main path with a bust of Frederick William II, as well as busts of the royal couple Frederick William III and Queen Louise. The author also expresses himself critically: he considers the park "tastelessly overladen". It is to be surmised that with "beyond his means" he was alluding to FL's financial distress, which had certainly become known.

It is unknown what his personal circumstances looked like during this outwardly so splendid period. His wife had, after all, unexpectedly left him and Breslau in 1799.
