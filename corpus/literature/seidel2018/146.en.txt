The matter concerned a lordship of Krotoszyn, of which the King wrote that it might yield an annual return of 60,000 reichsthalers. In any case he informed him that the matter lay with his Minister of State, von Struensee, and had not yet been decided.[^114] Evidently the King, in his reply, had in mind a proper procedure into which he did not wish to intervene.

It could indeed have been an application by Massenbach! He was interested in the estates. He stood close to the court, having given the King's son instruction in mathematics. In March 1798 he reported in a letter to FL that "His Royal Majesty has most graciously deigned to enfeoff me with South Prussian estates." He enclosed a list of these with the letter and thanked FL "for your gracious efforts." FL, then, must have interceded on his behalf. Massenbach even asked for advice

> *whether I should keep all these estates and lease them out […] or bring them into better condition […] so that they would represent double the value […] this would at a stroke make me a rich man, and my wish would not merely be fulfilled but exceeded.[^115]*

The appended list names the prices and former owners of six estates, among them two convents in Posen and the Archbishop of Gniezno. The estate at Bialokosch appears as No. 2 on the reproduced list, at a price of 2,580 Rth. Its former owner was the convent of nuns in Posen. Massenbach would keep it until his death and bequeath it to his children and grandchildren. But he too incurred debts. — Bialokosch still bears that name today. It appears on maps, not very far northwest of Posen.
