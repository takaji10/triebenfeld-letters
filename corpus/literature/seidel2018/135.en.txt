# Seidel (2018), p. 135
# translation by Claude

> *though I have no news of this as yet. Then you will no longer be plagued by me. This as a consolation. [signature of Friedrich Ludwig]*

This letter shows, for one thing, some linguistic peculiarities that are also found elsewhere. Unfortunately not everything can be clarified either: for example, what is meant by Bitbus (perhaps he means Bytkow)? For what purpose does FL need "your pen"? If the addressee is indeed Glenck, it must be a request for writing assistance from his former private secretary. For his own handwriting was and indeed still is not very easy to read, and he probably knew it. For which addressees did he — or rather the writer, who was apparently to place a whole day at his (FL's) disposal — take such trouble? – That he was glad actually to have raised money that he wished to pay out, or from which he wished to pay something, certainly pleased the Hofräthin Hahn — though whether it was all that he owed her is another matter. A (partial) resolution of what was at first puzzling suggests itself: Glenck is asked by FL to come to him because he, the head of the saltworks and former secretary, will shortly be traveling to Silesia, since there he can consult with the mining expert Baildon. In Silesia, after all, the coal-mining venture continued, which FL as a prisoner in Öhringen could not himself direct. He remained, as before, the owner in Silesia and had people on the spot, for example the Hofrath Hahn ("From Hahn I have a report"). His wife, with or without this title, was the Hofräthin Hahn. Hahn's activities have already been mentioned (see p. 122). Which women ("Weiber") surrounded FL in Öhringen — his daughters? He was mistaken in assuming he would soon get away from Öhringen; he was not to receive the corresponding news until 1808.

In 1810 there was particular "urgency" regarding decisions on continuing the Weißbach undertaking. To a letter in May Glenck enclosed an accounting, and this time sealed the whole thing.
