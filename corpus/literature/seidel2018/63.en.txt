# seidel2018  63  en
# Seidel (2018), p. 63
# translation by Claude

> *most of the cases. The wheel is turning mightily. God grant that I may act happily and effectively toward the liberation of good people. I stand at the head of the Prussian-Saxon army. Of the latter the Elector entrusted me yesterday with the supreme command. All the troops are full of courage, of the best will, and the Prussians are excellently equipped with horses for this. Bally has arrived with my equipage and everything is in good order and is only being completed still. Here the court treats me well. The King honors me with full confidence. The Queen and the Princesses in Berlin have shown me a rare degree of esteem. The King honored me with distinctions and many tokens of favor. I lodged in the Palace, in Frederick II's own rooms, had my own royal attendance, and equipage with Kurnen [...]. I was distinguished more than ever, and more than I expected. I was consulted above all on political and military matters, au fait and with great candor; my advice was heeded, and the present arrangements are to my liking. Princess Solms is here, and goes tomorrow to Berlin. At the latest, I shall on the 24th [...] take up my quarters in Freiberg. You had best address your letters directly by hand to the valet Tanesi in Bayreuth. Tauentzien is and remains, as I wish, in Hof. Greet everyone from me. God keep you all in health and blessing. I hope, and wish, for a swift reunion. Your loyal Father [signature of Friedrich Ludwig]*[^40]

This letter is the last one available before numerous discussions among the political and military leaders of the allied Prussian and Saxon army in Jena and other places, and indeed also before the defeat at Jena and Auerstädt. Private matters nevertheless still occur. Hohenlohe is by now already part of the Kingdom of Württemberg. But before all this comes to pass, the account must once more turn back to "the Ingelfingen years."
