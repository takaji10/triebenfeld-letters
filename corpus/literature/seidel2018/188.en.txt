document of its time from a student (admittedly a particularly well-off one) at the University of Tübingen:

> *Gracious Uncle!*  
> *For your gracious wishes on my birthday I give humble thanks and add my most heartfelt wishes for your welfare and long life, to which wishes your approaching birthday gives me occasion. Gracious Uncle! live happily and joyfully — these are the best wishes I can offer you for your birthday celebration. In accordance with your gracious command I shall give you a description of my courses. From 8 to 9 o'clock I attend Professor Dr. [Schuebler]'s lectures on Agronomy. This is a mixture of chemistry, agriculture, geography, and mineralogy; on the whole the ensemble is quite good, and it teaches the [components] of good and poor soils and what vegetables thrive best in them. There is also somewhat less physics involved, particularly with regard to the physical properties of soil types. Prof. Schuebler has come from [G.], where he delivered the lectures, and did not have time to work everything out fully, but it is to be hoped that next half-year he will certainly lecture quite well. From 9 to 10 o'clock I ride at the riding school here, which is quite well appointed and where I have already made fairly good progress. From 10 to 11 o'clock I attend Chemistry with Prof. Christian Gmelin. He has only just returned from distant travels, where in Stockholm, London, and Paris he visited and heard the great Schweiker everywhere. He lives quite in a lofty sphere and combines with his lectures a good many experiments, in which he is especially strong. From 11 to 12 I attend Euclid with P. Pfleiderer. This Pfleiderer is a very great mathematician, but unfortunately a man of 86 years who is also beginning to falter in speech, so that in the end one can no longer understand him at all. From 2 to 3 o'clock I attend Analytical Geometry with Prof. Bodenberger, who is an extremely skilful man. He also analyzes conic sections at the same time.
