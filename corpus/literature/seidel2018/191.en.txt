# Seidel (2018), p. 191
# translation by Claude

> *and also to come to an understanding with you on one point, which I do not wish to entrust to the pen, but which is of the utmost importance to me. I share brotherly in August's misfortune; she will, I hope, not doubt this. — Emilie's confinement went off very well. Both she and the child are enjoying the best of health. The weather here is splendid, and everything here gives grounds to hope for a truly fruitful year, which would be much to be wished, since grain prices have risen to the highest degree — for example, a monthly ration of horse-feed costs some 36 reichsthalers, where it formerly cost only 11 reichsthalers./Be so gracious, best of fathers, as to commend me to all those around you. — With the deepest respect and childlike love I remain/Your obedient son and Alexander Hohenlohe*

Not two weeks later, on 27 January, the next letter follows. This one must have been written in Berlin, for the King was worried, as we read, on account of the measles epidemic prevailing there:

> *Dear Father, Would that I, dear father, might follow this letter in person, so as to bring you in speech my childlike feelings and good wishes for your birthday. Only your well-being can be the foundation of my own, and this sheet would be too small for my wishes. The King is still unwell and is being especially careful this time, because measles is going about here, and already three royal princes and just as many princesses are laid low with this illness. This is the reason why I have not yet been presented to the King. I expect it daily, however. Since the Court, for fear of the measles, lives entirely in seclusion, I have nonetheless paid visits in other houses, as for instance to Count Moltke. — Adolf has at this moment gone away for a few days to Count Lenau's, for his hunts.
