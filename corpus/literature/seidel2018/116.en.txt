### 11.2 Move to Upper Silesia

On 4 January 1809, FL, having been expelled by the King of Württemberg, arrived from Öhringen in Slaventzitz, where he was to live for about another 9 years. The palace had formerly belonged to the family of Count Hoym, that is, to the early-deceased father of his ex-wife Marianne. FL had received it, transferred to him as his own property; it was presumably free of debt — a highly important consideration for the further life of the great debtor. He maintained a small household there. He was still a Prince, at least by way of this designation as a title of address, and still a Serene Highness, but he no longer possessed any sovereign rights — only property.

His mother-in-law had taken note of the exceedingly difficult financial situation. In 1808 she wrote him a letter on the matter (thus still while he was at Öhringen):

> *We would both now have a great deal less worry if you had earlier granted me your confidence, which I so wholly deserve. Through lack of confidence you have damaged the good cause, and it can no longer now be carried out as well as it could have been before. Nevertheless I shall do what I can, for I love your honor as I love my own.[^87]*

As grandmother of his children, she evidently still regarded herself as an entrepreneur and as a responsible family member acting in his favor. In the immediate term she was indeed able to help. But the misery continued. The biographer Fischer speaks, for the years 1810/11, of "total economic ruin."
