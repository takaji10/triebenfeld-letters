# Seidel (2018), p. 87
# translation by Claude

Much appears understandable. With the shared responsibility for all manner of contracts made by FL, which she too signed, and their foreseeable financial consequences for the future, she describes a critical point in the marriage. The harshness of some provisions in the estate documents appears striking (to me as well), particularly when one's own mother is responsible for them. Thus the Transact and the testament take little account of Marianne, at least in comparison with the children who are to carry forward "the flourishing of the House of Hohenlohe." Her daughters from the second marriage really do come up short – but that is an assessment of the circumstances that arose after the separation, not a justification for her step 12 years earlier. Is it really only the finances, FL's weakest point? It has, unfortunately, become apparent that she herself, in her new financial situation, for which she was responsible together with Princess Sacken, does not manage well (Bellin Castle and her complaint about Count Hardenberg, see p. 174f.). – And FL's infidelities will not have been the cause either.

From today's perspective, one may certainly state that the abandonment of the two small children Adolph and Alexander – that is, the "how" of the separation – appears unacceptable. On this point, the letter says nothing.
