After the definitively lost battles of Moscow and Stalingrad, when the leading Nazis see the growing threat, first of all a general mobilisation is proclaimed by Hitler. For the first time Goebbels publicly admits that the crisis on the Eastern Front has reached its peak, and that Stalingrad is a great alarm for the fate of the German nation. No German may think of a compromise. The nation is called to a hard war and is to be ready for all sacrifices if the führer gives such an order. In the sports palace, under the slogan "Total war – the shortest war", only selected and vetted guests were gathered. These are 14,000 fanatical Nazis, who sing and applaud after each of the harsher passages of this mainly anti-Jewish philippic. Goebbels calls with the words of the poet: Now, people, arise and let the storm break loose ("*Nun, Volk steh auf, und Sturm brich los*!"). The whole staging of the rally is meant, among other things, to reject the allied voices saying that the Germans had lost their faith in victory. But at the same time he wants to "feed" his ambitions, counting on Hitler granting him greater power.

On the same day a book publication of the *Sportpalastrede* appears. The minister of propaganda skilfully reverses the whole agitational machine. The calls about the new space being conquered for the great Third Reich fall silent. In short, it can be put as follows: Now Germany is "defending" old Europe, the whole world, against the Jews and the onslaught of the Bolshevik plague. Bruno Mielke, for his part, writes: "That the decision with Russia and thus the end of the war will come this year, we all feel it, which is why personal matters must now be set aside; when everything is over, there will be a beautiful return home and, as the crowning of the joy, let it be my wedding." On the fourth of March the "enjoying of quiet" in the rear comes to an end. An urgent order arrives, calling up the folksdojczowie (Germans from Poland, that is, the Kraj Warty) to report to the 2 batalion.
