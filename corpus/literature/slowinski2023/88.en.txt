We do not have to choose (presumably it was expected of the brother that upon taking office he would sign some ideological declarations?), "we have already fought in the difficult period of Poland, and woe to us if we depart from our so clearly defined path. The decision, therefore, lies on the other side: whether they will allow faithful people to work, or whether they will want to get rid of them." (Presumably the second option was chosen, for from another letter it is known that Gustav was soon called up for service in the navy.) "Now, when the time is dangerous, we must remain with the Lord. Our fate and that of our people is in His hands. Before the war, 'in the difficult period of Poland,' we persevered in the faith, whereas in the young Wartheland everyone became intoxicated with 'the new fame.' They abandoned it and fell far away." This "weakening" in participation in services was also described in his letter by Pastor Gross. Bruno confesses that he too was not free of this sin. However, at this moment, when the Lord has granted them the grace of conversion and forbearance, "we want to fight knightly, unto blood," so that nothing bad befalls them.

Bruno Mielke writes the letter in the first days of May 1944. It is striking that the remarks concerning the course of the war were not challenged by the censorship. Could this "loosening" of control perhaps be evidence of broader moods in the army? Never before in the writings available to us had he noted considerations of this kind. Although he does not write it outright, he nevertheless tries to prepare his loved ones "for the worst" – that is, the occupation of the Wartheland by the troops advancing from the East. I do not want to see the lament, the tears, the suffering that will befall our nation if the threatening dangers come to pass! How will they, the liberated Poles, then treat you, the poorest of you!
