He does not yet condemn Hans, who does not know the splendour of Christ. That is why he quietly wants to give them a Bible as a wedding present. He will not be able to come to the wedding, and in this situation he does not want to, either. He knows that Hans loves her, that he is kind to the family and to him, and this is what is terrible for him. He only hopes that his sister does not share this strict prohibition, and that they will be able to talk about everything. He writes at the end: "this is my request and my wish for you – that you should remain in your faith, which is clear and dear to you, steadfast and faithful. You might yet bring Hans to the faith. But it is not simple".

These deeply zealous concerns about the state of his sister's soul and the attitude of her fiancé must have been repeated, for a letter of Anna's has come to light in which she sets out her position, firmly grounded in reality. "I am sorry that I cause you restless hours, but believe me, there is no need for you to worry on my account. I can neither prove nor explain it to you, but I know that your worries are unnecessary, and they, on the other hand, unsettle me. If I were to leave Hans on your account or on yours, then I would after all gain nothing, since from his side no danger threatens me, if one may call it that. He loves me only too much to want to force me into anything I do not want. Am I to wait for a pastor who perhaps more than once has been insincere? I do not want, like my sisters, to be 40 or more years old and then suffer hysterical fits, because overburdened nerves lose hope. Those I have known were little different from Hans. He is at least loyal and trustworthy. And by no means is he an unbeliever."
