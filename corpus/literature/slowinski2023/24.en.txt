The deceased author, however, had no influence on whom the publishing house would invite to write the introduction to a new edition. It was Alfred Rosenberg, the creator of a criminal ideology, whose *Der Mythus des 20 Jahrhunderts* [The Myth of the Twentieth Century], alongside Adolf Hitler's *Mein Kampf*, played a decisive role in formatting German minds in the spirit of the racial superiority of the nation of masters, for whom an appropriate living space is to be conquered. The little library also contained books about important historical figures, a selection which – I believe – was not accidental. There is the well-known study by the outstanding Belgian classical philologist Joseph Bidez, *Julian der Abtrünnige*, Munich 1940. Julian the Apostate is the emperor who strove to abolish Christianity and restore the old Roman religious cults. It would be hard to find a clearer analogy to the Nazi idea of Germania. The collection includes a work by Leopold von Ranke, *Geschichte Wallensteins*. The history of the outstanding commander from the times of the Thirty Years' War. There is also a history of the King of Prussia, Frederick William IV. In the household sewn into the forest, distant from large cities and main routes, there is the popular novel by Wilhelm Raabe, *Die Chronik der Sperlingsgasse*, 1857 [The Chronicle of Sparrow Lane]. It is the story of a small-town loser who in his eccentricity is able to preserve his own personality, allowing him to cope with an aggressive reality. This "hero of a gray life" brings valuable humanistic ideals into the cold, capitalist society…[^7].

Among the books there are, of course, titles such as the Holy Scripture of the Old and New Testament (*Die Bibel oder die ganze Heilige Schrift des Alten und Neuen Testaments*, Vienna 1886). 


[^7]: *Mały słownik pisarzy niemieckich, austriackich i szwajcarskich* [A Small Dictionary of German, Austrian and Swiss Writers]. Warsaw 1973, p. 286.
