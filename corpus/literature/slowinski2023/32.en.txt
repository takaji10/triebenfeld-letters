# Słowiński (2023), p. 32
# translation by the editor's files

> ***I will praise the Lord as long as I live***  
> (Ps 146,2)

The first of the surviving letters from the period of service in the Wehrmacht was written in Vienna on March 30, 1942. Called up to the army, he sets off with other recruits on a long journey. First Chodzież, then Kutno, Poznań, and onward to Wrocław. From there, after eight hours, "we arrived safely in Vienna – the city of my dreams. […] Our final destination, where we will be trained, is Klagenfurt, located about 15 km from the border with Italy, in the south of Austria. It will surely be beautiful there in the Alps; it already was during the journey. Service in the mountains can be exhausting, for we are assigned to the *Gebirgsjäger* [mountain riflemen] unit. Everything was settled; choice did not come into it at all, besides, every thing and group has its good and bad (hard) sides. It is best to accept what has been given; one does not have to have any reproaches later". In the margin we read an addition: "We finally arrived. Everything is very beautiful and orderly. I feel as if in the boarding house of some seminary. The officers are kind. We get everything". He had taken so many things unnecessarily. And further: "And now my comrades. Most of them are ethnic Germans (without citizenship) and *Bessarabians*, some are also Germans with citizenship. The majority are weak, some are quite hopeless, and among them there are persons who exploit others. If they can manage, then I certainly can. The fact that among them there are persons who exploit others is not consoling, for as is known the whole group suffers because of it".
