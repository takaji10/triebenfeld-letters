A letter reporting the company's arrival at its destination has happily survived. "With God's help we survived "the first stage of the great march" to the training ground, where within 6 weeks we are to complete our training. First there were about 300 km, which we covered, doing up to 35 km a day. Usually we marched at night, from 8 in the evening to 3 in the morning, that is, as the night hours are called here. For we are in such a marvellous place where for months there is no night and no dusk. The sun sinks towards the horizon, remains there, to rise majestically again after midnight. All the time during the march you have the impression that you are travelling either in the evening hours or in the early morning. Only my watch told me whether it was evening or morning. Most of the days of the march were, thank God, fair and sunny, and so simply beautiful for walking. Our road led mostly through forest. In Finland forests predominate, but small forests (bare firs, spruces, pines), with bogs and hillocks. One would march gladly, were it not so hard for someone not accustomed to it. In the end I almost collapsed, or rather my legs refused obedience: they ached, pricked and swelled. Therefore for the last 15 km I rode in a vehicle together with the other limping ones to the destination; that saved me, otherwise I would have ruined my legs.
