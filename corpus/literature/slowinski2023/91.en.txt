This is interesting information in the context of what was to happen three months later. For on 4 September the Finns halted their participation in the fighting against the Red Army, and on the 19th of that month an armistice was signed between the USSR and Finland. One of its conditions was the participation of Finnish troops in an offensive against the German troops, which were withdrawing from Lapland to Norway. And so it happened. From 15 September the Finns are already fighting on the side of the Allies, together with their former and quite recent enemy: the Red Army. In October the operation of the Red Army – as it was named, the Petsamo–Kirkenes Operation – will set in motion with full force, as a result of which the German 20th Mountain Army was pushed out of the northern territories of Norway and Finland. Bruno writes: "How it will be for me here and how far my wishes will be fulfilled here remains in God's hands. His will has led me here, to His guidance I wish to continue to entrust myself."

How does he understand his part in this war? As an act of divine will. Is this war, then, also the work of God?

From the company in which he had served until recently, he was suddenly transferred to the medical company. He writes the letter, as usual, on Sunday afternoon. In the holy morning hours they had known no peace. They had to scrub the rooms and clean their weapons. Just before dinner he managed to slip away to a quiet place for "the eternal word." The last sentences of this letter, the last sentences of the 33-year-old Bruno Mielke that have reached us, are the words of a man who seems to perceive the whole tragedy of war. In them there is also a muffled trace that something is beginning to stir in his conscience.
