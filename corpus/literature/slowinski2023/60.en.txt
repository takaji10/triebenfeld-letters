On 30 January 1943, from Berlin radio, at the time of the daily communiqués, the sounds of the sombre adagio from Anton Bruckner's *Seventh Symphony* suddenly begin to flow. They precede the first official communiqué of this war about the lost battle of Stalingrad, the capitulation of the 6 Armia and the taking into captivity of the thousands of exhausted and starving soldiers encircled in the cauldron. There is no possibility that this communiqué did not reach the front. They surely also had some information channels of their own. Only speaking about this catastrophe threatened with an accusation of sowing defeatism and of repeating the news from the leaflets scattered over the Russian lines. With the obvious consequences.

What a strange letter, then, reaches his brother Gustav. About the planned marriage, about the fact that he will take his wedding vows only when he is released from service. For as a soldier he will get only a few days of leave. The engagement and the wedding are to be the concluding feast of thanksgiving, a joyful beginning of private life. Bruno describes at length the girl from Trzemeszno, Elfride Fiedler: that she is thrifty, lively and talkative despite her low education. But that is good, because he does not want a wife with higher education who would constantly instruct him. There is, however, a certain catch – the girl, her parents, her siblings belong to the community of Sabbath-keepers. But if she decides to marry him, she will have to convert to Lutheranism, and that before the engagement. "I have not the slightest desire to go to church alone."
