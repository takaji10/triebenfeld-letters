A hundred years later, by the effort of the whole community of Łazińsk, whose works from 1925 were directed by the cantor Gustav Adolf Mielke, a new building was erected, housing the classrooms, the office, and the dwelling of the headmaster and cantor in one person. Yet Gustav Mielke's engagement did not concern only educational matters and religious duties. He was an exceedingly active propagator of the German nationalists and of Nazi propaganda. This activity must have – even for such a multicultural region – aroused significant reservations on the part of the authorities, for Mielke was forced to leave Łazińsk in 1936 and to settle somewhere in Galicia. He returned together with the Wehrmacht in 1939. Throughout the whole war the school was housed in this building, sizable by village standards, along with a cantorate greatly curtailed in its rights, and the Mielke family lived there.

One must, however, also note further sentences from the reminiscences quoted above. "The aggressiveness of the local Germans towards the Poles and towards the third nation, settled in great numbers since old times in Zagórów – the Jews – will reveal itself only with unprecedented cruelty in the period of the Hitlerite occupation"[^6]. The entry of the Russians on 18 January 1945 and the rebuilding of the Polish administration put an end to the long history of this cantorate. All the householders in April of that year were expropriated. Eugenia Plitt, an inhabitant of Łazińsk, remembered that at the beginning it was said that this was a temporary measure… Years passed, and the old German school and the cantor's house was adapted into a residential building for teachers.
