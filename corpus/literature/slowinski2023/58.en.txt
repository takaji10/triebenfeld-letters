# Słowiński (2023), p. 58
# translation by the editor's files

 Let us recall: "Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? Shall tribulation, or distress, or persecution"… and the further verses, which say that no powers "nor height nor depth nor any other creation" will be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus, our Lord" (Rom 8:39)[^14]. The cited letter of St Paul is a confirmation of what we may suspect, and what Bruno Mielke did not write outright for a long time. Amid all the many-times-repeated declarations, the fidelity to the führer and the subordination to the superiors, there is a higher authority over them, to which this soldier gave himself completely. It is God.

The description of frontline everyday life is written on 27 January. The end of the month brings hope for a milder winter. Surely the worst has already passed, and in any case, compared with other sectors of the front it was milder for them – since the temperatures reached at most minus 20-25 degrees. The worst thing of all was the incredibly strong snowstorms, something they do not know in the Kraj Warty. He rejoices at the coming time when their company will be withdrawn a few kilometres to the rear. Probably there will be no "bread and butter" there either, but at least they will be able to sleep more peacefully, wash themselves properly and get rid of the lice. After six weeks, back to the front line. "Perhaps then I will be lucky and my leave will begin, and when I return from leave, that is, after your wedding, it will already be warm and bright; then the great offensives will begin and the war – thus shall God's grace ordain – will end. And what about your wedding? Have you already decided to go to Erika?"

It is hard to establish when the soldier Bruno received the letter written by his brother Gustav on 28 January 1943 (probably around the middle of February). The most important news is the information about the state of Bruno's savings recorded in his passbook (*Spaarkassenbuch*). The deposits were regular and the interest was added. At the end of the year 1942 he has at his disposal the sum of 4,871 marks. Gustav also reports that he was summoned to the Education Office in Konin. 


[^14]: The Letter to the Romans, quotations added – M.S.
