The German settlers, bound strongly by language and religion, literally hewed out or dug out their place on this land in the forests near Zagórów and on the Warta meadows. When they departed for Prussia, most considered that they were going on a visit. Their homeland was this scrap of earth into which their sweat and tears had soaked.

The German Reich, however, consistently conducts aggressive agitation not only through the state administration. Strong pressure is exerted on the clergy of the Protestant Church. This is the implementation of the idea of *Mitteleurope*, built for half a century – the subordination, or indeed the subjugation, of Central-Eastern Europe by Germany, the germanization and reduction to the role of slaves of the Slavs and of other nations regarded as lower in cultural and civilizational development. [^2]: Bundowcy – members of the General Jewish Labour Union in Poland, Russia, Lithuania (Bund – the party operated in the years 1897-1948). They regarded the Jews as a separate nation; they strove to create secular schooling and to develop culture in the Yiddish language, which was to strengthen their autonomy. They took part in various formations of the international movement of socialists and social democrats. They criticized the Bolshevik government of the USSR and at the same time urged cooperation with it. In interwar Poland they were a significant and well-organized party. They were opponents of the Zionists and of the religious Agudas Israel movement. Representatives of both these currents were present in Zagórów.

    Hakata – the Association of the Eastern Marches (1894-1934), operating under Bismarck's patronage, regarded as its main goal the Germanization of the Polish lands under the Prussian partition. The Hakatists had the means and a programme for driving Poles out of their land by every method. They strove to eliminate the Polish press and publishing houses, to buy up land from Poles, to bring Germans to Wielkopolska, to develop German schooling.
