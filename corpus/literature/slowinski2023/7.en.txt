## Introduction

In October 2020 there came into my hands a very well-preserved collection of letters by a Wehrmacht soldier, discovered in the wall during the renovation of the former house of the cantor Gustav Mielke at Łazińsk. The successive letters, as they were translated, proved to be increasingly fascinating reading – a source of precious information, conveyed from the position of a private soldier, about little-known events and fighting on the northern wing of the Eastern Front and about the everyday life of the soldier's family in the Kraj Warty. This brief introduction should help to situate this extraordinary story in place and time.

◼

In the year 1718, fourteen German families arrive from Silesia in eastern Wielkopolska, in the vicinity of the town of Zagórów situated on the southern slope of the Warta river valley; the lord of the manor of nearby Trąbczyn leases them in perpetuity forest lands and fallows, where in time a village comes into being by the name of Łazińsk. Thus begins the history of the German settlers on this small land. Apparently the first experiences proved positive, for in 1746 the owners of the estates in Trąbczyn, Katarzyna and Paweł Prusińscy, designate places for further settlements. The newcomers take possession of the leased land under Olęder law (prawo olęderskie).
