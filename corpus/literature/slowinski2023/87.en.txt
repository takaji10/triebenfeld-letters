 And all those who die faithful to their leader are to be counted as martyrs. This is only a very brief reminder of what the idea of Germania was.

We return to Bruno's letter. He fears whether his sister has taken over from her husband the faith in the new religion. In this "religion" even the concept of the soul was distorted in such a way as to serve as another element of propaganda[^26]. The soul in the thinking of national Christianity was to strive to fulfill the divine plan, in which Germany – the nation chosen by God – was to heal and make the world happy. This deep disquiet at the behavior of his sister, who had not been seen in church for a long time, is also a clear, though not directly expressed declaration. His sister faces eternal damnation if she does not turn back in time. Bruno Mielke rejects the new religion with all the might of a faithful Christian. He considers it a road to the perdition of man. A betrayal of God. If in the series of letters available to us he always renews his declaration of fidelity and faith in the Führer, of readiness to serve the nation and of the greatest sacrifices, then in each of these letters there will also be found many, sometimes very emotional, assurances of submission to God. He writes: "We gladly wish to do everything for the Führer and the fatherland, but to become a traitor and a Judas to ourselves and our conscience, and to the Lord of all lords – that is something no one may demand, and which a reasonable man (certainly also the Führer) will not demand. If they (those overzealous improvers of the world) do not want us as we are, do not want to have need of us, then let them 'release' us. It is better to follow this simple, ordinary life than to remain with a broken conscience and without moral support in this bloody – serious period"!
