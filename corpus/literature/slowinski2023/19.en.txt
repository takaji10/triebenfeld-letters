# Słowiński (2023), p. 19
# translation by the editor's files

The German settlers located under Olęder law in the villages and forest settlements surrounding the town of Zagórów constituted a closed community which, despite two hundred years of settlement on these lands, was not susceptible to assimilation. A strong and probably decisive binding agent was the Lutheran religion and the German language. All were free people, whose relations with the landowners, who leased out for use grounds of meagre quality, were regulated by separate laws, customs and self-government. Such was the case in the eighteenth-century Rzeczpospolita, under the partitions and in the reborn Poland. As one of the inhabitants of those days in Zagórów recalls – Professor of the Poznań university Lech Słowiński: "In many villages right up to the year 1939 one heard only the German language. It must nevertheless be admitted that until that time the coexistence between the Polish and the German population was on the whole correct"[^5].

The largest settlement in this ring of homesteads was Łazińsk – situated eight kilometres to the south of Zagórów. To this day it is a place that perfectly illustrates the manner of organizing, under Olęder law, the space entrusted to the settlers: the location of the farmsteads, the building of roads and of drainage 


[^5]: Lech Słowiński, *Mniej i bardziej ciekawe przypadki mojego życia. (Wspomnienia)* [Less and More Interesting Episodes of My Life. (Memoirs)]. Poznań 2000 (typescript).

    Lech Słowiński – born in Zagórów on 23. 09. 1922 – died on 29.07.2005 in Poznań. Professor of Adam Mickiewicz University in Poznań. I publish the memoirs thanks to the kind consent of the closest Family. The typescript is kept in a home archive.
