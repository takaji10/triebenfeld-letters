#### Under Prussian partition rule

Several further new villages were founded in the territory of the present-day Zagórów commune as part of the so-called Prussian colonization, developed especially in Greater Poland after its incorporation into Prussia following the Second Partition of Poland and its inclusion in the new province named South Prussia[^27].

This colonization was initiated in 1796, after the takeover, by decision of the Prussian king Fryderyk Wilhelm II, of the church estates and the expropriation of the leaseholders of the royal estates known as starosties. The confiscation covered the church estates, mainly the monastic ones, as well as the estates of the starosties whose leaseholders had taken part in the Kościuszko Uprising of 1794. In return for the lost landed estates, the Prussian state undertook to pay the monasteries annually 50 per cent of the lost income from the confiscated landed estates, and the leaseholders 25 per cent of their lost income. The aim of the colonization was the Germanization of the Polish lands and the raising of the income of the Prussian state. In November 1806, with the outbreak of the Franco-Prussian war, the colonization campaign in South Prussia came to an end.

Around Zagórów the estates of the Cistercian monastery in Ląd were subject to confiscation; these comprised among others the villages of Świątniki, Wardężyn, Sługocin, Sługocinek, Sławsk, Drzewce, Oleśnica, Wrąbczyn, Skokum, Kopojno, Koszuty, Wola Opacka, Ratyń, Myślibórz, Dolany, Jaroszyn, Rzgów, Świątniki, Szetlew, Grabienice, Kowalewo, Ląd Kolonia, Królików, and also the towns of Lądek and Zagórów. Most of these localities came into being thanks to a settlement campaign lasting hundreds of years, conducted by the Cistercians of Ląd. In addition, the monastery owned extensive forests and wastelands. In 1796 the new owner of these estates became the commander of the Prussian army, Prince Fryderyk Ludwik Hohenlohe Ingelfingen.
