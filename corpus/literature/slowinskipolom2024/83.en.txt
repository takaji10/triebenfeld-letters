Pastor L. Sachs in Turek stood at the head of the Citizens' Committee and engaged in the rebuilding of Polish schooling. In Koło, Konin and Zagórów, by contrast, clergy from the Reich were installed, who did not know the Polish language. This occasionally led to conflicts in the parishes, e.g. in the mid-1920s in Konin, where Pastor Adolf Löffler arbitrarily abolished the Polish services and church functions; the case of the funeral of the Konin mayor and bookseller Ernst Paweł Michl, whose wish was a burial in Polish, resounded far and wide. Pastor A. Löffler refused his consent; the burial was finally performed by a pastor of the Reformed Parish from Żychlin. The outcome of the conflict was the transfer of Pastor A. Löffler to the parish in Chodeń, and his post was taken by the conciliatory Rev. Robert Badke. Conflicts were also waged on the diocesan plane – after the adoption of the new church act, in 1936 elections of the senior of the Kalisz diocese took place. Pastor Adolf Ulbrich of Grodziec, elected by the votes of the German majority, was rejected by the state authorities, whereupon a commissarial senior was established in the person of the hitherto superintendent, Pastor Edward Wende. In the parish in Grodziec in 1937, out of more than eight thousand members of the parish, two persons declared Polish nationality. In many places German political associations were organized, e.g. the Jungdeutsche Partei in Polen or the Deutsche Volksverband in Polen, and the German press, shaping social moods in the German national-socialist spirit, was distributed.

Among the evangelicals declaring themselves as Poles one can find many important persons, meritorious for the work of self-government and for politics, officers of the Polish Army, people of education and culture.
