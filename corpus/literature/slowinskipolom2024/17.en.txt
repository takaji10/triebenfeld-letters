All the streets are laid with a road surface; there is erected a representative fire-station building with an auditorium; the Cooperative Bank puts up the municipal slaughterhouse and the People's House (1931); the town contributes to the upkeep of the bathhouse; the community of Evangelicals first builds the cantorate house (1913–1917), and in the year 1938 completes the construction of a spacious rectory for the pastor. A municipal power plant comes into being. The town is a living organism and, on the scale of the region, one of the centres of trade. Admiration is aroused by "the effort of modernizing the town undertaken by the inhabitants. Institutions and buildings serving all citizens they built themselves, and they managed their affairs so that they were able to maintain them"[^8]. Yet something disquieting is happening.

Dangerous cracks appear in the social fabric of the town and the region. The undoubted success of surviving the hardest years of the crisis – which came about thanks to the collective effort of the inhabitants – instead of integrating the community of Poles, Jews and Germans more strongly and more deeply, reveals divisions and tensions unknown or deeply hidden. In the years 1935–1937 a wave of anti-Jewish riots passes through Poland. It is the effect of extreme nationalist propaganda spreading across the country, of the actions of activists of the National Party, and of the import of Nazi ideology. There have survived photographs of the march of a compact column of Zagórów nationalists carrying high a banner reading "Zagórów free of Jews". On 5 February 1936 these tensions came to a climax. A crowd of assailants, stirred up by some squire from Wyszyna, Wawrzyniec Silski, burst into the market square, attacking Jews and destroying their shops and property. The scale of the riots was such (some 2,000 individuals were counted) that the police, in defence of those attacked, had to use live ammunition. Three persons died and a dozen or so were wounded. Two days later, in Szetlewek, during an attempt to arrest the participants of the Zagórów assault, three more ruffians perished, and there were also many wounded. The court sentenced 38 participants to terms of imprisonment.
