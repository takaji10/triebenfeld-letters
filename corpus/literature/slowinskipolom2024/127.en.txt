Cruel fate, in effect, shaped her itself. In the area, among the older members of the community, she was remembered as a woman who could arrange anything, obtain anything — an efficient organizer and trader. At last it is time to turn to the next of the siblings, the peer of Polish independence, the person whom I had the pleasure of knowing and thanks to whom the history of the Beutler family can be told in this book, for the information contained here comes mainly from her and from her daughter Lili Vogt. Gertruda Beutler was born, like almost all of Adolf Beutler's children, in Dąbrowica, on 26.12.1918. Looking at one of the earliest photographs, in which Gertruda stands on a chair beside her elder sister Ella, on the occasion of Ella's confirmation in 1922, it is hard not to conclude that this is a joyful child, with lively eyes full of vigour and warmth. It is surely the Gertruda I had the occasion to know. Her exuberance, her will to live and to fight radiated already from that childish figure, as from the very mature woman I came to know. Gertruda grew up in Dąbrowica and, like most children from Evangelical families, was taught respect for work. She performed various tasks connected with work on the farm. As the last and youngest of the daughters still remaining at home, it was decided to educate her all the more carefully. At first she was sent to school in Barłogi, then in Wrąbczynkowskie Holendry — for the duration of her schooling she lived with her grandfather Christian Münch — where the teacher was Julius Bauer from Łódź, who paid particular attention to the teaching of penmanship, the so-called Gothic script. From her mother she learned embroidery, sewing and good manners — as a girl from a respectable house she was to be a good match among the local youth. As a young woman, at a hunting party, she came to know Reinhard Schwemmer from Zarzewek, four years her senior, a young miller.
