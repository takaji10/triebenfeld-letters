### Out of oblivion I call to you… A short history of Ida Disterheft


Damian Kruczkowski

In the order of the world that it fell to all of us to come to know, as the approved and acceptable one, it is usually the case that every story has its beginning, development and end. We tell each other and pass on the stories of people and events that begin at some point on the timeline and end in another place, more or less distant. The story I am telling disrupts this accustomed order, but the disruption is the result of earlier catastrophic and cruel events, from the not so distant past, namely from the 1930s. My story begins at the end. From an end far more definitive than death – from oblivion after death. The theory of small miracles is a thing about the attraction of people, events, situations, but usually it is we ourselves who bring about that attraction. Yet it may happen that precisely out of oblivion, out of the depths of history, we will “hear” a call for remembrance, for being remembered, and in part – for being readmitted into the circle of life. I believe that such a situation occurred in the case I describe here.

Several years ago, as I was wandering the paths of the Municipal Cemetery in Konin, I approached the ossuary, that is, the large tomb into which are placed the exhumed remains of persons whose graves have not been paid for for another twenty years. On the outer walls of the tomb, faced in black granite, plaques are placed with the first name, surname and year of death of the persons interred within. I ran my eyes over all the plaques, and suddenly my attention was caught by a surname that was familiar to me. For on that one plaque there was the inscription: Ida Disterheft † 1988.
