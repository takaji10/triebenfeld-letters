When the Third Reich, in June 1941, attacked Stalin's Russia – its recent ally in the tearing of Poland apart – the Herman son received his call-up to the Wehrmacht and quickly found himself on the eastern front. His parents seemed proud of that fact, and often the father of the soldier, on a Sunday afternoon, would inform Grandpa Tomasz of his son's part in the victorious conquests. Until the time. One day a deputation of local notables strode into the yard. They brought the notification of the heroic death of the son fighting for the Führer and for a Greater Germany. Whole nights long there reached them from behind the wall the unending wailing and the loud weeping of the mother.

At the beginning of the nineteen-fifties the Office of Public Security controlled and intruded into all the areas of the social and economic life of our country, rebuilding itself from the wartime destruction. From the year 1949, after completing his military service, the Gminna Cooperative in Zagórów was directed by my father Zdzisław Słowiński. The style of governing of the authorities of those days was such that every undertaking was proclaimed an extraordinarily important campaign. And the propaganda of those days saw enemies and diversionists everywhere. One of the more important campaigns was the harvest. For the authorities it was an extraordinary event; for the majority of the people living on the land it was a natural duty of providing bread for themselves and for the country for the coming year. It had been so from time immemorial, and that is what my father thought – the son of a hard peasant. During the next harvest, officers from the county command of the UB entered my father's office with the information that there was a decision to designate the former German church in Zagórów as a grain warehouse. My dad never used strong or grand words. He calmly answered that there was no such need, because the warehouses for the reception of the grain were prepared. Unfortunately, this answer, instead of calming the envoys from Konin, positively irritated them. They declared that the decision existed and that the chairman was answerable for its execution. My father firmly refused. He asked one short question:

– Do you want to do the same thing the Hitlerites did with the Catholic church, turning it into a warehouse?!
