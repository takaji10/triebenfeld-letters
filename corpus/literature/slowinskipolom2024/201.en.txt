 German. Probably someone had informed that the householders were helping partisans, hence this unexpected visit. The woman had a basket of victuals with her, the man was dressed in civilian clothes. They sat down with their companion, to the terror of the householders, calmly waiting for the hungry men to appear. Meanwhile the householder, old Kazimierski, called his then ten-year-old son Jan and instructed him: "Listen, Feluś, run and tell them [the partisans] not to come for dinner today, because there are Germans here". The quick-witted boy slipped out unnoticed and warned the fighters. So says one of the local stories, repeated when Mrs the Doctor's wife is being recalled. What only slightly astonishes me is the fact that only two people took part in the ambush on the armed soldiers of the underground, one of them a woman. Or perhaps there is something we do not know?

In any case, in 1945 the ground began to slip from under proud Breede's feet. And when the Soviet troops came to liberate Zagórów, the inhabitants of the little town knew perfectly well whom to point out to the Soviet soldiers as an enemy of the allied nation. Among these persons was also the hated Doctor's wife.

– Fleeing from the Red Army men, she hid in our yard… – relates Halina Cieślewiczowa on the basis of her mother-in-law's stories. – There she jumped into the well. It was January… And once those Russians had searched the whole house, the whole homestead, the cellars, the outbuildings and had gone away, only then did she begin to call for help. Someone hauled her out of the well, turning the crank, and she held onto that chain and stood in the bucket. When she came out she was all soaked and terrified. On the other side of the street was the bakery of the Matelskis; she fled there and undressed there. She climbed up onto the bread oven; Matelska gave her some blanket, in which Breede wrapped herself, and her clothes dried by the oven. She herself told me about that incident, of course already after the war.

Mrs Halina relates it all so vividly that with my mind's eye I already see that dramatic picture: a shivering, humiliated, terrified woman huddles under the blanket handed over by the baker's wife, swallowing tears – in a whisper she gives thanks for the rescue.
