# translation by the editor's files

War damage and the wrongs inflicted at the changing of armies shaped everyday life, and punitive actions – such as the artillery shelling of Kalisz by German units at the beginning of the war – contributed to a negative image of the German troops. In the nationally and denominationally homogeneous rural areas there were no outbursts of nationalist zeal. The advancing German army, the officials of the administration and the field clergy encountered a German-speaking population which, thanks to its way of life rooted in family and religiosity, seemed to match the image idealized by German propaganda, but it was a population "undefined" in its Germanness, beyond a sense of national identity shaped in the German Empire. The idea was therefore invoked of a special calling and of a God-given mission of the German nation, in order to set in motion processes of transforming consciousness and bring about a national awakening. Soon, despite the Reich's policy marked by many contradictions, the declared Polish question stood to a certain extent in the field of tensions concerning the German population. As a result of the changes at the front, the German population faced the problem of shaping relations in the newly created Polish State, at the expense of the German Empire. Most of the clergy of the Evangelical-Augsburg Church, by reason of their social position, were open to the Polish efforts aimed at state independence. The clergy nevertheless found themselves in a situation of great pressures: as a result of the changes among the German population – whose driving force was the group of so-called Łódź activists linked with the Hakata – arguments were put forward about the German-speaking character of the majority of the Church's members, and consequently about the necessity of profiling the Evangelical-Augsburg Church unambiguously in the German-national spirit. Moreover, the German administration filled vacant church posts with clergymen-administrators of German orientation, in order to give the Evangelical-Augsburg Church a structure corresponding to the general evangelical and German interests.
