# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 223
# translation by the editor's files

During the occupation my mother employed Irenka Nowicka to help her, and after the war she searched for us through the Red Cross, even in Geneva. She received word that the Kuhn family was not in the register. She worried greatly about our fate. She also searched for us through the municipality, but received no information. Nobody knew anything. We met many years after the war in Zagórów, thanks to the help of Pastor Andrzej Mendrok of Konin.

#### Mama

Mama was forty-nine when she died of typhus. Our youngest sister Frieda was four then. Mama was taken to the infectious diseases hospital, and we could no longer have any contact with her. We received only a letter saying that she was no longer alive. She was buried in Konin. That was 1949.

#### Papa

The Germans did not take Papa into the Wehrmacht, because he was the sole breadwinner of a large family. We children were nine, including seven brothers. It seems that the number of sons in a family mattered here. Father had a reputation as a good carpenter. He was respected, often helped others and got on well with his neighbours. Right after the Russians entered in 1945 they took father, like all the men from our village. They gathered them in Grabina, and then ordered them to walk on foot in the direction of Pyzdry. More than one of them died on the way. From there they took them all to Russia, to a labour camp. In cattle wagons. They were given nothing to eat or drink. Many did not survive that journey. Our father died in the camp probably in 1946. I do not remember the name of that place. We do not know where he is buried. From the moment the Russians took him we had no contact with him whatsoever. The news that he was no longer alive was passed on to us by the few who had the strength and the luck to return from the camp in Russia. They said that there was no trace of the camp any more. The Russians liquidated it after about two years, in 1947 or 1948. There they destroyed very many people…
