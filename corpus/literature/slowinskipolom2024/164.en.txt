# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 164
# translation by the editor's files

 one of the December letters written on the Eastern Front by Bruno Mielke ends with words of hope bound up with the swiftly approaching New Year. That it will be the year decisive for victory, that "from the hand of God the Father we shall receive everything that He wishes to give us – joy and suffering, happiness and unhappiness".

#### Hold up my steps in thy paths (Ps 17,5)

The first decade of January 1943 brings the letters of sister Anna to the brother at the front (7 January 1943) and a letter of Bruno (10 January 1943) to Anna, which is, however, not an answer. They must have crossed in the post. Anna thanks for the birthday chocolate sent from the front, which will be the main ornament of the table. Together with her sisters she prepared a parcel, into which they put socks knitted on needles between the Feast and the New Year; she made a head protector, Kadzia wrist protectors, and Fela knee pads. Anna's fiancé Hans added flash bulbs for the camera. And everyone rejoices that he can take pictures and cannot wait for them.

A completely different character and weight has the letter of the brother. Everyone lives with Anna's planned wedding. Bruno hopes that it will not end with this one wedding. Perhaps Gustav, perhaps Kadzia too will follow in her footsteps this year. He himself too greatly desires the moment when he will be able to present a sister-in-law to them. He sends a complete set of photographs with the request that Anna paste them into his album. Yet beyond the ritual assurances of remembering in prayer and the birthday wishes, Bruno believes that in the first days of this year so important for his sister he must share the gnawing anxiety he feels about the… spiritual safety of his sister. He reports that her fiancé Hans in his first letter to him writes clearly and openly that he "rejects the teaching from Scripture and wants to protect you from such things". And so "it is permitted to me to speak freely and openly to all people, even strangers, of the happiness of faith, only not to you, my dearest sister!".
