After many attempts and fittings, the decision was that the association received the name Wielkopolskie Stowarzyszenie na rzecz Ratowania Pamięci FRYDHOF. The word "frydhof" is simply a Polonised version of the German word *friedhof*, meaning cemetery, which can be found in the dialects of several regions of Poland, mainly Upper Silesia and Pomerania. Many older people in those areas to this day call a cemetery precisely that. This name defines the aim of our activities and alludes to the historical multiculturalism of the region and the country. The seat of the Stowarzyszenie FRYDHOF is Konin in the Wielkopolska voivodeship. The territory of the association's activity is the whole of Poland. We have already worked in Pomerania and Lower Silesia, and with lectures we have been to almost every part of the country.

The association is a very diverse group, in which various professions and milieus are represented. Above all, however, it brings together people engaged in the protection of monuments and cultural heritage, with particular attention to the areas of eastern Wielkopolska and the Evangelical heritage. In our association it is above all people for whom their little homelands, their local history, multiculturalism and its heritage, and especially the areas of the sacrum, including cemeteries, historic architecture and archival collections, are the object of particular care and of scholarly, research, artistic and social activity. The association is made up of people who do not accept the removal, from the pages of history, from local spaces or even from social discourse, of the traces of the presence of those who have long been gone and who therefore can no longer stand in defence of the heritage they have left to us, the living. As it happens, in the case of the Hauländer settlers this heritage and what they left behind is strikingly visible. But so as not to diminish the merits of other social or confessional groups, it should be said openly that without the work of generations of people who were before us, people of many nationalities and many confessions, many of those places in which today all of us – without exception – live would be unfit for habitation, and even more of those places simply would not exist today.
