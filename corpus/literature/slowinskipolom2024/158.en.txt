Our dear brothers may be certain that the community of faith and love for the good Lord bind us to one another, even if thousands of kilometres divide us".

The letters dated December carry the addition: Eastern Front. "Which of us soldiers from the wilderness of Russia would not want to be with his nearest and dearest at Christmas!". "You surely think – he writes on 6 December – that I shall spend the festive days in sadness and dejection. It is the will of God and my superiors who sent me to the front. To oppose this in any form would be to oppose the will of God and of the powers above". Bruno treats himself as an envoy "for the family in the east, and for it and for my people I wish to offer this small sacrifice and be at the front for Christmas". He honestly admits that he has landed well – this is a fairly quiet sector. The Russians have so far attacked very rarely – almost not at all. His service consists in standing 6-8 hours of sentry duty (in pairs), bringing food and fuel and sometimes other things. Apart from that he sleeps, and when the light is good he writes letters and reads belated newspapers. Therefore he asks his brother not to submit an application for his release. "Firstly, it will achieve nothing; secondly, in winter it is better at the front. In the rear training, drill etc. are conducted. There is none of that here. It is indeed dangerous – but it is calm, and I firmly believe that nothing will happen to me".

Mielke had a great deal of luck… Under Stalingrad from the end of August a mortal struggle had been raging, entering its decisive phase in December 1942. But of this, silence in the Goebbels propaganda. Bruno asks his brother to send him the Führer's address of 1 October and pages torn from other newspapers with the speeches of Goebbels, Göring and Ribbentrop. In the battle lasting five months one and a half million German soldiers and their allies perished. Of the 91 thousand prisoners taken in the Stalingrad cauldron, about six thousand returned from captivity. But what news could this soldier read from the "belated newspapers"?
