It was the final journey of the peer of independent Poland to her homeland. Whenever her daughter Lila, living in Berlin, visited Poland, she invariably had to bring her mother some Polish delicacies: as a rule żur, grated beets, Polish apples, a bit of genuine sausage. Those were the tastes of Gertruda Schwemmer's childhood. Today, all over the world, further generations of the Beutler clan are being born and growing up; despite many storms and misfortunes, the family endures still. Gertruda could well be the personification of the history of the whole clan. As the words of the song in the opening credits of one Polish television series say: "every family is like a tree — it sways, it breaks, time changes it" — but in spite of everything it endures. Gertruda, like the Beutler family across the centuries, experienced years of plenty but also years of leanness, many successes and joys, but also many misfortunes and cares, and yet in spite of all she endured, not surrendering and showing inexhaustible energy and an appetite for life. With her a piece of the history of the Hauländers of the Konin and Zagórów region has passed away.
