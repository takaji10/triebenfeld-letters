 where for months there is no night and no dusk. The sun sinks towards the horizon, remains there, to rise majestically again after midnight. All the time during the march you have the impression that you are travelling either in the evening hours or in the early morning. Only my watch told me whether it was evening or morning. Most of the days of the march were, thank God, fair and sunny, and so simply beautiful for walking. Our route led mostly through forest. In Finland there are chiefly forests, but small forests (bare firs, spruces, pines), with bogs and hillocks. One would gladly march if it were not so hard for someone who is not used to it. At the end I almost collapsed, rather my legs refused obedience: they ached, pricked and swelled. Therefore for the last 15 km I rode in a lorry with the other lame ones to the destination; that saved me, otherwise I would have harmed my legs. At the beginning, that is, during the first days of the march, there were no obstacles. Everyone, I too, then went his own way in the quiet night, and each wrestled with his own thoughts, his spirit was somewhere else. I was always with you, my dear ones. Enlivened, I would then picture your house in Łazińsk". In the margin he adds that it is good to know Russian; interpreters with the rank of officer have a much easier life…

He also reassures the family that they are well supplied. Recently they received a temporary borrowed postal code. "You may […] therefore also write to me. Since the code is borrowed, please do not forget to draw a circle (the size of 1 Pfennig) under the sender, without that the letter will not reach me. Address: Soldier Bruno Mielke, postal code: 39 801 Sender: Gustav Mielke. Łazińsk, post office Dromin (Trabczyn) via Konin Wartheland (Germany) and then the circle. Parcels may also be sent, unfortunately only up to 100 g. That is not much. Only what will keep a long time and is light counts"[^56].
