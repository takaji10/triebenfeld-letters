# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 199
# translation by the editor's files

 Romuald Brzeg[^74] of the arrest. Can we take this as a certainty? Did the Breede couple really know all along what fate the mayor Juliusz Diesterheft had in store for the participants of the "Barka" action? Or did they too deceive themselves to the end that the tortured arrestees would be taken away by the gendarmes to forced labour? We do not know that… We can only surmise. Yet if they really did know, more than one person could have envied them nerves of steel. For that is what would have been needed, to assure the families of the victims with a stone face that their loved ones were not threatened with execution.

During the successive days of the occupation, a frequent guest in the doctor couple's home was Walery Sławek, a doctor from Rzgów. It is said the Breedes secured him and his family safety from deportation – so, among other things, Mrs Wanda Jurkiewiczowa told me in a telephone conversation. And Dr Sławek additionally learned a great deal from his more experienced colleague. It is said that even before the war the couples of doctors knew each other and were friends. At the beginning of the war Henryk Pluciński and his mother also often stayed at the Breedes'. Not as guests, of course – the doctor employed them to work around the house. Franciszek Pluciński, Henryk's father, was one of the victims of the German revenge for the "Barka". The wife and son of the murdered man avoided deportation to the Protectorate only thanks to the intercession of the German doctor.

– Pluciński would not suffer a bad word about Dr Breede. About her he did not much feel like talking – says Andrzej Bernat, a well-known regionalist. My interlocutor repeats to me the story he heard from Henryk Pluciński about the causes of the doctor's premature death. It is said Breede with full awareness infected the wound on his leg, broken once again in an accident. He wanted to be unfit for military service and not to end up on the Eastern Front. He was convinced that this war was already lost for the Germans, because no one had yet defeated the Soviets on their own land. And the German army needed doctors.
