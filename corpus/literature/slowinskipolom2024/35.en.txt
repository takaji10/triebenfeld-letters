#### II. Evangelical-German Settlement in the 18th–19th Centuries

The spread and further development of Protestantism came about above all in the eighteenth century, when the settlers then arriving in great numbers, chiefly German, were in their overwhelming majority Lutherans. It appeared anew in the period of Hauländer settlement (by colonists of Dutch or Flemish origin, living under Hauländer law), whose beginnings in the vicinity of Zagórów reach back to the year 1746. It was then that the owner of Trąbczyn, Paweł z Kolna Prusimski, permitted the founding, in the forest, of the colony of Łazińsk, and also of Trąbczyńska Huta. After the second partition of Poland (1793), the Zagórów land fell to Prussia. After 1804 the following colonies came into being: Olchowo (Sophiental), Mały Las (Ludwigslust), Tarszewo (Friedlichsfeld), Michalinów Oleśnicki (Emilienheim), Zalesie, Augustynów (Augustinhof), Anielewo (Ingelfingen), Chruściki (Adelhof).

In that period settlers came wandering to these lands from Lower Silesia, from the valley joining the Warta, the Odra and the Noteć. In their overwhelming majority they were Protestants. The lands on which they founded their settlements were wasteland – either they had to clear the forests and establish villages, or they settled in the Warta valley, where flood waters visited them several times a year. These settlers came in the hope of bettering their hitherto lot, and they stayed on despite the harsh conditions offered by the local realities.
