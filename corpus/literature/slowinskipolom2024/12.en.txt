Requisitions of grain and foodstuffs, and, as war misery grows – the plundering of craftsmen's workshops, the tearing of copper sheeting from the roofs of churches and the requisition of church bells to be melted down for the needs of the army complete the picture.

The authorities do not yet segregate citizens according to nationality, race or religion. But the time for that, too, will come after the victory. Their view seems clearly to be expressed by the declaration of the chief quartermaster of the Tenth Army. "For the Supreme Commander the life of one German means more […] than the entire population of the occupied country. Colonel Thugott von Sauberzweig". This is a strong echo of an old political conception. The idea of Mitteleuropa had been developed in Germany for over half a century. It assumed a "march to the east", the conquest of the territories of Central Europe and their economic exploitation, while the Slavs and other nations inhabiting them, as lower in development, were to be subjected to Germanization and driven to slave labour. Such was the essential aim, on the eastern axis, sketched during the First World War by the German Empire. In the next war the thought of the Supreme Commander proclaimed by Col. von Sauberzweig will be developed – if one may put it that way – and will be realized on a mass scale with extraordinary brutality. But life must go on. The town councillors made good use of the front-line crises of the German and Austrian armies and, availing themselves of the declarations made by high politicians and of the proclaimed manifestos on the reconstruction of the Kingdom of Poland, they attended to their own affairs.

In this way, in 1916, Zagórów regains its town rights. The Zagórów scouts and members of the Polish Military Organization were preparing for the fight. In the year 1918, when it was already clear that the war was lost, the German plundering and removal of goods from the still occupied territories took place on a great scale.
