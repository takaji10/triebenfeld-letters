# translation by the editor's files

 chairman of the Circle of Reserve Officers; Stefania Esse née Petschke, owner of the largest tenement house in the town, teacher, member of the Polish Military Organisation, who in 1921 welcomed Marshal Józef Piłsudski in Konin on behalf of the inhabitants; the sons of the Konin entrepreneur Daniel Sztark: Witold Sztark became a colonel of the Polish Army, and the son Heliodor Sztark – a diplomat; Oskar Bartel, historian, pedagogue, professor of the Christian Theological Academy; Ignacy Boerner, engaged in the activity of the Polish Socialist Party, who fought in the Legions and became an officer of the Polish Army, later the minister of post and telegraphs; Juliusz Ulrych, legionnaire and Piłsudski man, who in 1918, as head of the POW, took over power in Kalisz from the hands of the Germans and organized the 29th Infantry Regiment of the Kalisz Land, contributed to the founding of the Academy of Physical Education in Warsaw, in 1925 became minister of communications, and after the war in England was, among other things, president of the Józef Piłsudski Institute; his brother Aleksander Emil Ulrych, born in Turek, was a merchant in Kalisz, a member of the city council and a deputy to the Sejm; during the occupation, for refusing to sign the Volksliste, he was dispossessed and sent to forced labor in Wrocław, and for his activity in the Home Army he was arrested at the end of 1944 and placed in the concentration camp in Buchenwald, where he died of exhaustion shortly after the liberation; those meritorious in Kłodawa: Edward Woltersdorf, an activist in the POW, in the Polish Army and in the Fire Brigade; his brother Aleksander Woltersdorf, also active in the POW and in the Polish Army; and, from another branch of the family, Karol Woltersdorf, a member of the underground scouting movement in Kłodawa and later in Warsaw; the sisters Danuta Gerke, born in Słupca, and Monika, who, after the death of their parents – Konin merchants – were raised by relatives; both entered the Polish deaconess community Eben-Ezer in Dzięgielów, and after the Second World War Sister Danuta for nearly 40 years was the mother superior of the deaconate, struggling with the adversities of the PRL's measures. Another branch of the Gerke family moved in the interwar period to Warsaw, where its members during the Second World War were actively engaged in the underground: secret teaching and aid to the Jews
