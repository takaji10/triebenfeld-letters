In the year 1796 the Prussian prince Friedrich Ludwig Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen took over as his property the Zagórów estates hitherto belonging to the Cistercian monastery. In the year 1797 the Prussian King Friedrich Wilhelm II issued a Statute for the Jews of South Prussia and New East Prussia (the lands annexed after the partitions), which regulated the legal situation in those provinces. "From that time German colonists began to flow into the vicinity of Zagórów in great numbers. They founded their settlements mostly on former forest lands, on the infertile sands and wetlands of the Warta valley, unwillingly settled by the Polish population. In this way, with the passage of years, Zagórów was surrounded by a ring of numerous villages and settlements almost entirely German, such as: Olchowo, Tarszewo, Chróściki, Anielewo, Michalinów, Augustynów, Wrąbczynkowskie Holendry and others"[^3]. Schools (Kantoraty), houses of prayer and cemeteries came into being. On the territory of today's gmina of Zagórów, in 25 localities (out of a total of 52), Germans of the Evangelical faith lived for over 200 years as the dominant group. They formed the Zagórów Lutheran community, which came into being as a filial congregation in 1843 and became an independent parish in the year 1856. According to the research of the already-mentioned Zbigniew Chodyła, in the whole of Wielkopolska in the years 1721–1793/1795, 637 settlements were founded, of which 228 in the Kalisz voivodeship, to which Zagórów belonged[^4].

After the Congress of Vienna in 1815 Zagórów and its surroundings found themselves within the Kingdom of Poland subject to the Tsar of Russia. The town strives to attract craftsmen and merchants who could raise the town economically. These are, among others, the times of the building of numerous windmills.
