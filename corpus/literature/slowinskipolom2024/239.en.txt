The density of traffic diminished quickly because of the lack of travellers going only as far as West Berlin, and the road conditions deteriorated so badly that shortly before the Polish border part of the exhaust pipe came loose, and in a rattling car we reached our first destination. The border formalities were quickly settled, and the rest of the drive passed without major problems. After about nine hours of driving and 540 km covered, we reached our goal. Until the Tuesday after Pentecost we stayed at the "Bazar" hotel in the centre of Poznań. The day of our arrival was a lovely spring day, and in the afternoon we delighted in the beautiful old town of Poznań. But the damaged exhaust pipe gave us no peace. We dealt with this problem quickly as well. The kind receptionist from the hotel activated his acquaintances, and towards evening a young woman appeared at the hotel who, riding with us in our car, took us to an estate of single-family houses on the outskirts. There, in one of the houses, a perfectly equipped car workshop was to be found in the garage. All that was missing was the right exhaust pipe. But even that was no problem. After all, what are neighbours for! Before long a matching part was fetched and welded on. In the meantime we were entertained in the house by the owner, who in return received tights; for the repair, Western currency was gladly accepted, and we too were pleased to have a fully functioning means of transport again. For the first time in my life, after all these impressions, I drank Polish beer. It was good, but the next day beer could be bought neither in the town centre nor at the hotel. Today that is unimaginable.

On Sunday morning, on the day of Pentecost, full of tension we set out for our actual destination[^80]. We drove along the outbound road in the direction of Warsaw, and then along a two-lane road to Słupca (Grenzhausen).
