#### While I live will I praise the Lord (Ps 146,2)

The first of the surviving letters from the time of service in the Wehrmacht was written in Vienna on 30 March 1942. Called up to the army, he sets off with the other recruits on a long journey. First Chodzież, then Kutno, Poznań and on to Wrocław. From there, after eight hours, "we arrived safely in Vienna – the city of my dreams. […] Our last destination, where we shall be trained, is Klagenfurt, about 15 km from the border with Italy, in the south of Austria. It will surely be beautiful there in the Alps, it was already so during the journey. Service in the mountains can be exhausting, for we have been assigned to a unit of *Gebirgsjäger* [mountain rangers]. Everything was decided, there was no question of any choice; besides, every thing and every group has its good and bad sides. It is best to accept what has been given, then one need have no reproaches later". In the margin we read an addition: "In the end we arrived. Everything is very beautiful and proper. I feel as if I were in the dormitory of some seminary. The officers are kind. We are given everything". He need not have taken so many things. And further: "And now my comrades. Most of them are ethnic Germans (without citizenship) and *Bessaraber*, some are also Germans with citizenship. Most are weak, some quite incapable, and among them there are persons who exploit others. If they manage, then I surely will. That there are persons among them who exploit others is not consoling, for, as is known, the whole group suffers because of it".

Gustav wrote a long letter to his brother in training on 1 May. "During all the work one thought now wearies me – it appears since you have been far away. The will to work that I had when you were still here I no longer have. During the lessons you appear every moment in my thoughts. And when the break comes and I look through the window – then too I see you. You were always near, and now you are suddenly so far away –
