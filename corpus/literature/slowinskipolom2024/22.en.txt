He had gathered together enough "assets" and was sufficiently crafty to flee in good time and bury himself somewhere deep in Germany. He lived to a great age, and his family and descendants lived, and perhaps still live, in the conviction that he was not fleeing from the Poles, "whom he helped", but from the terrible Russians… From the spring of 1940 an open ghetto is organized in the town, whose principal seat was the People's House. Over the course of more than a year, 2,500 to 3,000 Jews were brought in. Some of the healthy and young men were sent to forced labour. The remaining group, of more than 1,500 persons, from September to 15 November 1941 was taken away to the forests near Kazimierz Biskupi and brutally murdered. Some were poisoned with exhaust fumes in trucks; others were thrown alive into pits with quicklime. This was a crime committed by the Konin branch of the Gestapo[^10]. The war in Zagórów showed its worst face. Among the numerous documents from the life of the German Evangelicals in the wartime years, which it proved possible to collect, only in one, a life story written by hand, does Gisela Schulz mention that she was a witness of how the Jews lined up for transport were brutally driven away. Apart from that, silence and the stillness of the dead.


In January 1945 the Russians enter the territories of the Zagórów land. Revenge has various faces, and this is a further act of the drama. The majority of the settlers are expelled. Their place is taken by the Polish farmers earlier thrown out, or by other families settled there. After months of uncertainty comes the decision to deport all of them to Germany. The winter removal of whole villages begins – most often of women, children and the old, for the men are being taken away to labour in Russia. Only a few manage to return. Heart-rending is the account of the tragedy of the families of Gerard Beutler and Leo Byczyński. In accordance with the order of the authorities, they were leaving with their wives and small children. In the village of Małe Cieśle, on 25 January 1946, they were attacked by unknown men, bandits.
