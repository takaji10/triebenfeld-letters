The prince energetically set about bringing colonists from Germany (often from areas lying far from Poland) in order to settle the forests and wastelands taken from the Cistercians. In 1805, 18 families were brought in, who in turn assigned smaller forest areas to further settlers, also hired labourers. The initiative to recruit further settlers and to settle them near the larger colonies was left to colonists possessing considerable areas of land. For the most part these were forest lands and meadows situated on the Warta. In total, during the period of South Prussia, 8 new settlements were founded within the boundaries of the present-day Zagórów commune: Kolonia Klein Ingelfingen (today Anielewo), Emilienheim (today Michalinów Oleśnicki), Kolonia Sofiethal (today Olchowo), Kolonia Ludwigslust (today Mały Las), Kolonia Frydrychsfeld (today Tarszewo), Kolonia Adelshof (today Chruściki), Adolfsberg (today Zalesie) and Augustinenhof (today Augustynów).

In addition, the villages of Schwentz (Święcia) and the colony of Wrąbczyn were founded. From the contract of perpetual lease of land concluded with the settlers it followed that they were personally free and were obliged only to very limited duties towards the manor and to certain payments in kind. After the expiry of four years free of obligations, they were also obliged to pay rent in two instalments, at Christmas and on St John the Baptist's day (24 May) – in the amount of 30 thalers a year from each cleared and cultivated Magdeburg łan. For the schoolteacher, the village headman (sołtys) and for the cemetery, 21 morgi were assigned. The colonists came mostly from the Margraviate of Brandenburg and from Pomerania. Not all the settlers remained in the settlements mentioned. In 1806 two families from Mały Las asked to be allowed to leave it and move to East Prussia, and in 1818 forty families from the village of Świecia made the same request.
