The existing stereotype – that the evangelical inhabitants of the towns became Polonized, while the inhabitants of the villages remained invariably attached to the linguistic traditions of the earlier generations – is not necessarily confirmed. Services in Polish were held from the end of the nineteenth century in the parishes in Kalisz, in Koło – where until 1915 the parish priest was Pastor Ryszard Paszko, though in the parish he administered in Babiak and in the rural cantorates there were no such services. Once a month and on feast days, services in Polish were held in Konin, conducted by Pastor Karl Henkl, who was at the same time a teacher of German at the local lyceum – reportedly called by his pupils by the catchy name of *der*, *die*, *das*. Polish services took place in Turek, where the parish priest was Pastor Leon Sachs, related by marriage to the family of Bishop Juliusz Bursche. In the parish archive there are preserved letters from the consistory of the Church, calling for the holding of services or for the ringing of bells on patriotic and commemorative occasions: e.g. the funeral of Marshal Józef Piłsudski, the 3 May holiday, or the anniversaries of independence. Services for Poles also took place in the parishes in Koźminek, Prażuchy and Sobiesęki, and branch services in Przystajń, in Dąbie, Zduńska Wola and Sieradz, as well as in the parishes of Chodeń and Przedecz, lying already in Kujawy. A German character at that time marked the well-developed denominational elementary schooling, with nearly 200 educational establishments, often combined with the parish rural cantorates. The development of Polish schooling influenced, slowly but distinctly, the bilingualism among the evangelical youth.

During the German occupation in the First World War, part of the clergy joined the current of social causes, for which repressions befell them. Pastor R. Paszko of Koło – a member of the POW – was arrested and interned in the prison in Celle near Hanover. Pastor K. Henkel of Konin died during the entry of the German troops into Konin in 1915.
