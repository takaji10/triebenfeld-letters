His sister Gertruda, eleven years older, very often looked after him, as the only daughter of the family still then living at home — the more so since Emilia Beutler née Münch, their mother, as an independent seamstress and besides that a housekeeper, was often busy with sewing, with the workers she employed, or with contacts with her clients. And so in his childhood Edmund thought that it was precisely Gertruda who was his mamma.

Warm and affectionate relations between the two lasted throughout Edmund's life; he died in Germany, in Mondorf, in 2013. In January 1945 it was not granted to the elder Beutlers, Adolf and Emilia, either to remain on the farm they had built with toil and sweat. Like the majority of citizens recognized by the German occupiers as Germans, and after the war once more recognized as such by the new people's authorities, they were dispossessed and evicted. Adolf was arrested for signing the Volksliste (the German Nationality List) and imprisoned in Warsaw, where he remained for three years. During that time his wife Emilia wandered from relative to relative; at first she lived with her daughter-in-law, Erna née Byczyńska Beutler, where eight persons had to fit into a single room. In 1948 Adolf was released from prison. Until 1958 they lived in Dąbrówka Mała in Lubusz Voivodeship, dwelling in a former manor house that had been converted into flats for the office workers of the PGR (state farm) created on the site of the former estate. In 1957 Adolf and Emilia still celebrated their golden wedding anniversary in Poland, and as early as the following year, together with their youngest son Edmund and his freshly wedded wife Selma, they left for Germany, settling in Burgdorf in Niedersachsen, where Adolf died in 1962, and Emilia ten years later, in 1972.

After twenty-five years spent in Germany, Gertruda all the while gladly returned to Poland. Here she visited her son's grave in Zarzewek, her husband's and her sister Natalia's at the Evangelical cemetery in Konin.
