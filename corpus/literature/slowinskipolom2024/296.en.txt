| 1793-1807 | After the second partition of Poland, the Kalisz Land – in which Zagórów lies – passes to Prussia and belongs until the year 1807 to the department of South Prussia. 5 estates in the Zagórów region (Trombczyn, Nowa-Wieś, Oswiery, Łazy and Szetlewek) are confiscated by Prussia and given to the prince zu Hohenlohe-Oehringen (Ingelfingen). He sold half to Karl Gottlieb Weigel, a landowner from Hennersdorf in Silesia and lord of Kopojno; the rest was designated for settlement. |
| 1807-1815 | Under Napoleon, Zagorow belongs to the Duchy of Warsaw. Famine caused by drought in the years 1808, 1811 and 1812.<br>In the times of Napoleon, Zagórów belongs to the Duchy of Warsaw. Famine caused by drought in the years 1808, 1811 and 1812. |
| 1815 | After the Congress of Vienna: The Duchy of Warsaw is divided; the western part goes to Prussia as the Province of Posen, the eastern part with the Kreis of Zagorow is attached to the Russian Empire as Congress Poland.<br>After the Congress of Vienna: The Duchy of Warsaw is divided; Prussia receives the western part as the Poznań Province, while the eastern part with the Zagórów county is granted to the Russian Empire as the Congress Kingdom. |
| 1842 | Founding of the first Evangelical filial church in Zagorow<br>Founding of the first Evangelical filial parish in Zagórów. |
| 1846 | Michelina Miaczynska is now the owner of the Trabczyn estate.<br>Michelina Miaczynska is now the owner of the Trąbczyn estate. |
| 1856 | From Christmas, 24 December, Zagorow is an independent parish with its own pastor, Pastor Wilhelm Winkler. The church language is German.<br>From Christmas, 24 December, Zagórów becomes an independent parish with its own incumbent, Pastor Wilhelm Winkler. The language in the church is German. |
| 1863 | The Polish Count A. Taczanowski acquires, after the Polish uprising, the estates around Zagorow.<br>The Polish Count A. Taczanowski acquires, after the (January) uprising, the estates around Zagórów.<br>Emancipation of the peasants and administrative reform. Estate districts become rural communes with a Vogt (Wojt); hereditary leaseholders can buy their land and freely dispose of real property. |
