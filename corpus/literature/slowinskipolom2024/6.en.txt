# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 6
# translation by the editor's files

In the years 1735–1760 the abbots issue successive decrees intended to restore the functioning of elementary rights. Unfortunately, without greater effect. Order is finally introduced by Konstanty Iłowiecki, who on 25.03.1760 issues a decree annulling all seizures, exchanges and sales of land made after 1750. And he firmly enforces the new law. March 1768 goes down in history on account of a great fire in which 53 houses, the town hall and numerous craftsmen's workshops burned down. The successive actions of the owners and town councillors, of the royal Commission of Good Order, lead to the rebuilding of the town. The governors of the town and its owners were surely aware of the losses that successive epidemics brought upon the male part of the community. There simply had to begin to be a shortage of hands to work, of labour.

Perhaps it is precisely this many years' work on restoring the rule of law in the town, but also some kind of agitation effectively disseminated, together with the economic processes taking place beyond the western border, that cause new arrivals to flow into Zagórów and its surroundings from the second half of the eighteenth century onwards. These are German settlers. All of them are Protestants – members of the Evangelical-Augsburg Church. After the year 1750 there also appear reports that Jews are reaching the town. Earlier already, in the year 1718, 14 German families arrive from Silesia, to whom the heir of Trąbczyn leases forest lands and fallow grounds in perpetuity. In time a village called Łazińsk arises there. The town grapples with its own problems, and the surrounding landed proprietors must think about who will till their difficult land. The owners of the estate in Trąbczyn, Paweł and Katarzyna Prusimscy, in 1746 designate places for the first two settlements. These were Trąbczyńskie Luźne Stare Olędry and Trąbczyńskie Luźne Olędry (today Łazińsk Pierwszy and Łazińsk Drugi). The settlers were German peasants of the Lutheran faith.
