### Hauländer settlement and Prussian colonization in the vicinity of Zagórów in the years 1746-1806


Przemysław Kowalski

Until the beginning of the 18th century the Puszcza Pyzdrska was a sparsely populated area, administratively divided into the counties of Kalisz, Konin and Pyzdry in the Kalisz voivodeship. The centre of the forest, with a predominance of poor soils in the dry places, was covered with extensive pine woods, while in the waterlogged places it was dominated by alder carrs with an admixture of oaks. This area was almost uninhabited. The few settlements were connected with forestry or smelting, e.g. Smoleniec and Huta. The situation was different on the outskirts of the Puszcza Pyzdrska, where villages had been founded since the early Middle Ages, among them Ruda Komorska, Szymanowice, Niniew, Piła, Brudzew, Lipe, Jarantów with its medieval fortified settlement, Kościelec, Kosmów, Przespolew Kościelny, Malanów, Grzymiszew, Dzierzbin, Dąbroszyn, Grabienice, Rzgów, Kopojno, Dolany, Ląd with its medieval fortified settlement and Cistercian abbey, Policko, Rataje and Dłusk, and the towns of Pyzdry, Chocz, Stawiszyn, Tuliszków, Rychwał, Zagórów, Lądek and Ciążeń, which have survived to this day (Lądek and Ciążeń lost their town rights at a later period). Settlement on the outskirts was favoured by better soils, the waterways of the Warta and Prosna, and the old trade route running from Kalisz to Konin. At that time, in the centre of the forest only the route connecting Stawiszyn with Ląd had been brought under cultivation, of which among other things the villages of Trąbczyn, Szetlewek and Królików with a church of Romanesque origin and a medieval fortified settlement are a reminder.
