 Chrościki, Anielewo, Michalinów, Augustynów, Wrąbczynkowskie Holendry and others. Schools (Kantoratsschulen) and houses of prayer were built and cemeteries laid out. In 25 localities within the territory of the present-day parish of Zagórów, Germans of the Evangelical confession lived for over 200 years as the dominant group. They formed the Evangelical community in the town, which came into being as a filial in the year 1843 and in the year 1856 became an independent parish. The name "Olędrzy" [Hauländers] was used for a long time in the parish of Zagórów and referred not to the nationality of the settlers but to the law under which the villages were founded and relations with the estate owners were regulated. This law was brought to Poland by the Mennonites who had fled from the Netherlands for religious reasons.

In August 2020 I paid a visit to Łukasz Parus – the director of the Cultural Centre in Zagórów – and laid before him the considerations described above. He was fire and flame for this idea, and what was important: he knew who should be invited to collaborate. Nor did one need to convince Waldemar Wunsz, the pastor of the Evangelical-Augsburg parish in Konin, within whose territory the Zagórów Evangelical church lies, of this idea.

And so the idea of undertaking work on the preparation of a publication on the history of the Evangelical population (of the Germans and Poles) in this region was realised. A few weeks later, at a meeting in the former Kantorhaus at Konińska Street 4 in Zagórów, an editorial committee was set up which set itself the goal of bringing the whole undertaking to a successful conclusion.

After almost three years, extensive material in the form of articles, interviews, reminiscences, documents and photographs has been gathered and compiled into a book.
