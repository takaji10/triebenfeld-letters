After the war lost by Prussia with France in 1806, Greater Poland found itself in the years 1807-1814 within the borders of the Duchy of Warsaw, and after the Congress of Vienna, in the years 1815-1832, the eastern part of Greater Poland found itself in the Kingdom of Poland (Congress Poland), while the western part was in Prussia. Zagórów and the Puszcza Pyzdrska found themselves in the Kingdom of Poland (Congress Poland). The Polish authorities of both the Duchy of Warsaw and the Kingdom of Poland (Congress Poland), owing to the difficult situation of the public finances, were unable to give financial support to settlement in the Polish lands. In order to compensate for the obligations the Prussian government had entered into towards the colonists, the promised material or financial assistance had to be converted into an extension of the period free of lease rent and taxes.

For the new applicants wishing to settle in the Kingdom of Poland (Congress Poland), suitable places were designated in the state estates, with a period free of lease rent and taxes. In the Zagórów commune, colonization on the new terms was not carried out.
