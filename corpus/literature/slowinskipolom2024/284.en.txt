# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 284
# translation by the editor's files

#### Hitler destroyed us all

Mum always told me that the whole family considered it unjust, and took it very hard, that during the occupation poorer German peasants were taking over the farms of wealthier Polish farmers, and hatred was deepening between Poles and Germans. And yet before the war Grandpa and his neighbour, a Pole, would team up their horses so that ploughing would be easier and quicker; they always lived in accord. My aunt from Lądek always said: “And what did that Hitler go and do? He took my brothers from me (they died in the war at the ages of sixteen and twenty-two) and set us against our neighbours… He destroyed us all.”

#### They called me “that German”…

Mum married a Catholic and therefore had to sign a document in which she undertook that the children born of the marriage would be raised in the Catholic faith. That was the requirement for the priest to agree to the wedding. My brother and I were therefore baptized and raised as Catholics, although I always felt closer to Protestantism. But at school they always called me “that German”. Today, both of us, my husband and I, although we live in Kostrzyn, look after the cemetery in Wrąbczynkowskie Holendry; we care for the graves, for the memory…
