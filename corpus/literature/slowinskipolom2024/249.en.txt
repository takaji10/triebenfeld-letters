My interlocutor opened the door for him, and the man began to explain in German what had brought him. At first she was somewhat stunned, listening to this speech, but after a moment she quipped that since she was a Pole and he a Pole, they would get along splendidly in their native tongue. The man was greatly surprised, just as she had been a moment before. After a while he confessed that all along he had been convinced that, since all the residents of the senior home belonged to the "German church", they were surely Germans and did not speak Polish. It is quite a telling picture of how little awareness of the identity of our neighbours of the Protestant faith a part of the inhabitants of Zagórów had, and still has to this day. Let us hope that reading the publication we have prepared will contribute to changing this state of affairs.

On 31 December 2007 Mrs Erika Fandrich retired. She had worked at the care home for almost 13 years. The entry in the chronicle under that date read: "For the years of work, care and sacrifices, on behalf of the residents we offer Mrs Erika our most heartfelt thanks with the words 'God repay you'".

On 6 April 2011 Mr Edward Szwarc died, the first and last resident of the Evangelical Senior Home. His passing was at the same time the end of the institution's activity, present in the landscape of the little town of Zagórów for sixteen years (1995-2011). The building remained empty for almost ten years, despite several ideas for its use being considered. None of the projects, however, came to fruition. Fortunately, since May 2021, after a thorough modernisation, a small, cosy guesthouse with the charming name of "Gęsi Puch" has been housed in it.
