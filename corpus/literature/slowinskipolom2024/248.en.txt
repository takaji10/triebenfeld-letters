 to the right room – says Rev. Kowalska – did not help; this lady's perceptive abilities were already so limited that she was unable to assimilate the new arrangement of the rooms. In connection with similar situations and the residents' self-reliance diminishing with every month, it became necessary to employ two more carers. The residents of the EDS were calm, warm-hearted people. They had their individual likings and habits, but the ritual common to all was… watching *Mody na sukces*. A moment before broadcast time all the seniors would sit down together in the dining room in front of the television, awaiting the next episode of their favourite telenovela in great suspense. At such moments, any attempts to engage them in some other activity were doomed to failure from the start! – Rev. Katarzyna tells me.


I mentioned earlier that Wednesday was the day of Rev. Mendrok's visits. His wife Rozwita would accompany him then, always arriving with a cake she had freshly baked. The residents of the house made coffee and sat down together with their pastor and his wife in the dining room. It was a time of Bible reflections, of singing hymns together and of celebrating the next meeting. The last Wednesday before Christmas was, by custom, the "Zagórów Christmas Eve", when all the residents, the carers, the residents' relatives and other invited guests met at the supper.

Warm, almost family-like relations developed between the seniors and the young inhabitants of the Evangelical Senior Home. My interlocutor recalled with particular emotion the moment when one of the residents, Mrs Ruth Miska, appeared unexpectedly at Kasia and Eneasz's wedding as far away as Brzeg, even though she had to hire a car with a driver to do so. On returning to Zagórów, another surprise awaited the newly-weds – the seniors, after prior deliberation, unanimously ceded one of the toilets to the young couple, having decided that the young marriage should have more space.
