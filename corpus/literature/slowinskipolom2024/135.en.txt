# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 135
# translation by the editor's files

Bogdan Karaszewski decided with his wife that the flat allocated to them required a substantial renovation. Possessing numerous skills that come in useful in such work, he set about the job in 1995. The scope of the work must have been considerable, since the interior doors also qualified for replacement. Throwing out a door together with its frame – so that the ceiling did not come down on one's head – requires a certain experience. The operation succeeded, and the extracted, rotted frame revealed an unexpected sight. It turned out that this partition between the rooms, which everyone had taken to be a single, monolithic wall, was built of two walls, between which there was a considerable space. When the dust settled, there appeared before Bogdan Karaszewski a sizeable pile of papers sealed up tight. Someone had carefully secured them and cleverly hidden them. The first packet brought out contained many letters stamped with the field post of the Wehrmacht as well as a Book of Baptisms. Further on lay wrapped coverings of church altars and carpenter's tools. When the find had been taken out, books began to slide down from above through the narrow gap into the empty space… This collection of remarkable books "falling" downwards was opened by a biography of Joseph Goebbels. In all, thirty-one volumes came into Karaszewski's hands. Apart from three gnawed and probably mouse-damaged ones, most of the books survived in good condition, with the marks of time visible on the covers or pages.

The finder, in his spare moments, tried to interest various people and institutions in the discovered materials, but without much success. Great respect is due to the way Bogdan Karaszewski handled the find brought out from the wall. Everything was packed into a cardboard box and placed in a safe spot. Thus almost 25 years passed. In the meantime the family changed flats. An announcement placed on the internet seeking materials for a history of the Zagórów Evangelicals, the old settlers from Germany, did not escape Karaszewski's notice. One October day in 2020 he walked into the Zagórów House of Culture and handed over to its director, Łukasz Parus, this entire collection, untouched by the years.
