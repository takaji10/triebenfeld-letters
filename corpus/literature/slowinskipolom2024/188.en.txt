In the letter of Gustav of 1 May 1942 one can find the "fruit" of these quite widely proclaimed teachings. He writes about the times of Christ: "Should wise people then have lived at all and held back God, and said: what are you doing, choose the Saviour from the Germanic people, and not from among the Jews. But it pleased God so". In May 1942 in Zagórów and the neighbourhood there was no longer a single Jew. But Gustav Adolf looks further with concern and, on the occasion of an occasion, encodes the question of what the authorities intend to undertake g. J. – this is the abbreviation of the words *gegen Juden* – against the Jews[^68]. "The scale of irrationalism, brutality, servility and cultism can be seen in the poetry of the brown poets" – writes Hubert Orłowski[^69]. In presenting the leader, words are chosen from the New Testament; Hitler is God's anointed, chosen by God or by other, vaguely defined powers. He is the saviour of the German nation. And all those who die faithful to their leader are to become martyrs. This is only a very brief reminder of what the idea of Germania was.

We return to Bruno's letter. He fears whether his sister has not taken over from her husband the faith in the new religion. In this "religion" even the conception of the soul was distorted in such a way as to serve as yet another element of propaganda[^70]. In the thought of national Christianity the soul was to strive to fulfil the divine plan, in which Germany – the nation chosen by God – is to heal and beatify the world. This deep disquiet at the behaviour of the sister, who has long not been seen in church, is also a clear declaration, though not expressed outright. Eternal damnation threatens the sister if she does not turn back in time. Bruno Mielke rejects the new religion with all the strength of a believing Christian. He considers it the road to man's perdition. A betrayal of God.
