In December Bruno Mielke wrote a great deal. The approaching Christmas summoned memories of the family home, of the sisters, the brother, the father and the mother no longer alive (she died in 1933). "I want to do it [write] in such a way that you receive more post from me especially in the period of the Christmas feast". The first joy was the three-kilogram parcel received from home, and in it a fir wreath with the head of a little angel and tinsel. "I at once fastened it to my cupboard made from an empty grenade crate with two boards inserted. Several comrades who 'happened' to be nearby and were gazing with longing eyes at the Christmas cake being taken out got a morsel to taste. They pronounced hymns of praise as to the delicacy of the dainty".


In the letter of 14 December there is also the information that soldiers who have no families should send stamps to the local party wardens, and these will pass them on to the Women's Leagues, which will prepare Christmas parcels for the front soldiers. The Wehrmacht too saw to richly furnished parcels for every soldier, in which, alongside snuff, cigarettes, sweets, chocolate and wool for darning, there were also envelopes for letters, a comb, a bottle of vodka and of wine. Further on there is a paragraph of thanks for the fulfilment of his earlier request and ("I hope I am not betraying a secret") for the sending of an ointment against lice, which everyone has here. "On Saturday I washed myself, smeared myself all over and put on fresh linen. Now no louse bites, that is, the ointment works". The question is for how long. On the order of the Wehrmacht there was produced the preparation T-14 – a repellent powder against lice, fleas and mites etc. Each soldier was issued two such packets[^57]
