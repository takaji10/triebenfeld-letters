# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 117
# translation by the editor's files

 of Andreas and Eva Rosine tilling the land was not enough; they did not want to be farmers like their forebears, especially as the times favoured them, the family was becoming well-off and relatively, by village standards, propertied. And so two sons of Andreas-Andrzej and Justyna became teachers: Wilhelm in Nowy Czarków, Andrzej in Święcia.

When one analyses and traces the histories of settler families, phenomenal and worthy of admiration is how, from generation to generation, despite many hardships and obligations, and often disinclination, these families strove to raise their social status. And very often it was precisely through persevering labour that they succeeded. I think that the cult of work, which has been characteristic of Protestantism from its very beginning, that is, almost since 1517, is the source of their success. These people did not work merely in order to consume and stand still; by working they multiplied their goods and converted them into the success of the whole family and into a social status ever higher and better. The sons of Andreas-Andrzej and Justyna clearly subscribed to the ethos of work. Two became teachers, the others continued to till the land, but now as prosperous husbandmen, enjoying esteem and respect, which found reflection in the registers of vital records, where very often they appear in their villages as godparents or witnesses. The Beutlers married: Gottlieb Julianna Schiewe, Andrzej Henrietta Hahn, Wilhelm Emilia Fryderyka Lauf, Ludwik Emilia Hermann.

The four lines of the Beutler family, as they grew, put down roots in the surrounding land, but above all in society. Although they were aware of their different origin from that of the majority of the inhabitants of these lands, the matter did not preoccupy them and did not rob them of sleep. They worked, cared for their families, entered into relations with neighbours, pupils, workers, and led normal and reasonably peaceful lives. In 1862 Justyna née Lehmann Beutler died, and in 1879 Andreas-Andrzej Beutler, the head of the family, passed away. One generation was leaving the stage of history, and then the next generation assumed full rule over its domains.
