# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 118
# translation by the editor's files

Ludwik Beutler, born on 29 May 1845 in Bilczew, is the progenitor of the line that shifted the centre of gravity of the Beutlers' family life to the south-west of Konin. In time this line emigrated almost entirely to the west, to Germany, for after the Second World War it could no longer find a place to live in post-war Poland. But that comes later. In the meantime, three sons were born to Ludwik and Emilia: Adolf, Julius and Ewald, along with five daughters. It is thus evident that Ludwik and Emilia upheld the family tradition of having a sizable flock of children. Large families were the norm in those days; people knew nothing of contraception, and modern medicine and the care of women in childbirth and of newborns were still in their infancy. The end of the nineteenth century, when Ludwik's children were being born, was a time of relative peace in Europe. The German Empire had consolidated itself and the thunder of cannon had ceased until the next great upheavals and revolutions — several decades of relative peace. The boys developed normally and most probably very quickly began to help their parents with the work on the family estate in Bilczew. But Bilczew was decidedly too little for healthy and strong, with time young, men, when the whole wide world stood open before them, and thanks to the solid and persevering labour of themselves, their parents and the generations before them, the family could afford to dispatch the boys into adult life beyond Bilczew. Ewald Beutler set out for Koło, where he settled and led the life of a townsman. His son Erwin Beutler became a pharmacist in Koło. He devoted himself to that profession without reserve, practising it even after leaving Poland, in Berlin, in Weissensee, where he settled. Julius went off to Łódź, where he opened and ran a butcher's shop — he was yet another of the Beutler clan to become a townsman. Julius's shop was said to be on Piotrkowska Street. Adolf, for his part, led the life of a farmer, in keeping with family tradition, but no longer in Bilczew — in Dąbrowica near Rzgów, not far from Zagórów. And it was he who, one December day in 1918, became the proud father of another daughter, who at her baptism was given the names Gertrud Adelheid — only after the war did the offices polonize the name to Gertruda.
