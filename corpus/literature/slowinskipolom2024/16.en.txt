It is worth noting that Tonn writes these words at a time of aggressive nationalist and Nazi propaganda, widely circulated and conducted among the German minority. In the Jewish milieu the Polish State was, for the settlers who had been established there for years, an administration new in its very essence and foreign. Many extremely difficult matters had to be resolved, connected with the consolidation of lands on which, for 123 years, separate legal systems had been in force, different money and economic rules, customs and culture. The customs border between the former Russian and Prussian partitions was abolished in 1923. Only in the nineteen-twenties was the joining of the railway lines running from Warsaw to Poznań carried out. The nearest station was in Słupca, 15 km away. Bus transport also began to function, by which one could reach the unhappily chosen provincial office in Łódź. The distance from the main railway lines and trade routes did not make business easier. The war ended, but the problems did not end, and their scale corresponded to the mass trauma and the death of millions of victims of that tragic conflict. These were the times of workers' revolts, of the bloody revolution in Russia, and at the same time of the war of the young state against the army of the Bolsheviks, which was trying to conquer Poland and carry the revolution westwards.
