# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 181
# translation by the editor's files

Many German writers worked on the creation of whole series of books intended for the soldiers of the Wehrmacht. These were the so-called Feldpost-Bücher. "Anthologies were published containing a fascist-nationalist mixture intended to provide entertainment, some form of spiritual nourishment and, above all, ideological, Nazi support".[^62] What could one read in these works? Well, the individual is nothing! He is worth as much as he subordinates himself to the "voice of the blood", to the "holy fatherland", to the sense of duty or to the order of the leader. "Since the leader sacrifices himself for all of us and for Germany, the sacrificing of oneself for Germany and the blind devotion to the leader is likewise necessary". This entire literature instils in the Germans, on countless pages, the supreme principle: in the consciousness of a historical mission there holds for you "obedience and fidelity unto death"[^63].

(In the Russian Pieczenga, the former Finnish Petsamo, where Bruno Mielke was stationed and fought, there is a cemetery where "Our 12,000 Fallen Comrades" ("Unseren 12000 Toten Kameraden") lie at rest)[^64].

It is not difficult to find in the soldiers' letters quoted here those Nazi canons, stated outright and repeated many times. At times, however, one may gain the impression that it is done mechanically, without any, even hidden, reflection. Mielke volunteers for a medic's course. He does so, as he writes, for two reasons: such skills may prove useful for a whole life; secondly, he hopes that in this field it will be easier for him to win promotion.
