# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 176
# translation by the editor's files

Nothing happens without the will and permission of the Lord. These are decisive and grave months; one must be able to sacrifice one's own wishes.

This is the first letter in which Mielke allows himself a certain generalisation of the situation on his sector of the front. Until now these were "narrow plans", showing the individual threats and dangers menacing him directly. Now he says: prepare yourselves for a hard time, for things do not look well. Whatever he had in mind and whatever his knowledge really was, in saying that the fighting is entering its decisive phase he was not mistaken. The next letter preserved in the cache is dated Eastern Front 27 July 1943. At the beginning of the month – on 5 July – the German attack on the Kursk salient begins. The struggle lasted until the end of August. The Russians knew the enemy's plans, code-named "Citadel", very well and adopted the defensive strategy proposed by Zhukov. When the forces of the attacking troops, bogged in the mud, dying on mines and under the murderous fire of the artillery, were exhausted, they moved to a counter-attack. It was the greatest tank battle in history. The lost battle caused the German command to be unable to seize the strategic initiative on the Eastern Front for the rest of the war. "With us it is comparatively quiet" – he writes. "A confirmation of the fact that the Russians are using all their forces in the south in order to reconquer the central areas. Here it is often gloomy and misty. Then it is dangerous, for the Russians readily make attacks then, but we are already watchful and our machines do not fail".

The thoughts of Bruno Mielke run to Łazińsk, where people can calmly gather the grain from the fields and run over the stubble fields. Here, on the deserted and harsh north, one feels nothing of what people in the countryside are living by now. And the harvests are important for the war and for the nation. He thanks his sister Anna for the parcel and in passing conveys to her the information that he is no longer writing to Elfride. And it is not recklessness or indifference, but rather responsibility. Apart from that he is healthy, and although they feed him well here, lately he has lost his appetite. "Would that the dear God were only with His grace near our people, so that everything ends well and mankind may again know peace".
