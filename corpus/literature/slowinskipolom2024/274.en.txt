#### “Once a month the pastor came from Grodziec…”

##### Reminiscences of Eugenia Wysokowska (1900-1975)

The solemn service was on, and heaps of Germans came in all sorts of village attire. Weddings began in the carnival season, and the priest announced the banns from the pulpit. A dozen or so carts, horses decked out, ribbons on the whips, a band, the whole street full of carts and carriages. We'd ask the lads, and they'd drive us about the town and round the marketplace and back in front of the German church, and the lad was glad he was driving girls from town, and best of all – the ride was the best part. From the Oleśnica side the young couple came to the wedding in carts, always with the witnesses in one carriage, then the other carriages and carts. They invited a lot of guests. The musicians rode with drums on one cart, the young people behind them. They would stand up, shout, wave their ribbons about. The horses had ribbons on their harness, and up at the top of the whip too. When they drove back from the wedding, everyone came out to have a look.

#### We were at a wedding in Olchowo…

Zygmunt Wolf was getting married, and the bride was from Olchowo. He lived across from the Jareckis, with his mother. The Jareckis too were at the wedding with the lads. Lots of carriages and carts, a band – they drove through the streets, through Oleśnica and along the canal. It was lovely. A clearing among the trees, a house, a farmstead. Supper – there was plenty of food, but whenever they set out a platter of meat, half of it was gone. The next one, we said, will be all ours! They told jokes and anecdotes. Then they held the capping ceremony: they take off the veil and pin a little flower in the hair. A little basket went round the tables and they sang, and everyone had to drop some cash into the basket for the young couple. Then someone crawled under the table and pulled off the bride's shoe; she had to buy it back. Afterwards everyone returned to dancing; they began to dance till morning on the clearing, some dozed off on the chairs, some went to the barn, to the benches, and some in front of the house. The young couple went to sleep in the barn towards morning. There were beds for them separately. The Germans, their friends, sneaked in quietly and tickled the soles of their feet, playing tricks on them, and there was laughter!
