It is on precisely this branch of the Beutler family that I would like to focus attention now — first, because it is the one best known to me, and second, because the fates of the children of Adolf and Emilia, as well as their own, make up an international mosaic of which many citizens of Poland after the Second World War formed a part. As I mentioned, Adolf was occupied with tilling the land and breeding horses, and the horses that were born and raised on Adolf Beutler's farm in Dąbrowica were of exceptional beauty. Emilia, besides being an exceptionally resourceful housekeeper — with so many children it is hard to be anything else — was also a seamstress, by which she supplemented the household budget among the women of the surrounding villages and small towns. The beginning of the twentieth century was marked by many turbulent events that resounded widely across the world and did not remain without influence on the life of the Beutler family. In the early years of the new century conflicts flared up anew after years of relative peace. In 1901 the long and peaceful reign of the British Queen Victoria came to an end; in 1905 the Russo-Japanese War broke out, which in a short time laid bare the internal decay and instability of the Russian Empire; the revolution of 1905; unrest in Europe, and as its consequence the outbreak of the First World War, called the Great War, in 1914; the revolutions in Russia in 1917; the fall of the Austrian, German and Russian monarchies; the end of the war and Poland's recovery of independence in November 1918. All of this formed the background against which the next generation of the descendants of Andreas and Eva Rosine entered into life. From 1918 all of them became full-fledged citizens of the Second Polish Republic. And here I must list all the children of Adolf and his two wives, with a brief description of their fates, interweaving the narrative with mentions of events that steered the fates of the young Beutlers in this and no other way. Each of these stories is a separate tale that shows people, events, times, and above all points to the extraordinarily rich multicultural heritage of the territories of present-day Poland.
