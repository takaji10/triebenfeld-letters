# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 156
# translation by the editor's files

It is worth pausing for a moment over this last sentence. How is it to be understood? As the pride of a soldier who is told that he is fighting for a homeland free of Jews and Russian hordes? Or perhaps in this "feeling", somewhere from the subconscious, there flows the question of what he is really taking part in? He does not write about combat operations – for it is obvious that the censor would never let such a letter pass. But he shows another enemy – nature, which in his account is more dangerous than the adversary. Or does he perhaps also indicate that that adversary has an extraordinarily dangerous ally? In reading letters from the front, the question will always arise. To what extent should they be taken literally, and to what extent should one read what is not there or what was said between the lines? In any case it is visible that the private Mielke has not yet come to know the true war machine grinding human flesh, and with growing apprehension awaits that encounter.

This letter is – in the language of war – loaded with emotions. Above all he writes of his great dream, of which he thinks unceasingly. "…if I return safe and in health, I shall prepare myself for the most beautiful of professions, in order to exercise it with the greatest blessing […] My heart is in any case full of longing for a life devoted by God – and as a pastor I can do that best of all". His thoughts also run to his future wife, who must have views such as his. That is the first condition about which he will first ask her. Does she belong to the Saviour and is she pious? Married life, as he imagines it, must be "a holiness of the Lord and blessed by the nearness of God and of the Spirit".

So too he advises the brother, who in a letter asks his opinion about Truthilde. "Observe her and talk with her thoroughly, how far she is sanctified by Christ". Gustav is 43 years old and, as one can gather from other letters, the search for a candidate for wife has been going on with varying success for some time. Yet the advice of the younger Bruno is invariable – he must know whether she is an inwardly searching, deeply believing soul. It would also be good if she were not ugly.

His thoughts return to the school years in Łazińsk, which were the most sunny and the happiest of all the periods of his life.
