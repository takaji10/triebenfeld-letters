Bruno tries to put some of his affairs in order and writes an application for leave so as to be able to go to Konin and visit Łazińsk. In the letter of 10 October he shares with his brother Gustav the news he obtained during some course at an NSDAP school in Będlewo near Stęszew. It turns out that an official subordinate to the Reich Governor expounded at length before the course participants visions of allocating land to village schools, in this way to encourage teachers to settle in the countryside. Bruno Mielke considers that "it is obvious that proper schools must be built in the Wartheland and that land must not be stinted after the war with Russia". And here follows a longer exposition of the advantages of such a solution as opposed to taking over complete village farms, which would be subject to far more severe regulations and production obligations. Foreign (read: Polish) farmhands cause the farmers particularly many troubles. Hence he urgently urges his brother to take a prompt interest in the possibility of obtaining such a grant – preferably near Zagórów or Konin. He writes that Polish owners used to be there. "I hope that not everything has yet been divided up". In this entire – one of the longest – letters there is not a crumb of reflection that he is urging his brother to take part in the plunder of the land and houses of those who for years were their neighbours. Mielke is undoubtedly a religious man. Yet one gets the impression that in relations with Poles some of the Ten Commandments had been suspended.

The autumn months of 1941 were a terrible time on this land. From September to November the liquidation of the Zagórów ghetto was under way. The Jews are transported in lorries to the forests near Kazimierz Biskupi (the Wygoda reserve), vehicles in which they are suffocated with exhaust fumes. Those brought there alive are shot or thrown alive into pits with lime. The letters from this period are few and are the last before Bruno Mielke's
