# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 255
# translation by the editor's files

### Where the lilies of the valley grow[^85]


The novelist Zdzisław Kulawinek[^85] was interviewed by Łukasz Parus

The novel is about people from our parts. Poles, Jews, Germans living in eastern Wielkopolska. Where does that subject come from?

In the mid-1950s I often went hunting with my father in the forests between Stawiszyn and Grodziec. The sandy forest roads and tracks did not allow the pleasure of a longer ride, whether by motorcycle or by car. It usually ended at the first small hills. We covered the rest of the way on foot. In time I came to love those walks. I had the feeling that thanks to them I was discovering a completely new world, unknown to me until then. We would enter one village, then the next. We knew all the peasants who lived here with their families: the poor Poles and the even poorer Germans, whom nobody wanted, hidden deep in the forest in their cottages. Here and there one could come upon traces left by the Jews. Even then I had a fair amount of knowledge about the centuries-old, difficult and not painless neighbourhood of three nations. The years went by. Time did not manage to erase the memories of my youth. Both the people and the places sank deep into my heart and gave me no peace. My wanderings through the villages founded by Hauländers drew ever wider circles. I visited places lying ever farther from the surroundings of Grodziec, Stawiszyn or Zbiersk.



[^85]: Zdzisław Kulawinek, a Kalisz native and a passionate student of local history, is the author of the novel *Tam, gdzie rosną konwalie*, which he published at his own expense in the year 2007. The publication reminded the readers of Eastern Greater Poland (including Borowiec, Grodziec and Zagórów) of the multicultural pre-war society, that is, of the German and Jewish neighbours who had inhabited Poland for centuries.
