From 1918, work began on a new church order for the Church, connected with a draft act on the relationship of the Evangelical-Augsburg Church to the Polish state. The church order envisaged a synodal-consistorial structure of the Church, whereas the draft act on the State–Church relationship was to give the Evangelical-Augsburg Church the status of a free and autonomous national church, enjoying the care and assistance of the state authorities in return for the supervision granted to the state in accordance with the provisions of the law. The decree of 25 November 1936 did not meet the expectations of the Polish or the German Lutherans – above all, the supervisory powers of the state were expanded, not allowing the Church in practice to make independent decisions in personnel matters. In 1937, in the church elections, a sharp church-political struggle took place, in which the main opposition to Bishop Bursche was the Working Community of German Pastors, founded in 1935, with the support of the foreign affairs office of the Evangelical Church of Germany. The leading role in the structures of the opposition was played by Pastor Eduard Kneifel, a native of Władysławów near Konin. In the four dioceses with a German majority: Kalisz, Płock, Łódź and Volhynia, the elections of the senioral authorities did not come to pass. Finally, Bishop Bursche decided to appoint commissarial seniors in them.


In the Kalisz diocese, the most serious candidate for the office of senior of the diocese was Pastor Adolf Ulbrich of Grodziec, but Pastor Edward Wende was appointed commissarial senior. For the Church, an important matter was the training of cadres and church workers. Until then, in the various partitions, the clergy had been educated at various institutions: in Vienna, at the German universities, and, from the Russian partition – in Dorpat.
