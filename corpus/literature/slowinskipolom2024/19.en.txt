# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 19
# translation by the editor's files

 Damian Kruczkowski describes in these pages, we see the dramatic quality of their fate. In a similar way the fate of Erik Starcki of Łazińsk would unfold; he broke through numerous cordons and took part in the bloody battle of the Polish corps at Monte Cassino.

From the first days after 20 September 1939, when the soldiers of the Wehrmacht occupy Zagórów, all the actions undertaken are directed in the first place against the Jews. One may have the impression that the German front-line soldiers, driving the terrified citizens into various humiliating tasks, cutting off the Jews' sidelocks, amuse themselves splendidly with their fear. It is a kind of release of the trauma brought back from the field of battle, on which at any moment you may perish. But there must be order. A new administration is quickly formed, at the head of which, as the new mayor, stands the aforementioned Disterheft. Now everything goes according to the plan long since settled in Berlin. The Polish schools and institutions are closed. All are obliged to bow to the uniformed occupiers. The behaviour of many German fellow-citizens, inhabitants of the surroundings and of Zagórów, undergoes a violent change. This is noticed not only by the Poles, but also by the astonished Pastor Gross, together with the dwindling group of the faithful, whom these newcomers and uniformed functionaries ostentatiously ignore. Christianity is a decadent religion of the lowly, the humble and the weak. Their faith is the Führer and the party. "Just behind the Evangelical cemetery, on the road to Imielno, there has survived a "cholera cemetery", overgrown with fine pines, in the midst of which stood a small brick "corpse house", a relic of the pre-funeral house from the times of the last pestilence. […] The German gendarmerie shut up for the night in that "corpse house" Poles caught out after the police curfew"[^9]. From 14 November the Jews are obliged to wear armbands with the Star of David, ten centimetres wide. All educational and cultural organizations and associations, and of course religious ones, are dissolved. The houses are to be marked with the German Jude. But this is only the beginning.
