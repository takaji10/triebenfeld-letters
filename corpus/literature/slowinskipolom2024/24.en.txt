Intrigued by some commotion in the street, she looked out of the window and saw a patrol of Red Army men leading, under the muzzles of their rifles, the German gendarme, an inhabitant of Zagórów – Edwin Rydl, who had stayed behind and did not intend to flee anywhere. She was certain that they were leading him out to be shot. She ran out into the street, barred the way of the marching men and began to beg them to let Rydl go, that he was a decent man who had harmed no one. "Surely my protests would have achieved little – Grandma added – had not a Russian officer come up." He listened none too willingly to these protective assurances, until Salomea Maciejewska, with weeping, forced out of herself that her husband was in a German camp, that she did not know whether he was alive, but that this man had helped us. They let Rydl go. My grandfather was liberated by the Americans, who on 11 April 1945 occupied the Arbeitslager Dora in Nordhausen, where in an underground camp factory the V-1 and V-2 were produced. He returned home. This substantial man weighed 44 kilograms. (The denouncer, arrested together with Grandpa, died in one of the camps). Rydl was, across the street, the neighbour of my grandparents for the whole rest of their lives, and I saw him many times at their house. He came most often in the evening and always spoke in a whisper. He had his duties in the Evangelical church, but he was also a trusted man of Tytus Pieszak – a universally respected Zagórów trader, who entrusted him with responsible tasks requiring discretion.

From my father's wartime memories there lodged in my memory the story of the Herman family, who were installed in the house of my grandfather Tomasz Słowiński on Nowa Street (the Germans renamed it Manfred von Richthofen Straße, in honour of the Luftwaffe ace of the First World War). They took over the whole farmstead, the spacious house, leaving to the very numerous family one room and the kitchen. The father and the brothers – those who had not been taken away to labour or were languishing in captivity – had to work their land for the German master. But the daily relations did not give rise to any particular tensions between the families. Grandpa Tomasz Słowiński, for many terms of office – until the First World War – was elected mayor of Zagórów.
