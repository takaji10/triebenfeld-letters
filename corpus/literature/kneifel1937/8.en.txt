# Kneifel (1937), p. 8
# translation by Claude

Built on a narrow congregational base, worn down by internal struggles, harassed by the Roman Catholic church, Protestantism in Poland moved toward its decline. In the Catholic Counter-Reformation there grew up against it an opponent whose attacks it proved unable to withstand in the long run[^p8-3] Until the granting of freedom of religion in our land (1768) there remained in Greater Poland only seven Reformed congregations, very much weakened in their existence, one of them in the Warthe lowland and another in the Kalisch land — at Żychlin (near Konin) and Wola Tłumakowska[^p8-4]

A vigorous strengthening and promotion of Protestantism in Poland came only in modern times through the German colonization. In the Kalisch land it began as early as about 1750. Numerous larger and smaller settlements arose here, as also in the Warthe lowland and in Kujawien[^p8-5] As an urban place of immigration Władysławów (Rosterschütz) is to be named for 1727. In the Prussian period seven large Swabian villages were founded: Neu-Renneberg (Wygorzele, formerly the parish of Dąbie, now Łęczyca), Schwedelbach, Rosental, Wilhelmstal, Friedrichstal, Lilienfeld, Czarnocice. In South Prussia, in the towns of Kalisch, Dąbie, Kowal, Przedecz, Chodecz and others, German officials, craftsmen, and clothmakers settled. In the period from 1815 to 1863 rural and urban settlement activity made further progress. In the Kalisch land the founding of Wojciechowo took place in 1845, after that those of Michalinów, Łagiewniki, Sołomina. In Kujawien the colonies were laid out: 1840 Morzyczyn, 1842 Władysławów, 1850 Marcinkowo, 1855 Szarłatowo, 1859 Mieczysławowo, 1866 Besiekiery. Of far greater economic and church significance was the settlement of the towns[^p8-6]
