As early as 1888 the filial considered erecting a new solid church at an expenditure of 6568 rubles 17.5 kopecks. The building plan, together with the cost estimate, was submitted to the Russian authorities for confirmation. But since this project was not approved by the authorities, the filial congregation was compelled to repair and enclose the old defective house of prayer, to lay a cement floor within, and even to put a wooden tower on the "church," as the plain house of prayer is here designated. Nevertheless the house of prayer remained much too small and cramped, so that the building of a new church was here felt to be the most pressing task. Pastor Henkel of Konin indeed supported the plan of the church building, but wished at first that the house of God should be built "not at Maślaki on the border of the filial, but in the market town of Kleczew, which forms the center of the filial congregation"4). In the meantime years went by. When Gen.-Sup. Bursche visited Maślaki in 1908, he emphasized the indispensable necessity of the church building. The members of the filial were in principle agreed with this. What divided them into two parties was the opposed conception concerning the place of building of the new house of God. While some advocated Maślaki, the others desired that the new church be erected at Kleczew. The filial assembly of June 29, 1910 indeed voted 15,000 rubles for the building, which were to be paid in three annual installments, yet the differences of opinion continued with unabated sharpness. The consistory definitively appointed Maślaki as the place of building, whereupon on April 10, 1913 the ministry of the interior granted the permission for the church building at Maślaki for the sum of 17,392 rubles 41 kopecks. The disunity in the filial, as also the outbreak of the World War in 1914, hindered the execution of this work. To the present day the question of the church building has unhappily not been settled here.

On April 29, 1912 the cantor Christoph Möller died, who had served the cantorate of Bolesławowo uninterruptedly for 58 years. "For 58 years he has proclaimed to us and to our forefathers the precious Word of God, taught the youth and called back to God many a hardened heart. His sermon readings and his singing penetrated into all hearts, and Möller was often envied by his colleagues because of his voice and his gift"5). On September 22, 1904 he was privileged to celebrate his 50-year jubilee as cantor.
