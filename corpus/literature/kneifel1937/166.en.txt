The deed of purchase of this plot was entered on October 15, 1825 (No. 928) before the notary Kroszczyński at Konin, but confirmed by the authorities only in 1827.8) In the year 1824 the Evangelicals acquired the former Roman Catholic church of the Holy Ghost (17 ells long, 14 ells broad and 8 high; built of timber 1801), which at that time lay on the place before the present evangelical church. The Evangelicals of Konin were cared for religiously by Pastor Bartsch of Władysławów and by the Reformed pastor Johann Scholtz of Żychlin.

In 1826 the congregation of Konin came into being, into which the former Slesin parish passed for the most part. Its first shepherd of souls was Pastor Johann August Kegler, a native of Görlitz (according to his passport No. 83 of July 22, 1826), who was installed on July 29, 1827 by the consistorial councilor Pastor Modl of Kalisch. To him goes back the "Kirchenbuch des im Jahre des Herrn 1826 neu errichteten evangelisch-lutherischen Kirchspiels der Kreisstadt Konin, enthaltend Taufen, geführt von August Kegler, erstem Pastor" [Church book of the evangelical-Lutheran parish of the county town of Konin newly founded in the year of our Lord 1826, containing baptisms, kept by August Kegler, first pastor]. Scarcely had he undertaken the preliminary work for a better development of the congregation when death overtook him on December 27, 1830.9) He was buried in that part of the Catholic cemetery that was appointed for the deceased of evangelical confession.10)

A serious time broke in upon the congregation of Konin after the death of its first pastor. The Russian-Polish war of 1830/31, the cholera, the waning of the religious-ecclesiastical sense and other unwelcome phenomena threatened the existence of the newly founded parish. For a time the sect of the "Zaftianer," which had many adherents in the country, was of special disadvantage to the congregational life. These sectaries held for a sign of "true conversion" when "the Spirit compelled the sinner to lamentation and crying out." The sect gained ground so long as it was combated with outward means. But when, in place of these, biblical instruction and teaching entered, it dissolved.11) How grave the economic situation of the congregation was we see from the letter of July 21, 1832, which Pastor Johann Scholtz-Żychlin, who administered Konin after Pastor Kegler's death, addressed to the consistorial councilor Karl Gottlieb Bartsch-Władysławów: ". . .
