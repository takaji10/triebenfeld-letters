# kneifel1937  57  en
# Kneifel (1937), p. 57
# translation by Claude

The evangelical cemetery in Kalisch, dating from the 17th (or indeed already the 16th) century, lies on an eminence which the common people formerly called the "Lutheran hill." On September 26, 1790 this cemetery was handed back to the Kalisch Evangelicals and fenced in by them in the following year. This proves that Evangelicals were likewise settled here at that time[^p57-6] They had their religious acts performed for the most part by the pastors of the Stawiszyn congregation.

Through the second partition of Poland (1793) Kalisch fell to Prussia. Into the newly acquired town came German-evangelical military men, officials, craftsmen and merchants. In the environs of Kalisch, in the "Dember Holland," evangelical farmers settled, whose "Schulhalter" (school-holders) were in 1796 Christian David Paetzolt, in 1805 Johann Gottlieb Schultz. In the year 1795 the number of those who had immigrated into Kalisch, together with the Evangelicals already settled here before, was so considerable that they petitioned the town administration to call a preacher for them. They also submitted to the government and to the consistory in Posen the request to establish in Kalisch "a church system of its own and to appoint a preacher, who was at the same time to fill the office of school-rector." Their efforts had complete success. On September 3, 1795 the "concession for the establishment of an evangelical church system of its own at Kalisch for the evangelical congregation there" was granted. Still in the course of that year Pastor Karl Michael Christian Sienell assumed the pastorate of the newly organized congregation. In a rented hall on the Old Ring, in the so-called cobblers' stalls ("jatki szewskie"), he held divine service for two years. Thanks to the intervention of the Prussian government Pope Pius VI, on the basis of the brief of August 27, 1797, made over to the Kalisch Evangelicals the Jesuit church standing empty since 1773 "cum pleno jure patronatus". The house of God, become evangelical by historical dispensation, which by Archbishop Karnkowski's will was to be a bulwark of work hostile to Protestants in the Kalisch land for all time, Pastor Sienell consecrated according to evangelical rite on the first Whitsun holiday 1798[^p57-7] The relief monument, which shows the primate Karnkowski before the cross of Christ in an attitude of prayer,
