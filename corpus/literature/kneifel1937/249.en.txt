# Kneifel (1937), p. 249
# translation by Claude

The school and prayer house were endowed with 18 Morgen of arable land and 2 Morgen of meadow, the cemetery with 2 Morgen 180 square rods of woodland.

Further foundations here are: Friedrichsfelde (Tarszewo), Adelshof (Chruściki), Emilienheim (Michalinów gmina Oleśnica), Sophiental (Olchowo), Ludwigslust (Mały Las), Ingelfingen (Anielewo), Augustinenhof (Augustynów). In the South Prussian period evangelical clothmakers also settled at Zagórów. The immigrants came from Silesia and the Posen land.

Until 1817 the pastors of Wreschen visited the Evangelicals in and about Zagórów, then those of the neighboring congregations of Grodziec, Konin and Żychlin (Reformed). In the year 1829 efforts made themselves felt to found an evangelical-Lutheran parish in the Zagórów region and to call a preacher. Karl Gottlieb Weigel, the lord of the manor of Kopojno, intended at that time to build on his possessions an evangelical church as well as the other needed buildings and to grant "for the clergyman to be appointed there an yearly quantum of 40 quarters of rye, 10 quarters of barley and 25 quarters of oats, as well as later additionally 6 barrels of beer . . ." The protocol taken on the manor farm at Kopojno on December 7, 1829, as also Weigel's two declarations of November 15, 1833 and July 8, 1834, made the expectation of the impending founding of a congregation appear justified, for which the Evangelicals from the Zagórów environs pleaded as warmly. On March 22, 1836 Weigel wrote, among other things, to Sup. Modl at Kalisch: ". . . Long since, and truly from purely Christian intentions, for the furtherance of religion and morality among the numerous evangelical colonists of my estates and their neighborhood . ., it was my most earnest wish, . . . to see the building of an evangelical church upon my territory really brought to fulfillment. The unhappy events in Poland have also held up this undertaking . . .

Weigel's death († 1836 at Breslau) worked out unfavorably in this, that his son and heir (Alexander Weigel) was finally unwilling to make any endowments whatever for the congregation to be founded. Nevertheless the matter did not rest. Still in 1836 men were elected into the "congregational board" (provisional church council): Matthäus Luther, Ludwig Sperling, Johann Kelsch, Johann Gottlieb Kittel, Karl Heit. These petitioned Sup. Modl to assist them in the church building at Zagórów and in the establishment of the parish. Sup. Modl let them know that he would energetically support their endeavors with the competent ecclesiastical and secular authorities. From the petition of the congregational board it appears that now the town of Zagórów was chosen as parish seat.
