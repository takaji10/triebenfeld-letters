Which river or stream, if even abandoning its original ^[*The ancient course of a river is assigned as the boundary.*] channel it diverts its course or flow through other places, without the agency of man but by its own natural force — nonetheless the original channel of that same river, where the flow of water previously was, shall be the true boundary of those estates. In which ancient river or stream, otherwise the channel of the ancient river, the people inhabiting either of those villages or estates may lawfully carry out their fishing, as they did when the water had its original or ancient course in that same ancient channel. But if a river or stream of this kind were diverted by the industry of man from its ancient course and channel, so as to flow into another new channel — then that new channel shall delimit the estates; and shall yield likewise as that ancient channel was accustomed to yield: one bank to one estate, the other to the other which it washes. In which case, when the diversion of the river is by the industry of man, one of those same estates — namely that in which the ancient channel remains — shall have full and entire ownership of the channel and banks of the ancient river or stream.
