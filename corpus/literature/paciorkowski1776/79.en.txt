# Paciorkowski (1776), p. 79
# translation by Claude

the office proceeded to the erection of mounds by virtue of the aforesaid amicable settlement. And first it caused the corner mounds to be renewed, with the placing of metal markers — charcoal, brick, and iron slag; proceeding from those mounds from the west toward the east, a wall mound was raised at the place called [name], first in number, etc. (and thus the erection of those same mounds shall be described in sequence; the boundary path may also be set out in the Polish language.)

All the corner and wall mounds having thus been raised and the boundaries determined, by the authority of his office he wills and declares the same to have the force of perpetual validity, and ordered the court messenger aforesaid to proclaim the same; if any of the collateral heirs wishes to contest those same boundaries, let him do so now. Which boundaries all the parties shall be bound to keep and observe, under a penalty of two thousand marks, by virtue of the present act.
