# paciorkowski1776  174  en
# Paciorkowski (1776), p. 174
# translation by Claude

charcoal (*Węgle*). *Lateres* — brick (*Cegły*). *Milium* — millet (*Proso*), concealed in a glass bottle into which a paper or parchment sheet is placed, with a description of the year and the act by which it was concluded and determined, so that it may be clear to posterity, upon the opening of the mounds, in the event of any future difference, pursuant to the intent of the decree.

These matters, therefore, having been declared and explained in the manner aforesaid as regards the boundary legal process — since it is not enough merely to establish boundaries through many and various turns of the law, and to erect them once established, but also to preserve them once erected, sound and intact — in the event therefore of the boundaries or mounds, whether of one, several, or nearly all of them, having been destroyed, ploughed up, and dispersed by the opposing party, or having collapsed with the passage of time, or having been erected by private authority and of one's own motion, omitting the ordinary field Sub-Chamberlain's office — what else remains for the person who suffered injury therefrom but to proceed at law for those that were destroyed or privately erected; to seek from the court the restoration of those dispersed and the demolition of those privately raised, with the decreed penalties; to bring the case on that account before the competent court — for which, to elicit the truth from the thing presented to the eyes, an official inspection by the court messenger and two noblemen is altogether required by law, with a description of the position of the places.

**Translator's Notes**

[T.N. 1] *Buxus* in classical Latin denotes the box tree (*Buxus sempervirens*); *Cis* (spelled *Ciss* here) in Polish denotes the yew (*Taxus baccata*) — a different species. Paciorkowski groups them as a single entry. Both are evergreen trees of similar dense-wooded character; the English rendering "box / yew tree" follows the source's grouping without resolving the botanical discrepancy.

[T.N. 2] *Terebintus* in classical Latin is the terebinth (*Pistacia terebinthus*), a Mediterranean tree not native to Poland. Paciorkowski equates it with Polish *Modrzew* (larch, *Larix decidua*), which is the ecologically appropriate Polish equivalent. The translation "larch" follows the Polish gloss as the operationally intended meaning in this context.

## TRANSLATION GLOSSARY

*Fixed-term renderings used throughout this translation. Latin and Polish originals are given with their English equivalents and contextual definitions.*

| Latin / Polish original | English rendering | Definition in context |
|---|---|---|
| *Succamerarius* | Sub-Chamberlain | The *Podkomorzy*; the principal judicial officer of field boundary proceedings. |
| *Camerarius Granicialis* | Boundary Chamberlain | Deputy to the Sub-Chamberlain; often conducting proceedings in his stead. |
| *Ductus* | boundary path | The path walked, demonstrated, and staked by a party during field proceedings. *Lucravit Ductum*: "won the boundary path." |
| *Actoratus Ductûs* | plaintiff's right of path | The procedural right to lead the first boundary path, awarded by the land court. |
| *Lucrum Actoratûs* | gain of the right of path | See *Actoratus Ductûs*. |
| *Sipatio* / *sipavit* / *sipatus* | erection (of a mound) | The formal act of building and staking a boundary mound. *Sipavit Scopulum*: "erected a mound." |
| *Scopulus angularis* / *acialis* | corner mound | Mound at the meeting point of three or four estates. Equivalent to *kopiec narożny*. |
| *Scopulus parietalis* | wall mound | Mound along the line of a boundary wall between two corner mounds. Equivalent to *kopiec ścienny*. |
| *Acialitas* / *Angularitas* | corner point | The meeting point of three (or four) estate boundary walls at a corner mound. Used interchangeably in the source. |
| *Paries* | boundary wall | The line of boundary mounds between two corner points. Not a physical wall; the "wall" is the line of demarcation. |
| *Terminus a Quo* | starting point | The point from which the boundary path begins. |
| *Terminus per Quem* | intermediate point | Waypoints demonstrated along the boundary path. |
| *Terminus ad Quem* | end point | The terminal corner where the boundary path concludes. |
| *Condescensio* | field session | The Sub-Chamberlain's or commissioners' descent to the field; a formal judicial site hearing. |
| *Remissa* | referral | The order sending a case from the land court to field proceedings, or from field proceedings to a higher court. |
| *Borra* / *bór* | pine forest | Dense pine forest used as a landscape feature in boundary proceedings. |
| *Pæna Bannitionis* | penalty of banishment | Formal outlawry penalty enforceable on the estate. |
| *Innotescentiales* | summons letters | The formal letters by which the Sub-Chamberlain or commissioners summoned parties to the field session. |
| *Ministerialis* | court messenger | The sworn court official who served documents and formally proclaimed sessions open. |
| *Relatio* | service return | The formal endorsed return confirming a document had been served. |
| *Referat* / *referatio* | opening (of a mound) | The judicial act of digging open a mound to inspect its metal markers. *In quo referato*: "in which, when opened." |
| *Signa metallica* / *Signa Granicialia* | metal markers | Materials (charcoal, brick, iron slag, glass, millet in a sealed bottle) placed inside a mound as permanent identifying marks. |
| *Dimoscorium ferreum* | iron slag | One of the standard metal markers placed inside mounds. *Dimoscorium vitreum*: glass slag. |
| *Inquisitio* | sworn inquiry | The formal examination of elderly witnesses on oath as to the historical position of boundaries. |
| *Deliberatio* | adjournment for deliberation | The Sub-Chamberlain's formal decision to reserve judgment; limited by statute to one occasion per case. |
| *Dilatio majoris* | principal postponement | One of the five statutory adjournment types; granted for eight weeks. |
| *Dilatio infirmitatis* | postponement of illness | Adjournment on grounds of genuine illness, proven by oath. |
| *Exemptus militaris* | soldier's exemption | Adjournment type available to those in active military service. |
| *Condictamen* | collusion | A prejudicial agreement between the principal party and a collateral heir, to the detriment of a third estate. |
| *Collateralis* | collateral heir | An owner of an adjacent estate whose corner point adjoins the disputed boundary line; formally summoned as a third party. |
| *Commissio* / *Commissarij* | royal commission / royal commissioners | The special commission issued by the King for boundaries between royal and noble estates. |
| *Capitaneus* | Captain | The holder of a royal estate in the capacity of Captain. Distinct from *Tenutarius*. |
| *Tenutarius* | leaseholder | The holder of a royal estate in the capacity of a leaseholder. Distinct from *Capitaneus*. |
| *Prosecutoriales* | prosecutorial letters | Citations issued to obstruct or unwind a commissarial act; declared void. |
| *Complanatio* | amicable settlement | The voluntary agreement between parties resolving boundary differences without a decree. |
| *Arbitri* / *Superarbiter* | arbitrators / chief arbitrator | Persons registered by the parties in the compromissorial procedure; the Sub-Chamberlain acts as chief arbitrator. |
| *Decretum* | decree | The formal decision of the Sub-Chamberlain or commissioners. |
| *Motio* | motion | A formal challenge or appeal motion. *Movit ad Tribunal*: "moved to the Tribunal." |
| *Avulsio* | avulsion | Land forcibly separated from its principal estate and wrongfully incorporated into another. |
| *Præscriptio* | prescription | Limitation period; in boundary cases, does not run against injured heirs. |
| *Rectilinium* | straight line | The legal requirement that boundary walls run in a straight line between corner points. |
| *Rotham* / *Rotha* | oath formula | The specific wording of the oath sworn at the final corner mound. *Rothisante Ministeriali*: "with the court messenger reading the formula." |
| *Instigator* | Instigator | The Crown's official prosecutor; retained as a title throughout. |
| *Judicium Terrestris* / *sąd ziemski* | land court | The principal first-instance court for hereditary matters; originating forum for boundary cases. |
| *Officium Castrense* / *sąd grodzki* | municipal court office | The subordinate court responsible for execution and enrolment of documents. |
| *Tribunal Regni* | Crown Tribunal | The supreme appellate court of the Kingdom, sitting at Piotrków (*Petricoviæ*). |
| *Odsypisko* | *odsypisko* | Polish term for land accreted by the natural action of a river on one bank at the expense of the other. Retained in italics; no established English equivalent. |
| *Upust* | *upust* | Polish term for a water sluice or mill-gate controlling the release of water from a mill pond or mere. Retained in italics; Paciorkowski himself glosses it as vernacular. |
| *Herburt* | Herburt | Jan Herburt's *Statuta i przywileje koronne*, the principal Polish statutory compilation cited throughout. Given with folio references. |
