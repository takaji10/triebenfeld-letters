Where, however, a natural boundary separates the estates, or a boundary road, the straight line is not observed.

If, however, there are boggy and inaccessible places where passage through them is impossible, or dense forests of great breadth and length, then between such estates ^[*A sworn surveyor is necessary for boggy and inaccessible places.*] a sworn surveyor shall be required to form the straight line artificially from the starting point to the end point, in the presence of the Right Honourable Sub-Chamberlain.

When, moreover, the boundary wall of two estates reaches a river and its bank, and on the other side ^[*The corner point in the vicinity of a river.*] there is a third estate, the Sub-Chamberlain's court must take note of the corner mounds of that third estate, in what place they stand, and toward them, or from the opposite side, it shall form the corner point and erect two mounds on this side of the river where the estates are situated, not crossing the river, and on the other bank a third, on the same estates meeting it collaterally on that bank where they are situated — not, however, all three estates on one bank, because the heir would thereby have usurped both banks of the river for himself, if a mound were situated for him by crossing the river. The laws of the Kingdom contained in this book, under the heading Sub-Chamberlain, ^[*The ancient natural course of the river is to be considered and assigned as the boundary.*] will inform you of the remaining rules regarding rivers and the diversion of rivers and the ancient course to which one ought to have regard.
