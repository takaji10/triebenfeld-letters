# Paciorkowski (1776), p. 43
# translation by Claude

The cited party leading further toward the southern quarter, demonstrated to the court a boundary marker cut in the manner of a cross in a pine tree, and not far from that place, about one furlong away, another marker, similar to the first, cut in a beech tree. Against which demonstration of allegedly boundary markers the Honourable [names], plaintiff, protested, reproducing an official inspection carried out by the court messenger and two noblemen at that place, acknowledged before the municipal records on the [day] of [name] in the year [name], of those same incisions cut unofficially by private authority by the former heir of the village of [name]. Proceeding further and leading southward through the forest, the cited party reached transverse roads — one public road from the town of [name] to the town of [name], another running from the village of [name] to the village of [name] — beside which roads he demonstrated a credible mound; the plaintiff's party denying it, requested its opening; in which, when opened, charcoal was found in smaller quantity, which the plaintiff's party alleged was from roots burned at the time of a fire in that same forest. The cited party continuing its boundary path further, toward a small river or stream flowing from the forests from the west toward the east; crossing it, reached a higher place and a bushy alder thicket, and passing through it in a straight line, half a furlong from that alder thicket, showed the court a hillock, asserting it to be a mound;
