### *Which Cases of Ecclesiastical Persons Fall Under the Terrestrial Law.*

^[*Herburt, fol. 452.*] Ecclesiastical persons, for estates or injuries to estates subject to the common law, shall proceed by the terrestrial law. The injuries are in respect of boundaries, peasants, fugitives, killing, and wounding — otherwise according to the statutes of King John Albert.

### *Judicial Cases Touching upon Land, Personal and Simple, and Postponements of True Illness and for the Principal.*

^[*Constitution 1611, fol. 78, vol. 3.*] In judicial cases that concern land and are called personal and simple, no postponements shall be permitted — except that if ^[*A postponement of illness is granted.*] an oath in such and other cases falls upon someone and is ordered to a party, then through the court messenger of that land, a resident with full authority, a postponement of true illness shall be permitted, both to the plaintiff and to the respondent, until the next sittings.
