# translation by Claude

### *On the Boundaries of Estates that Have Never Been Delimited.*

A boundary question also arises between estates that have never been delimited, having formerly been of one lordship and inheritance. In such a case the Sub-Chamberlain shall consider the starting point (if one can exist) from the corner point and form the boundary wall in a straight line to the end point; if that is lacking, it shall be lawful for the Sub-Chamberlain to reach, with a new boundary wall and newly erected mounds, the neighbouring boundary wall, not crossing it in the slightest, and to form a triangle — but this must be done in the presence of the heir of that boundary wall which he is joining, upon a prior summons of the same; and such boundaries are concluded without an oath. If, however, one party shall gain the boundary path in its entirety, then the party shall at that time establish by oath the thing gained, according to the intent of the statute, with six witnesses of the same rank.
