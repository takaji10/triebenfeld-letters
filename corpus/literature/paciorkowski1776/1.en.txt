### *On the Legal Process in Boundary Cases.*

In boundary cases, according to the ancient institution of the public laws, the legal process is fourfold. The first is the original terrestrial, that is, the process of the land law and the land court. The second is the final executory field process of the Sub-Chamberlain. The third is the arbitral compromissorial process. The fourth is determined by amicable means through an amicable settlement. The terrestrial process is the origin and preparatory stage of the future field process, which most often arises from a case of fact, which is the mother of the boundary case, and is pursued in one of two ways: either in contumacy, or through contested proceedings.

By contumacy: when the respondent, after a penalty of contumacy has been obtained against him at the first session (for actions of this kind are not of the first peremptory session), fails to appear at the second session of the second citation, he is condemned by the plaintiff before the land court in the gain of the plaintiff's right of path, and immediately ^[*After the gain of the right of path has been obtained, a referral to the field.*]
