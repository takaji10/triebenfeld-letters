# Paciorkowski (1776), p. 14
# translation by Claude

Now, if a river by its natural course diverts itself from one bank to the other, causing loss to one and gain to another, by means of what is called ^[*Odsypisko.*] *odsypisko*, each heir must be content with his own bank. [T.N. 1]

It often happens that the heir of one estate, ^[*On collusion between the parties.*] raising a boundary question against his neighbour, having entered into collusion with the collateral heir, acknowledging a corner point mutually to one another in a particular place, to the prejudice of a third estate — and this occurs most commonly when the mounds are not visible and the parties are wanting in proofs, or the position of the place where the corner point should be established is open to interpretation and explanation — in such case the Sub-Chamberlain's court must take firm notice of the injury to the third estate, and review the boundary wall of the collateral heir marked with mounds, not formally, but only for its own information, as to whether or not the straight line meets at this place where it acknowledges the corner point to the neighbouring estate; and if collusion is alleged against the collateral heir, he must then clear himself by oath, on the ground that he proceeded with no collusion with the party. If, however, the collateral heir shall prove the extension of the boundary wall in a straight line or by his own mounds to the place which he acknowledges to the party, then the collusion objected was unwarranted and the sworn discharge is already less necessary.
