# Paciorkowski (1776) [= Lochowski 1641], p. 38
# translation by Claude

the second wall mound, a hundred paces from the opened one, not particularly notable; the court likewise ordered it to be opened, in which, when opened, nothing was found save stones mixed with earth, found to be of poor quality. Proceeding further through the same pine forest, the leading party reached a boggy place of difficult passage, through which, with the path prepared and laid with timber, and crossing on foot, it reached a dry elevated place, at which place it demonstrated to the court a mound taken for a wall mound, whose opening the plaintiff's leading party requested; granting the application therefor, it ordered the same to be opened; in which, when opened, were found charcoal, fragments of brick, and iron slag, similar to what had been found in the prior mound. From which opened mound in a straight line toward the south, through the same dense pine forest grown with various kinds of trees, there follows a small river or torrent flowing from the west toward the east; crossing it by means of planks prepared for the crossing, it reached a public road running from the town of [name] to the village of [name], beside which public road it demonstrated to the court a wall mound sufficiently notable, having roundness and a circle, whose opening is less necessary since it is visible. From that mound, crossing the road by a straight path toward the south, a mound was found of similar roundness and height, immediately beside the aforesaid road, at a hundred paces
