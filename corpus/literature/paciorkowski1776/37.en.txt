# Paciorkowski (1776), p. 37
# translation by Claude

the aforesaid village of [name], began and commenced his boundary path from the place of the founded jurisdiction and from the three corner mounds demonstrated to the court and proved by the boundary act written above, or from their midpoint; he led the court in a straight line from the northern quarter toward the south (or from the east toward the west, or between the western and southern quarters, toward the eastern and northern quarter, as may be the case). And he demonstrated to the court a wall mound of lesser note, standing not far from the corner mounds, namely at eighty paces distance; against which demonstration the Honourable [names], originally cited, protests, alleging that it is not a wall mound but a hillock raised by worms, containing no roundness or circle; the leading party counter-protesting regarding the wrongful denial of that wall mound as sufficiently notable; and in verification thereof demanded immediate opening of that mound, granting the application therefor, he ordered the same mound to be opened; in which mound, when opened, in the presence of the cited party, boundary markers were found, namely fragments of brick, charcoal of no small quantity, and some fragments of iron slag. Proceeding further from it toward the same southern quarter as straight as possible through the dense pine forest, with the path prepared and cleared, he demonstrated to his court
