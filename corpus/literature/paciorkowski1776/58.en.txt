# Paciorkowski (1776), p. 58
# translation by Claude

protested against the Right Honourable [names] regarding the wrongful usurpation of one half of his mere, hereditary from antiquity and belonging to his village of [name], and proving it by a certain division made between the former co-heir brothers, drawn up in writing at [name] and confirmed before the municipal records of [name] on the [day] of [name] in the year [name]; and also regarding the demonstration of the boundary path along that same small river taken as the natural boundary, since both banks belong to his estate. The Right Honourable plaintiff counter-protesting against the aforesaid protest of the cited party — because he, on the basis of positive law, in the absence of boundaries and mounds between those same estates, took the natural boundary for himself — and proceeding further along the same torrent flowing from the sluice toward the east, going more than one part of a mile, he led the court to the corner mounds or particular corner mounds not far from that same small river, almost one furlong from its bank, in a more elevated place, erected and situated by the Sub-Chamberlain's court in the year [name] pursuant to the intent of a Tribunal decree, sufficiently notable, having the greatest roundness and a circle; at which place and around those same corner and particular corner mounds he concluded and determined his boundary path.
