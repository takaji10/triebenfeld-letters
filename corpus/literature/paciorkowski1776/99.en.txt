The plaintiff's boundary path having been reviewed and faithfully noted by the commissarial court, it proceeded to the review of the Right Honourable Captain's boundary path. And ^[*The Right Honourable Captain's boundary path.*] first he commenced his boundary path from this corner point or particular corner point, acknowledged by the Honourable [names], collateral heir, and proceeded toward the north from the south, and reached a oak forest called *Dąbrowa*, and there beside a road not much frequented running from the village of [name] to the village of [name], demonstrated to the court a hillock, alleging it to be the first wall mound, almost three furlongs from that same corner point; the plaintiff's party requested its opening; in which, when opened, nothing was found save what appears to be mixed earth. From that mound, proceeding in a straight line through that same oak forest, he reached transverse roads — one running from the town of [name] to the village of [name], the other from the village of [name] to the village of [name] — between which roads he showed the court a hillock in the manner of a mound; in which, when opened, nothing was found.
