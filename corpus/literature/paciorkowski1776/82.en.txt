# Paciorkowski (1776), p. 82
# translation by Claude

Before the form and model of a commissarial act — in respect of the boundaries between the estates of His Sacred Royal Majesty on the one part and the hereditary terrestrial estates of the nobility on the other, erected or to be erected — is set forth, the difference, in particular how far royal commissions differ from the termination or delimitation of estates on both sides terrestrial, explained above, must first be considered; which may readily be gathered from the notes below.

And first, that for a future commissarial act, the referral of the land court to the field — which in the process described above, between estates on both sides terrestrial, is laid as its foundation — is not necessary; indeed, instead of the referral, a royal commission or instrument of commission must be obtained from His Sacred Royal Majesty, from the Chancery of the Kingdom, directed to certain ^[*Five commissioners are to be present together with the Sub-Chamberlain.*] principal persons — two of the dignitary order, two of the terrestrial officials, and a fifth of the Sub-Chamberlain or Boundary Chamberlain of whatever palatinate or land they may most readily be engaged — (who, on account of the absence of one or two, are customarily named in greater number) for the purpose of the delimitation of estates and the erection of mounds; which commission may also be issued outside the borders of the Kingdom, together
