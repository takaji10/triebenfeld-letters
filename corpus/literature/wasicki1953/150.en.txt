Rather generally and cautiously, but at any rate it was stressed, that such changes ought to bring with them "a change in the relations between lords and peasants". These principles were to be put into practice, for example, by the Economic Society of Międzyrzecz, founded in 1802, and the Prussian authorities reckoned with the possibility of extending the network of similar societies over the whole area of South Prussia[^45]. Beside the towns, colonies of artisans and day labourers were to arise, needed for work in the productive enterprises developing in this period and relying on free hired labour. It cannot be said that particularly numerous industrial enterprises arose in South Prussia in this period; on the contrary, a gradual decline of those existing until then can be observed, as a result of the competition of other Prussian provinces where manufacture was highly developed, as for example in Silesia; the guidelines of the General Directory, however, amounted only to general principles of colonisation, without taking account in such cases of particular local considerations.

Unlike the earlier instructions issued to the chambers, this one ordered the officials to give the arriving settlers proper care[^46]. When, however, it happened that arriving colonists did not wish to settle in the place set aside for them, as was the case with 12 Württemberg families, minister Voss ordered the chambers to leave them to their own fate, without troubling about them further[^47].

Before we proceed to discuss the next stage of the colonisation of the Polish lands of the Prussian partition, lasting from 1800 until the defeat at Jena, we must sum up, however briefly, its results so far. Reliable data for this period, checked by us against archival source material, are given by A. Holsche, but only for the Poznań department[^48].

In all, 13 colonies were founded in this department by 1800, in which 109 families of colonists were settled, among them 32 families of day labourers (Tagelöhner). Reckoning 5 persons to a family, about 700 persons in all were therefore settled. These figures, however, do not agree with the list Holsche gives, so that only the list, not the totals, must be taken as the basis. The colonists were distributed in the following colonies:
