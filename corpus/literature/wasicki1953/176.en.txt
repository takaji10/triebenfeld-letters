From the contract of perpetual lease of the land that was concluded it followed that they were free and bound only to very limited services to the manor and certain dues in kind. After the expiry of four free years they were also bound to pay a rent: in two instalments — at Christmas and at St John's — of 30 thalers a year for each Magdeburg łan cleared and cultivated. For the teacher, the sołtys and the cemetery 21 morgen were allotted[^86]. The colonists came mostly from the Mark and Pomerania. We have no exact information on how long the German villages and colonies founded there lasted. Breyer reports that already in 1806 two families of colonists asked to be allowed to leave Mały Las and move to East Prussia, and in 1818 forty families from the village of Święcia came forward with the same request. The villages founded thus did not last long[^87].

We also have information that Count Łubieński founded 11 colonies in his starosty of Guzów, settling 167 colonists, for which he was decorated by Frederick William III with the Order of the Red Eagle[^88]. Konstanty Milewski, too, owner of the Borszewice estates in the district of Lutomiersk, concluded a contract with 10 colonists from the Netze district and allotted them 10 łany of forest[^89]. Attempts by the lessees of royal estates to settle cottager colonists did not succeed, since both the lessees themselves and the settlers, who found worse conditions there, were unwilling about this action because of the considerable money costs. The attempts at settlement ended most often in failure[^90].

2. Urban colonisation

Parallel with the conduct of rural colonisation, efforts were made to settle colonists in the towns. From the first instruction, however, it appeared clearly that what was meant was not to attract specialists for the further development of the manufacturing industry existing in Greater Poland, but only specialists in building, such as bricklayers, carpenters and the like. This by no means means that the settling of other arriving artisans was abandoned, but this came about when a fairly considerable number of newcomers from the left bank of the Rhine flowed into South Prussia.

Already in 1793, immediately after the entry of the Prussian troops, when the action of organising the new new province was in full swing, the chamber in Piotrków came forward with a proposal to settle in the towns colonists from the branch of crafts mentioned above, the lack of whom was particularly felt at that time[^91].
