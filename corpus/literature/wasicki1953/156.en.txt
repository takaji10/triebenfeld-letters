The property requirements were by no means given up or lowered; only this small change was introduced, that the colonist had to possess half his means in cash, while the other half he could supply within two to four years; this sum was to be secured by a judicial mortgage on the land received from the state, and its possession had to be proved by credible documents. A horse was counted into the colonist's cash means, at 54 guilders or 84 thal. each. The area of land assigned was decided, as before, by the amount of means possessed. According to these criteria the colonists were divided into six classes.

To the first class were assigned day labourers and cottagers (Tagelöhner und Häusler). The basic livelihood of this category of colonists was to be work on the larger agricultural holdings (ländliche Wirtschaften); it was not, however, explained exactly whether nobles' manor farms or royal estates were meant. In practice they were settled both on the royal domains and beside the larger colonies and near the towns, where they could find employment. This category of settlers was to receive only as much land as was necessary to satisfy their essential needs, on the assumption that the main source of livelihood would be wage work. Its area therefore varied between 4 and 6 morgen, and that again according to the quality of the soil. The means required of this category of settlers amounted to from 50 to 300 guilders (Reichsgeld), or from 27 thal. 12 gr. to 162 thal. 12 gr. Prussian[^69]. On settling they were to receive:

1\. 4 to 6 morgen of land in perpetual lease, paying after the free years an appropriate canon.

2\. Ownership of a dwelling house, with the obligation to keep it in proper repair.

3\. Travel money (Meilengeld) at the rate of 2 gr. a mile for each member of the family and each person coming with him.

4\. 3 to 6 free years (Freijahre) according to the kind of land received (land already cultivated, fallows, land requiring clearing).

5\. Reimbursement of the costs of clearing.

6\. The tools needed for the farm[^70].

7\. Livestock according to the amount of land received[^71].

8\. Exemption from the cantons for colonists and their sons.

[^68]: „... Die ländlichen Kolonisten müssen Kornbauer oder Korn- und Weinbauer zugleich, nicht aber letzteres allein sein ...“ [The rural colonists must be grain farmers, or grain and wine farmers at once, but not the latter alone].

[^69]: In later instructions it was stressed that possession of means could not be rigorously required of this category of settlers, provided the colonist produced a certificate attesting his industry. S. P. Z. vol. XV, fol. 192, of 2 May 1803.

[^70]: According to the instruction of 6 August 1802, colonists assigned to this category received the following farm tools: a spade, an axe, a rake, scoops, a dung fork, a wheelbarrow, a scythe, a spinning wheel, flails, a cabbage knife, a Flachsstock, a Flachshebel, and four thalers for buying garden seeds. S. P. Z. vol. XIV, fol. 159.

[^71]: Two cows, 1 pig, 4 geese, 4 hens and a cock.
