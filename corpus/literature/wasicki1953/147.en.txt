The instruction also speaks of teachers, who were to come to these lands together with the colonists. They were to receive 2 morgen of land and timber for building. The community, on the other hand, was to give them a suitable allowance in kind and pay them a fixed sum of money. Herdsmen were to be settled on similar terms.

In 1799, carrying out the royal command to conduct the colonising action on a broader scale, by recruiting settlers from the Reich more vigorously than until then, ministers Voss and Schrötter, and especially the latter, set about acting more energetically. Minister Schrötter thought it fitting to send a special commissioner to southern Germany to carry on recruiting among the population there. He intended, after some time, to use the settlers thus won for further recruiting in the Reich. Commissioner Weiss, chosen by minister Schrötter for this task, set out in May of that year for Württemberg, Baden, Hesse-Nassau and the Palatinate, in order to attract "very sturdy and well-to-do colonists". The order of the General Directory on this matter, giving the reasons why this was the right moment for recruiting settlers, read:

> "Advantage must be taken of the present circumstances, which will lead to emigration many an upright and well-to-do inhabitant of those parts who would never have thought of leaving his homeland, and the opportunity must be used to have a shrewd cameralist on the spot choose the most suitable and best material and persuade it to emigrate. All this is most important for attracting foreign colonists and must be used as zealously as possible. Weiss should therefore receive an instruction on the conditions on which and the colonists whom he is to engage for the West and East Prussian department, and especially how far hired labourers and gardeners are to be considered. Perhaps the owners of estates too could use such colonists"[^38].

In the principality of Nassau Weiss found a fairly favourable reception from the authorities there, and many willing to settle in the new provinces, who did not, however, possess the means required for this[^39]. He did not fare so well in Württemberg, because of the hostile attitude at that time of the authorities there to Frederick William III's colonising undertakings. Besides, the imperial armies withdrawing from Switzerland did not allow the action begun by Weiss to be carried on further. On his second stay in Nassau he could already see that the unfavourable reception of the first settlers from this province in South Prussia, who had returned to Nassau, had discouraged others from leaving. Commissioner Weiss returned from his journey almost empty-handed, because few candidates were found for settlement in the new Prussian provinces. But this was not the only cause of the failure. For, as appears from Weiss's report, the concessions provided in the instruction for colonists were too small in relation to the relatively high property requirements[^40].
