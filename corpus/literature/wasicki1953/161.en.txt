# Wąsicki (1953), p. 161
# translation by Claude

For the years 1805 and 1806 there are no detailed data. Nor does I. Rhode cite them, who used archival materials from this period in her work. Probably, however, the number of colonists settled in the other departments of South Prussia could not have been higher than that reached in the previous years, if not lower. It may therefore be supposed that in all about 6000 persons were settled in South Prussia in the years 1793—1806. The figure of 10,000 persons cited by Voss presumably covers the rural colonisation carried on by the state and colonisation in the towns. The overall figure of 10,000 persons settled in South Prussia, however, cannot be accepted, the best proof of which is the table cited, reflecting the results of the colonising action in the Poznań department.

For comparison we may cite that in New East Prussia in the period from 1795 to 1807, 600 families, that is 3507 persons, were settled.

Great difficulties also arose in establishing the number of newly founded colonies. Voss's report mentions that 2135 "établissements" were set up.

The list attached below, drawn up on the basis of archival materials, shows the number of colonies founded, settlements by villages and on manor farms in the department of the Poznań chamber up to 1804 (see p. 162 ff.).

For the Kalisz and Warsaw departments we draw the data from the work of A. Pytlak, who gives, it is true, the names of the places where colonists were settled, but does not give the number of colonists settled nor the area of land allotted. We cite here only the general list of colonies, villages and settlements that arose in this period, or of villages already existing enlarged by new settlers. Unfortunately we were not able to check their reliability, the more so since these are later data, coming from the time of the Congress Kingdom, though relating to the period that concerns us.
