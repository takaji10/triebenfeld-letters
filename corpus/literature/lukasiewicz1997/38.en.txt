These observations of the traveller were confirmed by the reports of Prussian officials, published by Rudolf Stadelmann, Rodgero Prümers, Max Lehmann, Peter Granier, Werner Hubatsch and Ingeborg Bussenius[^37].

Minister Hoym in September 1794 reported that the Polish lustrations of the present Prussian domains were deflated tenfold, while Buchholtz wrote of a twofold deflation. From this, in his opinion, arose the resistance of the clergy to passing onto a state pension amounting to 50% of the revenues declared by the leaseholders and the clergy for tax purposes. The Detaxation Commission, which set to work later, could henceforth act only for higher life pensions for those removed from the estates[^38]. In consequence the state's revenues from the domains under Prussian rule rose sharply to 1806. The mortgage valuation of the noble estates, at which it was in the nobility's interest that the valuation be correct and even inflated, showed the Prussian authorities how far deflated the tax valuations were. According to the contribution of 1793, which still took the Polish 10-percent tax rate, the value of the noble estates would have to stand at 27 million thalers[^39].

In 1805 Holsche, on the basis of a similar reckoning, indicated that from the mortgage valuations it follows that the contribution is deflated twofold[^40]. At the close of the nineteenth century similar conclusions were drawn by Christian Mayer[^41]. Later the same conclusions were reached by Roman Rybarski and Władysław Rusiński, only with reference to the last years of Poland before the partitions, which however does not matter to the extent that it relates to one and the same classification of the "Offiary" of 1790. Thus from different sides, by laborious effort, one arrived at the discovery already made by the sejm Equalisation Commission, which after the analysis of 1311 sale-purchase transactions assessed the tax declarations as deflating the real noble revenues by half[^42]. A glaring example of such frauds in the South Prussian times was pointed out by Janina Leskiewiczowa for the wilanów estate, where from the manor accounts for the year 1800/1801 there followed a "net revenue" of 90 000 złp., while from the tax declaration only 18 000 złp., which gave a contribution of 4 339 złp.[^43]
