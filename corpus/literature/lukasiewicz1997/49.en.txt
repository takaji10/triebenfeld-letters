# lukasiewicz1997  49  en
# Łukasiewicz (1997), p. 49
# translation by Claude

If the mortgage credit taken by the Prussian donataries was — as we have recalled — squandered, like the money taken by the Polish nobility for speculation and consumption, then, as Danuta Rzepniewska rightly pointed out in her monograph on the economy of Mazovia in the time of South Prussia, Feliks Łubieński was one of the glorious exceptions. The money taken on mortgage he invested and modernised the husbandry. It must however be mentioned that Łubieński's basic investment was the bringing in of 187 Protestant house-farmers, colonists from East Prussia, and that Łubieński earned for this with Frederick William III the Order of the Red Eagle in 1805 and the title of count, which he received in 1798[^97].

Mixed up in the giveaway of estates were also many other Poles. In the documentation there appear the landrat Potworowski[^98], Józef Chłapowski[^99], Józef Drzewicki, Michał Sokołowski[^100]. The list from the Zamoyski Archive states that the president of Berlin Eisenberg sold his donation to one Chełmski for 40 000. If we recall that among the donataries figured Prince Antoni Radziwiłł[^101], Prince Antoni Czetwertyński[^102], Count Łuba and Dzierzbicki, that the Marquis Lucchesini resold his donation to Prince Jabłonowski for 150 000, (and Jabłonowski obtained from it a judicial valuation of 500 000)[^103], it must become clear that in many cases the Prussian donataries unscrupulously resold the estates to Poles as soon as they got from them an advantageous price. Secret councillor Baumann resold the estate of Lubrzec to Count Kwilecki of Dobrojewo; councillor Unruh gave the starosty of Babimost in perpetual lease to the hitherto starosta of Babimost, Count Łukasz Bniński[^104]. Minister Hoym exchanged Guzów[^105], with which he had endowed himself, for the estates of Łubieński — Szczytniki[^106] and Kalinowo, near Kalisz; the Master of the Horse Krakwitz resold his estates to the landrat Potworowski of the district of Kościan. In turn Count Łuba resold the rights of use of his donation (the status of a lease for 44 years) to Biernacka, the daughter of General von Bischofswerder, then married for a short time to Count Gurowski[^107].
