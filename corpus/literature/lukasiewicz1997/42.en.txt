The central Prussian authorities assessed the phenomenon in such a way that on the one side stand the producers inclined to speculation, on the other the consumers, afflicted by the prices rising artificially through speculation. This was however to be a self-driving mechanism, brought about by other factors too. Thus the producers, from the wish for gain, hold back the grain, waiting for a rise in prices — for the same reasons the buyers try to buy it up as quickly as possible. The royal order speaks of the necessity of increasing the interventionist purchases of grain into the magazines, there where its greatest shortages occur[^55]. The Councillor of the Regency Juliusz Schwartz (b. 1759) — the organiser of the South Prussian mortgage, at the same time a satirist and editor of the „Südpreußische Zeitung" — rightly predicted already in 1802 that harmful speculation in estates and the winding up of the conjuncture of prices could lead to catastrophe, when the price of grain falls or when the government is forced to block its export[^56].

This came about after 1806. In 1809, however, the deputy of Konin Bielski explained the motives of speculation. Already at the threshold of Prussian rule many of the nobility foretold that the founding of the mortgage and the opening of cheap credit would automatically cause a rise in the price of land — it must therefore be bought and credits taken, since the rise in prices, with a premium, will even out the payment of interest[^57].

The corrupt aspect of the giveaway of estates

Likewise the giveaway of estates itself could in no way answer the intended aims. It quickly became obvious that events had gone in quite the opposite direction, and the new owners — chosen not according to economic reason but according to Hoym's comradely preferences — treated the estates acquired at half price as a source of easy enrichment. This fact also aroused particular indignation among the remaining part of the Prussian officials — Hans von Held and Joseph Zerboni di Sposetti[^58] were connected with the minister Karl August von Struensee[^59] and the people gathered around him (Schulenburg-Kehnert, Stein, Schrötter), with whom the young heir to the throne sympathised, the future Frederick William III.[^60]
