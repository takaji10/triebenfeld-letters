That means that the net revenue of an estate, after the deduction of the costs of husbandry, makes 5% of the value of that estate. Thus Held assessed the value of councillor Beyer's estate at 160 000 thalers on the basis of an income of 8 000 thalers; General Blücher's estates yielded 6 000 thalers a year and were therefore to be worth 120 000 thalers. In a similar way the donations were assessed of Major Hünerbein, Prince Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, Marshal Keyserlingk and the Master of the Horse Krackwitz[^26]. This assumption was received both in the Commonwealth and in Prussia, and then also in the Duchy of Warsaw and still in the Congress Kingdom. It made an essential premise for tax settlements, and then for assessments of the mortgaged value of estates[^27]. The tax sources have however this undeniable advantage, that they are the result of the fiscal endeavours of the state, so there can be no question here of a deliberate (on the part of the state itself) deflation of the value of the estate, as was the case with the nominal price of the donation. We possess the most diversified material for the department of Poznań, where the nominal value of the estates was 1 025 250 thalers, while from the tax returns there follows a value of 1 638 000 thalers, that is a sum almost twice as high. Nevertheless it is at the same time a magnitude twice as low as the one given by Held on the title page of the "Black Register" — the "true" value of these estates, that is 3 million thalers[^28]. That true value of the estates was either the price of their sale, the sum of the lease, or the income from which Held in the way we have described computed the value of the estate. In this way, after counting the estates of all the departments, we obtain the sum of 7 036 160 thalers. It is an approximate magnitude, because in the case of some estates the author of the list entered the description "insignificant" or "value unknown". Such items are however barely four and cannot have a significant influence on the magnitude of the sum of the values of the estates given away. Somewhat greater is the sum of the values of the estates that follows from the Polish list, for it comes to 7 243 675 thalers. It should further be added that the "Black Register" has not 52 donataries, as its title page declares and as is usually said, but only 48; there are, however, 52 donations, because Treskow, Hohenlohe, Lüttichau and Wendessen appear twice — in different departments. The number of estates given away amounts, if every separate item is counted, to 245, not 241.

[^26]: Materialien zur Geschichte polnischer Landestheile unter preußischer Verwaltung, H. 1, Leipzig 1861 [below: Materialen], s. 141-142, 144-146.

[^27]: The rate of 5% was applied in assessing the national income of the Prussian state first in L. Krug, Annalen der Preußischen Staatswirtschaft und Statistik, Bd. 1, H. 1, Halle und Leipzig 1804, s. 4; in idem, Betrachtungen über den Nationalreichtum der preußischen Staates, Bd. 1, Berlin 1805, a profitability rate of 4% was already adopted, s. 100, 101, 360. Later Ch. Mayer, op. cit., s. 329-330, applying this method, computed from the mortgaged value of estates their incomes, from which he inferred a very considerable deflation of the receipts from the contribution. The "Gazeta Południowo-Pruska", e.g. Nr 89 of 5 XI 1796, Nr 10 of 4 II 1797, in advertisements of the sale of an estate gave its value on the basis of income in the same way. For this way of proceeding of the Equalisation Commission see T. Korzon, Wewnętrzne dzieje Polski za Stanisława Augusta (1764-1794), Kraków-Warszawa 1897, t. 3, s. 21-22, 236, 334. Similar principles for Western Pomerania see W. Stępiński, Własność junkierska na Pomorzu Zachodnim w latach 1807-1914, Szczecin 1989, t. 2, s. 346-388; F. Skarbek, Ogólne zasady nauki gospodarstwa narodowego, Warszawa 1926, t. 2, s. 129-130 (1st ed. 1825). Finally the same assessment of the value of the Napoleonic donations on the basis of revenue.

[^28]: Because of the summary setting together of the estates of Treskow, Hohenlohe and Lüttichau, it was necessary to complete these values from the tax registers and by estimation. These are however insignificant completions.
