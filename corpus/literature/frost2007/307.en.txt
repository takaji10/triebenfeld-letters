Kula's portrayal of the magnate economy as a kind of perpetuum mobile should be viewed with some scepticism. No magnate producer was ever close to achieving this theoretical ideal; all had to be heavily involved as purchasers in both local and international markets. Despite high grain production, the Zamoyski entail could only meet between one-fifth and one-third of the demand for grain for the production of beer and the seven kinds of vodka it distilled; purchases from its own peasants made up some of the shortfall, but grain – especially barley – and honey had to be bought from outside. Kasperek, Gospodarka folwarczna, pp. 114–19. The production of seven kinds of vodka suggests that the peasant market was a discerning one.
