5. Summary

The transformation of the native landscape goes on constantly and inevitably, and in recent years we have seen it grow stronger. The rebuilding and modernisation of the village threatens to lose its valuable individual character for the sake of particular universal values, general and often alien, not only in our landscape but even beside other newly built architecture.

The knowledge of the features of traditional architecture, of its relation to the landscape and of their mutual influence, must be gathered and preserved. In the long term this may help to create a “style”, that is, a so-called “school of regional architecture.” A contemporary architecture must arise, suited to the present needs of the inhabitants but growing out of the native building tradition.

In the areas discussed the interdependence of the buildings and the natural environment is plain. The pattern of settlement is closely bound up with the historically formed ways of farming the land: in the classic pattern, manor house – manor farm – farmhands’ cottages – fields, and in the modern one of the Olęder villages and of those founded in the nineteenth century, houses in farmsteads – fields – woods among the fields.

It must be understood that it depends on the inhabitants of the region, who are the builders and the owners of old and new houses, what our surroundings will look like. A very great deal will depend on their decisions about architecture, about the detail of the composition of the landscape, and even about the decoration of buildings.

This should not, however, lead to a kind of artificial open-air-museum treatment of the native architecture: a creative approach to design is needed, one that takes account of regional features, native materials and the proportions of regional buildings.

And what future generations inherit will depend on the inhabitants of the region!
