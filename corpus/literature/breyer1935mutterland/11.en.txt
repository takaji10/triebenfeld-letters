From his entries of the couples married in the period 1795 to 1815 we take the following places of origin: of 298, the Silesian dialect area has 192, the Pomeranian dialect area 36, East Prussia 14, the rest of the Reich 36. That gives 69.3 per cent Silesians, 13 per cent Pomeranians, 5 per cent East Prussians and 13 per cent from the rest of the Reich. Among the Silesian towns Zduny stands first with 30 immigrants; there follow Breslau with 14, Ostrowo 12, Groß-Wartenberg 11, Rawitsch and Militsch 7 each, Kempen, Suhlau and Namslau 5, Festenberg, Krotoschin, Guhrau and Oels 4. From Bromberg came 8 persons, from Saxony 20, from Württemberg 8. By trade there were: bakers 17, joiners and masons 15 each, tailors 12, carpenters 9, hatters and shoemakers 8 each, windmillers and coachmen 6 each, merchants 5, saddlers and butchers 4 each, doctors, teachers, printers and soap-boilers 3 each, hairdressers, glaziers, girdlers, rope-makers, innkeepers, turners, confectioners, gardeners, wheelwrights and coopers 2 each. Of trades certainly new to the town life of that time may be named: a maker of sealing wax, a glove-maker, a stuff-maker, a fulling-miller, a maker of musical instruments, a bookbinder, a dyer, a furrier.

If the border town of Kalisch naturally received the strongest increase of German settlers from the neighbouring Silesian areas, at the county town of *Gostynin*, in the north of Congress Poland on the left bank of the Vistula, we find above all an inflow from the neighbouring Low German area. Here cloth-makers were settled from 1821 to 1828. Beside the old town a new cloth-makers' settlement grew up. On the strength of the notes of origin in the church books of the Lutheran congregation at Gostynin for the period 1825 to 1850, and of the records in the happily preserved guild books of the German cloth-makers' guild, it follows: of 88 statements of place, 68 or 78 in a hundred fall to the Netze district, the remaining 22 in a hundred to the Silesian areas. From the separate cloth-making towns of the Netze district there came: Radolin 15, Samotschin 13, Labischin 11, Schönlanke 9, neighbouring Jastrow 7, Margonin 4, Zielenzig 4, Lobsens and Rogasen 3 each, Wirsitz, Althütte, Kolmar, Ritschenwalde 1 each. From Bojanowo, Rawitsch, Breslau, Schwiebus, Schwersentz, Palmburg, Neudeck, Landeck and Schulitz came one cloth-maker each. In the first German cloth-makers' town in central Poland, in *Dombie*, 61 per cent of the cloth-makers came from the Netze district, 39 per cent from Silesia.
