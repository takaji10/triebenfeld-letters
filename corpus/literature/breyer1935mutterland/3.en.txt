# breyer1935mutterland  3  en
# Breyer (1935b), p. 3
# translation by Claude

Little preliminary work exists in this field. The inquiry must therefore be built on the *sources*. These come into question:

> 1. the church books and archives in the Lutheran congregations of the area in question, in the German superintendencies and in the Consistory at Warsaw;

> 2. the main features of the dialect (Wenker's sentences);

> 3. comparison of the first names and family names;

> 4. the way the houses are built, the way of farming;

> 5. hymn books and books of sermons;

> 6. folklore.

Sometimes the form of the village and of its fields allows a conclusion about the stock and the origin of the inhabitants. Beside these general sources there are special ones for particular groups of Germans. While for the village the *Schulzenlade* (the headman's chest of village papers) in most cases supplies first-class material on the question of origin, in the town its place is taken by the *Innungsladen* (the guild chests) of the German guilds, and often by old papers in the possession of families, passports, certificates and the like.

Questioning the old people succeeded only in the rarest cases. Among the country people, who have mostly lived for 200 to 300 years outside the borders of Germany among the surrounding Polish people (Umvolk), the memory of the old homeland has almost entirely vanished. A praiseworthy exception in this respect is the Swabians settled in the Prussian period, which is partly to be ascribed to their greater intelligence.

Mapping the places of origin found sometimes met great difficulties, especially for the small villages and hamlets. In many cases neither old nor new gazetteers helped. Old topographical maps sometimes gave excellent help, but so did newer maps on a large scale.
