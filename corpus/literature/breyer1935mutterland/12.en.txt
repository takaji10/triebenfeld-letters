tries in the church books of the congregations of Zduńska Wola, Konstantynow, Brzeziny and Pabjanice give, on the whole, the same picture. In the *northern* towns of central Poland the cloth-makers from the Netze district are in the majority, in those lying further *south* those from Silesia. On the strength of the 685 place names gathered so far, the statements from Ozorkow and in part from Tomaschow being unfortunately still missing, it follows that *66 in a hundred of the immigrant cloth-makers came from Silesia and 34 in a hundred from the Netze district*.

With the cloth-makers, German craftsmen of every kind also appeared in the newly laid-out cloth-making towns. Manifold personal and economic ties meant that they left their old home towns at the same time and sought new places of work together.

In the time that followed a strong regrouping took place in some cloth-making towns. Many have lost all their inhabitants by migration to the great industrial centres of the Łódź area, such as Dombie, Przedecz, Chodecz and Poddębice. The surrounding German villages, too, gave up their surplus population to the rising industrial towns. Their make-up by stock was thereby hardly changed much, since west and south of Łódź, Pabjanice and Zgierz the village population is mainly Silesian, while east of Łódź and south of Ozorkow live farmers of Pomeranian stock. New elements among the urban Germans are the proletarianised *Swabians*, who immigrated from the colonies founded in the Prussian period and in the decades after. Some towns, especially Tomaschow and Łódź, also have immigrants from *Bielitz* and Biala, mostly cloth-makers and weavers. So the Tomaschow cloth manufacturer Piesch is a man of Bielitz, and others.

Now something more about the origin of the *Lutheran clergy* in central Poland. The first German Lutheran congregation in central Poland is *Wengrow*, founded in 1650 by Prince Radziwiłł, who was himself a Protestant. A series of pastors from Greater Poland, such as *Tyräus, Rosentreter, Bachstrom*, served this congregation, lying lonely and much cut off, in the first century of its existence.

After the death of the first pastor of the newly founded *Warsaw* congregation, Johann Jakob *Scheidemantel*, a Silesian by birth, Pastor Gottlieb *Ringeltaube* was elected; he came from Gramtschen near Thorn, where his father was the Lutheran preacher. The afternoon preacher Friedrich *Cerulli*, whom the congregation had elected in 1779, came from Königsberg.
