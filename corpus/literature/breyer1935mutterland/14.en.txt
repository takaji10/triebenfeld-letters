# breyer1935mutterland  14  en
# Breyer (1935b), p. 14
# translation by Claude

The immigration of German Lutheran cloth-makers, weavers and other "Professionisten" (tradesmen) between 1818 and 1828, which led to the founding of eleven Lutheran parishes, also brought a number of Lutheran clergymen into the country, in most cases young candidates of theology. Of the pastors appointed at that time, seven called the Silesian lands of their stock their home: Pastor *Benni* from Lissa, whose descendants were well-known linguists, *Biedermann* from Zduny, *Gerbling* and *Rother* from Breslau, *Hayn* from Oels, *Rüdiger* from Schawone and *Sachs* from Rawitsch. The fathers of some were cloth-makers. Pastors *Modl*, *Berkau* and *Jaeckel* were from Posen, Thorn and Filehne respectively. Of the pastors who came into the country after 1830: *Stiller* from Rawitsch [Rawisch], a cloth-maker's son, *Wilhelm* from Birnbaum, a pastor's son, *Jeute* from Bojanowo, a cloth-maker's son, *Kattein* from Beuthen, a burgher's son, *Hinz* from Wilhelmsburg in East Prussia, where his father was superintendent, *Sray* from Postelwitz in Silesia. After 1840 these were newly appointed: Pastor *Häßner*, born in Hermersdorf near Görlitz, *Jarnecki* from Klein-Ellgut near Oels; *Cotta* from Winzig in Silesia; *Modl* from Herrnstadt in Silesia; *Menzmann* from Langenau in Prussia; *Kruschwitz* from Jaulitz in Lusatia. Pastor *Lemon* was born in Posen, the son of a privy councillor; he is the last in time of the pastors who immigrated into Poland from the neighbouring Prussian areas. He worked in Ozorkow from 1880 to 1910.

After 1850 sons of the native German burghers and craftsmen were already choosing to study theology. In the last decades of the past century the Russian government made insuperable difficulties over confirming foreign pastors. In the Warsaw Lutheran consistorial district there was a great shortage of clergymen before the World War, which can be regarded as remedied only today, now that the Warsaw theological faculty has been working at full stretch.

In sum we state that in the period 1780 to 1850 41 pastors immigrated into central Poland from Silesia and the Posen country. If we add the pastors whose fathers came into the country as Prussian officials, namely Schwarz, Hermes, Lembke and Börner, the number comes to 45. To these brave German Lutheran men the founding and building up of the Lutheran church and school system is owed without reserve. This, unfortunately, is all too often knowingly passed over in silence by the present Lutheran clergy.
