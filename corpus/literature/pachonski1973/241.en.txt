# Pachoński (1973), p. 241
# translation by Claude

Resolutely republican opinions, and even the wish to model Poland's future régime on revolutionary France, were represented by the former left of the Insurrection, led by Hugo Kołłontay and General Joseph Zajączek (Zaïonchek). But this faction was not united either. The situation in France was judged with some severity by the castellan D. Mniewski, who financed this camp, and by Generals Denis Kołyszko, François Wyszkowski and Michel Zagórski: in their view not all the French revolutionary ideas suited Poland. François Dmochowski, General François Łaźniński and Major Clement Liberadzki[^12], on the other hand, were won over by the French revolutionary régime.

Making contact with the minister Lallement

In the second half of December 1794 the closer watch of the Venetian police alarmed the Poles[^13]. Fearing complications, they sent a delegation to the French Embassy made up of S. Sołtyk, M. Ogiński and F. Dmochowski[^14]. The French ambassador at Venice at the time was Lallement, a man of about sixty[^15]. He knew the situation in Italy very well, having spent some thirty years in diplomatic posts at Ragusa, Messina and Naples.
