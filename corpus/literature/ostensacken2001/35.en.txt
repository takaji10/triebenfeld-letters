The collegiate foundation at Oehringen later became the starting point for a lordship reaching beyond the Hohenlohe territory, joined with the comital rights in the northern Kochergau and the right of high jurisdiction.[^p35-7] In 1418 the sole-reigning Albrecht von Hohenlohe received the sovereign territorial supremacy, after King Sigismund had granted him unrestricted jurisdiction at the Council of Constance. The lords of Hohenlohe were raised to the rank of count by Emperor Frederick III in connection with claims of inheritance to the counties of Nidda and Ziegenhain. In these regions, remote for the house of Hohenlohe at the time, it could not indeed hold its ground against the landgraves of Hesse, yet the comital title remained to it, which for the consolidation of the territory immediately surrounding it mattered in so far as it helped to stabilise and extend the territorial supremacy in the Hohenlohe heartland.[^p35-8]

Toward the Reformation, which elsewhere had not only split the faithful into two camps but had also become a matter of politics, the house of Hohenlohe behaved rather hesitantly and wait-and-see. An impulse to take "sides" came first from a petition of the town of Oehringen directed against the frivolous goings-on of the canons.[^p35-9] With the church order of 1553 the Reformation was sealed. Two years later the counts Ludwig Casimir and Eberhard resolved the partition of the land into the two main lines Hohenlohe-Neuenstein and Hohenlohe-Waldenburg. In the course of the seventeenth century further splittings of the land followed from rules of inheritance, and with them the arising of numerous side lines. From the main line of Neuenstein the lines of Langenburg, later Oehringen and Weikersheim, branched off in 1610. From the line of Langenburg there formed in 1701 the lines of Ingelfingen and Kirchberg. The main line Hohenlohe-Waldenburg divided once more in 1688 into the lines of Schillingsfürst, Bartenstein and Jagstberg. Already in October 1667 the sons of Count Georg Friedrich II of the line Waldenburg-Schillingsfürst, Count Christian (1627–1675), progenitor of the house of Bartenstein, and Count Ludwig Gustav (1634–1697), founder of the house of Schillingsfürst, had returned to the Catholic faith.[^p35-10] Confessional differences and notorious disputes of inheritance weighed on the relations of the various lines the more also because the Catholic counts had already been raised to the rank of prince in 1744, while the counts of the Neuenstein line were raised only in 1764.[^p35-11]
