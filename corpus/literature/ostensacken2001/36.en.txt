Though branded by many of his peers as a betrayal of emperor and empire, he had seen to it that Hohenlohe was included in the Peace of Basel. The neutrality of Hohenlohe prevented not only the danger of becoming a theatre of war; the prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen moreover achieved that it was freed from the payment of 300,000 livres of contributions, which would have been laid upon the principality of the Franconian circle of the Empire. Nonetheless the quarterings of Austrian and French troops beginning in 1798 weighed heavily on the budget of Hohenlohe[^p36-13].

Even before his marriage with the rich heiress Marianne Countess of Hoym the prince's finances had been in disorder. Friedrich Ludwig zu Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen cherished the pious belief that with the revenues of the estates lying in Upper Silesia he could set new projects on the way in his heartland. After the peace congress of Rastatt he tried to attach to the Hohenlohe territory the lands of the monastery of Schöntal, the Teutonic Order's district around Mergentheim and parts of the Palatinate as well as of the archbishopric of Mainz. After a then following new ordering of the German Empire he would have wished for himself the dignity of a duke of Franconia.[^p36-14] But of this nothing came, on account of the political development. Still, Friedrich Ludwig von Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen had in his heartland been able to streamline the house authorities by the creation of a central instance and to renew the police administration. To the economy he sought to help with the building up and extension of commercial undertakings. Thus in 1794 he had conceived the plan of founding a joint-stock company for Hohenlohe coal mining. As the diggings brought no hard coal to light, this undertaking had to be dropped, as had the reactivation of the princely salt works at Weißbach, existing still from before the Thirty Years War. Encroaching Kocher floodwater and the influence of carbonic acid gas had prevented a profitable operation.[^p36-15] Profit from these undertakings had been drawn above all by Hofrat Glenk as his adviser, who had counselled him to invest ever larger sums and to demand a high salary for himself, which was also granted him. The deficit that arose, which still in 1809 amounted to the enormous sum of 400,000 gulden, had later to be made good by his son and heir, August.[^p36-16] Realised
