If she could not render him harmless out of her own strength, she would have to have recourse to the help of the courts and withdraw from him the right of co-administration of the Silesian and Saxon possessions. The princess did indeed succeed in inducing the prince to renounce his rights of administration, but perhaps too late, for over the amount of the total mortgages the prince had taken up on his possessions she had for a long time no exact knowledge. The debts now weighed altogether on the son and heir alone, Friedrich August Carl zu Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen-Oehringen, to whom his father, shortly before the mediatisation, had ceded the Hohenlohe heartland and with it the likewise considerable liabilities.

The daughter of the princess Sacken, Marianne, had after eighteen years of marriage with the prince drawn her personal conclusions.[^p41-36] She separated from her husband in 1799 - and this was especially hard for her - also from her children, filed for divorce, which then took place judicially in 1801 at her wish.[^p41-37] Over the reasons that had moved her to this step she remained silent however at first toward her mother, so that a severe rupture came about between the two women. The princess Sacken reacted to her daughter's wish for divorce with complete lack of understanding and thereupon compelled her, in a house regulation drawn up on 9 November 1799 in Berlin, the so-called "Zransact", to renounce the sole right of inheritance due to her under the testamentary disposition of count Hoym in favour of the grandson August. Only later did Marianne zu Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen give her sons, prince August and prince Adolph von Hohenlohe, full knowledge of the primary motives that had induced her to part from her husband.[^p41-38] As compensation for the complete renunciation of the inheritance Marianne zu Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen received yearly 4,000 thalers from the Silesian possessions and 3,000 thalers from her mother, the princess Sacken.[^p41-39] After the marriage of Marianne with the prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen was divorced on 1 September 1801, she married yet in the same year the count Friedrich Ludwig von der Osten-Sacken, with whom she moved to his estate of Bellin in Mecklenburg. From this marriage two more daughters came forth: Angelika Friederike Natalie Louise, born on 4 May 1802, and another girl who received the names Auguste Friederike Wilhelmine Charlotte and came into the world on 5 September 1804.[^p41-40] Her last years of life Marianne countess of Osten-Sacken spent on the Marienhof near Rostock, where she died on 20 April 1840.[^p41-41]
