as a brilliant officer surrounded by the aureole of courage, he had met in the capital a rich and young widow of eminent virtues, Michalina née **Prusimska Ostroróg** [printed "Prasimskich"], Countess Dąmbska, wife of Józef, daughter of Antoni, starosta of Nieszczewice, and of Maryanna Dąmbska of the Godziemba arms,[^1] daughter of the castellan of Kowal (see vol. I p. 59; vol. VII p. 188), and having won her heart, he married her in August 1809. The marriage settlement — by which Michalina née Prusimska, Countess Dąmbska, wife of Józef, assigns to her future husband all the estates named above, while Count Stanisław secures to his future wife, out of the sum of 100,000 złoty of his share after his mother, already received, 90,000 złoty secured on the estates of Count Ignacy Miączyński, starosta of Kamieniopol, and the remaining 10,000 złoty placed on the property of Count Józef Ossoliński, his stepfather, and promises to settle on his wife the rest of his mother's share too after his mother's death, in all a sum of 280,000 złoty — was signed by: Stanisław Count Miączyński; Michalina Dąmbska née Prusimska; in the name of his wife, Teresa née Rafałowicz, by her first marriage Miączyńska, Count Józef Ossoliński; Józef Nowicki, colonel; Adam Broniec; Countess Agnieszka Skarbek née Dąmbska; Walenty Skorochód Majewski, notary; and so on.

The couple lived very happily, now on the Trąbczyn estates in the Kalisz country, now at Pawłowo near Kiszkowo in the Grand Duchy of Posen, where the trees planted by Michalina's hand still form a splendid park. Four sons brightened the evening of the brave Napoleonic veteran's life. When his health failed he went to Berlin for medical advice. There, feeling his strength ebbing, he disposed of his property as follows: to the eldest, Aleksander, he left the Pawłowo estates; to the second, Włodzimierz, then living in exile, capital; to the third, Mieczysław, already the heir of Wieniec in the Kingdom of Poland, he gave, in the form of a sale, the Trąbczyn estates and the village of Szetlewek in the Kalisz country; and lastly to the youngest, Witold, also in the form of a sale, the estate of Brzeź with its appurtenances in the district of Brześć Kujawski. — Count Stanisław's widow ended her life on 19 January 1851 on her estate of Trąbczyn, and was buried there beside her husband. The "Kuryer Warszawski" of the time (no. 65, of 9 March) gave her the following notice: "The late Countess Michalina Miączyńska, having begun the spring of her life in brilliant times in the great world, educated without conceit, beautiful without vanity, had the right to love the bustle of the world, which received her as its ornament; but a mother's feeling showed her another road. Choosing a quiet retreat on her native soil, she spent there a peaceful life, given to the duties of a model wife and model mother. After the loss of her husband the late Mrs Miączyńska redoubled her efforts, for her lofty heart told her to be a mother and to take the place of the dead father. Sparing in her own expenses, sparing in all the pleasures of life, she was generous in holding out a hand to suffering humanity, generous in helping her neighbour. A model citizen, a guardian of the peasants worthy to be followed, a kind neighbour and mistress of her house, she held to the last moments of her life to the principles and virtues that had adorned her from her youth."

1\. Count **Aleksander** (12th generation), *officer of 1831, knight of the golden cross Virtuti militari*, heir of the Pawłowo estates near Kiszkowo (8,500 Magdeburg morgen), recognised in the hereditary title of count of the Holy Roman Empire by the Prussian government by ministerial rescript of 5 May 1853, born 9 January 1810; as a young man he joined the national ranks in 1831 and, as a second lieutenant, received for bravery shown in various battles the golden cross Virtuti militari by order of the commander-in-chief of 15 September of that year. Forced to leave the country and go abroad, he wandered long through Germany, Switzerland and France, until at last in 1844 he received leave to return to the Grand Duchy of Posen and settled on the family estate of Pawłowo.
