Probably as early as about 1770 the Borowieckie Stare Ol. also arose on the same estates, founded by an unknown charter issued by Józef Wężyk[^66]. This is confirmed by a deed of purchase of land of 1770, signed by the sołtys and the assessors of this commune[^67]. According to the table of hearths of 1775 this settlement, described as "Olędry Borowiec i Huta", had 29 farms[^68]. The inventory of the properties of Grodziec and Królików of 1779 found 27 farmers living in the Borowieckie Ol., named with their first names and surnames (4 of them Polish), on holdings that differed in area[^69]. The existence of this settlement was confirmed by Wężyk's son-in-law and heir, Count Franciszek Stadnicki, also already mentioned, in the joint foundation charter for the Konarskie Ol. and for them of 25 August 1785, founding at the same time, it seems, the Borowieckie Nowe Ol. This charter contains the statement: *All these points and descriptions set out above, in keeping with the customary Dutch laws, the whole settlement of the Borowieckie Holendry most solemnly promises, and is bound, to observe*. The settlers, who were then settled in an unknown number, were Lutherans and Catholics by confession[^70] (Ill. 9).

Before and in 1771, and in 1791, the Grądy Stare and Nowe Ol. were also founded on the same property. The former were probably founded by Józef Wężyk himself[^71]. Their founding was confirmed, and extended to the Grądy Nw. Ol. as well, by Count Franciszek Stadnicki in an emphyteutic-perpetual foundation charter of 1791, meant for *the Olęders on the Grądy, who have already paid the grontgielt*; he declared that he did so *...to increase the population and multiply the intrats from useless woods, moved by the request of free incomers, partly already settled and partly still to settle...*, that is with a motive like the one he gave in the charters for the Konarskie Ol. and the Białobłockie Ol. (Ill. 10). The settlers, Catholics and Protestant Lutherans (judging from the mention of a little school and of the obligations to the parish priest of the Catholic parish church of Grodziec), were settled in the forest, and received probably 7 free years. This charter stated that: *The farmers newly settled not long ago on poorer lands* [probably in the Grądy Nw. Ol. – Z.Ch.] *after their rudowanie* [clearing], *when they are recognised as such by the commune office and the manor's jurisdiction* [authority], would have relief in their dues to the manor[^72] (Ill. 11).
