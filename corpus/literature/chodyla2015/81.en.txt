The area of a włóka measured with a rod of 7½ ells comes to 16.80 (more exactly 16.7962) ha, with one of 8 ells to 19.20, with one of 8½ ells to 21.60, and with one of 9 ells to as much as 24.20 ha. Exceptionally, measures of area other (at least in name) than the Chełmno włóka were used in Olęder settlements. One Warsaw ell as a measure of area, in turn, came to 0.354666 m². Land was sometimes measured in *ślady* identical with the Chełmno włóka (huba), but also sometimes equal to almost or exactly two such włóki (that is, of 50–60 Chełmno morgen). In isolated cases the sources mention land being measured with the Magdeburg rod, but there is no information on how many ells it contained. In the second half of the eighteenth century, in the districts of Kalisz, Konin and Pyzdry, land was often measured in the "rydlandska" measure, that is probably the Rhenish (the name probably comes from the word Reihnland), with a rod of 7½ ells. The Rhenish rod, from 1816 also called the Magdeburg rod (and so probably the same as the one mentioned earlier) or the Prussian rod (equal to about 3.766 m), was the measure generally in force (by a royal order of 1750) and the most widely used in Prussia, and from 1775 it was in force in all the provinces of the country except Silesia and the Polish lands newly annexed in the First Partition. In settlements founded with settlers coming from Silesia, especially up to 1740, holdings were also measured, at the settlers' wish, with the Imperial rod, probably equal to 8½ ells[^130].

In the district of Pyzdry, then, the area can be grasped for only 3 of the 5 Olęder settlements.

In the Ciążeńskie or Zarzeczne Ol., in the Ciążeń key of the estates of the bishopric of Poznań, 4 settlers received 3 włóki in all, probably of the Chełmno measure, so that on average each of them would have had a farm of 22½ morgen[^131].

In the royal-town Pyzdrskie Olędry, 19 settlers settled "on the forests of the town of Pyzdry" held holdings of 1 huba each, probably of the Crown measure, that is measured with a rod of 7½ ells (1 rod = 4 m 46 cm) by a surveyor brought in at their cost[^132]. So the area of the whole village was at least 19 włóki.

In the two settlements in the starosty of Pietrzyków, Wrąbczynek and the Wrąbczynowskie Olędry, the settlers received in 1781 more than 21½ włóki of arable land and meadows in all. In the first settlement they obtained 11 hubas [1 huba = 30 morgen] and 16 morgen of land *wyrudowany*, that is cleared for meadows, and one of them one and a half morgen; in the second settlement 9½ hubas.
