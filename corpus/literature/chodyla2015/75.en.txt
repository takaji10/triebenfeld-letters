This order in time of the founding of the Olęder settlements is not wholly clear-cut, however, since some of them were re-founded or had further settlers brought in during different sub-periods.

3) The cause of the founding of the settlements for the feudal founders was the wish to increase their income by settling and bringing into use the reserves of land they held, especially woodland not settled until then, or settled but long deserted, depopulated and overgrown again with forest, and, exceptionally, land that was ready, already tilled. The settling of Olęders, who answered jointly, in accordance with one of the basic principles of Olęder law, for paying money rents to the lords, assured these of a steady and regular income. This is also shown, indirectly, by the founding of some Olęder settlements on usurped land, that is land that was the subject of lawsuits between the owners of neighbouring estates. For the Olęders the motive for settling were favourable legal and economic conditions of life, far better than in the country and the place of their origin or their earlier residence in Poland, and the assurance of religious toleration and freedom of worship, guaranteed to the settlers in the foundation charters in accordance with the principles of Olęder law, which will be discussed in detail below. The foundation charters for the settlements in the Pyzdry Forest (apart from five, for Wielołęka, the Białobłockie, Konarskie, Grądy St. and Nw. and Gadowskie St. and Nw. Ol.) do not speak directly of these mutual economic advantages, since they were, so to speak, obvious.

4\. The character of the land on which Olęder settlements were founded in the Pyzdry Forest in the years 1746-1793 varied. Most of them were founded on wooded land or on land that had become overgrown again. In the light of the source information given above, at least 37 of the 58 settlements were founded on woodland. Of these, at least 27 Olęder villages were founded on raw root, 5 were founded on the site of settlements or parts of settlements that had existed earlier under the same names, even since the Middle Ages, and had been deserted and overgrown with forest at the end of the sixteenth and the beginning of the seventeenth century, and 5 on land partly tilled and ready, 1 of them (the Wrąbczynkowskie Ol.) also on the lands of a demesne farm.
