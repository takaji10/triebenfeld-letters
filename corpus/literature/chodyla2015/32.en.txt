Ol. of 1785, drawn up when they were handed over into the possession of Józef Wielowiejski by Ignacy Zaremba as guardian of the under-age Zarembas, names 22 Olęders with German names, 7 of whom had 3-6 free years left[^42]. In 1789, 25 hearths were recorded in Poroże Wielkie Ol., and in 1790, 37 hearths, that is farms, in the Przespolewskie Ol. in all, 12 more than in 1789; their number grew because further settlers were brought in[^43].

After 1754, and certainly before 1789 or by 1793, Łazy (Olędry) arose in the district of Konin, in the parish and on the noble estate of Trąbczyn, probably founded by those already mentioned above: Katarzyna née Rozdrażewska Prusimska, widow, or Antoni Prusimski, starost of Niszczewice[^44]. In the sources of the Old Polish period (as, for instance, in the inspection of the Trąbczyn estate of 1754) they are recorded without the addition *Olędry*, with which they appear only in the Prussian register of houses and population of 1796, which, however, gives them the same number of houses/hearths as the table of "self-tilling", that is farming, hearths of 1791. In 1791 they were inhabited by Catholics, 48 persons in all[^45]. It seems that, as an Olęder settlement, they arose through the transfer of their existing inhabitants, who had owed labour service, to Olęder law.

After 1765, and before 1793, the settlement of the Lisewskie Ol. (Olsz) was founded in the starosty and district of Pyzdry, in the parish of Szymanowice (whose centre lay in the district of Kalisz), by Michał Drogosław Skórzewski, then starost of Pyzdry in the years 1751-1789 (Ill. 5). Its founding can be linked, indirectly, with the conversion to rent of the village of Lisewo, until then a labour-service village, carried out after 1751 by the same starost.
