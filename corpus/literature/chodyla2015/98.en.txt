**Key: M – male, Ż – female, K – women, married (widows), Ch. – boys, Dz. – girls, kat. – Catholics, [ ] – estimated number.**

This table shows that in the 5 Olęder villages founded in the district of Pyzdry, within the Pyzdry Forest, 499 persons in all lived in the years 1789-1791. So the average population per settlement would have been 99.8 persons. For 3 of these settlements the demographic and social and occupational structure is known. Of the total of 449 inhabitants of the three settlements attested directly in the sources, 234 persons were male and 215 female. By age and marital status, 177 persons were adults, counted among husbands and wives and widowers and widows. On the other hand, 272 persons can be counted as children and young people, 146 of them male and 126 female. These figures are open to doubt, however, because of the number of sons over 15 (38), undoubtedly understated in order to protect them from conscription into the army, and also because the age limit is not stated for the daughters. By social and occupational structure, of the 449 inhabitants of these villages 90 were counted as farmers, 218 as cottagers paying rent, 9 as lodgers, 4 as craftsmen, 21 as farm servants, 53 as *luźni*, that is hired workers, and 54 in the category of other persons, among whom were counted teachers, but also persons described as "beggars", cripples and others. The total populations of two settlements, Wrąbczynek and the Wrąbczynkowskie Ol., from two sources of 1789 and 1791, differ by 99 persons. According to the first source 309 persons lived in the two villages, and according to the second (a year later) only 210. The second of these sources also gives the confessional composition of the population of the two Olęder villages. Of the total of 210 persons, 111 (in Wrąbczynek) were exclusively Catholics, and 99 (in the Wrąbczynkowskie Ol.) exclusively dissenters (Protestants).
