# Chodyła (2015), p. 146
# translation by Claude

As for spiritual culture, it can be said that the inhabitants of the Olęder settlements in the Pyzdry Forest were guaranteed religious freedom and freedom of public worship in the form proper to their religion. The place where it was practised in this period, in every village inhabited by Protestants, was the school-house of prayer, and the services were led, to a basic extent, by the teachers (readers) who taught the children in them, especially the basics of religion, and prepared them for confirmation. The Olęder dissenters (Protestants) also had their own burial grounds, whose remains have survived to this day in practically all the villages in which they lived. The culture of daily life of the people of the Olęder settlements was made up of their housing (the dwelling houses and their furnishings), work, clothing, food, and the customs and manners connected with their production, division and use. People dressed according to the customs brought from their places of origin, differently – as is obvious – on weekdays and on feast days, in clothes chiefly of their own making, of linen and sheep's wool, but partly also sewn from cloth bought in the towns and little towns. The food of the people of the Olęder settlements that arose in the Pyzdry Forest did not differ fundamentally from that of other settlements and was in proportion to their prosperity. They ate rye bread and other grain or flour dishes, groats, vegetables, butter, cheese, from time to time meat, in Lent probably herrings and other fish, which the Olęders had the right to catch in the waters adjoining their land, and honey from their own apiaries, and fruit; they drank beer, then general, and also milk. Various manifestations of the Olęders' material and spiritual culture showed differences, even after a century, compared with those known for the Polish rural population. This followed from the separateness of language, the religious separation and the unwillingness to assimilate on the Olęders' side, and also from differences in the mentality of the two sides. Nevertheless there were some contacts between them, and some practices were taken over or imitated, especially in farming and stock-raising, in which the Olęders led.

Summing up this outline of the earliest history of the Olęder settlements in the Pyzdry Forest, it must be said that, because of the number of these settlements and the detail of some of the source information on some questions, together with its absence on other matters, and the limited size of this study, it was impossible to create an even, full and exhaustive picture of it.

In appreciating the contribution of Olęder settlement to the development of the network of settlements and to the social and economic development of the area, it should be seen in the context of rural settlement as a whole, not only that of its own time but also earlier and later. For in the years 1794/1796-1863 further changes took place in the settlement structure of the area of the Pyzdry Forest. In this period at least 28 settlements were founded. Of these, at least 17 Olęder settlements arose by the 1840s, and by the beginning of the 1860s 11 other rent-paying settlements, among them such as Święcia, Olchowo, Emilienheim, that is Oleśnica, Michalinów, Tarszewo, Grądzeń Las, Mały Las, Chruściki, Grądzeń, Drzewce, Kopojno, Oleśnica Kolonia, Oleśnica Wielka and Zarzew, in the then communes of Dąbroszyn, Oleśnica and Dłusk.
