# chodyla2015  111  en
# Chodyła (2015), p. 111
# translation by Claude

In the Oborskie Stare Ol., or Wronów, and the Nowe Ol., which arose in 1783 and before 1788 in the district of Kalisz, Protestants, Germans, also or exclusively lived[^229].

In the settlements of the Orlińskie (Orlina) Małe and Duże Olędry, in the district of Konin, 19 settlers settled in 1784 (most with Polish names), Germans and Poles, Catholics and Lutherans. They included among others: Bogusław Krol, Michał Arent, Franciszek Maciejowski, Maciej Walczak, Maciej Jarmuszkiewicz, Wawrzyniec Kośmicki, Micha Wynt, Krystian Tenus, Johannn Samuel Weychnert(?), Franciszek Organiściak, Grzegorz Gojak (?) with their families. In 1790 there were 29 Catholics and 36 Protestants[^230].

In the Lisieckie Ol., in the starosty of Konin, in the Lisiec key (founded in 1783-1784 and before 1789), lived Germans, dissenters (20 in number in 1791)[^231], perhaps of the Reformed confession.

The inhabitants of Stara Huta – the Grójeckie Olędry (founded perhaps as early as about 1777, and certainly in the years 1785 and 1789), in the same district, were Germans and probably also Poles, Catholics, 51 persons in 1790[^232].

In the Siąszyckie or Złotowskie Olędry, which arose probably about 1786, and certainly before 1790, one of the two settlers known from the sources, Łukasz Łyszkowski, was an emigrant who had come back from abroad[^233]. Both settlers were probably Catholics.

In the Mycielińskie Stare and Nowe Olędry (today Kazala St. and Nw.), founded in the years 1786-1788 in the district of Kalisz, in the parish of Kościelec, on the noble estate of Mycielin, whose locators were Jakub Tonka and Godfryd Legner, 6 settlers in all settled (1 with a German name, 3-4 with Polish names and 1 whose name is unknown), most probably Protestants, since they were to receive land for a school[^234].

In the Stropieszyńskie, or Radzany and Gostynie, Olędry, in the district of Kalisz (founded in the years 1787 and 1791), the settlers were Germans and Protestant Lutherans. They were expressly described as Germans in the foundation charter. Their nationality is also confirmed, indirectly, by a separate charter for the honest Marcin Werner and his successors for the Bugaj inn, which lay on the Kalisz road, binding him, should the property be sold, to present as purchaser a man who knew the Polish language. The Protestant confession of the inhabitants of these settlements is shown by the assignment in the foundation charter of 6 XII 1787 of land for the *szkolny* (the teacher). The foundation charter of 1791, which at the same time confirmed and supplemented the first, also added to the commune the married couple Józef and Elżbieta Gierecki, manor servants, settled under a separate charter on ½ huba of ploughland [most probably Catholics and Poles]. In granting the Olęders at the same time land for a school and a cemetery, the charter confirmed their German nationality and Protestant confession[^235].
