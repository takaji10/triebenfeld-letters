To meet this challenge the Local Tourist Organisation "Puszcza Pyzdrska" was founded as early as 2012, a coalition of all the local forces and authorities with an interest in building the identity and the tourist potential of this large area. The Board of the Landscape Parks of the Greater Poland Voivodeship also took an active part in the revival of the Pyzdry Forest. Since 2014 the two institutions have together been carrying out the plan of an Ecomuseum of the Warta Valley and the Pyzdry Forest. Its purpose is to protect the natural, cultural and landscape values of the Nadwarciański Landscape Park and the Pyzdry Forest, treated as one region, and to make them accessible to residents and visitors. The Ecomuseum is a museum without staff, walls or display cases. It is a network of sites scattered across the land, which together form a collection showing the attractions of the region. It takes in monuments, valuable natural sites, traditional farmsteads, craft workshops and pieces of tourist infrastructure. By an Ecomuseum is meant an active, not a passive, way of saving the whole natural, cultural and landscape heritage, as three inseparable parts.

The publication "Zarys najstarszych dziejów osad olęderskich w Puszczy Pyzdrskiej (1746-1793)" by Dr Zbigniew Chodyła fits perfectly into the educational work carried on among the inhabitants of the Ecomuseum. It is the first publication on the history of the founding and the workings of the 58 Olęder villages of the Pyzdry Forest, based chiefly on archival material not known until now. This and other educational work will aim to strengthen among the people of the forest a sense of pride in living in a remarkable region, and this will make them more responsible for the state of the local heritage. The goal is an attractive tourist product centred on outstanding natural attractions (the unregulated river Warta, rare species of birds, the natural landscape, traditional farming) and cultural ones (old settlements, archaeological sites, "ghost" places, a style of architecture of its own, folklore, battlefields, cemeteries, regional shops and cooking) found within one geographically coherent area. All these attractions must be well described and marked on the ground and attractively promoted. But it is not worth waiting until the Ecomuseum is fully realised. I invite you now to immerse yourselves in the Pyzdry Forest. Following the trail of the Olęders by bicycle, we will discover a world full of secrets, until recently condemned to oblivion. We will come across abandoned fields and farmsteads, or only traces of them, such as old pear trees, chestnuts or lilac bushes. In secluded places we will come upon mysterious Protestant cemeteries. Paddling a canoe down the Warta or the Prosna we can admire the beauty of these rivers and the richness of their plants and animals.

President of the Local Tourist Organisation "Puszcza Pyzdrska"
