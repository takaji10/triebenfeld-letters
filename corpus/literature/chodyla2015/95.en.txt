# Chodyła (2015), p. 95
# translation by Claude

At the beginning of the clearing of wooded and overgrown land, the inhabitants of the Olęder settlements put up temporary huts (*budy*) to live in, and then, still within the free years, they were bound to put up cottages. Huts are mentioned in the foundation charters and other sources for the Cekowskie St. Ol., the Przespolewskie Ol., Świdle Nowa Kolonia and the Trąbczyńskie St. Ol.[^192]. The charters for some settlements stated that the settlers were to put up their buildings before their free years ran out (Gadowskie St. Ol.), within a set time of two or four years (Białobłockie Ol., Orlińskie D. and M., Lubieńskie Ol.), properly (Cekowskie Nw. Ol.), as quickly as possible, properly and "on a line" (that is keeping to the layout of a street village), in the first case under the supervision of the sołtys and assessor (Grądy St. and Nw. Ol.), within a set time of several years under threat of inspection and a money fine (Grądy St. and Nw. Ol., Borowieckie St. and Nw. and Konarskie Ol.[^193]). Such orders arose from the dawdling or laziness, or the limited means for investment, of some Olęders, as the case of the Trąbczyńskie St. and Nw. Ol. confirms[^194]. The area of the land assigned for the homestead, that is for the buildings of a farm, was stated only once, in the charter for the Mycielińskie Ol., at 1 morgen (Magdeburg?)[^195].

In the buildings and layout of the Olęder villages in the Pyzdry Forest, most of which were wholly or partly inhabited by people of the Protestant confession, important elements were the buildings for worship that were also educational (schools – houses of prayer, later churches), and the burial grounds, which lay on the whole on the edges of the villages, on hilly, wooded or poor land, and which will be discussed more fully in the last chapter of this study.
