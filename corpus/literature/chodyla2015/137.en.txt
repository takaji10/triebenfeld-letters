# Chodyła (2015), p. 137
# translation by Claude

The composition of their inhabitants by confession was not – as was shown above – uniform. That the greater part of the inhabitants of the settlements belonged to Protestantism is suggested by the information on the character and manner of the payment of dues to the Catholic Church, set out above, and by the confirmation of the existence of schools – houses of prayer and/or their teachers – and of cemeteries, in the form of their endowment with plots of land, in the charters and a few other sources (inventories, inspections).

The sources also speak of schools/teachers for 29 settlements, namely: Wrąbczynek and the Wrąbczynkowskie Ol. (a common one)[^343], the Białobłockie and the Orlińskie D. and M. Ol. (a common one)[^344], the Borowieckie and the Konarskie Ol. (a common one)[^345], the Gadowskie St. and Nw. Ol.[^346], the Grądy St. and Nw. Ol.[^347], the Trąbczyńskie St. and Nw.[^348], the Wielołęka Ol.[^349], the Cekowskie St. Ol. and the Cekowskie Nw. Ol.[^350], the Dębniałki and Piskory Ol. (a common one)[^351], the Dzierzbińskie – Zamęty and Zdanowiec Ol. (separate schools and teachers)[^352], the Kosmowskie[^353], the Mycielińskie St. and Nw. Ol.[^354], the Plewińskie St. and Nw. Ol.[^355], the Przespolewskie Ol.[^356], the Stropieszyńskie or Radzany and Gostynie Olędry[^357] and the Zbierskie Ol.[^358].

The teacher in the school was a person employed specially for the purpose or (as often happened) one of the inhabitants of the village who could read and write. His duties were to teach the children the catechism, to prepare them for confirmation at the age of 14, and to teach them the basics of reading, writing and arithmetic. The children of the Protestant settlers went to the schools, and until the First World War teaching was in German. The children of the Polish Catholic settlers were brought into schooling only at the beginning of the twentieth century.

Schooling lasted at first only two years and, as was general at the time, only in winter and early spring, because the children were used for herding cattle and for field work. Besides, because he could write and read, the teacher was at the service of the settlement's self-government. At the same time the teacher performed religious services to a basic extent (he led the Sunday services and funerals). Sunday services were held not only in the Protestant churches but also in the schools or in chapels built beside the schools, as for example in the chapels still standing today
