# Chodyła (2015), p. 88
# translation by Claude

The number and sizes of the holdings (farms) in 37 of the 58 Olęder settlements founded in the Pyzdry Forest, as they stood at the founding and sometimes also later, do not give a full statistical picture of their size at one time common to them all. This can be created only from the data of the tables of hearths and of hearth tax of 1789, drawn up in the course of the first census of population in Poland and the second in Europe and checked in 1790, and from the tables of farming hearths of 1791, drawn up in connection with the grain levy that the Diet voted on them. Since these are sometimes given jointly for one or even more settlements belonging to the same landed estate, and are sometimes even missing, their state in some settlements has to be established indirectly and by estimate (resorting to other sources, in the form of information from the foundation charters, the tables of hearths and hearth tax of 1775 (with suitable corrections), the survey of the royal estates of 1789, and the inventories and revisions of church estates of the years 1791-1793). Moreover, detailed tables of hearths, which make it possible to grasp their functional-economic structure, survive only for some of the Olęder settlements that concern us here, those in the districts of Pyzdry and Kalisz. For the district of Konin they have to be established indirectly from the table of the hearth tax of 1789 or the table of the grain levy of 1791. Before we proceed, however, to set out the numbers of hearths in general and of farming hearths of all the Olęder settlements in the Pyzdry Forest, taken from the sources named or estimated on the basis of them, and to interpret their numbers and in part the structure mentioned, some explanations are needed.

First, it should be said that in the census tables (protocols, sheets) of hearths of 1789, by a hearth in general (*dym*), treated as the unit of account of the tax called the hearth tax (*podymne*), was understood, in the countryside but also in the towns, above all, but not only, a farm with its dwelling and farm buildings, or, more exactly, a farmstead. For this term covered *cottages in the village and in the futory* [outlying farmsteads] and (or) *distant from the village – with one farmer*, but sometimes also *with two farmers*, and also the *garden* (*ogrodowe*) – smallholders' – ones (*with land and garden or without land and garden*), *craftsmen's, manufacturing, and manor* and *church hearths, mills* (water and wind), *breweries, inns* and *Jewish hearths*. The manor and parsonage buildings, on the other hand, of which there were none in the purely Olęder villages, and which in the mixed ones belonged legally to their labour-service parts and not to their Olęder rent-paying parts, were *counted as one hearth*[^184].

Second, it should be said that by the Diet's resolution, in accordance with the text of the constitution of the Diet of 1791, every hearth whose holder or inhabitant sowed land with grain, regardless of the nature of the estate and of whether he paid the offering of 10 or 20 gr., was to be considered a farming hearth subject to the grain levy for the army (in the amount of 2 korzec of rye and 4 korzec of oats). Because of the differences in the size of their land, farming hearths were allowed to be burdened with the tax in different amounts,

[^184]: W. Rusiński, *Struktura osadnictwa...*, p. 59. The notion of the hearth-farm did not always mean the same, however, as is shown by the "village cottages with two or more farmers"; such farms, or rather in this case farmsteads with 2 farmers, were, however, relatively few..
