# Chodyła (2015), p. 63
# translation by Claude

In 1783 two settlements were founded by one charter in the district of Kalisz, in the parish of Brudzew and on the estate of Lipe: the Piskory Ol., founded in the Lipe wood on the land of Bi[e]lawy, and Dębniałki, or the Bolimowskie or Brudzewskie Olędry, founded "*on the Brudzew land called Rosocha and Dębianki* [!]", with separate sołtyses and assessors. In the charter, issued at Tymianka on 17 VII 1783, only 3 are named (2 with German names and 1 with a Polish name), Catholics and Lutherans *...who ...have settled and will settle*; they took up the Lipe wood and forest and meadows, *ground long useless*, which needed *rudunek*, and accordingly received 7 free years counted from the time each settler settled; there were, however, more settlers (6 Olęders received free years to 1791 and 2 to 1792). The dissenters were also to receive plots for a burial ground and for a school, common to both settlements. The issuers of the charter and founders of both settlements were Wawrzyniec Antoni Łodzia Czarnecki, *stolnik* of the district of Inowłódz, deputy castle starost of the voivodeship of Łęczyca, owner of the little town of Stryków with the villages of Smolice Górne and Dolne, Lipe, Cesarka, Dobra and Kiełmina in the voivodeship of Łęczyca, district of Brzeziny, and in the voivodeship and district of Kalisz of the villages of Brudzew and Bolimów and of the former little town of Korab, and his wife Ewa née Nowowiejska Czarnecka, owners of the village of Lipe in the latter district and voivodeship[^102] (Ill. 18 and 19). In the second charter, issued by Ewa Czarnecka as a widow on 10 September 1790 at Lipe, the Piskory Ol. received free pasture in the forests and pastures, and the use of the pond at Piskory, in return for each of the 16 farmers ploughing within the year
