The form of the names seems to show that the population of this settlement was mixed, Polish-German, the German part, being in the minority, becoming assimilated fairly quickly, to judge from the change of a name (Pankow to Pankowski).

In the Ciemierowa/Tomickie/Szymanowickie Stare Ol., in the district of Kalisz, which arose before 1755-1756, it can be inferred from the form of the names (Cyft, Rotenberg(er), Dresler, Klim, Sypniewski, Vaber and others) and from the baptismal registers of the settlers' children that they were Catholics, but perhaps partly also Protestant Lutherans, mostly Germans, but also Poles[^204].

In the Zbierskie Ol., in the district of Kalisz (founded in 1761), 14 settlers with German and Polish names, Lutherans, were settled. Under the text of the foundation charter of that year the following settlers are named: Andrzej Hert, Bogusław Cherman (Herman), Marcin Krol, Krzysztof Szulc, Maciej Góra, Marcin Szulc, Krystian Kurtz, Paweł Hauman (or Hawman), Krzysztof Bonig, Marcin Weys, Marcin Reszka, Godfri(e)d(t) Wuntrach, Jędrzej Kamyszek, Krystian Ryska, the widow of Maciej Tak, settled in the years 1761-1769, three of them only temporarily. The inventory of the Zbiersk estate belonging to Gąsiorowski, royal chamberlain, drawn up in 1771 by the married couple Przespolewski, names 6 Olęders (Marcin Szulc, Marcin Krol, Maciej Góra, Maciejowa Takowa, Krzysztof Szulc and Jan Krzysztof Wajstak) and the amount of their dues, and 3 Olęders not yet paying them. The inventory of the same estate of 1775 found, among other things, 14 farmers in the Zbierskie Ol. By confession they were Protestants, perhaps even Reformed, since, as was mentioned above, they used the Reformed church at Żychlin[^205], though it is not out of the question that they were Lutherans going to that church because it was close to them.

In the Wielołęka Ol. in the district of Konin, founded before or in 1767, there settled, according to the charters of 1770, Jan Czarneski, Sebastian Rest, Walenty Ginter, Bogusław Sztruma, Wojciech August Ralcer, Arnold Jan Kimel, Krzysztof Fryze, Bogumił Wondrak, Krystian Kiejzer. According to the inventory of the properties of Grodziec and Królików of 1779, 15 farmers lived in this settlement (14 of them named with first names and surnames of German sound, 1 innkeeper and 1 *szkolny*, that is teacher, not named). In 1790, 168 dissenters, that is Lutherans, lived in this village[^206].

In the royal Cekowskie St. Ol. in the district of Kalisz, 9 settlers were settled in 1768 (2 with Polish names – Kazimierz Kuczka, *wójt*, and Andrzej Warmiński – and 7 with German names), Lutherans and Catholics, described as people "of the Olęder estate"; some of them most probably came from abroad, since the foundation charter guaranteed them the right to sell the farms granted them *...should any of them not like it in this country*[^207].
