**skarbnik latyczewski** (treasurer of Latyczów) – an Old Polish land official, in medieval Poland caring for the royal treasure – valuables, jewels and documents; in early modern times an honorary, titular office tied to a voivodeship or district or land, the last in the hierarchy of land offices; in this case tied to the district of Latyczów/Latyczew in Podolia.

**starosta** (starost) – "with judicial power" (castle starost, general starost) a judicial and administrative official appointed by the king, over a land, a district or a larger area, who also held on lease royal (state) estates forming the endowment of his office; "without judicial power" only a *tenutariusz*, that is the lessee and manager of larger or smaller organisational and economic units of the royal or state landed estates, called starosties, *tenuty* or leases (*dzierżawy*), and sometimes only of single villages, farms (sołtys or wójt holdings) and objects (such as mills).

**stolnik** – an Old Polish land official, in the Middle Ages in charge of the ruler's table, in early modern times an honorary, titular office of a voivodeship or a land.

**sucha beczka** ("dry barrel") – an empty barrel, not filled with beer, the symbol of a set money fine paid to the manor for evading or breaking the compulsion of propination.

**szambelan** (chamberlain), otherwise court chamberlain (*podkomorzy nadworny*) – a court official responsible for the monarch's residence, with authority over the chamber servants and courtiers assigned to serve the king, attending the king at the sessions of the Diet and of the court, on journeys and on campaigns; in the eighteenth century a purely titular, honorary office.

**szarwark** – the compulsory service of the peasant population, Olęders too, in labour or with teams, in repairing and building roads, bridges, dikes and flood embankments.

**talar** (thaler) – a large silver coin weighing about 28 g, of good metal, struck in many countries of Europe from the end of the fifteenth and the first half of the sixteenth century; after 1764 in Poland it equalled 8 złp.; from the sixteenth century in the countries of the Holy Roman Empire (the Reich) called the *reichsthaler*, the Imperial thaler, struck also to its proper standard in Polish mints.

**tatarka** – a grain, a species of buckwheat.

**tenutariusz** – see starosta.

**tynf**, **tymf** – the colloquial name of the silver Polish złoty pieces struck in the years 1663-1667 at Bydgoszcz and Lwów by Andrzej Tymf, lessee of the Crown mints, which had a set rate of 30 gr. but were really worth 12-18 gr.; their enormous production (about 6 million pieces) helped to intensify inflation.

**wiertel** – before the partitions of Poland a measure of farmland equal to 3,591 m² [sic]; later, as the Berlin wiertel (*Viertel*, the Prussian quarter), a measure of capacity of liquids and of the volume of dry goods (grain too), equal to 7.9 l.; here it occurs in the latter sense.

**wizja** (inspection) – a detailed official description of the real and movable property and of the rent drawn from peasant or burgher farms, drawn up most often in connection with a change of the holder of the estate, at the request of the parties concerned, carried out on the spot by a sworn court usher representing the office, the castle court or the land court, in the presence of witnesses, and differing in this from a private-law description such as other inventories.

**wojski** – an Old Polish land official, in the Middle Ages the castellan's deputy for military affairs, responsible for order in the district during a military expedition, guardian of the wives and children of the nobles at war; in early modern times an honorary, titular office, the last but one in the hierarchy of land offices.

**wolnizna** (free years) – the time of several years, set in the foundation charter, meant for taking up and bringing into use the land received as a holding (the area for a farm), free of obligations to the manor, the state and the Church; its length depended above all on the character of that land.

**wywód** (churching) – here: the blessing of a mother after the birth of a child, when she is led into the Catholic church.

**zagrodnicy** (smallholders) – otherwise *ogrodnicy*, a category of the peasant population with little land, mostly subject, existing in the thirteenth to nineteenth centuries, holding small farms, on the whole of 5-6 morgen; in the Olęder settlements there were also, besides such smallholders, "smallholders without land and garden", who had cottages but were probably in fact people living by hired work.

**złoty polski**, or złoty (*złotówka*) – a Polish silver coin, from 1764 equal to 4 silver groszy, 2 half-złoty, or 30 copper groszy.
