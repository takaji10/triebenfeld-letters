Its geographical and cultural unity is made by clear boundaries shaped by nature and by the Olęder landscape, a mosaic of woods, fields and meadows with lone farmsteads scattered among them and set into the landscape. Its natural unity comes from the large share of woodland and the great quantity of extensively used fields and meadows. There are many marshy wilds and high dunes within the woods, but also corners of villages full of colour, warmth and quiet. In the frequent floods the Warta valley turns into a vast expanse of water, a refuge of wetland birds of European importance. The cultural unity that sets the whole area apart from its neighbours is made above all by the rich heritage of Olęder architecture. Many inhabited traditional farmsteads, found elsewhere only in open-air museums, have survived to our day. A particular feature are the "houses of iron", that is, houses built of bog iron ore. The forest has the largest number in Poland of buildings built of this interesting material. Architectural surveys made in 2009 made it possible to pick out the common features of the building here. This is the basis for gradually defining a style of architecture of its own, which will lastingly and favourably distinguish this region in Poland and in the world. The isolation of the forest from the surrounding areas favoured the survival of old trades and ways of farming. The old crafts are represented by the potter's workshop in Czarny Bród, the only one in Greater Poland. Old ways of animal husbandry and the customs that go with them are also kept up to this day, such as the free grazing of the geese of Zagórów and the meadow communities in the Warta valley, unchanged since 1864.

The unity of the area, and at the same time its difference from the neighbouring areas, was noticed by local volunteers, gathered chiefly in the Cultural Society "Echo Pyzdr". In 2009 the long-forgotten name "Puszcza Pyzdrska" was restored. Thanks to grants won in competitions for non-governmental organisations, the first research in history on the cultural heritage of the area (architecture, design), which had until then seemed uninteresting, was begun. Its results were very fruitful and were partly presented in public, among other places in the Ethnographic Museum in Poznań, at the exhibition "Puszcza Pyzdrska. Olędrzy i ich domy z żelaza" (The Pyzdry Forest. The Olęders and their houses of iron). Workshops in old building techniques were held, teaching materials were prepared for teachers and teachers were trained in using them in lessons, and the first tourist map of the forest was made and published, with the publication "Puszcza Pyzdrska – olęderskie dziedzictwo" (The Pyzdry Forest – the Olęder heritage). In 2013 the first tourist guide to the Pyzdry Forest was prepared and published, and together with the map it received a distinction at the Tour Salon tourism fair in Poznań. A demonstration outcrop of bog iron ore at Wierzchy was opened, and the building of stops – museums was begun. In 2014 the first documentary film about the people of the Pyzdry Forest, "Ludzie z żelaznych domów" (People of the houses of iron), was made.
