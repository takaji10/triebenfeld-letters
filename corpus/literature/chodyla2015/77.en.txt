The settlers undoubtedly took part in founding the settlements, which, as the text of many foundation charters shows, also took place at their request. In the founding of a few settlements one may guess at the presence of a locator or locators (*osadźca*, *zasadźca*), founding entrepreneurs acting on the authority of the whole commune, but for the most part whole groups (communities) of settlers acted in this capacity. The settlers of most settlements were Protestants by confession (chiefly Lutherans), some of them Catholics, and by nation Germans and Poles, who in some settlements also settled together. The settlements were founded on the strength of charters, which had to be paid for. The process of founding the Olęder settlements lasted at least through the free years (that is mostly several years), but often, as the information quoted above shows, even longer, probably mainly because of the difficulty of finding enough settlers and their coming and going. That is why settlers sometimes received individual contracts, sometimes collective but preliminary ones, replaced only in time, after the free years of all the settlers had run out, by collective, final, emphyteutic-perpetual foundation charters guaranteeing the perpetual rent tenure of the farms they held. The way, that is the technique, of founding the settlements, by clearing and/or by draining, which consisted in digging or deepening drainage ditches, depended on the character of the land taken up. Both sides bore the costs of the founding. The manor not only gave land for founding the settlements but probably organised part of the transport of the colonists, granted them building timber free of charge (allowing them to sell what was left over after the buildings were put up, in order to raise cash), and sometimes helped financially with putting up the buildings, which we discuss in detail below.

An important cultural aspect of Olęder settlement in the area of the Pyzdry Forest was the toponymy of the settlements. Of the 58 villages, most had, beside their name proper, the additional element *(H)olędry* or *(H)olendry*, used to mark the legal and economic character and the spatial layout of the place (like the terms *folwark*, *kolonia* and so on). As a component of the name of a settlement, this element served at the same time to identify it and to distinguish it[^129].

Most of the settlements, founded sometimes on raw root, but mostly near old mother settlements, centres and estates and on their lands, received names that were:

1) possessive, that is, such as: *Lisewskie, Pyzdrskie, Wrąbczynkowskie, Gadowskie St. and Nowe; Grodzieckie/Grójeckie, Jaroszewickie, Kowalewskie, Lisieckie, Siąszyckie or Złotkowskie, Smuszewskie, Trąbczyńskie Stare and Nowe; Ciemierowskie/Szymanowickie Stare, Cekowskie Stare and Nowe, Jarantowskie, Oborskie Nowe, Plewińskie Stare and Nowe, Zbierskie – Olędry.*

2) topographical, derived from local names: of woods, thickets, forests and wilds, such as: *Borowiec/Borowieckie Stare and Nowe, Dębniałki, (Dzierzbińskie)*
