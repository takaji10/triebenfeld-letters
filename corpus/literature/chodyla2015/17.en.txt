# Chodyła (2015), p. 17
# translation by Claude

This publication, very richly illustrated, presents in other sketches by W. Kowalska the present state of the natural environment in the Pyzdry Forest, the kinds of building and other elements of the cultural landscape of the (former) Olęder villages of the area and the state in which they survive (Protestant churches and cemeteries), archaeological monuments, farming, orchards and gardens, craftsmen and folk artists, cooking traditions, markets, fairs and church feasts, place names, local activists and leaders, and hints useful for spreading knowledge of local history and keeping its memory alive among the present inhabitants of the region[^16]. Some information on certain of the Olęder settlements of the Pyzdry Forest at the end of the eighteenth century, and on their later fortunes in the nineteenth, was given by K. P. Woźniak in his book on the development of German settlement between the Prosna and the Pilica and the Vistula from the 1770s to 1866[^17]. He did so chiefly from the German literature, which deals with details and rests on manuscript sources to a limited degree; the most important of it are the studies of W. Maas, and above all of A. Breyer, which we have also used here[^18].

For the questions of the material and spiritual culture and the daily life of the inhabitants of the Olęder settlements, the ethnographic studies and the synthesis of the history of material culture cited below proved helpful.

The information on the oldest settlements founded in the Pyzdry Forest could be enriched to some extent from source editions: the lists of town and rural settlements, with their location by the church's administrative divisions and their owners, in the years 1783-1784, prepared by the Rev.
