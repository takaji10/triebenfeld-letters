From these lands, chiefly from the districts of the voivodeship of Kalisz (from towns and villages), came the Catholic and Polish settlers who lived, exclusively or partly, in a dozen or so settlements, especially those that were church property, but in a few cases also on noble ones[^241]. It seems that already in the first decades after the settlements were founded the confessional and ethnic composition of the villages changed, though to a very limited extent, as their inhabitants moved, because, among other things, of their personal freedom and the sales of land, and through marriages with women and men of neighbouring villages, especially rent-paying or Olęder ones. They were inhabited, at least half of them, wholly or partly, by German Protestant settlers, and afterwards, until 1945, by their descendants, who sometimes (especially in the settlements mixed in confession and nation) adopted Catholicism and in time became Polonised.

In three Olęder settlements, besides peasants, petty nobles also lived, though mostly on special terms, not being part of the peasant communes of the Olęder settlements and not subject to their self-government, or not willing to submit to it. Thus in 1791 one Różański lived under Olęder law on the land of the village of Ceków, a royal property[^242]. In 1792 there lived in the Białobłockie or Królikowskie Ol. Mikołaj Mąkowski, who then summoned before the landowners' court Count F. Stadnicki, the owner, the Olęders Józef and Jakub Hersa, brothers, Andrzej Langa, former sołtys, and Józef Koźmiński, commissary of the Królików estate, and other persons, for damage to his sown crops and pastures, for verbal and physical insult and for being treated like a peasant by the sołtys, and for the violent taking of his oxen. In reply Count F. Stadnicki summoned the same M. Mąkowski before the landowners' court, holding that by his marriage to an Olęder widow he was subject (like her previous husband) to Olęder law in matters with the sołtys and assessor and the persons of the commune, with the threat of a money fine for summoning them wrongly before the landowners' court[^243]. In Świdle, in turn, but not in the Świdle Ol., in the years 1789-1792, the noble Hieronim Nieszkowski held under a contract, that is under Olęder law, 5½ hubas and 3 morgen of land measured with the rod of 7½ ells. He remained, however, outside the Olęder commune created in 1790[^244].
