Another serious burden to the manor was the complete compulsion of propination, in force in all the Olęder settlements of the Pyzdry Forest. The Olęders were forbidden, under penalty, to make any drink *or to take it from anyone but the manor*. This was stated expressly, either in general terms or with the penalties for breaking this ban, by the foundation charters for the Pyzdrskie Ol., the Gadowskie St. and Nw. Ol., the Lubieńskie Ol., the Orlińskie D. and M. Ol., Wielołęka, the Cekowskie St. Ol., the Cekowskie Nw. Ol. (with the threat of a fine of 40 zł for a garniec of beer and 80 zł for a garniec of vodka), the Oborskie St. and Nw. Ol., the Dębniałki and Piskory Ol. (with the threat of a fine of 2 thalers), the Kosmowskie Ol. (with a fine of the price of 1 "*dry barrel*" and 3 garniec of vodka), the Kuszyńskie Ol., the Mycielińskie Ol. (under threat of paying a "*dry barrel*"), the Plewińskie Ol., the Przespolewskie Ol. and the Wierzchy Ol.[^311] On such occasions too as christenings, weddings and parties, the Olęders were always bound to drink the lord's drink. Different from the compulsion of propination, under which buying drink depended on the settlers' will, was the compulsion to buy a certain amount of drink, and so, indirectly, a compulsion to consume. It was rarely met with in the Olęder villages, but it did occur. So it was in the Oborskie St. and Nw. Ol. and the Wierzchy Ol., whose farmers were bound to buy *½ and 1 barrel of beer at the election of the sołtys*[^312].

In some Olęder settlements there was also a compulsion to buy other goods at the inn in a set amount, for instance in the Lubieńskie Ol. ("*the lord's herrings*" during Lent) and in the Stropieszyńskie – Radzany and Gostynie Ol. (20 herrings for each hearth)[^313].

The inhabitants of all the Olęder settlements founded in the eighteenth century in the Pyzdry Forest were also bound, like the inhabitants of non-Olęder villages, to render to the state taxes in money, paid every year in two instalments (March and September) – the poll tax (*pogłówne*), set in the tariffs for 1775 and 1789-1790, the *hiberna* in the settlements on royal estates in the tariff of 1775, and the tax of the offering (from 1789) of 10 gr. (on noble estates) and 20 gr. (on church estates) – and a tax in kind, the so-called levy (*zsypka*) from the "self-tilling" hearths (in the form of korzec of rye and oats per hearth, varying in the individual charters). Their payment is confirmed in the sources for the royal Cekowskie St. Ol. in 1775 (hearth tax of 126 złp from 18 hearths at a rate of 7 zł per hearth, and 56 for the *hiberna* together with Ceków[^314]), the noble Zbierskie Ol. (which paid 36 złp. of poll tax in 1775[^315]), the Ciemierowskie St. Ol., which in 1790 were in arrears with the payment of *112 złp. of hearth tax in the September instalment*[^316],
