The oldest Olęder settlements in the Pyzdry Forest, founded in 1746 in the district of Konin, in the parish and on the estate of Trąbczyn, were the Trąbczyńskie Stare Ol. and the Luśnie Ol. They were founded, on half of the wood Ostrowiec, half of the wood or rather marsh Stawisko, and on the woods Miąsykąt, Dziadulin and Luśnie, stretching from Stawisko towards Czarny Bród, by the married couple Paweł and Katarzyna née Rozdrażewska Prusimski, the owners of the estate at the time, by a charter of theirs that has not survived. This is known from the decree of the boundary commission court of 1776 on fixing the boundaries between the estates of Trąbczyn and Łukom. According to it, a part of the Trąbczyn estate together with the Olęder villages, which in 1771 had been wrongly adjudged by the boundary commission court and *tradowana*, that is officially handed over, to Stanisław Chełmski, the owner of the estate of Łukomia or Łomów, was to be restored (together with the income unlawfully drawn from it for 4 years) to the nephew and heir of the founder, Antoni Prusimski, owner of Trąbczyn Mały or Nowa Wieś, starost of Niszczewice [so called from the village of Niszczewice, the seat of a non-castle starosty in the district and voivodeship of Inowrocław]. On the strength of what this source says about the names, the Lutheran school and the cemetery, it can be said that the settlers placed in these Olęder villages were German peasants of the Lutheran, Evangelical-Augsburg, confession[^34] (Ill. 4). Since they were settled on wooded and marshy land, which needed clearing and draining, they probably received 7-8 free years to make the land fit for tillage and to put up their buildings. The inspection [description] of the Trąbczyn estate of 1754, eight years later, says that 15 Olęders lived in these settlements. Of these, 12 Olęders and an inn were in the wood Lusina, that is in the Luśnie Ol.[^35], which was taken to be the greater part of the Trąbczyńskie Stare Ol. in the wider sense, but was later also called the Łukomskie Ol.
