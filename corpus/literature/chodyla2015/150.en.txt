# Chodyła (2015), p. 150
# translation by Claude

**kontraktowy** (contract man) – a peasant (Olęder too), more often a craftsman, settled in a village under an individual contract, that is an agreement made with the feudal lord, the owner or the holder of the landed estate.

**kontrakt** (contract, from Latin *contractus*) – here the agreement of the founder of a settlement with the settlers or their representatives and the whole village commune, most often in the form of a unilateral charter issued by the founder; it could be individual and preliminary, or collective and final, and according to its legal character emphyteutic – short-term (for several decades) or perpetual.

**korzec warszawski** (Warsaw korzec) – a measure of capacity, from 1764 equal to 120.6 litres; it was divided into 2 Gdańsk korczyki and 32 garniec.

**lokacja** (from Latin *locatio*) – here the founding of a settlement, based on an agreement setting its legal and economic conditions, formulated by the founder in agreement with the settlers.

**lokator** (from Latin *locator*) – the *osadźca*, the representative of the settlers who made the agreement with the founder of the settlement on their behalf, a settlement entrepreneur who brought in the settlers and organised the new settlement; most often he settled in the village founded and (at least at the beginning) held the office of its sołtys, and also drew material benefits from his function (for instance in the form of additional land).

**łanowy układ przestrzenny** (*łan* layout) – a layout of arable land in the form of a łan, the area of a full peasant farm, whose size, according to how it was understood as a unit of measurement, ranged from 16.7 to 25.8 ha, and which was laid out with various kinds of rods and ells.

**łokieć** (ell) – a unit of measure; from 1764 the Warsaw ell equalled 59.6 cm (a measure of length) and was divided into 24 inches; the square Warsaw ell equalled 0.354666 m² (a measure of area).

**niwowy układ** (*niwa* layout) – a layout of arable land in the form of a *niwa*, that is a new area of land won for tillage chiefly by clearing forests, belonging to one or several farmers, its shape and size depending on the conditions of the ground and the property relations; it was characterised by the marking out of three basic fields (winter, spring and fallow), in which the peasants, sometimes with the lord's manor, held separate strips (*płosy*) forming a łan (włóka), which divided the *niwa* into long strips.

**oblata** (from Latin *oblatus*, and that from *offerre*, *oblatum*, to bring before; to leave) – here: the entering (copying) or taking of a legal act into the books of a court and office (for instance a castle court).

**okupne** (entry money; in German most often called *Grungeld*) – the payment for land used in perpetuity, for the perpetual right to hold it and draw its profits.

**plenipotent** – the attorney of another person, acting on his written authority, most often declared before an office.

**podkomorzy** (chamberlain) – an Old Polish land official, presiding over the chamberlain's court, the boundary court, legal representative of poor widows and orphans of the noble estate and (only in Greater Poland and only until the turn of the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries) a member of the land court.

**podwoda** – a transport service, otherwise called a journey (*droga*), rendered by peasants, in this case Olęders, to the manor (mostly once a year) as part of their labour services, one of the forms of manorial obligations, sometimes commuted into a payment; *podwody* were used to carry the demesne farm's grain and other goods to local markets (the so-called small journeys, up to about 35 km) and more distant ones (great journeys, up to about 84 km), made with the peasants' carts and two-horse teams.

**pręt** (rod) – a measure of length, from 1764 equal to 4 m 46 cm 6.54 mm; it was equal to 7½ ells.

przestęp – (white or red bryony) – a perennial, a common plant of the gourd family with black or red berries.

**przymus propinacyjny** (compulsion of propination) – the absolute obligation of the peasants (Olęders too) to buy the drink produced on their feudal lords' estates, under threat of heavy money fines and even removal from their farms.

**pustka** – the land of a village deserted for various reasons, overgrown again with forest and unused, needing clearing.

**rudunek** (from German *Rodung*) – clearing, also called *rumunek*.

**rumunek** – clearing, or cleared places, or a settlement founded on the site of felled woods; the name comes from the German *räumen* (to empty, to clear away, to remove).
