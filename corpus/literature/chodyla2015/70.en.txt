# Chodyła (2015), p. 70
# translation by Claude

In the years 1787 and 1791 the Stropieszyńskie, or Radzany and Gostynie, Olędry were founded in the district of Kalisz, in the parish of Kościelec. Their founder, and the issuer of the emphyteutic-perpetual foundation charters, was Ignacy Skarbek Kozietulski, treasurer and later *wojski* of the land of Czersk, owner of the village and demesne farm of Stropieszyn. According to the first charter, which he issued on 6 December 1787 at Stropieszyn, the settlers took up "Stropieszyn" land (of the village of Stropieszyn) that was ready, meadows, and overgrown land "under Bugaj and in Radzanów by the ponds", receiving 3 free years for setting up their farms (counted from 11 XI 1787). The village also received land for a school and for a burial ground[^113] (Ill. 22). From this last piece of information it may be inferred that the Olęders were Lutherans and Germans. The second charter, issued on 22 November 1791 at Stropieszyn, mentions an earlier agreement of 27 November 1787, and by it the issuer at the same time confirmed and supplemented the first charter for this village, adding to it also the married couple Józef and Elżbieta Gierecki, servants, settled under a separate charter on ½ huba of ploughland[^114].

Before 1789 the following arose in the district of Konin, in the parish of Zagórów, on the noble estates of Łukom and Łomowo, on the initiative of their owners of the time: the settlements of Bukowo/Bukowe or the Łomowskie Olędry[^115], Imielno Olędry[^116], and a village mixed in its legal constitution, "Myszaków with the Olędry", or more exactly Myszakówko[^117], and Stara Huta with the Olędry, or the Zagórowskie Ol. (today Huta Łukomska)[^118]. The people who settled in them were of various confessions and nations[^119].

In the same district, in the parishes and on the estates of Sławsk and Grodziec, there were founded: before 1789, the Kowalewskie Olędry[^120], and before 1792 the Jaroszewickie Olędry (today Jaroszewice Grodzieckie)[^121]. Their founders were the owners of these estates at the time, Stefan Zielonacki and Count Franciszek Stadnicki, who were also the largest landowners in the district of Konin in the years 1789-1791[^122].

[^121]: The court's decree of 13 VI 1792 in the case of the *przezysk* (judgment) of Stefan Chlebowski, lease holder of the church village of Jaroszewice, against J. Dobrzycki, standard-bearer of Chełm, civil and military commissary, states that, in making the agreement between himself and the plaintiff, he had wanted to settle 3 half-farmers in the first year, to make up 6, and to press the plaintiff to this under an oath sworn earlier, which he did, and forced him to pay the sum of 22,000 złp. lent with interest, in accordance with the lease and a separate *rewers* (written undertaking).
