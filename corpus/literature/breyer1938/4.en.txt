In some German villages live *professional fishermen*, who fish the Warthe with all sorts of gear and sell their catch in the small towns and the cities. That the "Brücher" are skilful boatmen and boat-builders goes without saying. Most of them, especially the less well-off, "went on the barge" in their young years or took up "Flötterei", that is, timber rafting, travelling long stretches of river, as far as Küstrin and Stettin, and indeed by the Oder to Breslau. Up to the World War the timber trade and the timber industry flourished in the Warthe region. Many found paying work sawing boards and cutting stave wood, just as since the beginnings of the immigration many German beam-hewers and "wood-cutters" were to be found in the forests of the Warthe meadows. In the church books we find signs that around 1830 German boat-builders lived in the Warthe village of Piersk, building Berlin barges and similar craft.

The yield of grain and potatoes is mostly enough for the household's own use. The rich stands of alder and pollard willow give the marsh farmer the fuel he needs, the more so as peat is also cut in many places. In building houses, wood used to prevail, and in places bog iron ore, the "ironstone". Today most new buildings are put up of fired or sand bricks.
