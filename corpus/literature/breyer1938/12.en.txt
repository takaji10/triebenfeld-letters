By a turn of fate, after a good two centuries, German colonists appeared again within sight of the monastery of Lond, to take up afresh the dormant work of cultivation of their fellow Germans. But of that in a later section. Looking back, let us now sum up the state of settlement of the Warthe marshes from the town of Peisern down the Warthe to south of Uniejów at the end of the Middle Ages. Around the middle of the 14th century single riverside villages followed one another almost without a gap on the right bank of the Warthe from Peisern up to Dombie. Only the 18 km strip of bank from Kramsk to Groß-Osiek was unsettled, because it was sandy. We find similar gaps on the left bank too, with the same causes. This is so south of Peisern and east of Konin. The land between the Warthe and the Ner, and its edges too, were already fairly densely inhabited in the Middle Ages. The soils here are middling, but there are ample meadows, which must have been of particularly great importance for the cattle farming then practised; moreover, the land between the rivers was free of floods. South of the town of Uniejów the Warthe villages stand with strikingly large gaps between them, which again is to be put down to the flat valley slopes covered with river sand; the river meadows here are also shallow and so yield little. The densely settled banks between Peisern and Uniejów owe this to the fertile loam soils of the land beside them; here farming paid well. The forest behind the riverside villages was only sparsely settled at the end of the Middle Ages. The soil hardly tempted anyone to clear the forest; moreover, the lack of roads and the wildness of the immense primeval forests deterred even the boldest clearer. Yet the population, rising over the decades and centuries, sought new means of living. New settlements slowly pushed in between the old riverside villages; some dared to lay out their farmsteads in the marsh itself, on raised spots. It is noteworthy that it was the marsh south of Koło that fell to this heavier settlement. Into the marsh between Koło and Konin the settler ventured only around the middle of the 18th century, and likewise into the marsh west of Konin. Here as there it was German marsh farmers who opened up this swamp wilderness.

The conditions of settlement in the Middle Ages are also partly reflected in the old place names. In the village of Bobrowo the duke's beaver hunters may have lived. In Barcie were the bee-keepers, a calling held in honour in the Middle Ages.
