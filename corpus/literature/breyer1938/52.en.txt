# Breyer (1938), p. 52
# translation by Claude

> *§ 7*

> *Besides the purchase and glove money, the takers of the hereditary lease undertake to pay the hereditary lord for each Hufe a yearly ground rent of 138 fl., say one hundred and thirty-eight Gulden, and also from these Hufen the tenth groschen at ten Polish Gulden a year; both the ground rent and the tenth groschen, which however is to be paid on all ten Hufen, to be settled at Martinmas each year.*

> *§ 8*

> *Further, the takers of the hereditary lease undertake to deliver to the lord every year for each Hufe two geese, three capons and forty eggs in kind, of which dues in kind the geese and capons must be handed in at Martinmas, but the eggs at Easter each year.*

> *§ 9*

> *As hearth money (Rauchfangs Geldern) the takers of the hereditary lease undertake to pay ten Polish Gulden, namely those who have until now leased the cottages (Kathen) lying on Lesznik and sold with it separately; this is likewise to be paid to the manor at Martinmas each year.*

> *§ 10*

> *As days of labour service (Scharwerk) the takers of the hereditary lease undertake to do the lordship six days a year for each Hufe, namely two days with the scythe, two days carting dung and two days with the plough, in which last two days two Morgen must be ploughed for each Hufe, each Morgen ten rods wide and thirty rods long.*

> *§ 11*

> *Besides these days of labour service the takers of the hereditary lease are also bound to give the lord help at harvest, which is set so that each holder of a quarter Hufe gives one day, of a half Hufe two days and of a whole Hufe three days with the sickle.*
