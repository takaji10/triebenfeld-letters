# Breyer (1938), p. 8
# translation by Claude

Konin to the Cistercian monastery at Lond in 1145.[^2] The charters of that time speak of fishermen further, as in the villages of Dobrow and Czarkow, where they say "cum piscatoribus" (with the fishermen)[^3]. In 1232 a charter of the village of Dobrow noted that fishing and the taking of beaver in the rivers Warthe and Ner were released to the inhabitants ("piscatio et castores in fluviis Wartha et Ner"[^4]). Moreover, the riverside villages were obliged to the monastery at Lond "to draw the net in fishing on the Warthe". In the same charter we also read of the wealth of grass of the Warthe marshes; at one place it says: "the rent-paying villages are obliged, at the time of the hay harvest, to bring three cartloads of hay each into the monastery's barns".[^5]

In the 12th and 13th centuries Polish provincial princes began to give single Warthe villages to church dignitaries. Thus around 1136 the Archbishop of Gniezno owned the villages of Chełmno, Uniejów, Kwiatków, Dombie and Jankowice, to which Rzuchów, Leszno, Grabina and Szadow were added in 1284. Towards the end of the 13th century the archbishop acquired, in the land between the Warthe and the Ner, the villages of Cechmiany, Grodziska, Orzeszków, Ostrowsko and Zbylczyce. The archbishop's property grew considerably south of the town of Uniejów, where the following villages were acquired or founded: Spicimierz, Wiejszcze, Kościelnica, Zielenin, Ubysław, Lubiszewice, Niemysłów, Boleszczyn, Smólsko and Popów. Downstream he acquired the villages of Skobielice and Trzęśniew, which bordered on his property. All the archbishop's villages together made up a wide property stretching a good 50 km along the course of the Warthe.

The Bishop of Poznań received from the ruling prince the village of Ciążeń in 1251 and Ratyń in 1261. West of the town of Koło lay the only property of the ruling prince, the village of Gozdów, since in 1362 the prince's villages of Bliżna and Nagórna Wieś were incorporated into the newly founded town of Koło. Among the archbishop's possessions a few noble villages are scattered, such as Balin, Białków, Powiercie, Grójec, Wola Podłężna, Daniszew and others.
