# Breyer (1938), p. 18
# translation by Claude

Downstream, German farmers settled south of Koło in *Leszcze*, in German Lesche, around 1783, as can be seen from the Rosterschütz books. In the marsh opposite Lesche, German settlers arrived around 1787 and laid out with success the typical marsh Holländerei of *Groß-Politz*. A school was founded here before 1800. The settlers in these German villages arising in old Polish times were mostly full-Hufe farmers (Vollhüfner). In terms of the law of property the first marsh settlements are to be called Holländer villages, since nowhere is there talk of an overriding importance of a village headman or of a special grant of land to him. The Pomeranian villages of the Leśnik language island, on the other hand, are to be regarded as headman's villages (Schulzendörfer).

The settling of German farmers by private lords set an example. On 4 September 1781 Ostrowski, Archbishop of Gniezno, had King Stanislaus Augustus confirm the privilege he had granted "to the honourable Hauländer newly establishing themselves and named in the contract, and to their heirs".
