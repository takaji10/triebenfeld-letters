They are however bound, during the free years, to clear the forests left to them entirely and to put up proper buildings."* Among other things, the founding charter of this Holländerei, called Rzuchow at first but Maidany from 1790, contains the right freely to sell the lands taken up, and also, which sounds strange for church property, complete freedom "from all dues and payments to the provost at Chełmsk". To the village headman and to the schoolmaster the archbishop gave half a Hufe of land each, "freed for ever from all rents and labour services". The charter names the following first settlers: Adam Kramer as village headman, [and as] his aldermen (Schöppen) Gottlob Klingbeil, Andreas Schindel, Michael Schindel, Christoph Ratay, Michael Ratay, Christian Ratay, Jacob Pinno, Jacob Schultz and Mathias Rautke. As entry money the new settlers bound themselves to pay 18 Gulden for each Hufe. The later yearly rent came to 60 Gulden for each Hufe. For the complete clearing of the pieces of forest left to them the Holländer received nine free years; in that time the farm buildings were also to be put up. According to a survey of the property made by the cathedral chapter of Gniezno in 1784, 11 full-Hufe and 2 half-Hufe farmers lived in the Holländerei of Maidany, owning 6 horses, 20 oxen and 30 cows.[^15]

The German villages of *Sobótka* and *Groß-Grabin* were settled on the archbishop's land under similar conditions. Sobótka arose in 1781; at first it was among the Holländereien richest in livestock, having 25 horses, 46 oxen and 51 cows, though we find in the village only 6 half-Hufe and 8 full-Hufe farmers. In the village of Groß-Grabin sat 7 full-Hufe and 13 half-Hufe farmers; this settlement was founded in 1779, at the same time as Maidany.

From the estate of Budzisław the marsh-edge village of *Budki Holland* was laid out in 1786. Around 1790 German settlers established themselves in the middle of the marsh of the estate of Koźmin and founded the new village of *Koźminer Buden*, the later Neu-Kwiatków. Around 1790 the lord of Brudzew had German farmers of Pomeranian stock settle beside the old villages of *Tarnowa* and *Neudorf*, who in this way built two new street villages of the same names.
