## III. The flowering of new evangelical life

## in the 17th and 18th centuries

### 1. Rural colonization

The German colonization movements in the East, beginning with the medieval early and high settlement period, were followed, as the next, by that of the modern era (1550–1800). Under Sigismund August it began, and under King Wladyslaw IV. it reached its height. The causes that triggered this eastward settlement were of a religious, economic, and social kind. Difficult, for example, was the situation of the Evangelicals in Silesia, where they could find no happiness in their faith. No less did the Thirty Years' War (1618–1648) lay waste wide areas of the German Reich, while Poland remained almost untouched by it. Under the protection of Polish nobles, who were often themselves Protestants, men hoped to be able to establish a new existence and to live in the evangelical faith without molestation. And so there poured forth from Silesia, Pomerania, Brandenburg, from the Neumark and from other territories a stream of immigrants into West Prussia and the adjacent land of Posen, where new towns and villages were founded and the old places were enlarged and peopled by the influx. The wave-ripples of this colonization movement reached central Poland as well. In the year 1605, as the first German village in the Weichsel lowland, Schlonsk (Slonsk) was founded *). It owed its origin to the Bromberg starost Smogolewski, who had left twelve Holländer from the Danziger Werder 25 hides in hereditary lease for forty years. As with the old villages under German law, the administration of Slonsk together with its jurisdiction lay in the hand of the **) Albert Ritschl dealt with the Socinians in his principal work "Die christliche Lehre von der Rechtfertigung und Versöhnung" ("The Christian Doctrine of Justification and Reconciliation"), vol. 3, pp. 65, 77, among other passages. They felt drawn toward Hugo Grotius. His irenic efforts they interpreted to mean that he wished to join them and become one of their own. Adolf von Harnack likewise took a position on them. After 1945 Polish researchers have concerned themselves very closely with the Socinians, among them J. Tazbir, W. Urban, K. Lepszy, A. Kaminska, Z. Ogonowski, and others.

*) Ernst Mielke: Schlonsk. Aus der Chronik eines deutschen Weichseldorfes. Jahrb. Weichsel-Warthe 1959, pp. 91–103.
