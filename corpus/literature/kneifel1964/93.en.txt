### 2. The Development of School and Church Affairs

In the year 1803 Samuel Gottlieb Linde (1771—1847), born in Thorn, the organizer of the Polish linguistic treasury (a dictionary in six volumes; completed in 1814), was called by Minister Voss to establish and direct a lyceum in Warschau. Linde earned merit for the institution, which was housed in the Saxon Palace and whose establishment cost almost 5000 thalers[^p93-12]

From 1814 to 1819 he was president of the Warschau church collegium and in 1814 had a count of the congregation carried out, which yielded almost 8000 souls. As excellent Lutheran town schools in Central Poland of that period are named: Warschau, Kalisch and Peisern. — In the countryside German-evangelical schools gradually also formed. Thus in 1795 in the Vistula lowland in Wölka-Turzynska and Chinow; further, around 1798 in Borki with 38 pupils, Troszyn with 37, Sady 22, Kazun 20 and Secymin 24. Likewise Lutheran schools in Famuiki and Myszory were mentioned.

In the Dobriner Land the number of German elementary schools and cantorates for the year 1807 is given as 29. Around 1800 there already existed in Kujawien about 42 elementary schools, some of which were endowed with 15 Morgen of taxable and rent-free land. But there were also individual poorly equipped schools, such as that in the "deserted place of Smol-nik", where the cantor's principal occupation was that of tailor and he lived in the shepherd's house. The school and cantorate community was far too small and too poor to erect a schoolhouse from its own means. In the Warthebruch around ten elementary schools were counted in the year 1800. Around the same time — according to a report drawn up by Pastor Grimm of Stawiszyn for the Posen consistory — 15 German-evangelical schools are named in the Kalisch country. Of these, as already existing in 1797: Zamenty, Zakrzyn, Przespolew, Czachulec, Grodziec and Mycielin. In the Gostynin country, around the turn of the 18th century, according to the information of the Ilow pastor Witthold, there were already eleven reasonably well-equipped German elementary schools. According to the "General-Tabelle von dem Zustand aller evangelischen Schulen... im Jahre 1798", which the Ilow pastor Witthold likewise compiled, there existed in the Lodz area the schools in Slowik, Groß-Bruzyca, Klein-Bruzyca, Radogoszcz, Kaly, Dombrowa-Holl., Swendow, Glogowiec, Mileszki and Domaradzyn. According to the school register of the year 1805, schools further formed in Chociszew, Jankower-Holl., Erazmow, Felicjanow and Katarzynow.
