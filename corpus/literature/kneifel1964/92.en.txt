The other towns of the Kalisch country, such as Stawiszyn, Turek, Wladyslawow, likewise received evangelical immigrants. Likewise in the Warthebruch the towns of Konin, Zagörow and Kolo. In Kujawien, in Dombie, German clothmakers settled. Already in 1798 a German clothmakers' guild formed itself here. In the small town of Wiskitki, clothmakers, cloth-shearers, dyers, linen weavers and leather tanners settled. Around the same time craftsmen and small burghers are met with in the Gostynin country, in Gombin. The same holds for Pabianice, Lentschütz, Petrikau and Brzeziny, where individual cloth weavers also found their livelihood. In Kowal and Chodetsch numerous craftsmen immigrated, among them a number of weavers. In the year 1797 the Prussian king granted to Count von Lüttichau the Grodztwo of Kowal at the town's boundary there. During the Prussian rule many evangelical civil and military officials, craftsmen, merchants and other seekers of livelihood moved to Warschau7)

Thereby the number of souls of the Warschau Lutheran congregation increased considerably. The father of the writer Bogumil Goltz, born in Warschau, was director of the city court here8)

E. Th. A. Hoffmann (1776—1822), the poet of German Romanticism, musician, music writer and painter, stood in 1802 in the service of the Prussian administration in Plozk, then from 1804 to 1806 in Warschau, where he composed several operas and led the musical society of the Prussian officials as conductor[^p92-9]

Apart from Warschau, the towns of Kalisch, Peisern, Plozk, Bialystok, Tykocin, Kaiwar ja, Wilkowyszki, Wyszogrod, Rypin, Lipno and others had many new citizens. Bialystok belonged to the Hetman Xaver Branicki, whose heirs sold the town in the year 1802 to the Prussian king for 270,970 thalers. It was the administrative center of the province of Neuostpreußen. This naturally had as a consequence an increased influx of administrative officials, craftsmen and other persons. After the Congress of Vienna the Prussian king sold Bialystok, ceded in the Peace of Tilsit in 1807 at Napoleon's instigation, to the Russian Tsar Alexander I for 200,000 rubles. In Neuostpreußen about 1292 families with approximately 5560 souls were settled at an expenditure of 200,000 thalers.

The Prussian colonization in Central Poland, which embraced a period of practically only seven years (1800—1806), founded new German settlements and strengthened and enlarged those already existing through the inflow of additional forces. Everywhere there were starting points to which new villages could be attached, and further school and cantorate communities as well as parishes could be organized. Thus the shaping of school, church and other life in the German-Lutheran colonies took place step by step.
