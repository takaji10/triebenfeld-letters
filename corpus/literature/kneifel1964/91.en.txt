# Kneifel (1964), p. 91
# translation by Claude

Bernhard Zimmermann of Sulzfeld/Württemberg rendered service in the laying-out of Sulzfeld, which was designed in star form. "Geometrically clear forms were the ideal of Prussian colonization and its surveyors." In all these colonies Swabians were settled mainly. In addition, families from Baden, from Bavaria (the Palatinate), from Hesse, Prussia, Saxony, from the Posenschen and in part from Lorraine also immigrated into Neusulzfeld in the first decades of the 19th century. Swabians came to Königsbach, and Alsatians as well to Grünberg. The first settlers of Effingshausen came from Baden and Saxony. In Königsbach, Swabian manner and speech were preserved longest and purest. In Hochwald, Swabians and Alsatians made themselves at home. The settlement of the colonists in Hochwald took place as in the other royal villages. Roads were cut through the woodlands, then sites were surveyed and assigned to the individual families in the form of hereditary lease deeds (Erbzinsverschreibungen). The building of dwelling and farm buildings and the acquisition of livestock, seed and implements were attended to by the war and domain chambers. It should be noted that in the early period the colonists suffered severely under the plague of wolves.

Private colonization kept pace with state colonization, and indeed through Polish Catholic landowners. Near Lask, around 1800, the settlements of Hahnenfelde and Zamlynie were founded on the lands of the estate of Wojslawice. In the forests of the estates of Bedon, the landowner Tuchecka settled 29 German farmers on 30 hides in 1805. In the year 1805 the village of Żakowice arose near Brzeziny. To the efforts of the Prussian recruitment commissioner von Nothardt goes back the establishment, in 1801, of the four Swabian villages on waste and overgrown soils of the domain office of Leszno Wola: Alt-Ilvesheim, Neu-Ilvesheim, Ludwigsburg and Schwiningen. Most of the colonists came here from Ilvesheim in Franconia. Near Warschau the village of Szopy Niemieckie was laid out; in the domain office of Potycz the Swabian settlement of Kanstatt (Ka,ty). Around the year 1800 Count Felix von Lubienski founded eleven German colonies in the forests of his estate of Guzow: Josefhof, Marienfeld, Heinrichsdorf, Felixdorf, Teklin, Zader-Buden, Babsche-Buden, Moritzin, Bieganow, Antoniew and Franciszkow. Near Kawa, south of the railway station of Skierniewice, the settlement of Strobow was laid out around 1800. Around 1802 the Swabian villages of Erdmannsweiler (Kochanow) and Birkenfeld (Brzozow) arose. In Neuostpreußen, in the region of Plozk, Wyszogrod, Plonsk, Lomza, Bialystok and in the farthest north of the province, 32 colonies in all were founded. On more than 1500 Magdeburg hides around 600 families with approximately 3500 persons were settled, of whom 82 families with nearly 480 persons came from the land itself, while all the rest came above all from Württemberg and Mecklenburg °). Thus in the year 1797, 149 families with a total of 913 souls were settled on 247 hides in the first colony of Schröttersdorf (Maszewo). Soon after followed the villages of Güntersruhm, Agnesenau (Biele-Brzeznica), Luisenhuld (Cieszkowo) and Königsdorf (Wiciejewo). To the time around the turn of the 18th century belongs the foundation of the linguistic island of Königshuld (Paproc Duza), colonized with immigrants from Mecklenburg. It comprised the villages of Königshuld, Luisenau, Wilhelmsdorf and Mecklenburg. Around 1800, east of Sierpc, the colony of Sokolowy Ka,t owed its origin to private initiative. Alongside the rural colonization, the urban also went forward. Thus after the last two partitions of Poland numerous evangelical military personnel, officials, craftsmen and merchants immigrated.
