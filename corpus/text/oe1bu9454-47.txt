--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Answers four queries after meetings with justice-burgomaster Hawich and Inspector Zagorowo's Inspector Hecker: the King of Saxony's three-year General Moratorium shields the estates provided interest is paid, and the Invalids' Fund's 50000 Rthl holds the first mortgage, unchallenged by French or Saxon authorities, though the banker Oppenheimer's broken undertaking has brought suits in Berlin and Kalisz; rejects the Meyer's building claim and his offer to raise money from sequestrated estates; reports suits against Giese and consorts for 25000 M. and against v. Viedrenricht; urges refusal of the Augustinower colonists' plea for remission; and pleads that his own Kontop estates' sequestration and Berlin arrest delay his journey to Schlawenschitz.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Your Honour,

Most particularly highly honoured Court Councillor!

These past days I have had the justice-burgomaster Hawich and the inspector Hecker from Zagorowo here with me, and have rendered report on everything to His Most Serene Highness by an express. As concerns meanwhile the 4 matters most kindly communicated to me, I most submissively reply thereto that 1. the general moratorium granted by the King of Saxony protects those estates for 3 years from all pressure, provided only that the interest be duly paid - And to settle this I have laid it upon the souls of the people there. I am also certain that the same has already been settled by this time, for as the

Honourable Mr. Hawich reported to me, Hönnricht, who had had himself created sequestrator, had already some weeks ago delivered up the monies to the authority for onward transmission. The 50,000 Rthl which at that time the Invalids' Fund [uncertain: lich] to His Most Serene Highness are registered as belonging upon the estates, and require, as aforesaid, nothing further than that the interest be paid. So far as it goes, no one disputes these monies to the Invalids' Fund from either the one side or the other, for even the French general government ordered me to repay the advanced interest, which had been levied from me in error by military execution and arrest, as His Serene Highness's mandatary, because this item of debt belongs to the Invalids' Fund. Nor do I believe that the Invalids' Fund has been threatened that this capital would ever be disputed to it on the part of the King of Saxony, still less of the French.

it seems rather to be a precautionary measure of the curators. Meanwhile these men are not to be blamed, [uncertain: since] they do their duty, and they might well be assured on the part of His Most Serene Highness that His Highness will likewise exert himself in the matter and will see to it that the Invalids' Fund shall always receive its interest promptly, and also that in case it should be sought, in error, to collect it on the part of the King of Saxony, His Highness would exert himself in the matter so far as possible. For the rest, the Invalids' Fund has the first mortgage upon the estates in question, must take precedence of all other creditors, and may not and cannot be endangered; nor would His Serene Highness pay interest, still less capital, to anyone more willingly than to the Invalids' Fund, the more so as the Indian banker Oppenheimer has not kept his word given before the court as to the full satisfaction of the Invalids' Fund, and on which account a suit has already been [uncertain: instituted] these several months in Berlin and just now in Kalisz.

As concerns 2. the claim of the Meyer on account of buildings, that is as foreign to me as optics to a man born blind; I would indeed be willing to swear that the Meyer is sooner indebted to His Serene Highness than His Serene Highness to him, for so far as I know this person has never been in His Serene Highness's service, but has merely resided upon the estate of the Honourable Building Councillor Glenck.

As to what the Meyer says of Giese and consorts, the needful has been set in motion against the fellows, and it is pending on account of 25,000 M. But it is a gross state of affairs

the boys owe the remainder of the suit.

that the fellows are said not to have received all the lands which were promised them in the contract - the matter has, as aforesaid, already stood in suit these 4 years. The deceivers wished to hide themselves behind the protection of the [uncertain: Royal] defence, and the lands have been measured out by 2 sworn surveyors, and the matter set in motion - As to the wood in which the [uncertain: petitioners] are to cut timber, that has been allotted to these devils; if during the course of the war acts of violence were committed thereupon through the sequestration, then the [uncertain: Olerwiger] may hold to the sequestrator according to their contract, and His Serene Highness is in no case bound to grant a remission.

As concerns von Viedrenricht's affair, a suit is pending thereon, and certainly everything has been set afoot by the [uncertain: Zürich] servant that is required for the best possible cover of His Serene Highness; that moreover this wretched person wishes to procure revenues out of the estates is a piece of braggadocio, for so long as the sequestration lasts no one can take a heller from it without its consent. Nor are all the arrears of interest yet deposited; once so much is levied in / upon which Hawich will now surely insist / then the sequestration ceases forthwith and the matter comes back into its track. The saddest thing is that everyone complains of the war and the bad times and no one pays - the nobleman and the burgher, on the other hand, now hide themselves behind the moratorium granted for 3 years (which of course also stands His Serene Highness in very good stead), but the coffers remain empty thereby. The Meyer would meanwhile be to be told that, according to the answer of the said von Triebenfeld, the Meyer never stood in His Serene Highness's service, that His Serene Highness owed him nothing, and that no use could be made of his offer to procure monies out of the estates, because by the laws no one may draw monies out of estates which are placed under sequestration

were placed, without the foreknowledge of the curator or sequestrator.

As concerns finally the petition of the Augustinower and other colonists, nothing further is to be entered into upon it than to dismiss these people with their request in such manner that they are bound under their hereditary lease contracts to pay the interest without regard to any remission whatsoever. For there still prevails no judgment that either the aforesaid Dombcha or any other person had a right to displace them from their lands; that the times are bad and the war has brought great distress cannot be denied, but since their contracts all comprise every fortuitous case (casus fortuitus), and it is expressly said therein that this does not shield them from their dues and burdens, His Serene Highness could, however near it went to his heart, give them a favourable answer to their request, inasmuch as His Serene Highness was himself pressed by his creditors and compelled to pay the interest. It is very necessary to keep these people as far off one's body as possible, for if one grants it to one of them they all come; I am beleaguered enough by these people - indeed no post day passes on which I do not have to pay several thaler for packets of letters full of [uncertain: complaints]. And since these colonies at Drevic have over 6,000 Rthl to pay to all these Johannische, the debt would in consequence

grow too high, the more so since all others would follow this example, and His Most Serene Highness would never receive interest.

As concerns for the rest my journey to Schlawenschitz, I burn with eagerness to lay myself personally at His Serene Highness's feet; but obstacles which I cannot possibly remove at once do not permit me to depart hence any sooner than until I am in some measure clear with my affairs - A misfortune which befell me at Berlin through no fault of my own had the consequence that I had to sit there a whole year and even to endure arrest. The devastating war consumed all my revenues, I could draw nothing from here and had to contract debts; my creditors awoke and my Kontop estates were sequestrated; even now I must suffer the sequestration on that account and look upon my own property with my back turned. I am very [uncertain: unhappy] and cannot get away any sooner than until I am clear with my creditors.

With the most perfect esteem I am Your Honour's

wholly most devoted friend and servant

v. Triebenfeld
