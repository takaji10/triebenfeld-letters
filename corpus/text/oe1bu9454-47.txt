--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld antwortet einem Hoffrath auf vier Punkte. Das sächsische Moratorium schütze die Güter 3 Jahre, wenn die Zinsen gezahlt würden. Die 50000 Rthl der Invaliden Casse stünden als erste Hypothek, gegen Oppenheimer liefen Prozesse in Berlin und Kalisz. Meyers Forderung sei unbegründet, gegen Giese et Consorten laufe ein Prozess wegen 25000 Rthl, ebenso in Niedźwieckis Sache. Das Gesuch der Augustinower Colonisten um Remission soll abgewiesen werden, da ihre Erbpachtcontracte zur Zahlung verpflichten.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld replies to a Court Councillor on four points. He says the Saxon moratorium protects the estates for 3 years provided the interest is paid. The 50000 Rthl of the Invalids' Fund, he says, stand as the first mortgage, and lawsuits against Oppenheimer are pending in Berlin and Kalisz. Meyer's claim, he says, is unfounded; a suit for 25000 Rthl is pending against Giese et Consorten, as is one in Niedźwiecki's case. The Augustinow colonists' petition for remission should be rejected, since their hereditary lease contracts oblige them to pay.

Your Honour,

Most particularly highly honoured Court Councillor!

These last days I have had the justice-burgomaster Hawich and the inspector Hecker from Zagórów here with me, and have made report of everything to His Most Serene Highness by an express courier. As for the 4 matters most kindly communicated to me in the meantime, I answer most devotedly thereupon that

1. the General Moratorium granted by the King of Saxony protects those estates for 3 years from all pressure, if only the interest be duly paid — And to settle this I have bound the people there upon their souls. I am also certain that the same has already been settled by this time, for as Hawich reported to me, Honrichs, who had himself created sequestrator, had already some weeks ago delivered over the monies to the authority for further transmission — The 50000 Rthl which at that time the Invalids' Fund [text lost] of His Most Serene Highness are entered as attaching to the estates, and, as said before, nothing further is required than that the interest be paid. So far as it is a matter of adhering, no one disputes these monies with the Invalids' Fund, from either the one side or the other, for even the French General Government ordered me to pay back the advanced interest which, by an error, had been exacted from me by military execution and arrest as the Serene Highness's mandatary, because this item of debt belongs to the Invalids' Fund. Nor do I believe that the Invalids' Fund has been threatened that this capital will ever be disputed with it on the part of the King of Saxony, still less of the French.

it seems rather to be a precautionary measure of the curators. Meanwhile the men are not to be blamed [uncertain: if] they do their duty, and they might well be assured on the part of His Most Serene Highness that His Highness would himself intervene in the matter and see to it that the Invalids' Fund should always receive its interest promptly, and also that, in case it should be wished to collect it in error on the part of the King of Saxony, His Highness would intervene for it as far as possible. For the rest, [I said that] the Invalids' Fund has the first mortgage on the estates in question, must take precedence of all other creditors, and may not or cannot be endangered; also that His Serene Highness would willingly pay interest, still less capital, to no one but the Invalids' Fund, the more so as the Jewish banker Oppenheimer has not kept his word given before the court concerning the full satisfaction of the Invalids' Fund, and that on that account a lawsuit has already been commenced some months since in Berlin and just now in Kalisz.

As concerns, 2nd, Meyer's claim on account of buildings, this is as foreign to me as optics to a man born blind; I would also readily swear upon it that Meyer is sooner in debt to His Serene Highness than His Serene Highness to him, for so far as I know this man was never in the service of His Serene Highness, but merely resided on the estate of the building councillor Glenck.

As for what Meyer says of Giese and consorts, the necessary steps have been taken against the fellows, and the lawsuit is pending over the 25000 Rthl which the rogues owe in arrears. But it is a gross state of affairs

that the fellows are said not to have received all the lands which were promised them in the contract — the matter stands, as said before, already 4 years in litigation. [uncertain: Royally] the swindlers wished to hide themselves behind the protective shield, and the lands have been re-measured by 2 sworn surveyors, and the matter awaited its course — As to the wood in which the Oleśnica people are to cut timber, that is to be laid to the charge of these devils; if in the course of the war acts of violence were committed thereon through the sequestration, then the Oleśnica people may hold to the sequestrator, according to their contract, and thus His Serene Highness is not bound in any case to grant a remission.

As concerns the matter of von Niedźwiecki, a lawsuit is pending on that account, and certainly everything has been set in train by the [uncertain: Zürich] servant that is required for the fullest possible covering of His Serene Highness; that this wretched man moreover wishes to procure revenues out of the estates is a piece of braggadocio, for so long as the sequestration lasts no one can take a farthing thence without its consent. Nor are all the arrears of interest yet deposited; once so much has been collected / upon which Hawich will now surely insist / then the sequestration ceases at once and the matter comes back into its track — the saddest thing is that everyone complains of the war and the bad times and no one pays — the nobleman and the townsman, on the other hand, now hide themselves behind the Moratorium granted for 3 years / which of course also comes very much to His Serene Highness's advantage / but the coffers remain empty thereby — Meyer, however, is to be told that, according to the answer of von Triebenfeld, Meyer never stood in the service of His Serene Highness, that His Serene Highness owed him nothing, and that no use could be made of his offer to procure monies out of the estates, because according to the laws no one may draw monies out of estates which are placed under sequestration

are said to be, without the knowledge of the curator or sequestrator.

As concerns, finally, the petition of the Augustinow and other colonists, nothing further is to be entered into upon it than to refuse these people in their request in such manner: that they are bound under their hereditary lease contracts to pay the interest without regard to any remission whatever. For moreover no judgment is in force that either Dąbska or any other person had a right to displace them from their lands; that the times are bad and the war has brought great distress cannot be denied, but since their contracts all comprised every fortuitous case within them, and it is expressly said therein that this does not shield them from their dues and burdens, His Serene Highness could, however near it went to his heart, give them a compliant answer to their request, inasmuch as His Serene Highness is himself pressed by his creditors and compelled to pay the interest. This is very necessary, to keep these people off one's body as much as possible, for if one is granted it they all come; I am stormed enough by these people, indeed no post day passes on which I do not have to pay several Thaler for packets of letters full of [uncertain: nonsense]. And since these colonies near Drzewce have over 6000 Rthl to pay to all these [uncertain: Johannischen], the debt would in consequence

grow too high, the more so since all the others would follow this example, and His Most Serene Highness would never receive interest.

As regards, for the rest, my journey to Sławięcice, I burn with eagerness to lay myself personally at His Serene Highness's feet; but hindrances, which I cannot possibly remove at once, do not permit me to depart from here any sooner, until I am in some measure clear in my affairs — A misfortune which befell me at Berlin through no fault of my own had the consequence that I had to sit there a whole year and even endure arrest. The devastating war consumed all my revenues, I could draw nothing from here and had to incur debts, my creditors awoke and my Konotop estates were sequestrated; even now I must endure the sequestration on that account and look upon my property with my back turned. I am very impatient and cannot get away any sooner than when I am clear with my creditors.

With the most perfect esteem I am Your Honour's

wholly most devoted friend and servant

v Triebenfeld
