--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Written while sick in Vienna, a report that everything now depends on reaching a settlement with the Curish co-heiresses, after which the State Chancellor will release the advance and start the administration; Triebenfeld awaits J. C. Eberhardt urgently. A resolution on the Polish estates is again promised, but delayed until the status of estates taken by the Poles from grantees after the Peace of Tilsit is decided. Much news of Napoleon's return, his outlawry and the troops sent against him.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

What now still matters is that a real settlement be made with the Curish heiresses. If that indeed comes about, the Prince State Chancellor will see to it that the advance is given and the administration set in train. I therefore await the arrival of Mr J. C. Eberhardt with the greatest longing — five Hohneldte Bartenstein Ge Winzingerode and the good Ghl Rath von Göschel have written to Langenburg and Kirchberg etc. — I think that Prince August will probably have demanded the power of attorney back. If Cosmar's breastwork is destroyed, then he stands quite bare, and his ruin is decided, even though in that crossed-out codicil God knows what measures of security he may have hedged in for himself.

from Pourtales an answer has still come in, and therefore B. hopes with the greatest confidence that he will soon come himself. The coal matter will yet cause me great sorrow, for the von Kayser is my move, and Landskoy is selling everything, and has from there mightily — Amstetten and Zerboni follow good examples.

Of the end of the Congress there is still nothing to be heard; rather the labours on account of the Napoleonic affair are piling up greatly, and prevent all other business being taken up. People do say the joke will not last long; nevertheless 60000 Frenchmen and 30000 Austrians must march thither — the robber is said now to be lurking with his band in the mountains and to be keeping quite still there. He had [uncertain: with difficulty] pushed forward as far as Gap on the road to Grenoble towards Lyon, but since Count Stuben threatened him in Mükken he withdrew in all haste as far as Castellane — and plays at hiding in the mountains. For the rest, he is now declared an outlaw, and since his officers or adherents, if they do not return within 8 days, are to share the same fate with him, it is believed that his own people will seize him and hand him over.

At bottom some cunning head must after all have led the poor fool onto the ice — for he received on Elba the most certain intelligence that the Congress had broken up and that all the Sovereigns had parted on the 13th of February in the greatest bitterness, and that one had at once declared war on the other. — The good

Empress Mar: Lein is said to be very distressed, and truly the poor woman is now to be pitied. Her court household, which still consisted of pure Frenchmen, has been dismissed at once, and pure German persons given to her — the reason is not stated. — Just now they say here that Napoleon has after all pushed forward as far as Grenoble and that a whole regiment of Frenchmen has already gone over to him. Likewise also a regiment of Pumonteſer — It would nevertheless be singular if a man totally [uncertain: despised] should still find such a following.

At this moment a resolution concerning the Polish estates is again in the works for me — the State Chancellor says one must have patience until it has been decided what is to be determined regarding those estates which the Poles took from the donatories after the Peace of Tilsit. It is dreadful how all this drags on. Oh, if only 1/2 p Eberhard could come here, so that we might settle the matter with the Con- heirs, then at least the most essential thing would have been done. In 14 days the Sovereigns intend to depart from here. In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's

I lie sick today in Eichen.

Vienna, the 18th of March

1815

most humble and obedient servant

von Triebenfeld
