--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Complains of long silence and want of funds while lobbying in Vienna, where the Prince Chancellor is inaccessible and the attendants who really decide must be entertained nightly at 20–30 Rthl; he asked 1400 Rthl for four or five weeks but has had only 640, owes Barbe 94 ducats, and demands 100 ducats more. Lists six benefits promised: recovery of the Polish estates, shedding Leixner and Meyer Bernhard, discharge of the Curish debt of 461000 Rthl, and release from the Koschentin bonds. Enclosed receipts for monies from Referendarius Bally; begs 200 Rthl for his starving children; asks letters be sent via Chief Post Director Mann in Vienna and the Koschentin sale kept secret from creditors.

It is very sad that I must always wait so long for an answer and for support. Since everything is going so exceedingly happily through the honest Be endeavour, I do not willingly wish to break off, but rather to bring everything to the desired goal – that this cannot now be done here, in these times, with empty hands, is surely easy enough to judge; daily I must be together with the men who actually do the work, for the Prince Chancellor is too seldom to be seen, and in truth has no time to occupy himself with matters of this kind. Only these good men who are always about him must alone do the work and direct everything — no evening passes on which they do not visit me - 20-30 Rthl go the way, I know not where they end up - it is said that on good news a good draught tastes well, and truly I give it to him gladly, men of that sort deserve it of him - I believed I should be here 4 - 5 weeks, and for that I asked 1400 Rthl. Now I have already been sitting here about 12 [uncertain: weeks] and in all that long time I have received 640 Rthl - today I am already 94 ducats in debt to Barbe. Even if you now send 100 ducats, what remains to me, for I must hand over his money to Barbe – It then amounts to 940 Rthl that I have received, but that certainly and truly does not suffice for so madly long a time. My affairs stand badly there; I am forsaking my children and my own, for since everything is at last going happily, I will now also bring your affairs entirely to the desired goal - and rescue you wholly, as it has always been my wish - after which I may with every right hope for rescue from you in turn, most gracious Prince - It is no small thing that I have already done here, and am still

to bring about.

You must get your Polish estates back again; by that you will get air and throw off Leixner and the S. Jew Meyer Bernhard.
