--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Complains that a messenger put an earlier letter in the post at Pol Wartemberg instead of carrying it by express to the district administrator König, and asks the Prince to direct his awaited reply to Dresden, marked to lie at the post office until called for: Triebenfeld must leave that night for Naumburg an der Saale, where his eldest son lies shot through both thighs. With the Prince's letter to the Emperor he promises to recover Trąbczyn and Kaemen. Trzciński, König, Zerboni and Meierowitz all evade him; only Meierowitz faces execution; Radke is hemmed in by some 80,000 Russians.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord, Your Most Serene Highness, I wrote lately by a messenger, sent the letter to the district administrator König and could and can and can also forward the same to him by an express to Schlawenschi - but the fellow did not do this, and instead put the letter into the post at Pol Wartemberg. For some days now I have been hoping for a gracious reply from Your Serene Highness. Now I can wait no longer, I must set out tonight for Dresden, for my eldest son lies wounded at Naumburg an der Saale and has been shot through both thighs. I am therefore going thither, as said above, today by way of Dresden, and shall be back in Dresden within twelve days at the most. May Your Most Serene Highness only be pleased to send the letter now to Dresden, and most graciously have it noted upon the address that the letter should remain lying at the post office until I myself call for it. – As soon as I have the letter from Your Serene Highness to His Majesty the Emperor, drafted [uncertain: Fieder] in the manner set out, I shall certainly get Trąbczyn and Kaemen back. Neither Trzciński nor König nor Zerboni nor Meierowitz have I to this day set

eyes upon, and even to all my letters I receive nothing but dizzying evasions. – Against Meierowitz alone execution has been laid. – Radke cannot get away at present, for in his neighbourhood there stand some 80000 Russians. Perhaps matters will go better when I return from Dresden. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's Kalisz

the 29th of April 1813 most humble and obedient servant v Triebenfeld
