--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Lord! Gladly do I endure all the gossip of fools, if only I may see Your Most Serene Highness's affair brought to the wished-for end; and that this will come about within a couple of months I pledge to Your Serene Highness upon my honour and my property. That grass-speaker will meanwhile do the least of all towards it, for I believe, and regard, the matter as settled. A man wholly insignificant and unknown will be the one to decide the affair. This I cannot entrust to paper — Each thing I have had the grace [text lost] to the Princess's Serene Highness [text lost] to convince of the truth. The whole affair will resolve itself of its own accord, and will be brought to an end for Your Most Serene Highness as honourably as it is advantageous — that an assessment of Your Serene Highness's estates is being obstructed springs from the source most fully known to Your Serene Highness

and it is precisely from that quarter that the whole thing is to be called a mere matter of grace — My sense of honour, however, always rebels against everything that carries so much as an appearance of begging, and in this regard I go quite quietly and steadfastly forward along the straight road marked out for me; and I am certain that, for all the prattle, I shall reach the goal of my wishes. The Monarch wishes Your Most Serene Highness well, and the exchange has the King's approval, provided the estates hold the balance one against the other — thus the balance is what decides it — and the King's friendship does no more here than that, for estates lying far off, nearer ones are given; and to thank the Monarch for that is no disgrace. As for the equality of the estates, provision has been made — and in this way everything will come about of itself. Count Schlabrendorff I have persuaded so far that he will take from me, for the interest payable to him, a bill of exchange payable in a year's time. I go to him on Sunday to conclude this business. Everything else I shall attend to in the best manner. For the rest, I still note humbly that Prittwitz

is a pitiable liar — the man said not a word further to me, and that in the presence of Wildegans — save: My God, Her Serene Highness torments me, I am to take the matter in hand — I have already told the gentleman that I cannot do this, and I shall write it once more to Her Serene Highness. Since I now knew everything better, I let the fool talk and sought to advance the matter for myself. My sense of honour would never permit speeches of this kind, and the discourse could not have ended so humiliatingly on my side as he wishes to boast. Behind my back, however, the man may say what he pleases; I shall bear it all in silence, gladly and willingly, until we are at the goal of our wishes — Let Your Serene Highness grant me only the continuance of Your most high good will, and so I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's. About the 16th of April I go from here to Berlin, where I remain until the end of May. The estates which I sold to Prince George now come out, according to a valuation by a Justice and Chamber Councillor, at over 700000 Rthl, etc. As it was intended that the gentlemen [uncertain: should be brought down] through me by some 500000 Rthl, I am too good-natured a simpleton and often do harm to myself and to my children. Breslau, the 3rd of April 1799, most faithful and humble servant v. Triebenfeld
