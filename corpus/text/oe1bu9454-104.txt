--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Pledges on his honour that the Prince's business will be concluded within two months, honourably and profitably, and insists a valuation of the estates is being blocked from a quarter the Prince knows. The King has approved the proposed exchange provided the estates balance in value, which Triebenfeld says is secured; he refuses anything resembling begging for favour. Count Schlabrendorff will take a bill of exchange at a year for his interest; Prittwitz is denounced as a liar. Estates sold to Prince George are now valued above 700,000 Rthl.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord!

Gladly do I endure all the talk behind my back from Thorn, if only I may see Your Most Serene Highness's affair brought to the desired end; and that this will happen within a couple of months I pledge to Your Serene Highness upon my honour and my property. That grass-speaker will meanwhile do the least of all in the matter, for I believe, and regard, the affair as settled. A person quite insignificant and unknown will decide the matter. To paper I cannot entrust this — Every [thing] I have had the grace to [?] to Her Serene Highness the Princess of the truth. The whole affair will resolve itself of its own accord and will be brought to a conclusion for Your Most Serene Highness as honourable as it is advantageous — that a valuation of Your Serene Highness's estates is to be prevented arises from the source most fully known to Your Highness

known [to Your Highness], and it is precisely from that quarter that they wish it to be called a mere matter of grace. My sense of honour, however, always rebels against everything that carries even the appearance of begging, and in this regard I walk quite quietly and [uncertain: steadfastly] onward along the straight road marked out for me; and I am certain that, despite all the idle chatter, I shall reach the goal of my wishes. The Monarch wishes Your Most Serene Highness well, and the exchange has been approved by the King provided the estates hold the balance against one another — the balance therefore decides it; and the King's friendship does no more in this than that for estates lying far off, nearer ones are given — and there is no shame in thanking the Monarch for that. The equality of the estates is meanwhile provided for — and in this way everything will resolve itself of its own accord. Count Schlabrendorff I have [uncertain: persuaded] to accept from me, for the interest payable to him, a bill of exchange payable in a year's time. I shall go to him on Sunday to settle this business.

Everything else I shall attend to in the best manner. For the rest, I note humbly for myself further that Prittwitz

is a pitiful liar — the man said not a word to me further, and that indeed in the presence of [uncertain: Wildegans] — but rather: My God, Your Serene Highness torments me, I am to take the matter upon myself. I have already told the gentleman that I cannot do this — and I shall also write it once more to Your Serene Highness. Since I now knew everything better, I let the fool talk and sought to advance the matter for myself.

My sense of honour would also never permit speeches of this kind, and the discourse could not have ended so humiliatingly on my part as he wished to boast. Behind my back, however, the man may say what he likes; I shall willingly and gladly bear it all in silence until we are at the goal of our wishes — If Your Serene Highness will but grant me the continuance of Your most gracious good will, then I remain, until death, as Your Most Serene Highness's

About the 16th of April I go from here to Berlin, where I shall remain until the end of May. The estates which I sold to Prince George now come out, according to a valuation by a councillor of justice and a chamber councillor, at over 700000 Rthl etc. Since [uncertain: the gentlemen] meant, through me, to be ruined to the tune of 500000 Rthl, I am too good-natured a fool and often do harm to one and to children.

Breslau, the 3rd of April 1799

faithful, most humble and obedient servant
v. Triebenfeld
