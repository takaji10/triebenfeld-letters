--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Pro Memoria tallying the Prince's claims: the Kaemen estates, granted him in 1796 after Anton v. Prussiemski but pledged to Broniewski for over 100000 Rthl, cost more than 6000 Rthl in a lawsuit lost in two instances and 55000 Rthl paid in compromise in 1804, with interest to 1815 of 33550 Rthl and 4164 Rthl in Senate treasury fees; against a judicial valuation of 180000 Rthl. Trąbczyn, assessed at 316000, carries debts to Lichnowski, Weigel, Grotowski, Seeher Thoch and v. Zerboni of 138000, of which 60000 is repaid. Totals: 94550 Rthl due from the née Prusimska and 367164 Rthl from the Sovereigns.

P[ro]. M[emoria].

In 1796 the King conferred upon the Prince of Hohenlohe the Kaemen estates, after a certain Anton von Prussiemski. The said Prussiemski had pledged these estates to a certain Broniewski for more than 100000 Rthl, so that the Prince could never come into possession of them - the lawsuit pending on that account has cost the Prince over 6000 Rthl. In the first and second instance the Prince lost and was condemned to pay the pledge sum. Broniewski at last offered a settlement, and the Prince paid 55000 Rthl in cash to Broniewski in 1804. - makes 61000 Rthl. On this, then, from 1804 to 1815, the interest is 33550 Rthl, [uncertain: outlays] upon the estates; he has therefore at the most to demand thereon 94550 Rthl.

The house has paid to the [uncertain: Senate Court] treasury for the instrument 4164 Rthl, which must be [made good] to the Prince Z[illegible]erbowski 4164 Rthl. [uncertain: Sinnen] of the adducing of proof 98714. NB Leixner together with M. Bernhard have given precisely the moneys as 55000 Rthl. But the capitals have been registered to them upon Betsche and the Latmian estates, and M. B. also has the Culm[?]ian estates entirely in possession for his claims; consequently the 94550 Rthl belong to the Prince alone, and the born Prusimska, so greatly favoured, must therefore return to the Prince the pledge sum paid together with interest; but the Prince is entitled to retain the estates in pledge-possession until his satisfaction — Now the estates have meanwhile been judicially assessed at 180000 in value; if then the

55000 Rthl be brought into deduction, there remains in our valuation 125000 Rthl
