3 fachen Gewinn gewähren.
Graf Götzen empfiehlt ſich zu Gnaden freut ſich über die Colonnasche Sache und kan ſelbſte

Er habe die Königin als Tochter angenommen, und ihr ſein ganzes Haabe bis auf einigen
Legate vermacht —
Übrigens hat Hoffrath Hahn recht wenn er sagt das Testament ist zur Erhebung der
Erbschaft für der Königin nicht legal genug — Aber nach dem Urtheil eines Klagen
Rechts Freundes kan der Karten leicht zerhauen werden, und dies soll meine Sache
sein. Solte indes der Teichulman die Unterschlagung des Original Testamentes bekennen,
dann wären alle Fehden gehoben — daß ein Testament zu Gunsten der Königin existirt
hat wißen viele Menschen, ſelbſt Gratschreiber, hat vor etwan 3 Wochen hier öf¬

ein Ey dem Gastheim im Neste legen, daß eine Geburt hervorbringen solle die
ihm alles zernagen solle — Es existire ein Testament was nur er, wieße wo es sey.
Indes haben Ewer Durchlaucht die Ihrem vermachten oder logirten 100000 rthlr schon so gut als
im Sache, den nach dem Landrecht
steht § 165 Tit XII. Th. I fast
"Legate, welche den zwanzigsten Theil des Nachlaßes wahrscheinlich, oder nach der Ver¬
sicherung des Erblaßers, nicht übersteigen, können durch eigenhändig geschriebene und
unterſchriebene Codicille, ohne gerichtliche Übergabe verordnet werden."
Da nun Ihre 10000 rt, so wie auch die andern legate den 20 sten ja kaum den 30sten
Theil übersteigen, so steht Ihnen Gnädigster Fürst und Herr schon nichts mehr in Wege.
weil 20 Zeugen die Unterschrift des Colonna anerkennen und beschwören.
Mann könte, wann sich der dumme Bardthe nicht hätte ins Bockshorn jagen laßen, und seine
50000 rt entsagt hätte, einen ſchönen Revach gemacht haben. Noch gebe ich dem Gedanken auch
nicht auf — Wann Sie ihm gnädigster Fürst und Herr errathen, so bewahren Sie ihm als
das tiefste Geheimnis bis ich zurük komme. Ich bitte sehr darum —
Was dem PostMeister Schulz in Strelitz anbetrift, so halte ich den Kerl vor einem
Windbeutel. Der E. behauptete daß das Anonime Schreiben vom Bardtke und
der Brief deßen Hand sey — Das ist nicht wahr, die Gräfin Henckel auf
Schimanowitz hat den Brief geschrieben und alles an der Fürſtin Sacken geſchickt
Es ist das Henckelsche Waapen und der Gräfin ihre Hand — Sie hatt die Papiere
vom Jungen Harraszowski der viel in Ihren Hause, und gerne von ihr gesehen ist.
Sie hatte als Anverwandte mit sehr auf einen Theile des Colonnaſchen Nachlaßes gerechnet —
Jezt will sie sich rächen — Es sollen aus dieser Quelle noch viele Nachrichten zu schöpfen seyn.
Sie geruhen, gnädigster Fürst und Herr Sich zu überzeugen, daß ich glücklicher im Auffinden
bei als Sie — ich bin auch gewis daß ich alles am erwünschten Ziele bringen werde,
Nur eine Sache beunruhiget mich. Der Graf Götzen wünscht sehr, daß man diese Erbschafts Angen
legenheit jezt nicht mit zu großer Heftigkeit betreiben möchte. weil dadurch der Gräf Wra¬
tislaw, der mit dem Erz Bischoff von / ich glaube Bruna / alles über den Kayser vermag, und
diesen ganz nach unsern Willen leitet, aigrirt, und der großen Sache schädlich werden könte.

bald melden müße, aber er räth alle Behutsamkeit an, ehe man die Sache nicht mit
Eclat zur Publizität bringen kan.
Ich habe nun den Vorschlag gemacht — die Königin nimmt die Güter die a 4 pc gerechnet
an 3 Millionen werth sind — und die Gastheim haben solte — Kan Wratislaw so viel würken
ſo läßt man dem das Geld wovon noch die Legata berichtiget werden müßen.
auf der Art wäre alles beseitiget, Wratislaw nicht nur beruhiget, sondern er

Güter und Bestände müste Gastheim zurückgewähren, auch Reibnitz Gärtner und Gebüh¬
ter die 150000 rt wieder vom sich geben.
Ich habe Graf Götzen ein Schreiben an der Königin aufgesezt, worin er ihr zu
der Erbschaft Glück wünscht, übrigens ihr seine Besorgniße wegen den Wratislaw
Mittheilt um den vorgenannten Vorschlag macht. Er schlägt dabey vor, daß die

Zeit in Kontop eintrift, außerdem müßte ich wie eine demontirte Lauvun[?]

--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld berichtet, van der Lahr gehe nun auch in Breslau gegen ihn vor. Er wartet auf 1000 Centner Eisen in Konotop. Zur Colonnaschen Erbschaft für die Königin kann Graf Götzen Zeuge sein, rät aber wegen Graf Wratislaw zur Zurückhaltung. Triebenfeld schlägt vor, die Königin solle die Güter von an 3 Millionen Wert nehmen und Wratislaw das Geld lassen. Für Götzen hat er ein Schreiben an die Königin entworfen. Dazu Kriegsnachrichten und Bethusys Schulden von an 500000 Rthl.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld reports that van der Lahr is now proceeding against him in Wrocław as well. He is waiting for 1,000 Centner of iron at Konotop. On the Colonna inheritance for the Queen, he says Count Götzen can serve as a witness, but advises restraint because of Count Wratislaw. Triebenfeld proposes that the Queen should take the estates, worth about 3 million, and leave Wratislaw the money. For Götzen he has drafted a letter to the Queen. With this, war news and Bethusy's debts of about 500,000 Rthl.

55

Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

At this very moment, as I return from Kłodzko, I find before me Your Most Serene Highness's two letters of the 7th and 9th of this month together with their enclosures — that van der Lahr is now attacking me here as well, Your Most Serene Highness will have seen from Rho[uncertain: ]ens's letter — what I suffer thereby only You Yourself, with the most [uncertain: gentle] feelings peculiar to You, can feel. — Meanwhile this pond too will be drained — if only the 1,000 Centner of iron were soon at Konotop. The securities are falling greatly now and this adds to my distress; in the meantime this [uncertain: ſniken] shows Your Serene Highness, as well as myself, a happy future, if only I have got these cares out of the way, for then what now seems little to us will afterwards yield us threefold gain.

Count Götzen commends himself to Your favour, rejoices over the Colonna affair and can himself give evidence — that Colonna told him he had adopted the Queen as his daughter and bequeathed to her his whole property save for a few legacies — For the rest, Court Councillor Hahn is right when he says the will is not legal enough for the Queen to take up the inheritance — But in the judgement of a [uncertain: Klagen] friend at law the [uncertain: Karten] can easily be cut through, and this shall be my business. Should meanwhile the [uncertain: Teichulman] confess the suppression of the original will, then all quarrels would be settled — that a will in the Queen's favour has existed is known to many people; even [uncertain: Gratschreiber] recounted publicly here some three weeks ago — that Reibnitz and the Government councillors at Brzeg ought all to be hanged, for having delivered the inheritance over to unlawful heirs. He alone would lay an egg in the Gastheim's nest which should bring forth a birth that would gnaw everything away from him — There exists a will of which he alone knew where it was.

Meanwhile, Your Serene Highness has the 100,000 Rthl bequeathed or lodged to You as good as in [uncertain: hand], for according to the Landrecht

there stands § 165, Tit. XII, Pt. I, almost: "Legacies which probably, or according to the testator's assurance, do not exceed the twentieth part of the estate may be ordered by codicils written and signed in the testator's own hand, without judicial delivery." Now since Your 10,000 Rthl, as well as the other legacies, exceed hardly the 20th, indeed hardly the 30th part, nothing at all any longer stands in Your way, Most gracious Prince and Lord, because 20 witnesses acknowledge and will swear to Colonna's signature. One might, had the stupid Bardthe not let himself be frightened out of his wits and renounced his 50,000 Rthl, have made a handsome profit. I do not even now give up the notion — If You, Most gracious Prince and Lord, guess it, keep it as the deepest secret until I come back. I beg this most earnestly — As for the postmaster Schulz in Strelitz, I hold the fellow to be a windbag. E. maintained that the anonymous letter was from Bardtke and that the letter was in his hand — That is not true; Countess Henckel of Schimanowitz wrote the letter and sent everything to the Princess Sacken. It is the Henckel arms and the Countess's own hand — She had the papers from young Harraszowski, who is much in her house and gladly seen by her. As a kinswoman she had very much counted on a share of the Colonna estate — Now she means to avenge herself — Many further items of intelligence are said to be drawn from this source. May You, Most gracious Prince and Lord, be pleased to satisfy Yourself that I am more fortunate in discovery than You — I am also certain that I shall bring everything to the desired end. Only one matter disquiets me. Count Götzen wishes very much that this inheritance business should not be pursued just now with too great vehemence, because thereby Count Wratislaw — who with the Archbishop of / I believe Bruna / can do anything with the Emperor, and leads him quite according to our will — might be embittered, and could become harmful to the great cause. Count Götzen begs that the inheritance affair be held back a few weeks more, until it is first known what party our [uncertain: Höst] will take. He finds it right that it must soon be reported to the Queen, but he counsels all caution, until the matter can be brought before the public with éclat.

I have now made the proposal — the Queen takes the estates, which reckoned at 4 per cent are worth some 3 millions — and which the Gastheim were to have — If Wratislaw can accomplish so much

then one leaves him the money, out of which the legacies must still be settled. In that way everything would be disposed of, Wratislaw not only pacified, but he would have to regard it as a favour of the Queen that she left him some 600,000 in cash. Estates and stocks the Gastheim would have to restore, and Reibnitz, Gärtner and Gebühter likewise to give back the 150,000 Rthl.

I have drawn up for Count Götzen a letter to the Queen, in which he congratulates her on the inheritance, and for the rest communicates to her his apprehensions concerning Wratislaw, and makes the aforesaid proposal. He therein proposes that the Queen might submit his proposal to Your Serene Highness's examination, because Your Highness, there, through Your Lord Brother and other acquaintances, could have the sentiments of the great men sitting at the Austrian helm precisely explored in this matter. Count Götzen has promised me to send this letter tomorrow by orderly; I met him yesterday morning already below Kłodzko, where he was hastening to Legnica to investigate an Excellency and to dispose of a matter which might be of favourable consequence. As soon as I have the letter from the Count I shall set off, provided with all weapons, for Königsberg — Heaven grant only that the iron arrives at Konotop at the right time, otherwise I should have to lie idle like a dismounted [uncertain: Lauvun] and let van der Lahr's hirelings keep watch on me; political news here is of many kinds — the war between France and Austria is declared; on our side the intention is / as I have from sure sources / to watch the game until the Austrian troops stand on the Elbe, and then the further payment of the contribution will first be refused, and further declarations made. Without exciting [uncertain: Brint], the organising of the [uncertain: Armen] is going forward with us; until a side has been taken, however, one is to proceed very cautiously. Today it is said here generally that Rüchel, if the King takes part in the war, is to have the command in Silesia — He has arrived at his sister-in-law's at Cossar below Krosno Odrzańskie, where he will remain in secret until he receives the orders from the King, upon which he will at once travel round all the fortresses in Silesia and the frontiers.

An officer who served under his regiment is with him at Cossar, and has now arrived here, who has trumpeted out this news — for the honour of humanity I am unwilling to believe these ravings

Mr von Massow has, four weeks ago, again written asking for his discharge, because he has been so restricted and subordinated to Count Dohna that he cannot even issue a pass for relay horses — And as the councillor of war Merckel, who returned from Königsberg the day before yesterday, assures me, Massow will receive his discharge with the remark — that he must meanwhile continue to serve until another subject qualified for this post has been found. If Geil and Ign. do not raise too strong a dam, the hope of seeing Hard: here may be revived afresh. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's. Bethusy lies very ill at Matzdorff; I have meanwhile sent to him and asked him whether he will follow after me to Konotop — I await his answer — Upon all his estates there lie some 500,000 Rthl of debts — But they are said to be worth, as between brothers, 1 million Rthl — that, however, requires a sensible inquiry.

Wrocław

the 12th of April

1809.

most faithful and obedient servant

v Triebenfeld
