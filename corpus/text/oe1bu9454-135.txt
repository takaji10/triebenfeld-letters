Als ich von des regierenden Fürsten u Hohenlohe durch deßelben Gen[eral]. Bevollmächtigten dH. Bau Rath Glenck den
Auftrag erhielt, deßelben in Südpreußen belegene Güter Kaemen und Kolno zu verkaufen; so erhielte ich von Höchst denenselben bei
liegenden bündigem Revers über 2 pc. Provision den ich Er Hochverabschriften und mit den Original völlig gleichlautend, ganz
gehorsamst überreiche. Da ich nun mit Gewisheit erfahren habe, daß der Handel durch den Kammer Rath Ludolff zu Stande gekommen
ist; so würde, Sie doch auf den Grund dieses Reverses verpflichtet sein, um die verschriebene Provision von 2 pc. und zwar aus
der Ursache zu bezahlen, weil ich den Käufer nachgewiesen haben würde, zu Bezahle,
Wenn nun gleich diese Nachweisung vollständig von mir erwiesen werden kan, da ich im Monath Decembr a. c. diese Güter
Kaemen und Kolno Ein Hahn in Vorschlag gebracht, auch dH B[au]. R[aths]. Glenck als bevollen ihr durch Ewr. Hochwohl. persönl. praes. habe
so läßt mich doch die Denkungsart des Bevollen, nicht des Fürsten, der Bekanntermaßen den vortreflichst Charakter
besitzt, besorgen, daß die Verbindlichkeit zur Zahlung dieser mir verschriebenen Provision von 2 pc. mir unter diesen oder
genugleichten Vorwande bestritten werden mögte, und zwar in der Absicht um von diesem mir ausgesezten Honorario eine
bedeutende Summa abzuhandeln. Ich habe dahero den sichersten Weg hiedurch einschlagen und Ewre H. diesen Vorgang durch abschrift
Mittheilung des Fürstl. Reversse, deßen Original u. zu jederzeit zu produciren bereit bin, gehorsamst melden, wollen und den
unterthänigst gehorsamste Bitte zu beifügen. Diese mir von so durch verschriebene, und auf die ersten Kaufgelder angewie¬
seine Provision von 2 pc. von denen ersteren Kaufgeldern gewögentlichst um zu bestellt, und meine Ordre auszahlen zu laßen.
Ich bin von Ewr H. gerechten und billigen Charakter mit Gewisheit erwarten, daß ich, da hochdieselben dieses Verkaufs Geschäft
dirigiren, ohnmöglich wegen diese meine gerechten Ansprüche an denen an Sr Durch zu bezahlenden Kaufgeldern, von Seiten
dHl. B. R. Glenck hintergangen werden kan — und dahero in dem Fall eines würkh verkaufs dieser Güter nicht nötig
haben werde, einen gerichten Arrest auf die Kaufgelder zu extrahiren. Ewr: H. habe sich bisher als meinen Gönner noch
Freund bewiesen. Ihnen sind auch die unglücken Schicksahle, die ich und meine Familie erlitten haben, besonders an den Erbgrafen
v Stollberg bekante, an den ich 30/m rt. verlohren. Leider muß ich, nach solchen Aufopferungen, mich und meine Ihnen bekannte Famil
durch mühsame Bearbeitung dergleichen Geschäfte, von denen unter hunderten wohl eins geräth, unterhalten. Ich beisorge dahero
Da Ew. H: dieses Geschäft elirignen mich von Seiten des Glenck berin chicane ausgesagt zu sein und durch Ew H. Gewogenheit, das
mir von Seiten Sr. D[urchlaucht]. unter deßelben fürstlichen Siegel zugesicherte ArbeitsLohn zu erhalten, wofür es besonders danken,
und erkenntlich sein werde —
darf ich gehorsamst bitte, wie durch ein paar Zeilen zu melden: wie hoch die verabredete Kaufsumme für Kaemen
und Kolno betrage?
Ich verharre mit aller Verehrung.
Ewer Hochwohlgeb.

--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Abschriften zum Verkauf von Kamionna und Kolno. Kabinets Rath Werner fordert beim Cammer Director 2 pc Provision laut Revers des Fürsten zu Hohenlohe vom 17. Dezember 1803, da er einen Käufer vorgeschlagen habe. Er fürchtet Einwände Glencks. Glenck fragt an, ob der Adressat bei einem Wert von 200/m Rthl hinter 100/m Rthl 60000 Rthl als zweite Hypothek leihen würde. Stubenrauch schickt die Papiere zurück und lehnt ab, weil die Güter durch Makler in üblen Ruf gekommen seien.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Copies concerning the sale of Kamionna and Kolno. Cabinet Councillor Werner asks the Chamber Director for 2 pc commission under a written undertaking of the Prince of Hohenlohe of 17 December 1803, since he says he proposed a buyer. He fears objections from Glenck. Glenck enquires whether the addressee would lend 60000 Rthl as a second mortgage behind 100/m Rthl on a value of 200/m Rthl. Stubenrauch returns the papers and declines, saying the estates have fallen into bad repute through brokers.

Right Honourable Sir,

Most highly honoured Chamber Director. When I received from the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe, through his General authorised representative, Mr Glenck, Building Councillor, the commission to sell his estates Kamionna and Kolno situated in South Prussia, I received from His Highness the same, enclosed herewith, binding written undertaking concerning 2 per cent commission, which I most obediently submit to Your Right Honourable [uncertain: Hochverabschriften] and entirely identical with the original. Since I have now learned with certainty that the transaction has been brought about through Chamber Councillor Ludolff, you would nevertheless be obliged, on the ground of this written undertaking, to pay the stipulated commission of 2 per cent, and that for the reason that I should have pointed out the purchaser, to pay. Even though this pointing-out can be fully proved by me, since in the month of December of this year I brought forward these estates Kamionna and Kolno [uncertain: Ein Hahn] in proposal, and also Mr Glenck, Building Councillor, as [uncertain: his authorised representative] through Your Right Honourable personally present, yet the manner of thinking of the authorised representative — not of the Prince, who as is well known possesses the most excellent character — makes me apprehend that the obligation to pay this commission of 2 per cent stipulated to me might be disputed under this or the like pretext, and that with the intention of bargaining down a considerable sum from this honorarium settled upon me. I have therefore hereby wished to take the surest course and most obediently to report this proceeding to Your Honour by communicating a copy of the princely written undertaking, the original of which I am at all times ready to produce, and to append the most humble and obedient request: to arrange most graciously for this commission of 2 per cent, stipulated to me in such wise and assigned upon the first purchase moneys, to be paid out of those first purchase moneys, and to my order. I am to expect with certainty from Your Honour's just and equitable character that, since Your Honour directs this business of sale, it is impossible that I should be deceived on the part of Mr Glenck, Building Councillor, with regard to these my just claims upon the purchase moneys to be paid to His Serene Highness — and that therefore, in the event of an actual sale of these estates, I shall not have need to extract a judicial arrest upon the purchase moneys. Your Honour has hitherto shown himself as my patron and friend. You are also acquainted with the unhappy fates which I and my family have suffered, particularly with the hereditary Count von Stollberg, with whom I lost 30/m Rthl. Unhappily, after such sacrifices, I must maintain myself and my family, known to you, by the laborious working-up of business of this kind, of which perhaps one in a hundred succeeds. I therefore apprehend, since Your Honour [uncertain: directs] this business, that I am [uncertain: exposed] to chicanery on the part of Glenck, and, through Your Honour's favour, to obtain the wages of labour assured to me on the part of His Serene Highness under his princely seal, for which I shall be especially thankful and grateful — may I most obediently beg to be informed in a few lines: how high the agreed purchase sum for Kamionna and Kolno amounts to?

I remain with all reverence,

Your Right Honourable's

most obedient servant,

the

Cabinet Councillor Werner

Berlin, 19 May 1804.

Neue Schönhauser Straße No. 13.

If Chamber Councillor Werner in Berlin shall point out to me, directly or indirectly, a purchaser for my estates Kamionna and Kolno situated in South Prussia, with whom I come to terms of bargain, then I promise the same, or him to whom he may give order and to whom he may assign his rights out of this written undertaking, for this his pointing-out, his exertion, and the service thereby rendered to me, a voluntary remuneratory compensation for exertion, 2, in writing two, per cent of the sum of the purchase price upon which I may come to terms with the purchaser. These shall be paid immediately after conclusion of the contract out of the first purchase moneys, and shall be paid by the seller in payment to the bearer hereof, who has received order from Mr Werner.

This I promise to fulfil under my princely signature and seal. Wrocław, the 17th of December 1803 (L. S.)

Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe, Prince.

Your Honour will permit that I follow up my oral conference of the day before yesterday with a written enquiry also, respecting the purchase and sale of the estates Kolno and Kamionna.

Since my Prince, His Serene Highness, presses on that account for a speedy sale of his estates Kamionna and Kolno, because he must have, 8 days from the next coming Midsummer, over and above the 100000 Rthl needed for the cancellation of the present Broniewski protestation, a further 60/m Rthl for other use — but the purchase of these estates cannot be effected so speedily, inasmuch as a thorough investigation is first to precede; I therefore most submissively enquire — in case, upon the present investigation of the value of the estates Kamionna and Kolno by Mr Ludolff, Councillor of the Order's Commission, it should be found that these can have a true value of 200/m Rthl, whether Your Honour would then be pleased, behind the above-mentioned 100/m Rthl, to lend 60000 Rthl on a second mortgage in such wise that it can be paid 8 days before Midsummer on my Prince's, His Serene Highness's, account; whereby it is understood that my Prince [illegible] must acquiesce in the restrictions of free disposition demanded in return, as also that he can further sell these estates to no one other than the lender of these 60/m Rthl.

I await your command hereupon, as to when I might have the honour of making my personal attendance once again.

Your Honour's

most entirely devoted servant,

Glenck

the 12th of May 1804

at the Golden Eagle, the Square No. 32.

Your Most Noble Honour, I return herewith enclosed all the papers of the estate purchase offered to me. I add to these a note just received from a certain Werner, whom you will kindly put off, because I never engage with people of this sort. In general I regret being unable to serve Your Honour in this matter, inasmuch as these estates, through the manifold offering-about by brokers of every kind, have come into a very ill repute.

Stubenrauch

Berlin, the 22nd of May 1804.
