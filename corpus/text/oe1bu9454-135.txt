Als ich von des regierenden Fürsten u Hohenlohe durch deßelben Gen. Bevollmächtigten dH. Bau Rath Glenck den
Auffrag erhielt, deßelben in Südpreußen belegene Güter Kaemen und Kolno zu verkaufen; so erhielte ich von Höchst denenselben bei
liegenden bündigem Revers über 2 pl. Provision den ich Er Hochverabschriften und mit den Original völlig gleichlautend, ganz
gehorsamst überreiche. Da ich nun mit Gewisheit erfahren habe, daß der Handel durch den Kammer Rath Ludolff zu Stande gekommen
ist; so würde, Sie doch auf den Grund dieses Reverses verpflichtet sein, um die verschriebene Provision von 2 pl. und zwar aus
der Ursache zu bezahlen, weil ich den Käufer nachgewiesen haben würde, zu Bezahle,
Wenn nun gleich diese Nachweisung vollständig von mir erwiesen werden kan, da ich im Monath Decembr a. c. diese Güter
Kaemen und Kolno Ein Hahn in Vorschlag gebracht, auch den 6 t. G. Glenck als bevollen ihr durch Ewr. Hochwohl. persönl. praes. habe
so läßt mich doch die Denkungsart des Bevollen, nicht des Fürsten, der Bekanntermaßen den vortreflichst Charakter
besitzt, besorgen, daß die Verbindlichkeit zur Zahlung dieser mir verschriebenen Provision von 2 jul. mir unter diesen oder
genugleichten Vorwande bestritten werden mögte, und zwar in der Absicht um von diesem mir ausgesezten Honorario eine
bedeutende Cumm abzuhandeln. Ich habe dahero den sichersten Weg hiedurch einschlagen und Ewre H. diesen Vorgang durch abschrift
Mittheilung des Fürstl. Reverske, deßen Original u. zu jederzeit zu produciren bereit bin, gehorsamst melden, wollen und den
unterthänigst gehorsamste Bitte zu beifügen. Diese mir von so durch verschriebene, und auf die ersten Kaufgelder angewie¬
seine Provision von 2 pl. von denen ersteren Kaufgeldern gewögentlichst um zu bestellt, und meine Ordre auszahlen zu laßen.
Ich bin von Ewr H. gerechten und billigen Charakter mit Gewisheit erwarten, daß ich, da hochdieselben dieses Verkaufs Geschäft
dirigiren, ohnmöglich wegen diese meine gerechten Ansprüche an denen an Sr Durch zu bezahlenden Kaufgeldern, von Seiten
dHl. B. W. Glenck hintergangen werden kan - und dahero in dem Fall eines würkh verkaufs dieser Güter nicht nötig
haben werde, einen gerichten Arrest auf die Kaufgelder zu extrahiren. Ewr: H. habe sich bisher als meinen Gönner noch
Freund bewiesen. Ihnen sind auch die unglücken Schicksahle, die ich und meine Familie erlitten haben, besonders an den Erbgrafen
v Stollberg bekante, an den ich 30000 Rthl verlohren. Leider muß ich, nach solchen Aufopferungen, mich und meine Ihnen bekannte Famil
durch michsame Bearbeitung dergleichen Geschäfte, von denen unter hunderten wohl eins geräth, unterhalten. Ich beisorge dahero
Da Ew. H: dieses Geschäft elirignen mich von Seiten des Glenck berin chicane ausgesagt zu sein und durch Ew H. Gewogenheit, das
mir von Seiten Fr. D. unter deßelben fürstlichen Siegel zugesicherte ArbeitsLohn zu erhalten, wofür es besonders danken,
und erkenntlich sein werde —
darf ich gehorsamst bitte, wie durch ein paar Zeilen zu melden: wie hoch die verabredete Kaufsumme für Kaemen
und Kolno betrage?
Ich verharre mit aller Verehrung.
Ewer Hochwohlgeb.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A dossier of four enclosures on the attempted sale of Kaemen and Kolno in South Prussia: Chamber Councillor Werner of Berlin claims his 2 per cent commission from the first purchase monies, citing the Prince's sealed undertaking of 17 December 1803 (transcribed here), alleging he named the purchaser through Hahn and fearing chicanery from plenipotentiary Glenck, and recalling 30,000 Rthl lost on Hereditary Count v Stolberg; Glenck seeks 60,000 Rthl on second mortgage behind 100,000 Rthl for the Braniuschisch protestation; Stubenrauch declines, the estates being discredited by brokers.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Right Honourable Sir, most highly honoured Chamber Director. When I received from the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe, through his General Plenipotentiary, the Herr Building Councillor Glenck, the commission to sell his estates Kaemen and Kolno situated in South Prussia, I received from His Most [Serene] Highness the same, herewith enclosed, binding written undertaking for 2 pl. commission, which I most obediently present to Your Right Honourable in transcript, entirely identical with the original. Since I have now learned with certainty that the transaction has been brought about through the Chamber Councillor Ludolff, you would nevertheless be bound on the ground of this written undertaking to pay the stipulated commission of 2 pl., and that for the reason that I should have pointed out the purchaser, to pay. Even though this pointing-out can be fully proved by me, since in the month of December of the current year I proposed these estates Kaemen and Kolno to one Hahn, and also on the 6th [uncertain: G.] Glenck as plenipotentiary through Your Right Honourable's personal presentation, yet the manner of thinking of the plenipotentiary — not of the Prince, who as is well known possesses the most excellent character — gives me cause to fear that the obligation to pay this commission of 2 jul. stipulated to me might be contested under this or some like pretext, and that with the intention of bargaining away a considerable sum from this honorarium set out for me. I have therefore chosen the surest way hereby, and have wished most obediently to report this occurrence to Your Honour by communicating a copy of the Princely written undertaking, the original of which I am ready to produce at any time, and to add thereto the most humble and obedient request: to have this commission of 2 pl., stipulated to me by so [text lost] and assigned upon the first purchase monies, most graciously ordered out of the said first purchase monies, and paid out to my order. I may with certainty expect of Your Honour's just and equitable character that, since Your Honour directs this business of sale, I cannot possibly be deceived on the part of the Herr B. W. Glenck in respect of these my just claims upon the purchase monies to be paid to His Serene Highness — and that therefore, in the event of an actual sale of these estates, I shall have no need to extract a judicial [uncertain: arrest] upon the purchase monies. Your Honour has hitherto shown yourself my patron and friend. You are also acquainted with the unhappy fates which I and my family have suffered, particularly in the matter of the Hereditary Count v Stolberg, on whom I lost 30000 Rthl. Sadly, after such sacrifices, I must maintain myself and my family, known to you, by the laborious working-up of such transactions, of which perhaps one in a hundred succeeds. I therefore apprehend — since Your Honour [illegible] this business — that I am exposed to chicanery on the part of Glenck, and [hope] through Your Honour's favour to obtain the wages of labour assured to me on the part of the Prince, His Serene Highness, under his princely seal, for which I shall be especially grateful and appreciative — may I most obediently request that it be reported to me in a few lines: how high the agreed purchase sum for Kaemen and Kolno amounts to? I remain with all reverence, Your Right Honourable's most obedient servant, the Chamber Councillor Werner. B[erlin?] the 19th May 1804. neue Schönhauser Straße No. 13.

If the Chamber Councillor Werner in Berlin shall point out to me, mediately or immediately, a purchaser for my estates Kaemen and Kolno situated in South Prussia, with whom I come to terms of trade, then I promise to him, or to whomsoever he may give order and to whom he may wish to speak of his rights out of this written undertaking, for this his pointing-out, his exertion, and the service thereby rendered me, a voluntary remuneratory compensation for exertion, 2, written two, Rht. of the [uncertain: sum] of the purchase price upon which I may come to terms with the purchaser. These shall be paid immediately after the contract is concluded, out of the first purchase monies, and shall be paid to the seller for payment to the bearer hereof, who shall have received order from Herr Werner. This I promise to fulfil under my Princely signature and seal. Breslau the 17th Xbr. 1803. L. S. Friedrich Ludwig Fürst zu Hohenlohe.

Your Honour will permit that, following upon my verbal conference of the day before yesterday, I let a written enquiry follow concerning the purchase and sale of the estates Kolno and Kaemen. Since my Lord, His Serene Highness, on that account presses for a speedy sale of his estates Kaemen and Kolno, because such [monies], 8 days before the next coming St John's day, besides the 100000 Rthl required for the cancellation of the present Braniuschisch protestation, must [text lost] still be had for other use — but the purchase of these estates cannot be effected so speedily, inasmuch as a thorough investigation is first to precede — I most humbly enquire: in case, upon [uncertain: thorough] investigation of the value of the estates Kaemen and Kolno by the Herr Councillor of the Order Commission Ludolff, it should be found that such can have a proper value of 200000 Rthl; whether Your Honour would then be pleased to lend, behind the above-mentioned 100000 Rthl, 60000 Rthl upon a second mortgage, in such wise that they can be paid 8 days before St John's day on the account of my Lord, His Serene Highness — against which it is of course understood that my Lord, His Serene Highness, must submit both to the restrictions of free disposition demanded in return, and also to this, that these estates can further be sold to no other than to the lender of these 60000 Rthl. I await your command upon this, and whether I may perhaps have the honour of making my personal attendance once again. Your Honour's most devoted servant, Glenck. the 12th May 1804, at the Golden Eagle on the Square No 32.

Your Right Noble and Honourable Sir, I return herewith enclosed all the papers concerning the estate transaction offered to me. I add to these a note just now received from a certain Werner, whom you will kindly put off, since I never have dealings with people of this sort. In general I regret that I am unable to serve Your [Honour] in this matter, inasmuch as these estates have fallen into very ill repute through the manifold hawking about by brokers of every kind. Stubenrauch. B[erlin?] the 22nd May 1804.
