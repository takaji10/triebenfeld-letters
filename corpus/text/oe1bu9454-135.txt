ist; so würde, Sie doch auf den Grund dieses Reverses verpflichtet sein, um die verschriebene Provision von 2 pl. und zwar aus

so läßt mich doch die Denkungsart des Bevollen, nicht des Fürsten, der Bekanntermaßen den vortreflichst Charakter
besitzt, besorgen, daß die Verbindlichkeit zur Zahlung dieser mir verschriebenen Provision von 2 jul. mir unter diesen oder

seine Provision von 2 pl. von denen ersteren Kaufgeldern gewögentlichst um zu bestellt, und meine Ordre auszahlen zu laßen.
Ich bin von Ewr H. gerechten und billigen Charakter mit Gewisheit erwarten, daß ich, da hochdieselben dieses Verkaufs Geschäft
dirigiren, ohnmöglich wegen diese meine gerechten Ansprüche an denen an Sr Durch zu bezahlenden Kaufgeldern, von Seiten

Da Ew. H: dieses Geschäft elirignen mich von Seiten des Glenck berin chicane ausgesagt zu sein und durch Ew H. Gewogenheit, das

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Chamber councillor H. K. Werner claims a 2 per cent commission on the sale of the Prince of Hohenlohe's South Prussian estates Kaemen and Kolno, enclosing a copy of the Prince's sealed undertaking of 17 December 1803 and threatening a judicial attachment on the purchase money if the general attorney Glenck disputes it. Also filed: Glenck's enquiry about a 60,000 Rthl second mortgage behind 100,000 Rthl on estates valued at 200,000 Rthl, and Stubenrauch's refusal, the estates being in ill repute from repeated touting.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Right Honourable Sir,

Most highly honoured Chamber Director. When I received from the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe, through his general attorney, Mr. Building Councillor Glenck, the commission to sell his estates Kaemen and Kolno situated in South Prussia, I received from His Highness the same enclosed binding written undertaking for 2 pl. commission, which I most obediently present to Your Right Honourable in copy, entirely conformable with the original. Since I have now learned with certainty that the transaction has been brought about through the chamber councillor Ludolff, you would nevertheless be bound on the ground of this written undertaking to pay the stipulated commission of 2 pl., and that for the reason that I should have pointed out the purchaser, to pay. Although this demonstration can be fully proved by me, since in the month of December of the current year I proposed these estates Kaemen and Kolno to a certain Hahn, and also on the 6th [uncertain: instant] proposed Mr. Glenck as authorised, through Your Right Honourable's personal presentation, yet the cast of mind of the attorney — not of the Prince, who is known to possess the most excellent character — makes me apprehensive that the obligation to pay this commission of 2 jul. stipulated to me might be contested under this or a like pretext, and indeed with the intention of bargaining down a considerable sum from this honorarium settled upon me. I have therefore chosen the surest course hereby, and wish most obediently to report this proceeding to Your Honour by communicating a copy of the princely written undertaking, the original of which I am at all times ready to produce, and to add the most humble and obedient request: to have this commission of 2 pl., stipulated to me by such means and assigned upon the first purchase moneys, most graciously ordered from those first purchase moneys and paid out to my order. I may with certainty expect of Your Honour's just and equitable character that, since Your Honour directs this business of sale, it is impossible that I should be defrauded, as regards these my just claims upon the purchase moneys payable to His Serene Highness, on the part of Mr. B. W. Glenck — and that therefore, in the event of an actual sale of these estates, I shall not have need to procure a judicial attachment upon the purchase moneys. Your Honour has hitherto shown himself to be my patron and friend. You are also acquainted with the unfortunate fates which I and my family have suffered, particularly with the hereditary Count von Stollberg, with whom I lost 30000 Rthl. Unhappily, after such sacrifices, I must maintain myself and my family, known to you, by laborious working-up of such businesses, of which perhaps one in a hundred succeeds. I therefore apprehend, since Your Honour [uncertain: directs] this business, that I am exposed to chicanery on the part of Glenck, and [hope] through Your Honour's favour to obtain the wages of labour assured to me on the part of the Prince His Serene Highness under his own princely seal, for which I shall be especially thankful and grateful — may I most obediently request that you inform me in a few lines: how high the agreed purchase sum for Kaemen and Kolno amounts to?

I remain with all reverence,

Your Right Honourable's

most obedient servant

the

H. K. Werner. B[erlin?], 19th May 1804.

neue Schönhauser Straße No. 13.

If the chamber councillor Werner in Berlin shall, mediately or immediately, point out to me a purchaser for my estates Kaemen and Kolno situated in South Prussia, with whom I come to terms of trade, then I promise to the same, or to him to whom he may give order and assign his rights out of this written undertaking, for this his indication, his exertion and the service thereby rendered to me, a voluntary remuneratory compensation for his trouble of 2, written two, Rht. of the sum of the purchase price upon which I may come to terms with the purchaser. These shall be paid immediately after the contract is concluded, out of the first purchase moneys, and shall be paid to the seller for payment to the bearer of this, who has received order from Mr. Werner.

This I promise to fulfil under my princely signature and seal. Breslau, the 17th December 1803.

L. S. Friedrich Ludwig, Prince of Hohenlohe.

Your Honour will permit that I follow up my oral conference of the day before yesterday with a further written enquiry touching the purchase and sale of the estates Kolno and Kaemen.

Since my Prince His Serene Highness presses for a speedy sale of his estates Kernen and Rullno, because such [moneys], 8 days from next coming Midsummer, over and above the 100000 Rthl required for the cancellation of the present Braniuschisch protestation, must still be [available] for other use — but the purchase of these estates cannot be effected so speedily, inasmuch as a thorough investigation is first to precede — I most humbly enquire: in the event that upon the [uncertain: thorough] investigation of the value of the estates Kaemen and Kolno by the Mr. Order Commission Councillor Ludolff it should be found that such may have a true value of 200000 Rthl, whether Your Honour would then be pleased, behind the above-mentioned 100000 Rthl, to lend 60000 Rthl on second mortgage in such manner that they may be paid 8 days before Midsummer on account of my Prince His Serene Highness — against which it is understood that my Prince must submit to the restrictions of free disposition thereupon demanded, as also that he may not further sell these estates to any other than to the lender of these 60000 Rthl.

I await your command herein, as to whether I might have the honour of paying my personal attendance once again.

Your Honour's

most humbly devoted servant

Glenck

12th May 1804

at the Golden Eagle on the Square No. 32.

Your Right Honourable, I return herewith enclosed all the papers of the estate transaction offered to me. I add to these a note just received from a certain Werner, whom you will kindly put off, since I never have dealings with people of that sort. In general I regret that I cannot serve Your Honour in this matter, inasmuch as these estates have fallen into very ill repute through the manifold offering-about by [uncertain: Mädler] of every kind.

Stubenrauch
