--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports falling ill with bilious fever in Posen and denounces the Bruniewski settlement as so faulty it gave security "not worth a Groschen"; it must be redrawn, but Bruniewski's wife is untraceable, said to be at Kollo or Warschau. Accuses Wünster of colluding with Bruniewski and Guderian and foisting the compromise on the simple Glenck. The Jew M Bernhard and Simon offer only 6000 instead of 13000 Rthl; pledges to press Metzer for the full 66000 Rthl deposit and bring 30000 Rthl from Betsche plus 13000 Rthl.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Through all the vexation I have contracted a bad bilious fever and must keep to my bed here in Posen. The affairs are all so tangled that on every road one meets with so many matters as keep forcing one back from the goal. Thus I found the situation here in respect of the Bruniewski business — Not enough that the accursed compromise has been concluded. Nine! It is so defective that it afforded no security worth a Groschen; all this is to be cleared away and altered, and for that the wife of Bruniewski is required, and she is God knows where — now it is said she is in Kollo, now she is in Warsaw. In short, the people are out for nothing but to [uncertain: bear] Your Serene Highness.

And frankly confessed, I hold Wanstern to be the worst rogue of all — He is forever thick with Bruniewski and Guderian, assuredly receives a couple of thousand d from the former, and has therefore forged the most disadvantageous compromise, slipped the same into Glenck's hands so that he had to conclude it — but he himself has full prudently made off out of the dust, so as to be able afterwards to lay everything upon the simple Glenck. The Jew M. Bernhard is here with Simon. These sharpers would pay Your Serene Highness only 6000 Rthl instead of 13000 Rthl — but this shall not succeed for these knaves — First Bruniewski alone shall furnish sufficient security, which is promised for tomorrow — then one shall press at once with Metzer for the deposit of the full 66000 Rthl, against which

Bruniewski shall at once judicially concede the delivery of possession of the estates; then the Jew and consorts are satisfied in this matter. Thereafter Baragnieur takes his 53000 Rthl and Glenck the 13000 for Your Serene Highness. In Betsche the Jew wished to undertake a like distraint and to confuse the people's heads — meanwhile he shall succeed there as little as here — My resolve is absolutely to carry through for Your Serene Highness the whole 30000 Rthl out of Betsche and the full 13000 Rthl from here. Thereafter the highest may pay off each according to his desert. That base fellow, the Wünster, I scarcely get to see at all. He has his woman here, has placed her at the theatre, and gives balls for the whole gang so that they may take the buck's hide up among themselves — that costs much, and therefore the knave must strike out on every road to snatch something. Concerning Glenck Your Serene Highness does me great [uncertain: wrong] — I am no cringing sinner, but go gladly straight and open — What I write to Your Serene Highness, I say to him — indeed, he read everything that I write. This, I believe, justifies my conduct. In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Posen, the 4th of August

1804

humble and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld
