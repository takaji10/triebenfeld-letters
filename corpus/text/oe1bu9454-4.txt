--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Berichtet über Napoleons Rückkehr: Grenoble sei in seiner Gewalt, er habe etwa 30/m Mann, auf seinen Kopf seien 10 Millionen Francken gesetzt. Bittet den Fürsten um einen Korbwagen für den Diener von Hoffrath Barbe, der von Sławięcice aus Barbes Schwester aus der Schweiz holen soll, und um ein Fuhrwerk mit 2 Pferden für Glaskisten der Hoffräthe Barbe, Heym und Philipsborn aus Prag. Wünscht Eberhards Kommen, fragt nach F. Augusts Schritten wegen der Vollmacht und erbittet weitere Blanquets.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports on Napoleon's return: he says Grenoble is in his power, that he has about 30/m men, and that 10 million Francken have been put on his head. Asks the Prince for a basket wagon for the servant of Court Councillor Barbe, who is to travel from Sławięcice to fetch Barbe's sister from Switzerland, and for a vehicle with 2 horses for glass crates belonging to the Court Councillors Barbe, Heym and Philipsborn from Prague. Wishes Eberhard would come, asks what steps F. August has taken about the power of attorney, and requests further blank forms.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
4

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord. The Napoleon affair is causing a grievous sensation here, and the heavens threaten daily with clouds more heavy with storm — Meanwhile one [uncertain: hopes] France will crush the head of this basilisk that has broken forth — Others again maintain that Bonap[arte] finds much of a following and daily advances further. Grenoble and Lyon are already in the power of the wicked disturber of the peace; by now he is said to be some 30/m men strong and to have taken to himself the whole of the artillery that stood at Grenoble. Soult, Lefebvre and Bertrand are his greatest adherents — Great sums have been set upon the heads of these rebels. Upon Napoleon's head 10 millions of francs have been set on the part of the government, and Massena has promised to pay 3 millions of francs out of his own fortune in addition — It is believed that this will bear the most fruit.

Court Councillor Barbe, who most respectfully commends himself to Your Serene Highness, will shortly send his servant thither to Sławięcice, who from there is to travel with my horses to Switzerland and fetch his sister, whose husband is being appointed in Berlin in the Bureau of H. E.; He and I beg that Your Serene Highness might be pleased to have a really good basket-wagon given there, over which, however, a ticken awning must be made, with which the servant may travel thither and fetch his sister. — Do let this be done — B[arbe] will assuredly make it good to you — also the 3 Court Councillors from the H. Bureau, Barbe, Heym and Philipsborn, have several little cases of glass lying at Prague, which is to be conveyed to Sławięc-

-ice. They have a free pass for it — so may Your Serene Highness be pleased to give a [uncertain: cart] with 2 horses to fetch the glass to Sławięcice. To my two fellows, the drummer and the coachman, give a pass that they may return to Blizanow — It might be that Your Serene Highness could make use of the coachman there.

That these men, and Barbe, Philipshorn and Heym, will again do some good turn, you may believe, most gracious Lord — for otherwise they would be ashamed to ask all this. Within a few days I am to learn something very good in a couple of matters — There will then be time 3) enough to tell it to Your Serene Highness.

If it were possible in the world, [uncertain: send] Eberhard here — for it is possible that he may settle the matter as he says — the ladies need and seek monies. Eberhard writes just now that I should procure that the Chancellor should have him summoned, give him a relay pass and pay his travelling costs. — O heavens, what do people imagine — I have many a friend, but I could [illegible] and would still see no [illegible] or costs — To give here means, be more blessed than to take. Full of longing I await the news whether Eberhard will come or not. Just so I heartily wish to learn what Prince August has done in respect of the power of attorney — for now the gauntlet is thrown down and the fight must be fought out honestly.

For the rest, Your Serene Highness has not answered us, whether you, should that case arise, would wish yourself there as administrator — Assuredly one will call to mind a man who is to direct everything there. Your Serene Highness should send a few blank forms along as well — they are all gone but the one at the Emperor's — and I could now use some to the highest advantage. But this would have to be done quite entirely. I hope my thousandfold thereto, and the trouble shall nevertheless be rewarded. In deepest respect I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's

faithful and obedient servant

19th March 1815

Now come after Eberhard

v Triebenfeld

Here there is henceforth an ebb and flood of news: at one moment [illegible] the report that Napoleon has been taken — in the following hour he has already marched into Paris with 100000 men, and so the reports shape themselves, according as the Jews and stock-jobbers find it agreeable to their convenience. What meanwhile excites the apprehension of the Most High Lords here are the Bonapartist appeals to the commonest class of men — properly speaking to the rabble; to these he makes great promises and pledges them all the riches and goods of the world — It is feared indeed that this has won him some following, and one would gladly have very sure news out of Poland, how this [uncertain: Na[v?]e] thought there. An innocent Pole said here yesterday, Ah God be thanked, our redeemer [uncertain: draws near] / She is said to be quite mad for joy. What will the Grand Duke now do with his 40000 men?
