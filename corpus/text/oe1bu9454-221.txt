--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that the 18,000 Rthl expected from the Zagorowo estates cannot be sent, since nothing can be got from them until two lawsuits are won; hopes only that the Christmas interest is paid so the moratorium holds. Sets out two rival schemes (A and B) for selling Zagorowo to Stössel, with Count Leopold von Wartensleben, Weigel and Count Sandrelzki involved, an exchange of Blizanow for the Pila estates, and figures totalling 470,000 Rthl. Rejects Constadt and Schurgast.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Your Serene Highness [uncertain: bestohlen] that 18000 Rthl are to be sent to you from the Zagorowo estates. It pains me more than grievously to have to tell Your Serene Highness that for the present this cannot be done. There is a prospect that 2 lawsuits will shortly be won, but until that happens nothing can be got out of the estates. On my knees I shall thank God if only the Christmas interest is paid, so that the moratorium remains in force. For the rest, I have by the most direct route set in train the matter of the alienation of the Zagorowo estates to Stossel.

Count Leopold von Wartensleben has been with me together with young Stössel. They have viewed the estates, and Wartensleben, who has every influence over Stössel (for he is Stössel's son-in-law), has gone to Breslau to bring the business onto its tracks.

My plan is the one under Letter A, and Wartensleben's the one under B. I await at every hour the declaration of the two old fellows. A certain Chief District Administrator König I have sent to Breslau on this account, to put [uncertain: Heymen] in the right frame of mind.

König has at the same time travelled to Count Sendralzki, who may perhaps exchange with me, Blizanow against the Pila estates — the business goes better, for Weigel will not take Blizanow so willingly as the estates there, and Weigel must in my opinion be [uncertain: abholen] settled with at the same time as Stössel. Then Koschentin is saved – you then have 60000 Rthl in Koschentin; these must be given to you out of the monies designated for the entailed estate, and with them you pay all your debts and still have a large sum left over. From another matter you will perhaps get so much that you can pay the Courland sum — the how and the where, by word of mouth. If all succeeds, you will yet live quietly and happily. With Constadt there is nothing to be done — I have viewed the nest, and I will have nothing further to do with it – even Schurgast is a waterlogged hovel and too dear — with Pila it would be best. I hold my Blizanow at 127000 Rthl; 27000 Rthl stand upon it, and consequently 100000 remain.

Sandrelzki holds the Pila estates at 250000 Rthl; 125000 Rthl of debts stand upon them, leaving 125000 clear; consequently Sandrelzki receives (if the matter goes thus) a further 25000 Rthl, which he wants at once in cash, and this Weigel would have to provide; something towards it could also be taken from the Zagorowo arrears once the lawsuits are [uncertain: taken]. In any event Weigel receives by this route 100000 Rthl. On Zagorowo he has 52500 standing. Stossel remains owing to be compensated. 20000. Weigel would still have to receive 17500 Rthl.

Makes 190000 Rthl. Stössel must, as the plan under A shows, take the Zagorowo estates. But he demands, as Leopold Wartensleben says, that Russinowitz be taken along with them, because both families wish to move out of [uncertain: Schlimmeraus] and into Poland. In this way Stössel receives, if he really has so much to claim, 280000

which amounts to

470000 Rthl. These are covered: 1. For Zagorowo

30000

2. For Weigel, entered there, 12500. 3. Through Blizanow or Pila 10000. 4. By the additional payment of the 17500.

Makes

470000 Rthl. If all this goes happily and is brought to an end, then after these days' work I shall come to you, best of Princes, and live and die with you — the 100000 Rthl, as well as what I have already given on account and for [text lost] you will repay me thereafter in instalments, so that it falls burdensome to me, to you, and to Prince August. With most faithful attachment and the deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's — May Your Serene Highness be pleased to send the papers back to me; I have [uncertain: kannen] letter. This moment I am at my writing; I must go to Neisse again.

Blizanow

the 20th of November 1811.

most faithful and humble bondsman v Triebenfeld
