--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Dismisses the estate-purchase scheme put forward through Court Fiscal Michaelis by the Jew Dann, who claims he can bring in Herr v. Eckart, buyer of the Lichtenau houses, as mere brokerage and swindling, already touted to Prince George. Reports that a contumacial decree of the Posen government threatens the recovery of the Brzyzer estates, that the Chambers and forest officials intrigue for the forests, and that the exchange affair is stalled, the Ministers acting en souverain. Urges the Prince, after his fall from his horse, to take care.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Lord! The Court Fiscal Michaelis has communicated to me Your Most Serene Highness's letter concerning the purchase of estates proposed by the Jew Dann. Since, however, the Jew had already been with me before the arrival of this gracious letter, I am in a position to reply to Your Most Serene Highness upon it at once, most deeply obediently: that Dann believes he can persuade Herr v. Eckart, who has bought the Lichtenau houses here, to enter into an Empletu of this kind. This, then, is an ordinary brokering scheme trimmed out with Jewish swindling — for the same Jew has laid this and other such plans before Prince George no less.

For the rest, matters look ill. Following a contumacial decree issued by the Posen government, it is absolutely intended to take the Brzyzer estates back from Your Most Serene Highness — and when there is added to this that the Chambers, together with the forest officials there, do nothing but intrigue over the forests and mean to wrest the same from Your Serene Highness, then on that side all is lost. For my part I cannot see what is to become of the business. On every road the Chambers make chicanery; moreover, proceedings are being framed from several sides, frightful costs and squanderings of money are incurred, and notwithstanding this the end of them cannot be foreseen, and in this respect there is no thinking of any peaceable possession of these estates. The exchange affair too lies stalled on an endless road, and no one gives a thought either to the inspection or to the valuation of the estates; and speaking without circumlocution

I believe that the Ministers here are acting en souverain and would never let the exchange come to pass, unless the Monarch commands it. By a trustworthy man I would report all further matters to Your Serene Highness most humbly. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's —

This very moment I receive Your Most Serene Highness's two gracious letters with the enclosures. — 6 The ill will, of which I shall inform Your Serene Highness by another and safer route, shews itself here only too plainly. This very day he receives an exceedingly polite letter which is said to have been enclosed to Wildeganchen — meanwhile nothing is to be gained by this hero, unless Your Most Serene Highness now concur in everything there with the man who recommended him to the P. — the times are now singular, and in this respect one may speak freely from the heart. Your Serene Highness has, as Eysenhardt tells me, fallen with the horse — for God's sake I beg, Most gracious Prince and Lord, do take some care of yourself.

Berlin, the 25th of May 1799.

Faithful most humble and obedient servant
v. Triebenfeld.
