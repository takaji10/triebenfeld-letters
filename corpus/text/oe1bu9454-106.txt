--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Dismisses a purchase offer relayed through the court fiscal Michaelis as a broker's scheme by the Jew Dann, who claims he can bring in von Eckart, buyer of the Lichtenau houses, and has put similar plans to Prince George. Warns that after a decree for non-appearance by the Posen government the Brzyz estates are to be taken back, that the Chambers and forest officials are intriguing for the woods, that costly suits multiply, and that the ministers will block the exchange unless the monarch orders it.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

The court fiscal Michaelis has communicated to me Your Most Serene Highness's letter concerning the purchase of estates proposed by the Jew Dann. Since, however, the Jew had already been with me before the arrival of this gracious letter, I am in a position to reply to Your Most Serene Highness upon it at once, in deepest obedience.

that the said Dann believes he can persuade Mr. von Eckart, who has bought the Lichtenau houses here, to enter into a purchase (Empletu) of this kind [uncertain: entvire]. It is therefore an ordinary broker's scheme, trimmed out with Jewish swindling - for the same Jew has laid this and other such plans before Prince George no less.

For the rest, matters look ill. Following a decree of contumacy issued by the Posen government, there is an absolute intention to take the Brzyz estates back from Your Most Serene Highness - and when there is added to this that the Chambers, together with the forest officials there, are making nothing but intrigue over the forests and mean to wrest them from Your Serene Highness, then on that side all is lost.

For my own part I cannot see what is to become of the affair. The Chambers chicane at every turn; besides this, suits are being got up from several quarters, frightful costs and dissipations of money are incurred, and notwithstanding all this no end of them can be foreseen, and in this regard there is no thinking of any quiet possession of these estates. The exchange business likewise lies upon an endless road, and no one gives a thought either to the inspection or to the valuation of the estates; and to speak without circumlocution

I believe that the ministers here act as sovereigns (en Souverain) and would never let the exchange take place, unless the monarch should command it. By a trusty man I shall report everything further to Your Serene Highness most humbly.

In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness. This very moment I receive Your Most Serene Highness's two gracious letters with the enclosures - 6 ill will, which I report to Your Serene Highness by another and surer route, shows itself here only too plainly. This very day he receives an extremely polite letter which is said to have been enclosed to Wildeganchen - meanwhile nothing is gained by this hero, unless it be that Your Most Serene Highness now concur in everything there with the man who recommended him to P. The times are now strange, and in this regard one may speak freely from the heart. Your Serene Highness has, as only Eysenhardt says, fallen with the horse - for God's sake I beg, Most gracious Prince and Lord, do take some care of yourself. Berlin, the 25th of May

1799.

faithful most humble and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld.
