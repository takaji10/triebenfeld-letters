Art entrire —"
Es ist dies also ein gewöhniglicher Mecklen Plan
der mit Jüdischen Schwindel verbrämt ist — da

hierzu genommen die Cammern mit dem dortigen Forst

--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld hält den vom Juden Dann vorgeschlagenen Güterkauf für Schwindel. Dann wolle H. v. Echart, den Käufer der Lichtenauschen Häuser, dafür gewinnen, und er habe auch Prinz George solche Pläne vorgetragen. Nach einem Contumacial Decret der Posener Regierung sollen dem Fürsten die Güter in Brzyce wieder genommen werden. Die Cammern intrigieren wegen der Forsten, Prozesse verursachen hohe Kosten, und die Tauschsache stockt. Er glaubt, die Minister würden den Tausch nur auf Befehl des Monarchen zulassen.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld thinks the purchase of estates proposed by the Jew Dann is a swindle. He says Dann wants to win over Mr. von Echart, the buyer of the Lichtenau houses, for it, and that Dann has also put such plans to Prince George. He reports that under a decree by default of the Poznań government the estates in Brzyce are to be taken from the Prince again. The chambers are intriguing over the forests, lawsuits are causing high costs, and the exchange matter is at a standstill. He believes the ministers would permit the exchange only on the monarch's order.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
106

Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord!

The court fiscal Michaelis has communicated to me Your Most Serene Highness's letter concerning the purchase of estates proposed by the Jew Dann. Since, however, the Jew had already been with me before the arrival of this gracious letter, I am in a position to reply to Your Most Serene Highness upon it at once, in deepest obedience — "that Dann believes he can persuade Mr von Echart, who has bought the Lichtenau houses here, to [uncertain: thither] enter into a purchase [uncertain: Empletu] of this kind —" It is therefore an ordinary broker's plan [uncertain: Mecklen], trimmed out with Jewish swindling — for the same Jew has laid this and other such plans before Prince George no less.

For the rest, it looks bad. Pursuant to a contumacial decree issued by the Poznań government, they absolutely mean to take the Brzyzer estates back from Your Most Serene Highness — and when to this is added that the Chambers, together with the forest officials there, are doing nothing but intrigue over the forests and mean to wrest them from Your Serene Highness, then on that side all is lost.

For my own part I do not see what is to become of the matter — the Chambers chicane at every turn; moreover, lawsuits are being framed from several quarters, dreadful costs and fritterings-away of money are incurred, and for all that no end to them can be foreseen, and in this respect there is no thinking of any quiet possession of these estates. The exchange business also lies on an endless road, and no one thinks either of an inspection or of a valuation of the estates, and to speak without circumlocution

I believe that the ministers here act as sovereigns and would never let the exchange take place, unless it were that the monarch commands it — by a trustworthy man I shall report all further particulars to Your Serene Highness most humbly.

In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's — This very moment I receive Your Most Serene Highness's two gracious letters with the enclosures — P's ill will, of which I shall inform Your Serene Highness by another and surer route, shows itself here only too plainly. This very day he receives an extremely civil letter which is said to have been enclosed to [uncertain: Wildeganchen] — meanwhile nothing is to be gained by this hero, unless it be that Your Most Serene Highness should now concur in everything there with the man who recommended him, P — the times are strange now, and in this respect one may speak freely from the heart. Your Serene Highness has, as [uncertain: only] Eysenhardt tells me, had a fall with the horse — for God's sake I beg, most gracious Prince and Lord, do take some care of yourself — Berlin, the 25th of May

1799.

faithful, most humble and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld.
