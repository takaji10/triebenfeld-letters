--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that a royal letter dispatched by the same post explains the King's wish to have the true value of the Prince's South Prussian estates, and of Krotoszyn and Polajewo, more closely investigated so that the properties can be balanced against one another; assures the Prince that, barring weighty impediments, the King will gladly grant the proposed exchange. The writer, Zartmo[illegible] at Berlin, thanks the Prince for his letter of the 7th and, though the business lies outside his department, offers his warm support.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! From the letter of His Majesty the King, which goes out by today's post, Your Most Serene Highness will graciously be pleased to gather in fuller detail how His Majesty wishes to have the true value of Your Highness's South Prussian estates, and that of Krotoszyn and Polajewo, more closely investigated, in order thereafter to be able properly to balance these possessions against one another; and I can assuredly assure Your Highness that, if no weighty impediments stand in the way of the intended exchange, it will be a pleasure to His Majesty the King to have complied with Your Highness's wish in the matter. For the gracious confidence which Your Most Serene Highness is pleased to show me by Your gracious letter of the 7th of this month, I render to Your Highness [uncertain: and] my most deeply obliged thanks; and although the affair itself does not pass through my hands, inasmuch as it does not fall within my department

falls [within my department], I shall nonetheless be no less glad to see it brought to Your Most Serene Highness's complete satisfaction, inasmuch as the singular reverence which I cherish for Your Highness assuredly makes me take the warmest part therein. Nothing in the world could make me happier henceforth than to be able to lay before Your Most Serene Highness proofs of the true veneration with which I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's

Berlin, the 18th of December 1798.

wholly most humble and obedient, faithful and most obedient
