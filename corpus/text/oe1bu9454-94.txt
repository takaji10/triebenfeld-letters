--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Notice that the King, in a letter going out by the same post, wants the true value of the Prince's South Prussian estates and of Krotoszyn and Polajewo examined more closely so that the properties can be weighed against each other; the writer assures the Prince that, barring serious obstacles, the King will gladly grant the proposed exchange. He thanks the Prince for his confidence of the 7th, notes the business falls outside his own department, and promises his warm support.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

From the letter of His Majesty the King going out with today's post, Your Most Serene Highness will be pleased to gather more fully how His Majesty wishes to have the true value of Your Highness's South Prussian estates, and that of Krotoszyn and Polajewo, more closely examined, in order then to be able to balance these possessions properly one against the other; and I can assuredly assure Your Highness that, if no weighty impediments stand in the way of the intended exchange, it will be a pleasure to His Majesty the King to have complied with Your Highness's wish in this matter.

For the gracious confidence which Your Most Serene Highness has been pleased to show me by Your gracious letter of the 7th of this month, I render Your Highness my most deeply obliged thanks; and although the matter itself does not pass through my hands, inasmuch as it does not fall within my department,

I shall nonetheless rejoice no less to see it brought to Your Most Serene Highness's complete satisfaction, since the reverence I harbour for Your Highness will assuredly make me take the warmest part in it. Nothing in the world could make me happier hereafter than to be able to lay before Your Most Serene Highness proofs of the true deference with which I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's Berlin,

the 18th of December

1798.

entirely most humble, faithful,

most obedient

Zartmo[uncertain]
