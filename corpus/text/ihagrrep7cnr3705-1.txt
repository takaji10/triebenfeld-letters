--- SUMMARY (German) ---
v. Triebenfeld bittet als General Bevollmächtigter des Fürsten den König, der Regierung in Kalisz die Sequestration des Gutes Oleśnica (Herrschaft Trąbczyn und Zagórów) zu befehlen. Die Erbpächter Giese, Bagans und Gietzinger zahlten vom Erbstandsgeld (20000 Rthl) nur 10000 Rthl aus Geldern von Untererbpächtern. Seitdem schulden sie Zins und Abgaben und veräußern Gutsteile, während die Kammer den Vertrag nicht bestätigte. Er widerlegt die ablehnenden Resolutionen, verlangt die Abforderung der Akten und bietet Kaution an.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Von Triebenfeld, as the Prince's general plenipotentiary, asks the King to order the Government at Kalisz to sequester the Oleśnica estate (lordship of Trąbczyn and Zagórów). The hereditary lessees Giese, Bagans and Gietzinger paid only 10,000 Rthl of the 20,000 Rthl entry money, and that out of money from sub-lessees. Since then they have owed rent and taxes and sold off parts of the estate, while the Chamber has not confirmed the contract. He rebuts the government's refusals, asks for the files to be called in, and offers security.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene and Most Mighty King, Most Gracious King and Lord! The reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Öhringen and Ingelfingen, as manorial lordship (Dominium) of the lordships of Trąbczyn and Zagórów, most obediently prays that the Government at Kalisz be most graciously commanded, in his suit against the so-called hereditary lessees Giese, Bagans and Gietzinger, to sequester his estate of Oleśnica.

Your Royal Majesty's Government at Kalisz compels me to lay a petition before Your Most High Self, of

whose lawfulness I am as entirely convinced as the damage is great and irreparable which is being inflicted merely by the hitherto refusal of the said government to grant my petition.

The matter has the following connexion, conformable to the truth and to the contents of the files.

On the 22nd of February of last year I concluded, with three persons of the common burgher estate, by the names of Giese, Bagans and Gietzinger, respecting the estate of Oleśnica belonging to my lordship of Trąbczyn and Zagórów situated in South Prussia, the hereditary lease contract appended here in copy (A), according to the contents of which /§. 4./ the hereditary lessees bound themselves to pay an entry money of 20,000 rthr. in two equal instalments of 10,000 Rthl:, and, besides the discharge of all sovereign dues and public burdens, /§. 7./ also an annual hereditary lease rent of 2,000. rthtr; likewise in two equal instalments; and at the same time acknowledged the condition that if the payments of the entry money should not follow in the manner appointed, the contract should be null and void, and that over and above a conventional penalty of 10,000. Rthl. should take place. The hereditary lessees were also indeed granted permission to sub-let the estate held in hereditary lease again in hereditary sub-lease and to dismember it, /§. 10./ yet I reserved to myself that the hereditary sub-lease contracts should not be concluded without my special consent, and that all entry monies of the hereditary sub-lessees should be paid to me directly,

in return for which I undertook to procure the requisite approbation of Your Royal Majesty's War and Domains Chamber to this contract.

Upon the ground of this contract the estate itself was now handed over to the said hereditary lessees naturaliter, though without any particular proceedings of delivery of possession, and the area of 3,338. Morgen 14. rods Magdeburg measure, which had previously been exactly surveyed and revised by three sworn surveyors, Winitzki, Chwalowski and Cabanis, was assigned to them. After they had in this manner been placed in the full natural possession, they sub-let in hereditary sub-lease and dismembered a great part of the estate to the sub-lessees named in the annexed specification (B), and out of these monies, already levied contrary to the contract and payable directly to myself, they paid me the first half of the entry money with 10,000 rthll:, and they also discharged successively the first year's lease rent. With the second half of the entry money, however, they remained in arrear, and in the second year they paid neither the hereditary lease rent nor the sovereign dues, which were thereupon levied from myself by execution by Your Royal Majesty's War and Domains Chamber to the amount of 431 Rthl;

17. Groschen 11. Pfennig and 17 Rthl. 21. Groschen. I also received from the said Chamber, upon my petition for the confirmation of the hereditary lease contract, the resolution that such could by no means take place, in conformity with the statutory provision published after the conclusion thereof in Your Royal Majesty's own most high

Cabinet Order of /: in Hoff and Mathies's juristic monthly, vol. II, p. 103. /. In this posture of the matter nothing else remained to me but to proceed by suit against the said hereditary lessees, and my then attorney, Criminal-court Councillor Mondro, thought it expedient to institute three separate suits on that account, in which, upon the ground of the clausula cassatoria contained in

§. 4., he moved for the annulment of the contract and demanded the current hereditary lease rent for two terms. In what posture these three suits at present stand, Your Royal Majesty will be pleased to see at large from the appended copy of my representation to the Government at Kalisz of the 12th of August of this year.

(C) Now while these suits pursue their course, the hereditary lessees have not only made use of all the revenues of the estate, but have also not ceased to alienate single parts of the same, and to collect the entry money coming in for them from the sub-lessees, as well as the current rent, without paying the least thing to me, and without so much as discharging the sovereign dues and onera publica. They have also fallen into gross and violent disputes with their sub-purchasers, whom they have deceived in manifold ways,

nay, they have even presumed to give themselves out publicly as hereditary lords of the estate of Oleśnica, and in the record (D) of the 6th of August to slander me in the most shameful manner, as though I caused them to be evicted arbitrarily and to be treated with violence, although the judicial attestation of the (E) 12th of August of this year proves that this calumnious allegation is an impudent and punishable lie. It could not be a matter of indifference to me that not only all the revenues of my considerable estate were withheld from me without any right whatever, and its appurtenances arbitrarily alienated, deteriorated and devastated; it must also have vexed me sensibly that I was moreover to bear all the current burdens and dues and to pay them out of my own means. This determined me to petition Your Royal Majesty's Government at Kalisz for the sequestration of this estate in two representations, which, together with the (F) (G) (H) resolutions received thereupon, I append in copy, and herewith once more solemnly assure that all the factual assertions contained in the submissions are conformable to the contents of the files.

I cannot conceive that those dismissive resolutions are conformable to the laws, because from them would follow the impossibility of restraining an insecure hereditary lessee who finds himself without any right whatever in the natural possession of an estate, and institutes prolix and vexatious suits against the proprietor, but pays nothing in rent and dues, from, during the

course of these suits, using the estate itself, alienating it, laying it waste, and inflicting irreparable damage on the proprietor. Such a means most assuredly exists in the form of process introduced by Your Royal Majesty with such infinite wisdom, and according to the opinion of all persons learned in the law, in every case where the possession and use of an immovable estate, or of a living movable thing, is in dispute, the mutual right of the parties must be secured by sequestration. The grounds which Your Royal Majesty's Government at Kalisz has adduced against this universally valid principle can convince me the less, as they already of themselves stand in contradiction with the principles of the legislature. For as concerns

I. the resolution of the 13th of August of this year, it is

a.) contra jus in thesi that sequestration is merely an executive and not also a means of security, since the General Law Code, Part I, Title

15. §. 103-105., Title 18. §. 344., Title 21. §. 140. and 244., expressly determines that sequestration shall be ordered in all cases where the possession, the detention, the administration and the use of an immovable or living movable thing is in dispute, and at the same time danger and insecurity for the contending parties is present; and consequently, under the like legal modalities, in the case of movable inanimate things, judicial deposit is

requisite.

As to b. and c., however, it remains an inward and irreconcilable contradiction when the government assumes that the opponents, upon the ground of an imperfect contract which is null and void ex clausula cassatoria, and has moreover been declared by the Chamber authority, by virtue of a special law, to be absolutely invalid, should nevertheless, and although no titulus possessionis has been entered for them, and chiefly also no purchase money and no entry money has been paid by them out of their own means, be so far possessed as that they must be held in the legal sense to be settled, and on account of their settledness to be secure. Upon this I refer myself simply to the contents of my representation of the 12th of August of this year.

II. Equally insufficient are the grounds in the resolution of the

20th of August of this year. For

1.) it is an altogether incorrect exposition of §. 204., Title

21., Part I, of the General Law Code, when the reporting councillor assumes that sequestration according to this statutory provision could find application in respect of the arrears of hereditary rent only if such were acknowledged

by the hereditary lessee as undisputed. In such a case execution takes place, and sequestration may indeed also be ordered in via executionis secund: §. 116., Title 24., Part 1, of the General Court Ordinance. But also "for the security of the hereditary lessor" sequestration is, according to the clear determination of the law, to be ordered in via arresti, and it goes without saying that in that case only those general principles can be applied which obtain in cases of attachment generally; wherefore it is sufficient if the claims are only in some measure attested, grounds of the insecurity of the party attached are adduced, and securities are furnished by the party attaching.

2.) As incorrect as this interpretation of the law is in itself, just as contrary to the files is the assertion that no liquid arrear of hereditary rent exists. The opponents, in that suit which concerns the first arrear of the 1,000. Rthl., and which was instructed and decided before my patrimonial court, have unconditionally

admitted the correctness of this arrear; the matter has, but for a small error in the formalities of the citation, really been dealt with in an executive process, and in the first instance judgment was given against the opponents for payment of the arrears of hereditary lease rent of the 1,000. Rthl., because, as the ipsa verba of the sentence run: "they had simpliciter acknowledged their obligation to pay; their objections, however, being exceptiones ulterioris indaginis, belonged in part to the principal suit concerning the annulment of the hereditary lease contract, and in part must be referred ad separatum — ". The opponents were also expressly informed in the publication record of this sentence of the 2nd of March of this year that the appeal at once lodged by them against it had only effectum devolutivum, and that execution must nevertheless be ordered. In my most obedient judgment, execution upon these 1,000 Rthl., which I repeatedly moved for, ought therefore to have been ordered and carried out; but even if regard is to be had to the in themselves wholly immaterial errors in the formularies of the citations, and the matter is therefore to be considered as having been dealt with only in processu ordinario, still so much is beyond doubt certain, that by a condemna-

tory judgment, even if not yet in legal force, which is grounded upon an acknowledgment of the debt made simpliciter on the part of the defendant, the claim itself is at least so far sufficiently attested as the laws require in the case of a petition for attachment.

All the objections which the opponents have opposed to their obligation to pay, in the appellate stage of this suit and in the first instance of the second suit pending concerning the other arrear of hereditary rent, consist in the assertion that they had not received the contractual number of Morgen of the area of 3,338. Morgen 14. rods, and that the Chamber's consent had not been procured by me. Both exceptions are in themselves immaterial, and moreover false; for I have already shown the correctness of the area by attestations of the sworn surveyor who measured it, and have already remarked above that the Chamber's consent, repeatedly applied for by me, was refused

upon the ground of the more recent statutory ordinance in the Cabinet Order of the 10th of April of this year. In general all this belongs to the principal suit concerning the annulment of the contract. The hereditary rent is paid for the use of the estate and the collection of its continuing revenues /: General Law Code, Part

1., Title 21., §. 187. 190. /; the opponents are in possession and enjoyment of all the estate's revenues, and must therefore also pay the continuing canon until it is decided upon the nullity of the contract, or in the worst case at least deposit it. But even the deposit has not been made, although I have expressly had them called upon to do so, and it lies quite plainly at day that the opponents can as little pay as they will.

3.) The opponents are indeed highly insecure, and the alternative set up in the resolution is neither conformable to the files nor logically correct. aa, It is contrary to the files that the opponents had paid the first half of the entry monies with 10,000. Rthl. out of their own fortune, and that I am bound to repay them this sum if the hereditary lease contract were annulled. But I have already mentioned that this payment was discharged out of the

sum of 11,089 Rthl. 8. Groschen levied by them from their sub-purchasers contrary to the contract and payable directly to myself. All the contracts which the opponents concluded with the sub-purchasers, and upon which they levied those 11,089. Rthl. 8. Groschen, were concluded without my consent, expressly reserved to me in §. 10., and are for me so far not binding that I am not bound to acknowledge the payments made by the sub-purchasers to the opponents. If I chose to make use of this right, all the sub-purchasers, who for the most part have no other fortune than the entry money paid in, would in the most irresponsible manner lose what is theirs and be brought to the beggar's staff. This I would never allow, and I have already declared ad Acta that I reserve to myself to credit these 10,000. Rthl. 8 Groschen to the poor sub-purchasers so shamefully deceived by the opponents, pro rata of their payments, and to depart from the rigour of my right. Your Royal Majesty will certainly regard this declaration of mine as in the highest degree lawful, equitable and worthy of my person.

But if I thus credit these

10,000 Rthl. to the opponents' sub-purchasers, and thereby prevent their being fraudulently deprived of what is theirs, it is clear that I do not hold them as an object of security for the latter, and can never restore them to them. Moreover I have claimed these 10,000. Rthl. under

§. 5. of the hereditary lease contract as a forfeited conventional penalty, and this point too is litispendent in the principal suit.

bb, the second case of the alternative, that the contract should stand and be declared valid, is not conceivable at all, and is really a casus non dabilis, since on the one part the clausula cassatoria of §. 5. takes effect, because the opponents have not paid the second half of the entry money nor deposited it, and on the other part the Chamber's consent is refused, and according to the most recent statutory provisions must be refused.

cc, As concerns finally the unsettledness of the opponents, again assumed by the reporting councillor, this supposition rests quite evidently upon a manifest confusion of the legal conceptions of natural and civil possession, and

the counter-representation contained in my submission of the 12th of August has not been taken into consideration at all. I am utterly unable to see how anyone is implicitly placed in civil possession by delivery and the yielding up of natural possession; I do not comprehend how anyone for whom no titulus possessionis is entered in the mortgage book, and who, because his contract remains void, can only be so entered, should be an estate possessor in the legal sense —; it remains inexplicable to me how I am supposed to have conceded civil possession to the opponents by having demanded of them the current hereditary lease rent for the enjoyment of the natural possession, until such time as the contract is annulled and the ejectment of the opponents realised —; and I absolutely do not understand how anyone can be held to be possessed, settled and secure who finds himself sine titulo et jure in the natural possession of an estate for which he has yet paid nothing at all out of his own means. Most Gracious King and Lord! Never would I trouble Your Most High Self with this prolix petition, in a

matter unhappily very confused, were I not quite properly compelled to it. I have absolutely no other purpose than to be secured against the manifest and wholly irreparable injuries of the opponents, who continue to alienate and lay waste my estate, while I must effect the discharge of all burdens and dues without receiving any income or revenue of the estate whatever. If there is another lawful means of securing myself than sequestration, I will gladly submit to it. But the jurists with whom I have conferred on the matter assure me that no other means exists, and therefore I must persist in my petition for sequestration. I desire nothing further than that the estate revenues which the sequestrator shall collect be paid ad depositum judiciale, and I have even offered to furnish security for the sequestrator proposed, or for another solid one. In my judgment all conceivable scruples are thereby entirely removed, and I must most humbly beg that the whole of the files be called in from the Government at Kalisz,

and that thereupon sequestration be graciously ordered, and its immediate realisation commanded to the government upon remission of the

files.

It is not unknown to me that ordinarily only a report is required in such cases as this; but I expressly beg for the calling in of the files, because a mere report would not turn out so complete, and would in no case so thoroughly demonstrate the perfect lawfulness of my petition for sequestration, as if — for which I most respectfully beg — Your Royal Majesty would be pleased to have the same laid before Your Most High Self in comparison with the court files.

The principal suit is being instructed at length; the taking of evidence on the spot and with the calling in of sworn experts will be hard to avoid, and it is only too certain that, since the matter has been introduced merely in processu ordinario, for at least two years yet there can be no thought of a judgment and of an

execution grounded upon it. So much the more must I be intent upon the hastening of my being secured, and I therefore hold myself convinced that I may look forward to a favourable resolution with that confidence and reverence with which I remain Your Royal Majesty's Wrocław

the 18th of September

1805.

most humbly and most faithfully obedient

v. Triebenfeld, as general plenipotentiary
