--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports on dealings with Herr Wiener, Meyer Bernhard and consorts, who after evasions in Berlin (Friedländer's absence) now promise to remit 76000 Rthl plus some 20000 Rthl in bank obligations by courier, while withholding 30000 Rthl on the pretext of the repurchase of Betsch; Meyer Bernhard adds 10000 Rthl. Notes the deposition of the Brunieweisch moneys pushed through via the Grand Chancellor and General Köckritz, the King's civil reply on compensation and an offer of estates in hereditary lease rather than money, and Princess Sacken's calling in of 30000 Rthl on Koschentin through Herr Cosmar.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Lord! Herr Wiener, Meyer Bernhard, and consorts are bad fellows. First they promised to pay everything in Berlin – in Berlin the word was again that Friedländer had gone away. They would settle everything in Frankfurth. Now that I arrive here today, they assure me that they will send the 76000 Rthl, as well as a further 20000 Rthl or so, by express courier in bank obligations, precisely to [uncertain: Bernhardi gebschick]. To the question why they are holding back 30000 Rthl – Answer: we are already paying too much, for Your Serene Highness has, after all, bought back Betsch. I am still doing battle with the [uncertain: Schl] and just this moment Meyer Bernhard promises to add a further 10000 Rthl, but the wretch stays behind by 20. In Berlin, since Wanster had drawn up the obligation defectively, I was still able to obtain the money. Only some few thousand t. did I take on bills of exchange

in Trąbczin to be able to offer face in every direction, and thereby that Your Serene Highness may find money at hand. The deposition of the Brunieweisch moneys will nonetheless doubtless be ordered; I have pushed it through by way of the Grand Chancellor and Gen. Köckritz. This is honourable for Your Serene Highness, and certainly some seventy thousand [uncertain: Kl.] will be gained thereby. Had I acted sooner, alone and with the [uncertain: dreißigkeit], everything would already have been disposed of. The reply from the King concerning the compensation is quite civil and seems to betoken good will. Meanwhile General v. K. has charged me to convey his most cordial greetings to Your Serene Highness and to tell you most highly that you should quite certainly receive estates, and indeed right good ones, in hereditary lease, upon which something might be gained. Money the King would not be in a position to give, and since Schlabrendorff wishes to return, this follows of

itself. In any event the hereditary lease should be arranged advantageously. For the rest, the Princess Sacken has, out of especial grace and mildness, been pleased, as soon as she heard that Your Serene Highness had purchased the estates, to have the capital of 30000 Rthl standing upon [illegible] Koschentin called in through Herr Cosmar. Oh, the shame of it! Herr Gen. v. K. despises her [illegible], and so does every honest man. May God but bring Your Serene Highness back in health – all will nonetheless go well, and Your Highness will in every way yet stand as victor. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's Frankfurth on the Oder, the 21st of June 1804. most faithful servant v Triebenfeld
