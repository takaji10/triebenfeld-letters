--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Wiener, Meyer Bernhard and associates are denounced as bad faith debtors: after promises in Berlin they now undertake to remit 76,000 Rthl plus some 20,000 in bank obligations by courier, withholding 30,000 on the pretext that the Prince has bought back Betsche, with a further 10,000 wrung from Meyer Bernhard. Triebenfeld reports securing, through the Grand Chancellor and General von Köckritz, the deposit of the Bruniewe monies, worth perhaps 70,000 Rthl; Köckritz holds out estates in hereditary lease rather than cash, and Princess Sacken has called in 30,000 Rthl on Koschentin.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

Mr. Wiener, Meyer Bernhard, and consorts are bad fellows. First they promised to pay everything in Berlin – in Berlin the word was again that Friedländer had gone away. They would settle everything in Frankfurt. Now that I arrive here today they assure me that they mean to send the 76000 Rthl, and likewise a further some 20000 Rthl by express courier in bank obligations, directly [uncertain: to Bernhardi]. To the question why they are holding back 30000 Rthl – answer: we are already paying too much, for Your Serene Highness has bought back Betsche.

I am still doing battle with the [illegible], and just now Meyer Bernhard promises to add a further 10000 Rthl, but with 20 the wretch holds back.

In Berlin, since Wanster had drawn up the obligation faultily, I was still able to obtain the money. Merely a few thousand th. have I taken up on bills of exchange

in Trąbczyn to be able to present a face in every respect, and so that Your Serene Highness may find money on hand.

The deposition of the Brunieweische monies will nevertheless without doubt be ordered; I have carried it through by way of the Grand Chancellor and General Köckritz. This is honourable for Your Serene Highness, and certainly some seventy thousand [uncertain: Rthl] will thereby be gained. Had I acted earlier, alone and with boldness, everything would already have been disposed of. The answer from the King concerning the compensation is quite obliging and seems to betray good will. Meanwhile General v. K. has charged me to convey his most cordial greetings to Your Serene Highness and to tell you most highly that you should most certainly receive estates, and indeed right good ones, in hereditary lease, from which something might be gained. For money the King would not be in a position to give, and since Schlabrendorff wishes to return, this follows of

itself. In each case the hereditary lease should be arranged advantageously. For the rest, the Princess Sacken, out of particular grace and mildness, has deigned, as soon as she heard that Your Serene Highness had bought the estates, to have the capital of 30000 Rthl standing on [illegible] Koschentin called in through Mr. Cosmar. O the shame. General v. K. despises her [illegible] and so does every honest man. God bring Your Serene Highness back only in health – all will nevertheless go well, and Your Most High Self will in every way yet stand as victor.

In deepest respect I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Frankfurt an der Oder, the 21st of June

1804.

most faithful servant

v. Triebenfeld
