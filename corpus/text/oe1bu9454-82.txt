Durchlauchtigster Fürst
Gnädigster Fürst u Herr
der Herr Minister v. Voss nach zu Havelberg ist, so iſt das
von St. Majestät dem Könige ihm in Ew Durchlaucht Sache zuge¬
ferliche Cabinets Schreiben dahin nachgesandt worden, es hätte
dieses Cabinets Schreiben bereit nicht schon an das Depuite
ment alhier von dem H. Minister können eingesandt
wurden seyn ich habe durch den meinen Freunde deshalb in
der Kanzeley genau nachgeforscht, allein bis diesen Mit¬
tag ist noch nichts eingegangen und kan es aber wohl seyn
daß Er Excellenz die Einsendung deshalb unter laßen
um solche selbst mit anhero zu bringen, weil der
falle 14 Tage, früher zurück kommt, als eigentlich sein Ur¬
druk abgelaufen u also schon nächsten Donnerstag hier
intrifft, sollte das Königl. Cabinet Schreiben an
dH. Minister so decidirt lauten, als es mir dH. von
Triebenfeld verführen wollen, so bin ich gantz über
zugt, daß alles nach Wunsch ausfallen wird, als
weshalb vnd meinen hiesigen Gunnen, schon die
heiligste Versicherung erhalten habe.
Zugleich zeuge unterthänig an, wie dH. Triebenfeld

zu der Durchlaucht Gebrauch und für Hochdero Reichnung

dessen Schein auch sogleich und ihm gezahlt habe.
Der ich übrigens in tief der Dection erstert
ſie Durchlaucht
Berlin den 13 Octo

unterthäniger Diener
C. F.[?] G. Eysenhardt seinen

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Eysenhardt reports from Berlin that Minister v. Voss is at Havelberg, whither the King's Cabinet order in the Prince's affair has been forwarded, so that it has not yet reached the Department; enquiries in the chancery have produced nothing, though Voss, returning fourteen days before his leave expires, may bring it himself by Thursday. If the order is as decided as Triebenfeld suggests, success is assured. He also notes paying Triebenfeld 300 Prussian Courant against written acknowledgement.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord,

since the Herr Minister v. Voss is [uncertain: nach — reading "nach zu Havelberg", i.e. gone/away to Havelberg] at Havelberg, the Cabinet order sent to him by His Majesty the King in Your Serene Highness's affair has been forwarded thither after him; this Cabinet order could not already have been transmitted by the Herr Minister to the Department here. I have on that account had careful enquiry made in the chancery through my friend, but up to this midday nothing whatever has come in; it may however well be that His Excellency has forborne to transmit it in order to bring the same hither himself, because [the case?] returns 14 days earlier than his leave properly ran out, and so will arrive here as soon as next Thursday. Should the Royal Cabinet order to the Herr Minister be worded as decidedly as Herr von Triebenfeld would have me [uncertain: verführen — "lead me to believe / mislead me"], then I am wholly convinced that everything will fall out according to [Your] wish, of which, moreover, and from my patrons here, I have already received the most sacred assurance. At the same time I most humbly report how Herr Triebenfeld

has demanded of me, for Your Serene Highness's use and on High Account, 300 Pr[ussian] Cour[ant], which against his written acknowledgement I likewise paid over to him at once. I who for the rest, in deepest reverence, I remain, until death, Your Serene Highness's

Berlin, the 13 Octo[ber] 1798

humble and obedient servant
C. F.[illegible] G. Eysenhardt
his own [uncertain: seinen]
