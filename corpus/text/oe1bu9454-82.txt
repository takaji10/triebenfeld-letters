--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A report that the King's cabinet letter in the Prince's matter has been sent after Minister von Voss at Havelberg and has not yet reached the department in Berlin, enquiries in the chancery having produced nothing; Voss, back from leave a fortnight early, may bring it himself on Thursday. If it is worded as firmly as Triebenfeld suggests, a favourable outcome is assured. Also notes 300 Prussian Courant paid to Triebenfeld on the Prince's account against a written note.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord, since the Minister von Voss is [uncertain: to] Havelberg, the cabinet letter issued to him by His Majesty the King in Your Serene Highness's matter has been forwarded thither; this cabinet letter could not already have been sent in to the department here by the Minister. I have on that account made precise enquiry in the chancery through my friend, but up to this midday nothing whatever has come in; yet it may well be that His Excellency has omitted the transmission of it in order to bring the same along here himself, since he is returning 14 days earlier than his leave properly runs out and thus already arrives here next Thursday. Should the Royal cabinet letter to the Minister be worded as decidedly as Mr. von Triebenfeld would have it appear to me, then I am wholly convinced that everything will turn out as desired, concerning which [illegible] and my patrons here I have already received the most sacred assurance. At the same time I most humbly report how Mr. Triebenfeld

has demanded of me 300 Prussian Courant for Your Serene Highness's use and on your high account, which I also at once paid over to him against his written note. For the rest, I remain, until death, in deepest [uncertain: reverence], [illegible] Serene Highness

Berlin, the 13th of October

1798

humble and obedient servant

C. F.[illegible] G. Eysenhardt his
