--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Zerboni berichtet Hardenberg: Kolno und Kamionna habe der Fürst vor 1806 an Leixner verkauft, Trąbczyn nicht; alle Güter gelangten 1806/7 an die Erben des vorigen Eigentümers zurück. Ein gewisser Schenk fordere als Mandatar 318500 Rthl Entschädigung, am besten durch das Amt Sokolnik. Zerboni hält die Berechnungen für Unsinn; der Fürst habe Trąbczyn deterioriert und mit Schulden überladen. Das Kabinett könne nur das russische Kabinett bewegen, den Fürsten zu entschädigen. Ein Randvermerk nennt Triebenfeld und Leixner verstorben.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Zerboni reports to Hardenberg: he says the Prince sold Kolno and Kamionna to Leixner before 1806, but not Trąbczyn, and that all the estates passed back in 1806/7 to the heirs of the previous owner. He says a certain Schenk, acting as authorised agent, is claiming 318500 Rthl in compensation, best provided through the office of Sokolnik. Zerboni considers the calculations nonsense; he says the Prince let Trąbczyn fall into disrepair and burdened it with debts. The cabinet, he says, can only induce the Russian cabinet to compensate the Prince. A note in the margin records that Triebenfeld and Leixner have died.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Poznań, 2 November 1815.

By the order of 6 June of this year Your Serene Highness transmitted to me two letters of the Prince of Hohenlohe, to Your Serene Highness and to His Excellency the Minister Baron vom Stein, of 12 May

of this year, with the command that I express myself upon the steps which would have to be taken in favour of this Prince with the Imperial Russian cabinet.

The Prince of Hohenlohe received from His Majesty the late King, as a gift, the estates of Kolno and Kamionna in the Poznań country, and the estates of Trąbczyn in the Kalisz department. Both estates were formerly the property of the Starost Prusimski, who had been implicated in the insurrection in the year 1794, and had fallen to the State by confiscation.

The estates of Kolno and Kamionna the Prince sold to the bank cashier Leixner before the year 1806. The Trąbczyn ones, however, he did not. All the estates have, through the political events of the years 1806/7, passed back to the heirs of the former owner, with whom, in these circumstances, they remain under the Vienna treaty of 3 May of this year; against which His Majesty is inclined to compensate the donees for their loss.

After the Prince of Hohenlohe had communicated with me variously concerning this compensation, partly by word of mouth through

[Written by the receiving office:]

c. 3278 fr: Paris 18 Nov 1815. To Councillor of Legation Balan. The estates of Kolno and Kamionna, which lie in the Grand Duchy of Posen, no longer belong at all to the Prince of Hohenlohe, since he already sold them in the year 1805 for 142,000 Rthl to the bank cashier Leixner, for whom the state of possession has also long since been put in order. There is no ground whatever on which the Prince can be mixed into this affair; his Mandatoris, the deceased von Triebenfeld, also appears to have interested himself in it out of pity for Leixner, who has in the meantime likewise died. This matter will now be brought up particularly through the HauptAmt, as Inselung of the intabulated capitals, and it just comes down for me to this:

that the Royal Ministry of Foreign Affairs for many Princes of Hohenlohe on account of the Trąbczyn estates no [illegible]. Most [uncertain: Meisten]. court; this matter is therefore to issue again to Mr Gericht Jordan, on 13 April St[ä]g[emann].

the councillor of war von Triebenfeld, partly in writing by letters devoid of content, a certain Schenk has at last come forward as his authorised representative, and has deduced to me from a volume of accounts that a compensation of 318,500 Rthl. is due to the Prince for the loss of the Trąbczyn estates, and that this could best be made good by the domain office of Sokolnik, lying in Poland close against the frontier, which pays 15,000 Rthl. a year in rent. The Prince moves that it be procured with the Russian cabinet that this domain office be conferred upon him heritably by His Majesty the Emperor.

In respect of the estates of Kolno and Kamionna the authorised representative acknowledges that the compensation would doubtless have to be demanded in the first place by Leixner as owner.

The calculations laid before me are full of nonsense and loathsome to read. The Trąbczyn estates can originally hardly have had a third of the value of the domain office of Sokolnik; they have been run down by the Prince and loaded with debts. I scarcely believe that he has any compensation left to demand.

This being the posture of the affair, what can be done on the part of our cabinet, without compromising itself, to set the Prince of Hohenlohe's mind at rest would reduce itself to this:

"to move the Imperial Russian cabinet to the resolution of compensating the Prince for the loss of Trąbczyn," and thereafter to leave it to His Serene Highness himself

to settle with the Russian authorities themselves: for what they are to be compensated, and in what manner they are to be compensated? The original letters transmitted to me and mentioned at the opening of this report I return most humbly. Poznań, 2 November 1815. Zerboni di Sposetti

To His Serene Highness the Royal Prussian State Chancellor the Prince of Hardenberg at

Paris
