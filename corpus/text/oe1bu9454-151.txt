--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Die Mennonisten sind in Trąbczyn und wollen beide Güter zusammen kaufen, doch hoher Schnee verhindert alle Arbeiten. Die Taxe muss umgearbeitet werden: Swięcia bringt statt 1800 nur 918 Rthl, Wrąbczyn statt 1500 nur 1123 Rthl. Hundert Hufen Wald bei Grądzyń und Drzewce sollen 3000 Rthl Zinsen, also 60000 Rthl Capital, bringen. Lichnowski hat einen Teil der 10000 Rthl der Oleśnicer mit Arrest belegt. Laut Nerlich verweigern die Wrąbczyner die Weihnachtsrate.

Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord!

I have the honour to have just now received Your Most Serene Highness's two letters of the 30th of November and the 2nd of December. It gladdens me most heartily that Your Highness has in Mr von Nothardt so genuine a friend, through whom much good will be able to be effected. That he was to become Director I did indeed already know, but the particular manner of it was unknown to me. The business with Mr von Harlem and the councillor of war Nöldichen is meanwhile causing much terror — and Mr von Nothardt has every reason to pray to Heaven that Mr von Voss does not avenge his favourite upon him. — Things go very badly for me here — It seems as though Heaven were setting itself against all business — the Mennonites are here in Trąbczyn and wish to buy both estates together. I am having the people provided for and every kindness shown them, so that I may keep them in good humour — Meanwhile, on account of the great snow, which lies more than a foot deep, nothing can be undertaken. One hundred Hufen of the Grądzyń and Drzewce forest I am selling for [uncertain: perpetual] rent. This amounts to 3000 Rthl in rent and consequently to 60000 Rthl of capital. Which, in case I do not do the latter, then the poor grounds remain to me, and the valuation would turn out very low

especially as such dreadful shortfalls are appearing. For Swięcia yields, instead of 1800 Rthl in rent, only 918 Rthl in all; Wrąbczyn, instead of 1500 Rthl, only 1123 Rthl.

Upon the forest, with timber prices so low, very little is to be reckoned; I must therefore think upon artificial funds, so that a value proportionate to the matter may be arrived at in the valuation, inasmuch as by means of the loan, so that nothing here be squandered away, all ill will may be forestalled and the principal debts paid.

The valuers had at first taken the matter according to the tableaux, but when it came to the examination of the people, whereby the revenues were to be proved, everything fell so far short that we must alter the whole valuation.

Lichnowski has laid an arrest upon a part of the ten thousand Rthl which the Oleśnica people are to pay at Christmas, so that only some 6000 Rthl will thereby be received. With the Wrąbczyn people I must deal very warily, for, as Nerlich tells me, they refuse to pay the Christmas instalment. Even if in truth no snow were lying, it would still be very inexpedient to proceed now with the measuring out of the lands. Meanwhile at Wrąbczyn nothing remains over for Your Serene Highness, and the fine village brings in nothing further than the sum censured above, which, according to the area contained, if the Morgen were reckoned at 1 Rthl, ought to amount to 2958 Rthl.

These however are matters which now can no longer be altered — I meanwhile shall give away no Morgen at under 1 Rthl rent, however poor the land may be, and in the case of lands and meadows, with the good marshes, nothing goes for less than 300 Rthl in ready money and 15 Rthl annual rent; for this I stand surety. Were the weather not so very much against me, I should perhaps already be finished; as it is, however, I must let God rule, and hold to the drawing up of the valuation.

The interest to the Lichnowski party is being paid here — Your Serene Highness will nevertheless receive from here about 8000 Rthl at Christmas. For the Schlabrendorff and the Lichnowski principal sums I must fetch the capital from Berlin, which can then, when the Mennonites pay, be paid off again.

What good will and human powers can accomplish, I shall exert, in order to guide everything, in spite of the frightful weather, to the best end. In deepest reverence and esteem I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

most humble and obedient servant

Trąbczyn,

the 7th of December

v Triebenfeld

1804
