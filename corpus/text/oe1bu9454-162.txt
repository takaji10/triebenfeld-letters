--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that a General Finance Department order, provoked by Prince George's arrangement to have Councillor of War Korn revise valuations Korn himself had drawn up, now bars Chambers from ordering revision of judicial valuations without prior consent, halting Triebenfeld's work; President v. Goldbeck has applied for permission, forwarded by express courier with Amelang and Langen enlisted, though Voss may refuse. Meanwhile he will submit dismemberment plans, notes the Olesnice case returned to the patrimonial court through v. Husslarzewski, and twice begs for three bronzed busts and a lavoir for Goldbeck.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

An order issued a fortnight ago by the General Finance Department to all the Chambers here hinders the course of my business extraordinarily, and it may be long before I come to the end of the revision of the valuations, if indeed such is still permitt

ed at all. The cause of these new difficulties

is Prince George together with the local councillor of war Korn. The former, some time ago, applied to the Chamber of Kalisz for Korn as reviser of his valuations of the estates situated in this, the Posen, and other departments; the Chamber had also granted this. But since, unluckily, the judicial valuations which were to be revised had been drawn up by Korn himself, and were contested in the [uncertain: dispatch], the matter had to be laid before the head of the Department; incensed at these swindles, that a member of the Chamber should revise the valuations which he himself had made, and against which so many objections are raised, and make the Chamber answerable, the Minister issued the order that

that no Chamber should any longer be permitted, without the foreknowledge of the General Finance Department, to order revisions of a judicial valuation, whereby the revision of Prince George's affairs would at once be forbidden for ever.

The President von Goldbeck has meanwhile written at once to the said Department and sought permission. I have sent this by express courier to Berlin, and at the same time have begged Amelang and Langen to help obtain the permission and to see to it that the same be sent here with all speed. It now remains to be seen whether the malicious Voss will concede the revision.

In the meantime I must lie here at anchor and await the answer. Meanwhile I shall submit the plans of dismemberment for approbation, and smooth everything in this matter. Mr. von Goldbeck was at first very lukewarm; at the last he reproached me that I had not helped him to the two [uncertain: Strahlitz], which Dolfs afterwards obtained, and which the Minister had so firmly promised him. To furnish proof that I had done everything for him was not difficult for me

and we came to agreement again. He wishes very much to have, as soon as possible, three [uncertain: bronzed] busts, which he means to set upon his bookcase. Likewise he has begged me to procure him, as soon as possible, a washbasin, so as to be able to wash his ailing abdomen daily. May Your Serene Highness have the grace to let three busts of that kind, and likewise a washbasin, be bought, and send me these here by the first post. The washbasin is to be had in the joiners' warehouses.

I should dearly like to surprise the good man with them quite promptly. The affair of Olesnica has, through the friendly mediation of Mr. von Husslarzewski, been sent back to the patrimonial court for decision; this is for us a great satisfaction. — I shall now procure the speediest prosecution of the matter, and meanwhile quiet Lichnowski, so that — Heaven only grant

that I may be able to dismember at leisure, that I may obtain the permission for the revision of the valuations as soon as possible. The accursed swindles of Prince George have already caused much terror, and will yet stir up much misfortune and mistrust. He is truly a dreadful man — I cannot praise God enough that I am parted from that man. In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's — If only I might soon receive the 3 [uncertain: bronzed] busts and the washbasin, it would give much joy. Kalisz, the 24th of February 1805.

faithful, humble and obedient servant v Triebenfeld
