--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Complains that pleading is useless: the Prince has not paid a single Groschen of interest, so Triebenfeld must himself meet the 700 Rthl promised for the inventory under his guarantee, plus 175 in interest since 1799 and 35 Rthl costs of suit, 910 Rthl in all. His treasury is empty, having paid 1537 Rthl 12 gg the previous day in the Nudnich affair; Wildegans and Heyman Pincus promise settlement but nothing comes. Asks for 900 Rthl to avoid execution, offering timber monies at Midsummer.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
All pleading is of no help, I am to and must pay. The Prince has, as the enclosure shows, not settled even a single Groschen of interest. I must therefore pay the 700 Rthl promised by the Prince for the inventory under my guarantee, and from 1799 the interest amounting to 175 b, likewise costs of suit 35 Rthl, together 910 Rthl. My treasury is exhausted, for only yesterday I paid 1537 Rthl 12 gg in the Nudnich affair – which the Prince, according to fourfold judicial documents, so solemnly promised to settle. Wildegans and Heyman Pincus promise to settle everything – only God knows when. I must pay in the meantime, if I do not wish to have execution in the house. Could Your Serene Highness help me with the 900 Rthl? Then have the graciousness – At Midsummer I can again support Your Serene Highness with the timber monies. Breslau, the 2nd of May 1804. v Triebenfeld
