Durchlauchtigster Fürst
Gnädiger Herr!
Ewr Durchlaucht Verfügungen welche Höchst Sie durch den H. Hofrath Meyer an den Hn. Geh. Rath Ame¬
lang erlaßen haben, haben mein zumres erschüttert, und meine Cörperliche Leiden dergestalt ver¬
wehrt, daß ich meine völlige Herstellung wohl um 4 Wochen später hoffen darauf.
wenn wäre es Ihnen gnädiger Fürst und Herr möglich solche Entschlüße zu faßen die mich in diesen
Zeiten und bey den Gesinnungen des H. Bau von Stein ins tiefste Elend hätten stürzen können -
Sie weisen auf auf jener Sanke Anweisung, die ich schon vor 3/4 Jahren für Null und nichtig acklärk¬
noch heute Summen an, und H. p. Meyer sagt, daß sie dies aus Barmherzigkeit für mich thäten
indem Sie Ihren lezten Nothpfennig angriffe. Wie wehe diese Ausdrüke meinen Herzen
Chun bin ich nicht im Stande zu schildern —
Sie wollen durchlauchter Herr aus Barmforzigkeit für mich, von mein Eigenthum
2600 Rthl anweisen — Was soll das? ich habe hier für Sie ungerecht und schrecklich gelitten
ja sogar Arrest (was ich in meinen Leben nicht erduldet, ausstehen müßen — ich muste
das Geld, wan ich nicht fortwährend 2 Gensdarmes in meer Stube haben wolte, die
mir hier täglich an 20 Rthl kosteten anschaffen und bezahlen - der würdige Minister
et Zastrow half dazu, indes mußte ich mich gegen diesen verpflichten, nicht ehender
von hier zu gehen, als bis die 6000 Rthl bezahlt und er seinen Wechsel zurückhal¬
würden. Ich meldetere Durchlaucht diesen unerhörten Vorfall, und bitte Sie beizutragen
daß die 6000 Rthl, welche ich für sie hier bezahlen müßen, berichtiget werden mögten.
Ich beth zugleich mir jene bancke Anweisung auch auf [?]000 Rthl zurück zu senden, indem solche vor
ungültig erklärt, und ich schon mehreremahle von der Band monirt worden selbige
zurück zu schaffen, damit mir meine Kontopper Hypotheque nebst meinen Wechsel
der nur auf 3 Monathe gestellt und längst abgelaufen war retradirt werden könte.

Ewr Durchlaucht antworten auf mein Schreiben erreichen bedauern mich höchlich, erwahnte aber
von der Bande Anweisung nichts - hingegen schreibt Herr, Meyer an den Gehl Rath Arme¬

Der G. Rath, meinen Gelde durch den Königl. Band an der ich dafür mein Eigenthum ver¬

das ist doch wahrlich mehr als unerhöhrt – Ich werde von Marcul in den wichtigsten An¬
Gelegenheiten hierher geschickt - ich soll, wan ich hier die Geschäfte beendiget habe als
nach Wien gehen, ich werde hier auf eine im denkbar gewesene Art um 5000 Rthl Zinse
die Euer Durchlaucht zahlen sollen, als Ihr bevollmächtigter attaquirt, ich beweise daß ich
diese Zahlung zu leisten nicht verbunden bin - alles dies hilft nichts - man arretirt mich
legt mich 2 Genedarmen in die mich bewachen müßen, und die ich hier in Gasthaft täglich
ernähen muß - Ich soll vor yre mal gre bezahlen -
die mir aufgetragenen Geschäfte, durfte und konte ich unter einer solchen Aufsicht

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Bitter protest at dispositions issued through Court Councillor Meyer to Privy Councillor Amelang, which assign 1600 Rthl to Triebenfeld and 3400 to Eysenhardt out of the very 6000 Rthl bank instruction he had declared void. Reports his arrest in Berlin over 5000 Rthl of arrears of interest plus 125 costs, guarded by two gendarmes at his own cost, and the loan raised through Minister von Zastrow from the banker von der Laar, repayable 14 May. Begs 3125 Rthl and the return of the instruction, so he may travel on to Vienna.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Gracious Lord! Your Serene Highness's dispositions, which you were most highly pleased to issue through Court Councillor Meyer to Privy Councillor [uncertain: Amelang], have shaken my [uncertain: innermost] and have so aggravated my bodily sufferings that I may well hope for my complete recovery only some 4 weeks later. How was it possible for you, gracious Prince and Lord, to take such resolutions as might have plunged me, in these times and given the disposition of Herr Bau von Stein, into the deepest misery — You order [uncertain: still] today sums upon that [uncertain: bank] instruction, which I already three quarters of a year ago [text unclear: declared] null and void, and Herr Meyer says that you do this out of mercy toward me, in that you were drawing upon your last penny of need. How painfully these expressions [uncertain: touch] my heart I am not able to depict — You wish, Most Serene Lord, out of mercy toward me, to assign 2600 Rthl of my own property — What is the meaning of this? I have suffered here for you unjustly and dreadfully, indeed I have even had to endure arrest (which I have never in my life undergone) — I had to procure and pay the money, unless I wished to have 2 gendarmes continually in my chamber, who cost me here some 20 Rthl daily — the worthy Minister von Zastrow helped in this, but I had to bind myself to him not to depart from here any sooner than until the 6000 Rthl were paid and he had his bill of exchange back. I reported this unheard-of occurrence to Your Serene Highness, and beg you to contribute so that the 6000 Rthl, which I have had to pay here for you, may be settled. I begged at the same time that that bank instruction, likewise for [illegible]000 Rthl, be sent back to me, inasmuch as it was declared invalid, and I have already several times been admonished by the [uncertain: bank] to return the same, so that my Kontopper mortgage together with my bill of exchange — which was drawn only for 3 months and had long since expired — might be restored to me.

Your Serene Highness's replies to my letter [uncertain: express] the deepest sympathy for me, but mentioned nothing of the [uncertain: bank] instruction — on the contrary, Herr Meyer writes to Privy Councillor Amelang in a threatening style and, out of mercy toward me, assigns 1600 Rthl to the [text unclear] Privy Councillor, which he is to have paid out of my money through the Royal [uncertain: bank], for which I have pledged my property and given my bill of exchange. That is truly more than unheard-of — I am sent hither by Marcul on the most important affairs — I am, when I have concluded the business here, to go on to Vienna; here I am attacked, in a manner not to have been imagined, for 5000 Rthl of interest which Your Serene Highness is to pay, as your plenipotentiary; I prove that I am not bound to render this payment — all this avails nothing — they arrest me, place 2 gendarmes upon me who must guard me, and whom I must feed here at the inn daily — I am to pay [text unclear] — the business entrusted to me I neither might nor could pursue under such surveillance — the consequences thereof will yet have great influence upon the fate of many a man. Yes, in this [uncertain: extremity] the honest Minister von Zastrow advised that the money be raised and paid. The interest for 2 years amounted to 5000 Rthl and the costs 125. I had 2000 Rthl of travelling money by me; for 5000 Rthl the Minister v. Z. borrowed from a certain banker von der Laar in state paper, repayable in Courant on the 14th of May of this year — the 5000 Rthl were converted into Courant, and we scarcely brought the sum together to be able to settle the interest. The term is now again at the door for paying von der Laar the 5000 Rthl — I have already moved heaven and hell to raise the money. But in these hard times it is in vain. I have fallen ill over it and lie here wretched — I have run out of time, have no travelling money, and, what is worst of all, I cannot act in the business entrusted to me — To this is now further added that Minister Stein is not my patron, and that I am to give back the instruction on the bank for those 6000 Rthl lent 2 years ago, and in this way I am, for Your Serene Highness's cause's sake, in continual

arrest — To Königsberg I cannot write the cause, for Your Serene Highness's honour's sake — I have had just cause hitherto to excuse myself on the ground of illness, but in the end the matter will nonetheless come to light, and it might prove prejudicial to Your Serene Highness yourself. With an honest heart I have striven to find a remedy, I hoped to obtain something through your help, and I have already 2000 Rthl; there are wanting only a further 3125 Rthl. Now I learn through Amelang, to my very greatest horror, that the 2000 r. which Herr Meyer, out of compassion for me, assigns — are to be taken from those 6000 Rthl which I two years ago instructed for Your Serene Highness. Had this not been disclosed to me by Amelang — the monies with 1600 Rthl to me and with 3400 Rthl paid to Eysenhardt — then the bank, or Minister Stein, since the bill of exchange given by me for the 6000 Rthl has long since expired, [text unclear] and would forthwith have proceeded according to bill-of-exchange law and formally arrested me anew. I now beg Your Serene Highness most urgently to have a remedy provided, so that at least the 3125 Rthl may be paid here. For with my 2000 Rthl and with the costs I have laid out I am willing to wait, and to procure myself money for the journey to Vienna. Thereupon I beg just as urgently that an order be sent at once to Privy Councillor Amelang, that he restore to me the bank instruction for 6000 Rthl, since Eysenhardt has deposited it with him in the original — This is the only means whereby these notorious matters may be set aside, Your Serene Highness's honour and my own saved, and I be made free again, so as to pursue the business most graciously entrusted to me — once my health, which has been quite [uncertain: shattered] by these accursed events, has been restored — and, if possible, to re-establish much. In all other matters I refer to my preceding letters, and leave it to Your Serene Highness's judgement what you may be pleased to do. Only for the payment of the 3125 Rthl provide the most speedy remedy, and write to Herr Amelang that he restore to me at once the bank instruction for the 6000 Rthl, to which he is willing and ready — for all too unwillingly would I let the matter become noised abroad in Königsberg, and make known the true cause of the delay of my journey. In the hope of the most speedy compliance with my request I remain Your Serene Highness's faithful and most obedient servant. Berlin, the 25th of April 1808. I observe that the term of payment to von der Laar falls due on the 14th of May, and that there are only 22 days net remaining — these I will yet await here; then let it go as God wills. v Triebenfeld
