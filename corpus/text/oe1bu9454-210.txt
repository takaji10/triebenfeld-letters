--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld reports on three lawsuits. He hopes to recover Trąbczyn by vindication and so save 22,000 Rthl of dowry money. Falz, President Smagorzewski and the assessors received payments. He says the people of Oleśnica owe 39,270 Rthl, and that Polish peasants from Oleśnica wish to enter into their contract. Niedźwiecki owes 6,000 Rthl and the tenant of the propination rights 3,000 Rthl. Hawich must go. By New Year he hopes for 44,000 Rthl, including 12,000 Rthl from the old Hollanders and 6,000 Rthl from Radzinski of Szetlewek. The Prince is asked to obtain leave from Danckelmann for the clerk Sommer.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
210

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord, I have this moment received three letters from Your Serene Highness, against which You must also have received three from me.

The business here is of such consequence that it almost robs me of all my senses; I do not know where to turn first. The newer courses of business in the local Zürich and other bureaux were as strange to me when I came here as optics are to a man born blind — Now I know them and it goes better.

Here one sets a term in a case, but then the case is not taken up; rather, the Ups, or the so-called entry in the registers, must first be made. Then, as it pleases the President, he takes up the register, and one must wait from nine in the morning until noon to see whether the matters one has in hand come up. So I still stand with the Trąbczyn one — today it is still the 11th case on the register; it may now come up at any hour, for many times a lawsuit lasts only half an hour, and often too they go per non sunt? and then a new term must again be petitioned for and a new term set, which then always, according to the value of the object, costs, as with Trąbczyn, 237 Rthl. — Now since there are 3 such lawsuits pending here, I must guard the nest

Meanwhile matters stand very well; Trąbczyn I hope quite certainly to vindicate. Falz is a cold man — He is held here to be the cleverest, and the judge listens to his word — He has now received 300 Rthl, and I have promised him much more if we prevail — The President of the Tribunal, a certain Smagorzewski, is an old acquaintance of mine — He has accepted 100 #.

Two assessors who are old friends of the referendary Sommer have each received thirty; there are two more whom I am working upon in another manner, and the wine already costs me some 160 Rthl. By Trąbczyn a very great deal is to be won — for besides the estates we save at once the 22,000 Rthl of dower money registered, and the remaining costs which Dąbska must make good are then indeed twice as high again — this is the judges' own sentiment — She also gives and means to force it through intercession and great [uncertain: fehlen] — But God will help me — The 2nd matter is with the Oleśnica lads.

I must have the fellows out, for they already owe — 16,000 Rthl canon; further, in dues laid out for them, 8,270 Rthl; the remainder of the entry money, 10,000 Rthl; to this the interest and costs with at least 5,000 Rthl; total 39,270 Rthl. They sat in the enjoyment; everything they drew out of the village they gave to the lawyers; the Prefecture has now forbidden that any further rents be given to the [illegible], and now they are checkmated — Unfortunately, however, the fellows have nothing

and the money is lost — now it has been managed that several Polish peasants from Oleśnica have declared themselves willing to enter into the contract and to pay the 10,000 Rthl of entry money, and also to pay the 2,000 Rthl of rent yearly in advance, and perhaps it may succeed that they also undertake to settle a part of the dues in arrears — the matter is in hand and, since I have given 30 ducats to the lower judge before whom it lies, is being briskly pursued; I also hope to obtain the eviction decree here quickly. The 3rd matter is with Niedźwiecki — he owes 6,000 Rthl; he is to pay and to quit, but will have nothing more than 2,000 cords of alder wood, which already lies at the water, likewise 60 head of cattle and a few horses, all of which I have had placed under arrest with the utmost haste. Hawich is the only scoundrel who stirs everything up, especially all the rent peasants, for they have paid nothing yet and will not pay either. Now the Prefecture has granted me military execution, but that avails nothing; they thrash the soldiers out, and so I stand bare and forsaken. Hawich must go now — then I think it will get better. Besides, I have the liberty to throw all the fellows out, provided only that I put other farmers in their place, so that the conscription lists are not [uncertain: Adict]. Now the Oleśnica Pol[ish] peasants offer to bring up as many [uncertain: Poladen] that all the holdings can be occupied and that will gladly pay the rent. This last motive, I think, ought to work. For the German oxen have not believed it — the Poles find themselves honoured that

one has shown them this confidence, and will do their utmost to come into possession of the holdings. The Oleśnica people offer to lay down 4 to 5,000 Rthl. in cash at once — the 5th matter is with the propination tenant — the fellow owes 3,000 Rthl. — he has to pay, but refuses, and wants to have a remission, or to give up the lease, because that is open to him under the Code Napoléon.

I have another already in reserve, who pays in advance, and therefore I sued the rogue the day before yesterday and applied for harsh execution, which will follow within a few days. At present the need is only so great because I am to effect too much with empty hands. But if only Trąbczyn is first vindicated, the tenant will pay 7,000 Rthl at once.

The old Hollanders, upon the contract they entered into with me, which they have solemnly assured me of, 12,000 Rthl. The Oleśnica Pol[es] can do 5,000 Rthl.

For Niedźwiecki's wood and cattle there may come in 3,000 Rthl. The propination lease from Nassalski 3,000 Rthl. The new tenant pays in advance 3,000 Rthl.

From the present Radzinski of Szetlewek 6,000 Rthl. From the outstanding rents at most 5,000 Rthl. Total 44,000 Rthl.

This could come in by New Year if all went happily. By the next post I can, on looking into it, write more on this; the referendary Sommer renders me very good services. May Your Serene Highness be pleased to write with all haste to the President Count Danckelmann at Wrocław, that he grant Sommer a further leave of 4 weeks, for did I not have the man, I should have to go under and return with the business unaccomplished — May Your Serene Highness therefore procure him the leave with the utmost haste. In deep submission I remain, until death,

Serene Highness's

Kalisz, the 13th of November

1810

humble and obedient servant

v Triebenfeld

Meierowitz also owes the Schlieben 7,800 Rthl and Your Serene Highness 3,000 Rthl of canon; he will pay the first sum at once as the remainder of the purchase money, but as to the 3,000 Rthl he [uncertain: renigirt] himself — that gives rise to another lawsuit, and I am always in a fix — but the first money I can raise goes to the Invalids' Fund, and Meierowitz must [uncertain: fehnden] the Schlieben at least a few thousand rt so that I get air.
