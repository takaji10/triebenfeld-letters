--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports on three (numbered up to five) suits pressed at Kalisz: the Trąbczyn vindication against the Dąbska, which would also save 22000 Rthl of registered dower, with fees to the advocate Falz (300 Rthl), Tribunal President Smagorzewski (100), assessors and 160 Rthl of wine; eviction of the Olesnica lads, who owe 39270 Rthl, and the offer of Polish peasants to take the holdings; arrest of Niedzewiecki's wood and cattle; and action against the propination lessee Nassalski. Complains of Hawich, useless military execution, and empty hands; expects 44000 Rthl by New Year and begs four weeks' further leave for Referendarius Sommer from President Count Danckelmann at Breslau.

Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Just this moment I receive three letters from Your Serene Highness, against which you must also most highly have received three from me.

The business here is of such importance that it almost robs me of all my senses; I do not know where to turn first. The newer courses of business in the Zürich and other bureaux here were as strange to me when I came here as optics to a man born blind — now I know them and it goes better.

Here one appoints a term-day in a cause, but then the cause is not taken up; rather the Ups, or the so-called entry in the registers, must first be made. Then, as it pleases the President, he takes up the register, and so one must wait from 9 o'clock in the morning until midday to see whether the causes one has in hand come up. So I still stand with the Trąbczyn one — today it is still the 11th cause in the register; it may now come up at any hour, for often a suit lasts only half an hour, and often too they go per non sunt? and then a new term-day must again be petitioned for and a new term-day made, which each time, according to the object, as with Trąbczyn, costs 237 Rthl. Now, since three such suits lie here, I must guard the nest.

Meanwhile matters stand very well. Trąbczyn I hope quite certainly to vindicate. Falz is a cold man — he is held here to be the shrewdest, and the judge listens to his word. He has now received 300 Rthl, and I have promised him a great deal more if we obtain judgment. The President of the Tribunal, a certain Smagorzewski, is an old acquaintance of mine — he has accepted 100 [uncertain: rt].

Two assessors who are old friends of the referendary Sommer have each received thirty; and there are still 2 whom I am working upon in another manner, and the wine already costs me some 160 Rthl. By Trąbczyn a very great deal is to be gained — for besides the estates we at once rescue the registered 22000 Rthl marriage portion, and the remaining costs which the Dąbska must make good are then indeed twice as high again — this is the sentiment of the judges themselves. She too gives way, and would compel it through intercession and great failings — but God will help me. The 2nd cause is with the Olesnica lads.

The matter is in hand and, since I have given the lower judge before whom it lies 30 ducats, is being pressed briskly; and I hope here soon to obtain the decree of eviction. The 3rd cause is with Niedzwiedzki — he owes 6000 Rthl; he is to pay and to quit, but will have nothing further either than 2000 fathoms of alder wood, which already stands at the water, likewise 60 head of cattle and several horses, all of which I have had placed under arrest with all haste. Hawich is the one rogue who stirs up everything, and above all the rent-paying peasants, for these have as yet paid nothing and will not pay either. Now the prefecture has granted me military execution, but that helps nothing: they thrash the soldiers out, and so I stand bare and forsaken. Hawich must now go — then I think it will grow better. Besides this I have the liberty to throw out all the fellows, provided only that I install other farmers, so that the conscription lists be not [uncertain: Adict]. Now the Olesnica Polish peasants offer to bring up so many Poles that all the holdings may be occupied, and who will gladly pay the rent. This last motive, I think, must work. For that the German oxen have not believed — the Poles find themselves honoured that

one has shown them the confidence, and will bid everything to come into possession of the holdings. The Olesnica men offer to lay down 4 to 5000 Rthl in cash at once. I have another in petto already, who pays in advance, and therefore I laid a complaint against the scoundrel the day before yesterday and applied for harsh execution, which will follow within a few days.
