te vom 28ten Septbr. c. a. und dessen Beylagen
werden Ew Hochfürstliche Durchlaucht gnädigst zu
entnehmen geruhen, daß der Sigismund
von Rzadkowski das in Głazewo belege¬
ne Erbzinsguth an seine Halbschwester die
Susanna geborne Treben verkauft hat.
Da nun Ew. Hochfürstl. Durchlauchten das
Vorkaufsrecht zustehet, so bitten wir höchst¬
dieselben
(Der Contract ist unter Vorbehalt
der Fürstln. Rechte approbirt worden.)
sich darüber, ob Höchstdieselben von dem
Vorkaufsrechte Gebrauch machen wollen,
binnen S. Wochen gnädigst zu erklären.
weil nach Ablauf dieser Frist den Gesetzen gemäß

Sie Wir mit tiefster Ehrfurcht beharren
Ew Durchlauchten
unterthänigste
Das Gerichts Amt hieselbst
Metzig
Neumann
Betsche
d 10ten Aprill 1805

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
The Betsche court office, over the signatures of Metzig and Neumann, forwards a copy of the purchase contract of 28 September by which Sigismund von Rzadkowski sold his hereditary quit-rent holding at Głazewo to his half-sister Susanna née Treben, the contract having been approved under reservation of the Princely rights. It asks the Fürst to declare within the stated weeks whether he will exercise his right of pre-emption, since silence after that term counts in law as renunciation.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

From the purchase contract of the 28th September of this year, appended here in Copy, and its enclosures, Your Most Serene Highness will most graciously be pleased to gather that Sigismund von Rzadkowski has sold the hereditary quit-rent estate situated in Głazewo to his half-sister, Susanna née Treben. Since now the right of pre-emption belongs to Your Most Serene Highness, we beg that the same (the contract has been approved under reservation of the Princely rights) may most graciously declare within S. weeks whether the same wishes to make use of the right of pre-emption; because after the lapse of this term it is assumed, in accordance with the laws, that the same has renounced this right in this instance.

She/We remain, in deepest reverence, Your Serene Highness's

most humble and obedient

The Court Office here

Metzig

Neumann

Betsche

the 10th April 1805
