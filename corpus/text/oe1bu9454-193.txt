Durchlauchtigster Fürst
Gnädigster Fürst und Herr!
Ewr Hochfürstliche Durchlaucht gnädiges Schreiben erhalte ich so eben (es ist der

Der Plan wegen der Reiſe ſcheitert dadurch; zudes würde ich Euer Durchlaucht

Aufsehen und mann kann dorten nicht so ungeſtört handeln nur ſprechen.
Ich komme noch diesen Monath, und ehe ich nach Berlin gehe so wahr ein Gott über
uns ist selbsten nach Schlawenschitz. Den es ereignen sich jezt Fälle worüber
ich Erer durchlaucht hohe Willensmeinung wißen muß, um in Berlin reden zu

Nach Obrist Scharffenbergs Versicherung gähret es in Peterburg bis zum Aufkrauß
Ich richte mein Augenmerk ſehr dahin —
Die Pohlnische Güter Angelegenheiten werden sich gut enden, die Dabsha
hat schon 2 mahl den Termin ausgewesen und zahlt alle Kosten um mir Zeit
zu genommen, der Prefect Garczynski läßt jezt dem Honrichs aufs
unun arretiren. Ich habe ihm selbst gebeten alles streng untersuchen zu laßen,
Honrichs ist trag den Attenten des Podprefecten Sarzechscha dennoch wie
der Prefect Decretirt hat ein Schelm-
Er ist über 10000 Rthl schuldig. — Mann hatt ihm immer gehen laßen, weil er
durch Winke die Menschen irre geleitet als brächte er das Geld an Ewr

Durchlaucht oder nur - da sich die Prefectus um von dem Gegentheil
überzeugt, daß er auch keinen Groschen hierher gegeben hat, so greift
man ihm um an – der Podprefect hat Caution für den Patron
geleistet — Mann glaubt daß der auch das mehreste aus die Güter
durch Honrichs gezogen. Ein Glück, daß ich die 600 Rthl von den Kerl

Garczynski Läß Heckern unter Assistence des Gerichts angestelten

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports the prince's letter took ten days to arrive, wrecking the travel plan, and urges him not to come as far as Parchwitz, being too conspicuous; Triebenfeld will instead visit Schlawenschitz before going to Berlin. Relays Colonel Scharffenberg's word that Petersburg is close to an uprising. On the Polish estates, the Dabsha delays and pays costs; Prefect Garczyński has Honrichs, a debtor of over 10000 Rthl, arrested, with Heckern and Administrator Radecki investigating so arrears may be paid and the estates returned; notes 600 Rthl recovered and Count Trzcinski's involvement concealed.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! Your Most Serene Highness's gracious letter I receive only this moment (it is the 10th, 5 o'clock in the afternoon /, so it has been 10 days on the road. The plan concerning the journey is thereby wrecked; besides, I would also most humbly beg Your Serene Highness not to make the journey as far as Parchwitz. It attracts notice, and one cannot there act, nor even speak, so undisturbed. I shall come yet this month, and before I go to Berlin — as surely as there is a God above — myself to Schlawenschitz. For cases are now arising upon which I must know Your Serene Highness's high will and opinion, in order to be able to speak in Berlin. By all probability the whole theatre is changing. — According to Colonel Scharffenberg's assurance, things are fermenting in Petersburg to the point of an uprising. I direct my attention very much thither — The Polish estate affairs will end well; the Dabsha has already twice [uncertain: let the term pass] and pays all the costs in order to gain time from me; the Prefect Garczyński is now having Honrichs arrested [illegible]. I have myself begged him to have everything strictly investigated. Honrichs is, notwithstanding the attestations of the Sub-prefect Sarzechscha, nevertheless, as the Prefect has decreed, a rogue — He owes over 10000 Rthl. — One has always let him go on, because by hints he led people astray, as though he were bringing the money to Your

Serene Highness, or merely — since the Prefect has convinced himself of the contrary, that he has not given a single Groschen hither, they are now laying hold of him — the Sub-prefect has furnished security for the Patron — It is believed that he too drew the greater part out of the estates through Honrichs. A piece of good fortune that I got the 600 Rthl back from the fellow, in order to let him know nothing of the connexion with Count Trzcinski — Garczynski is having Heckern, with the assistance of the court-appointed Administrator Radecki, search everything out, so that the arrears may be quickly paid and the estates delivered up to Your Serene Highness free again. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's — Kontopp, the 10th of April 1810. — most faithful and most obedient servant v Triebenfeld
