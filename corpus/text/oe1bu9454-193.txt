--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A letter ten days in transit has wrecked the travel plan; Triebenfeld urges the Prince not to come as far as Parchwitz, where they would be noticed, and promises to come himself to Schlawenschitz before going to Berlin, needing the Prince's decisions first. He reports unrest brewing in Petersburg on Colonel Scharffenberg's word, delaying tactics by the Dabsha woman, and the arrest of Honrichs, who owes over 10,000 Rthl and passed nothing on; 600 Rthl have been recovered.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Your Most Serene Highness's gracious letter I receive only this moment (it is the 10th, 5 o'clock in the afternoon /, so it has been ten days on the road. The plan concerning the journey is thereby wrecked; moreover I would also most humbly beg Your Serene Highness not to make the journey as far as Parchwitz. It attracts notice, and one cannot there act, let alone speak, so undisturbed. I shall come this very month, and before I go to Berlin — as sure as there is a God above us — myself to Schlawenschitz. For cases are now arising upon which I must know Your Serene Highness's high will and opinion, in order to be able to speak in Berlin. By all likelihood the whole theatre is changing — According to Colonel Scharffenberg's assurance, matters in Petersburg are fermenting to the point of an outbreak. I direct my attention very much thither — The Polish estate affairs will end well; the Dabsha woman has already twice let the term pass by and pays all the costs in order to gain time from me; the prefect Garczynski is now having Honrichs arrested [uncertain: aufs unun]. I myself begged him to have everything strictly investigated. Honrichs is, despite the attestations of the sub-prefect Sarzechscha, nevertheless, as the prefect has decreed, a rogue — He owes over 10000 Rthl. — He has always been let go on, because by hints he misled people, as though he were bringing the money to Your

Serene Highness, or merely — since the prefect has convinced himself of the contrary, namely that he has not given a single Groschen over to this place, they are now laying hands upon him — the sub-prefect has furnished security for the patron — It is believed that he too drew the greater part out of the estates through Honrichs. A piece of good fortune that I got the 600 Rthl back from the fellow, in order to let him know nothing of the connexion with Count Trzciński — Garczynski is having Heckern, with the assistance of the administrator Radecki appointed by the court, search everything out, so that the arrears may be quickly paid and the estates handed back to Your Serene Highness free of encumbrance.

In deepest respect I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Kontop, the 10th of April

1810.

faithful and most obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld
