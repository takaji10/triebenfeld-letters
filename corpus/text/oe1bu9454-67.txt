Durchlauchtigſter Fürſt
Gnädigster Fürst und Herr!
Ewer Durchlaucht höchst gnädiges Schreiben von 24 ten erhielt ich heute als
ich ſo eben im Begriff war an Höchſtdieselben zu ſchreiben.
Ewr Hochfürstliche Durchlaucht wolte ich nur ganz unterthänigst melden
daß ich von hier noch nicht abgehen kan, indem Coste nicht gekommen ist.
Ich habe zwar köntig aber Schweinere noch nicht verpachtet, und
in Ansehung der Berliner Geschichte bin ich mich nicht aufs reine, den
Dies ist ein mit stumpfen Eysen geschlagene Wunde die immer wieder
aufbrucht, und die mir noch das Lebenkosten wird - Indes muß Gott
helfen, es daure nun so lange als es wolle.
Nächst diesen hält mich die Pohlnische Angelegenheit hier nun gefaßelt
Henrichs war wie Ewr Hochfürstliche Durchlaucht ich lezthin anzeigen
hier; was er vorgeschlagen habe ich höchst denenſelben gehorſamſt
eingesandt - Nach seiner Abreise erhielt ich ein Schreiben vom
Hawich, der mir anzeigt
daß Honrichs ein Erzbetrüger sey.
Ob dies nun zwar erst erwiesen werden muß, so habe ich doch so
fort dem Honrichs um Hawich hier her beschieden, um die Sache

zu untersuchen, auch will ich wenn die L: hier sein werden. Den Actuarius
von hier der Teutsch ein pohleisch Bau nach Zagorowo senden, damit
der dorten alle Leute zusammen berufen und genau recherchinen soll
was Honrichs, oder irgend jemand von den Unterthanen und Pachter
genommen hat. Honrichs und jeder muß sich den ausweisen wohin
die Gelder verwandt sind, und da muß sich der Betrug zeigen¬
Ich kan das mit allen Rechte da die Sequestration nach den beigefunden
Decretin aufgehoben und nur ein Aufseher, daß kein Geld dort
wegenommen werden darf, angestellt ist, nicht glauben.
Nach diesen Verfügungen darst doch Honrichs nichts nehmen es muß doch ans Licht
wer Gelder gezogen und wohin er sie verwandt hat. Ew Durchlaucht
haben nichts erhalten und ich
ich nicht.

Leute habe ichs doch immer selbst gehört, daß sie jezt nicht zahlen könten.
Seis indes wie ihm wolle, die Sache muß am Tage
So viel ist übrigens ganz gewis daß weder Henrici oder irgend jemand

Der einzige Henrichs hat blos gegen das Gebahren der Sambstia und

Honrichs und Heckert haben sich Hufen zugeeignet und angebaut

mir nebst den 3 Resolutionen zurück zu senden.
Mein nach Zagorowo geſandter Bothe, ist noch nicht zurück - Hawich
und Hecker müßen antworten, oder eigentlich kommen - bleiben sie aus den
bin ich überzeugt, daß es Lube sind
Ich werde genau zu erforsahm suchen was an der Sache ist, im voraus
bin ich überzeugt deß Hawich und Hecker Canalle sind, den wie¬
können die Liebe sagen das Geld aus den Lande gefahren ist, ich habe
keinen Groschen geſehen, auch kan Honrichs nichts nehmen, weil es

Honrichs thut wenigstens seine Pflicht, daß er die Pohl Hunde nichts nehmen

gegen alles muthig protectirt.
Ich werde von Brzechsta um Garczynski alles erfahren hat Hecker
und Hawich falsche Anzeigen gemacht so werde sie gezüchtiget wer¬
Hecker soll von seine Papierne Caution nie was wieder sehen.
Mit dem Spiritus und Eysen wäre viel zu machen, da von Ewr

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports he cannot yet leave Kontop: Coste has not arrived, Schweinere is not leased, and the Berlin affair remains an open wound. Hawich denounces Henrichs/Honrichs as a swindler, so both are summoned and an Actuarius is to be sent to Zagorowo to question subjects and lease-holders about monies taken; the sequestration is lifted and only an overseer appointed. Alleges Hawich, Honrichs and Heckert have appropriated and built on Hufen. Notes Köckritz, Kleist, Count Götz, the moratorium extended to Johanni 1810 or 1812, and asks for the freight, toll and weighing notes for the iron.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! Your Serene Highness's most gracious letter of the 24th I received today, just as I was on the point of writing to Your Most Exalted Self. I wished only most humbly to report to Your Most Serene Highness that I cannot yet depart from here, inasmuch as Coste has not come. I have, it is true, leased out [uncertain: köntig], but not yet Schweinere, and as regards the Berlin affair I am not clear in my own mind, for this is a wound struck with a blunt iron which breaks open again and again, and which will yet cost me my life. — Meanwhile God must help, let it last now as long as it will. Next to this, the Polish business now holds me fettered here. Henrichs was here, as I lately reported to Your Most Serene Highness; what he proposed I have most obediently forwarded to Your Most Exalted Self. — After his departure I received a letter from Hawich, who informs me that Henrichs is an arch-swindler. Although this must first be proved, I have nonetheless at once summoned Henrichs and Hawich here, in order to investigate the matter

— and I also intend, when the L: are here, to send the Actuarius from here, who [is] German [and] a Polish [uncertain: Bau], to Zagorowo, so that there he may call all the people together and investigate exactly what Honrichs, or anyone else, has taken from the subjects and lease-holders. Honrichs and everyone must then give account of where the monies have been applied, and there the fraud must show itself. I cannot with all right believe this, since the sequestration, according to the decrees found enclosed, has been lifted and only an overseer has been appointed, so that no money may be taken away there. Under these dispositions Honrichs surely may take nothing; it must yet come to light who has drawn monies and to what he has applied them. Your Serene Highness has received nothing, and I have not either. Whatever there is in the matter shall and must come out; still I do not believe Hainrich's reports, for more than 12 deputations from the communes have been here and have constantly begged for a term of payment. From these people I have always heard it myself, that they could not pay at present. Be it however as it may, the matter must come to light. This much moreover is quite certain, that neither Henrici nor anyone else has acted [uncertain: trom — possibly "against", possibly "according to"] the instructions and powers of attorney which I always gave. Henrichs alone has frankly protested against the conduct of the Sambstia and the clergy there, so that no matter could ever become legally binding whereby Your Serene Highness's entitlements were secured. As I believe, the gentlemen have all defrauded [us]: Hawich, Honrichs and Heckert have appropriated Hufen to themselves and built upon them

— fortunately, however, this is unlawful and [uncertain: approved] by me; one can here chastise and let go. The holdings, too, are so handsomely laid out that each is worth several thousand Rthl. — If the gentlemen had defrauded, there is something to hold on to. When this is finished, and I am clear here with the Widows' Fund concerning my own affairs, then I shall come to lay myself at Your Serene Highness's feet. Just now Grevenitz writes from Berlin that Gen. v. Köckritz has said much that is laudable and good of Your Serene Highness. — God grant that in this year now begun everything may go according to Your Serene Highness's wish, wherein Your Most Exalted Self may be compelled to learn to forget all your sufferings. I was written that General L. v. Köckritz would retire, and that General Kleist would [take] his post with the King; I have asked him myself about it, but have as yet received no answer. That the false Count Götz goes ad patres is well; I have learned wondrous things of the man. — Of the new organisations some hope much — I nothing; the money business will yet cause great terror. — Grevenitz writes that the general moratorium had been prolonged to Johanni 1810, and it is said that by a more recent resolution it is to last until Johanni 1812. — This too I do not believe; against it B — and Lecht Meyer Wolff will contend. May Your Most Serene Highness yet deign most graciously to send me soon the freight, toll and weighing notes etc. for the iron, sent to you in May, so that I may convince the boatmen that I should have to pay here a second time; otherwise I shall not get the iron out. In deepest respect I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's humble and obedient servant. Kontop, 1 January 1840. Just at the [illegible] v Triebenfeld

— moment at which I meant to dispatch this letter, I receive by an express the enclosed letter of Honrichs, which Your Most Serene Highness will deign to return to me together with the 3 resolutions. My messenger sent to Zagorowo is not yet back. — Hawich and Hecker must answer, or rather come — if they stay away, then I am convinced that they are liars. I shall seek to ascertain exactly what there is in the matter; in advance I am convinced that Hawich and Hecker are scoundrels, for how can the dear fellows say that the money has been carried out of the country? I have not seen a Groschen; nor can Honrichs take anything, because the people are forbidden to pay monies to Honrichs, and that this is welcome to the rent-paying folk one may believe without an oath. Honrichs at least does his duty, in that he lets the Polish dogs take nothing. I am already content that he obeys my orders and confronts the rabble there without fear, lets nothing be taken, and courageously [uncertain: protects] against everything. I shall learn everything from Brzechsta and Garczynski; if Hecker and Hawich have made false denunciations they shall be chastised, [and] Hecker shall never see anything again of his paper security. With the spirits and the iron much might be done; since nothing can be sold from Your Serene Highness's foundry, I shall no doubt have to obtain iron elsewhere. — I must make the trial and see what comes of this [uncertain: Barotto Hamel].
