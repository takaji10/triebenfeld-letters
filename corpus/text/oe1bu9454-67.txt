--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports he cannot yet leave Kontop: Coste has not arrived, Schweinere is not leased, and the Berlin affair remains an open wound. Hawich denounces Henrichs/Honrichs as a swindler, so both are summoned and an Actuarius is to be sent to Zagorowo to question subjects and lease-holders about monies taken; the sequestration is lifted and only an overseer appointed. Alleges Hawich, Honrichs and Heckert have appropriated and built on Hufen. Notes Köckritz, Kleist, Count Götz, the moratorium extended to Johanni 1810 or 1812, and asks for the freight, toll and weighing notes for the iron.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Your Serene Highness's most gracious letter of the 24th I received today, just as I was on the point of writing to Your Most Serene Self. I wished only to report most humbly to Your Most Serene Highness that I cannot yet depart from here, inasmuch as Coste has not come. I have indeed [uncertain: köntig], but have not yet leased out Schweinere, and with regard to the Berlin affair I am not clear in my own mind, for this is a wound struck with a blunt iron which keeps breaking open again, and which will yet cost me my life — Meanwhile God must help; let it now last as long as it will.

Next to this, the Polish business holds me here now [uncertain: gefaßelt]. Henrichs was here, as I lately reported to Your Most Serene Highness; what he proposed I have most obediently forwarded to Your Most Serene Self — After his departure I received a letter from Hawich, who informs me

that Honrichs is an arch-swindler.

Although this must first be proved, I have nevertheless at once summoned Honrichs and Hawich here, in order to investigate the matter

and, when the [uncertain: L:] are here, I also intend to send the local court clerk, who is a German with Polish training, to Zagorowo, so that he may there call all the people together and investigate exactly what Honrichs, or anyone else, has taken from the subjects and tenants. Honrichs and every man must then show where the monies have been applied, and there the fraud must come to light. I cannot, with every right, believe this, since by the decrees found the sequestration has been lifted and only an overseer has been appointed, so that no money may be taken away there. Under these dispositions Honrichs surely may take nothing; it must surely come to light who drew monies and to what use he applied them. Your Serene Highness has received nothing, and I

I have not either.

What truth there is in the matter shall and must come out; still I do not believe Hainrich's reports, for more than 12 deputations from the communities have been here and have constantly begged for time to pay. From these people I have always heard it myself, that they could not pay at present. Be it as it may, the matter must come to the light of day. So much, moreover, is quite certain, that neither Henrici nor anyone else has acted contrary to the instructions and powers of attorney which I always gave. Henrichs alone has frankly protested against the conduct of the Sambstia and of the clergy there, so that no matter could ever acquire legal force whereby Your Serene Highness's rights would be secured. As I believe, the gentlemen have all cheated; for Hawich, Honrichs and Heckert have appropriated Hufen to themselves and built upon them

but fortunately this is unlawful and was never approved by me; here one may chastise and let go. The holdings are also so handsomely laid out that each is worth several thousand Rthl — If the gentlemen had cheated, there is something to hold on to.

When this is finished, and I am clear here with the Widows' Fund concerning my own affairs, then I shall come to lay myself at Your Serene Highness's feet.

Grevenitz writes just now from Berlin that Lieutenant-General von Köckritz has said a great deal that is [uncertain: Mühliches] and good of Your Serene Highness — God grant that in this year now begun everything may go according to Your Serene Highness's wish, where Your Most Serene Self are compelled to learn to forget all your sufferings.

I was written that Lieutenant-General von Köckritz would retire, and that General Kleist would take his post with the King; I have now asked him myself about it but have as yet received no answer.

That the false Count Götz is going ad Patrim is good; I have learned wondrous things of the man — From the new organisations some hope much — I nothing; the money business will yet cause great terror — Grevenitz writes that the general moratorium had been extended to Midsummer 1810, and it is said that by a more recent resolution it is to last until Midsummer 1812 — This too I do not believe; against it B — and Lecht Meyer Wolff will contend.

May Your Most Serene Highness deign most graciously to send me as soon as possible the freight, toll and weighing dockets etc. for the iron, sent to you in May, so that I may convince the boatman that I should have to pay here a second time; otherwise I shall not get the iron out. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's humble and obedient servant. Kontop

1 January 1840. Just at this [uncertain: moment] v. Triebenfeld

moment when I was about to dispatch this letter, I receive by an express the enclosed letter of Honrichs, which Your Most Serene Highness may deign to return to me together with the 3 resolutions.

My messenger sent to Zagorowo has not yet returned — Hawich and Hecker must answer, or rather come — if they stay away, I am convinced that they are [uncertain: Lube]. I shall seek to ascertain exactly what truth there is in the matter; in advance I am convinced that Hawich and Hecker are scoundrels, for how can [uncertain: the Lieben] say that the money has been carried out of the country? I have not seen a Groschen of it; nor can Honrichs take anything, because the people are forbidden to pay any monies to Honrichs, and that this is welcome to the rent-paying people may be believed without an oath. Honrichs at least does his duty, in that he lets the Polish dogs take nothing. I am already content that he lives up to my orders and steps up to the rabble there without fear, lets nothing be taken, and boldly protects against everything.

I shall learn everything from Brzechsta and Garczynski; if Hecker and Hawich have made false reports, they shall be chastised, and Hecker shall never see anything again of his paper security. Much might be done with the spirits and iron; since nothing can be sold from Your Serene Highness's foundry, I shall doubtless obtain iron elsewhere — I must make the trial, and see what comes of this [uncertain: Barotto Hamel].
