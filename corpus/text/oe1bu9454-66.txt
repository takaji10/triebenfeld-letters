--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports the expected verdict against the false denouncers (six years' fortress imprisonment) and the agitators; pleads for Heiler while noting Honrichs's claim against him, and urges refusing the colonists' and old peasants' requests for remission of interest, since arrears can be reckoned in on a sale. Recommends gifts of roebucks to the Polish Upper Prefect and tribunal president, retails rumours of the King, Napoleon and Russian moves in Galicia, presses for receipts to free 70-74 Centner of iron arrested at Neusalz by the skippers Kulawy and Neumann (243 Rthl 14 s. freight outstanding), complains of soaring process costs (Trąbcziner, Niedrewiciliische, Octaviowshische) and of his own want of money, and proposes delivering 2000 Centner of bar iron for 8000 Rthl.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
The judgment will now, as I am written, turn out thus: that the false denouncers are to be punished with six years' fortress imprisonment — the agitators, however, [uncertain: flogged out] and banished from the country. As regards Heiler I shall nevertheless do everything possible, for his father's sake, who is Upper Consistorial Councillor in Berlin; but Honrichs raises a claim against him which exceeds his paper security threefold, and from Your Serene Highness's side too he is being held to account, for it was through him that the administration was introduced. If otherwise the colonists there and the old peasants beg for a remission of the interest for the future (for at present nobody pays anything, because Honrichs is not permitted to take anything, but neither does he let any other take anything par forte), then one must not listen to it. If the fellows have nothing, one can for the time being take nothing from them; but in the case of a sale one has the advantage that one can nevertheless reckon in all the arrears. And a Prefect in Poland has an extensive power — if Briechfa buys, he would soon torment the poor beasts — he can spare them again in respect of public taxes and burdens; consequently Your Most Serene Highness need for the present pay no heed to the [uncertain: daubings] and concede nothing to the fellows; they pay in any case — I let nothing go, and they have long given nothing.

here shoot three roebucks, whereof Honrichs is to take one along to the Upper Prefect Parczyneli, one for the President of the High Tribunal Zahrzewshe, and one for the [uncertain: sind] Prefect Arsechsa; that, I think, will work more than money, for the fellows have everything in their hands and cut their pipes at will. If it goes as wished, then Honrichs will get the lease-rent back at New Year, the administration will be entirely lifted, and then we shall receive much money. — One has there at present complete power over the subjects, and therefore this class of men all wish the Prussian government back; but meanwhile one can [uncertain: pain] them. So much for the Polish affairs. Whether the King will really come to Berlin is still doubted by many; if he really comes, then he will also go to Paris — for only a few days ago somebody wrote to me: if it goes back to Berlin, then it goes to Paris as well, for whence should Louise take the strength to refuse the invitation, not to see the place where the finest fashions in the world beckon. — Meanwhile I believe nothing at all of the promises that are said to have been made to our good King. They will likely leave us lying in the Skat, and our wretched

[uncertain: Existenz] overlooked as a mere point on the map of Europe, as at Tilsit and Schönbrunn. — It was said a few days ago that we should get back Hannover, a piece of Hessen, Braunschweig, Magdeburg and the Halberstadt territory; but for this a greater faith is required than that which Frederick the Only ascribed to the [uncertain: prelate] of Scharnewand when he was to have the mountain by Cont carried away. Here people will absolutely have it that Napoleon suffers from dropsy of the chest and that his passport is already lying on the little table at Pluto's for signature. Were that so, then something might yet be hoped, especially if with us one no longer organised but acted with energy. Something must indeed be afoot: the Poles were to be on the return march, to move on the 11th into their winter quarters and their garrisons; they were already as far as [uncertain: konen] Kalisz and those regions — but on the 8th came a counter-order, that they must go back the same instant as far as Galicia, because the Russians were occupying Galicia and pressing back the Poles standing there. Honrichs says that since then everything is full, the Russians can [text lost] in order to appropriate to themselves the province of Warsaw. — As soon as I am finished here I shall come at once to Schlawenschich, and then I shall stay there a long while. — I will lease everything out, only let Coste keep [illegible] and let me first have a free hand again. That the Princess Emilie may be right happy I wish from the bottom of my heart — she is the crown of the female sex, if [uncertain: all that I have seen]. For the continuance of Your most gracious favour I beg, and I remain, until death, in deepest reverence, Your Most Serene Highness's Kontop, the 16th of December most faithful servant There lie here in v Triebenfeld
