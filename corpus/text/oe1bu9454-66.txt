--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports an expected verdict of six years' fortress imprisonment for the false denouncers and banishment for the agitators; advises granting the colonists and old peasants no remission of interest since arrears can be reckoned into a sale, and describes three roebucks sent to Polish officials to secure the lifting of the administration and Honrichs's lease. Adds war and political rumours, asks for receipts to free 70 hundredweight of iron held at Neusalz for freight, complains of mounting lawsuit and stamp costs, and proposes delivering 2,000 hundredweight of bar iron for 8,000 Rthl.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
The judgement will now, so I am told, come out to the effect that the false denouncers are to be sentenced to six years' fortress imprisonment, while the agitators are to be [uncertain: flogged out] and banished from the country.

As regards Heiler I shall nevertheless do everything possible for his father's sake, who is Upper Consistorial Councillor in Berlin; but Honrichs is advancing a claim against him which exceeds his paper security threefold, and he is also being held answerable on Your Serene Highness's side, for it was through him that the administration was brought in. If otherwise the colonists and old peasants there come begging on account of a remission of the interest for the future (for at present nobody pays anything, because Honrichs is not permitted to take anything, but neither does he allow anyone else to take anything par force), then one must not listen to it. If the fellows have nothing, then nothing can be taken from them for the time being. In the case of a sale, however, one has the advantage that all arrears can nevertheless be reckoned in. And a prefect in Poland has extensive power — if Briechfa buys, he would certainly torment the poor creatures — he can spare them again with respect to public taxes and burdens; consequently Your Most Serene Highness need pay no heed for the present to the [uncertain: trivialities], and should concede nothing to the fellows, for they pay nothing anyway. I am having

nothing, and have long given nothing.

here have three roebucks shot, of which Honrichs is to take one along to the chief prefect Parczyneli, one for the president of the high tribunal Zahrzewshe, and one for the [uncertain: sind] prefect Arsechsa; that, I think, will have more effect than money, for the fellows have everything in their hands and cut their pipes at their own pleasure. If it goes as wished, then Honrichs gets the lease back at New Year, the administration will be wholly lifted, and then we shall receive much money. — One now has there complete power over the subjects, which is why this class of men all wish the Prussian government back; but in the meantime one can [uncertain: grieve] them. So much for the Polish affairs. Whether the King will really come to Berlin is still doubted by many; if he really comes, then he will also go to Paris — for someone wrote to me only a few days ago: if it goes back to Berlin, then it goes to Paris as well, for where should Louise find the strength to refuse the invitation and not see the place where the finest fashions in the world beckon. — Meanwhile I believe nothing at all of the promises which are said to have been made to our good King. They will probably leave us lying in the skat, and our wretched

[uncertain: Executent] overlooked as a mere point on the map of Europe, as at Tilsit and Schönbrunn. — It was said a few days ago that we should get Hanover back, a piece of Hesse, Brunswick, Magdeburg and the Halberstadt territory; but this requires a greater faith than that which Frederick the Only ascribed to the prelate of Scharnewand when he was to move the hill near Cont for him.

Here people will absolutely have it that Napoleon suffers from dropsy of the chest and that his safe-conduct lies already on the little table at Pluto's for signature. Were that so, then something might still be hoped for, especially if with us there were no more organising but action with energy. Something must indeed be afoot: the Poles were to be on the march back, to move on the 11th into their winter quarters and their garrisons; they were already as far as [uncertain: konen] Kalisz and those parts — but on the 8th a counter-order came that they must go back at once as far as Galicia, because the Russians were occupying Galicia and pressing back the Poles standing there. Honrichs says that since then everything is full, the [uncertain: Müßen] can, in order to appropriate the province of Warsaw to themselves. — As soon as I am finished here I shall come at once to Schlawenschitz, and then I shall stay there a long while. — I mean to lease everything out, if only [uncertain: Coste] holds and I first have a free hand again.

That Princess Emilie may be right happy I wish from the bottom of my heart. — She is the crown of the female sex, if indeed what I am to see. Begging the continuance of your most high goodwill, I remain, until death, in deepest reverence, Your Most Serene Highness's

Kontop, the 16th of December

most faithful servant
