--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Writing while ill and impoverished, Triebenfeld urges the Prince to recall the bank Anweisung from Eysenhardt lest he be ruined, and sets out a scheme to raise gold, cede his 250 Rthl mortgage and settle the Oppenheimer, Eysenhardt, Invalids' Fund and Madame Schlabrendorff claims for half their sum. He warns against general powers of attorney and Blanquette, citing Hecker's report that Honrichs halved the price of the Hufen from 600 to 300 Rthl, and reports obtaining French authorisation for military collection of arrears in South Prussia, complaining of Minister von Stein's disfavour.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord, Your Serene Highness's honoured letter of the 28th of last month I have just received, and I do not fail (although very [uncertain: ill] and weak) to effect it.

The [uncertain: eight-writer] copy is not yet back, and for this reason I should also have laid aside the letter enclosed to Amelang; only thus have I never [illegible] it, for which I have to [uncertain: trouble] Your Serene Highness. There is a second brother which most highly wounds your credit and mine to the wall. I hope to obtain the money upon my plain countenance. Only I beg Your Serene Highness to demand back the bank order (Banko Acarnisung) from Eysenhardt at once; otherwise I should again be called upon by the bank, and then I should be totally ruined.

[illegible] Serene Highness and my [uncertain: need] must now be to get the Gold together and to pay all the people – for that I had a prospect, that I should pledge the mortgage of 250 Rthl against it; but if Your Serene Highness has it there, so much the better. – As soon as a capital of 150 Rthl is procured, Your Serene Highness can buy 250/M and treasury notes for it, and then at once pay Oppenheimer, the principal [uncertain: Epratum], Eysenhardt, and God knows whom, and the Invalids' Fund nevertheless receives the 50000 mk in Courant, for this must be satisfied in ringing gulden; it has not deceived you. But the other rogues, it does them no harm, and supposing that Madame Schlabrendorff too [uncertain: doubts] it, in this way you will be rid of your whole debts for the half, and no one can on that account make you a reproach for it; it is [uncertain: sayable]. I refer in everything to my letter sent off three days ago; there you will find the yield and the debts to be paid, so far as I know them. I [uncertain: refer] Your Serene Highness once more to the table which I gave you two years ago,

back; through me or with my knowledge the matter there has in nothing changed, unless it be that your newly created and multiple general attorneys' chancery has done so. – O God, most gracious Lord, do be cautious in issuing general powers of attorney and blank forms. Do not [uncertain: chide] me over this and do not imagine that I wished here to play the guardian; that thought will remain forever foreign to my heart. Only let this be permitted, that I advise you as a friend and faithful adherent. But the Chief Inspector Hecker writes to me that Honrichs, on the ground of the power of attorney given him, has reduced the price for the Hufen to be sold from 600 to 300 Rthl. Is that not enough to drive one mad and frantic? Do you then mean by force to put your fortune into rogues' hands? O my most gracious Prince, that is not good – I too have let it become bitter as blood to bring matters so far; I have staked rest, health and everything on it, without in the rogues' thought having had thereby a penny to gain. It was merely the pride of having rescued you out of all [uncertain: distressed] positions and especially out of the [uncertain: three] claws of the [uncertain: Weihnrer]. O, and now that is shattered again. I repeat it once more, that in silence I effect ten times more than the whole [uncertain: crowd] of your general attorneys. – Already I have obtained from the French authorities the order that in South Prussia, by military assistance, the arrears are to be [uncertain: collected] for Your Serene Highness. – A greater and far larger thing I hope to effect from another side (for which God will soon give fortune and opportunity). My wish is soon to be well, so as to be able to get away from here. I wish this the more ardently since the Minister von Stein does not appear to be my great patron, and since they are [uncertain: bent] on chicaning me here in your affair (for otherwise they cannot get at me).

That you, my most gracious Prince and Lord, lack peace of mind I readily believe. But this lot now falls upon every man of worth. Only [uncertain: S.] rejoice – everything here on earth has after all its beginning and its end. I know religion is not foreign to you. Things too often have dazzling exteriors; the ignorant man will cast the brilliant among the heap, throw out with the rest what nature makes only for short times; but one warming ray of sun annihilates their existence, and the [uncertain: brilliant] remains. – Our misfortune is the want of unity, and the all too great goodness of heart of the King; the [illegible] and the other allies do what they please, they hate one another; only in this are they agreed, to injure the man of worth, to push him back, to make him suspect in the eyes of the world, to [uncertain: drive] him into the last corner of existence, and though the State should go to ruin over it.

To speak with Your Serene Highness I too very ardently wish. – Much would soon become clear to you. If you could really obtain there at least 150/0 of it, I should come in haste with the original mortgage and the original mortgage certificates – would cede the mortgage, and the business would be settled lightning-quick without any dispute. But I cannot travel on chance, for the misfortune that befell me here has consumed my hitherto [uncertain: peasant] store, and I am poor as blood – from my estates I have nothing – there my poor worms have scarcely enough to live on, and I remain true to my former resolve, in this cursed [uncertain: council] to take or to beg nothing from the King nor from anyone whatever. Be pleased to write very soon on this matter also; I will still await the answer. If you can obtain the Gold there in that way, then I am there quickly – cede, as aforesaid, the mortgage of 250 Rthl. The money will then be ordered here or paid, must be receipted at once and the new credit procured; every [uncertain: Weisern] has a clean secure mortgage, and so much must remain over that the [uncertain: Weise] costs are paid. – perhaps also more if you obtain more than 150/3 Rthl, so that your treasury too may be [uncertain: supported]. Now I cannot go on for weakness – but forever Your Serene Highness's
