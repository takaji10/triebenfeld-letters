--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Writing while ill and impoverished, Triebenfeld urges the Prince to recall the bank Anweisung from Eysenhardt lest he be ruined, and sets out a scheme to raise gold, cede his 250 Rthl mortgage and settle the Oppenheimer, Eysenhardt, Invalids' Fund and Madame Schlabrendorff claims for half their sum. He warns against general powers of attorney and Blanquette, citing Hecker's report that Honrichs halved the price of the Hufen from 600 to 300 Rthl, and reports obtaining French authorisation for military collection of arrears in South Prussia, complaining of Minister von Stein's disfavour.

Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! Your Serene Highness's glorified letter of the 28th of last month I receive just now, and I do not fail, although very [uncertain: brauch — "in need"/"weak"] and weak), to effect it. The eight-writer's copy is not yet back, and for this reason I should also have laid aside the letter enclosed to Amelang; only thus have I never [sent] the same off, and for that I hold Your Serene Highness dear. There is a second brother which wounds your credit and mine most highly against the wall. I hope to obtain the money upon my plain countenance. Only I beg Your Serene Highness to demand back at once the bank Anweisung [order to pay] from Eysenhardt; I should otherwise be called upon again by the bank, and then I should be totally ruined. [uncertain: bar] Serene Highness, and my need must now be to bring the Gold together and to pay all the people — I had prospects for that, that I should pledge the mortgage of 250 Rthl against it; but if Your Serene Highness has it there, so much the better. — As soon as a capital of 150 Rthl is procured, Your Serene Highness can buy therewith 250/M and Treasury notes, and if at once the Oppenheimer chief [uncertain: Epratum] Eysenhardt and God knows whom be paid, and the Invalids' Fund nevertheless receives the 50000 [uncertain: mk] in Courant, for this must be satisfied in ringing gulden, it has not deceived you. The other rogues, however — it does them no harm; and supposing it so, also Madame Schlabrendorff the [uncertain: zweist]; out of the kind you will be rid of your whole debts for half the sum, and nobody will thereupon make you a reproach for it; it is [uncertain: ge saglich]. I refer in all things to my letter sent off 3 days ago; there you will find the yield and the debts to be paid, so far as I know them. I point Your Serene Highness once more to the tableau which I gave you 2 years ago

back; through me, or with my knowledge, the matter there has in nothing altered, unless it be that your newly created and manifold general plenipotentiaries have made a chancery — O God, most gracious Lord, do be cautious with the granting of general powers of attorney and Blanquette; do not [uncertain: Schulten — scold?] me over this, and do not imagine that I wished to play the guardian here; the thought will remain forever foreign to my heart. Only let this be permitted, that I advise you as a friend and faithful adherent. But the Chief Inspector Hecker writes to me that [uncertain: Paß] Honrichs, on the ground of the power of attorney given him, has reduced the price for the Hufen to be sold from 600 to 300 Rthl. Is that not enough to drive one mad and raving? Do you then mean by force to put your fortune into scoundrels' hands? O my most gracious Prince, that is not good — I have let it become so bitterly hard for me to bring the affairs thus far, I have staked rest, health and all upon it, [and] in the scoundrels' thoughts had thereby not a denier to gain. It was merely the pride of having rescued them out of all wretched positions, and especially out of the claws of the [uncertain: Weihnrer]. Oh, and now this is shattered again. I repeat it once more, that in silence I effect 10 times more than the host of your general plenipotentiaries — already I have obtained from the French authorities the order that in South Prussia one will, by military assistance, have the arrears collected for Your Serene Highness — a further and far greater thing I hope to effect from another side (for which God will soon give fortune and occasion). My wish is to be soon well, so as to be able to get away from here. I wish this the more ardently as the Minister von Stein does not appear to be my great patron, and as [they] here in your affair are intent on chicanery against me (for otherwise they cannot get at me).

That you, my most gracious Prince and Lord, lack peace of mind I readily believe. This lot, however, now befalls every honest man. Only [uncertain: S.] rejoice — everything here on earth has after all its beginning and its end. I know religion is not foreign to you. Things have too often dazzling exteriors; the ignorant man will [uncertain: biswürften] the brilliant among the heap, throw out along with it what nature makes only for a short time; but one warming sunbeam annihilates their existence, and the brilliant remains. — Our misfortune is the lack of unity, and the all-too-great goodness of heart of the King; the M[inisters] and the other allies do what they will, they hate one another; in this alone are they agreed, to harm the honest man, to push him back, to make him suspect in the eyes of the world, to [uncertain: entsterene] him even into the last corner of his existence — and though the State should go to ruin over it. To speak with Your Serene Highness I too wish most ardently — much would soon become clear to you. Could you really obtain there at least 150/0 of that, I should come in haste with the original mortgage and the original mortgage certificates — I should cede the mortgage, and the business would be settled quick as lightning, without any dispute. But at hazard I cannot travel, for the misfortune that has befallen me here has consumed my hitherto [uncertain: bauren] store, and I am poor as blood — from my estates I have nothing — there my poor little worms have scarce enough to live on; and to my resolution I remain faithful, in this cursed [uncertain: Raat] to take nothing from the King nor from anyone whatever, or to beg for it. Deign also on that account to write very soon; I mean still to await the answer. Can you obtain the Gold there in that way, then I am there quickly — I cede, as aforesaid, the mortgage of 250 Rthl. The money will then be [uncertain: angewiesen — ordered/assigned] here, or and paid; must receipt at once, and the new credit procured; every [uncertain: Weisern] has a clean, secure mortgage, and so much must remain over that the [uncertain: Weise] costs be paid. — perhaps also more, if you receive more than 150/3 Rthl, so that your treasury too may be propped up. Now I cannot go on for weakness — but eternally Your Serene Highness's Berlin, the 7th of April 1808. in most faithful [uncertain: Verehrung — veneration] and most humble and obedient servant v. Triebenfeld. The old [uncertain: Fenig] s — should be as healthy as she alone wished to live.
