--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that the Invalids' Fund has at last released the Maritime Trading Company bonds, enabling payment to the banker, with a further hundred thousand to follow once the Chamber has reviewed the assessments; brokers take 6 per cent. Gerard has been paid 900 Rthl interest against a promise not to call in the capital, Seebald's deal collapsed but saved 1,800 Rthl brokerage, and Eysenhard is next. Urges expelling the Olesnica men, who have protested against dividing the woods over their firewood rights.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

It is boundlessly bad how all business is now made difficult here. Only today does the Invalids' Fund release the Maritime Trading Company bonds, after I first had to pledge my poor soul besides, and only then am I in a position to satisfy the bank[uncertain: Livi], who was so good as to give the order to pay remitted to Your Serene Highness a few days earlier than he had the papers from the Invalids' Fund. The hundred thousand [uncertain: Studen] are to follow as soon as the assessments had.

have been reviewed by the Chamber. I have settled with my friends that they are to keep watch on the monies so that no one else snatches them away, and if indeed someone should procure a cabinet order, then one had better risk the interest for a quarter of a year.

The men of business take 6 percent. It is much, yet in these hard-pressed times very little, the more so as the capital at 5 per cent can stand for half an eternity.

With Gerard I am agreed; I have paid him 900 Rthl interest and given him a written undertaking that I stand surety for the due payment of the interest. In return he must give me a written undertaking back

that the capital may stand a few years longer without being called in. With Seebald nothing came of it, for there 1800 Rthl brokerage were to be paid out again at once; these are now saved. With Eysenhard I shall bring the matter into order tomorrow, and without the meddling of other people arrange everything for the best. On the 5th I then think certainly to depart from here; I take my route by way of Kontop and Glogau, where on account of my being pressed about hither and thither I shall find much lent out and vexation enough. For I have missed 2 court terms and shall no doubt be [uncertain: contumacious].

The Olesnica fellows must be thrown out, for the fellows have had protests entered against the estate and protest against the dismemberment of the woods because they were to have free firewood. Although this is easy enough to attend to, since they are assigned only to a district of 330 Morgen, they are nevertheless a [uncertain: hateful] brood which one must get off one's neck, because I had constantly to fear fresh quarrels – the rogues themselves have not given a farthing; rather the subsequent purchasers there have paid the 10000 Rthl, and these people undertake to pay the other 10000 Rthl forthwith, and to render the interest annually.

In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Berlin, 2 February

1805

faithful and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld
