--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Acknowledges receipt of the Prince's letter of the 11th with dispatches for M. v. Haugwitz, Faudel and Glenck, but complains that the power of attorney for Court Fiscal Pochammer and the blank forms are missing, leaving him idle for weeks in Berlin; begs for these and for judicially attested blank powers, the stated sum needing amendment. Reports writing to Cabinet Councillor Beyme and Minister v. Voss, and planned calls on Haugwitz and Count von Keller. Denounces Gratsenauer's demands for money, a title and a Justice Councillorship at Breslau.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! At this very moment I receive Your Most Serene Highness's most highly esteemed letter of the 11th of this month. It contains the dispatches to Herr M. v. Haugwitz, to Herr Faudel, together with complaint and countercomplaint [uncertain: Glagte und Beclage], a letter to Herr Glenck, a letter to myself on 1½ sheets, and a postscript in Your Most Serene Highness's own hand. The power of attorney for Herr Court Fiscal Pochammer and the blank forms — both of which items I need above all else — have been forgotten to be enclosed. I have already been here several weeks and can do nothing, because it is precisely these pieces that are wanting. To the King I am not to write in Your Serene Highness's name; these blank forms all still lie there, but smaller ones, to write to men who absolutely must be brought to act, I have no more of. May Your Serene Highness therefore deign to let me see, at the very soonest, the power of attorney for Herr Doct. Pochammer and the necessary blank forms, and also a few judicially attested powers of attorney in blank, so that I may have them filled out as they may be required. To the Privy Cabinet Councillor Beyme and to the Minister v. Voss I have written in Your Serene Highness's name upon the last two blank forms, recommending the Court Fiscal Pochammer as plenipotentiary, so that he may at least to some degree be

effective. Both copies I shall send with the next post, so that Your Serene Highness may gather from them the steps I am taking in every direction and how the matter is being conducted. Tomorrow morning, or today — for it is quite [text lost] o'clock in the night as I receive the express courier — I shall hasten to Herr v. [uncertain: Tandel / Faudel], shall also meet with Count v. Haugwitz, and shall afterwards speak with Herr Count von Keller, who is lodging here at the Golden Eagle and who is now to take over the portefeuille when Herr v. Haugwitz, as Herr von Hardenberg has already done, goes away on leave for several months. What I hear here and there I shall report by the first post. Only may Your Serene Highness deign to send me speedily the blank forms, the power of attorney for Herr Pochammer, and especially a few judicially attested powers of attorney in blank, so that in the latter case I may fill out another power of attorney for Herr Pochammer. For the [uncertain: Aufgabet] ought not to stand as it is — I had forgotten in my haste to strike it out — and it might also easily be that one would not wish to give so much money; in that case a lesser sum would have to be inserted.

The very great distance from Your Serene Highness makes the conduct of business harder, and when on top of that the power of attorney and other papers are wanting, then everything comes to a standstill. It is more than sad for me that I have not a single man about me who means truly and honestly by Your Serene Highness; all wish only to enrich themselves, they reason about everything, know nothing at bottom, and do absolutely nothing at all. The lazy [uncertain: Amel] here writes not a syllable, often leads me astray by his lies, and in the end he is the very first to stretch out his hand. Gratsenauer is truly out of his senses; I can use him for nothing more — now he cries out for money, now he wants a title, now a certificate that he really is a Hohenlohe-Ohringen official and does not belong here at all. If he is to work, then he is sick; if he is to travel and conduct a lawsuit here or there, then he raves. In short, the fellow is not to be used. Money I have given him, for he has his salary drawn a year and a half in advance — but titles and false certificates I shall never grant him. I have referred him, being somewhat nervous, to rest.

Now I hear he means to petition Your Serene Highness — to appoint him Justice Councillor; whereupon he will form a Justice Collegium and at Breslau make himself the Princely Hohenlohe Justice President, and he also wishes to be assigned upon the Silesian estates for his salary. Should he really come before Your Serene Highness with such proposals, may You deign only to tell him briefly that in the matter of his salary, if he believes he has anything to demand, he should apply to the Councillor of War and Forests v. Triebenfeld, who would duly instruct the South Prussian treasury to pay everyone who has anything to demand. But titles You do not give [uncertain: any] of Your officials, unless it be that one or another had rendered himself particularly deserving toward You. For the rest, however, You [uncertain: vermischte — did/do not mix] the South Prussian affairs either with the Silesian ones, still less with the Hohenlohe ones; and whoever is not content with these arrangements and with the course of business once introduced, him You would have to leave to seek a situation more agreeable to his wishes. I will answer for it that, if Your Serene Highness will speak in such a tone, every man will do his duty. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Serene Highness's Berlin, the 16th of May, in the morning at [uncertain: 3] o'clock, 1806 most faithful and obedient servant v Triebenfeld
