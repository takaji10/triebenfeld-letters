--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Complains that the Prince's dispatch of the 11th omitted the two things needed most: a power of attorney for the court fiscal Pochammer and blank letter forms, leaving Triebenfeld idle in Berlin for weeks; asks urgently for these plus judicially attested powers of attorney in blank. Reports writing in the Prince's name to Privy Cabinet Councillor Beyme and Minister von Voss, and planned calls on von Haugwitz and Count von Keller. Urges the Prince to refuse Gratsenauer's demands for a title, a certificate and a judicial appointment.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

This very moment I receive Your Most Serene Highness's most honoured letter of the 11th of this month.

It contains the dispatches to the Mr. von Haugwitz, to Herr Faudel, together with [uncertain: Glagte] and [uncertain: Beclage] a letter to Mr. Glenck, a letter to myself of 1½ sheets, and a postscript in Your Most Serene Highness's own hand. The power of attorney for the court fiscal Pochammer and the blank forms, both of which things I need above all else, have been forgotten and not enclosed - I have already been here several weeks and can do nothing, because only these pieces are wanting. I am not to write to the King under Your Serene Highness's name; these blank forms all still lie there, but smaller ones, to write to men who absolutely must have effect, I have no more of - May Your Serene Highness therefore deign to let me have at the very soonest the power of attorney for Herr Doctor Pochammer and the necessary blank forms, also a few judicially attested powers of attorney in blank, so that I may have them filled out as they are required. To the Privy Cabinet Councillor Beyme and to the Minister

von Voss I have written in Your Serene Highness's name on the last two blank forms, and recommended the court fiscal Pochammer as authorised representative, so that he may to some degree be

effective. Both copies I shall send with the next post, so that Your Serene Highness may gather from them the steps which I am taking in every direction and how the matter is being conducted.

Tomorrow morning, or today, for it is a full hour into the night as I receive the express courier, I hasten to Mr. von Tandel, and shall also see the Count von Haugwitz, and speak afterwards with the Count von Keller, who is lodging here at the Golden Eagle and who is now to take over the portfolio when Mr. von Haugwitz, as Mr. von Hardenberg has already done, goes on leave for several months. What I hear here and there I shall report by the first post. Only may Your Serene Highness deign to send me speedily the blank forms, the power of attorney for Mr. Pochammer, and especially a couple of judicially attested powers of attorney in blank, so that in the latter case I may fill out another power of attorney for Mr. Pochammer.

for the [uncertain: sum stated] was not to stand thus - I had forgotten in my haste to strike it out - also it could easily be that they would not wish to give so much money, in which case a lesser sum would have to be inserted

The very great distance from Your Serene Highness makes the course of business difficult, and when on top of that the power of attorney and other papers are lacking, then everything comes to a standstill. It is more than sad for me that I have not a single man who means faithfully and honestly by Your [uncertain: Highness]; all of them wish only to enrich themselves, they hold forth about everything, know at bottom nothing, and do absolutely nothing at all. The lazy [uncertain: Amel] here writes not a syllable, often leads me astray by his lies, and in the end he is the first to stretch out his hand.

Gratsenauer is truly mad; I can use him for nothing more - now he cries out for money - now he wants a title, now a certificate that he really is an official of Hohenlohe-Öhringen and does not belong here at all. If he is to work, then he is ill; if he is to travel and conduct a lawsuit here or there, then he raves - in short, the fellow is not to be used. Money I have given him, for he has his salary drawn a year and a half in advance - but titles and false certificates I shall never grant him. I have referred him, somewhat nervous, to keep quiet.

Now I hear he intends to petition Your Serene Highness - to appoint him councillor of justice; whereupon he means to form a court of justice and in Breslau to play the princely Hohenlohe President of Justice; he also wishes, as regards his salary, to be assigned upon the Silesian estates. Should he really come to Your Serene Highness with such proposals, may You deign only to tell him shortly,

that as regards his salary, if he believes he has anything to demand, he should apply to the councillor of war von Triebenfeld, who would duly instruct the South Prussian treasury to pay everyone who has anything to demand. But titles You give to none of Your officials, unless it be that one or another had made himself especially deserving towards You - For the rest, however, You do not mix the South Prussian affairs either with the Silesian, still less with the Hohenlohe ones, and whoever is not content with these arrangements and with the course of business once introduced, him You would have to leave to seek a situation suited to his wishes.

I vouch for it that, if Your Serene Highness will speak in such a tone, everyone will do his duty. In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's           Berlin, the 16th of May, in the morning at [uncertain: 3] o'clock 1806

most faithful and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld
