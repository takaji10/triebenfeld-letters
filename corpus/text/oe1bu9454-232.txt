--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports grave illness, an empty treasury and universal sequestration at Kopojno: no doctor can be paid, and of 4 Rthl received that day all went to Mr Sommer, so the ordered 300 Rthl cannot be found until Brzechsta is active. Proposes appeals to the King of Saxony and Attorney General Lipski against the subjects' chicaneries, notes Count Kwilecki's move against the Seymanous, and asks urgently for a declaration authorising him to raise 4219 Rthl (principal 3125 Rthl with interest and costs) from the interest of Swięcia, Wrąbczyn, Old Olesnica, Skokum, Sophienthal, Kopojno and Adelow.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord,

That Your Serene Highness has sent someone here who has satisfied himself of the situation here and of my misery, is well. — For many weeks now I have lain very ill; only [uncertain: the same] already an [uncertain: Exquilete] — the daily eyes wear me out; not a Groschen of money is in the treasury; I cannot engage a doctor for myself and must yet perish in my sins (Hohenlohe[?]: "Indeed so!"). Whether the leasing-out will still stand, God knows; one exhausts oneself in chicanery, and with [uncertain: abſabet] today to lease out the propination rights separately, in order to obtain old arrears from 1808 which the sequestrator caused with 2000 d. Brzechsta has declared that he would pay on St. John's, but goes on [uncertain: in Catalirt]. I therefore write to those persons, as soon as the Justizrath Kuntzel comes, in the name of Your Serene Highness, to the King of Saxony, and beg him that he might [uncertain: to the General Procurator Lipski upon war] to come hither to

now to fix and establish a norm between the Dominium and the subjects, and to draw the chicaneries into a fiscal investigation. Count Kwilecki, who was with me here just now, is [uncertain: collecting] [uncertain: Stiennen] in order to press for the removal of the [uncertain: Seymanous], who is our great friend; this may drag on 4 weeks, and in the meantime I shall still have to suffer [uncertain: much reading]. The [uncertain: manorial] treasury has now [proceeded to] execution upon the arrears of interest, but the Procurator will have the friendliness to command that all the remainders, as I direct them, be accepted as day-money, levied by force, and the interest owing discharged. If that succeeds, then I come out clear here, and there remain at the most 4000 Rthl over, if the contract with Brzechsta holds. My and Brzechsta's plan is, once I have been [uncertain: recovered], to go to Warsaw. To pursue the Trąbczyner matter, likewise the Wrąbczyner and others; from there we [uncertain: turn] back and come to Schlawenschitz; in the meantime the lawsuit with Mielrowitz[?] will also be (Hohenlohe: "as promised?")

decided; if it turns out favourably, of which there is no doubt, then Your Serene Highness will obtain the 4000 Rthl, and through the [uncertain: Schläfen] it will also be paid. Your Serene Highness writes to make available to Herr Sommer the 300 Rthl. That is not possible until Brzechsta is in activity — for already for many days there has been no [uncertain: Denen] in the treasury; today 4 Rthl came in, and these Herr Sommer has received. Everything here is at present laid under sequestrations. I do not know from whence I am to take [uncertain: a Groschen] to live upon and for the costs. Now the Procurator Goſcipischen sends word through Chomanowskii that I should press for the payment of the interest which at that time the [uncertain: manorial] treasury caused to be taken from me by arrest and French execution [in] Berlin. For since I had a decree of the French General Government, and the Invalids' Fund was here seeking its satisfaction, this claim had the precedence — only Your Serene Highness would have to give a declaration that the Councillor of War and Forests v. Triebenfeld must demand and receive the principal sum of 3125 Rthl, which with the [uncertain: D.] plain interest and costs amounts to 4219 Rthl; wherein I not only fully [consent] hereby, but I hereby direct him to raise these 4219 Rthl out of the interest

of the villages of New and Old Swięcia, of the village of Wrąbczyn, of the village of Old Olesnica, Skokum, the colony Sophienthal, Kopoyno, and the colony Adelow, and to have them paid against receipt; also I consent that Herr v. Triebenfeld may have this [uncertain: sum] levied by execution through a komornik, without its first having to be sought by judicial assistance. This, issued [uncertain: by the courts of Your Serene Highness], I beg may be sent to me by a [uncertain: returning] express with the utmost dispatch, so that I may keep my foot in the [uncertain: Carruen] and be able to have some money levied. For the rest, there is here a distress that surpasses all conception; the [uncertain: through-marches] have consumed everything — grain, cattle, horses, sheep, and [uncertain: also] fodder taken, so that the people eat grass together with the cattle. As concerns the [uncertain: Brieges Precz?] most graciously sent to me, it does not hold, for this sum is determined and assigned according to the [uncertain: Kontonper] contract, and undertaken to be paid to him.

In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's

Kopoyno, 16th June 1811

most humble and obedient [servant], v Triebenfeld
