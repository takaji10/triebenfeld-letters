--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports grave illness, an empty treasury and universal sequestration at Kopojno: no doctor can be paid, and of 4 Rthl received that day all went to Mr Sommer, so the ordered 300 Rthl cannot be found until Brzechsta is active. Proposes appeals to the King of Saxony and Attorney General Lipski against the subjects' chicaneries, notes Count Kwilecki's move against the Seymanous, and asks urgently for a declaration authorising him to raise 4219 Rthl (principal 3125 Rthl with interest and costs) from the interest of Swięcia, Wrąbczyn, Old Olesnica, Skokum, Sophienthal, Kopojno and Adelow.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord, that Your Serene Highness has sent someone here who has satisfied himself of the situation here and of my misery is good – I have lain very ill for many weeks, [uncertain: only the same already an Exquilete] – the daily eyes wear me down, not a Groschen of money is in the treasury, I cannot engage a doctor for myself and must perish in my sins (Hohenlohe[?]: "Indeed so!") yet! Whether the lease will still hold, God will [know]; one exhausts oneself in chicaneries, and with [illegible] today lease out the propination rights separately, in order to obtain the old arrears from 1808 which the sequestrator has caused to the amount of 2000

d. Brzechsta has declared that he would pay on St. John's, but goes on [uncertain: cataloguing]. I am therefore writing to those persons, as soon as the councillor of justice Kuntzel comes, in the name of Your Serene Highness, to the King of Saxony, and beg him that he might [direct] the Attorney General Lipski upon the war[?] to come here to

now to establish and set a norm between the manorial lordship (Dominium) and the subjects, and to draw the chicaneries into a fiscal investigation. Count Kwilecki, who was just now here with me, is gathering [uncertain: Stiennen] in order to press for the removal of the Seymanous, who is our great friend; this may take 4 weeks of dragging out, and in the meantime I shall still have to suffer much [uncertain: privation].

The [uncertain: lordly] treasury now has execution against the outstanding [uncertain: interest]; but the Attorney will have the friendship to order that all the remainders, as I direct them, be taken as day-money, collected by force, and the interest owing be discharged. If that succeeds, then I come clear here, and at most 4000 Rthl remain over, if the contract with Brzechsta holds. My own and Brzechsta's plan is, when I have been [there], to go to Warsaw. To pursue the Trąbczyn matter, likewise the Wrąbczyn one and the others; from there we return and come to Schlawenschitz, and in the meantime the lawsuit with Mielrowitz[?] will also (Hohenlohe: "as promised?")

be decided; if it turns out favourably, of which there is no doubt, then Your Serene Highness will receive the 4000 Rthl, and payment will also be made through the [uncertain: Schläfen].

Your Serene Highness writes that the 300 Rthl are to be found for Mr. Sommer. That is not possible until Brzechsta is in activity – for these many days there has been no [uncertain: Denen] in the treasury – today 4 Rthl came in, and these Mr. Sommer has received. Everything here is at present laid under sequestrations. I do not know whence I am to take anything to live on and for the costs. Now the Attorney Goscipischen sends word through Chomanowskii that I should press for payment of the interest which at that time the [uncertain: lordly] treasury caused to be taken from me by arrest and French execution in Berlin. For since I hold a decree of the French General Government, and the Invalids' Fund here seeks its satisfaction, this claim had priority – only Your Serene Highness would have to give a declaration that the Councillor of War and Forests v. Triebenfeld must demand and receive the [uncertain: quaet] sum of 3125 Rthl, which with the interest and costs proper amounts to 4219 Rthl; to which I not only hereby fully consent, but I hereby direct him to raise these 4219 Rthl out of the interest

of the villages New and Old Swięcia, of the village of Wrąbczyn, of the village of Old Olesnica, Skokum, the colony Sophienthal, Kopojno, and the colony Adelow, and to have them paid against receipt; I also consent that Mr. v. Triebenfeld may have this money collected by execution through a komornik, without its first having to be sought by judicial assistance.

This, drawn up from here by Your Serene Highness's courts, I beg may be sent to me with the utmost dispatch by a [uncertain: rentencken] express, so that I may keep my foot in the [uncertain: Carruen] and may have some money collected. For the rest, there is here a distress that surpasses all conception; the [uncertain: warsche] have consumed everything, have taken grain, cattle, horses, sheep and [illegible] fodder, so that the people eat grass alongside the cattle.

As concerns the [uncertain: Brieges Precz] most graciously sent to me, it does not hold, for this sum is, according to the Kontop contract, determined, assigned, and undertaken to be paid to him.

In deepest respect I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Kopojno

16th June 1811

most humble and obedient [servant]

v. Triebenfeld
