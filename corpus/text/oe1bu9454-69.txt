--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Bally reports, after Lombardini's earlier word, that the money could not be collected at once in Breslau and that dreadful roads and weather delayed him; even so he reached Kontop by midday on the 25th, still in time to find Honrichs and Cabanis, who left the same day once Honrichs had 6,000 f. and the original deeds of donation. Councillor of War von Triebenfeld, his right arm badly affected, promises some weeks at Schlawentz once his own business is done. Bally awaits instructions before returning.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Your Most Serene Highness will already have learnt through Mr.

Mr. Lombardini that I was not able to receive the money at once in Breslau, and that I was moreover detained even without the great hindrances of the indescribable roads and weather. Notwithstanding this, however, I so hastened my journey from Breslau that on the 25th at noon I was here and found Honrichs and Cabanis

still there, who then also, after Honrichs [had received] 6000 f. and the deeds of donation in the original, with the most joyful prospects, departed that same day. Mr. Councillor of War von Triebenfeld is suffering greatly in the right arm; yet he has promised me that as soon as he shall have concluded his own business here, he will come at once for some weeks to Schlawentz. reply After all the most graciously given instructions concerning this shall have been received, I shall hasten to return. In deepest submission I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's Kontop

the 26th of February 1810.

most humbly, faithfully and obediently

Bally
