--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Complains that only Triebenfeld's dispatch of 29 April has reached him since Schlawenschitz, an earlier letter having been lost when the post-house at Würtemberg burned, and that Trzciński, Zerboni and Koenig have paid nothing while Weigel presses hard; he hopes to pacify Weigel with the Trzciński bills. Encloses an open letter to the Russian Emperor for use as thought fit, reports that the King, State Chancellor and Minister of Justice refused to support his proposed compromise with creditors at Brieg and a maintenance allowance, and asks for instruction, hoping for something from Maierowitz and Ratke.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Right Honourable Sir,

Highly Honoured Sir, Councillor of War and Forests.

Your messenger arrived only this forenoon; he excuses himself for having been so long on the road by saying that he had gone astray. From his march route I see that he was also with the chief district administrator Koenig, [uncertain: where] he says that the latter was at home and took the letters from him in person. Neither Trzciński, Zerboni, nor Koenig[illegible] lets anything be heard of himself; the first has still paid nothing, the second has likewise as yet fulfilled none of his obligations. Herr Weigel presses me very hard; I shall now try whether I can pacify him by means of the Trzciński bills of exchange. These monies from Großen I needed for true necessity, for unhappily everything here still stands as you know it. Your complete silence has filled me with sorrow, since I conclude from it that all your efforts on my behalf have been fruitless. I sent Your Right Honourable a messenger, who however brought back only a certificate of receipt from the district administrator at Blizanow, which Herr Schenck — after he had detained this express four days — expanded to the effect that you had travelled away, would return in fourteen days, and that I should then soon receive an answer; this, to my great regret and real disadvantage, did not follow. Only today do I see from Your Right Honourable's dispatch of the 29th of April that you had written to me earlier, which lines I did not receive. I can assure you upon my word that the letter I here answer is the first I have received from you since you left Schlawenschitz; in vain, alas, have I long yearned for it. I

I am glad to see from it that I am not wholly [illegible: effaced] from your [text lost] — I will believe that the letter, the loss of which the negligence of Herr Koenig has brought upon me, may at least have contained instructive disclosures for me; for that very reason it was inexcusable that he put it in the post at Würtemberg. What has become of the letter I can only guess, particularly as you did not indicate the date to me. From the newspapers I see that in the fire at Würtemberg the post-house was burned down as well, and many letters lying there became a prey to the flames. — The conclusion of all this is that I know nothing of what you wished me to do; meanwhile I enclose herewith a letter to His Majesty the Russian Emperor under open seal, so that, if you find it suitable, the necessary use may be made of it. In any case I have done what I was able to do, and I beg Your Right Honourable for further instruction; I have hitherto omitted nothing in order to prove, with all resignation, my patriotism and good will. What I wrote, and how I was answered, the enclosure shows. — To the King, to the Lord State Chancellor and to the Minister of Justice I applied myself anew in my pressing concern, that they might vigorously lend their weight at Brieg to the compromise settlement I have proposed with the creditors, and to favour the fixing of a

of a fair maintenance allowance for myself; this intercession also was refused me. Otherwise they keep silent about everything, and it seems they wish in the end to abandon me to death by hunger. You already know all of this, wherefore I find it useless to say anything further of it. My situation is dreadful and the conduct towards me cruel. General von Roeder represented this to Herr von Hardenberg while still at Breslau, but I too remained uninformed as to the result. — I have implored anew the protection of the two monarchs in certain cases, so as not to let the reproach of a neglect be charged to me, and I entrusted my letter to the King to the State Chancellor, and that to the Emperor to Herr Minister von Stein, for delivery; this too I did not wish to withhold from you, but I beg that the most cautious use only be made of this disclosure. [uncertain: Release] me, Your Right Honourable, [uncertain: without] early news of the arrival of this letter, and instruct me as to the course of your negotiations. I could not comply with your wishes more quickly, since the letter goes off tomorrow morning by the mounted post and will probably arrive in Dresden on the 11th of this month. As I hear today, the two monarchs had already departed for Altenburg; in no case, however, can you be so far distant from them that you could not personally open the negotiations

The distinction conferred on your eldest son has given me great pleasure, and I take part in his sufferings from the wound. By your personal visit you act as an excellent father. Which I esteem. — Do report to me soon when you return, and where we can most conveniently speak together; I still hope that something will soon be obtainable from Maierowitz, since help is all too needful to me. It appears, to my comfort, that you still expect some benefit from Ratke's handling of the matter; have you then heard anything more particular from him in the meantime? If Your Right Honourable then not at all during the presence of the high personages at Kalisz. For your friendly support I beg with confidence. With wishes for [uncertain: thunderous] health and a happy journey, I remain in especial esteem

Your Right Honourable's

Schlawenschitz

9th May 1813

most faithfully devoted friend and servant

F. L. P. of Hohenlohe
