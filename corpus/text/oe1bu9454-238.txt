--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Complains of Triebenfeld's long silence and of lost correspondence — one letter posted by Koenige at Würtemberg probably burned with the post-house — and of the delays caused by Schenck detaining an express four days. Trzcinski and Zerboni have paid nothing, while Weigel presses hard; the Großen monies went on necessities. Encloses an open letter to the Russian Emperor for use as Triebenfeld sees fit, reports refusals from the King, State Chancellor and Minister of Justice over the creditors' composition at Brieg and a maintenance allowance, and hopes for money from Maierowitz and Ratke.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Right Honourable Sir, Highly Honoured Sir Councillor of War and Forests. Your messenger arrived only this forenoon; he excuses himself for having been so long on the road by saying that he had lost his way. From his march route I see that he was also at the Chief Bailiff Koenig's, where he said that the latter was at home and had taken the letters from him himself. Neither Trzcinski, Zerboni, nor Koenig[?] lets anything be heard from him; the first has as yet paid nothing, the second has likewise fulfilled none of his obligations, Herr Weigel presses me exceedingly, I shall now attempt whether I can quiet him by means of the Trzcinski bills of exchange. These monies from Großen I needed for the veriest necessities, for unhappily everything here still stands as you know it. Your entire silence has filled me with sorrow, since I conclude therefrom that all your endeavours on my behalf were fruitless. I sent Your Right Honourable a messenger, who however brought back only a receipt from the bailiff at Blizanow, which Herr Schenck — after he had detained this express four days — enlarged to the effect that you were gone away on a journey, would return in a fortnight, and that I should then soon receive an answer; this, to my great regret and real detriment, did not follow. Only today do I see from Your Right Honourable's dispatch of the 29th of April that you had written to me earlier, which lines I did not receive. I can assure you upon my word that the letter of yours which I answer here is the first that I have received from you since you left Schlawenzitz; in vain, alas, have I long yearned for it. I

I rejoice to see therefrom that I am not wholly [uncertain: effaced] from your remembrance — I am willing to believe that the letter, the loss of which the negligence of Herr Koenige brought upon me, may at least have contained instructive disclosures for me; on that ground alone it was inexcusable that he put it into the post at Würtemberg. What has become of the letter I cannot guess, especially as you did not indicate the date to me; from the newspapers I perceive that in the fire at Würtemberg the post-house burned down with the rest, and many letters lying there became a prey to the flames. — The upshot of it is that I now know nothing of what you wished me to do; meanwhile I enclose herewith a letter to His Majesty the Russian Emperor under open seal, so that, if you find it suitable thereto, the necessary use may be made of it. In any case I have done what I was able, and beg for Your Right Honourable's further instruction; I have hitherto omitted nothing to prove, with all resignation, my patriotism and good will. What I wrote, and how I was answered, the enclosure shows. — To the King, to the Herr State Chancellor and to the Minister of Justice I applied myself anew in my pressing concern, that they might vigorously lend their weight at Brieg to the composition-agreement proposed by me with the creditors, and might favour the fixing of a

of a reasonable maintenance allowance for me; this intercession too was refused me, otherwise one keeps silence about everything, and it appears that in the end one wishes to abandon me to death by starvation. You already know all this, wherefore I find it useless to say anything further of it. My situation is dreadful and the conduct towards me cruel. General v. Roeder laid this before Herr v. Hardenberg while still at Breslau, but I remained likewise uninformed as to the result. — I have implored the protection of the two Monarchs anew in certain cases, so as not to let the reproach of a neglect be charged to me, and I entrusted my letter to the King to the State Chancellor, and that to the Emperor to Herr Minister v. Stein, for delivery; this too I did not wish to withhold from you, but I beg that of this disclosure only the most cautious use be made. [uncertain: Let] me, Your Right Honourable, [text lost] without early news of the arrival of this letter, and instruct me concerning the course of your negotiations. I could not comply with your wishes more speedily, since the letter goes off tomorrow morning by the riding-post and will probably arrive on the 11th of this month at Dreßden. As I hear today, the two Monarchs had already departed for Altenburg; in no case, however, can you be so far removed from them that you could not personally open the

negotiations. The distinction conferred on your eldest son has greatly rejoiced me, and I take part in his sufferings from the wound. In making your personal visit you act as an excellent father. Which I esteem. — Do let me know soon when you return, and where we may most conveniently speak together. I hope after all that something will soon be obtainable from Maierowitz, since help is all too sorely needed by me. It appears, to my consolation, that you still expect advantage from Ratke's working of the matter; have you then heard anything more particular from him about it of late? If Your Right Honourable then not at all during the presence of the exalted personages at Kolisch. For your friendly assistance I beg with confidence. With wishes for [uncertain: thunderous] health and a prosperous journey, I remain, in especial high esteem, Your Right Honourable's Schlawenzitz, 9th May 1813, most faithfully devoted friend and servant F. L. F. zu Hohenlohe
