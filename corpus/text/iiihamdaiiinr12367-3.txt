Mon cher Schmidt, voici votre Dubois ; je rougis presque de Vous dire avec quel intérêt j'ai lu toutes ces scélératesses, cela ne m'empêche pas cependant de Vous en demander la continuation avec instance. — Je ne manquerai pas de pousser l'affaire Weigel & soyez tranquille sur le reste je Vous promets que nous nous en tirerons avec honneur. J'ai ici un tems affreux qui glace parfois mes passions champêtres.
L. B. d. Mohrenheim
Mardi.
Pourriez Vous m'accorder pour 2 j. le [Courier?] de [Londres?] ?

Undatiertes Billett Mohrenheims an Schmidt, an einem Dienstag geschrieben. Er schickt ihm dessen „Dubois“ zurück, den er mit großem Interesse gelesen habe, und bittet dringend um die Fortsetzung. --- SUMMARY (English) ---
An undated note from Mohrenheim to Schmidt, written on a Tuesday. He returns Schmidt's “Dubois”, which he says he read with great interest, and asks pressingly for the continuation. He will push the Weigel matter; Schmidt should be easy about the rest, they will come out of it with honour. He has dreadful weather where he is, which at times dampens his pleasure in the country. In a line written sideways in the margin he asks Schmidt to let him have something for two days; what it is has not been securely read, perhaps the London newspaper The Courier.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
My dear Schmidt, here is your Dubois; I almost blush to tell you with what interest I have read all these villainies, which does not, however, prevent me from asking you urgently for the continuation. — I shall not fail to push the Weigel affair, & be easy about the rest: I promise you that we shall come out of it with honour. I have frightful weather here, which at times chills my rustic passions.

L. B. d. Mohrenheim

Tuesday.

Could you let me have for 2 days the [uncertain: Courier] of [uncertain: London]?
