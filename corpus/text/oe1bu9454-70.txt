--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that Hecker, whom he had meant to dismiss, must stay owing to a misunderstanding with Ober-Präfect Garczynski, who wished to hand everything to Heiller; Hecker has pledged payment by St John's, but will be summoned to Costen with Honrichs, bound before the court, and watched by Cabanis as controller. In the Trąbczin suit Kramplitz failed to appear, a new term set for 2 April; Raczkowski, Künzel and Honrichs are acting. Hopes to free Zagorowo and recover Traperin, defends staying away from Schlawenschitz, plans six days in Berlin, notes 600 Rthl spent on Hungarian wine for Garczynski's cellar, and complains of the Landschaft's 4000 ℔ sequestration costs.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord!

From the accompanying letter of the said Hecker Your Most Serene Highness will most graciously deign to satisfy yourself that God often leads His own in wondrous ways. — I wished to let Hecker go, but through a misunderstanding of the Ober-Präfect Garczynski I must now let him stay on [uncertain: sezzen / "sit"] until a milder wind passes over our fields. Garczynski believed he had to hand over everything to Heiller, and with that in view had him summoned. So as to hinder nothing, I let the matter proceed; Hecker, out of [uncertain: demmen] zeal, has pledged himself body and soul. He vows to have paid everything by St. John's. That I shall accept most gladly. But in order to go entirely securely, I have summoned him, together with Honrichs, to Costen, where I shall speak with him myself and once more have everything stipulated by him before the court. His — the Wecker's — security I shall assign [Anweisung] to Garczynski, so that one has the fellow properly in hand; meanwhile I shall not only not let him have sole control over the cash-box, in spite of the paper security, but shall, through [uncertain: Parczynchen / Garczynski], place Cabanis (who is a thoroughly honest man) at his side as controller; thus [uncertain: Parcynchi / Garczynski] will make good everything again, and if Hecker blunders in, he may not cry out against Your Serene Highness or against me. In this way the matter stands wonderfully well.

As regards Trąbczin we already stand at a great advantage, for the knavish Kramplitz, as the enclosure shows, did not appear. The 2nd of April has been set as a new term. — Kirchli remains a further 2 months. — Hence nothing is lost. Raczkowski and Künzel, together with Honrichs, are conducting the matter — everything is [uncertain: jagt] disposed in favour of Your Serene Highness's interest. These estates will assuredly be vindicated, and if we have Zagorowo free again, and Traperin back, then it cannot fail; Placzkowzin's and Künzel's letters I enclose, but I beg to have everything sent back to me quickly. That I do not come to Schlawenschitz — over this Your Serene Highness must not [illegible: "reinen"]. It is for your own best interest that I sit here, vex myself, and go hungry. — I mean truly well by you, most gracious Lord. I am saving everything for you, and by God I shall procure you still more than you think; but do not be mistaken, and do not construe my actions wrongly. The moment is not far off when we shall be able to laugh our enemies to scorn. — From Berlin there is little to be hoped, and therefore we must not be over-hasty; compliments are out of place. When I have spoken with Rector and Honrichs, then, for your sake, I shall go to Berlin for 6 days, before ever I come to Schlawenschitz. — That I know more, most gracious Prince and Lord, you may believe — otherwise I should not travel thither. — My pride is bowed by no god. — The affair concerning Colloma has been begun; I shall bring it to an honourable end, and in Berlin shall already know wherewith I can stop every man's mouth. Afterwards one deals with you [uncertain: zu und ankbar]. I suffer no humiliation, and shall

forgive nothing, either to you or to myself. You are the only one on this earth to whom I have poured out my heart and to whom I have lamented a sorrow. To the King himself I wrote as my feeling demanded. He is indebted to me in a total sum, and for all my sacrifices did not count out even one Groschen — so I could speak freely [uncertain: Leck], and now it shall be done by word of mouth with still greater emphasis. — Rochad means well by Your Serene Highness and by me — but in real terms he does little. — I will endeavour to [uncertain: to become] his feelings, and therefore I shall make the journey in haste. The 600 Rthl which Honrichs took with him have been laid in in 2 feet of old Hungarian wine and have flowed into Garczynski's cellar. — They work well. — My estates have been handed over to the tenants; everything is so ordered that no new debts can be contracted, and the tenants must pay the interest punctually half-yearly on their own responsibility. — But the Landschaft is liquidating, for its 2 years' sequestration, in the course of which it too has delivered up not a Groschen, a further 4000 ℔ in costs. There the surplus rents for a whole year go to nothing, and I must meanwhile live wretchedly with my family. Yet for all that I do see the end of my sufferings.

In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's

Kontop, the 25th of March 1810.

faithful humble and obedient servant
v Triebenfeld
