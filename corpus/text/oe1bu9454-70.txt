--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Hecker is to be kept on after a misunderstanding with Chief Prefect Garczynski, having pledged himself to pay all by Midsummer; he is summoned with Honrichs to Costen to be bound before the court, with Cabanis appointed controller. In the Trąbczyn suit Kramplitz failed to appear and a new hearing is set for 2 April; Raczkowski, Künzel and Honrichs press the Prince's interest. The 600 Rthl sent with Honrichs became two casks of old Hungarian wine for Garczynski's cellar. Promises a six-day journey to Berlin over the Colloma affair, and complains of the Landschaft's 4000 further costs.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord, From the accompanying letter of the said Hecker Your Most Serene Highness will most graciously be pleased to convince yourself that God often leads His own in wondrous ways. – I wished to let Hecker go, but through a misunderstanding of the Chief Prefect Garczynski I must now let him remain seated until a milder wind passes over our fields.

Garczynski believed he had to hand everything over to Heiller and has had him summoned with that in view. So as to hinder nothing I let the matter take its course; Hecker out of [uncertain: that same] zeal has gone surety with body and soul. He vows to have everything paid by Midsummer. That I shall very gladly accept. But in order to go quite securely I have appointed him to come with Honrichs to Costen, where I shall speak with him myself and have everything once more stipulated by him before the court.

His, the Wecker's, security I shall assign to Garczynsken, so that the fellow may be properly held in hand; in the meantime I shall not only let him have the disposal of the treasury despite the paper security, but shall have Cabanis (who is a thoroughly honest man) set at his side by Parczynchen as controller, so Parcynchi will guarantee everything back, and if Hecker blunders in he may not cry out against Your Serene Highness or against me; this matter stands in that way wonderfully well.

As regards Trąbczyn we already stand at a great advantage, for the knavish Kramplitz has, as the enclosure shows, not appeared.

A new hearing has been appointed for the 2nd of April – Kirchli remains a further 2 months — Therefore nothing is lost.

Raczkowski and Künzel are conducting the matter together with Honrichs – everything is [uncertain: eagerly] disposed in favour of Your Serene Highness's interest. These estates will certainly be recovered by suit, and once we have Zagorowo free again, Traperin back, then it cannot fail; Placzkowzin's and Künzel's letters I enclose, but beg that everything be sent back to me quickly.

That I do not come to Schlawenschitz, over that Your Serene Highness must not [uncertain: complain]. It is done for your own best good, that I sit here and vex myself and go hungry — I mean truly well by you, most gracious Lord. I am saving everything for you and shall, by God, procure you still more than you think; but do not [uncertain: mistake] me and do not construe my actions wrongly. The moment is not far off when we shall be able to laugh at our enemies – From Berlin there is little to hope, and for that reason we must not act over-hastily; compliments are of no avail. When I have spoken with Rector and Honrichs, then, for your sake, before I come to Schlawenschitz I shall go to Berlin for 6 days — that I know more, you may believe, most gracious Prince and Lord, – otherwise I should not travel thither – My pride is bowed by no god – The affair concerning Colloma has been begun; I shall bring it to an honourable end, and shall know already in Berlin wherewith I can stop every man's mouth. Afterwards one deals with you and thankfully. I suffer no humiliation, and shall

forgive nothing, either to you or to myself. You are the only one on this earth [uncertain: sore] to whom I poured out my heart and to whom I lamented a suffering. To the King himself I wrote as my feeling required. He owes me a sum total, and did not pay even one Groschen for all my sacrifices — I could therefore speak freely, and now it shall be done by word of mouth with still greater emphasis. – Rochad means well by Your Serene Highness and by me – but in real terms he does little — I shall be at pains to [uncertain: win] his feelings, and for that reason I shall make the journey in haste.

The 600 Rthl which Honrichs took with him have been converted into 2 casks of old Hungarian wine and have flowed into Garczynski's cellar — They work well — My estates have been handed over to the tenants; everything is so arranged that no new debts can be contracted, and that the tenants must pay the interest promptly half-yearly on their own responsibility – But the Landschaft is liquidating, for its 2-year sequestration, in which it likewise remitted not one Groschen, a further 4000 ℔ in costs. There the surplus rents for a whole year go to that too, and I must meanwhile live meagrely with my family. Still, for all that, I see the end of my sufferings.

In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Kontop, the 25th of March

1810.

faithful, humble and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld
