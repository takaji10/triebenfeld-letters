--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Abschrift eines königlichen Rescripts an die Kammern zu Posen und Petrikau: Die Übergabe der dem Prinzen von Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen verliehenen südpreußischen Güter sei bloß oberflächlich erfolgt; Personen mit Pfand- und Pachtverträgen, teils nachweislich gefälschten, seien ohne Prüfung ihres dinglichen Rechts im Besitz gelassen worden. Die Kammern sollen die Ansprüche prüfen, die Güter den fürstlichen Mandatarien zur freien Wirtschaft übergeben, Streitpunkte möglichst gütlich beilegen, nötigenfalls durch die Fiscale klagen, die Competenzgelder für die Geistlichkeit schleunig festsetzen und über jedes Gut einzeln berichten.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A royal rescript to the chambers at Poznań and Petrikau, complaining that the handing over of the South Prussian estates granted to the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen was a superficial ceremony: persons holding pledge or lease contracts, some proven false, have been left in possession without proof of any real right. The chambers are to examine and settle such claims quickly, settle disputes amicably or through the fiscal officers, put the Prince's agents in free possession, and fix and report the clergy's competence monies.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Copy.

Friedrich Wilhelm etc. In the taking-over and delivery, commanded of you, of the South Prussian estates of your department granted to His Lordship the Prince of Hohenlohe, it has been Our will and intention that the same should at once, and as far as is in any way possible without prejudice to the rights of a third party, be left to His Lordship's free disposition, and hence also to his husbandry and improvement as he shall please. Notwithstanding this, We come to learn that the delivery carried out has consisted merely in a superficial ceremony, and that the remark made in some places, that His Lordship is to settle all debts charged upon these estates, has confirmed various persons in the false notion that they may seat themselves in the estates and frame all manner of claims. Both in the confiscated noble and in the ecclesiastical estates granted to His Lordship the Prince there are said to be persons seated who have indeed produced pledge or lease contracts, but in respect of which it was not at all thoroughly investigated at the delivery whether these contracts were in themselves validly concluded, and, even were that the case, whether the holders thereof also really have a real claim upon this or that estate, and whether rather mere personal obligations were not contracted. It is said even to be already proven that some of these persons have false pledge contracts, and on that account the suits are said to be already pending before the Governments. But you yourself perceive that if His Lordship the Prince of Hohenlohe must first laboriously and through all instances conduct suits of this kind, Our Most High intention of putting His Lordship soon into the full enjoyment of the granted estates is thereby entirely delayed. Also the Treasury is bound, in pursuance of the deeds of grant, to procure for each donee as far as possible an unencumbered estate (fundum liberum). The delivery commissioners ought therefore also to have

more closely ascertained those claims which have been made upon these estates by outside persons, and to have left in possession of such an estate only those claimants who were able at once to prove a real right in one or the other estate and have exercised such real right by actual possession of the estate, and not to have acted so superficially, and to have conceded to many persons the continuing possession and further use of these estates even when they had by no means laid down undoubted proofs of their right. Now, in order not to burden His Lordship with unnecessary and protracted suits, yet also not to injure anyone in well-acquired rights, you are yourselves to take every pains to examine and settle the various demands upon these estates as soon as may be, to have these estates, so far as can in any way lawfully be done, handed over to the princely mandatories for the sole disposition and free husbandry of the Prince, to compose all disputed points amicably so far as ever possible — the more so as His Lordship himself, with sacrifice of his own interest, shews every inclination thereto — and, in those cases where it absolutely cannot be otherwise arranged without great danger to the Treasury and without legal proceedings, to take the shortest ways, and, according to the circumstances, to have the suit conducted either by the fiscal officers alone, or by them as assistants of the princely mandatory. For the complete settlement you will have to examine most exactly the circumstances of each single estate, and then to set the further matter in train upon the constant presupposition that His Lordship be at once in

stalled in all rights and powers that can in any way be lawfully transferred by the Treasury. Also, since the detailed valuations of all the ecclesiastical estates have already been remitted to you with approbation, you will very easily and soon be able to determine the dues and the competence monies which His Lordship the Prince of Hohenlohe has to pay; and since the discontent of the clergy grows ever greater the longer you delay the fixing and advance payment of these competence monies, you are to ascertain the same with the utmost despatch and to make report thereof to Us. In general you are to report to Us fully upon the settled business of delivery of possession of these estates, and what difficulties have arisen therein, and, according as the estates belong together or are separate, to report upon them severally; wherein We hold Ourselves assured that you will carefully order everything according to this Our will and intention, and will urge the Domains Establishment Commission, the delivery commissioners and the office of the Treasury, each in its own relation, to the speedy fulfilment of what has been ordered. [uncertain: Sinde G.] Wrocław, the 20th October 1797.

A. S. B. signed Count von Hoym. To the p Chamber at Poznań and Petrikau.
