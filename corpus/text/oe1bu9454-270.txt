--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports from the Congress that even Kalisz will fall to Russia, and that recovery of the Traperin looks hopeless: the Emperor is unlikely to overturn the Chief Administration Commission and may compensate the Prince otherwise, while Prince St.K. forbids any démarche. Notes Moszynski's and Count Landskoy's friendship, Wellington's arrival as Ambassadeur Extraordinair, Metternich and Talleyrand's intrigues, talk of restoring the King of Saxony with a 1,200,000 Rthl appanage, and, at home, delays with Stägemann and Cosmar's attachment on the entailed estates.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

If no quite extraordinary events intervene, it is settled that even the city of Kalisz will become Russian - I must now work solely on that side to recover Traperin again. But as yet no one from that quarter has given me any comforting assurance; rather it is said that the Emperor [uncertain: Räyser] would scarcely quash what the Administration Commission had done, and would sooner compensate the Prince in some other way. I am on that account in a desperate situation: the Prince-Chancellor [uncertain: FürstStK.] forbids me to make any démarche whatever on that side, and there I must obey, and yet all the while I fear that everything will be concluded quickly, and that then the gate of grace may be shut - Moszynski, husband of the born Prusimska, and Count Landskoy are bosom friends, so not much good is to be hoped for there. Yesterday the great Wellington arrived here as Ambassador Extraordinary; he is said to look somewhat askance at the cost [uncertain: Kosten], and from that something might be surmised. Meanwhile the harmony between our King and the Emperor continues firmly, whereas Prince Met: and Ernst Tail: are making cabals without end - and on that account there is much talk here of one and of great wars. Today it is said that Dresden and Leipzig together with the whole tract beyond the Elbe are to be taken from us again, and the King of Saxony to be reinstated there - Were that to happen, we should have gained nothing for all our exertion, but rather, since Russia [uncertain: muet] all of Poland, have lost much - It is to be hoped that our acknowledgedly brave King will after all not let this happen.

The Prince-Chancellor is Your Serene Highness's true friend, and will certainly help, as His Excellency Herr von Humboldt assured me only yesterday - if the labours here were not so very numerous, what can be done here would already have been settled - the matter lies, as I reported last, before the Privy Councillor of State Stägemann, but on account of the exorbitant volume of business he cannot get to it. B — who after all sits with him daily, himself excuses him — But the matter will turn out well - In short: once the Lux are satisfied, once Cosmar's attachment is lifted from the entailed estates, and Your Serene Highness is reinstated as actual lord of the entailed estate, then Cosmar has no further share in these estates - here he must at once withdraw, his knaveries will be discovered, and his end will be a sorry one — What chiefly alarmed me, moreover, was the estimate of revenues from the entailed estates appended to the Brieg protocol; that departs frightfully from Your Serene Highness's statement. How could one ask the hamsters whether they had dragged in much or little, and whether there is anything still to be gathered in? I fear this will in the end cause me some grief. At this moment the Privy Councillor and Land [uncertain: Triechses] von Fullenbaum was here [uncertain: bezwieder] assuring me that Wellington too was speaking in favour of the King of Saxony - the King himself, however, they will not have led [uncertain] - a kind of [uncertain: Nanage] of 1200000 Rthl he had granted for the old gentleman, but nothing more – For the rest, the Austrian Emperor too would cede to Russia what he holds of Poland - and would receive in exchange a considerable piece on the Lotharingian [uncertain: Lotterongenſehen] side. The most effective person here at present is the lady whom Your

Serene Highness once destined as consort for Herr August. She is the betrothed and hotly beloved of Prince Met. Since she is now the niece of the Prince [uncertain: Eürz. Failli.], she stands between the two and does great business.

Finally, and to my alarm, I report that the Emperor Franz has ordered the lodgings for the friends here, and likewise the people hired for their service, to be maintained and kept on at his cost for a further 2 months. God stand by us. By the next post I shall be able to report more, and certainly something of importance already.

In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Vienna, the 2nd of February

1815.

most faithful humble and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld
