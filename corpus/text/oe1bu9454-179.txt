--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports progress in Berlin: the dismemberment and the Betsche affair approved by the King, and the Kalisz Chamber's valuation fixing estate revenues at 40,260 Rthl, against debts of 698,000 Rthl. Urges the Prince to execute and return quickly a power of attorney for Court Fiscal Pochammer, who is to secure treasury notes at 3 per cent to clear all debts; encloses schedules and an amortisation plan. Also advises against consenting to Glenck's land sale, promising him 8,000 Rthl, and against approving the Santomist sale.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Your Most Serene Highness's most gracious letters of the 19th and 27th of March I received so late only because they lay a very long while in Słupca. It is more than sorrowful for me that I must live so far removed from Your Serene Highness and have news so seldom. It falls particularly hard upon me to act when Your Serene Highness leaves me so long without any answer whatever. Meanwhile I give myself the honour of reporting to Your Most Serene Highness most obediently how all matters have prospered.

After I had put everything in order on the estates, I betook myself hither again to Berlin, in order to work here and if possible to bring the matter to a conclusion.

The dismemberment, as Your Serene Highness knows, has been approved most honourably, and His Majesty the King himself has vouchsafed me his high approbation. The Betsche affair too has been approved at the Chamber, laid before the King for approbation by General Honrichs's department, and this very day it came in here executed by the King. This affair, which threatened Your Serene Highness with great danger, is now likewise quite cleared up. The assessments of the Kalisz Chamber are likewise completed; they have

A) turned out as the copy under A shows, and lie at this moment before the General Finance Department, from which tomorrow I am to receive an authentic attestation concerning the permanently established revenues of the estates

and in which the yield is fixed, after all possible examinations, at 40260 Rthl. – However, neither the forest nor the transfer fees (Laudemium) nor the jurisdictional incidents have been taken into account here, but have all been left to Your Serene Highness's disposition.

B) Great as the yield has turned out to be, yet the debts, according to the schedule appended under B, are not less great and weighty, and a series of years will be needed before all is cleanly paid off.

It was a piece of good fortune that I arrived here at just this time, for the matter of the Courland post and of the interest payments was again pending, the more so as Minister von Stein is now clearing everything up and to that end has requested a commission to be set up, consisting of Minister von Voss, the Grand Chancellor and himself, over against Herr [uncertain: Münsterbge] von Schulenburg, which is to investigate everything and have everything secured, so that one may see clearly, especially with regard to the Bank and the Maritime Trading Company, where great deficits are suspected.

I have conferred much on this matter with the Herr General von R., and in the end agreed with him to petition His Majesty the King for as many treasury notes as are required for the payment of Your Serene Highness's debts altogether.

The whole sum, as the schedule states, is however 698000 Rthl, exclusive of the items due to myself, with which I shall wait until happier events. I have accordingly wished to write under my own name, as the enclosure

C) under Letter C shows, and the more so since the King is only now letting the justice due to me be done me. Yet the Privy [uncertain: Lab.] Councillor B. has not yet given his fiat, and the worthy General feared, with every right, that the matter might founder entirely. Therefore the honest friend proposed that the bosom friend of Herr B. – the Court Fiscal Pochammer – should push this matter forward.

With him I have now come to an agreement. He will attend to everything and demands for his trouble an honorarium of the like measure as he has been promised by the [uncertain: questions] of von Hesse[?].

D) In the meantime he must have a special power of attorney, and to that end he has just now sent me the form appended under Letter D, which I convey to Your Serene Highness with the most obedient request to have the same drawn up there in proving form / in so far as it all has Your Highness's approbation / to execute it, and to send it hither with all speed, so that I may work quickly and bring everything to completion – the more so since Midsummer Day is at the door – and since in these most hard-pressed times not a Groschen is to be had from anywhere else, all the treasuries being closed in particular, wherewith to pay the most pressing debts.

Everything will depend on Herr Pochammer's disposing the Cabinet Councillor so that we obtain the monies or treasury notes at 3 per cent. The Prince of Orange must indeed give 4 per cent; meanwhile Herr Pochammer believes he will fully succeed and prise the capital loose at 3 per cent. Were this so, then Your Serene Highness might esteem yourself exceedingly fortunate. You would be rid of all your debts at one stroke, would after some 30 years have the estates entirely clear, and would in the meantime nevertheless draw yearly some 10000 Rthl from the two lordships, and would be quit and free of all cares and sorrows and especially of the vexation of the dishonouring lawsuits. The amortisation plan I have drawn up to that end and especially for Your Serene Highness's survey, and append it E) under Letter E most obediently herewith. Deign, most gracious Prince and Lord, to examine everything, and send me above all the power of attorney for the Court Fiscal Herr Pochammer – so that this work, so exceedingly important and tending to Your Serene Highness's welfare, peace and honour, may be pursued most zealously and once happily brought to a close. It is most rewarding for me that the Monarch himself could not withhold his approbation, and that this matter has excited the astonishment even of the Cabinet Councillor Beyme; but what has pleased me above all is that Minister von Voss has held up my conduct to his Chambers as a model.

As concerns now the Glenck letter of the 28th of February, I reply thereto most obediently that the Prussiemck affair is as foreign to Glenck as colour is to a man born blind. Were it possible for me to be everywhere at once, I would pursue this business too with equal force. But since this cannot happen without appropriating to oneself divine attributes like a madman, I must keep to the principal matters and work upon these with honest zeal, in order to establish Your Serene Highness's peace. For the rest, I console myself with this, that Herr Glenck has merely feigned this affair with his year in order to have material for writing to Your Serene Highness and to recall his beggarly importunities to Your Highness's memory.

He has attained his purpose, that I must now see and speak with him. My judgement was, however, correct, for of the Prusimski affair he knew nothing; only the consent respecting the sale of his lands was the subject upon which he reasoned. He boasted of his many years' service and that he had made 42 journeys in Your Serene Highness's affairs – for which, in his opinion, he believed he had deserved a far greater remuneration than this trifle. To make an end of the thing, I asked him what he actually wanted if he were to sell the lands back to Your Serene Highness. His answer was that he could not let them go for less than 8000 Rthl. For he had already made a present to Your Serene Highness of 330 Morgen of land which Your Highness had promised him over and above the bailiwick and the 300 Morgen of forest – this, surely, was already more than reasonable, and in case anyone should wish to refuse him the consent, he would bring suit and demand this too. In order to make an end of all this haggling of Glenck's, I have promised him the 8000 Rthl under [uncertain: specrati], and submit myself herein to Your Serene Highness's commands.

the consent

If I obtain the capital from the King, then Your Serene Highness indeed obtains much – but that is all used up if all the debts are to be paid. The latter must be, because the mortgage certificate must be clear. Whence then to take it? The only case would be if I could obtain money on one of my mortgages on Schwimmern and Kontop, or that the hereditary purchasers in Zagorowo and Trąbczyn should come in numbers and bring money to pay, but [uncertain: it would be needful] that I could be at home or in those parts; that, however, will not do, for I must absolutely be at work here. If there is nevertheless a possibility, then may Your Serene Highness count upon my zeal to realise your wishes in every way.
