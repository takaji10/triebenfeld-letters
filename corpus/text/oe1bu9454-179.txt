--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports from Berlin that the dismemberment and the Betscher matter have won royal approbation, and that the Kalisch Chamber's assessments fix the estates' yield at 40260 Rthl, excluding forest and Laudemien; debts total 698000 Rthl (enclosures A–E, including an amortisation plan). Seeks a power of attorney for Court Fiscal Pochammer to secure Treasury notes at 3 per cent through Cabinet Councillor B. Recounts Glenck's demand of 8000 Rthl, warns against consenting to the Santomist/Sartomiel sales, doubts raising 30000 Rthl, and complains of Grattenauer, Schenck, Grimm and Malzern.

Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! Your Most Serene Highness's most gracious letters of the 19th and 27th of March I received so late only because they lay a very long while in Słupca. It is more than sad for me that I must live so far distant from Your Serene Highness and have news so seldom; it is particularly hard for me to act when Your Serene Highness leaves me so long without any answer. Meanwhile I give myself the honour of reporting most obediently to the same how all matters have prospered. After I had put everything in order on the estates, I betook myself again hither to Berlin, in order to work here and, if possible, to bring the matter to a finish. The dismemberment has, as Your Serene Highness knows, been most honourably approved, and His Majesty the King himself has vouchsafed me his high approbation. The Betscher matter too has been approved at the Chamber, submitted by General Honrichs's Department to the King for approbation, and only today has it come in here executed by the King. This matter, which threatened Your Serene Highness with great danger, is now likewise entirely cleared up. The assessments of the Kalisch Chamber are likewise completed; they have turned out A) as the Copy sub A shows, and lie just now before the General Finance Department, from which tomorrow I am to receive an authentic attestation concerning the permanently established revenues of the estates

and in which the yield is fixed at 40260 Rthl after every possible examination. – However, neither the forest nor the Laudemien and jurisdictional dues are here taken into account, but rather everything has been left to Your Serene Highness's disposition. B) Great as the yield has now turned out to be, the debts, according to the schedule annexed sub B, are no less great and weighty, and a series of years will be needed until everything is clean paid off. It was a piece of luck that I arrived here just at this time, for the matter concerning the Curische post and the interest payments was again pending, all the more so as the Minister v. Stein is now clearing everything up and to that end has petitioned for the appointment of a commission, consisting of Minister v. Voss, the Grand Chancellor, and himself, vis-à-vis the Herr Minister v. Schulenburg, which is to investigate everything and have everything secured, so that particularly with regard to the Bank and the Maritime Trading Company, where great deficits are suspected, one may see clearly. I have conferred much upon this matter with the Herr General v. R. and in the end have agreed with the same to request of His Majesty the King as many Treasury notes as are required for the payment of Your Serene Highness's debts altogether. The whole sum is, as the schedule states, however 698000 Rthl, exclusive of the items due to myself, with which I shall wait until happier events. To that end I have wished to write under my own name, as the enclosure

C) sub Litt. C shows, and all the more so since the King is only now letting the justice due to me be done to me. However, Herr Privy [uncertain: Lab.] Councillor B. has not yet given his fiat, and the worthy General feared, with every right, that the matter might founder altogether. Therefore the upright friend proposed that the bosom friend of Herr p. B. – the Court Fiscal Pochammer – should conduct this matter. With him I have accordingly come to terms; he will attend to everything and demands for his pains an honorarium of like measure to that which he has been promised by the Count v. Hesse[?]. D) Meanwhile he must have a special power of attorney, and to that end he has just now sent me the form appended sub Litt. D, which I convey to Your Serene Highness with the most obedient request that the same be drawn up there in forma probante – insofar as it all has Your Highest approbation – be executed, and be sent hither with the utmost haste, so that I may work quickly and can bring everything to a conclusion – all the more so as St. John's Day is at the door – and in these so most straitened times not a Groschen is to be had from any other quarter, since in particular all funds are blocked, with which to pay the so pressing debts.

Everything will depend on Herr P. Pochammer's disposing the Herr Cabinet Councillor so that we obtain the monies or Treasury notes at 3 per cent. The Prince of Oranien must indeed give 4 per cent; meanwhile Herr Pochammer believes he will quite succeed and will prise the capital loose at 3 per cent. Were this so, then Your Serene Highness might account yourself exceedingly fortunate. You would at one stroke be rid of all your debts, would after some 30 years have the estates entirely clear, and would in the meanwhile nevertheless draw yearly some 10000 Rthl from the two lordships, and would be quit and free of all cares and sorrows and particularly of the vexation of the dishonouring lawsuits. The amortisation plan I have drawn up to that end, and particularly for Your Serene Highness's survey, [uncertain: um begellige] E) sub Litt. E most obediently herewith. Be pleased, most gracious Prince and Lord, to examine everything, and send me particularly the power of attorney for the Court Fiscal Herr Pochammer – so that this so exceedingly important work, tending to Your Serene Highness's welfare, repose and honour, may be pursued most zealously and at last be happily brought to an end. It is most rewarding for me that [uncertain: nur] the Monarch could not withhold his approbation, and that this matter has excited the astonishment even of the Cabinet Councillor Beym; but what has pleased me above all is that Minister v. Voss has held up my conduct to his Chambers as a model.

As concerns now the Glenck letter of the 28th of February, I reply thereto most obediently that the Prusimski matter is as foreign to Glenck as colour is to one born blind. Were it possible for me to be everywhere at once, I would pursue this affair too with equal force. But since this cannot happen without arrogating to oneself divine attributes like a madman, I must keep to the principal matters and work upon these with honest zeal, in order to establish Your Serene Highness's repose. For the rest, I console myself with this, that Herr Glenck has merely feigned this matter with his year in order to have material for writing to Your Serene Highness and to recall his importunities to the memory of the same. That purpose he has attained, that I must now see and speak with him. My judgment was, however, correct, for of the Prusimski matter he knew nothing; only the consent for the sale of his lands was the object on which he discoursed. He vaunted his many years of service, and that he had made 42 journeys in Your Serene Highness's affairs – for which, in his opinion, he believed he had deserved a far greater remuneration than this trifle. To make an end of the thing, I asked him what in fact he wanted if he sold the lands back to Your Serene Highness. His answer was that he could not let them go for less than 8000 Rthl. For he had already made Your Serene Highness a gift of 330 Morgen of land, which the Highest had promised him besides the Voigtey and the 300 Morgen of forest – this was surely already more than reasonable, and in case one should wish to refuse him the consent, he would bring suit and demand these as well. – In order to make an end of all these machinations of Glenck's, I have promised him the 8000 Rthl subject to ratification, and I submit Your Serene Highness's commands herein. The consent

to his sale, however, Your Serene Highness cannot give him, unless you should prefer some 20 new lawsuits – for every peasant and cottager who has land within the 700 Morgen would not be willing to yield, and would protest against the dismemberment and against everything. [We shall] now see how to make it possible to raise a part of the monies and to pay Glenck. As concerns the consent regarding Santomist, I remit the piece received thereof, since the middle sheets are missing, with the most humble request that it be cancelled at once. Scentonial is an appurtenance of the lordship of Betschen which has been sold to the subjects – be it a demesne or a rustical parcel – enough that everything has been sold to the people, and everything is now exactly [uncertain: wirt] what has been struck to this or that matter. — Your Serene Highness has reserved to yourself no right over the parcels, but only the right of overlordship, in such wise that you will approve everything which those people, who are now called proprietary possessors, lay before you, and will receive for it your 10 per cent Laudemium. The so-called hereditary tenants have also already taken the Landeberg heirs to task and demand the estate back, since the contract of the [uncertain: Radte] is false. Were Your Serene Highness now to approve matters contrary to this and to the contract, and to consent to sales without the hereditary possessors' knowledge, then these people would have a right to complain, and every single individual could raise against you so costly a lawsuit as would consume all the advantages gained. Moreover it is also again not lawful to grant a burgher now demesne

rights. That is altogether forbidden by a new Cabinet Order and entails the loss of the Incolat; even alienations out of these estates to persons not qualified to possess are altogether prohibited, and are on no account to be tolerated. For the economy hereditary lessees this Cabinet decree and the edicts published in consequence of it are quite salutary, for by these alone must the [uncertain: Lüben] clear out. But above all, besides all this, I must further make it observable to Your Serene Highness that you yourself, even if you truly had the right to it, would lose frightfully, if for the sake of the wretched 6000 Rthl you were to approve this shameful plan. Sartomiel will thereafter, when the present hereditary possessors divide it up, be worth some 100000 Rthl. Every 20 years the holdings round about are sold, and then Your Serene Highness and your descendants have the property paid for anew through the 10 per cent Laudemien. This is surely so essential a matter that no lord of the soil signs it away for eternity. As concerns the 30000 Rthl, I still see no possibility how I am to procure them so quickly. If I obtain the capital from the King, Your Serene Highness will indeed receive much – but that goes entirely upon the payment of all the debts. The latter must be, because the mortgage certificate must be clean. Whence then to take it? The only case would be if I could get money upon one of my mortgages on Schwimmern and Kontop, or if the hereditary purchasers in Zagorow and Trąbczyn came often and brought money to render; but that requires that I could be at home or in those parts, which will not do, for I must absolutely work here. If, however, there is a possibility, then let Your Serene Highness count upon my zeal to realise your wishes by every means. By the first post I shall write to the same further upon this.

Perhaps some means may somewhere be found to obtain something further behind the great loan. Perhaps it will succeed, for everyone now wishes to help, since even the King has loudly praised my dealings and let justice be done to me, in order to appease the creditors. The saddest thing for me is that Your Serene Highness now lives so far from here, and that I must bear all these burdens so entirely alone. Upon no man can I rely; I may arrange what I will at my departure, and nothing is done – [uncertain: what] I must come again, I have only to thank God that I find it as I had left it. – Grattenauer is now continually ill, can neither write nor still less travel – Schenck has transformed himself into the laziest of hands – Grimm lets everything lie until he thinks I might climb on his roof again. Malzern I ought not even to depict, for his brutality and idleness are known to Your Serene Highness quite sufficiently. But if God now helps me to obtain the loan, to pay all the debts, and thereby to be rid of the great part of all the lawsuits, then I shall also lay before Your Serene Highness a plan according to which the useless [uncertain: bestimmen] may be put into [uncertain: Staat]. For now I must still go cautiously, so that the rogues do not become traitors and harmful to the good cause. If it is in the world possible, may Your Serene Highness be pleased to answer me with the utmost speed and, if need be, by express courier; otherwise I must lie here too long in vain. – In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Serene Highness's — If I obtain the Treasury notes I shall endeavour to [uncertain: abzureisen] this and that. – May His Serene Highness be pleased to send me a few more Blanquets for letters, with / most devotedly the / wholly devoted The / Berlin, the 1st of May 1806. faithful and most obedient servant v Triebenfeld
