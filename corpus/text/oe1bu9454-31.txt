--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Written from Berlin while ill and under arrest for unpaid interest, Triebenfeld begs urgently for the return of the order to pay on 6,000 Rthl so he can recover a pledged 10,000 Rthl mortgage, and urges raising 150,000-250,000 Rthl in Holland, debts to be cleared cheaply with treasury notes standing at 48 per cent, including Ephraim's claim. He protests at Honrichs's general power of attorney, proposes a cheaper slate of estate officials saving 1,900 Rthl, and dismisses the Otocki, Radzinski, Iwanski and Przespolewski suits as fabricated.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord. Over the misfortune that has befallen me here, or rather over the arrest on account of the outstanding interest, I have vexed myself to such a degree that I contracted an inflammation of the abdomen and cramps; through the efforts of an honest physician I have indeed been placed out of danger, but I should also have had to keep to my room and bed for a couple [uncertain: of weeks] had I been entirely [text lost]. The grief over all these adverse events wears me down still; from my estates I have or receive not a Groschen, from the State and the King I draw nothing, from no man do I take anything — on what am I to live? I have given up everything so as not to become the mockery of men — I was sent hither and was to go from here to Vienna, but instead I had to suffer this disgrace, and my career, just then embarked upon, was undone. Now I sit here and heap debt upon debt, for I cannot get away — I therefore repeat the entreaty that the former order to pay on the 6000 Rthl be sent back to me with all possible speed, so that I may help myself thereby, inasmuch as I shall get back the mortgage of 10000 Rthl that was given in pledge.

As to the further proof demanded by Your Serene Highness, the same follows herewith together with the mortgage certificate. I was on the point of obtaining the money from Holland; but if Your Serene Highness can obtain it there, so much the better for me. If they give you the full 250000 Rthl, then I need only cede the mortgage, and you will retain 102000 for yourself, and likewise the outstanding arrears, which (should the times change) can be swiftly recovered. For the rest, there will now be a sorry confusion upon the estates there, and I shall again have much vexation.

I had engaged the judicial commissary Mitschke at Kalisz, that he should act as justiciary on the estates and at the same time attend to the conduct of all suits. For this I had promised him 300 Rthl salary and, when he travelled, 1 Rthl [uncertain: per day of service].

Your Serene Highness has now, however, appointed a certain Hawich from [uncertain: Peision] as justiciary, without my having known a word of it; you have also sent Honrichs a general power of attorney — how is this now to be [uncertain: rectified]? Henrichs and Niedrierechi hold together, and the result is that the fellows will now cheat Your Serene Highness in common, especially since the one of them is authorised to do so. For two years the [illegible] have not remitted a farthing, but have already pocketed everything; I had therefore taken on a [uncertain: Cassus] and have just now sent him thither with extensive powers. Today I receive from this man — he is the son of the Privy Chief Consistorial Councillor Hecker here — the news that the general authorised representative of His Serene Highness, Mr Honrichs, Doctor, will not tolerate them. What am I now to do with this man and with [uncertain: Mitschke]? Honrichs has at once taken a lease from the Poles; he is a knave — I took him on at the time provisionally, in order to get the notorious Witów affair out of the world, but never was I minded to grant him a power in which he could do harm.
