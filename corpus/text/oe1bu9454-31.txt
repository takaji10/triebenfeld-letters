--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld, nach der Arretirung wegen rückständiger Zinsen erkrankt, bittet um Rücksendung der Anweisung über 6000 Rthl, um die verpfändete Hypotheque von 10000 Rthl zurückzuerhalten. Er beklagt, dass der Fürst Hawich aus Pyzdry als Justitiarius angestellt und Honrichs eine General Vollmacht gegeben hat, die zurückgefordert werden soll, und schlägt Gehälter von zusammen 1800 Rthl vor. Zu Prozessen: Ignatz von Radzinski ist als Erbpachter von Szetlewek im Rückstand; die Przespolewskische Sache gehört zu Trąbczyn und den Prusimskischen Erben.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld, who says he fell ill after his arrest over unpaid interest, asks for the return of the order for 6000 Rthl so that he can recover the pledged mortgage of 10000 Rthl. He complains that the Prince has appointed Hawich of Pyzdry as justiciary and has given Honrichs a general power of attorney, which he says should be demanded back, and he proposes salaries totalling 1800 Rthl. On the lawsuits: Ignatz von Radzinski is in arrears as hereditary lessee of Szetlewek; the Przespolewski case belongs to Trąbczyn and the Prusimski heirs.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord. Over the misfortune that has befallen me here, or rather over the arrest on account of the interest still outstanding, I have vexed myself to such a degree that I contracted an inflammation of the abdomen and cramps; through the efforts of an honest physician I have indeed been put out of danger, but I should also have had to keep to my room and bed for a couple of [uncertain: weeks] [text lost] as I was entirely. The grief over all these adverse events wears me down still; from my estates I have or receive not a Groschen, from the State and the King I draw nothing, from no man do I take anything — on what am I to live? I have given up everything so as not to become the mockery of men — I [text lost] was sent hither and was to go from here to Vienna, but I had to live through this disgrace, and my career, just then entered upon, was undone. Now I sit here and heap debt upon debt, for I cannot get away — I therefore repeat the request that the former instruction for the 6000 Rthl. be sent back with all possible speed, so that I may help myself thereby, inasmuch as I shall recover the pledged mortgage of 10000 Rthl.

As concerns further the evidence Your Serene Highness has required, the same follows herewith together with the mortgage certificate. I was on the point of obtaining the money from Holland, but if Your Serene Highness can get it there, that is the more agreeable to me; if they give you the full 250000 Rthl, then I need only cede the mortgage, and then you keep 102000 for yourself, and the outstanding arrears as well, which — when the times change — can quickly be recovered. For the rest, there will now be a bad confusion on the estates there, and I shall again have much vexation.

I had engaged the justice commissary Mitschke in Kalisz, that he should act as justiciary on the estates and at the same time attend to the conduct of all the lawsuits. For this I had promised him 300 Rthl. salary and, when he travelled, 1 Rthl. [uncertain: service money].

But Your Serene Highness has now appointed a certain Hawich from Pyzdry as justiciary, without my having known a word of it, and you have also sent Honrichs a general power of attorney — how is that now to be put right? Honrichs and Niedźwiecki hold together; the result is that the fellows will now cheat Your Serene Highness in concert, especially since the one of them is authorised to do so. For two years the villains have remitted not a heller, but have already pocketed everything; I had therefore taken on a [uncertain: case] and have just now sent him thither with extensive powers of attorney. Today I receive from this man — he is the son of the privy senior consistorial councillor Hecker here — the news that the general authorised representative of His Serene Highness, Mr Honrichs, Doctor, will not tolerate them. What am I now to do with this man and with Mitschke? Honrichs has at once taken a lease from the Poles; he is a knave — I took him on at that time on an interim footing, to get the notorious Witów affair out of the world; but never did I intend to grant him a power in which he could do harm. Hecker is putting up a security of 4 to 5/m Rthl. in treasury notes; with those one could, even though they are worth only half their value, still cover something — the matter has been settled here before the court — what am I now to begin upon? My advice is that Your Serene Highness write to Hawich that he is to apply to me in everything, and likewise to Honrichs, and that you demand back from the latter the general power of attorney forthwith, with the remark that such a thing is of no use to him since he is no man of the law, but that you require men versed in justice; he could remain in the capacity in which Triebenfeld had appointed him. Your Serene Highness's letter is of 23 March and I received it on the 3rd of April, thus eleven days on the road; wherefore I repeat once more that nothing more is needed than the cession of the mortgage, and that it is very easy to deal in this matter. The returns then come out as I have described them, indeed rather higher still — Of what Your Serene Highness writes me concerning various lawsuits I know not a word — All this, apart from the Przespolewski matters, must be new business, of which the knavish Honrichs has not written a word either; I shall write what is needful in that regard at once by tomorrow's post — for at bottom, as I see it, it is all invented and fabricated, rascally claims. Your Serene Highness will pardon my somewhat confused writing; I am so weak that I have had trouble in bringing it about.
