--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld schildert Hardenberg den Fall: 1796 erhielt der Fürst Kamionna und Kolno, Trąbczyn mit Dörfern im Kreis Konin und Brzyce, die dem entwichenen Prusimski konfisziert waren. Kamionna und Kolno verkaufte er 1804 an Bankdirektor Leixner. 1807 nahm die polnische Verwaltungskommission alle Güter ohne Urteil und gab sie Prusimskis Tochter Miączyńska; das Tribunal zu Kalisz erklärte sich für nicht ermächtigt. Er bittet, Leixner seine Güter zurückzugeben und beim russischen Kaiser die Rückgabe der Trąbczyner Güter samt 36500 Rthl Revenuen zu erwirken.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld describes the case to Hardenberg: in 1796 the Prince received Kamionna and Kolno, Trąbczyn with its villages in the district of Konin, and Brzyce, which had been confiscated from the fugitive Prusimski. He sold Kamionna and Kolno in 1804 to Leixner, a bank director. In 1807 the Polish administrative commission took all the estates without a judgment and gave them to Prusimski's daughter Miączyńska; the tribunal at Kalisz declared that it had no authority in the matter. He asks that Leixner's estates be returned to him and that the Russian Emperor be moved to return the Trąbczyn estates together with 36500 Rthl in revenues.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

His Majesty the late King of Prussia conferred in the year 1796 upon the Prince of Hohenlohe, according to the appended copy of the instrument under Letter A, the estates of Kamionna and Kulm in the Poznań department, likewise the villages of Trąbczyn, Szetlewek, Nowawies, Lazy, Laske, Osiny and Trąbczyner Hauland in the district of Konin, as also the village of Brzyce together with its appurtenances in Cujavia. These estates formerly belonged to a certain von Prusimski, and after his flight they were confiscated and conferred upon the Prince of Hohenlohe.

The Prince possessed these estates, except Brzyce, which the daughter of Prusimski, now the married von Miączyńska, claimed as her maternal inheritance, for over 11 years in the most undisturbed possession. Of these he sold in the year 1804 to the bank director Leixner at Frankfurt an der Oder the Kamionna and Kulm estates, which Leixner likewise possessed undisturbed for 3 years.

Only in 1807, and indeed 14 weeks after the peace of Tilsit, did it occur to the so-called Polish administrative commission to take all the estates from the Prince and from Leixner by force, without any judicial judgment whatever, and to give the same to the married Miączyńska. Prince Hohenlohe, immediately upon his return from the Empire, brought suit before the peace court at Konin, which forum, however, von Miączyńska did not recognise at all. The Prince thereupon caused the complaint here most humbly appended under Letter B to be submitted, in the prescribed manner, through his attorney, to the tribunal at Kalisz, which now also points to everything for further inspection. Von Miączyńska replied thereto through her actuary, according to the enclosure under Letter C, that she was also not not bound to submit herself to the tribunal in this matter. The said tribunal then drew the matter out longer than a year, and at length issued the decree appended under Letter D, in which, after many shallow and unintelligible circumlocutions, it says — that the tribunal did not hold itself empowered to annul the ordinance of the administrative commission, and that this belonged to the King alone — that von Miączyńska should therefore remain seated in the estates, and the Prince pay the costs. So the matter stands even now: Miączyńska sits in the estates, draws in all the revenues, and since 1806 has paid not a Groschen of interest. No one was made more unfortunate by this shameful and violent act of the oft-mentioned administrative commission than the bank director Leixner; this man saw himself not only robbed of his fortune, but he lost his bread and his freedom. After the happy turn which Europe's fate has now taken through Your Serene Highness's foresight and exertion, may Your Most High Self deign to command that Kulm and Kamionna be given back to Leixner, in order that the Royal Bank may be satisfied, and by Your Most High and powerful intercession with His Majesty the Russian Emperor bring it about that to the Prince of Hohenlohe the Trąbczyn estates also, which lie on the far side of the Prosna, must be given back by von Miączyńska, together with the revenues drawn by her through 9 years, which according to the leases amount to 36500 Rthl.

Your Most Serene Highness will, by the granting of this just request, make happy many persons languishing in misery, and the Royal Bank will likewise receive its money back.

In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Vienna, the 2nd of March

1815

most humble and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld
