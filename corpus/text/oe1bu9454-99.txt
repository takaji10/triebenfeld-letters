--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports on lobbying in Berlin for the Prince's acquisition of the Krotoszyn estates: von Zastrow's reply is enclosed, von Voss has moderated his tone, Nöldichen is in difficulty, and von Köckritz says the matter will go to Knobloch and Prittwitz, whom Triebenfeld has urged to take Wildeganst as assistant. He passes on Berlin talk of imminent war and of new cabinet councillors, asks for a commission councillor's patent for Carl Theodor Fischbach at Betsche, and promises to prove Broniewski has drawn more from the estates than was owed him.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

My most fervent wishes are still only these, that Your Most Serene Highness may have arrived at Öhringen quite happily and content; the affairs which otherwise lie upon my heart will assuredly be brought to the best of conclusions and everything brought to the desired end. Upon the letter lastly also submitted to [uncertain: d. D. c.] Zastrow only the enclosed answer has indeed followed; each [time] I have departed from everyone with the first assurance that Your Most Serene Highness will obtain the Krotoszyn estates. Even Herr von Voss is said to have toned down his manner very much, and the notorious Nöldichen is in straits. His Serene Highness von Köckritz, who at present conducts himself quite tolerably in this matter, says that the conclusion of the affair will be entrusted to the 9th [uncertain: f. v.] Knobloch and Prittwyl. Here Wildeganst can do everything; he is the intimate friend of them both and he sees more clearly than many — I have therefore

already so framed the matter in the letter to Herr Prittwitz that he will take Wildeganster as his assistant. On my departure from Berlin everything seemed to be full of anxious expectation on account of the war. Almost everyone held it to be unavoidable, and even the King is said to have shown an inclination to strike out with the whole army. The cabinet [councillor] Mencke would not permit Brand: to travel away for 10 days, because he believed that important things would shortly come to pass, and that even in the Ministry changes might occur. It was also said quite certainly that one or two more cabinet councillors were to be appointed, for which a couple had already been proposed — this is indeed to be wished for, since then a cabinet secretary will no longer be at liberty to instruct a reigning Prince and first commander, nor Ministers and whole judicial boards either,

when they write to the Sovereign. The draft which the councillor of war von Cöln made concerning Krotoszyn, and which the King gave to the cabinet [uncertain: Bey:] for all, was handed over by the latter to Herr von Voss — who, however, wrote too haughtily to the King and moved for the dismissal of this man — the [uncertain: O v Ko.] is greatly incensed at this and holds 13 to be false — He at once turned the matter so that the [uncertain: Löte], as the most honest man, is to be denied to Breslau — this has now turned out better than anyone could have [expected]. For Carl Theodor Fischbach at Betsche may it please Your Serene Highness to have the patent as commission councillor made out. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's. Broniewski will surely not be permitted to receive a single heller, for now I have witnesses at hand that the fellow has taken more out of the estates than is due to him — I shall now pursue the matter with zeal — also well if one [uncertain: rescues a sum here]. Breslau, the 1st of February 1799.

faithful most humble and obedient servant v. Triebenfeld
