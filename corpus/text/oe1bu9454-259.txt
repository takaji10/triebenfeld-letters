--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that Hardenberg has ordered the Brieg court to investigate the Prince's affair with the utmost dispatch and to report briefly, sparing it the delays of Berlin, and that the Chancellor is having the 42000 rt. paid. Urges the Prince to declare everything freely before the Commissarius at Brieg or Schlawenschitz, to prove the settlements fraudulent and the debts fictitious, to expose Cosmar's forgeries and the coerced acknowledgement of 300000 Rthl. extracted at Öhringen, and to act only on Court Councillor Hahn's advice.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Now I can demonstrate to Your Most Serene Highness with the highest proofs that your sufferings will have an end. At every minute I await the altogether excellent resolution of the elevated burgher-prince Hardenberg, to the effect that the Brieg O[uncertain: S]G is to investigate the whole matter with the utmost dispatch and to submit its report in the shortest possible [illegible]; had the business gone to Berlin, it might well have landed in the long volume. Now this worry too has been removed. But how graciously and forbearingly my submissions remain behind with the Prince - yet this Your Serene Highness will indeed [uncertain: gather] from the resolution and the order to the Supreme Provincial Court

2. The answer of the Brieg B. L. G. being conformable with one of the submissions, the Prince Chancellor thus has the 42000

rt. paid and everything then proceeds according to my proposal; [uncertain: it is now a matter for a Serene Highness to press the thing there] - You are now yourself to declare everything before the commissary, whether in Brieg or in Schlawenschitz. For God's sake I beg you, most gracious Prince, speak now freely and without any [illegible], say straight out to me how one had induced you - prove then in brief that the settlements were drawn up to the detriment of the creditors, and that the debts entered there are fictitious. Prove by [uncertain: Beymo Bruch] how you were enticed into believing Cosmar - Draw the commission's attention to the fact that Cosmar forged everything together with the old [one] in Berlin, and that everything was sent to you at Öhringen) where you were under arrest, and that you were carried away into acceding by a thousand false representations and also by threats. The declaration that you are to pay and will pay the 300000 Rthl - as also the codicil and all

of Cosmar's devilish fabrications, lay them out plainly and without any fear; in every case do consult Court Councillor Hahn. He may perhaps be [uncertain: abrasive], but he goes straight and is faithfully attached to you. - Spare neither your son nor anyone else; speak the truth in every particular, and that without [illegible]. Your children will afterwards have to honour and love you for it. -- At present I doubt both. The struggle here has been happily sustained, the most splendid course is opened; step forward there with head and heart and crown the day - honour, peace and contentment will then return, and will attend you to the very edge of the grave. But once more I beg you, do nothing without the honest [uncertain: Hahn]. Towards the officials, or properly speaking towards the worshipper of the false idol and towards the executor of the testament, you could [illegible] with the dignity otherwise proper to you, and avoid all familiarity. Ah, far more heavily will the birds then whistle, when the matter truly comes to be spoken of.

There is no time to be lost; so I now [report] all this to you, better Prince, so that you may be forewarned, by a courier as far as Neisse - from Neisse an express courier is to be sent to you, in order to get the [uncertain: Ratismann] quickly into your hands -- You need fear nothing more now; the aforementioned [uncertain: burgher]-Prince Kagler will and shall give real help - and my noble friend ß -- works on as a thoroughly honest man, whom you can never thank enough, with glowing zeal. This much today in the greatest haste; the day after tomorrow more. From Gärtner and Göschel I hear that the divine man, P[rince] Hard[enberg], takes up the Hohenlohe affair before all others - This too will turn out well. It is with the greatest veneration that I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

the end of the Congress is still

not to be foreseen, and is perhaps still far distant

Vienna, 8 December 1814.

faithful and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld
