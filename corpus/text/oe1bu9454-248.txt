--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports from Vienna that the Prince State Chancellor and Minister Baron v Humboldt are pressing the Prince's claims, Humboldt having demanded copies of all submissions; the King refuses to settle "nach keiner retour", raising hopes of annulling the settlement, gaining 314000 Rthl, advancing 112000 Rthl and recovering nine years' salary. Count Winzingerode's petition for Prince August's 10000 Rthl from Schlawenschitz and Lasswitz is countered by the 1799 deed. Adds rumours of French troops and the Monarchs' departure.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! If Your Most Serene Highness's wishes do not succeed now, then it may never come to pass. The upright Prince Chancellor has the best will, and His Excellency the Herr Minister Baron v Humboldt is exceedingly active. The day before yesterday the latter told me that the Curish heirs have approached the King and petitioned for their satisfaction. His Excellency has required of me copies of everything which I have submitted to His Serene Highness the Prince State Chancellor, so that the reply might be suited to Your Serene Highness's wishes. — The King has now caused it to be answered that he would not settle the matter after any retour [uncertain: nach keiner retour]. His Excellency is very glad of this and holds the whole game to be won. The Monarch perceives that he must pay — and gladly will one also annul the settlement, gain 314000 Rthl and advance the 112000 Rthl. The Prince will, as I know for certain, also bring into discussion the salary due to Your Serene Highness from 9 years, and in this too God will help. Just now I come from Count Winzingerode; he showed me a letter to His Majesty just begun, in which he petitions in the name of Prince August that His Majesty might be pleased to command that there be paid to him with the utmost speed 10000 Rthl, in accordance with a testament of his deceased grandmother the Princess Sacken, out of the revenues of the entailed estates Schlawenschitz and Lasswitz. I did not dissuade the Count from submitting the matter; meanwhile I told him that His Majesty had firmly resolved to have Your Serene Highness's affairs brought to a conclusion first of all, and that the King would perhaps himself have the estates Quldt taken into sequestration for years. — For the rest, it would be striking that the Princess Sacken should wish to dispose of the entailed estates even after her

death, of which she was only the possessor by advitalitium. — Here the greeting faltered greatly, and said, is that possible —? Oh, what things people do say! I hope that the decision given through Minister Humboldt will also be favourable to Your Serene Highness, and will fall out to this effect, that Your affairs are to be put in order first. Concerning the P. Warschau nothing has yet come to publicity, although the [uncertain: Pohlenlaas] has fallen; in the meantime the Trzwismer and Remeure matter rests on that account — the affair will not be so easily settled as was believed at Schlawenschitz — from that side everything is being brought to bear and much is being sacrificed — added to which is that our King is all too good. — The F. H. is very full of care on that account. The affairs of the Empire will end very well; I have heard various things about it in confidence, and have communicated what was necessary to the persons concerned. Yesterday there was a great Brink here. It was said that France had declared that as soon as Prussia occupied Saxony, France would let 200000 men march across the Rhine. Today, however, I heard from one of my patrons that this is a rumour without any foundation. In the Baden and Württemberg lands it is beginning to grow very restless; by the next post I shall write more about it. The departure of the Monarchs takes place 18 days from today. Most reverently I remain Your Most Serene Highness's Vienna, the 5th of November 1814. v Triebenfeld most faithful and obedient servant

It has constantly been said that all the estates had belonged to the old Princess Sacken, and that she had made her testament only in favour of Prince August and had excluded the old Prince entirely from everything, with a slender appanage. — I answered briefly: the deed of 1799, sanctioned by His Majesty, proves the contrary of all this. — According to it the Old Prince is the actual lord; he will now re-enter into all his rights and remain lord of all his goods until his end. —
