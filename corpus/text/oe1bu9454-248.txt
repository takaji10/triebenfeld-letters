--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Optimistic report from the Congress of Vienna on the Prince's claim against the King: the State Chancellor and Minister Baron von Humboldt are pressing it, the Courland heirs have petitioned the King, and the King appears resigned to paying, with 314,000 Rthl to gain, 112,000 Rthl to advance and nine years' arrears of salary to be raised. Count Winzingerode's rival demand for 10,000 Rthl for Prince August from Schlawenschitz and Lasswitz is countered by citing the deed of 1799. Also rumours of French troops and unrest in Baden and Württemberg.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

If Your Most Serene Highness's wishes do not succeed now, then it can never happen. The honest Prince Chancellor has the best will, and His Excellency the Minister Baron von Humboldt is extremely active. The day before yesterday the latter told me that the Courland heirs have applied to the King and have petitioned for their satisfaction.

His Excellency has required of me copies of everything which I have submitted to His Serene Highness the Prince State Chancellor, so that the reply might be conformable to the wishes of Your Serene Highness. — The King has now had it answered that he would settle the matter after no return. His Excellency is very glad of it and holds the whole game to be won. The Monarch perceives that he must pay — and the settlement too will gladly be annulled

to gain 314000 Rthl and to advance the 112000 Rthl. The Prince will, as I know for certain, also bring into discussion the salary due to Your Serene Highness from 9 years, and in this too God will help. I have just this moment come from Count Winzingerode; he showed me a [uncertain: justi-] begun letter to His Majesty, in which he asks in the name of Prince August that His Majesty might be pleased to command that there be paid to him with the greatest speed 10000 Rthl, in accordance with a testament of his deceased grandmother the Princess Sacken, out of the revenues of the entailed estates of Schlawenschitz and Lasswitz. I did not advise the Count against submitting the matter; meanwhile I told him that His Majesty had firmly determined to have Your Serene Highness's affairs brought to a conclusion first of all, and that the King would perhaps himself have the estates of Quldt placed under sequestration for years. — For the rest, it was striking that the Princess Sacken should wish to dispose of the entailed estates even after her

death, of which she was only the life-tenant possessor. — Here the [uncertain: greeting] faltered greatly, and said, is that possible —? Oh, what things people do say. I hope that the decision given through Minister Humboldt will likewise be favourable to Your Serene Highness, and will turn out to the effect that Your affairs are to be set right first.

Concerning the P. Warsaw nothing has yet come to publicity, although the Polish [uncertain: Pohlenlaas] has fallen; in the meantime the Trzwismer and Remeure matter rests on that account. — The business will not be so easily brought to an end as has been believed in Schlawenschitz. — From that side everything is being mustered and much sacrificed — and to this is added that our King is all too good. — The F. H. is on that account much troubled. The affairs of the Empire will end very well; I have heard various things about it in confidence, and have communicated what was necessary to the men concerned. Yesterday there was a great [uncertain: Brink] here. It was said that France had declared that as soon as Prussia occupied Saxony, France would let 200000 men march across the Rhine. Today, however, I heard from one of my patrons that this is a report without any foundation. In the Baden and Württemberg territories things are beginning to grow very restless; by the next post I shall write more about it. The departure of the monarchs takes place 18 days from today. Full of reverence I remain

Your Most Serene Highness's

Vienna,

the 5th of November

1814.

von Triebenfeld

faithful and most obedient servant

It has always been said that all these estates had belonged to the old Princess Sacken, and that she had made her testament solely in favour of Prince August and had shut out the old Prince entirely from everything, with a slender appanage. — I answered briefly: the deed of 1799 sanctioned by His Majesty proves the contrary of all this. — According to it the old Prince is the actual lord; he will now re-enter into all his rights and remain lord of all his property until his end. —
