--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld reports that the state taxes have been paid apart from 900 Rthl; he hopes to have the sequestration lifted soon. Because no one is paying, he is conducting 43 lawsuits, among them against Niedźwiecki (6,000 Rthl), Nossalski (3,000 Rthl), the people of Oleśnica (about 33,000 Rthl), the Wrąbczyn peasants, and in 29 cases against settlers with 39,000 Rthl of unpaid interest. The costs of the lawsuits come to 1,450 Rthl, of which 750 Rthl are for the Trąbczyn lawsuit and the Meierowitz one. Hardenberg demands proof of the new seizure on the sums given from Prussia to Poland.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Of my illness I am cured; my first journey was to Konin, to settle what is owed to the State and at last to [uncertain: been] the oppressive sequestration entirely. On the way, however, I had the misfortune of being overturned and of so crushing my head that on one side I may pass for an African and on the other for a European. In the meantime I hope to be restored in a couple of weeks. For the rest, by the exertion of all human powers I have got so far that the State taxes, on account of which above all the sequestration continues, are paid down to 900 t., and since the suit with the tenant of the propination rights lies before the Kalisz tribunal in a wording whereby Nassalski must at once pay 3,000 r., I hope next week not only to settle the remainder of the taxes but also the interest on the bank capital, and thereby to lift the costly sequestration altogether. With regard to the sums from the Invalids' Fund, Mendel Oppenheimer, Weigelt, Haugt, Seher Thos, etc., etc., I shall set myself on my hind legs, to which end the orders of the tribunal and of the land court, and the attestations in the original, will be despatched tomorrow to Berlin by express courier — for if the Bielski plan, that interest and capital shall be paid over 40 years at 6 per cent, has already, as men say, been accepted, and if on that account the new

arrest has already been laid, then one need give no man a good word any longer. Indeed it were to be wished that a like benefit were granted to the estate-owners in Silesia. Here everything can be made prosperous by this favour. For the rest it is a sad thing here that I must bring everything before the judge and recover every Groschen by way of law; [uncertain: Kühn] by reason of the new ordinances of the Emperor of the French and of the King of Saxony, every man refuses to [uncertain: pay] a single Groschen. Every [uncertain: Cur] says: the Prince is a foreigner, we need pay him nothing; I am therefore of necessity compelled to have all the refractory held to their duty through the judge, and on that account there are now already some 43 suits pending. The most considerable are

1. With Niedźwiecki, for 6,000 Rthl. Here I doubt that one will obtain so much as a [uncertain: deiner].

2. With Nossalski, for 3,000 Rthl. This one will be won in a few days, and he is rich.

3. With the 3 Oleśnica lads, for about 33,000 Rthl. These fellows can pay nothing; if only they can be got out, the Polish peasants will step into the contract, pay 10,000 Rthl. entry-money and the yearly rent of 2,000 r.

4. With the Wrąbczyn peasants, for arrears of rent, 1,200. These fellows say they were cheated at the conclusion of the contract in Wrocław, and will not keep the contract unless they receive an abatement — I shall gladly consent to the annulment of the contract, take everything from the fellows, and, as the laws permit, drive them out to their wages, the more so as I already have people enough, namely Poles, who will give 2,000 Rthl. in cash for the holding and the same rent — this amounts, for 9 whole and 2 half peasant holdings, to 20,000 Rthl.

5. There has long been a suit pending with the Kopojno, Skokum and Świątniki peasants concerning the grounds; this one is already won in the land court and lies before the tribunal for my approval — the people must reimburse 742 Rthl. of costs, which will not come hard to them —

6. With Berda, who has a [uncertain: spiritual] privilege according to which he is to have only 23 Morgen of ground, but who has secretly appropriated to himself some 70 Morgen, and who is to pay some 1,190 Rthl. of arrears of rent. 1,190 Rthl. The fellow refuses to pay; I therefore wish to see the contract annulled; for the holding I shall then at once receive 3,000 Rthl. in cash and a higher rent than now —

7. With 8 Hollanders who owe some 5,000 Rthl. of purchase-monies. 5,000 Rthl. This suit will be won, the holdings taken in and let out elsewhere — whereupon the 5,000 Rthl. come in, but the fellows go away empty.

8. With Ribbeck at Drzewce, for 225 Rthl. of arrears of rent — this suit is compounded; Ribbeck pays the 225 Rthl. on the 1st of April of this year, on pain of losing his goods.

9. With the miller Mex[illegible] of Swięcia, for 500 Rthl. of purchase-money with interest. The suit is somewhat held back because [illegible] promises to pay within 4 weeks.

10. 29 suits with the colonists, who owe 39,000 Rthl. of interest and will pay nothing at all. 89,115 Rthl. The judgments will probably fall out so that the fellows can be removed and others put in their place; I [uncertain: doubt] that more than 6,000 Rthl. will be got out of these arrears, for the people are too obstinate and go off in secret with their goods — There are still many purchase-monies and rents outstanding among good people, but these I must for the present allow time, so that they do not join the wicked ones too, and until I have first wholly overcome the wicked — by Midsummer everything must in the meanwhile be in order, and from then on up to 14,000 Rthl. of clear interest must flow into the treasury. For 3 demesne farms which I am restoring, 9,000 Rthl. a year is offered after the propination rights; this amounts then to some 23,000 Rthl. of clear revenue; the interest, including the amortisation fund, would come to 14,000 Rthl. yearly, and therefore 9,000 Rthl. would flow to Your [Highness's] [uncertain: Causa] from Midsummer onward — 48,467 Rthl. would certainly come in by Midsummer — out of which a part of the arrears, [uncertain: Staunz], the land tax, the costs etc. must still be settled. The deficit is 40,648 Rthl.

The [uncertain: torments], care and sorrow which I endure here in the meantime are not to be described with any pen; I do not know when I shall first hear, or whither I am to turn — The newer ordinances of Napoleon turn everyone's heads; no one gives a Heller willingly. In the 3 months that I have been here, 2,800 Rthl. have been recovered by execution — the suits already cost 1,450 Rthl., of which the Trąbczyn and Meierowitz ones alone, which I omitted to mention overleaf, have eaten up 750 Rthl.; I have had taxes paid to the amount of 1,500 Rthl. This already makes 2,950 Rthl. Now I am again to send in some 600 Rthl. of advances to Konin and Kalisz — if I do not, all is lost; the sequestor liquidates daily, his new account amounts to 2,236 Rthl. He is the favourite of Prefect Garczynski; I must flatter him as far as possible, so that he only does not [uncertain: hiesigt] and vex us, otherwise all is lost — indeed, they will chase me off from here like the cap from the dovecote. Gladly would I procure for Your Serene Highness but a 40,000 Rthl., so that you might once again have a penny against need; I also strive to be able to show you a wholly fixed revenue, so that it may be reckoned upon with confidence, and to determine the end of the debts, so that your descendants may know what they will one day have from these estates — Here there are new rumours every day, which however are seldom confirmed; so much, however, is known to me, that in Berlin they live as in a madhouse, think only of extortions, and for the rest not a word of what passes in the neighbouring cabinets. Thus Hard[enberg] himself does not know one jot of it, that a fresh arrest has been laid upon all sums conceivable that have been given out of Prussia into Poland, and that the interest and amortisation fund has been fixed at 40 years. Zerboni has made a frightful long nose at them all and now laughs here into his own house; in your negotiations aim only at the welfare of Saxony and Poland. Hard[enberg] demands of me proofs concerning the new attachment and the amortisation [uncertain: Platz]; I send him the same tomorrow — I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's Świątniki, the 27th of January 1811

most humble

v Triebenfeld
