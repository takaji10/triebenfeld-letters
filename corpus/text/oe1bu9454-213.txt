--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Recovered from illness but injured when his carriage overturned on the way to Konie, Triebenfeld reports the state taxes holding up the sequestration paid off but for 900 Rthl, with 3,000 Rthl expected from Nossalski via the Kalisz Tribunal. He lists some 43 suits against defaulting tenants, colonists and Hollanders, including Niedzwiedzki (6,000 Rthl), the three Olesnicer lads (33,000 Rthl) and Berda (1,190 Rthl), projects 23,000 Rthl of clear revenue by Midsummer against a 40,648 Rthl shortfall, and complains of the sequestrator, Prefect Garczynski and Napoleon's new ordinances.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Of my illness I am cured; my first [uncertain: journey] was to Konie, in order to settle the state debt and at last to be entirely [illegible] of the oppressive sequestration. On the way, however, I had the misfortune of being overturned and of so crushing my head that on one side I may pass for an African and on the other for a European. Meanwhile I hope to be restored within a couple of weeks. For the rest, I have, with the exertion of all human powers, come so far that the state taxes, on account of which above all the sequestration continues, are paid off save for 900 Rthl; and since the suit with the propination rights lessee lies before the Kalisz Tribunal as a [uncertain: matter of record], wherein Nassals must pay 3000 Rthl at once, I hope next week not only to settle the remainder of the taxes but also the interest on the bank capital, and thereby wholly to remove the costly sequestration. With regard to the sums from the Invalids' Fund, Mendel [illegible] humer, Weigel, Haugt, Seher Thor etc., I shall set myself on my hind legs, to which end tomorrow the Tribunal's and the land court's ordinances, and the attestations in the original, will be despatched to Berlin by express courier — for if the Bielsh plan, that interest and capital are to be paid over 40 years at 6 per cent, has already, as people say, been adopted, on which account too the new

distraint has already been laid on, then one need give nobody a good word any longer. Indeed it were to be wished that in Silesia a like benefit were granted to the landowners. Here everything may be made fortunate by this act of grace. Sad it is, for the rest, that here I must bring everything before the judge and recover every Groschen by course of law; [uncertain: Kühn] by reason of the new ordinances of the Emperor of the French and of the King of Saxony, every man refuses to pay a Groschen. Every [uncertain: parish] says: the Prince is a foreigner, we need pay him nothing; I am therefore compelled of necessity to have all the refractory held to their duty by the judge, and on that account some 43 suits are now pending. The most considerable are

1. With Niedzwiedzki concerning 6000 Rthl; here I doubt that one obtains anything [uncertain: at all].

2. With Nossalski concerning 3000 Rthl

this one will be won within [uncertain: a few] days, and he is rich

3. With the 3 Olesnicer lads for about 33000 Rthl; these [illegible] can pay nothing; if only they can be got out, then the Polish peasants will enter into the contract, pay 10000 Rthl entry money and the annual rent at 2000 Rthl

4. With the Wrabczyner peasants for arrears of rent, 1200.

these fellows say they were cheated at the conclusion of the contract in Breslau and will not keep the contract unless they get a remission – I shall gladly consent to the annulment of the contract, take everything from the fellows, and, as the laws permit, drive them out of the land, the more so as I already have people, namely Poles, enough who will give 2000 Rthl in cash for the holding and the same rent — this amounts, for 9 whole and 2 half peasant holdings, to 20000 Rthl

5. There has long been a suit pending with the Kopojno, Skokumer and Swiątniki peasants concerning the lands; this has already been won in the land court and lies before the Tribunal for approbation – the people must reimburse 742 Rthl in costs, which will not fall [uncertain: hard] upon them

6. With Berda, who has a [uncertain: clerical] privilege by which he is to have only 23 Morgen of land, but who has secretly appropriated some 70 Morgen, and who is to pay some 1190 Rthl of arrears of rent. 1190 Rthl. The fellow refuses to pay; I therefore wish to have the contract annulled; for the holding I shall then at once receive 3000 Rthl in cash and a higher rent than at present

7. With 8 Hollanders who owe some 5000 Rthl of purchase moneys - 5000 Rthl; this suit will be won, the holdings resumed and let out elsewhere - whereupon the 5000 Rthl come in, but the fellows go away empty

8. With Ribbeck at Drzewce concerning 225 Rthl of arrears of rent - this suit is settled by agreement; Ribbeck pays the 225 Rthl on the 1st of April of this year on pain of forfeiting his goods

9. With the miller M[uncertain: e]x of Swięcia concerning 500 Rthl of purchase money with interest. The suit is somewhat held back because the [uncertain: miller] promises to pay within 4 weeks.

10. 29 suits with the colonists, who owe 39000 Rthl in interest and will pay absolutely nothing. 89115 Rthl. The judgments will doubtless fall out so that one may drive the fellows off and put in others; I [uncertain: reckon] that of these arrears more than 6000 Rthl will be got, for the people are too obstinate and make off in secret with their goods. There are still many purchase moneys and rents outstanding among good people, but these I must for the present grant a term, so that they do not likewise join the bad ones, and until I have first got the bad ones wholly over[come]. By Midsummer everything must meanwhile be in order, and from that time forward up to 14000 Rthl in clear interest must flow into the treasury. For 3 demesne farms which I am restoring, 9000 Rthl a year is offered besides the propination rights; this amounts then to some 23000 Rthl of clear revenues; the interest, including the amortisation fund, would amount annually to 14000 Rthl, and therefore Your [Serene Highness] in the first period 9000 Rthl for the [uncertain: fund], but from Midsummer onward 48467 Rthl would certainly come in by Midsummer - out of which a part of the arrears of rent, [uncertain: Staunz], land tax, costs etc. must still be settled. The shortfall is 40648 Rthl

The toils, care and vexation which I meanwhile endure here are not to be described with any pen; I do not know [uncertain: when] I shall first hear, nor whither I should turn. The newer ordinances of Napoleon turn everybody's head; nobody gives a heller willingly. In the 3 months that I have been here, 2800 Rthl have been recovered by execution - the suits already cost 1450 Rthl, of which the Traperius and Meierowitz matters alone, which I have [uncertain: neglected] to mention overleaf, have eaten up 750 Rthl. In taxes I have had 1500 Rthl paid; this already makes 2950 Rthl. Now I am again to send in some 600 Rthl of advances to Konin and Kalisz – if I do not, all is lost; the sequestrator liquidates daily, his new account amounts to 2236 Rthl. He is the favourite of the Prefect Garczynski; I must flatter him so far as possible, only that he may not [uncertain: press] and vex, otherwise all is lost, indeed I shall be chased off from here like the cat from the dovecote. Gladly would I procure for Your Serene Highness but a 40000 Rthl, so that you might once again have a penny against need; I strive too to be able to show you a wholly fixed revenue, so as to reckon upon it with confidence, and to determine the end of the debts, so that your descendants may know what they will one day have from these estates - Here there are new rumours every day, which however are seldom confirmed; so much however is known to me, that in Berlin they live as in a madhouse, think only of extortions, and for the rest not a word of what passes in the neighbouring cabinets. Thus Hard himself does not know a [uncertain: jot] of it, that attachment has been laid anew upon every conceivable sum that has been lent out of Prussia into Poland, and that the interest and amortisation fund is fixed at 40 years. Zerboni has played a frightful trick on them all and now laughs [illegible] in his own house; in your negotiations aim only at the good of Saxony in Poland. Hard: demands of me proofs concerning the new attachment and the amortisation [uncertain: plan]; I send him the same tomorrow - I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's Swiątniki, the 27th of January 1811

most humble and obedient v Triebenfeld
