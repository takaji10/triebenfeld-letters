--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Petition to the King, written in the Fürst's own name, recounting how 3125 Rthl of interest owed to the Invalids' Fund on the 50000 Rthl capital secured on the Zagorower estates was extorted from von Triebenfeld by the French administration under arrest and military execution, forcing him and Lieutenant-General von Zastrow to raise 5140 Rthl in paper from the merchant van der Lahr. With 3000 Rthl still outstanding and judgment obtained, Amelang at Berlin is to pay 1000 Rthl and deliver bar- and hoop-iron; asks that von Triebenfeld be released ex nexu.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
In the year 1808, up to the 14th of February, I had become indebted to the Invalids' Fund for the interest on a capital of 50000 Rthl which His Majesty the King had had given to me out of the same upon my Zagorower estates, situated in the Duchy of Warsaw, in the amount of 3125 Rthl. My plenipotentiary, the Councillor of War von Triebenfeld, was at that time sent from Prussia to [uncertain: Stettin], and since the French Government had at that very time arrogated to itself the administration of the Invalids' Fund, the said Government extorted from von Triebenfeld, by arrest and military execution, the above-named 3125 Rthl together with costs. Von Triebenfeld did not have so much money with him and was obliged, against a [uncertain: solidary (jointly binding)] bill of exchange drawn by him and by the Lieutenant-General von Zastrow, to take up from the merchant van der Lahr 5140 Rthl, which the latter would not give otherwise than in paper and wished to have back in ringing Courant. The papers stood very low; meanwhile necessity compelled von Triebenfeld to comply with the will of von der Lahn. Subsequently I sent von Triebenfeld 1000 Rthl for the settling of this matter; but since van der Lahr now had to have 5140 Rthl back, there remains a residue of 3000 Rthl, for which von der Lahr has now sued him and has at present obtained a judgment favourable to himself on the ground of the more recent cabinet order, whereupon he now threatens von Triebenfeld with harsh execution.

Since I now cannot allow that the said von Triebenfeld should be pressed in this matter, which it is for me alone to answer for, I have charged my mandatary, the Privy Post Councillor Amelang at Berlin, to come to terms with von der Lahr in my name, to pay him 1000 Rthl, and to offer to deliver the remainder in finished bar- and hoop-iron by May. Von der Lahr has accepted this, according to his original letter enclosed herewith; only there still subsists a small difference in respect of the iron price, about which I have written once more, and as soon as I receive the [uncertain: definitive] answer from the said Amelang, the money shall be paid and the iron delivered. Your Royal Majesty I meanwhile beg most obediently to leave the said von Triebenfeld ex nexu, and not to compel him by execution or distraint to a sum for the discharge of which I am bound, and to which I have also, as Your Majesty will deign to gather from von der Lahr's writing, obligated myself. [uncertain: Pf] I remain, until death, Your Royal Majesty's
Schlawenschitz, the 8th of April 1809.
most obedient
