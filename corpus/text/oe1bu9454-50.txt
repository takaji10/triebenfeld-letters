--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A draft petition to the King, written in the Prince's own voice, over a debt of 3,125 Rthl in arrears of interest on a 50,000 Rthl capital advanced from the Invalids' Fund against the Zagorowo estates. The French administration extracted the sum from von Triebenfeld by arrest and military execution, forcing him to borrow 5,140 Rthl in depreciated paper from the merchant van der Lahr, who is now suing for the remaining 3,000 Rthl. The Prince, settling through his agent Amelang in Berlin with cash and iron deliveries, asks that Triebenfeld be released from liability and spared execution.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
In the year 1808, up to the 14th of February, I had fallen into debt to the Invalids' Fund for the interest on a capital of 50000 Rthl, which His Majesty the King had caused to be given me out of the same upon my Zagorowo estates, [uncertain: we] situated in a Duchy of Warsaw, in the amount of 3125 Rthl. My authorised representative, the councillor of war von Triebenfeld, was at that time sent out of Prussia to Stecten, and since the French government had at that very time taken upon itself the administration of the Invalids' houses, the said government extorted from von Triebenfeld, by arrest and military execution, the above-named 3125 Rthl together with costs; von Triebenfeld did not have so much money with him and was obliged, against a Lohderian bill of exchange drawn by himself and by Lieutenant General von Zastrow, to take up 5140 Rthl from the merchant van der Lahr, which the latter would give in no other form than in paper, and wished to have back in ringing Courant.

The papers stood very low; nevertheless, necessity compelled von Triebenfeld to comply with the will of von der Lahn.

Subsequently, for the settlement of this matter, I sent von Triebenfeld 1000 Rthl; but since van der Lahr now had to have back 5140 Rthl, there remains a balance of 3000 Rthl, for which van der Lahr has now sued him and has for the present obtained a judgment favourable to himself on the ground of the more recent cabinet order, upon which he now threatens von Triebenfeld with harsh execution.

Since I now cannot allow that the said von Triebenfeld should be pressed in this matter, which it is incumbent upon me alone to answer for, I have charged my mandatary, the privy postal councillor Amelang at Berlin, to come to terms with van der Lahr in my name, to pay him 1000 Rthl, and to offer to deliver the remainder in finished bar and [uncertain: ripe] first iron by May. Van der Lahr has accepted this, according to his original letter annexed hereto; only there still remains a small difference in respect of the iron price, concerning which I have written once more, and as soon as I receive the [uncertain: definite] answer from the said Amelang, the money shall be paid and the iron delivered. Your Royal Majesty I meanwhile most obediently beg to release the said von Triebenfeld from the obligation (ex nexu), and not to compel him by execution or distraint to a sum for the discharge of which I am bound, and to which I have also, as Your Majesty will graciously be pleased to gather from van der Lahr's writing, obliged myself.

[uncertain: Pf] I remain, until death,

Your Royal Majesty's

Schlawenschitz,

the 8th of April 1809.

most obedient
