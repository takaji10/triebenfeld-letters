bis d 10t dauren vom 11ten an stünde er zu Befehl um jeden Tag nach Zagorowo
abzugehen, ich habe indes gestern an ihm selbst zu expresse, den Lombardini
abgeschickt hat, geschrieben, und ihm befragt, wann er zu reisen gemeint sey und
wo wir sich treffen wolten. Ich erwarte deßen Antwort um darnach weiter
zu operiren. Weigel ist wieder sehr gut gestimmt – Er sagt
Will auch Stössel die Bischdorffer Güter gerne haben, so laße ich mir es ge¬
fallen, wann ich dorten eingetragen werde, gerne will ich den dem Fürsten
Quittiren und auf Koschentin alles löschen laßen. Wir sind dicke Freunde
er besucht mich öfter mit seine Kinder in Schwimmen, den die Korn, das ist
seine Tochter, die schließt sich sehr an meine beide Töchter an¬
Kurz auf der Seite hoffe ich alles nur mögliche Güte, umsomehr als es nun
ausgemacht ist, daß alle in Pohlen ausstehende Gelder bezahlt werden müßen
und außer Landes gezogen werden können.
Die wittwen Cassen Bauk, Seelandlung, Pupillen Depositoria und alle Privat
Männer erhalten ihre Gelder, die Pohlen sind auf einemmahle alle Bettler

sind pure Schein Freunde - also sehen die Sich vor - ich bitte Sie besser

Fürst herzigst darum — Für mich ist mehr als Gift Dolch und Hollen fein Ihnen
edelster der Menschen zu sagen was vorgeht und was im hinterhalt läuſcht. Ich habe
daher einen Weg eingeschlagen, wo Sie alles erfahren, darnach richten Sie alles

rechnen Sie darauf daß ein kurzen Evenements eintreten werden, wo Sie die glän-
zendeste Vollen spielen und Ihre Feinde gedemüthiget sehen werden.
Ich bin ein armer Erden Müller, etwas wißen Sie über mein unglückliches Geschick, aber viel
leicht thue ich mehr zu Ihren Nuzzen als alle Großen der Erde —
Mitt unbegränzter Verehrung erſterbe
Ewr Hochfürstliche Durchlaucht

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A report on an exchange transaction being pushed forward: Knobelsdorff, delayed by a commission from the General Director of the Landschaft, is expected to travel to Zagorowo after the 10th, and Weigel will accept the Bischdorff estates and clear the charges on Koschentin provided he is entered in the register. Notes Köchlich's departure and Köckritz's regards, Polish debts now payable, fears of war between France and Russia, prospects for iron sales, and warns the Prince that trusted confidants are false friends.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

This morning at 5 o'clock Köchlich departed; I accompanied him as far as his carriage; the King meant to follow an hour thereafter — Köckritz commends himself once more to Your Serene Highness's favour. As concerns the exchange transaction, I am pursuing it with head and heart — I hope it shall succeed — Knobelsdorff wrote some days ago to Fischbach – He said he had a commission from the General Director of the Landschaft, that the holding of this commission would last until the 10th; from the 11th onward he would stand at command to set out for Zagorowo any day. In the meantime I wrote yesterday to him myself by express, which Lombardini has dispatched, and asked him when he intended to travel and where we should meet. I await his answer, in order to proceed further accordingly. Weigel is again very well disposed – He says: if Stössel too would gladly have the Bischdorff estates, then I am content with it, provided I am entered [in the register] there; gladly will I then give the Prince a receipt and have everything struck out upon Koschentin. We are thick friends; he visits me often with his children at the swimming, for the Korn, that is his daughter, attaches herself very much to my two daughters. In short, on that side I hope for every possible kindness, the more so as it is now settled that all monies outstanding in Poland must be paid and may be drawn out of the country.

The widows' funds, [uncertain: bank], the sea-trading house, the wards' deposit offices and all private persons receive their monies; the Poles are all at once beggars, and it now stands close to this, that they cannot manage and all go over to the [uncertain: Küssen]. [uncertain: Nochrich] now fears everything and holds the war between France

and Russia to be unavoidable; likewise he believes that the Poles will make the turn-about; and if that happens, then at Galicia West Prussia too will join in, and the Poles will throw themselves directly into Russia's arms, in order to save themselves – for from where should the poor inhabitants of the little province of Warsaw, which after all makes up only the tenth part of the former Kingdom of Poland, take 27 millions. There smoulders here a fire beneath the [uncertain: Hegel], which could bring forth a frightful explosion. – Meanwhile a ray of hope dawns here which of itself builds up prospects for Your Most Serene Highness, prospects which I await with a high-beating heart. Private [reports] from Vienna assure us that the peace between Russia and Turkey is at this moment already concluded – God grant that this be confirmed, for then a couple of gloomy clouds, which now still darken the brightness of our star of fortune, would be [uncertain: dispelled] and we should already see a brighter dawn. Since – as God gives it, we who wander the earth take it from His hand – the upright man suffers but a short time, and then the matter goes better. This very moment the honest Lombardini was with me; he will send to put in, so now I can write more freely — For the present this further matter: Labage has been sent to Paris and is to offer linen and iron for the contributions to be paid – half and half, this mode of payment has been accepted – should it succeed, iron would be a commodity of new value – I learn of it first and shall tell Your Serene Highness faithfully what profit is to be drawn from it; the Swedish war offers glad prospects, for the iron will be sold at ever higher prices.

Now, from the heart, and to you, noblest of men and, alone among all princes' children, a man practised — to you, upright Prince, frightful [uncertain: Grüben] — believe and trust nothing of whatever anyone may tell you. – Your goodness of heart is frightfully abused by your enemies, and even those who so often enjoy your highest confidence are pure sham-friends – so let them look to themselves – I beg you better

Prince, most heartily for it — For me it is more than poison, dagger and hell itself, to tell you, noblest of men, what is going on and what lurks in ambush. I have therefore struck out upon a course by which you will learn everything; order all things accordingly, and show yourself a great man with the dignity that has always been your own. Count upon it that within a short time events will occur in which you will play the most brilliant part and will see your enemies humbled. I am a poor miller of the earth, you know something of my unhappy fate, but perhaps I do more for your advantage than all the great ones of the earth — With boundless veneration I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Breslau, the 11th of Sept.

1810.

faithful humble and obedient servant
v. Triebenfeld
