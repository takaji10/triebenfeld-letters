# oe1bu9454-283  283  letter
# 1815  Wien
# /documents/oe1bu9454/283/

Monseigneur,
Il suffit que Votre Altesse Serenissime daigne me donner des ordres, pour
que je mʼempresse de Lui obeïr selon mes facultés, je ne suis pas assés
presomptueux pour oser croire quʼil soit possible que mon intervention
ajoute un prix à une lettre qui vient de la part de Vôtre Altesse
Serenissime ; Pour executer neanmoins Vos volontés Monseigneur, je remettrai
incessament au Prince Chancellier dʼEtat la lettre qui lui est addressée
je saisirai cette occasion pour lui mettre sous les yeux les desirs que Vôtre
Altesse Serenissime a daignée me temoigner. Trop heureux si en executant
les ordres du Prince Chancellier dʼetat je pouvois être lʼorgane dont il ſe
servit pour remplir les volontés de Vôtre Altesse Serenissime. Ce nʼest
quʼici à Vienne que jʼai appris à connoitre Mr. de Triebenfeld qui

mʼetoit entierement inconnu jusquʼà present. Il lui suffisoit du reste
de me parler de Vôtre Altesse Serenissime pour que je pretasse la
plus grande attention à tout ce quʼil avoit à me dire.
Daignéz agréer Monseigneur lʼexpression du très profond respect
avec lequel jʼai lʼhonneur dʼêtre
Monseigneur
de Votre Altesse Serenissime
le très humble & tres soumis
serviteur. Beugelin
Vienne le 26 Mars 1815.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Beugelin undertakes to hand the Prince's letter to the Prince Chancellor of State without delay and to put the Prince's wishes before him in person, hoping to be the means by which the Chancellor satisfies them. He notes that he met von Triebenfeld only at Vienna, having known nothing of him before, but gave close attention to all he said once he spoke on the Prince's behalf. Otherwise a letter of compliment and assurance.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
My Lord, It suffices that Your Serene Highness deign to give me orders, for me to hasten to obey Him according to my faculties; I am not presumptuous enough to dare believe it possible that my intervention could add any value to a letter which comes from Your Serene Highness. Nevertheless, in order to carry out Your wishes, My Lord, I shall without delay deliver to the Prince Chancellor of State the letter which is addressed to him, and I shall seize this occasion to lay before his eyes the desires which Your Serene Highness has deigned to express to me. Too happy should I be if, in carrying out the orders of the Prince Chancellor of State, I might be the instrument of which he made use to fulfil the wishes of Your Serene Highness. It is only here at Vienna that I have come to know Mr. de Triebenfeld, who

was until now entirely unknown to me. It sufficed, moreover, that he speak to me of Your Serene Highness for me to lend the greatest attention to all that he had to say to me. Deign to accept, My Lord, the expression of the very profound respect with which I have the honour to be, My Lord, Your Serene Highness's very humble and most submissive servant. Beugelin

Vienna, the 26 March 1815.
