--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports on an exhausting recruiting journey through West and East Prussia: the Mennonites are willing to build and promise to come to Breslau on 19 December to sign, though they scattered too widely to sign heads of agreement on the spot and want the drink-selling rights valued in. Settlers from Landsberg and the Oderbruch have been summoned as rivals, and parcelling of plots is refused. The estate assessment drags for want of data; Glenck's countermanding orders and accusations prompt a threat to lay down the pen.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord!

I have completed the most toilsome journey I have ever made in my life, into West and East Prussia. The Mennonites are willing to build and have assured me, in their fashion, that with some money they will arrive in Breslau on the 19th of December and there conclude the contract. I could have drawn up heads of agreement with several of them on the spot, only it would have been too little for me, in one spot, since these people dwell singly, scattered about, and I could not bring them together, because the weather was too bad and the roads bottomless. Besides, they expressly wished to have the propination rights included in the present valuation, and this moved me to depart and to summon them to Breslau.

Since in the meantime the biblical lawgivers taught — to test all things and keep what is good — I have today sent after the Landsberg and Oderbruch people, am having these come, and shall hear their proposals [uncertain: effecten] — if these are better I shall conclude at once and commend the [uncertain: Mannamsten] to the [illegible]: I am no weakling — but the way to the [illegible] — sons has after all become too much for him; I have wrestled with life and death, and in the end the [illegible] made new strangling-bands for me — So if these people show themselves willing, I shall conclude and let the hypocrites go. There are men beyond number who wish to buy; but I cannot possibly

let myself in for a parcelling-up of the plots, since otherwise the poorer land would in the end be left on my hands. The best of it the Olesnica lads and the Swierer have taken away at such low prices that it turns many heads; they try this way and that, whether some little gap cannot be found through which to press in — I must therefore hold strictly to the plans once drawn up and stay true to my principle.

With the assessment of the estates here, matters go very slowly, for all the good will of the clerk, because everywhere the data are wanting — God knows where one is to look for anything — everything must therefore be ascertained anew, and a cruel amount of time squandered. In this regard I must remain here another 14 days, for if I depart everything will again lie idle.

In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's

Trąbczyn, the 25th of November

1804.

most humble and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld

This very moment Glenck was here. He reproaches me grossly for wishing to bring him to ruin in his fortune. Your Serene Highness is said to have told him himself. That I had made the highest reproaches to Him, that Your Highness had given Glenck too much. Your Serene Highness will most graciously forgive me if I, so as not to make my poor children fatherless orphans besides — since I must fairly vex myself to death — lay down the pen in all these affairs. Gladly would I win for Your Serene Highness all the happiness on earth, and would give up goods and blood for Your Serene Highness — but I will not let myself be branded by Glenck and Kochlitz. The people here are all becoming confused — for now Glenck has given dispositions here, in part contrary to my own orders — I will not speak out loudly about it, for otherwise it might be thought I was pressing myself into his place — meanwhile my honour and Your Serene Highness's interest suffer thereby. The justice-burgomaster Schema begs for early instructions as to how to proceed concerning the Trąbczyn lands, because otherwise he cannot go further with the assessment; to the manorial lordship (Dominium) there now remain, at [uncertain: Wegberin], 53 Morgen — so the assessment cannot turn out very high.
