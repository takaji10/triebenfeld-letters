--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports an exhausting journey through West and East Prussia: the Mennonites, scattered and unreachable on impassable roads, promise to come to Breslau on 19 December to conclude the contract, but insist the propination rights be included in the valuation, so Landsberg and Oderbruch settlers have been summoned as alternatives. Refuses to parcel the plots, blaming cheap purchases by the Olesnice and Swierer men; assessment at Trąbczin is delayed by missing data. A postscript protests bitterly at Glenck's reproaches and contrary dispositions, mentions Kochlitz, Schema's request for instructions on the Wrąbczin lands, the Dominium's 53 Morgen at Wegberin, and the Voigtey valued at about 11000 Rthl.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Lord! I have completed the most laborious journey I have ever made in my life, to West and East Prussia. The Mennonites wish to build [uncertain: build] and have assured me, in their own fashion, that with some money they will arrive in Breslau on the 19th of December and there conclude the contract. I could have drawn up a set of preliminary points on the spot with several of them, but it would have been too little for me, in a single spot, since these people dwell scattered singly about, and I could not bring them together, because the weather was too bad and the roads bottomless. Besides, they expressly wanted to have the propination rights included in the present valuation as well, and this moved me to depart and to summon them to Breslau. Since, however, the Biblical lawgivers taught — to prove all things and to hold fast that which is good — I have today sent to the Landsberg and Oderbruch people, am having them come, and shall hear their proposals — if these are better, I shall conclude at once, and commend the Mennonites to the L[ord]: I am no weakling — but the road to the L[ord]'s sons has become too much for its own [illegible], I have wrestled with life and death, and in the end the L[ord] made me new strangling-bands — So if these people prove willing, I shall conclude and let the hypocrites go their way. Of men who wish to buy there is no end; but I can by no means possibly

engage in the parcelling-up of the plots, for otherwise the poorer land would in the end be left on my hands. The best of it the Olesnice boys and the Swierer folk have carried off at such low prices that it turns many heads; they try this way and that, whether some little gap cannot be found for pushing in — I must therefore hold strictly to the plans once drawn up and remain true to my principle. With the assessment of the estates here it goes very slowly, even with the best will of the writer, because the data are everywhere lacking — God knows where one is to look for anything — everything must therefore be ascertained afresh, and a cruel deal of time squandered. In this respect I must remain here another 14 days, for if I depart, then everything again lies untouched. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Serene Highness's Trąbczin, the 25th of 9br 1804. most humble and obedient servant v Triebenfeld

This very moment Glenck was here. He reproaches me grossly with wishing to bring him out of his fortune. Your Serene Highness is said to have told him yourself that I had made the highest reproaches to Him, that Your Highness had given Glenck too much. Your Serene Highness will most graciously forgive me if I, so as not to make my poor children fatherless orphans as well, seeing that I must all but vex myself to death — lay down the pen in all these affairs. Gladly would I win for Your Serene Highness all the fortune on earth, and one would give goods and blood for Your Serene Highness — but I will not let myself be branded by Glenck and Kochlitz. The people here are all becoming confused — for now Glenck has been giving dispositions here, in part contrary to my own ordinances — I do not wish to make a noise about it, for otherwise one might think I was pressing myself into his place — meanwhile my honour and Your Serene Highness's interest suffer by it. The justice-burgomaster Schema begs for early instructions as to how he is to proceed regarding the Wrąbczin lands, since otherwise he cannot go further with the assessment; the Dominium now remains at Wegberin with 53 Morgen — there the assessment cannot turn out very high.
