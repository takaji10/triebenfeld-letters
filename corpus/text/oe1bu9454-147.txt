--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Der Schreiber (unterzeichnet v. Triebenfeld) meldet nach einer Reise durch West- und Ostpreußen, die Mennoniten wollten am 19. Dezember in Breslau abschließen, verlangten aber die Propination. Er hat Landsberger und Oderbrücher kommen lassen, um deren Angebote zu vergleichen. Stückweisen Verkauf lehnt er ab. Die Taxe durch Schenck geht langsam voran. Im Nachsatz beklagt er Glencks Vorwürfe und Anordnungen gegen seine eigenen. Der Justizbürgermeister erbittet Weisung zu den Wrąbczyner Gründen. Die Voigtey dürfte auf etwa 11000 Rthl taxiert werden.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
The writer (signing v. Triebenfeld) reports, after a journey through West and East Prussia, that the Mennonites want to conclude on 19 December in Wrocław, but are demanding the propination rights. He has summoned men from Landsberg and the Oderbruch in order to compare their offers. He rejects a sale in separate pieces. The valuation by Schenck is proceeding slowly. In a postscript he complains of Glenck's reproaches and of his orders issued against his own. The mayor for judicial affairs asks for instructions on the Wrąbczyn lands. The bailiff's estate is likely to be valued at about 11000 Rthl.

Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord!

I have completed the most toilsome journey I have ever made in my life, to West and East Prussia. The Mennonites wish to [uncertain: purchase] and have assured me in their fashion that, with some money, they will arrive in Wrocław on the 19th of December and there conclude the contract. I could have drawn up a memorandum of heads of agreement with several of them on the spot, only it would have been too little for me, in a single place, since these people dwell singly, scattered about, and I could not bring them together, because the weather was too bad and the roads bottomless. Besides which, they expressly wished to have the propination rights included in the present valuation, and this moved me to depart and to summon them to Wrocław.

Since, however, the Biblical lawgivers taught — to prove all things and to hold fast that which is good — I have today sent for the Landsberg and Oder-Bruch men, am letting them come, and shall hear what they can effect — if these are better, I shall conclude at once and commend the Mennonites to the H[?]. I am no weakling — but the road to the H. — sons has nevertheless become too [uncertain: seinen], I have wrestled with life and death, and in the end the H. made me new strangling-bands — So if these people show themselves willing, I shall conclude and let the [uncertain: hypocrites] go their way. Of men who wish to buy there are numbers without end; but I cannot possibly

let myself into a parcelling-up of the plots, for otherwise the poorer land would in the end be left on my hands. The Oleśnica [uncertain: Bube] and the Swięcia men have taken the best away at prices so low that it turns many heads; they try this way and that whether some little gap cannot be found to press in through — I must therefore hold strictly to the plans once drawn up and remain faithful to my principle.

With the assessment of the estates here, matters go very slowly, for all Schenck's best will, because the data are lacking at every turn — God [uncertain: knows] where one is to look for anything — everything must therefore be ascertained anew, and a cruel deal of time squandered. In this regard I must remain here another 14 days, for if I depart, then everything is left lying again.

In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's

Trąbczyn, the 25th of November

1804.

most humble and obedient servant

v Triebenfeld

Glenck was here this very moment. He reproaches me grossly, that I meant to deprive him of his property. Your Serene Highness is said to have told him yourself that I had most highly reproached him, that Your Highness had made Glenck too large a gift. Your Serene Highness will most graciously pardon me if I, so as not to make my poor children fatherless orphans besides, since I must vex myself almost to death — lay down the pen in all these affairs. Gladly would I win for Your Serene Highness every happiness on earth, and one would give up goods and blood for Your Serene Highness — but I shall not let myself be branded [uncertain: Laich] by Glenck and Rochlitz. The people here are all becoming confused — for now Glenck has given dispositions here, in part contrary to my own ordinances — I will not speak out loud about it, for otherwise it might be thought that I was pressing into his place — yet my honour and Your Serene Highness's interest suffer by it. The justice-burgomaster Schenck begs for early instructions concerning the Wrąbczyn lands, because otherwise he cannot proceed further with the assessment; the manorial lordship (Dominium) is now left with 53 Morgen at Wrąbczyn — so the assessment cannot turn out very high. The bailiwick, however, might now be assessed at about 11000 Rthl, for Glenck has applied for an assessment —
