--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld meldet, die Sache laufe nach Wunsch: Der König hat über Beyme geschrieben, Minister v Voss soll schleunigst berichten. Glenck soll die Anschläge passend zur Designation und zu den Contracten gründlich ausarbeiten. Triebenfeld hat einen Mann in Potsdam gewonnen und sorgt für eine Anleihe. Michaelis werde um eine Vollmacht schreiben. Triebenfeld bittet um unterschriebene Blanketts. Nachschrift: Buerensprung wurde Land Forstmeister, Wedel bleibt im Amt, Lindenau erhielt den Titel Excellenz.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld reports that the matter is going as wished: the King has written by way of Beyme, and Minister v Voss is to report with all speed. He says Glenck is to work out the estimates thoroughly, to match the schedule and the contracts. Triebenfeld has won over a man in Potsdam and is arranging a loan. He says Michaelis will write asking for a power of attorney. Triebenfeld asks for signed blank forms. Postscript: Buerensprung has become Land Forestmaster, Wedel stays in office, and Lindenau has been given the title of Excellency.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
77

Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord!

That everything is going according to wish, Your Most Serene Highness will be graciously pleased to be convinced [uncertain: Fuh aus] by the letter which you received today from His Majesty through the Privy Councillor Beyme. The matter goes off today to Minister von Voss, that he is to render his report with the utmost dispatch — In this matter provision has now also been made that the business shall not be dragged along the long road. In the meantime it would now be very good if Glenck were to draw up the valuations quite thoroughly, so that they agree with the designation submitted and with the contracts mentioned therein. I have all the contracts here; only those from Titz must Glenck have there, and keep in the best order. Before everything is in the clear (that is to say, what is to be done here), I shall not depart from here. A man in Potsdam — I have engaged, who

will be and remain active not only for now but hereafter too — but I must expend something upon it — Mr Michaelis will write today for a power of attorney; let Your Most Serene Highness send this through him, only so that various things may be settled here on the spot without having to become long-drawn-out — I hope to God that the matter will now be settled so entirely according to wish, and even better than I at first believed — I shall assuredly attend to everything in the best way with the most glowing zeal — I am at the same time providing for a loan suited to the matter, so that one does not fall into embarrassment and can keep one's word to the good King. Only this shall I always keep in mind, that the estates remain what they are, indeed that they shall ever become and remain the first jewel of all the property belonging to Your Serene Highness — and in this regard I shall appear truly effective only upon the finalisation of the business. Only for now I still wish to stand in

the background, so that envy may not play Your Serene Highness some trick on account of my insignificant person — The Minister, who here is in truth in every respect quite well disposed — commends himself most cordially to Your Serene Highness — One thing more I find necessary: let Your Serene Highness send me a couple of blank sheets for letters which I might have to write to someone in haste, that is, on the other side with a signature and thereupon [uncertain: dreiber ergebenster dier] most devoted servant

— H.

I shall use them only in the most pressing cases, and so [uncertain: mippen] that Your Serene Highness will never be able to refuse me your satisfaction, for one of them is already necessary for the man in Potsdam. Tomorrow I go to Her Serene Highness the Princess Sacken, to bring her the glad news of how far the matter has already prospered.

In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's

Mr von Buerensprung has however been made Land Master of Forests, and Wedel remains for the present what he is — But in general one [uncertain: sich] sees several changes ahead. Lindenau has received the title of Excellency. Berlin, the 6th of October 1798

most humble and faithful servant

von Triebenfeld
