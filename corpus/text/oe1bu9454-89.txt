--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports confidently on the exchange negotiation, enclosing Glenck's letter with copies of the writings to the King and to Noch, and citing Minister v. Struensee's declaration that Krotoszyn and Polajewo can yield no more than the lease figures (23–30,000 Rthl, not the 49,000 claimed by v. Voss's man Nöldichen). Sets out Glenck's nine-year general lease at 44,500 Rthl, the 10,000 Rthl security, 64,000 Rthl advanced, and the claims of Broniewski and Trąpczynski; rebuts suspicion of self-interest, and reports the Princess will give only 30,000 Rthl, leaving 7,000 Rthl unfound.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

The exchange transaction will, as I hold myself firmly convinced, certainly be brought to a conclusion to Your Most Serene Highness's satisfaction. The enclosed letter of the honest Glenck contains the Copy of the letter to the King, and one to Noch: To B and Z like letters have been delivered which comprise this whole matter, but for the sake of form and order are always put in other words. According to the utterance of Mr. p B, the Minister von Struensee might well now be called upon to give his candid judgement upon the whole affair, and this turns out thus: That Krotoszyn and Polajewo can never bring more than 50 Rthl, that Mr. von Voss wished to grant this, and that the present tenants cannot render such either, but are in arrears by many thousands. From this declaration it will not be

difficult for Your Most Serene Highness to draw the conclusion - I only wish that the thread of argument I have taken up in this matter may meet with Your Most Serene Highness's approval - My chief plan was to make the attack at once from the side of honour, so that the quill-pen heroes, who have no proper conception of it, should be thrown into all the greater disorder – Men of head and heart approve it; Heaven grant that Your Serene Highness may approve it also. The error on the part of Mr. p von Voss is very great, and it should not be difficult, given the batteries laid in place, to shoot his follies full of holes, so that one may press into them and bring the ravings of his Nöldichen to light.

In general my plan is that the King shall absolutely appoint a commissary, or else determine the matter decisively at once and grant Your Serene Highness the estates in such manner that the affair be settled according to equal yield and value.

The manner of handling and the execution of my idea is then, firstly, with respect to Your Serene Highness's estates. Of these Glenck is general lessee (for he must for the present remain in play there, so that he may carry the business through at the estates, since otherwise other spirits would make [uncertain: Bäbe bereien] and could cause Your Serene Highness cost and vexation.

Glenck has a 9-year lease contract and pays annually, inclusive of the forest revenue ascertained, 44500 Rthl; all sub-leases depend upon him, he has the liberty to [uncertain: reden] to dismember, and as regards the [uncertain: Nuppen] of the estates, to do and to leave undone freely what he will. To this end he has, with his associates, furnished a security of 10000 Rthl — next to this, Your Serene Highness has paid him the former sums for the putting of the estates in order and other things etc., 64000 successively, and has also assigned him funds for the settlement of what still lies upon the possessors Broniewski and Trąpczynski - concerning which Your Highness holds his receipts, and need trouble yourself no further as to how

he has applied and employed this or that. With this contract, with the receipts, Your Serene Highness comes forward, hands these over, furnishes the warranty of title thereupon, and has nothing further to do than to look with pity upon the quill-pen heroes when for fear they gnaw at their nails. As regards the Krotoszyn estates. Here Mr. von Voss's favourite says / for he himself does not appear at the end / Krotoszyn is to bring 49000 Rthl; the lease contract indeed states only 30000 Rthl, and the valuations only 23–25 and 27000 Rthl, yet it is known that it did bring 49000 Rthl. To this it is answered, whereby is this to be proved? For the declaration of Minister von Struensee states only the highest figure, which would be founded upon Mr. von Voss's manifold results, at 27 or 30 Rthl; then the Nöldichen answers: Here I have the accounts of the rent-master Schmudel, who kept them at the time of the Triebenfeld administration; these state so much or less, the remainder one thinks to make up by improvements.

Then Your Serene Highness's authorised representative demands that T. might also be questioned.

T. appears and asks [uncertain: wirt Zwonie] what is desired. The talk is then of the revenues received from Krotoszyn - It is said that these amounted to 40 or 50 Rthl - He answers that it might well be so. He had applied himself with a glowing zeal to arrange large and great receipts at his rent-office - he had thereby always expressed the ardent wish that such might be trumpeted abroad as far as possible, in order to find someone who should thereafter have an appetite for the lease of Krotoszyn and might take it off his hands - many and great expenditures he had, on the other hand, met in silence, letting his [uncertain: Cassini] know nothing of them, so that this man, without coming too near to his already troubled conscience, might be able to assure every curious enquirer of a great receipt – for at bottom he had, as lessee, never had cause to give anyone information, or to answer, concerning gain or loss - [uncertain: Jedes] his plan had succeeded for him;

he had obtained someone who not only wished himself into his [uncertain: Plappe], but moreover gave him a sum for standing aside, and who, if the contract were prolonged, would in the latter years pay the King some 5000

etc. more - this the King himself had approved by cabinet order, had likewise accepted estates in place of payment from the new lessee, had the sum for standing aside paid out to T. in advance, and afterwards had it drawn in again from the estates.

For the rest, however, he, T., must - if he were called upon to speak upon his duty and honour - assure them that it must come hard to anyone to render the lease raised by him to as much as 35 thousand. This was proved also by the condition of the present lessee, for he gave only 30000 etc. in cash, yet he always remained behind by large sums.

The sub-lessees engaged by him, moreover, could not maintain themselves either - for he, T., when he engaged them at the high rents, had thereby always sought to ease for them the discharge of the

lease, in that he took back from them all their produce in return for the lease they rendered at the [uncertain: Caesen], paying for grain, cattle etc. at prices once again as high as those they actually stood at, without letting it be noised abroad; and merely on this ground had every one of them been satisfied, and his business had thereby acquired the most brilliant gloss. For the rest the result would remain - that Nöldichen is a wretched official who, upon the hearsay of wretched men, has so conducted his [uncertain: Chase] that he has had to tell the King lies. The result of the whole then follows of itself. This I have wished to set down for Your Most Serene Highness's high insight and for Your Highness's reassurance, so that Your Serene Highness may see quite through the matter. For as it seems to me, Your Highness believes that I might be got at on account of Krotoszyn, and that I had perhaps even laid that plan before Your Highness with the thought of slipping through by means of it - By God, this is never the case, and can never be – I have always acted uprightly toward every good man, consequently I could least of all do otherwise toward my princely patron – It was the purest and most sincere intention, and Heaven will crown the end. The Minister Count Schulenburg has assured the Princess, Her Serene Highness, that Your Serene Highness could obtain the estates of both kinds,

only Your Highness would have to pay as much as should be brought out upon the investigation of the Krotoszyn and Polajewo estates and of Your Serene Highness's estates – against this nothing can be said, and everything depends merely upon an orderly conduct.

Regarding the money negotiation, the Princess, Her Serene Highness, writes today to Mr. Nising - the Princess neither can nor will by any means give more than 30000 Rthl - Nising is to draw up the instruments and to alter in them only a very little according to the formulary sent in here, and then the money shall lie ready here at once – Now it still stands at about 7000 Rthl – I do not know where these are to be got - I am taking every possible pains - God knows where I shall have luck with it. From the Prince I receive nothing more at all; Her Serene Highness assures me she would gladly do it, only she could not now, in the shortness of the time, find further means. If I sell my Schwiner oaks tomorrow, then I shall give; but if this does not go forward, it will be hard to find means here – I shall indeed bid everything up; perhaps it will yet go – meanwhile let Your Most Serene Highness also make provision there.

In deepest respect I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Berlin[illegible] the 12th of December 1798.

faithful, humble and obedient servant

von Triebenfeld
