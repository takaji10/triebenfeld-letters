Durchlauchtigster Fürst
Gnädigster Herr!
Wann ich auf irgend einer Art die 25000 Rthl, ohne das große
Geschäfte zu machen, erhalten können, so hätte ich solches längst
mit den glühendesten Eyfer bewürkt.
Nehme aber das wenigere so kostet es und die Hände
sind mir gebunden, weil ich die schweren Pappiere
bey den Kleinen Anlohm, so gut als bey den großen
verpfänden muß. Noch habe ich keine erste Versiche
rung war ich die Capitalien erhalten ſoll. Mann
findet die Pappiere gut, und die Sicherheit Lafficiat
aber man zögert, Gott weis warum.
Ich thue gewis alles was Menschliche Kräfte vermög
und Ruhe noch Raste nicht, wen an Ziel aller Wünsche
zu gelangen. Nur erzwingen läst sich hier nichts
Schon 2 mahl glaubten wir das Geld gewis zu haben
aber ein böser Mensch hat es hintertrieben, wie ich denke

ist es der junge Eyſenhardt - der will an Ewr
Durchlaucht auch 18000 Rthl zu fordern haben, für diese
will ihm der Hof Rath Meyer die 20000 Rthl angeben
und die Schuld annehmen – ich habe dies vor der
Hand abgewiesen - weil der Prozeß wegen den
Janvelen noch nicht entschieden ist und weil, die
9000 Rthl auf Szetlewek eingetragen daher er
mit den Zinsen zufrieden sein muß die ich ihm selbst
zu zahlen eine Versicherung gegeben habe.
Eben so werde ich mit der Ziemeckischen Sache machen
Von Bernhardis Benehmen schweige ich — den er wolte
mich ärger als der gröste Wucherer in Staate behandeln
und sezte mich durch eine 11 tägige Verzögerung in die
allergrößte Verlegenheit — Gott lob ich habe die Sache
glücklich als ich es denken konte beerdiget und laße den
Bauer laufen.
Solte die Schlabrendorffen unartig sein, so ist hier für
alles gesorgt, und sie allen würde ein thörigtes Benehmen
zu bereuen haben.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Explains why the 25,000 Rthl loan cannot be raised in smaller portions, since the same securities must be pledged either way, and blames delays on young Eysenhardt, whose claimed 18,000 Rthl and Court Councillor Meyer's proposed transfer of 20,000 Rthl Triebenfeld has refused pending the Janvelen suit and because 9,000 Rthl are secured on Szetlewek. Complains of Bernhardi's diverted 3,200 and 1,200 Rthl. Encloses a copy of Countess von Schlabrendorff's threat to resume her Berlin suit over her children's overdue settlement money.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

If I could have obtained the 25000 Rthl in any manner whatever, without making the great transaction, I should long since have effected it with the most burning zeal.

But if I take the lesser [sum], it costs, and my hands are bound, because I must pledge the heavy papers for the small loan just as much as for the great one. As yet I have no first assurance [uncertain: from which] I am to obtain the capital sums. The papers are found good, and the security [uncertain: Lafficiat], but there is delay, God knows why.

I am certainly doing everything that human powers are capable of, and take neither rest nor repose until the goal of all wishes be attained. Only, nothing here can be forced. Twice already we believed we had the money for certain, but an evil man has thwarted it; as I think

it is young Eyſenhardt — who also claims to have 18000 Rthl to demand of Your Serene Highness; for these the Court Councillor Meyer will assign him the 20000 Rthl and assume the debt — I have for the present refused this, because the lawsuit concerning the Janvelen is not yet decided, and because the 9000 Rthl are registered upon Szetlewek, so that he must be content with the interest, which I have myself given an assurance to pay him. I shall do just the same with the Ziemecki affair. Of Bernhardi's conduct I say nothing — for he wished to treat me worse than the greatest usurer in the state, and by an eleven-day delay put me into the very greatest embarrassment — God be praised, I have buried the matter as happily as I could have thought, and let the peasant run.

Should the Schlabrendorff woman be uncivil, provision has here been made for everything, and she and all of them would have a foolish conduct to repent of.

156 a)

Copy.

Your Serene Highness caused me to be assured, through the councillor of war von Triebenfeld [uncertain: Feld] to the Chancellor Hemuth, in the most binding terms, that at the Christmas just past I should quite infallibly receive the arrears of the settlement moneys for my children.

In full confidence upon the word given me by Your [Serene Highness] I have disposed over this sum, payable already these two years, and now find myself in the embarrassment of not being able to keep my own word.

By any further forbearance I should make myself answerable to my own children, and there remains to me therefore nothing further than to have the suit in Berlin continued, should payment not follow by the next post-day.

Of this I most respectfully give Your [Serene Highness] notice herewith, and have the honour

Breslau, 16th January

1805.

Countess von Schlabrendorff

I have not yet wished to make use of General von Köckritz's assistance, because Amelang thinks to get through without it.

The General commends himself to Your Serene Highness in the most respectful manner.

The free pass for 120 [uncertain: Eignier] of wine Your Serene Highness will doubtless already have there. In deepest submission I remain Your Serene Highness's. Just now I hear that Bernhardi has played another knavish trick. He was to hand over the 3200 Rthl paid to him, together with 1200 Rthl next March, to my Professor Rhode. But this he has, in order to keep the moneys with himself, directed to the [uncertain: zu den] Liep Meyer; and this fellow now does not pay the money, and I am anew in embarrassment.

Berlin, the 19th January

1805.

humble and obedient servant

von Triebenfeld
