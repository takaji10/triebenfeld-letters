Durchlauchtigster Fürst
Gnädigster Herr!
Wann ich auf irgend einer Art die 25000 Rthl, ohne das große
Geschäfte zu machen, erhalten können, so hätte ich solches längst
mit den glühendesten Eyfer bewürkt.
Nehme aber das wenigere so kostet es und die Hände
sind mir gebunden, weil ich die schweren Pappiere
bey den Kleinen Anlohm, so gut als bey den großen
verpfänden muß. Noch habe ich keine erste Versiche
rung war ich die Capitalien erhalten ſoll. Mann
findet die Pappiere gut, und die Sicherheit Lafficiat
aber man zögert, Gott weis warum.
Ich thue gewis alles was Menschliche Kräfte vermög
und Ruhe noch Raste nicht, wen an Ziel aller Wünsche
zu gelangen. Nur erzwingen läst sich hier nichts
Schon 2 mahl glaubten wir das Geld gewis zu haben
aber ein böser Mensch hat es hintertrieben, wie ich denke

ist es der junge Eyſenhardt - der will an Ewr
Durchlaucht auch 18000 Rthl zu fordern haben, für diese
will ihm der Hof Rath Meyer die 20000 Rthl angeben
und die Schuld annehmen – ich habe dies vor der
Hand abgewiesen - weil der Prozeß wegen den
Janvelen noch nicht entschieden ist und weil, die
9000 Rthl auf Szetlewek eingetragen daher er
mit den Zinsen zufrieden sein muß die ich ihm selbst
zu zahlen eine Versicherung gegeben habe.
Eben so werde ich mit der Ziemeckischen Sache machen
Von Bernhardis Benehmen schweige ich — den er wolte
mich ärger als der gröste Wucherer in Staate behandeln
und sezte mich durch eine 11 tägige Verzögerung in die
allergrößte Verlegenheit — Gott lob ich habe die Sache
glücklich als ich es denken konte beerdiget und laße den
Bauer laufen.
Solte die Schlabrendorffen unartig sein, so ist hier für
alles gesorgt, und sie allen würde ein thörigtes Benehmen
zu bereuen haben.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports on the stalled effort to raise 25000 Rthl in Berlin: the papers are approved but lenders delay, and two near-certain arrangements were thwarted, probably by young Eyſenhardt, who claims 18000 Rthl and whom Court Councillor Meyer would satisfy by assigning 20000 Rthl; Triebenfeld refused, citing the undecided Janvelen suit and 9000 Rthl charged on Szetlewek, offering interest only, and similarly over Ziemecki. Complains of Bernhardi's eleven-day delay and his diversion of 3200 Rthl due to Professor Rhode to Liep Meyer. Encloses a copy of Countess von Schlabrendorff's threat to press her Berlin suit for her children's arrears of settlement money; mentions General von Köckritz, Amelang, and a free pass for 120 Eimer of wine.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Lord! If I could obtain the 25000 Rthl in any manner whatever, without making the great transaction, I should long since have effected it with the most ardent zeal. But if I take the lesser sum, then it costs, and my hands are tied, because I must pledge the heavy papers for the small loan just as much as for the great one. As yet I have no first assurance where I am to obtain the capital sums. The papers are found good, and the security [uncertain: Lafficiat], but they delay, God knows why. I certainly do everything that human powers are capable of, and neither rest nor repose until the goal of all wishes is reached. Only here nothing can be forced. Twice already we believed we had the money for certain, but an evil man has thwarted it; as I think

it is the young Eyſenhardt — who claims to have 18000 Rthl to demand of Your Serene Highness as well; for these the Court Councillor Meyer will assign him the 20000 Rthl and take over the debt — I have for the present refused this — because the suit concerning the Janvelen is not yet decided, and because the 9000 Rthl are entered upon Szetlewek, wherefore he must be content with the interest, which I have myself given an assurance to pay him. In the same way shall I proceed with the Ziemecki affair. Of Bernhardi's conduct I say nothing — for he wished to treat me worse than the greatest usurer in the state, and by an eleven-day delay put me in the very greatest embarrassment — God be praised, I have buried the matter as happily as I could have thought, and let the peasant go his way. Should the Schlabrendorff woman be unmannerly, provision has here been made for everything, and she above all would have a foolish conduct to repent of.

156 a) Copy. Your Serene Highness caused me to be assured, through the Councillor of War von Triebenfeld to the Chancellor Hemuth, in the most binding terms, that at the past Christmas I should quite infallibly receive the arrears of the settlement moneys for my children. In full confidence in the word given me by Your Serene Highness, I have already disposed of this sum, payable these two years past, and I now find myself in the embarrassment of not being able to keep my own word. I should make myself answerable to my own children by any further indulgence, and there remains to me therefore nothing else but to have the suit in Berlin carried forward, if payment should not follow by the next post-day. This I hereby most submissively report to Your Serene Highness, and have the honour Breslau, 16th Jan: 1805. Countess von Schlabrendorff

Of General von Köckritz's assistance I have not yet wished to make any use, because Amelang thinks to come through without it. The General commends himself most reverently to Your Serene Highness. The free pass for 120 Eimer of wine Your Serene Highness will doubtless already have there. In deepest reverence I remain, Your Serene Highness's [text lost]

I have just this moment heard that Bernhardi has played another knavish trick. He was to hand over the 3200 Rthl paid to him, together with 1200 Rthl prox. March, to my Professor Rhode; but this he has, in order to keep the moneys by him, assigned to the [illegible] Liep Meyer; and now this fellow does not pay the money, and I am in embarrassment anew. Berlin, the 19th January 1805. humble and obedient servant v Triebenfeld
