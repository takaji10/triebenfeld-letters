--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Written from Vienna during the Congress, with conflicting rumours about when the King of Prussia and the Russian Emperor will leave for the army, and an enclosed note on the German settlement. Triebenfeld reports approaching Lieutenant Colonel von Thiel about presenting Prince Adolph to the King on the 20th and securing him a place in the army; Thiel promises the court will stay until the 22nd. Also news of Murat at Mantua and Bonaparte's build-up, and anxiety over the Kosten estates and compensation for the Prince.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

The reports, or rather the rumours, about the departures of the monarchs from here change like ebb and flood. One hour it is said that on ☽ [Monday] - in 4 days everything will depart - in the next one speaks of the departure as weeks away; thus it was said, for example, day before yesterday - next Saturday the King of Prussia and the [uncertain: Russian] Emperor go from here by way of Berlin to the army - today one is assured that [uncertain: this] is only to take place at the beginning of May, since the affairs of Germany must first be absolutely arranged. The enclosed note is a standard of what will happen. Not everyone is content with it; Harum in particular is an object of dispute. I was meanwhile [text uncertain] only to learn precisely whether the King too would still be here on the 20th, that is, until the arrival of Prince Adolph; therefore I went to the Lieutenant Colonel von Thiel. I made Your Serene Highness's and the Prince's resolution known to him, and begged him to tell the King that Prince Adolph would be here on the 20th, and would wish to be presented to His Majesty, whereupon he would ask to be placed in the army. I described the Prince and his circumstances precisely, and wished to know whether he would still find the King here. Baron von Thule charged me to commend him most reverentially to Your Serene Highness, and assured me that the King would certainly take great pleasure in it. He added: He is of good race, and if his brave father's spirit is not upon him, then the King is now making an important purchase — for the rest I can assure you that we shall not depart from here before the 22nd. With Prince Hardenberg I have not yet spoken, and could not lay this before him; but then this is not his business either - the military department and the civil are 2 heterogeneous things. I held it my duty to report this to Your Serene Highness. Your affairs, for all the thousand fine assurances, still lie in the mire, and share their fate with many

King Murat is making himself very loud in Italy, and has already pushed forward as far as Mantua — the Austrians are still holding up very well, and many troops are hastening thither to fight this monster – the [uncertain: Russians] seem to be stirring as well; they are obliged to march without a rest day, that is to say, they march for 3 days and on the 4th they are carried by waggon, which takes the place of the rest day - in short, from every side lords come hastening up. Bonaparte, for his part, is likewise forming a fearsome army – he draws all his troops together in and about Paris, heaps up great stores and magazines, and draws [uncertain: a chain] of entrenchments that would be hard to break through. - Meanwhile considerable scenes are said already to have occurred between the soldiers and the townspeople (the latter being much oppressed by the military), and this may yet become a motive by which the bloodiest war may be much softened.

The business of the Kosten estates likewise causes much anxiety; one does not know at all where the matter is now to be taken hold of. - An equivalent proportionate to the matter must be given to Your Serene Highness — but who is to do it, and where is one to seek it? Everyone writes complaining of frightfully great labours, and calls this a bagatelle — [uncertain: I could go mad] that no creditor shall sink his claws into it; for that I vouch to you. You may in any case issue a mortgage upon me at Zagorowo; there everything stays with him, yes indeed.

In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's

faithful and obedient servant

Vienna, the 15th of April

1815.

v. Triebenfeld
