Durchlauchtigster Fürst
Gnädigster Herr!
Die Nachrichten oder Sagen über die Abreisen der Monarchen von hier, wechseln wir Elte und Fluth.

Greise erst Anfangs May geschehen solle mit Deutschlands Angelegenheiten noch erst Absolut
zu Arrangiren wären. Beigelegene Nota ist eine Norm von den was geschehen wird.
Nicht jeder ist damit zufrieden besonders ist Harum ein Gegenstand des Streites

Ich schilderte den Prinzen und seine Umstände genau, und wünschte mir zu wißen

dH. B v Thule trug mir auf ihm Eine Durchlaucht ehrerbietigst zu empfehlen, und versicherte

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Conflicting rumours in Vienna about the monarchs' departure — one day the King of Prussia and the Russian Emperor leaving by Berlin for the army, the next not before May; an enclosed note on Germany's arrangements, with Harum a point of contention. Lieutenant-Colonel v. Thiel confirms the King will still be present on the 20th for Prince Adolph's presentation and request for army service, and that nothing departs before the 22nd. Reports Murat's advance to Mantua, Russian marches and Bonaparte's build-up around Paris; the Kosten estates still await an equivalent, and Zagorow is offered as security.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Lord! The reports, or rather the rumours, concerning the departures of the monarchs from this place change [uncertain: we] like ebb and flood. One hour it is said that within ☽ 4 days everything will set off — in the second one says the departure is weeks off, so it was said, for example, the day before yesterday — next Saturday the King of Prussia and the Russian Emperor go from here by way of Berlin to the army — Today it is asserted that their journey is only to take place at the beginning of May, since Germany's affairs would first have to be absolutely arranged. The enclosed note is a norm of what will happen. Not everyone is content with it; in particular Harum is an object of contention. I was, meanwhile, only to learn precisely by report whether the King too would still be here on the 20th, and consequently until Herr Adolph's arrival; therefore I went to the Lieutenant-Colonel v. Thiel. I made known to him Your Serene Highness's and the Prince's resolution, and begged him to tell the King that Prince Adolph would be here on the 20th, and would wish to be presented to His Majesty, whereupon he would beg to be placed in the army. I described the Prince and his circumstances exactly, and wished to know whether he would still find the King here. The Right Honourable Baron v. [uncertain: Thule / Thiel] charged me to commend him most reverently to Your Serene Highness, and assured me that the King would certainly take great pleasure in it — He added: he is of good race, and if the spirit of his brave father is not upon him, then the King is now making an important purchase — for the rest, I can assure you that we shall not depart from here before the 22nd — With Prince Hardenberg I have not yet spoken, nor been able to lay this before him; but then this is not his affair either — the Military Department and the Civil are two heterogeneous things. I held it my duty to report this to Your Serene Highness. Your affairs, despite the thousand fine assurances, still lie in the mire, and share their fate with many

King Murat is making himself very broad in Italy, and has already pushed forward as far as Mantua — The Austrians are still holding up very well, and many troops are hastening thither to combat this monster — The Russians seem [uncertain: we/as] to be stirring too; they are said to march without a rest-day, that is to say, they run for 3 days and on the 4th they are carried, which takes the place of the rest day — In short, from all sides gentlemen are hastening hither. Bonaparte, for his part, is likewise forming a fearsome army — he draws all his troops together in and about Paris, heaps up great stores and magazines, [uncertain: draws] small [lines] of entrenchments which are hard to break through. — Meanwhile, between the soldiers and the townspeople (the latter being much oppressed by the military) considerable scenes are said already to have occurred, and this may yet become a motive by which the bloodiest war can be greatly mitigated. The affair of the Kosten estates likewise causes much anxiety; one does not know at all where one is now to take hold of the matter. — An equivalent suited to the case must be given to Your Serene Highness — but who is to do it, and where is one to seek it? Everything writes and hunts about frightfully great labours, and calls this a bagatelle — [uncertain: Unfäng] I should like to become, that no creditor shall then strike his claws in; for that I stand surety to you — You may issue a mortgage upon me at Zagorow; there everything remains to him. Yes indeed. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Serene Highness's faithful and obedient servant. Vienna, the 15th of April 1815. v Triebenfeld
