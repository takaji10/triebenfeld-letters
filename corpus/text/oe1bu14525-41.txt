--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Hoym reassures the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen that his South Prussian estates are being handed over, blaming a misunderstanding, and encloses dispositions sent by express courier to the Posen and Petrikau chambers ordering the quickest possible transfer. He explains that the clergy have held the estates for the Treasury since 1 June 1796, may manage them only until Midsummer 1798, and that valuation assessments, not the surveys, now fix their maintenance allowances; existing leases should be tolerated to that term rather than risk compensation claims. Wieniec and Wolica are left to the Bishop of Cujavia.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Gracious Lord!

It is exceedingly agreeable to me that Your Serene Highness has been pleased, by Your most honoured letter of yesterday's date, to put me fully in the picture concerning the affair of Your South Prussian estates.

Mere misunderstanding lies at the bottom of it if Your Serene Highness has been in the least disquieted concerning their transfer, and in proof of this I need only append herewith a copy of the dispositions issued to the respective Chambers at Posen and Petrikau, and despatched by express couriers for the expediting of the matter, of the 26th of last month, in order to convince Your Serene Highness that I have most conscientiously ordered the speediest taking-over and transfer of all the estates, so as to see everything set right, if possible, during the time of [the] presence in the province. I have accordingly acted in this not only on Your behalf, as [I have] for the named gentlemen donatories, but have even instructed the respective Chambers as to the transfer earlier than the instrument of grant concerning the last estates was made out. Your Serene Highness may therefore also, and indeed from Midsummer of this year onward, regard Yourself simply as sole lord and proprietor [uncertain: his own] of the same, of the [uncertain: ecclesiastical] estates reserved to Yourself by His Majesty. For the hitherto ecclesiastical possessors have since the 1st of June 1796 no longer been sitting on their own account but on account of the Treasury, in these estates already at that time drawn in to His Majesty's revenues. As soon as they receive their competence according to principle, they must vacate the estates any day, for they are already now sitting upon competence moneys and [illegible] them, and must pay out to the Treasury everything that goes beyond such. There is only this difference, that the clergy, who have hitherto remained in the

natural possession of the estates up to now, do not yet know, up to now and before the taking-over of their estates, how they are to receive their competence, and the reason lies in this, that with so great a quantity of such estates the valuation assessments could not all be prepared within this short time, nor the consolidations for the new domain offices be completed. The Bishop of Cujavia would therefore likewise have had to vacate the estates of Wieniec and Wolica, had Your Serene Highness not been graciously pleased to declare Yourself willing to leave them still to him. Probably, however, the following circumstance has given occasion to the misunderstanding: according to the first plan, all the estates in South Prussia which had been declared drawn in for the royal account already since the 1st of June 1796 were to have been taken from their then possessors on the 1st of June 1797; this was not possible for the reasons adduced above, and because through the numerous donations of estates the establishment of the domain offices was constantly [uncertain: delayed], and therefore the general determination was made that these hitherto possessors might continue to manage such estates until June 1798 in the manner hitherto, that is, for the account of the Treasury. Various of these possessors have made use of one estate or another by leasing it out, and if their lessees are de facto evicted, these demand compensation from their lessors, namely the clergy, and if these excuse themselves on the ground that not they but the Treasury threw them out of the lease, then the Treasury must compensate such lessees, and thereby the most prejudicial lawsuits arise for the royal treasury. Now the clergy may indeed be [in the case] of long-term lease contracts, but since they received only the authority to manage the estates, in the manner hitherto allowed, until Midsummer 1798, neither can the Treasury lawfully be expected to declare these leases valid for longer than up to this term. Perhaps in the whole body of estates which have been granted to Your Serene Highness there is no case in which a valid lease contract

exists between the hitherto possessors and a lessee; but should such a case nevertheless be found here or there, the lessee will probably be brought by amicable means to vacate. If this cannot be effected without disadvantage to You, then Your Serene Highness will Yourself be inclined rather to leave him in the lease until June 1798 and to receive the lease money instead, since otherwise the royal treasury would not only lose the revenues but would also have to hold the lessee harmless. Only with regard to the competence moneys is it to be observed that their determination, according to the [uncertain: Hohlinschen] surveys, turns out so low that particularly in the case of the monasteries the monks [illegible] could not subsist at all. His Majesty has therefore also commanded by cabinet order that the valuation assessments be taken as the basis, and since these are already low as against the true yield, the clergy thereby already suffer loss, inasmuch as by the royal declaration concerning the drawing-in of the estates everything has been assured to them that remains of the yield after deduction of 15 per cent administration costs and 50 per cent [uncertain: dues]. This detail lays before Your Serene Highness an exact survey of the established principles, and, with Your good way of thinking, You will find them just and equitable. Should You desire any special dispositions to the War and Domains Chamber, I most obediently beg to be informed of it, and it shall be exceedingly agreeable to me to be able to act entirely in accordance with Your wishes. For Your Serene Highness has proofs enough that it has for many years been my endeavour to be of service to You, and thereby also to lay down the most perfect veneration, in which I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's

Breslau, the 3rd of October

1797.

most obedient servant Hoym
