--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A short note from Gruemann, replying to a representation of the 23rd, which had asserted that the Prince of Hohenlohe undertook in the Settlement of 1811 to pay the capital of some 500,000 owed to the Courland heirs himself and to release the Lady Countess from her joint liability. Unable to locate the passage in the enclosed Settlement, he asks that it be marked in red chalk and returned by the bearer.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Your Right Honourable have, in your representation of the 23rd of [uncertain: Ors.] of this year, observed: that the Prince of Hohenlohe did, in the Settlement of 1811, undertake to pay himself the capital owed to the Courland heirs of [text lost] 500000[uncertain] and to [uncertain: relieve] the Lady Countess of her joint obligation for — I therefore most respectfully request you, since I cannot find the passage in the enclosed Settlement, to be so obliging as to mark it with red chalk or otherwise, and thereupon most kindly to send it back to me by the bearer. Commending myself most respectfully, Gruemann[uncertain] herein, the 7th of Oct. 1814
