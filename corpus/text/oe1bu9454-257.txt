--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Gruemann questions a claim made in a submission of the 23rd that the Prince of Hohenlohe had undertaken, in an 1811 settlement, to pay the capital owed to the Courland heirs (a figure of some 500,000) himself and to release the Countess from joint liability. Unable to locate any such passage, he returns the settlement and asks that the wording be marked in red chalk and sent back by the bearer. A brief, damaged note.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Your Right Honourable have observed in your representation of the 23rd of the [uncertain: present] month of this year:

that the Prince of Hohenlohe, in the settlement of 1811, undertook to pay himself the capital sum owing to the Courland heirs of [text lost] 500000[uncertain] and to [uncertain: relieve] the Countess of the joint obligation. I therefore most respectfully request that you be so kind as to mark the passage in the enclosed settlement with red chalk or otherwise, since I cannot find it, and then graciously send it back to me by the bearer. Commending myself most respectfully,

Gruemann[uncertain]

therein, the 7th of October 1814
