--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Copy of an appeal, transmitting a further legal opinion drawn from the records and pleading the Prince's cause against Cosmar's obstruction: if Your Serene Highness will merely give written hope that 112,000 Rthl for the settlement with the L. heirs be advanced, however not in cash, the compromise with the Mandatarii can be concluded at once, the Transact and codicil fall away, the guarantee and the 35,000 Rthl advanced in 1790 return, and the Crown avoids a loss of 461,000 Rthl. Delay is dangerous; the Prince is some 66 years old and worn down.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Copy. Most Serene Prince, etc. The E. E. zu höherliche has, in the matter lying before his E. H. D., had a further opinion drawn up out of the records which lie with the Brugen OCG, by the man of law already made known to Your Serene Highness, in order to see the matter quite clearly. He has sent this to me with the charge to lay it, subsequently, [uncertain: drotest] at [Your] feet. His just complaint over his boundless unhappy situation, in which the M. R. holds him in fetters, cuts through every feeling heart, and for all this reason I venture to comply with the Prince's charge and to trouble Your S. D.[illegible]. Have mercy, most gracious [Prince] and Lord, that [upon] this suffering, upright man. May Your Serene Highness deign only to grant the Prince the written hope that the 112000 Rthl for the settlement with the L. heirs shall be advanced (though not in cash at this moment); then the Prince is placed in a position to conclude the compromise at once in the most legally binding manner with the [uncertain: Q] Mandatarii, and everything will resolve itself of its own accord — for more than that, thereafter, the Maj[orat] estates would have to be administered for the King's account. The Trans[act] and the codicil, of those matters which was chiefly for that reason separated from the loc., in order to deprive the Monarch of Mb. d. and also other creditors of what is theirs, then fall of themselves. He M. receives back the guarantee at once, without it costing the King a heller, and taken also still the 35000 Rthl advanced to the Prince in 1790. Most submissively, however, I venture to observe that every delay brings danger, since the said Cosmar [makes] his web firmer and confuses the matter daily more, etc.; and the Prince is some 66 years of age, bowed down by care and worn down, and might well succumb to the misery, according to the wish of Cosmar, etc., and of the young Prince. For nothing is registered against the Majorat estates; the 2 heirs, who have on their side the guarantee of the late most blessed Hr. M. / by whose Most High [authority] the debts were originally contracted / and who also drew the interest until 1806 from the royal treasury, would rightly obtain thereby that he M. must pay, and in that manner the Monarch would lose, inclusive of the 35000 Rthl, 461000 Rthl. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Vienna, 19th November 1814, most humble and obedient most faithful servant v Triebenfeld
