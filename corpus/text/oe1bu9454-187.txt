--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that powers of attorney and blank forms have arrived and been used: with help from a General and others the Lord High Chancellor will advance 90,000 Rthl from the Justice Fund to satisfy the most pressing creditors Weigel, the Schlabrendorff woman and Lichnowski, freeing 30,000 Rthl for the Prince. Complains that the Zagorowo and Trąbczyn mortgage certificate will bear no further loans, that Oppenheimer has paid only 32,000 of 113,600 Rthl, and that Meyer Bernhard is suing despite royal approval of the Betsche lease; sets out a journey to Betsche, Kalisz and Trąbczyn to raise money.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

The powers of attorney and the blank forms have arrived; of the first I have at once made the necessary use, and I am now assured of the best success – through the mediation of the worthy General and of the other gentleman – the Lord High Chancellor will give 90000 Rthl out of the Justice Fund – out of this I shall pay the 3 most restive creditors – that is, Weigel, the Schlabrendorff woman, and Lichnowski. The last-named must return to me 20000 Rthl against a Schweiner mortgage which I pledge to him; 10000 Rthl I must contrive to raise in this way, and then Your Serene Highness will receive the desired 30000 Rthl. It is a bad thing that the mortgage certificate of the Zagorowo and Trąbczyn estates is so motley; no one will lend further upon it – what is given is against Jura Caisa. In time, however, all of it will be made to work out.

My sole endeavour is to be rid soon of Oppenheimer, who last year promised to give 160000 Rthl and who worked upon Amelang

only, so that I may get back the mortgage of 250000 Rthl.

The scoundrel had claims of his own upon Olesnica. 20000 fl. He undertook to pay the Invalids' Fund 50000 Rthl, Lichnowski

36000

Meyer Bernhard

7600.

Which is 113600 Rthl. Of all these he has paid only 32000 Rthl to Lignowchen, together with the money which Your Serene Highness received at Fürth, and notwithstanding this the fellow reckons the whole Pro[uncertain: x]enitirum[uncertain: Pro?enitirum] at 11000 Rthl. Once I am clear of Weigel and the others, I shall apply everything to paying this Gmür what he has actually given, and shall at once procure the return of the above-named mortgage, which is thereby annulled eo ipso, whereby the matter then takes on a quite different shape.

Today I go to Beteche, and there to set everything in order, and above all to stop the mouth of that wretched Jew, Meyer Bernhard. The fellow has brought an action against Your Serene Highness, notwithstanding that I had made it known to the knave that His Majesty had approved the Betsche

lease and had removed all difficulties in such a manner that Your Serene Highness were, as it was said, ex nexu.

From Betsche I go on to Kalisz and Trąbczyn, arrange everything there, see what money I can drive together in that quarter, and hasten with it back here in order to smooth out the principal business. Eighteen days from today I expect to be in Berlin again, and if all now goes as wished, then I shall perhaps myself bring to Öhringen the sum Your Serene Highness desires.

The constellations, for the rest, are at present very evil, and it is much wished here that Your Serene Highness might only be quite fully restored to health very soon, and that you would take your return journey by way of Berlin. Through my long stay here I was lately very nearly placed in a favourable [uncertain: Lutuation]. The Glogau Government had ordered that the 30000 Rthl standing upon Kontop be paid to the von Wulffen woman. The mandates had been left lying in Breslau, so that I knew nothing of it until it was brought down on my neck here. Meanwhile the upright General helped at once – he gave me in cash what I needed, and the Lord High Chancellor had 200000 Rthl paid upon one mortgage, and thus these two honest men furnished me – Now I have complete quiet and can attend to all Your Serene Highness's affairs.

With unbounded veneration I remain as

Your Serene Highness's

Berlin, the 17th of June

1806.

faithful, humble and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld
