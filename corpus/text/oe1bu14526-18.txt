--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Hereditary-lease and sale of the whole lordship of Betsche by Prince Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, through his general attorney building councillor Johann Wilhelm Glenck, to the burgesses of Betsche and the villages of Stalluhn, Kulligowo, Schartzig, Stocki, Schwichodzyn and Lowin, represented by Matheus Młynkiewicz and Jan Chmara: 180,000 Rthl purchase sum plus 1,000 Rthl annual ground-rent, largely discharged by assuming 137,000 Rthl of intabulated mortgages. Reserved are jurisdiction, hunt, Laudemium, castle and forest supervision; a Polish interpreter, Echaust, attended. With royal confirmation of 1806.

the proceedings of the 28th of July of this year, as also the supplement of the 29th of July of this year, set out more fully, reading thus: Actum Betsche, the 28th of July 1804. There appeared

1, the Princely Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen general attorney, building councillor Glenck, to whom His Most Serene Highness has assigned the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, hereditary lord of Schweinerne and Kontop, as His Most High friend and assistant.

2, the burgher and town representative Matheus Młynkiewicz as authorised representative of the burgesses of Betsche,

3, the free village mayor Johann Chmara of Stalluhn as authorised representative of the village communities of Stalluhn, Kulligowo, Schartzig, Stocki, Schwiechodzyn, Lowin

with the motion to record a contract between His Most Serene Highness the reigning Prince Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, the burgesses of Betsche and the village communities named above, on the basis of the heads of agreement dated Alt Schartzig, the 16th of July 1804. The Princely Hohenlohe attorney, the building councillor Johann Wilhelm Glenck, produces the general power of attorney of the Prince of Hohenlohe of the 26th of April 1803, according to which he is authorised to conclude agreements in valid form, of which power of attorney an attested copy was taken to these acts.

In like manner Młynkiewicz also submits a judicial power of attorney of the 5th of April 1804, according to which he is authorised, in the name of the town, to conclude with the reigning Prince of

Hohenlohe, His Serene Highness, a contract over all the plots of ground and profitable rights belonging to the lordship of Betsche.

Likewise Chmara submits a judicial power of attorney of the 5th of April 1804, according to which he is authorised by the communities of Stalluhn, Kulligowo, Schartzig, Stoki, Schwiechoczyn, Lowin, on the same footing as Młynkiewicz, to conclude a contract. After negotiations carried on between the parties, and after the heads of agreement of the 16th of July 1804 had been produced, and the personal capacity of the appearing parties to conclude contracts was subject to no doubt, the contract was recorded as follows.

§ 1. His Most Serene Highness the reigning Prince Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, Governor at Breslau, General Inspector of Infantry and Knight of the great Royal Prussian Orders, as hereditary lord of the lordship of Betsche situated in the district of Meseritz, makes over to the burgesses of the town of Betsche and the inhabitants of the villages of Kulligowo, Stalluhn, Schärtzig, Stoki, Schwichodczyn, Lowin, all the demesne farms belonging to them, fields, gardens, meadows, marshes, lakes, ponds, pond-sites, rivers, pasturage, heaths, shops, forests, woods, thickets, brushwood, money and payments in kind, the propination rights, the brewing and distilling rights, brick and lime kilns, the Obrn demesne farm, in short each and every profitable right and privilege which the Prince of Hohenlohe,

His Serene Highness, has hitherto as proprietor used and enjoyed, and can, may and shall enjoy, in Pausch und Bogen, by whatever name they may be called, in particular also the right of pre-emption over the village mayor's holdings and demesne farms granted under hereditary quit-rent tenure, excepting the jurisdiction and the transfer fee (Laudemium), and the hunt, which His Most Serene Highness reserves to Himself and His Most High successors as supreme proprietor and perpetual ground-lord of Betsche, as their true hereditary property and possession, in such form and manner that the said town commune of Betsche and the mentioned localities may deal with it as with their own property, may sell, exchange and give it away, can, may and shall, for a

§ 4. The handing over follows as soon as the 28000 rtl. which are to be paid on the 5th of August have been paid. Since however the former tenant Bornstädt is still in possession of the demesne farm here, and a lawsuit over his ejection is pending, the

communities do not indeed insist that possession of the demesne farm be granted to them at once, but will rather be content that, so long as the judgment of ejection is not legally final, he remain therein and be evicted only when the judgment of ejection is legally final; yet for this there shall be given to the communities by His Serene Highness the Prince a fair and suitable compensation proportionate to the matter. As regards on the other hand the forests, these shall at once be closed, as indeed they already are now, and the timber felled therein, insofar as it has not been felled for the husbandry, remains the property of His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen.

§ 5. All the manorial dwelling, husbandry,

forest and other buildings are made over to the communities likewise as hereditary property, yet the communities are bound to leave the foresters the dwelling of one room in the foresters' houses until Michaelmas of this year, as also the use of their gardens. His Serene Highness also reserves the castle as the seat of the court, the dwelling of the court servant, and a dwelling for His Most High self or His commissioners, also stabling for 2 horses, which they themselves maintain in repair. In the case of building works on this edifice, the communities are bound to let His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen and his successors have the bricks at cost price, and to deliver the timber for the construction from the heath free of charge. Also

the communities are bound to furnish and cart in, free of charge from the forest, the wood needful for heating the courtroom and the court servant's quarters. In like manner the communities are also bound, when His Most Serene Highness or his commissioners are present here, to furnish and cart in the needful firewood free of charge.

§ 6. As regards the timber purchase contracts concluded between His Most Serene Highness and certain merchants, His Most High self promises to satisfy the timber purchasers, without the communities making any contribution thereto.

§ 7. The town and village communities mentioned at the outset undertake, upon alienations of their

plots of ground, to render 10 per cent transfer fee (Laudemium) on the purchase sum to His Most Serene Highness and his successors in possession. From these transfer fees, however, are excepted those holdings which the communities now intend to lay out; but if these are sold in future, then the transfer fee of 10 per cent is likewise to be rendered thereon.

§ 8. Further, His Most Serene Highness reserves that, when His Most High self or his successors in possession come hither, or send their commissioners hither, the communities are bound to provide the relay teams over a distance of 4 miles out and back.

§ 9. Further, His Serene Highness reserves, should he

obtain the right of patronage over the church at Betsche, the right of patronage to himself; yet the town and village communities shall have the right to choose the clergyman and to propose him, which proposed clergyman His Most Serene Highness [promises] to present to the episcopal consistory.

§ 10. promise.

The lawsuits between the community of Stocki and the Prince of Hohenlohe over ownership of the Stocki marsh; between the Prince of Hohenlohe and the possessor of Zilomyśl[?] over the right of admission; and between the district administrator von Schmaeling as possessor of the demesne farm in Głazewo and His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe over confirmation of the privilege; as also the

lawsuit between the Prince of Hohenlohe and Hertzig in Lowino over restitution of the Szalestwo, the communities take over at their own risk and cost, and whatever they may win at law or obtain by way of settlement falls to the communities, without His Serene Highness having the least claim thereto.

§ 11. The competence taxes and other public and further dues hitherto rendered by His Serene Highness as the manorial lordship (Dominium) of Betsche, whatever(5) name(6) they(7) may(8) bear(2) (3) and costs(4), among which also belong the carriage of the sick into the Schiller and Puncten Hauländer, as also the carriage for fetching and conveying away the court, and by whatever other name they may be called, the mentioned town and village communities take over, and the latter, should these dues be

raised in future, can demand no compensation from His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe; the communities also take over the contribution of 50 rthl. which

His Most Serene Highness promised to render to the town school here for 1 year.

§ 12. Both contracting parties undertake to have this contract publicly proclaimed before the competent authorities and to seek confirmation; and His Most Serene Highness herewith binds himself to exert himself as best he may, that this contract be confirmed by the competent authorities, and that the communities also be granted the consent for the further dismemberment of what has been made over to them and [text lost].

§ 13. As soon as the delivery of possession of the Obra demesne farm here

takes place, there shall at the same time be handed over to the town and village communities that livestock and husbandry inventory which is included in the sale under the purchase and hereditary-tenure sum, in the number in which the tenant von Bornstädt received the same in the year 1800 on the handing over.

§ 14. This contract shall be binding for all time upon His Most Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen for himself and his Most High heirs and successors, as also on the part of the town and village communities. And the annual canon or ground-rent of 1000 rtl. to be paid shall be rendered in half-yearly instalments of 500 rtl., that is, each time at the term of Christmas and the term of St John the Baptist, yearly, sub paena executionis, and sent carriage

free to Breslau.

§ 15. So long as the communities do not alienate or dismember the plots of ground and rights made over to them, the status possessivus remains in its present condition. But as soon as the demesne farm is dismembered, the hereditary purchasers are bound to have the intabulated items transferred onto the plots of ground and struck off in the name of His Most Serene Highness.

§ 16. For the stipulated canon of 1000 rtl. yearly all the communities are liable in solidum and consent to the entry thereof in the mortgage book upon their plots of ground, just as on the other hand His Most Serene Highness also consents that this present contract

be entered in the mortgage book of the manorial lordship (Dominium) of Betsche and appurtenances.

§ 17. Finally the contracting parties renounce each and every objection, by whatever name they may be called; in particular the town and village communities renounce the plea of injury beyond the half, which was explained to them as meaning that, if the agreed purchase and hereditary-tenure sum should exceed the true value of the plots of ground and rights made over by double, it would be open to them to withdraw from the contract.

§ 18. His Most Serene Highness reserves to himself the supervision over the forests, which he will entrust to the Royal head forester Feller, and the communities undertake to compensate the latter for

his exertions and to come to agreement with him on that account.

Thereupon, since there was nothing further to adduce, the proceedings were closed, the protocol read out, interpreted by the sworn interpreter Echaust to the deputies of the town and village communities, who are not perfectly versed in the German tongue and had requested the keeping of a separate subsidiary protocol in the Polish tongue, approved and signed; whereby it was further noted by the contracting parties that the costs of the recording, engrossment, proclamation and confirmation shall be borne by both parties in halves.
