--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Vidimated engrossment of the hereditary-lease and sale of the lordship of Betsche in the Meseritz district by Fürst Friedrich Ludwig zu Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, through General Plenipotentiary Johann Wilhelm Glenk, to the town of Betsche and the villages Stalluhn, Kulligowo, Schartzig, Stocki, Schwichodzyn and Lowin, represented by Matheus Młynkiewicz and Freyschulze Jan Chmara: 180,000 Reichsthaler plus 1,000 rtl annual canon, 137,000 rtl of it by assuming intabulated mortgages, with reserved jurisdiction, hunt, Laudemium and castle; appended is the supplement of 29 July and the royal approval of the dismemberment.

After negotiations conducted on both sides, and after the Punctation of the 16th of July 1804 had been produced, and the personal competence of the appearing parties to conclude contracts was subject to no doubt, the contract was drawn up as follows.

§ 4. The handing over follows as soon as the 28000 rtl., which are to be paid on the 5th of August, have been paid. Since however the former tenant Bornstaedt is still in possession of the demesne farm here, and a lawsuit is pending over his eviction, the

communes do not indeed insist that possession of the demesne farm be conceded to them at once, but are rather willing to allow that until the judgment of eviction is legally binding he shall remain therein, and only when the judgment of eviction is legally binding shall he be evicted; yet for this the communes shall be given by the Prince, Serene Highness, a fair and appropriate compensation suited to the matter. As regards the forests, on the other hand, these shall be closed at once, as indeed they already are now, and the timber felled therein, in so far as it has not been felled for the estate economy, remains the property of His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen.

§ 5. All manorial dwelling, farm, forest

[forest] and other buildings are made over to the communes likewise as hereditary property; the communes are however bound to leave the foresters their dwelling in one room in the foresters' houses until Michaelmas of the current year, as also the use of their gardens. His Serene Highness also reserves the castle as the seat of the court, the dwelling of the court servant, and a dwelling for His Highness Himself or His commissioners, also stabling for 2 horses, which they shall themselves maintain in good structural repair. In the case of building works on this edifice, the communes are bound to let the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, Serene Highness, and his successors have the bricks at cost price and to furnish the timber for the construction from the heath free of charge. Also are

the communes bound to furnish free of charge from the forest, and to cart in, the timber needed for heating the courtroom and the court servant's quarters. In like manner the communes are also bound, when His Most Serene Highness or His commissioners are present here, to furnish and cart in the necessary firewood free of charge.

§ 6. As regards the timber-purchase contracts concluded between His Most Serene Highness and certain merchants, His Highness promises to satisfy the timber purchasers without the communes making any contribution thereto.

§ 7. The town and village communes mentioned at the outset undertake, upon alienations of their

parcels of land, to render 10 per cent Laudemium of the purchase sum to His Most Serene Highness and his successors in possession. From these Laudemia are however excepted those holdings which the communes now intend to lay out; but if these are sold in future, the Laudemium of 10 per cent shall likewise be rendered thereon.

§ 8. Further His Most Serene Highness reserves that, if He Himself or his successors in possession should come hither, or should send their commissioners hither, the communes are bound to render the Vorspann conveyance over a distance of 4 miles thither and back.

§ 9. Further His Serene Highness reserves, should he

obtain the right of patronage over the Betsche church, that right of patronage; yet the town and village communes shall have the right to elect the clergyman and to propose him, which proposed clergyman His Most Serene Highness shall present to the episcopal Consistory.

This contract shall be binding for His Most Serene Highness the Prince zu Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen for Himself and His Most High heirs and successors, as also on the part of the town and village communes, for all time. And the annual canon or ground-rent of 1000 rtl. to be paid shall be rendered in half-yearly instalments of 500 rtl., namely each time at the term of Christmas and the term of St. John the Baptist, yearly, sub paena Executionis, and sent carriage free to Bres-

§ 15. So long as the communes do not alienate or dismember the parcels of land and rights made over to them, the status possessivus remains in its present condition. But as soon as the demesne farm is dismembered, the hereditary purchasers are bound to have the intabulated items transferred onto the parcels of land and struck off in the name of His Most Serene Highness.

§ 16. For the stipulated canon of 1000 rtl. yearly all the communes are liable in solidum, and they allow the entry thereof in the mortgage book upon their parcels of land, just as on the other hand His Most Serene Highness allows that this present contract

be entered in the mortgage book of the Dominium Betsche and its appurtenances.

§ 17. Finally the contrahentes renounce each and every objection, by whatever names they may be called; in particular the town and village communes renounce the plea of injury beyond the half, which was explained to them as meaning that if the agreed purchase and entry sum exceeded the true value of the parcels of land and rights made over by double, they would be free to withdraw from the contract.

§ 18. His Most Serene Highness reserves the supreme oversight over the costs, which He will entrust to the Royal Chief Forester Feller, and the communes undertake to indemnify him for

his exertions and to come to agreement with him thereon.

Thus, as nothing further was to be adduced, the proceedings were closed, the protocol read aloud, interpreted by the sworn interpreter Eichaust for the deputies of the town and village communes not fully conversant with the German language, who had requested the keeping of a separate side-protocol in the Polish language, approved and signed; whereby it was further noted by the contrahentes that the costs of the drawing up, the engrossment, the publication and the confirmation shall be borne in halves by both parties. Neumann (Copy) Friedrich Wilhelm, King of Prussia, p. p. Our gracious greeting first. Whereas the investigation occasioned by the dismemberment and alienation of the lordship of Betsch to the burghers and inhabitants there, initiated by the Prince zu Hohenlohe-Ingelfin-

-gen, has shown that the hereditary-lease acquirers will be able to subsist under the contract concluded with them and to remain in a lasting and prestation-capable condition, and that the public dues resting upon the lordship are secured, We have Ourselves Most Highly, upon the enquiry made to Us on this account, now, by the tenor of the Cabinet Resolution of the 29th of last month, been pleased to grant Our approval to the said alienation and dismemberment in the manner initiated; and since We have found nothing to object to in the applications made by the hereditary acquirers, that

1, their society be always regarded as a district estate, which through

the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen is represented, and whose rights have been exercised in his name by them;
