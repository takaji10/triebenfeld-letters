--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld bittet per Estaffette um Hilfe: Die Gemeinden in Pszczew wollen, von Rochlitz aufgehetzt, vom Kauf zurücktreten. Verlangt werden die Cassation des Contracts zwischen Leixner und v. Brandenstein, Glencks Herausgabe des Societaets Contracts und eine Abmachung mit Bornstädt. Der Fürst soll Glenck die Papiere abfordern und Brandensteins Revers schicken. Triebenfeld fragt, was er Bornstädt wegen der zugesagten 1000 Friedrichsd'or antworten soll. Glenck habe Meyer Bernhard abgeraten, die 66/m Rthl am 29. in Posen zu zahlen.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld asks for help by express courier: the village communities at Pszczew, stirred up by Rochlitz, want to withdraw from the purchase. What is demanded is the cancellation of the contract between Leixner and v. Brandenstein, Glenck's surrender of the partnership contract, and an arrangement with Bornstädt. The Prince is to demand the papers from Glenck and send Brandenstein's written undertaking. Triebenfeld asks what he should reply to Bornstädt about the promised 1,000 Friedrich d'or. He says Glenck has advised Meyer Bernhard against paying the 66/m Rthl on the 29th in Poznań.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

My first resolve was to report no more unpleasant matters whatsoever to Your Most Serene Highness, and to let things which could no longer be altered take their course. Compelled by necessity, however, I must give up this resolve and [uncertain: recta mirn] Your Most Serene Highness's help — the matter is this.

Before I came here I received the letter of von Bornstädt at Poznań, enclosed under Letter A. I had no good opinion of the writer and sought to humble him by having a mere acknowledgment of receipt given him through Glenck, to the effect that a letter from Mr von Bornstädt had come in in His Serene Highness's affairs, upon which he, as general attorney, would give him an answer.

At Poznań I then further sought to win over Melzer and the [uncertain: Pre: Braunschweig] and to do everything possible so that the Berlin order might be upheld and the monies deposited, but above all that Broniewski might be evicted at once. This was promised me, and I travelled on that same night here to Pszczew, sent a mounted messenger at once to Frankfurt an der Oder and summoned Meyer Bernhard and associates here. The enclosed copy under B says the rest.

After this expedition [uncertain: bis] I had the deputies who were at Wrocław called; it was in the highest degree strange to me that not one of them would appear; at length I learned that the people had been stirred up to withdraw from the purchase. I sought to find out the ground of it, and heard to my horror that Rochlitz was confusing the people's heads and threatening them, to tear the whole thing apart, because he [uncertain: um] his brother-in-law Klein had a contract in his hands under which they would take over the

demesne farm [uncertain: Abre] and the greater part of the forest.

The justice-burgomaster Melzig, who is appointed as the community's assistant, at last declared to me quite straight out that Mr von Glenck was intent on drawing the whole negotiation to Wrocław, that he had large claims which he would assert, and that he, Glenck, if he, Melzig, would side with him, would be very appreciative. He, Melzig, further declared that he did indeed see that Your Serene Highness was to be cheated, but that as the communities' assistant he had to insist upon it

that, first, this matter be entirely removed, and that, secondly, everything be first settled with Mr von Bornstädt; before that the communities can pay no money and enter into nothing. After this sorry experience I received the letter of von Bornstädt enclosed under C, and learned besides from several residents that

Mr Glenck had asked them to withdraw from the contract and to annul it; he would make them all happy for it. Why Glenck does this remains a riddle.

For the rest I awaited his and Wunster's arrival — Glenck's followed the day before yesterday. I took pains to probe everything in the gentlest way, but got nothing further out of him than that he knows nothing, inasmuch as he lacked the papers; to the question where the contract concluded between him and Leixner was, he answers that Lieutenant von Brandenstein has it in his keeping, and would press for his estate or the money for it.

But the enclosed letter of von Bornstädt under D proves that Glenck has lied to me and that the contract has already been ceded to the merchant Klein, a cousin of Rochlitz. I still doubted, and sought to arrange everything with the people in the best way, until I received the enclosed note under C from Meyer Bernhard. This moves me to send this express courier in haste to Your Serene Highness.

Both the communities and the Frankfurt men insist that this contract concluded between Leixner and Mr von Brandenstein be above all things produced [uncertain: um] wholly cancelled. Secondly, Bernhard and Leixner will pay not a Groschen further until Glenck gives up the partnership contract concluded between them.

Thirdly, the communities insist that everything be settled with Bornstedt; before that they will agree to nothing further. My advice [uncertain: um] request is this, that Your Most Serene Highness write to Glenck by express courier that he immediately give up all these papers, and that both he and Mr von Brandenstein give a judicial written undertaking as to the cancellation of both contracts, that they can or may claim nothing further from the Frankfurt gentlemen — or from the Pszczew lands. Von Brandenstein, who can hardly know anything of all Mr Glenck's twistings, will certainly issue the undertaking there at once, and I humbly beg that Your Serene Highness may not withdraw your grace from this young man, who may well be quite innocent

in the matter. Nor shall I grow weary in this in my [uncertain: Erster].

May Your Most Serene Highness be pleased to send me the written undertaking from Mr von Brandenstein here by the express courier with all possible haste. For otherwise I can settle nothing with the people.

But let Your Highness command Glenck that he immediately procure back the ceded contract here, and hand it over together with the partnership contract, and judicially renounce all claims.

I would gladly have spared Your Serene Highness this business. But I cannot get through otherwise, for the people insist upon it.

With Bornstädt everything looks frightfully confused. Glenck, Rochlitz, [uncertain: Werlitz], Bornstädt, and whatever else the rabble are called, have all along squabbled like tinkers under Your Serene Highness's eyes, but at bottom they have all cheated in brotherly concord, so that anyone's hair would stand on end. It was always Your Serene Highness whom they would all gladly have plundered clean.

The forest is quite ruined, and had the thieves gone on housekeeping here another year, the fine estates would not have been worth 50/m any longer — Now I am at pains to outflank Bornstädt really soundly. He seemed to be making use of the sale to the subjects; but these I have brought so far that they, against my guarantee, will let him [uncertain: sit] until the matter is settled. Then I shall no doubt get the better of the hero — It is a shame to me that such infamies occur in the files; thus Bornstädt says, for example — On

entering upon my lease I had to pay Glenck 500 Rthl as a broker's fee; another tenant promised him more after a year's term, and for that reason he wanted to drive me out, so as to extort another 600 Rthl. It must indeed be untrue, he says further, that the Prince, as Glenck boasts of himself, knows of such swindling — etc., for the Prince has after all the reputation of the most exalted and greatest of men and a friend of mankind — I could go mad when such a thing comes before my eyes, and I can wish nothing further than to set aside all these dirty things and to get them out of the world in such a way that Your Serene Highness may have no further [uncertain: Ver dens].

What torments me most at present is this — Your Serene Highness has promised Bornstädt 1,000 pieces of Friedrich d'or if he helps to sell Pszczew for 200/m Rthl. Glenck has confirmed this here before Melzig. What am I, poor man, now to answer to that? The fellow tenders the oath on the point to Your Serene Highness — to Glenck and Rochlitz. What am I to answer the fellow on this, and with what am I to satisfy him? Be pleased, most gracious Prince, to give me an instruction in the matter.

The lease business I would settle at law, and by God conceal nothing, but in this affair I can do nothing further without Your Serene Highness's command. Generals, and God knows what people, all take an interest in the poor wretch and his wife, who seems to be a true [uncertain: [k?]antigen]

to be; therefore I wish to be rid of the wretched B. in a decent manner.

As regards the timber contracts it looks just as ghastly — for so far as [uncertain: da seid], they belonged to Rochlitz under other names — the name-bearers now all cry out that they want no money but the sums of timber fixed in the contracts. This is now again to be a motive for annulling the purchase with the subjects. But here I shall speak loudly to the rogues. For the whole communities will come forward and prove the villains' infamies aloud — Your Serene Highness will be astonished at all this knavery.

This very moment I learn of a new intrigue of Glenck's — I had, as said above, brought Meyer Bernhard so far that he meant to be at Poznań on the evening of the 29th or early on the 30th with the 66/m Rthl. At this moment Wunster tells me that Glenck has talked Meyer Bernhard out of paying the money on the 29th, and into going to Wrocław and asking Your Serene Highness what he should do — for it would not be necessary before the 2nd of August that the money be paid over to Broniewski.

The Jew now twists about again, says he would do it, for he has so many claims upon Your Serene Highness; we all sit here now like fools — It is more than too bad

what these people permit themselves — As regards the Kamionna and Kolno dismemberment I can likewise do nothing for the present. For Glenck has already betrayed everything to him / of which the enclosed letter of [uncertain: Liebt] is the proof / everything, in order to earn something. O God! what shameful people are these? I shall see what the Jew will do; if he insists on the journey to Wrocław, then I shall only see how I can get half-way finished with the subjects here, and shall come there in due time too, but then I beg Your Serene Highness also most heartily to work all these B. over really roughly, so that they may at last be brought to respect and drop their accursed machinations. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's — May Your Most Serene Highness be pleased to send the express courier with all the papers to old Reinhardt at Poznań. If I can still turn everything about, I shall be there — if not, then he must send them to me here. Ah, it is true torment of hell to bring these people to reason. The letter to my daughter I most submissively beg may be delivered, and if anything has come in there from South Prussia etc., that it may be sent along to me by the express courier. Pszczew, the 26th of July

1804

faithful, humble and obedient servant

v Triebenfeld
