--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Appeals to Colonel von Köckritz to press his petition on the King after the proposed exchange of estates collapsed and left his honour impugned. Explains that he sought the Krotoszyn estates because the nobleman von Karnhowsky would advance 90000 ducats only on such security; defends dropping Polajewo; and disputes Minister von Voss's report, on Councillor of War Nöldichen's advice, that the State would lose 17400 Rthl, insisting Krotoszyn yields only 30100 Rthl against his own estates' 41500 Rthl.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
To Colonel von Köckritz

Right Honourable Sir etc.

The exchange of estates in question has taken a more than disagreeable turn for me, such that I find myself in a pitiable situation. My purest intention was to rid myself of the debts weighing upon me without becoming burdensome to the State in any manner whatsoever, so as to devote my services to the King undisturbed, and without being plagued by domestic cares, for as long as my strength should last. The estates conferred upon me by the Most Serene Monarch turned out, after I had expended some 50000 Rthl upon them, so productive that I at once formed the resolution to employ them for the greater part in order to borrow a sum upon them, so as to pay the debts incurred during my years of service.

But every attempt failed me, since unspeakable obstacles were sought out, particularly on account of the obstructiveness of the Chambers, so that I should never have attained my object. A South Prussian nobleman named von Karnhowsky, who was willing to advance 90000 ducats, declared frankly "that upon all my estates, which were in part confiscated and for the greater part taken from the clergy, he could give nothing. If, however, I could furnish him, for his security, with estates such as those of Krotoszyn, then he was willing and ready to advance me this and an even greater sum." Hereupon the wish stirred in me to acquire the Krotoszyn estates for myself.

On the part of the Posen Chamber it was believed that Polajewo was inseparable from Krotoszyn, and therefore I made to the King, as Your Excellency knows, the proposal that was made. Several men, however, were again of the opinion that Polajewo could not be made over to me for ready money, and I at once wrote to Minister von Voss that I would renounce Polajewo and wished to exchange only the Krotoszyn estates for those conferred upon me. The King gave only a gracious answer, and all other knowledgeable men regarded the matter as settled, until at length first Minister von Voss and thereafter the King only wrote that this exchange was to the detriment of the State, inasmuch as the Krotoszyn estates yielded 17400 Rthl more than mine.

In this matter I now appear in a light as though I had wished to enrich myself at the cost

of the State as well, and to have played a dishonourable and self-serving part. I cannot and shall not rest content in this until the matter is brought entirely into the clear and this false appearance is [uncertain: rumoured]. I gladly renounce every good fortune; only in my honour can I not allow myself to be injured. In the matter of the exchange I have done nothing without having first informed myself fully of everything.

I had the assurance from the Chambers and from all the world that Krotoszyn had never really yielded, nor could yield, more than 30,000 Rthl, because the town has too many prerogatives prejudicial to the manorial lordship (Dominium).

I heard that Minister von Voss was by no means willing to take over the administration of these estates if he were to render up 30 from Krotoszyn and 20000 Rthl annually from Polajewo.

Minister Count von Höym further assured me that this was a noble proposal on my part, one truly suited to the Royal interest, because the King would thereby see himself covered for the future with respect to the guarantee given to the Maritime Trading Company, and would at bottom be rid of a business which, sooner or later, were it to remain in Royal hands, might yet be censured in the ugliest fashion by Goerne and his heirs and take a disagreeable turn.

I learned moreover that many boundary suits were still pending, and particularly with General Count Kalbreuth, which look so unpromising that through them yet a great part of Krotoszyn might be torn away. However dreadful some of these pictures may appear even to myself, I nevertheless formed the resolution to propose this exchange to the King all the more readily — since it was said that I was acting nobly and in accordance with the Royal interest.

It is now more than striking to me that Minister von Voss told the King

that the State would thereby lose some 17400 Rthl annually. I cannot and shall not rest content under this portrayal by Mr. von Voss; I renounce Krotoszyn, as I have said, if it is believed that I gain anything more thereby than the opportunity to negotiate moneys upon it.

It is demonstrable that Krotoszyn yields no more than 30100 Rthl, and that, according to the statement of Councillor of War von Coeln, by every art it can at most one day be brought up to 36000 Rthl.

My estates, upon which, however, I must still pay some 70 to 80 thousand Rthl to two creditors, already yield some 41500 Rthl, for which I should have furnished the King with my warranty until his departments had themselves become convinced of it; and in this way the State would annually have come by more than 11000 Rthl, which I pledge upon my princely word. More than harsh and oppressive to me, therefore, is the treatment by Minister von Voss, who, upon the one-sided advice of Councillor of War Nöldichen, without instituting the least local enquiry into the one or the other estates, rendered to the King that report which rests for the greater part only on far-fetched and shallow speculations; and I have therefore, in vindication of my honour, this day petitioned the Monarch to have this matter investigated by an impartial person — and to have laid before him from Minister von Stoll ensée the files containing the correspondence between the late blessed King, Minister von Voss, von Struensee, and Count Höym concerning the taking over of the Krotoszyn and Polajewo estates, from which the rest will at once appear.

I beg Your Right Honourable most urgently to lay my peti-

tion to the good Monarch's heart, so that I may not have petitioned in vain. It is with the purest friendship and devotion that I have the honour to remain.

Breslau, the 7th of December 1798.
