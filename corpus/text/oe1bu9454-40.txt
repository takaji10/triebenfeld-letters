--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Fears further interception of letters; revises his itinerary via Wirr, Königsberg, Holland, Paris and Öhringen. Urges a loan of 250000 Rthl on the Zagorowo mortgage, with which 750000 Rthl of paper could be bought and all debts cleared, advances of 46000 Rthl to Oppenheimer, the Invalids' Fund and Meyer Bernhard deducted; 300000 Rthl of obligations bought for 90000 would settle the King's Lursch debt. Warns of enemies at Court, reports new valuations impossible, his own want of money and 4000 Rthl borrowed for von der Lahr, and French promises of military help to collect Zagorowo arrears.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince.

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

To Your Most Serene Highness I have not only answered promptly upon each of your most exalted letters, but have also sent off several further letters to your most exalted [self]. I am therefore in anxiety whether letters have not after all again been intercepted. With regard to my journey to Holland and Paris a small alteration has occurred; I must first go to [uncertain: Wirr], and when I return from the 2nd by way of Königsberg I am first to go to H. and Paris. The detour which I must make shall not deter me from laying myself at Your Serene Highness's feet here in Öhringen.

As concerns the loan business, I know of nothing more considerable and more profitable than this — for if one gives Your Serene Highness the 250000 Rthl upon the Zagorowo mortgage, you can buy 750000 Rthl in paper with it and thereby pay your whole debts. The mortgage may only be ceded judicially, and since it depends upon obtaining the monies in part beforehand, I will stand surety for it with the whole of my landed property, for it is necessary that one obtain some money beforehand in order to buy the paper and hand it over to the creditors so as to compel them to a speedy cancellation. Oppenheimer, known, who has about 28000 Rthl in Courant

the Invalids' Fund for their 50000 - 15000 - Meyer Bernhard and the trifles amounting to 3000, are together an advance of 46000 Rthl; for this the mortgage of 250000 Rthl is then quite clear. For Your Serene Highness there would accordingly remain some 204000 Rthl. My unpresumptuous advice would then be that Your Serene Highness should indeed have 300000 Rthl of negotiation bonds bought for 90000 Rthl, and should pay off to the King the [uncertain: Lursch] 300000 Rthl; thus you would gain 210000 Rthl and might for the sake of this matter be able to give a good word — likewise you can extinguish all household and other small debts, still retain for yourself a considerable sum, and may then look at anyone over the shoulder. As regards the security of the creditors who give the loan, there serves the mortgage of 2500 Rthl and the statement I have furnished, which I guarantee with my goods. New assessments

can by no means be drawn up at present, because there everything is in the greatest confusion; the old ones are too low and are now worth nothing any more, because through the dismemberment that has been begun everything has come into a different situation.

Concerning your affair at court I can now disclose a good deal to you and to Prince August, whom I shall still await here, in private.

Your Serene Highness has many enemies, and you are deliberately kept at a distance so that the [illegible] may talk themselves clear and shift the blame onto your most exalted self — I had everything on the best track. Only when M. St. withdrew did the matter at once take on another aspect, and even those who had previously worked zealously — drew back. Now something is afoot; if that succeeds, then everything will after all turn out for the best. Prince August, then, I shall still await; on the 14th I go to W:, and for the sake of Your Serene Highness's affair I shall arrive back here within 12 days — from here I go in haste to Königsberg; if I find that something can be done in the loan business, then I shall come from here directly[?] to Öhringen — discuss everything there, hasten from there to Paris, and send merely a merchant to Holland, who is to see to everything there and bring me the news to Paris; if then my presence in Holland is not urgently necessary, I shall go from Paris back to Öhringen in order to help arrange everything, and then I go again to K., where I shall no doubt learn the further particulars — the most painful thing for me at present is the pressing want of money. I do not know whence I am to take all the travelling costs. — From the King I take nothing whatever, and I shall not stop half-way. What I have begun and have hitherto pursued so laboriously through a thousand obstacles must absolutely be carried through. This very day I have borrowed 4000 Rthl in Münze in order to pay the still outstanding 2000 Rthl together with interest to von der Lahr, arising from the [uncertain: verdanken] Invalids' business. — I cannot lose this. When Your Serene Highness has happily settled your affairs, you would have to give me the first [thing]. For the 4000 Rthl in Münze I have received 2200 Rthl Courant, and this I must hand over to the vampire of a banker. (and in 6 months would have to write to pay Courant [illegible]) If the war does not break out too quickly, the French Government has promised me military assistance in order to recover the arrears and, if possible, the monies for the Invalids' Fund from the Zagorowo estates. If this succeeds, Your Serene Highness gains much, for without this help one obtains not a single denarius from them. Now all the interest is already due, the free years having expired; only the subjects refuse to pay, because the King of Saxony has forbidden anything to be handed over to anyone here on penalty of double the value.

In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Berlin, the 9th of July

most faithfully obedient servant

1808.

Peter[uncertain: s]
