Gnädigster Fürst und Herr!
In der Broniewskischen Sache habe ich weiter noch nichts gethan, weil
der Instructionis Termin auf den 14 ten d. M ansteht.
Der Patron will sich jezt sehr gerne vergleichen und ich glaube daß
er mit etliche 20000 Rthl dermalen verlieb nehmen würde.
Allein Rapackis neuerdings gelieferte reelle Beweise zeugen
klaar, daß ihm kein Groschen gehört – dies ist der Grund warum
ich den Vergleich noch aussezze. Nimmt die Regierung diese Gründe
an so findet kein Vergleich statt – es sey dann daß es der
Krotoszyner Tausch erheische, nach welchen ich allenfals einige Tausend
und, um gleich in Possession zu kommen opfern würde. Die Angst
ist bereits bey ihm sehr groß, den er erbietet sich schon eine Obliga-
tion die ich von Minister auf Kempen habe anzunehmen.
Von Hr Schlabrendorff werde ich heute Nachricht habe, ob er
mit meinen Wechsel auf ein Jahr zufrieden ist. In alle Fälle
werde ich den zu beruhigen suchen.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports on litigation: the Broniewski Instructionis Termin falls on the 14th, the patron willing to compound for some 20000 Rthl, though Rapachi's proofs suggest he is owed nothing; settlement deferred unless the Krotoszyn exchange demands it. The Chelmski suit is won in second instance; Brzyze and Winzingerode remain troublesome. Answers Prittwitz's and the Minister's charge that too many estates were received, with a computation of yields (Kaemen, Kulm, Trąbczyn, Szetlewek, Brzyze) totalling 42640 Rthl, citing comparable sales.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! In the Broniewski matter I have as yet done nothing further, because the Instructionis Termin is set for the 14th of this month. The patron is now very willing indeed to compound, and I believe that he would for the present be content with some 20000 Rthl. But the real proofs lately furnished by Rapachi show clearly that not a Groschen belongs to him – this is the reason why I still defer the settlement. If the Government accepts these grounds, then no settlement takes place – unless it be that the Krotoszyn exchange should require it, on which account I would if need be sacrifice a few thousand, and this in order to come into possession at once. His alarm is already very great, for he offers even to accept an obligation which I hold from the Minister upon Kempen. From Herr Schlabrendorff I shall have word today whether he is satisfied with my bill of exchange at one year. In any case I shall seek to quiet him. The Chelmski suit has been won in the second instance — it now remains to be seen whether he will go further – through Rapacki and [uncertain: Kordaszewski] I shall probably obtain real intelligence concerning the Brzyze estates; arrangements have also been

made for me to get into my hands the documents concerning the Winiges forest; these will no doubt at once refute from the ground up the foolish babble of the witnesses – the Chelmski and Broniewski matters will be brought to a good end; only the Brzyze and Winzingerode ones require every attention – because behind them lies the worst web of malice. In general I am now sitting over a description of those estates presented to Your Most Serene Highness. A quarrel [uncertain: Zänderey] between Prittwiz and Plench, as also the constant raisonnement of the Herr Minister – as though Your Serene Highness had, quite contrary to the [uncertain: intention] of the King, received too many estates, in which Prittwitz seems to concur, give me occasion for it. Out of the one envy roars, and out of the other clumsy ignorance. For if one takes it that the King commanded that estates be given to Your Most Serene Highness yielding 7 to 8000 Rthl in revenue, then this has been fulfilled only to the letter. The confiscated Prusimski estates, which were given first, were to answer the King's will; but the valuations of the Kammerath Beukert, chosen by the Minister himself,

the Kammerath Beukert, proved that twice as much debt still lay charged upon those estates as the value of the same was ascertained to be, according to the yield established by Herr Beukert. Now, in order to cloak the evil intention previously entertained, and to comply with the [uncertain: intention] of the Monarch, the Minister then proposed the several ecclesiastical estates, which were to bring in 8640 Rthl of revenue. The Prusimski estates are now to be regarded as purchased, since one must first vindicate them at law and fight for them step by step, and must moreover still pay in considerable sums. They are therefore no gift at all, and stand upon the same footing as purchased allodial estates, with which one often fares more happily than with these. Meanwhile the yield from them turns out afterwards in the following manner:
1. from Kaemen and Kulm 6000 Rthl
2. from Trąbczyn 8400 Rthl
3. from Szetlewek 1600
4. from Brzyze 4000
the sum is 20000 Rthl
The estates now really given, in which the will of the King has been exceeded, are how many times over assessed, and were to yield 8640 Rthl; through sums paid since then and three years' revenues applied thereto, a surplus of about 14000 Rthl has been brought out; this makes together 22640 Rthl, in all 42640 Rthl.
NB to the 8640 Rthl there come also about 1400 Rthl of building moneys, which may be reckoned off against the 14000; so they are improved only by about 12000 Rthl of yield.

The yield of the estates given is therefore now raised by somewhat more than the duplum, through diligence, expense and industry. This, however, is no wonder to the clear-sighted man; still less does the least offence lie here, but merely spiteful envy, grounded in the daily prattle of men. For take the Bischofswerder estates: these were to yield at most, by 4 valuations, 1500 Rthl – I have sold them for 207000 Rthl. The Zastrow ones were to yield, the [illegible] about 2000 Rthl; they bring in indisputably some twenty thousand. The Dolfi ones were to yield about 500 Rthl; Treskow is now selling them for 360000 Rthl. General Ruchil's were to yield 940 yearly; they have been sold for 140000 Rthl. Of all these not a word is spoken; only with Your Serene Highness's estates does one indulge the vice of envy, and cannonade without knowing at what. Sad enough for the State, that men are constantly kept in pay who act from passion, who dwell upon trifles and upon the everyday chronique scandaleuse, but never [uncertain: pursue] the whole. Glenck shall meanwhile lay this presentation of the matter before Prittwitz under his own name, and after that we shall indeed see what sheep's faces the one or the other will show. God grant that Your Serene Highness may soon return to me in health; if not, then I shall come, I suppose, from Berlin to Öhringen, Ansbach, or wherever I may find the same. So the wretched war has truly broken out after all – rather is this, however, a salutary poison – I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's
Breslau, the 13th of March 1799.
most faithful servant
v Triebenfeld
