--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports on the lawsuits over the granted estates: instruction in the Broniewski case falls due on the 14th, the opposing patron would settle for some 20,000 Rthl, but Rapacki's proofs show he is owed nothing, so settlement is deferred unless the Krotoszyn exchange demands it; the Chelmski suit is won at second instance, while Brzyza and Winzingerode need watching. A defence against Prittwitz and the Minister's claim that too much was given, comparing revenues of 8,640 and 20,000 Rthl and sale prices of other estates, is to be submitted by Glenck.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

In the Broniewski matter I have as yet done nothing further, because the term for the taking of instruction is set for the 14th of this month. The patron is now very willing indeed to come to a compromise, and I believe that he would for the present be content with some 20000 Rthl. But the real proofs lately furnished by Rapacki testify clearly that not a Groschen belongs to him – this is the reason why I am still deferring the compromise. If the Government accepts these grounds, then no compromise will take place – unless the Krotoszyn exchange should require it, for the sake of which I would at need sacrifice a few thousand, and in order to come at once into possession. His anxiety is already very great, for he is already offering to accept a bond which I hold from the Minister on Kempen. From Mr Schlabrendorff I shall have news today, whether he is satisfied with my bill of exchange at one year. In any event I shall seek to set him at ease.

The Chelmski suit has been won in the second instance — it now remains to be seen whether he will go further – through Rapacki and Kordaszeweben I shall probably obtain real information about the Brzyza estates; arrangement has also been

made that I shall get into my hands the documents concerning the Winiges forest; these will no doubt at once refute from the ground up the ragged rubbish of the witnesses – the Chelmski and Broniewski matters will be brought to a good end, only the Brzyza and Winzingerode ones require every attention – because behind them lies propped the worst web of malice. Altogether I am now sitting over a description of those estates bestowed upon Your Most Serene Highness. A wrangle between Prittwiz and Plench, as also the constant reasoning of the Minister – as though Your Serene Highness had received far too many estates, quite contrary to the intention of the King, which Pribwitz appears to concur in, give me occasion for it.

Out of the one, envy roars; out of the other, coarse ignorance. For if one takes it that the King commanded that estates be given to Your Most Serene Highness yielding 7 to 8000 Rthl in revenue, then this has only been punctually fulfilled in all points. The confiscated Prusimski estates, which were given first, were to answer the will of the King. But the valuations of the chamber councillor chosen by the Minister himself,

Beukert, proved that twice as much debt again was charged upon those estates as the value of the same was ascertained to be according to the yield determined by said Beukert.

In order now to cloak the evil intention that had gone before, and to comply with the intention of the monarchy, the Minister then proposed the various ecclesiastical estates, which were to bring in 8640 Rthl of revenue.

The Prusimski estates are now to be regarded as purchased, because one must first vindicate them in court and fight for them step by step, and must also still pay in considerable sums. They are therefore no gift at all, and stand on the same footing as purchased allodial estates, with which one often fares more happily than with these. Meanwhile the yield thereof afterwards works out in the following manner:

1. from Kaemen and Kulm 6000 Rthl

2. from Trąbczyn 8400 Rthl

3. from Szetlewek 1600

4. from Brzyza 4000

is in sum

20000 Rthl. The estates now actually bestowed, in respect of which the will of the King is held to have been exceeded, have been valued how many times over, and were to yield

8640 Rthl; through sums paid out [uncertain: for them] and revenues applied therein over three years, a surplus of about 14000 Rthl has been brought out; this makes together 22640 Rthl, in all 42640 Rthl. NB to the 8640 Rthl there come also about 1400 Rthl of building moneys, which may be reckoned off from the 14000, so they are improved by only about 12000 Rthl of yield.

The yield of the bestowed estates has therefore now been raised by somewhat more than double, through diligence, expense and industry. To the clear-sighted man this is however no wonder, still less does the least offence lie here, but merely spiteful envy, at the bottom of the daily bleating of men. For take the Bischofswerder estates: these were at the highest to yield 1500 Rthl according to 4 valuations – I have sold them for 207000 Rthl. The Zastrow estates were to yield, the [illegible] about 2000 Rthl; they bring in indisputably some twenty thousand. The Dolfi ones were to yield about 500 Rthl; Treskow is now selling them for 360000 Rthl. General Ruchel's were to yield 940 yearly; they have been sold for 140000 Rthl. Of all these not a word is spoken; only with Your Serene Highness's estates does one indulge the vice of envy, and cannonade without knowing at what. Sad enough for the State that men are constantly kept in pay who act toward me out of passion, who linger over trifles and the everyday chronique scandaleuse, but never persevere with the whole.

Glenck is meanwhile to lay this presentation of the matter before Prittwitz under his own name, and after that we shall indeed see what sheepish faces the one or the other will show. God grant that Your Serene Highness may soon return to me in health; if not, then I shall probably come from Berlin to Öhringen, Ansbach, or wherever I may find the same. The tiresome war has then truly broken out after all – rather is this however a salutary poison – I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's

Breslau, the 13th of March

1799.

faithful servant v. Triebenfeld
