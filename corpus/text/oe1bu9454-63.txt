--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld berichtet nach siebenwöchiger Krankheit, er müsse vor Verpachtung von Konotop 8000 Rthl Rückstände zahlen und hoffe auf einen Holzkäufer. Honrichs ist eingetroffen; Triebenfeld rät, auf den Prefect einzugehen, der die Güter kaufen will und bei der Wiedererlangung der Trąbczyner Güter helfen kann, Dąbska die angeeigneten Güter zu entreißen und den Administrator abzuweisen. Hecker und Hawich stifteten Niedźwiecki und Ostarszewski zu einer Denunziation an, die Honrichs' Verhaftung bewirkte; die Denuncianten haben sie schriftlich widerrufen.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Writing after a seven-week illness, Triebenfeld reports that before Konotop can be leased he must pay 8000 Rthl in arrears, and that he hopes for a timber buyer. Honrichs has arrived; Triebenfeld advises coming to terms with the Prefect, who wants to buy the estates and can help in recovering the Trąbczyn estates, in wresting the appropriated estates from Dąbska and in turning away the administrator. He says Hecker and Hawich incited Niedźwiecki and Ostarszewski to make a denunciation that brought about Honrichs' arrest, and that the informers have retracted it in writing.

63

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord, I was just on the point of writing to Your Most Serene Highness by express when I received Your Highness's most gracious letter of the 9th, which took 6 days in transit. I thank Your Most Serene Highness most heartily for the goodwill expressed therein, which I most devotedly beg to retain. I have lain ill for some 7 weeks, half of which time I lived quite happily, for I was robbed of my senses and felt nothing of my cares. For about 8 days now I have again been a thinking man and am labouring to set aside my troubles — whether I shall succeed in this I cannot determine precisely — I do indeed have a tenant for Konotop, but I must first pay 8000 rt. of arrears of interest and debts. Carte of Berlin, who has grown rich through the French deliveries, who from a beggar-dog or dyer's journeyman has raised himself up to be the richest private man and has acquired 1,600000 through the short war, wants to buy timber from me and to pay everything in advance; I expect him daily, and if he does so then I shall at last have a free hand to lease out and to bring everything into its track. Within the space of 8 days all this must be settled, or I am lost as regards my possessions. If it goes well, then I shall be at Sławięcice by the New Year to lay myself at Your Most Serene Highness's feet.

Now as regards Your Serene Highness's affairs — In Königsberg these are said to stand very well, the more so as B. is gone from there.

In Poland things go on in a motley fashion; my endeavour to procure money from thence was in vain, as the enclosed decree proves. To that end I had Honrichs come here, and he arrived here only today. The enclosed Pro Memoria proves his plans — may Your Serene Highness deign to grant it your attention and give me the very speediest answer before I depart from here.

1. I believe that the sub-prefect who wishes to buy the estates is not to be turned away, and that one must enter into dealings with him, because he can be very helpful towards the recovery of the Trąbczyn estates.

2. I believe that all quarters should now at once be set in motion, in order to wrest from Dąbska those estates which she has violently appropriated to herself.

3. That it be insisted upon with all force that the moneys, now that the sequestration is lifted, must absolutely flow to Your Serene Highness and not into the coffers of the improperly appointed administrator; also that this man, who has not yet admitted Honrichs into the estates, be at once turned away.

Provisionally I gave Honrichs the power of attorney for this, but I must also have Your Serene Highness's authorisation for it very quickly. If it succeed that the Russians or we move in there, then the rest will find itself, and we shall need no foreign help; the Russian Batek will in any case be enough for us.

As for whatever else may be written to Your Serene Highness from Sławięcice, there is nothing in it to engage with — Hawich and Hecker are rogues who deserve chastisement. I engaged the latter because I believed I had found in him a faithful man; he furnished a paper security of 5/m rt. — But he gave up the service, took land for himself, wished to be called his own master, and attached himself to a Polish hag, namely to the sister of Niedźwiecki. Honrichs was obliged to sue Niedźwiecki for 7000 rt of arrears of rent, and likewise Lenski and Ostarszewski, the latter of whom also owe some 5000 rt in cash — with these Hecker made common cause and denounced Honrichs as having railed against Bonaparte, indeed as having had Bonaparte's portrait / namely a woodcut which the Poles venerate like the Black Mary at Częstochowa / carried in procession across the court [uncertain: Hoft] to the — with respect — privy and there buried away — and also that Honrichs was constantly attempting to smuggle travelling moneys out of the country, and was indeed even endeavouring to stir up a reconfeileration. Hecker had this denunciation brought to Wybicki; Honrichs was at once arrested and was to be shot — the sub-prefect Zychlinski of Kalisz gave me notice of it; I wrote at once to Wybicki, to Wo[uncertain: e]da at Warsaw, to Garczynski, and to all the devils and holy Pensrats —

I succeeded in saving Honrichs so far that he was released from his arrest, and the matter is now being pursued in ordinary process — the charge that he had buried Bonaparte in the privy was quashed, because it had happened at the beginning of the war. Besides this, the process is still pending as to Honrichs having called Bonaparte a robber shortly before his arrest — But now Ostarszewski the first and the second, and also Niedźwiecki and consorts, have given from themselves a written acknowledgement that they had been incited by Hecker and Hawich, that they had had everything only from hearsay, that out of spite, because Honrichs had always thought pro Prussia, they had denounced him out of Woche — wherefore they withdraw their denunciation and must willingly submit themselves to the penalty for a false denouncer, because they could neither prove the charge against Honrichs nor still less swear to it.

This judicial written acknowledgement of the denouncers set up by Hecker and Hawich has now been submitted to the Presidium of the Criminal Court, as also to the Warsaw Ministry and the Chief Prefect, whereupon I may with all right expect a speedy end.
