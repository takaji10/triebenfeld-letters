--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A reply, drafted with von Weithausen of Cosel and Bolli, to a tenancy offer for the Zagorowo estates from Mr von Brzechsta: the Prince welcomes it but wants rent paid half-yearly in advance, cash security in hard coin, all burdens on the tenant, claims for remission forestalled, and a fixed sum for withdrawal should the estates be sold. Talks with General von Schimmelpfennig have stalled; little is expected of the Weigel and Baron von Stössel sale plan. He urges Triebenfeld to come soon with 300 Rthl for L. Sommer, game and a dog.

Right Honourable Sir

Highly Honoured Sir, Royal Councillor of Forests!

The mounted messenger who brings Your Right Honourable this answer to your letter of the 1st of January. [uncertain: arrived] the day before yesterday in the afternoon, and I at once dispatched a mounted man with your letter to Mr. von Weithausen at Cosel, who did me the friendly service of coming here yesterday, where we took everything into consideration together with the assistance of the good Bolli, whereupon this our mutual friend had the kindness to let this letter of mine be dictated to him, so that it therefore at the same time bears the stamp of his concurrence; the tenancy offer of Mr. von Brzechsta deserves my attention, and since Your Right Honourable's counsel and [uncertain: opinion] carry great weight with me in all cases, I shall, honouring your insight as I do, consider it with particular favour; it is to be hoped that one will not wish to stop at the first bid. Concerning the putting in order of the 3 demesne farms we shall doubtless come to agreement; the half-yearly payment of the tenancy rent seems to me advisable, and such must naturally be made in advance; some cash security seems to me necessary for proper assurance, and I ask Your Right Honourable to make the [uncertain: requisite] arrangement in this regard: that the tenancy contract be so concluded that with all foresight all claims for remission and recourse are forestalled lies in the nature of the matter; the security offered out of the whole property of the tenant may be very well, but in present times almost only hard ringing coin is a [uncertain: secure] pledge; that the bearing of all burdens should fall upon the tenant [uncertain: likewise] I find natural; to fix a sum for withdrawal in case of a possible sale during the term of the tenancy I myself find [uncertain: proper], and I shall let myself be found reasonable enough on that head at the conclusion of the contract; in the contract everything on this point would then have to be exactly determined; this, and any other conditions bearing upon a sale of the estates, would then emerge more particularly in the negotiations here

After these expressions of mine I believe that Your Right Honourable may come further with your friend, in which case I shall gladly lend a hand to the conclusion of the tenancy; in doing so, in my opinion, the term of the tenancy, the amount of the rent and the necessary conditions would also have to be quite exactly determined first; it follows of itself here, to be sure, that Mr. von Brzechsta would have to bring several thousand Reichsthaler with him in order to be able to conclude everything at once. With Mr. General von Schimmelpfennig I have not advanced a single step further in the matter of our estate dealings, which delay has arisen partly through the severe illness he suffered, partly through the negotiation of his sons in Berlin concerning the capital of their lady mother, which has not advanced so much as could be wished. But since I received the news yesterday that the General is now again taking his affairs in hand himself, I shall send to him and learn his decision more particularly. I confess that any respectable sale of the Zagorowo estates will always be the agreeable thing to me, and therefore I will here too so act that I need not reproach myself with any neglect of my duty towards myself; but since your friend in the tenancy is always to be covered by the stipulation of indemnification, I hope Your Right Honourable will let nothing hold you back from coming here with him as soon as possible, since for many reasons I must particularly wish that on the arrival of my children I may also be able to show that from the Zagorowo estates, through Your Right Honourable's exertions and preparatory work, as useful a return as possible has been drawn; further, we may hold it that political occurrences, and the pressing situation in which you find yourself on account of the dues to be rendered from the estates, make haste in the undertaking advisable, in which I would not hinder you. Your Right Honourable's project of sale to Mr. Weigel and Baron von Stössel

would likewise be welcome to me, if only I might still regard it as feasible after the disclosures made to me by Mr. cavalry captain von Stössel of Ruschinowitz. The old Baron von Stössel has fixed his arrival with me for these days, and I have been expecting him since the 6th of this month; on this occasion I mean to try to lay your plan before him once more, so as not to make myself guilty of any sin of omission in this matter either, notwithstanding that I expect nothing of it. We must now strain every effort together to procure me more assistance in money and to achieve a proper arrangement of my affairs. God bless your exertions in the Olesnica and Trąbczyn business, if only a considerable sum comes in thereby soon. Do seek to come to me very soon and bring me other good news besides; I count upon it that you will soon [send] 300 Rthl for [uncertain: L. Sommer], which I would only send here [uncertain: by offer]; for good measure I shall have a written undertaking given me as to the manner in which these monies can be procured there without disadvantage to me; the choice of means remains, moreover, left to the same, only I shall regard it as a great favour shown to myself if this matter is negotiated very soon. Since you wish to obtain a couple of roebucks, I have instructed my huntsman to procure me a few head of game, of whatever kind it may be; I shall then send them off at once to Blizanow in the company of your foot-messenger, whom I am therefore keeping back here yet a while. For the large dog, already described to me, which Your Right Honourable intends for me, I give my most devoted thanks, and I wish that you may send him to me soon or bring him with you. It remains for me to return Your Right Honourable's good wishes for the New Year from a true heart, and to embrace in my wishes for your welfare and contentment everything that

everything that can only further your own [uncertain: self-sufficing] happiness; and herewith I beg of you anew the continuance of your friendship, which I shall certainly return faithfully. I look forward to a speedy reunion and remain always in the most distinguished

esteem, your most faithfully devoted friend and [uncertain: servant]

F. L. Prince of Hohenlohe Schlawenschitz, the 27th of January 1812
