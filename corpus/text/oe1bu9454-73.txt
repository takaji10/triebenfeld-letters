Vorliebe berücksichtigen hoffentlich wird man bei dem ersten Geboth
nicht stehn bleiben wollen, über die instandisetzung der 3 Vorwerke
werden wir uns wohl einigen die halbjährige Zahlung der Pacht Pension
scheinet mir röthlich und muß solche natürlich prenumerando erfolgen
einige bare Caution scheint mir zur gehörigen Sicherstellung noth¬

Einleidung zu treffen: Daß der Pact contract so abgeschloßen wird

Unterhandlungen hier, sich näher ergeben
Nach diesen meinen Aeußerungen glaube ich daß Ewr Hochwohlgeb. bilde

Rechstl. mitbringen müste, um alles sofort beendigen zu können
Mit dem Hr Ger v Schimmelpfennig bin ich noch wegen Unseres

Von welcher Art es sey zu verschaffen ich werde sie dann

--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Der Fürst stellt Bedingungen für Brzechffas Pachtangebot auf die Zagorower Güther: halbjährige Pachtzahlung prenumerando, eine bare Caution, Schutz vor Remissionsklagen, alle Lasten beim Pächter, ein Abstandsquantum bei Verkauf. Triebenfeld soll bald mit Brzechffa kommen, der mehrere tausend Rthl mitbringen soll. Der Güterhandel mit Schimmelpfennig stockt. Den Verkaufsplan an Weigelt und Stössel hält er für wenig aussichtsreich. Er wünscht Erfolg in der Oleschnicer und Trąbczyner Sache und hofft auf eine bedeutende Summe.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
The Prince sets conditions for Brzechffa's offer to lease the Zagórów estates: lease payments every half year in advance, a cash security, protection against claims for remission, all burdens to be borne by the tenant, and a sum to be paid for withdrawal in case of a sale. Triebenfeld is to come soon with Brzechffa, who is to bring several thousand Rthl with him. The estate dealings with Schimmelpfennig have stalled. He thinks the plan to sell to Weigelt and Stössel has little prospect. He wishes success in the Oleschnica and Trąbczyn matter and hopes for a considerable sum.

Your Right Honourable Sir,

Highly Honoured Sir, Royal Councillor of Forests!

The mounted messenger who brings Your Right Honourable this reply to your letter of the 1st of January. Arrived the day before yesterday in the afternoon; I [uncertain: sent] at once a mounted man with your letter to H. v. Weithausen at Koźle, who did me the friendly office of coming here yesterday, where we took the whole matter into consideration together, with the good Bally called in as well, whereupon this our common friend then had the kindness to let this letter of mine be dictated to him, so that it accordingly bears at the same time the stamp of his concurrence; the lease offer of Mr v. Brzechffa deserves my attention, and since Your Right Honourable's counsel and [uncertain: opinion] carries great weight with me in every case, I shall therefore, honouring your insight as I do, take account of your proposal with particular favour; it is to be hoped that one will not wish to stop at the first bid. As to the putting of the 3 demesne farms into repair we shall no doubt come to agreement; the half-yearly payment of the lease rent seems to me advisable, and such payment must of course be made in advance; some cash security seems to me necessary and requisite for proper assurance, and I beg Your Right Honourable to make the arrangements accordingly: that the lease contract be concluded in such a way that with all caution every claim of remission and of recourse be guarded against lies in the nature of the thing; the security offered out of the whole of the tenant's means may be very well, but in the present times almost only hard ringing coin is a [illegible] secure good pledge; the bearing of all charges on the tenant's side I find natural; to fix a sum of relinquishment against a possible sale during the term of the lease I myself find [uncertain: prudent], and I shall let myself be found as reasonable as possible in that matter at the conclusion of the contract; in the contract everything on this head would then have to be determined precisely; this, and whatever other conditions may bear upon a sale of the estates, would then emerge more fully in the negotiations here.

After these declarations of mine I believe that Your Right Honourable may [uncertain: soon] come to further agreement with your friend, in which case I shall gladly lend my hand to the conclusion of the lease; herewith the term of the lease, the amount of rent and the necessary conditions would now, in my opinion, first have to be determined quite precisely; and in this it follows of itself that Mr v. Brzechffa would have to bring several thousand Rthl with him in order to be able to conclude everything at once. With the general Mr v. Schimmelpfennig I have not advanced a single step further in the matter of our estate transaction, which delay has arisen partly through the severe illness he has suffered, partly through the negotiation of his sons in Berlin concerning their lady mother's capital, which has not advanced as much as could be wished. Since, however, I received news yesterday that the general is now again taking his affairs into his own hands himself, I shall send to him and learn his final resolution more nearly. I confess that any decent sale of the Zagórów estates will always be the agreeable thing to me, and therefore I will here also so act that I may have no reproach to cast upon myself for a breach of my duty of omission towards myself; but since your friend is always to be covered in the leasing by the stipulation of indemnification, I hope Your Right Honourable will let nothing hinder you from coming here with him as soon as possible, since for various reasons I must particularly wish that, on the arrival of my children, I may also be able to show that by Your Right Honourable's exertion and preliminary work some useful [uncertain: portion] has been drawn from the Zagórów estates; thereafter we can hold it so: political occurrences, and the pressing situation in which you find yourself on account of the dues to be rendered from the estates, make haste in the undertaking advisable, wherein I would not hinder you. Your Right Honourable's plan of sale to Mr Weigelt and Baron v. Stössel

would likewise be welcome to me, if only I might still [illegible] regard as feasible the disclosures made to me by the cavalry captain Mr v. Stössel of Rusinowice. The old Baron v. Stössel has fixed his arrival with me for these days, and I have been expecting him since the 6th of this month; on that occasion I will try to lay your plan before him once more, so as not to make myself guilty of any sin of omission in this matter either, notwithstanding that I expect nothing from it. We must now together strain every effort to procure me more help in money and to obtain a decent arrangement of my affairs. God bless your exertions in the Oleśnica and Trąbczyn matter, if only a considerable sum comes in thereby; do indeed seek to come to me very soon, and bring me other good news as well. I count upon it that you will soon [uncertain: 300 Rthl for the L. Sommer, which I would only send here as an offer]; for good measure I [uncertain: let] a written undertaking be given me concerning the manner in which these moneys may be procured there without disadvantage to me; the choice of means remains for the rest left to him, only I shall regard it as a great favour shown to myself if this business is negotiated very soon. Since you wish to obtain a couple of roebucks, I have charged my [uncertain: hunting staff] to procure me a few head of game, of whatever kind it may be; I shall then send them off at once to Blizanów in the company of your foot messenger, whom I have therefore kept back here still. For the large dog already described to me, which Your Right Honourable intends for me, I give my most devoted thanks, and I wish that you may send him to me soon or bring him with you. It remains for me still to return Your Right Honourable's good wishes for the new year out of a faithful heart, and to embrace in my wishes for your welfare and contentment everything that

everything can only further your own [uncertain: self-sufficing] happiness. Herewith I beg of you anew the continuance of your friendship, which I shall certainly return faithfully; I look forward to a speedy [uncertain: meeting again], and remain always in the most particular esteem

Your Right Honourable's

Sławięcice, the 27th of January 1812. Most faithfully devoted friend and servant

F. L., Prince of Hohenlohe
