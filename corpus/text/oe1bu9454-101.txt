--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Abschrift einer Vorstellung des Fürsten von Hohenlohe an den König zum Tausch seiner südpreußischen Güter gegen Krotoszyn und Polajewo. Sie widerlegt die Ablehnung durch Minister von Voss: 30100 Rthl sei der Nettoertrag, das angebliche Vorwerk eine von Triebenfeld erworbene Holländer Hufe, die Wälder seien verwüstet. Neues Angebot: Verzicht auf Polajewo, jährlich 8000 Rthl auf Krotoszyn, Ausgleich des Wertunterschieds in bar an die Seehandlungs Societaet. Erbeten werden eine Untersuchung durch die Posener Kammer und ein Bericht Hoyms.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Copy of a petition from the Prince of Hohenlohe to the King for the exchange of his South Prussian estates for Krotoszyn and Polajewo. It rebuts the rejection by Minister von Voss: he says 30,100 Rthl is the net yield, the supposed demesne farm is a Hollander Hufe acquired by Triebenfeld, and the woods are laid waste. New offer: he gives up Polajewo, pays 8,000 Rthl a year on Krotoszyn, and settles the difference in value in cash to the Maritime Trading Company. He asks for an investigation by the Poznań Chamber and a report from Hoym.

Most Serene: The Prince of Hohenlohe's further most humble submission concerning the exchange of his estates situated in South Prussia for the lordships of Krotoszyn and Polajewo. From a letter addressed to me by the Privy Minister of State von Voss, dated the 1st of December, I learn that he has not found the exchange of my estates in South Prussia for Krotoszyn and Polajewo, which I most humbly offered to Your Majesty on the xxxxx th of xxxxx, consonant with Your Royal Majesty's interest. The grounds adduced in this regard are:

I) that in the case of this district office the true principles of husbandry have not been followed, because

a) the lessee has been granted unusual and unwarranted great advantages, by way of a so-called Reservations Fund, whose annual capital amounts to 9,540 Rthl.

b) that an entire demesne farm, which yields 1,500 Rthl. of rent, was not brought into the valuation and was passed over altogether.

c) that the very profitable mast in the woodlands has been made over to the councillor of war Triebenfeld by way of indemnification for the small sum of 3,327 Rthl. in super-inventory monies paid to his predecessor upon his entering into the lease.

II) that by an altered arrangement in the forests, which hitherto have yielded no return whatever, a considerable income could now already be obtained; and therefore, by adding these items to the existing lease sum of 30,100 Rthl., which I wished to take as the basis of the exchange, that sum would have to be raised as high as 60,000 Rthl.

III) that these district offices, being domains, afford me just as little security as the estates already received from His Majesty.

To refute the above grounds the Count von Hoym would indeed be better able than I, since these estates have hitherto stood under his supervision, inasmuch as an unfavourable light is thereby cast upon the previous administration; yet through my acquaintance with the locality and with the lessees of that time I shall be in a position to refute these assertions thoroughly, and in what follows to lay most humbly at Your Royal Majesty's feet a plan still more advantageous to Your Highest interest, more in order to remove the appearance

that I wished to enrich myself at the expense of Your Majesty's treasury, than for the sake of my own advantage. On point I, concerning the excessive advantages of the present Krotoszyn district lease: on a) the Privy Minister of State von Voss appears still too new to and unacquainted with the constitution of the district office, when he has quite incorrectly asserted that the fund of

9,543 Rthl.

of the Reservations Fund could accrue to the lease sum, and when he will not distinguish between gross and net yield. The gross yield does indeed consist of 39,643 Rthl. From this, however, the necessary outlays have not been deducted at all, and there always remains to the Royal treasury only the surplus of 30,100 Rthl.

Now, since I took the latter sum as the basis of the exchange, it goes without saying that I have undertaken to bear those outlays.

These outlays consist of

24 per cent land contribution = 2,722 Rthl.

Salaries for the judicial officials of the district office = 1,234 Rthl. For remissions to the subjects, reckoned one year with another, = 2,000 Rthl. = 5,956 Rthl.

There accordingly still remains over = 3,587 Rthl.

This remaining amount is far from sufficient to maintain the buildings of the district office yearly, and to this item would have to be added all those advantages which forest and mast may perhaps afford.

On b) it is just as incorrectly asserted that an entire demesne farm has hitherto been left out of the valuation. The case is quite otherwise. This is no demesne

farm, but 1 Hollander Hufe, which von Triebenfeld acquired from the possessors of two Hollanders as far back as Polish times. A demesne farm has indeed been laid out by him upon it, and the same ceded to the present lessee; nonetheless it cannot be regarded as an appurtenance of the manorial lordship (Dominium). And even supposing that von Triebenfeld had acquired it illegitimate modo, it would be the former possessors, and not the Treasury, who could vindicate it. On c) the Privy Minister of State von Voss can never have seen the Krotoszyn woodlands.

It is notorious that they have already been devastated by the ex-minister von Goerne, who for a year and a day had stave-cutters, six hundred persons of them, at work therein; and although the terrain is indeed large enough, yet a whole century must first pass before it becomes serviceable again, if one will but consider that all the subjects receive free timber for building and firewood, whence it comes that neither forest nor mast usage has hitherto been of any importance.

If therefore the Privy Minister of State von Voss asserts that Krotoszyn must in future yield 60,000 Rthl., then I most humbly and most urgently beg Your Royal Majesty to hold him to his word at the end of the lease in 1800, should nothing come of my offer; and I shall not fail then to remind Mr von Voss of it. The meliorations to be looked for, however, are not to the point here, inasmuch as a large capital outlay is required for them.

On 3, the [uncertain: Kan.] Voss betrays no knowledge whatever of the matter, inasmuch as the district offices of Krotoszyn and Polajewo are no domains at all, but at best may after 36 years develop into such, which likewise I do not doubt.

These estates are originally of noble quality; they were bought by Minister

Goerne qua tales, and thereupon sequestrated for the account of the Maritime Trading Company, to which von Goerne owed [uncertain: nearly] 1 million thaler.

When South Prussia was occupied, the Maritime Trading Company was eager to cover that capital together with the interest, and exerted itself to bring about that the estates should be handed over to the Poznań Chamber in administration on the condition that an amortisation amount of 50/m Rthl. should be discharged yearly.

At that time this plan was entirely repugnant to Mr von Voss, and for that reason I cannot now explain his contradiction to myself either, since the matter stands this very day just as it stood then.

After Mr von Voss had lost the department, the plan of the Maritime Trading Company was confirmed by a cabinet order of the 4th of March 1795, but it was at the same time laid down therein that only after 36 years, when that burden of debt had been discharged, should these estates assume the character of domains. Until then they are to be regarded as private estates, which is sufficiently proved already by the fact that they bear the burdens of noble estates, such as the 24 per cent dues.

Having sufficiently refuted the assertions of Mr von Voss, I cannot forbear adding the following, which

Mr von Voss has probably passed over in silence deliberately:

1. Krotoszyn has lost the propination rights in the town of Krotoszyn, whereby the revenue is reduced by 4,000 Rthl. according to the valuation.

2. The most important part of the forest is in a boundary dispute with the manorial lordship of Kozmin.

3. The Jews have brought it about by judgment and law that

they have been reduced to lower dues.

4. Polajewo is leased at 6,000 Rthl. higher than it was valued, on which account sequestration has already been set in motion there three times, and the lessee, Count von Flotow, has thereby been ruined, who, as I am prepared to prove, has already sunk 20,000 Rthl. in it.

5. Polajewo is so totally ruined that the costs of restoration have been put at

300,000 Rthl.; to which [uncertain: there is added] that the forest is stripped of all building timber; and

6. I pledge my honour that Polajewo must come down to a net yield of 12,000 Rthl. net if the restoration is to be effected out of the revenues.

I appeal to the opinion of the Poznań Chamber that this is the true state of the matter. Now may it please Your Royal Majesty most graciously to have my further proposal most humbly laid before you.

1. I renounce the possession of Polajewo. But because the [uncertain: district office] is not in a position to afford the present yield any longer, I am willing to undertake to pay 8,000 Rthl. yearly on that account, and to be content that the same be transferred onto Krotoszyn, of which therefore the net yield would be 38,000 instead of 30/m, whereby I afford more than if I were, on my first offer, to take over Polajewo as well.

2. I undertake, as goes without saying, all the public burdens (onera publica), buildings, remissions etc. charged upon Krotoszyn, which have hitherto been paid out of the Reservations Fund.

3. My estates must be valued, or my lease contracts taken as the basis of the exchange, and from these the net yield

be constituted; this is to be balanced against the net yield of Krotoszyn, reckoned to capital at 5 per cent, and whatever Krotoszyn is worth more than my estates I will pay in cash to the Maritime Trading Company, whereby then the yearly amortisation amount is proportionately reduced, which Krotoszyn and Polajewo have hitherto discharged at 50/m r. yearly, and which in future Polajewo and my estates will discharge.

The advantages which I gain thereby are:

1. those already adduced in my submission of the —

2. that I obtain a rounding-out of my estates, which hitherto lie scattered in South Prussia

3. that Krotoszyn lies only 8 miles distant from Wrocław, and can be managed in the neighbourhood of my domicile. The advantages which Your Royal Majesty gains are:

1. it can be a matter of indifference to Your Royal Majesty whether my estates lie scattered, since they do after all border one upon the other domains.

2. Pszczew contains building timber, firewood and meadow growth; this is wanting to the intendancy of [uncertain: Buck] and Międzyrzecz,

3. Polajewo is relieved by 8,000 Rthl.

4. The amortisation amount is reduced. Since I am firmly convinced that Your Royal Majesty, as well as I, gains by this exchange, and since my point of honour suffers when the matter is represented otherwise by Mr von Voss, I must most humbly beg:

that Your Royal Majesty may be pleased to direct the Poznań Chamber by cabinet order to have the investigation carried out by 2

members of the Chamber who are acquainted with the matter, whose vote should circulate among the remaining members of the collegium, each of whom should then add his own vote, from which the correctness of my statement will certainly appear; at the same time I also beg that a report be required of the Count von Hoym, and I remain, until death, in deepest fidelity,

Your Royal Majesty's.
