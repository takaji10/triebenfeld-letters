--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Copy of the Prince of Hohenlohe's renewed petition to the King, rebutting Minister von Voss's rejection of the exchange of his scattered South Prussian estates for Krotoszyn and Polajewo: the Reservation Fund of 9540 Rthl is gross, not net, leaving only 30100 Rthl after contributions and remissions; the alleged demesne farm is a Hollander Hufe acquired by Triebenfeld; the forests were devastated under von Goerne; and both offices remain noble estates until the Maritime Trading Society's debt is amortised. He now renounces Polajewo, offering 8000 Rthl yearly transferred to Krotoszyn, and asks that the Posen Chamber and Count von Höym report.

Most Serene Sovereign. The Prince of Hohenlohe's further most humble representation concerning the exchange of his estates situated in South Prussia for the lordships of Krotoszyn and Polajewo. From a letter issued to me by the Privy Minister of State von Voss of the 1st of December, I learn

that he has not found the exchange of my estates in South Prussia for Krotoszyn and Polajewo, offered by me most humbly to Your Majesty under the [text lost] th, suited to Your Royal Majesty's interest.

The reasons adduced in this regard are:

1.) that in the case of this office [administrative district] there has been no proceeding according to true [uncertain: economic] principles, because

a) the tenant has been conceded unusual and undue great advantages, by way of a so-called Reserve Fund, whose annual fund amounts to 9540 Rthl.

b) that a whole demesne farm, which yields 1500 Rthl in rent, was not brought into the valuation and was entirely passed over.

c) that the very lucrative mast [pannage] in the woodlands has been made over to the councillor of war Triebenfeld as indemnification for the small sum of 3327 Rthl in super-inventory moneys paid to his predecessor at the commencement of the lease.

II) that by an altered arrangement in the forests, which hitherto have yielded no return whatever, a considerable income can now already be had; and therefore, upon adding these items to the hitherto rent quantum of 30100 Rthl, which I proposed to take as the basis of the exchange, that sum must be raised as high as 60,000 Rthl.

III) that these offices, being domains, afford me just as little security as the estates already received from His Majesty.

To refute the above grounds the Count of Höym would indeed be better able than I, since these estates have hitherto stood under his supervision; inasmuch as an unfavourable light is thereby thrown upon the former administration. But through my acquaintance with the locality and with the tenants of that time, I shall be in a position to refute these assertions thoroughly, and to lay most humbly before Your Royal Majesty in what follows a plan yet more advantageous to Your Most High interest — more to remove from myself the appearance

that I had wished to enrich myself at the cost of Your Majesty's treasury, than for the sake of my own advantage. Ad I, concerning the exaggerated advantages of the present Krotoszyn office lease. Ad a) the Privy Minister of State von Voss appears still too new to and unacquainted with the constitution of the office, when he has quite incorrectly asserted that the fund of 9543 Rthl of the Reserve Fund could accrue to the rent quantum, and has been unwilling to distinguish between gross and net yield. Of this, the gross yield does indeed consist of 39643 Rthl, but the necessary expenditures are not deducted at all, and there always remains to the Royal treasury only the surplus of 30100 Rthl. Now if I have taken the latter sum as the basis of the exchange, it goes without saying that I have undertaken to bear those expenditures.

These expenditures consist in

24 per cent land contribution

2722 rl.

[uncertain: Salaries] for the office's judicial officials. 1234. For remissions for the subjects, reckoning one year with another, 2000

= 5956

3587. This remaining quantum is far from sufficient to maintain the buildings of the office annually, and to this item would have to be added all such advantages as forest and mast may perhaps afford.

Ad b.) It is just as incorrectly asserted that hitherto a whole demesne farm has been left out of the valuation. The matter stands quite otherwise. This is no demesne

farm, but 1 Hollander Hufe, which von Triebenfeld acquired from the possessors of two Hollanders still in Polish times; he has indeed made and laid out a demesne farm from it,

and the same has been ceded to the present tenant, but none the less it can be regarded as an appurtenance of the manorial lordship (Dominium). Even granting that von Triebenfeld had acquired it by illegitimate means, it would be the former possessors, and not the fisc, who could vindicate it. Ad c.) The Privy Minister of State von Voss can never have seen the Krotoszyn woodlands.

It is notorious that they have already been devastated by the ex-minister von Goerne, who for a year and a day had stave-cutters, 600 persons indeed, in them; and even if the district is large enough, still a whole century must first pass before it becomes usable again, if one will consider that all the subjects receive free building and firewood, whence it comes that neither forest nor mast use has hitherto been of any importance.

If therefore the Privy Minister of State von Voss asserts that Krotoszyn must in future yield 60,000 Rthl, I most humbly and most urgently beg Your Royal Majesty to hold him to his word at the end of the lease in 1800, should nothing come of my offer; and I shall not fail then to remind Mr. von Voss of it. The improvements to be effected, however, do not belong here, inasmuch as a great capital outlay is required for them;

Ad 3, Mr. von Voss betrays no expert knowledge whatever; inasmuch as the offices of Krotoszyn and Polajewo are not domains at all, but may at most after 36 years come to be such, which I likewise do not doubt.

These estates are originally of noble quality; they were by Minister

Goerne accumulated as such, and thereafter sequestrated for account of the Maritime Trading Company, to which von Goerne owed nearly 1 million thaler.

When South Prussia was occupied, the [uncertain: Negotiating] Company was glad to cover that capital together with interest, and exerted itself to bring matters to the point that the estates should be handed over to the Posen Chamber in administration on the condition that an amortisation quantum of 50000 rl. should be paid off annually.

At that time this plan was wholly contrary to Mr. von [uncertain: Voss], which is why I cannot now explain his contradiction either, since the matter stands today just as it did then.

After Mr. von Voss had lost the department, the plan of the Maritime Trading Company was confirmed by a cabinet order of the 4th of March 1795, but at the same time it was therein laid down that only after 36 years, when that burden of debt should have been paid off, these estates should take on the character of domains. Until then they are to be regarded as private estates, which is sufficiently proved by the fact that they bear the burdens of noble estates, such as the 24 per cent dues.

Having sufficiently refuted the assertions of Mr. von Voss, I cannot forbear adding the following, which

Mr. von Voss has probably deliberately passed over in silence:

1, Krotoszyn has lost the propination rights in the town of Krotoszyn, whereby the revenue is diminished by 4000 Rthl according to the valuation.

2, The [uncertain: soundest] part of the forest is in a boundary dispute with the manorial lordship of Kozmin.

3. The Jews have brought it about by judgment and law that

they have been reduced to lower dues.

5, Polajewo is so totally ruined that the costs of restoration have been set at

6.) I pledge my honour that Polajewo must come down to a net yield of 12000 Rthl net, if the restoration is to be effected out of the revenues. I appeal to the [uncertain: opinion] of the Posen Chamber that this is the true state of the matter. Now may Your Royal Majesty most graciously allow my further proposal to be laid most humbly before you.

1) I renounce the possession of Polajewo. But because the [uncertain: office] is not in a position to afford the present yield any longer, I will undertake to pay 8000 Rthl yearly toward it, and am content that such be transferred onto Krotoszyn, whereof the net yield would thus be, instead of 30000, 38000; whereby I afford more than if, according to my first offer, I were to take Polajewo over as well.

2.) I undertake, as goes without saying, all public burdens (onera publica), buildings, remissions etc. resting upon Krotoszyn, which hitherto have been paid out of the Reserve Fund.

The advantages which I thereby gain are:

1) those already adduced in my representation of the [text lost]

2, that I obtain a rounding-out of my estates, which hitherto lie scattered in South Prussia

3) that Krotoszyn lies only 8 miles from Breslau, and can be managed in the neighbourhood of my domicile. The advantages which Your Royal Majesty gains are:

2.) Gitschen contains building timber, firewood and meadow growth; this the intendancy of Buck and Meseritz lacks,

3. Polajewo is relieved by 8000 Rthl.

4, The amortisation quantum is diminished. Since I am firmly convinced that Your Royal Majesty gains by this exchange as well as I, and since my point of honour suffers if the matter is represented otherwise by Mr. von Voss, I must most humbly beg:

May Your Royal Majesty be pleased to enjoin upon the Posen Chamber by cabinet order to have the investigation effected by 2

at the same time I also beg that a report be required from the Count of Höym, and I remain, until death, in deepest fidelity,

Your Royal Majesty's.
