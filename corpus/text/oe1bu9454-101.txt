--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Copy of the Prince of Hohenlohe's renewed petition to the King, rebutting Minister von Voss's rejection of the exchange of his scattered South Prussian estates for Krotoszyn and Polajewo: the Reservation Fund of 9540 Rthl is gross, not net, leaving only 30100 Rthl after contributions and remissions; the alleged demesne farm is a Hollander Hufe acquired by Triebenfeld; the forests were devastated under von Goerne; and both offices remain noble estates until the Maritime Trading Society's debt is amortised. He now renounces Polajewo, offering 8000 Rthl yearly transferred to Krotoszyn, and asks that the Posen Chamber and Count von Höym report.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Copy. Most Serene of All. The Prince of Hohenlohe's further most humble representation concerning the exchange of his estates situated in South Prussia for the lordships of Krotoszyn and Polajewo. By a letter addressed to me by the Privy Minister of State von Voss, of the 1st of December, I learn that the same has not found the exchange of my estates in South Prussia for Krotoszyn and Polajewo, which I most humbly offered to Your Majesty on the [text lost, day left blank], suited to Your Royal Majesty's interest. The grounds adduced on this head are: 1.) that in the case of this office [Amt] the proceedings have not been according to true economic principles, because a) the tenant has been conceded unusual and unbecoming great advantages by means of a so-called Reservation Fund, whose annual fund amounts to 9540 Rthl; b) that an entire demesne farm, which yields 1500 Rthl of rent, has not come into the valuation and has been entirely passed over; c) that the very profitable mast-feeding in the forests has been left to the Councillor of War Triebenfeld as indemnification for the small sum of 3327 Rthl in super-inventory monies paid to his predecessor on his entering upon the lease. II) that by an altered arrangement in the forests, which hitherto have yielded no return at all, a considerable income can already now be had; hence, on adding these items to the hitherto rent-quantum of 30100 Rthl, which I wished to take as the basis of the exchange, the same would have to be raised as high as 60,000 Rthl. III) that these offices, being domains, afford me just as little security as the estates already received from His Majesty. To refute the above grounds the Count von Höym would indeed be better able than I, since these estates have hitherto stood under his supervision; inasmuch as thereby a disadvantageous light is cast upon the former administration, yet through my acquaintance with the locality and with the tenants of that time I shall be in a position to refute these assertions thoroughly, and to lay before Your Royal Majesty in the following, most humbly, a plan yet more advantageous for Your Most High interest, more in order to remove from myself the appearance

from myself, as though I had wished to enrich myself at the cost of Your Majesty's treasury, than for the sake of my own advantage. On I, concerning the exaggerated advantages of the present Krotoszyn office-lease: on a) the Privy Minister of State von Voss appears [uncertain: still] too new to and unacquainted with the constitution of the office, when he has quite incorrectly asserted that the fund of 9543 Rthl of the Reservation Fund could accrue to the rent-quantum, and appears unwilling to distinguish between gross and net yield. Of this, the gross yield does indeed consist of 39643 Rthl, but the necessary outlays are not deducted at all, and there always remains to the Royal treasury only the surplus of 30100 Rthl. Now if I have taken the latter sum as the basis of the exchange, it goes without saying that I have undertaken to bear those outlays. These outlays consist of 24 per cent land contribution 2722 rl.; salaries for the office's judicial officials 1234; for remissions to the subjects, reckoning one year with another, 2000 = 5956. There thus remains over 3587. This remaining quantum is far from sufficient to maintain the buildings of the office annually, and to this item there would have to be added all those advantages which forest and mast-feeding may perhaps afford. On b.) It is just as incorrectly asserted that hitherto an entire demesne farm has been left out of the valuation. The matter stands quite otherwise. This is no demesne

farm, but 1 Hollander Hufe, which the said von Triebenfeld acquired still in Polish times from the possessors of two Hollander holdings. It is true that he made and laid out a demesne farm from it and ceded the same to the present tenant; nevertheless it cannot be regarded as an appurtenance of the domain. Even granting that the said von Triebenfeld had acquired it illegitimate modo, the former possessors, and not the Fiscus, would be able to vindicate it. On c.) the Privy Minister of State von Voss cannot have seen the Krotoszyn forests at all. It is notorious that they have already been devastated by the ex-Minister von Goerne, who kept stave-cutters in them for a year and a day, and 600 persons at that; and though the extent is indeed large enough, yet a saeculum must first pass before it becomes useful again, if one will consider that all subjects receive free building and fire wood, whence it comes that neither forest nor mast-feeding usufruct has hitherto been of importance. If therefore the Privy Minister of State von Voss asserts that Krotoszyn must in future yield a rent of 60,000 Rthl, I most humbly and most urgently beg Your Royal Majesty to hold him to his word at the end of the lease in 1800, should nothing come of my offer; and I shall not fail then to remind Herr von Voss thereof. The meliorations to be [uncertain: effected] do not, however, belong here, inasmuch as a large capital of outlay is required for them. On 3, Herr Voss betrays no knowledge of the matter whatever, inasmuch as the offices of Krotoszyn and Polajewo are no domains at all, but at most may after 36 years come to be such, which I likewise do not doubt. These estates are originally of noble quality; they were by Minister

Goerne accumulated qua tales, and thereafter sequestrated for the account of the Maritime Trading Society, to which the said von Goerne owed nearly 1 million thaler. When South Prussia was occupied, the [uncertain: Trading] Society was glad to cover that capital together with interest, and endeavoured to bring it about that the estates should be handed over to the Posen Chamber in administration on condition that an amortisation quantum of 50000 rl. should be discharged annually. At that time this plan was quite contrary to Herr von [illegible], on which account I cannot now explain his contradiction either, since the matter stands today just as it did then. After Herr von Voss had lost the department, the plan of the Maritime Trading Society was confirmed by a Cabinet Order of the 4th of March 1795, but it was at the same time laid down therein that only after 36 years, when that burden of debt should have been discharged, these estates should take on the character of domains. Until then they are to be regarded as private estates, which is already sufficiently proved by the fact that they bear the burdens of noble estates, such as the 24 per cent dues. Having sufficiently refuted the assertions of Herr von Voss, I cannot forbear to add the following, which Herr von Voss has probably deliberately kept silent about: 1, Krotoszyn has lost the propination rights in the town of Krotoszyn, whereby the income is diminished by 4000 Rthl according to the valuation; 2, the [uncertain: most correct] part of the forest is in a boundary dispute with the Dominium Kozmin; 3, the Jews have brought it about by judgment and law that

they have been reduced to lower dues. 4, Polajewo is leased at 6000 Rthl higher than it was valued, on which account sequestration has already three times been set in train there, and the tenant Count von Plotho has thereby been ruined, who, as I will prove, has already sunk 20,000 Rthl in it; 5, Polajewo is so totally ruined that the costs of re-establishment have been set at 300,000 Rthl, to which is added that the forest is stripped of all building timber; and 6.) I pledge my honour that Polajewo must come down to a net yield of 12000 Rthl net if the re-establishment is to be effected out of the revenues. I appeal to the opinion of the Posen Chamber that this is the true state of the matter. Now may Your Royal Majesty most graciously permit my further proposal to be laid most humbly before you. 1) I renounce the possession of Polajewo. But because the [uncertain: office] is not in a condition to furnish the present yield any longer, I will undertake to pay 8000 Rthl annually towards it, and will suffer the same to be transferred to Krotoszyn, whose net yield would thus be, instead of 30000, 38000, whereby I furnish more than if, according to my first offer, I were to take over Polajewo as well. 2.) I take upon myself, as goes without saying, all the onera publica burdening Krotoszyn, buildings, remissions etc., which have hitherto been paid out of the Reservation Fund. 3) My estates must be valued, or my leasehold contracts taken as the basis of the exchange, and from these the net yield

be constituted; this is to be balanced against the net yield of Krotoszyn, reckoned to capital at 5 per cent, and whatever Krotoszyn is worth beyond my estates I shall pay in cash to the Maritime Trading Society, whereby then the annual amortisation quantum is proportionately diminished, which hitherto Krotoszyn and Polajewo have discharged at 50000 Rthl annually, and which in future Polajewo and my estates will discharge. The advantages which I thereby gain are: 1) those already adduced in my representation of the [text lost]; 2, that I obtain a consolidated block of my estates, which hitherto lie scattered in South Prussia; 3) that Krotoszyn lies only 8 miles from Breslau and can be managed near my domicile. The advantages which Your Royal Majesty gains are: 1) [uncertain: that] it must be indifferent to Your Royal Majesty whether my estates lie scattered, since they nevertheless border one with other domains; 2.) Gitschen contains building timber, firewood and meadow-growth; this is wanting to the intendancy of Buck and Meseritz; 3. Polajewo is relieved by 8000 Rthl; 4, the amortisation quantum is diminished. Since I am firmly convinced that Your Royal Majesty gains by this exchange as well as I, and since my point d'honneur suffers under it if the matter is represented otherwise by Herr von Voss, I must most humbly beg that Your Royal Majesty be pleased to enjoin the Posen Chamber by Cabinet Order to have the investigation carried out by 2

members of the Chamber who are acquainted with the [uncertain: matter], their vote to circulate among the remaining Membris Collegii, to which each shall then add his own vote, from which the correctness of my statement will certainly appear; at the same time I beg also that a report be required of the Count von Höym, and I remain, until death, in deepest fidelity, as Your Royal Majesty's.
