--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports from Berlin on the exchange negotiations: Krotoszyn is now certain to be granted for the Suprenstis possessions, while Polajewo still meets objections, though Privy Finance Councillor Schulze gives hope; some 100000 Rthl might be gained there despite obstruction from the Chambers. Urges that Prince George be detained until Triebenfeld reaches Breslau, since only he knows the province and language. Notes Minister Hoym's journey to Pomerania, Voss's return, letters to Schulze, H. C. W. Beym and the Colonel, the King's low spirits, Reppnin's ukase, Mencke's recovery, and rumours of a French move on Hannover.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Lord! The letter graciously issued to me by Your Most Serene Highness I have duly received, together with all the enclosures, through Herr Michaelis. As to the business itself, I can say with the most joyful heart that everything is going as we wish — Krotoszyn they are already giving Your Most Serene Highness quite certainly for the Suprenstis possessions. Only on account of Polajewo do they still seem to voice some misgivings — but according to the assurance of the Privy Finance Councillor Schulze, Your Most Serene Highness may obtain this one as well. The chief aim of my most ardent wish and of my honest zeal for Your Serene Highness's peace and contentment is as good as attained already, for Krotoszyn is and remains an inestimable [uncertain: Begrat] — with Polajewo too there are some 100000 Rthl to be gained; only the obstruction from the Chambers will cost me many a struggle — yet this too should be surmountable. My only sorrow is that His Serene Highness Prince George may not arrive there sooner than I am in Breslau — for I do not wish to depart from here sooner

to depart than I am sure of the matter. Should the Prince arrive in the Forte before I do, then let Your Serene Highness by all means detain him until I come, for otherwise I shall be made a liar and the good Prince will be led astray. It is, by God, no boasting — but I can assuredly affirm that none of all our officials knows the province, still less the interior of the affair, save he alone; and in Prince George's business it is required that a man who has knowledge of the matter and understands the language should come forward, and then much may be gained. Minister Hoym departed today for Pomerania for 10 days — the Monarch received him very graciously. On Thursday Minister von Voss returns, and then the matter will soon be brought into the clear — I sent him yesterday again an obliging letter, one likewise to Schulze, one to H. C. W. Beym, and one to the Colonel — the effect has throughout been very good, for I referred myself in every instance to the Cabinet letter as well as to that of the Herr Cabinet Councillor. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's Berlin, the 15th of October 1798. faithful most humble and obedient servant v Triebenfeld

The good Monarch is said not to be at all in good spirits. That truly grieves me — Prince Reppnin is said, so it is reported, to have received an ukase on his way not to show himself in Petersburg again — who knows whether this is not perhaps a masque? The good Privy Cabinet Councillor Mencke is said to be in activity again, since his health has notably improved. Between certain men, it is said, discord is supposed to reign; it would be a pity, for they are a pair of honest people. Her Serene Highness the Princess Sacken is in good health. That Count Hoym has now gone to Pomerania I much censure. The X Spinne was just here and says he knows for certain that the French mean to take Hannover; that this is why the King is so out of humour. But I do not believe a word of what he says; he has lied too much already.
