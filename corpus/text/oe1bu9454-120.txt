--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports Cabanis's return with news that Hussites in Hussenich, Rentsch and thereabouts are willing to buy, but too few for the number of Hufen, so an express has gone to their Moravian brethren; they accept 300 Rthl per Hufe and 15 Rthl yearly interest and mean to appear in corpore within a fortnight. Countess Schlabrendorff refuses to release the instrument before receiving the other 50; Hornuth arranges extension to 1 January 1805. Awaits Kunckel, and begs a couple of Thaler for Kunckel's destitute, sick mother.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Cabanis came back yesterday with the good news that the Hussites wish to buy.

In Hussenich, Rentsch and in that neighbourhood there are already a fair number who have at once shown the best will; since, however, the number of Hufen is too great for these, they have immediately sent an express to their Moravian brethren in the faith. The price of 300 Rthl per Hufe and 15 Rthl annual rent they have found fair, and they believe that, notwithstanding that the oppression in Bohemia has ceased, on account of the acceptable price they will soon have the number of required farmers together. At the latest within 14 days they wish to appear as a body – God grant His blessing – Cabanis is nevertheless to go out again [uncertain: hinweitem] in order to encourage the people, through the clergyman at Hussinitz, to the speediest pressing forward of the matter. The messenger from the Countess Schlabrendorff is also back. Here is the answer; it does not correspond to Your Most Serene Highness's wishes – She refuses [illegible] to hand over the instrument [uncertain: InstruQuist] any sooner, until she has not received the other 50 ℔ [uncertain: v.]. The term until the 1st of January 1805 is effected by Hornuth. He will, however, draw up the receipt in

such a form that Your Serene Highness may use it separately – The acceptance for the second instalment too is to be so drawn up that no misfortune can occur any more. I await Kunckel with longing – He does indeed write to his mother that he will arrive here these next days with several people; in the meantime, however, the time is dragging somewhat too long – I wish he were here already – Kunckel's mother lives here in the greatest destitution; if Your Serene Highness should wish to perform a work of mercy on this day, then send me a couple of Thaler for this poor woman, who lies sick and wretched – I do indeed give her some little, but this does not go far towards keeping oneself alive in these dear times.

Breslau, the 2nd of April

1804

v. Triebenfeld
