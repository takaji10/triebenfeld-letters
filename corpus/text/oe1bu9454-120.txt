--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports Cabanis's return with news that Hussites in Hussenich, Rentsch and thereabouts are willing to buy, but too few for the number of Hufen, so an express has gone to their Moravian brethren; they accept 300 Rthl per Hufe and 15 Rthl yearly interest and mean to appear in corpore within a fortnight. Countess Schlabrendorff refuses to release the instrument before receiving the other 50; Hornuth arranges extension to 1 January 1805. Awaits Kunckel, and begs a couple of Thaler for Kunckel's destitute, sick mother.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Cabanis came back yesterday with the good news that the Hussites wish to buy. In Hussenich, Rentsch and thereabouts there are already a considerable number who have at once shown the best will; since however the number of Hufen is too great for these, they have immediately sent an express to their Moravian brethren in the faith. The price of 300 Rthl per Hufe and 15 Rthl yearly interest they have found reasonable, and they believe that, notwithstanding that the oppression in Bohemia has ceased, they will soon, on account of the acceptable price, have the required number of farmers together. Within a fortnight at the latest they mean to appear in corpore — God grant His blessing — Cabanis is meanwhile to go out again [uncertain: hinweitem] in order to encourage the people, through the clergyman at Hussinitz, to the speediest prosecution of the matter. The messenger from the Countess Schlabrendorff is also back. Here is the answer, not corresponding to Your Most Serene Highness's wishes — She refuses flatly to give out the Instru[uncertain: Quiſt:] sooner, until she has received the other 50 ℔ v. The extension until the 1st of January 1805 Hornuth effects. But he will draw up the receipt in

such a form that Your Serene Highness may use it separately — The acceptance for the second term is likewise to be so drawn up that no mishap can occur any more. I look forward with longing to [uncertain: Wunchel/Kunckel] — He writes indeed to his mother that he will arrive here these days with several people; meanwhile the time does drag somewhat too long — I wish he were here already — The mother of the Kunckel lives here in the greatest destitution; should Your Serene Highness be minded to do a work of mercy on this day, then send me a couple of Thaler for this poor woman, who lies sick and wretched — I do give her a little, to be sure, but this does not go far enough for her to subsist in these dear times. Breslau, the 2nd of April 1804. v. Triebenfeld
