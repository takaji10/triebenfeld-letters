--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A royal grant by Friedrich Wilhelm, King of Prussia, adding to the estates of Betsche, Zagórów and Wienietz already given to his uncle Friedrich Ludwig, Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, on 19 June the outlying parcels: the villages of Stołuń, Kuligowo, Szarcz, Silna, the bailiwick of Zielomyśl, the Pomherr Hauländer settlement and Heydemühle; Swięcin; and Konty, Szedlemin, Botkow and Karezin. Granted heritably and in full property, subject to a fixed sovereign due and the allowance payable to church foundations, with authorities forbidden to disturb possession. A certified copy of 1802.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
We Friedrich Willhelm, by the Grace of God King of Prussia etc., do hereby, and by force of this Our open letter, for Ourselves and Our descendants and successors to the Crown, the Electorate, and Our remaining lands, witness and declare that We, out of the particular friendship and affection We bear towards the high-born Prince, Our amicably beloved uncle Friedrich Ludewig, reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, and likewise in order to give him a fresh proof of Our royal gratitude for the distinguished and fruitful services rendered to Us and to Our Royal House, have found Ourselves moved to grant, confer and convey to Your Lordship, in addition to the estates already conferred upon him by the instrument of the 19th of June, namely Betsche, Zagórów and Wienietz, the appurtenant parcels belonging thereto, namely:

1.) at Betsche, the villages of Stołuń(7), Kuligowo(8), Szarcz(9), the bailiwick of Zielomyśl(10), and Silna, and among the Hollander settlements the Pomherr Hollander settlement(12) and Heydemühle.

2.) at Zagórów, the little village of Swięcin, and finally

3.) near Wienietz, Konty, Szedlemin, Botkow, and Karezin[uncertain: Karezin], against payment of

1.) the sovereign dues hitherto discharged by the said villages, Hollander settlements and landholdings, as a fixed

and unalterable canon, and

2.) of the allowance due therefrom to the ecclesiastical corporations and foundations, to cede the same to him heritably and in full property, to confer and to convey it.

We do so likewise hereby, and by force of this present, out of royal power and plenitude, for Ourselves and Our descendants and successors to the Crown, the Electorate and Our remaining lands, in such manner and so that Our above-mentioned amicably beloved uncle, the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, shall irrevocably have, possess and enjoy the above-named appurtenant parcels belonging to the estates of Betsche, Zagórów and Wienietz, with all rights and privileges attaching thereto, as such have been exercised up to this time or may lawfully be exercised, with all the lands belonging thereto in fields, woodlands and meadows, likewise in ponds and waters, according to the measure of the lawful boundaries and bounds, just as everything has hitherto been used or may of right be used, from now on for all time, for himself and his descendants and heirs, as a true, well-acquired and unrestricted property from Us and Our descendants and successors; and that to him there shall also be

annexed the power and authority to dispose thereof at pleasure, subject to the conditions mentioned above, unhindered by any man.

We accordingly command and enjoin all Our supreme provincial collegiate boards, as also all authorities whom this concerns, herewith as graciously as earnestly, that they in no way hinder or disturb either the Lordship of the oft-mentioned Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen in the heritable and proprietary possession thus conferred upon him of the above-named appurtenant parcels belonging to the estates of Betsche, Zagórów and Wienitz, nor him or those to whom he may one day bequeath the same by force of this Our letter of conferment, or otherwise lawfully transfer it by other acts inter vivos aut mortis causa; but rather that they let His Lordship, and eventually also them, enjoy the same freely and quietly, do nothing themselves against it, nor occasion or permit that such be done by others, on pain of Our most supreme displeasure and unfailing severe punishment. In witness whereof etc.

Berlin, the 1st of December 1797.

Friedrich Willhelm

Letter of conferment for the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, to the heritable and proprietary possession of the appurtenant parcels belonging to the estates of Betsche, Zagórów and Wienietz.
