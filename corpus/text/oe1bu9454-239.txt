Durchlauchtigster Fürst
Gnädigster Fürst und Herr!
Mein Podagra behinderte mich vorgestern bis nach Neisse zu kommen
ich müste in der Nacht, weil der Weg Boden los war unter freiem
Himmel hinter Steine buvaquiren, da es um so fürchterlich
regnete so exponirte ich meinen Fuß völlig der Traufe - der
Schmerz ward nach und nach minder so daß ich heut schon den Stiefel
angepreßt habe - der König war 3 Tage hier fuhr eben als
ich hier eintraff mich Holz ab um in der Gegend nach Topliz
zu gehen, wo der Rußische Kayser Ihm bey seiner Schwester ist
wan spricht K. Franz würde auch dort eintreffend, denckende
Männer aber zweifeln daran – so viel man hier weiß
und übel zur höchsten eodem vergewißert ist, so tritt Osterreich
nicht zu – Es fängt alles im Lau zu werden, doch verspricht
es völlig neutral zu bleiben, daher auch in den Osterreichschen Staats
allewege die Zeichen der Neutralität öffentlich angeschlagen und
bekannt gemacht ſind - die Combinirten Mächte ſund durch den
Waffenstillstand schrecklich duplirt - Bülow und Czerniczeff

gar nicht anerkennen wollen, indes haben sie sich fügen müßen, und stehe
jezt in Berlin - das Corps was nach Hannover gegangen ist, in

gar aufgerieben werden, den Napoleon ist nach Paris geeilt
die guten Alte, und zum Theil kluge Männer prochezenen nicht viel
Gutes

Gen Gneizenau ist im Hauptquartier auch R. Valentini - ich muß heute
nach Neustad zum Gen. Ortel / den mein Plan hatt Beifall / so
wie ich von da retournire gehe ich nach Franckenstein pp. Min¬
Hard: sucht in der Meinung aller Güte sehr
Ew Durchlaucht geruhen der Frau v. Hahn zu sagen, daß ihr Brief nach

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Wartime news from Silesia: gout and a bottomless road forced a night in the open short of Neisse; the King has just left for Töplitz, where the Russian Emperor is, and Austria's accession is doubted, neutrality being posted publicly in the Austrian states. The armistice is judged to favour the enemy, Bülow and Chernyshev having had to submit, Napoleon hastening to Paris. Reports the government's move to Frankenstein, Gneisenau and Valentini at headquarters, approval of his own plan, and letters forwarded for Madame von Hahn and Chief Forest Master von Kleist.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

My gout hindered me the day before yesterday from getting as far as Neisse; in the night I had to bivouac under the open sky behind stones, because the road-bed was bottomless, and as it was raining so frightfully I exposed my foot entirely to the drip — the pain grew gradually less, so that today I have already pressed on the boot — the King was here 3 days; just as I arrived here he drove [uncertain: wood] off, to go into the district towards Topliz, where the Russian Emperor is with his sister; it is said King Franz would also arrive there, but thinking men doubt it – so far as one knows here, and it is ill assured to the highest eodem, Austria is not acceding – Everything is beginning to grow lukewarm, yet it promises to remain entirely neutral, and therefore in the Austrian states the signs of neutrality have everywhere been publicly posted up and made known - the Combined Powers are frightfully duplicated by the armistice - Bülow and Czerniczeff

would not acknowledge at all; meanwhile they have had to submit, and he now stands in Berlin - the corps that went to Hanover, to disembark itself at Emden, will now likewise remain ineffective, or even be worn down entirely, for Napoleon has hastened to Paris; the good old men, and in part clever men, prophesy little good. From here everything goes off and to its post; tomorrow the government too departs for Franckenstein - Merkel was here - and sets out thither early in a few [days] – the post, or the courier service, was re-established 8 days ago already - I therefore send this letter by the post. General Gneizenau is at headquarters, and R. Valentini also - today I must go to Neustad to General Ortel / for my plan has met with approval / and as soon as I return from there I go on to Franckenstein etc. Min Hard: seeks, in the opinion of all, much good. May Your Serene Highness deign to tell Madame von Hahn that her letter has been forwarded to Breslau - likewise I handed over the one to Chief Forest Master von Kleist, who however was away travelling, to his wounded son - It is in deepest reverence that I remain, until death, Your Serene Highness's

Neisse

19 June 1813

humble and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld
