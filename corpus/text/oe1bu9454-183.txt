--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports a lampoon circulating under the title Prussian Characters, which attacks the Prince as a man greatly in need of money and depicts Triebenfeld himself as an intriguer who exploits his employer's affairs to enrich himself, a former servant risen to nobility. The copy he had sent has been confiscated by the French authority. Authorship is attributed to Colonel Massenbach or Professor Buchholz; if Massenbach is proved the author, Triebenfeld offers his pistols. Rochritz is likewise abused.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
There has appeared here a lampoon under the title Prussian Characters, in which Your Serene Highness is indeed very much attacked, and even my own insignificant self figures in its account. It says: the Prince needs a great deal of money; he has a man of business in the person of the Councillor of War von Triebenfeld, but this man appears rather to make use of his own interest in the Prince's affairs and to enrich himself. He is an intriguing fellow who has worked his way up from a servant to a nobleman. – 1 had sent the book along, but even the French authority has had it confiscated – for the rest, the author is said to be Colonel Massenbach or the local Professor Buchholz. It [is] only fully proved that Massenbach is the fabricator, [in which case] my pistols stand at his service.

the [uncertain: better] Rochritz is also cruelly handled
