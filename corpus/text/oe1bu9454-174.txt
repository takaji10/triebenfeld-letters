--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Argues that combining the justiciary's post with cashier's duties would end fraud over actuary's fees, carriage services and board and save some 600 Rthl, with rising fees of 1200 Rthl leaving a surplus and making an advocate unnecessary. Asks the Prince to have Hahn frighten his own steward Käufer and forester Pelzold at Schweiner, chases 1390 due in the Lichnowchi matter, reports buyers ready to pay cash for several Hufen at Trąbczyn, returns an Amelang letter, and relays a 100,000 loan stalled by revision of assessments and news of over 300,000 Russian troops mobilised.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
the actuary is always paid separately, and at every act there is [uncertain: added] — this fraud amounts yearly to a good 200 Rthl; the carriage-services one could likewise reckon, as well as the board, at 200 Rthl, so that already a profit is made here. And if it be further assumed that he also takes over the post of a cashier and all the other business, then again at least 600 Rthl are saved here, and in all as much is gained as the barn brings in. Besides this, the fees will rise in future and amount to at least 1200 Rthl; in that way the justiciary will cost nothing, no advocate need be kept any longer, and there will still remain several hundred as the annual surplus.

Always, wherever anything can be effected by my good will, I shall strive with the most ardent zeal to advance Your Serene Highness's profit. It is however a misfortune for me that, while I labour here without rest for Your Serene Highness, my own household goes entirely to ruin — I cannot look to my estates, because I must be here. I hope this will have effect and the fellows would not go on cheating quite so unobserved, for a Prince is feared more than a knight errant wandering about. Has Your Serene Highness sent the 1390 dkg in the Lichnowchi matter? I have firmly promised it to the man.

Some people are awaiting me at Trąbczyn, who wish to buy several Hufen of land and to pay at once in cash; as soon as I get out there I shall see to this, and then Your Serene Highness will have the money back again at once – This would have been done already, but I cannot get away from here so as to leave the most important matter of all standing half-finished.

The Amelang letter I send back – May Your Serene Highness be pleased to write to the patron after all.
