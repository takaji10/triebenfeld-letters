--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports from Berlin that he will stay until the Quat: affair is clear, and sends Glenck ahead to take the Prince's orders. The King, Köckritz and Zastrow are well disposed, and the royal invitation should be accepted with a stay of three or four days. Recounts the Court scandal of Prince Solms's secret marriage to the widowed Princess Louis, her loss of Royal Highness rank and his transfer to Ansbach; warns against Hoym and Wedell's double-dealing, dismisses the secularisation business as a farce better pursued in Vienna with Prince George, and notes Haugwitz's rise and Schulemburg's removal.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

However full of torment my stay here gradually becomes, I shall nevertheless hold out until I see the matter of the quarters [uncertain: Quat:] set straight — Glenck, however, I am dispatching with all haste, so that Your Most Serene Highness may have someone there to whom Your Highness can give all commands.

How hard the struggle here has been Glenck will depict to Your Serene Highness; good will be the fruits of the [uncertain: victory] all the more [uncertain: full of blessing] and

proved.

That the King, Köckritz and Zastrow are well disposed toward Your Serene Highness is proved by the invitation — it makes the journey a little toilsome for Your Highness, but the honour is all the greater, and if Your Serene Highness departs from there on the 14th or at the latest the 15th, then

Your Highness might very conveniently remain here 3 to 4 days — The Monarch must have no good reasons for wishing Your Serene Highness's route to be laid here by way of Berlin. Thoroughly I know nothing — every [uncertain: evil] probably has to do with Prince Solms. He has already, five months ago, married the widowed Princess Louis in secret. The King is greatly incensed at this, and it contributes much to his indisposition; the Queen, however, still passes from one chamber into the other. Yesterday evening the Princess was stripped of the dignity of Royal Highness, her court household withdrawn, other dispositions made regarding the children, and now she is to depart in haste with her husband, who is being transferred as Major with the Hussars to Ansbach — that this creates a mighty terror at Court, Your Serene Highness can

easily imagine. And I believe it will be an alleviation to the Monarch if he can have Your Highness here for a couple of days.

The pregnancy of the poor [uncertain: widowed] Princess at first gave the public much occasion for unspeakable [uncertain: gossip], and still no one knows the least thing about this matter, actually settled beforehand. Be pleased to say nothing about it there either.

Of Minister Hoym's conduct more by word of mouth. On one occasion he spoke very well before the King in Köckritz's presence — but in secret, with his Wedde, he delivered a contemptible opinion, on which the very same Herr von Voss keeps relying. For that very reason I was glad to see that the King invited Your Serene Highness here so flatteringly — for now the B— will tremble again — because it will torture their conscience. To Her Serene Highness the Princess of Sacken, Your Serene Highness

say nothing of it here, that I have disclosed to Your Highness the base tricks of Hoym and Wedell. Also let

nothing be noticed there. As to the secularisation business, Your Serene Highness should by all means say nothing — this is a mere farce; Prince George strongly advises against it — should anything ever be able to be done in the matter, then we will do it in Vienna — I promise Your Serene Highness to work with Prince George [uncertain: Borten] in due season as well as here — for from our side nothing is being done — and if you can obtain something and enlarge your realms, then it is all one whether you obtain it from thence or through action here — settled it remains — if you have much of it, all the more compliments will be paid you.

Haugwitz is now [uncertain: squinting at] a great role, but Schulemburg they are removing, because the latter always wants war. In deepest submission I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Berlin, the 9th of January

1799.

most humble and faithful servant

v. Triebenfeld.
