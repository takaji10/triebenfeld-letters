--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports from Berlin that he will stay until the Quat: affair is clear, and sends Glenck ahead to take the Prince's orders. The King, Köckritz and Zastrow are well disposed, and the royal invitation should be accepted with a stay of three or four days. Recounts the Court scandal of Prince Solms's secret marriage to the widowed Princess Louis, her loss of Royal Highness rank and his transfer to Ansbach; warns against Hoym and Wedell's double-dealing, dismisses the secularisation business as a farce better pursued in Vienna with Prince George, and notes Haugwitz's rise and Schulemburg's removal.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Lord! However full of torment my stay here becomes by degrees, I will nevertheless hold out until I see the Quat: matter brought into the clear — Glenck, however, I dispatch with all haste, so that Your Most Serene Highness may have someone there to whom Your Highness can give all commands. How hard the struggle has been here, Glenck will describe to Your Serene Highness; good things will be the fruits of the victory, all the more delightful and proven. That the King, Köckritz and Zastrow are well disposed toward Your Serene Highness is proved by the invitation — it makes the journey a little laborious for Your Highness, but the honour is all the greater, and if Your Serene Highness departs from there on the 14th or at the latest the 15th, then Your Highness could

Your Highness might very conveniently remain here 3 to 4 days — The Monarch must have no good reasons for wishing Your Serene Highness's route to be laid here by way of Berlin. Thoroughly I know nothing — every [uncertain: Bees] probably has to do with Prince Solms — He married the widowed Princess Louis in secret five months ago. The King is greatly incensed at it, and this contributes much to his indisposition; the Queen, however, keeps moving from one chamber to another. Yesterday evening the Princess was stripped of her dignity as Royal Highness, the household was withdrawn, other arrangements made concerning the children, and now she is to depart in haste with her husband, who is being transferred as Major with the Hussars to Ansbach — that this causes a mighty terreur at Court, Your Serene Highness can

easily imagine. And I believe it will be a relief to the Monarch if he can have Your Highness here for a couple of days. The pregnancy of the Princess, [uncertain: verwerten] by the [uncertain: Armez], at first gave the public much occasion for unspeakable speculation, and still no one knows the least of this matter now actually settled upon. Deign to say nothing of it there either. Concerning Minister Hoym's conduct, more by word of mouth. On one occasion he spoke very well before the King in Köckritz's presence — but in secret, with his Wedell, he gave a base opinion, upon which the said v. Voss constantly relies. For that very reason I was glad that the King invited Your Serene Highness here so flatteringly — then the B— will tremble again — because their conscience will torture them. Tell the Princess Sacken, Serene Highness — Your Serene Highness

say nothing here of my having disclosed to Your Highness the wicked tricks of Hoym and Wedell. Let nothing be noticed of it there either. As for the secularisation business, let Your Serene Highness by all means say nothing — this is a mere farce; Prince George strongly advises against it — should something ever be able to be done in the matter, then we will do it in Vienna — I promise Your Serene Highness to work with Prince George there in due time as here — for on our side nothing is done — and if you can obtain something and enlarge your realms, it is all one whether you obtain it from that quarter or through action here — settled it remains — if you have much upon it, the more compliments will be paid you. Haugwitz now plays a great part, but Schulemburg is being removed, because the latter always wants war. In deepest submission I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's Berlin, the 9th of January 1799. most humble and faithful servant v. Triebenfeld.
