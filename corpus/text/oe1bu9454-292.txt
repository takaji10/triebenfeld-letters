--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A report from Vienna on efforts against the administrator Cosmar, who has never deposited the sums fixed in the 1799 instrument and the 1811 settlement with the Brieg Higher Provincial Court, leaving creditors unpaid; Stägemann defends him furiously and would refer all to the Minister of Justice, while the State Chancellor Hardenberg was moved by the plea that the old Prince must live. Also the compensation claim before the Emperor, Stein's support, Prince August's 100,000 Rthl now ordered paid, and a proposal to intercept Hardenberg at Neisse.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Serene Highness, Prince!

etc. etc.

The compensation matter is acknowledged and lies before the Emperor; from the Russian side, His Excellency the Minister von Stein has promised to do everything possible. From the Prince Hardenberg I shall also obtain the assurance in writing. In the Courlandish matter little more will happen; the result is, the King and the Prince Chancellor have promised the ladies of S. to pay, and there one shall indeed see what the gentlemen will do — every thinking man says that Your Serene Highness must now come out of the bond (ex nexu). Mr. p. Stägemann plays the man of business for the 7th Pingatilli — it is enough to drive one mad. The order concerning the depositing of the monies on Cosmar's side has been sent to the Minister of Justice with the reminder to hold the same to good order. Since in the meantime, however, complaints have come in from von Kuh, from Sobottendorff, from Hahn, Mr. Stägemann has

proposed to the Chancellor not to plunge into the [uncertain: Wesben] besides, but to put everything away from himself and refer it to the Minister of State for Justice. Mr. p. Stägemann now plays the defender of Cosmar besides, and screws up rage and vengeance that one should wish to blacken so honest a man.

In a short conference held 3 days ago he flew up in a fury. Your Serene Highness cannot occupy yourself further with the matter, it all belongs before the Minister — the querulousness of the old Prince bears the stamp of untruth — the old Prince is a nullity in the matter, he has nothing to seek in the estates, he has been settled with – Prince August is here and he knows Cosmar as an honourable man, he has himself named him his 9th plenipotentiary. Mr. von Triebenfeld acts most indiscreetly in running to His Serene Highness so [uncertain: quaett]. I answered that I was far from wishing to be burdensome to His Serene Highness. I demand only that my princely client be protected from death by hunger

<PAGE BREAK>

together with those about him, so both the Prince and I claimed only, and that with the greatest right, that Cosmar should deposit the sums determined in the instrument of 1799 and in the settlement of 1811 with the Brieg Higher Provincial Court, wherein this court of justice had admonished him so many times; on Cosmar's side that never happens. He always turned himself out defiantly, and the creditors with whom the Prince [illegible construction] and pleaded with the Prince for what was theirs – the sums heaped up from year to year, Cosmar would not be solvent for them, and the question arose who was one day to make good these sums.

The Prince Chancellor was moved and said, the old Prince must indeed live, and where then do the revenues go?

Stägemann [uncertain: wept] that this would come out well enough if the matter were examined closely. Certainly the Prince would have received everything, and Cosmar too would surely have had to pay much to the creditors. After several further turns of phrase the matter was broken off — and I am still awaiting the first line of an answer. From more recent letters I hear that Cosmar is really said to have been denounced, on account of embezzled family monies, from Courland, by a man from abroad – and that this matter is now to be investigated first in Berlin. Upon Prince Adolph's affair the matter is said to have great influence. Stägemann lately wished to defend him here too.

Prince August's matter is now so firmly settled by Cabinet Order that the 100000 thaler are to be paid to him absolutely and at once. Cosmar wished at the last to save himself with the evasion that the old Prince owed the Landgrave of Hesse a sum of 300000 Rthl and that the Schlawenschitz estates stood liable for it in subsidium; but the Privy Councillor Philippsborn reported to the King and moved that the King ought surely to value his own subjects rather than

<PAGE BREAK>

foreigners. I proved that the debt lies solely upon the principalities of Öhringen, Neuenstein and [uncertain: Ingelfingen], and thereupon the King ordered the Chancellor that the monies should be paid; the matter nevertheless lay another 14 days, and about 6 days ago a fulminant letter went out to M. Kircheisen – that these 100000 Rthl should be paid at once and without any contradiction.

Ernst August has thanked the Prince Hardenberg handsomely and at the same time expressed the wish that Your Serene Highness should no longer be so pressed by Cosmar. I believe he will now, since Hahn is coming thither, soon withdraw the power of attorney – make common cause with Your Serene Highness, and only then will Cosmar's grave be dug.

The Prince Chancellor will probably not depart from here until the 5th of June – he goes by way of Neisse: how would it be if Your Serene Highness were to approach him there in person? It is necessary to speak with him about your military matter – I would hasten ahead with courier horses – The cook Murat has received his wages – vengeance has swiftly overtaken this gentleman.

Reverently I remain,

Your Serene Highness's

The King departed last night

from the Countess Zichy.

Vienna, the 27th of May.

1815

most faithful and obedient servant

von Triebenfeld
