So weit ich Ewr Durchlaucht Laage kenne und so viel bisher Verstand

schnellere und beßere Rettung nicht für Ewr Durchlaucht, als diese
Stössel und Weigel werden befriediget — Sie erhalten den von die

--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld meldet, dass Baron Stössel die Güter von Zagórów nach seinem Plan für seine Forderungen annehmen will, und bittet dringend um Antwort, ob der Fürst die Sache so mit Stössel und Weigel abgemacht wissen will. Stössel verlangt ein Schreiben des Fürsten; Graf Wartensleben rät, darin zu erklären, wegen des Testaments der Schwiegermutter könne Koszęcin nicht bezahlt werden, daher gebe er Zagórów, dem Triebenfeld die Güter von Blizanow zufügen wolle. Köckritz ruft Triebenfeld nach Berlin.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld reports that Baron Stössel is willing to take over the Zagórów estates for his claims according to his plan, and urgently asks for an answer whether the Prince wants the matter settled in this way with Stössel and Weigel. He says Stössel demands a letter from the Prince; Count Wartensleben advises that it should state that, because of the mother-in-law's will, Koszęcin cannot be paid for, and that he is therefore giving Zagórów, to which Triebenfeld wishes to add the Blizanow estates. Köckritz calls Triebenfeld to Berlin.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

That Baron Stössel is willing to accept the Zagórów estates for his claims according to my plan, Your Serene Highness will be pleased to gather from the enclosed letter of the chief district officer Königs. Now everything depends on the Baron's coming here soon; then the matter is also settled with old Weigel himself — For a couple of days now I have been awaiting the future [uncertain: return] of the messenger I sent to Your Serene Highness on this account. Every delay may be harmful to the good cause; had I people on whom I could rely, and horses, then one rider should now be chasing the next, but ever since my old honest Richter took up winter quarters I have had no faithful man, and no money and no horses either.

Accordingly I am sending this messenger as well, and beg for a speedy answer: "Whether Your Serene Highness wishes the matter with Stössel and Weigel to be settled according to my plan, or whether Your Highness has some other plan?

So far as I know Your Serene Highness's situation, and so far as Mother Nature has hitherto given me understanding, I see no more honourable, swifter and better deliverance for Your Serene Highness than this: Stössel and Weigel are satisfied — You then receive from the

the moneys left behind by the deceased Princess Sacken, which are to be applied to the purchase of two entailed estates for Prince Adolph and Prince Alexander, back in cash to the amount of 670000 Rthl — with that, in the present times, you can defy hell to its face — In Berlin, where only the money-bag counts, this will make a din that will go thundering up through the clouds. I beg for the speediest answer, and I remain, until death, Your Serene Highness's

Blizanow, 1st December

1811

wholly most obedient and faithful servant

v Triebenfeld

P[ro]. M[emoria].

I had already sealed this letter and handed it to the messenger [uncertain: when] Count Cesur Wartensleben, who comes directly from Wrocław, reports that Stössel, by his assurance, will enter into the business together with Weigel. He wishes, however, to have a letter from Your Serene Highness stating that you yourself would consent to the plan drawn up by me. Cesur Wartensleben advises that Your Serene Highness might write to him, old Stössel, with the greatest haste — "that you had always been minded to keep Koszęcin, and that through the death of your mother-in-law you had believed you would be put in a position to be able to pay for Koszęcin — but since the Princess had made her will in such a manner that nothing remained for you to dispose of, you therefore wished to give the Zagórów estates in satisfaction of him / the said Stössel / and of Weigel, to which Triebenfeld wished to add the Blizanow estates as well. To that end you had charged Triebenfeld with settling everything, and requested him, if this were agreeable to him, to enter into negotiation with Triebenfeld over all these matters." I am now wholly, wholly firmly convinced that this business will come about, if Your Serene Highness has no other plans. — To that end I am [uncertain: sending] in all haste a mounted messenger — who is still to be given 5 Rthl there

to be given — May Your Serene Highness be pleased to send me by this fellow the letter and all your other commands in the very [uncertain: speediest] manner, so that I myself may hurry with the letter to Wrocław and fetch Stössel in person — the messenger has promised to be back here in 5 days — Be pleased to make haste — the matter is important, great and [illegible], and secures for you, gracious Prince, the happiest future.

For the rest, Köckritz writes to me: I am to come to Berlin soon, soon. Much was in train and all would yet come right. My eldest son, who was serving in the 2nd [uncertain: Silesian Hussar] Regiment, has been called to Berlin and will be appointed in the Garde du Corps as second lieutenant — the King spoke with him at length and enquired after one thing and another, particularly Köckritz perhaps — In the [illegible] matter only by word of mouth.

v Triebenfeld
