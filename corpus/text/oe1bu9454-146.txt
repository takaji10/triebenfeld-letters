--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that the Mennonites, through their elder Pankratz, mean to buy Zagorowo and Trąbczyn outright at the Prince's price and divide the land among themselves; Triebenfeld will travel to Lunau to close with them. He asks the Prince to obtain written undertakings from Building Councillor Glenck returning 300 Morgen and admitting the komornik holdings were wrongly sold to von Małachowski, since these claims have stalled the valuation. Complains of the surveyors and of district administrator von Blomberg, and reports an execution for 6,500 Rthl in the Przespolewski case.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord!

From the enclosed letter of Pankrat Your Most Serene Highness will graciously be pleased to satisfy yourself that the Mennonites will buy. Pankratz is one of their principal elders, and what he promises every member of that sect is bound to keep. I shall therefore travel tomorrow to Lunau and try my fortune. The verbal assurance given by Pankratz to the old district messenger is that the Mannauſten intend to buy the Zagorowo and Trąbczyn estates altogether and to divide them among themselves at the price fixed for them by Your Serene Highness. God grant now its further prospering. For the rest, I greatly fear that I shall return with a second bilious fever. The only honest man who is here is the [uncertain: suntitz] burgomaster Schenck. His assessment will turn out well, and in case of need money can at any time be raised upon it. Only he lacks the contract with the miller's [text: "das Müller weil"] in Trąbczyn - Mr. Glenck has that - and then the contract concluded on the 5th of September with Mr. Building Councillor Glenck stands against them, in which 300 Morgen of manorial land and the settlement of all dues from the bailiwick are promised to him. [uncertain: Gleich] has sent this contract here

to Tappert, so that he should give it out publicly. Tappert at once handed the contract over to the assessment commission and protested that they ought not to bring those lands into the valuation there. The commune has grown suspicious at this and protests against the taking away of these lands, a part of which belongs to it as pasture - this now hinders the commission, so that it cannot work further, the more so as [uncertain: Suppert] has also produced a sale contract of Glenck's, according to which Glenck has sold along with it the komornik holdings in the whole of Wrąbczin to a certain von Małachowski. It would be necessary that Glenck issue a written undertaking in which he says that he has given back the 300 Morgen to Your Serene Highness, and also that he declare that he could not sell the komornik holdings and that this was an error. Then all the negotiations with the Swięcia people are missing here - perhaps Glenck has something of them there; so too the contract of the miller's wife from Wrąbczin would be there, as also the contract with the Swięcier Hollanders; and if Glenck were willing to issue the written undertakings, may Your Serene Highness graciously be pleased to transmit them to me by this express messenger, so that the matter might be put in order.

The dismemberment will in any event be brought to a happy conclusion, for a great many people, who have been noted down here as ready to buy at any [uncertain: Wird], have come forward here besides the Mennonites, so that one has a choice. Meanwhile I wish to conclude with the Mennonites, because from them I receive the most money and go the most securely.

Otherwise they have only played the fool and yet sown the fields - von Otocki has followed this example at Kopojno, and there will not be much left to sell. These are indeed worse than wicked men – I believed a great deal was to be got out of it there; what will now become of it?

It is singular that when a German comes to Poland he is at once corrupted to the very blood: thus for example the surveyors have not yet finished the map, and nevertheless the laziest of them, Leopold, has already had 368 Rthl paid out to him – which has also been given to the wretch – Wollenhaupt is a crafty rogue; meanwhile I shall dispatch this scoundrel so neatly that he shall not know how he got out of Trąbczyn.

The district administrator von Blomberg is the worst [uncertain: Nabuliet] that ever was on earth - if one did not need the miserable

scoundrel so exceedingly for the dismemberment, I would spit in the fellow's face – In the meantime I shall treat him with coldness and such freedom as is possible until I no longer need him - Your Serene Highness may meanwhile be assured that he shall do you no harm, for I have taken care of that in every way. To set down his knaveries here, and what base cunning he has again employed in order to learn the state of the funds here, would be too lengthy; I shall relate them to Your Serene Highness by word of mouth; enough that he is a bad fellow, who is better suited to the head of a band of robbers than to be district administrator - I must gather all my patience, so as not to betray myself.

In the matter of Przespolewski the government has sent execution here, that Your Serene Highness should at once pay 6500 Rthl. I have immediately had what is necessary submitted against it.

1. Przespolewski has ceded the claim to a certain Magnuski

2. Szczucki has laid an attachment upon it

3. The document has been lost; nothing can be cancelled and consequently nothing paid either, for Honrichs too would pay nothing sooner than when all had been cancelled. This here the government [uncertain: gut]; how can it proceed here so infamously? In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's — In the matter of Radzinski everything is provided for, but the registry is in a devilish disorder; thus I found today the enclosed piece among the [uncertain: Sentachen] papers, and this belongs here altogether, one before the town [illegible][uncertain: uft] here — most humble and obedient servant, von Triebenfeld

Trąbczyn

the 17th of November

1804
