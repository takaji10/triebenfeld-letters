--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Point-by-point report: receipt of 1000 Rthl through Master of the Horse Bally, 255 rt sent for his children, and 245 Rthl for redeeming iron. Honrichs cleared, Hawich unmasked and expelled by Count Kwilecki, Hecker discharged, Cabanis defended. Presses for lifting the Zagorowo sequestration by depositing 5100 Rthl interest against Mendel Oppenheimer's disputed 102000 Rthl claim; Stenger dismissed for Raszkowski at 300 Rthl; vindication of Trąbczyn and Kaemen from the Dąbska, with 60000 Rthl damages, remission of a third of colonists' interest, Betschin and Wyttow arrears, the Collonmasche affair, and Brzechsta's inability to buy the South Prussian estates.

P. M. ad 1. the 1000 Rthl were duly delivered to me by the Master of the Horse Herr Bally against my receipt and that of Honrichs; of these 600 Rthl were sent in cash within the hour without fail, and out of the remainder various costs were satisfied, in the matter in which I had execution against me. ad 2. I express my most deeply moved thanks for the two hundred and fifty-five rt. in coin sent to my children, which I shall gratefully return or bring to account. Should the 245 Rthl which had been assigned to me for the redemption of the iron, but which are here charged again, still be received, I would most humbly bring this too into account with the iron delivery. ad 3. Honrichs, Hawich and Cabanis have been here. Honrichs's affair has been cleared up and he was not found to be a villain, whereas Hawich has been unmasked as the worst of fellows. Hecker will now be rewarded according to his dealings, his paper security returned to him, and he will be discharged. Cabanis remains, in my unassailable opinion, ever true to his generally known honesty, cash controller as soon as God helps and Your Serene Highness has all your estates back in possession. Hawich will be entirely dismissed, and Count Kwilecki has already thrown him out of the court-room himself as a slanderer and Nabulistic pettifogger and forbidden him all access; as to what concerns ad 4., the situation of the Zagorowo and Trąbczyn estates, the sequestration at Zagorowo must now absolutely be lifted; to that end I have had a register given me by Honrichs and Cabanis of what can be collected in cash at once. The sum stated by the Jew Mendel Oppenheimer would amount to 102000 Rthl (which, however, is again more than 20000 Rthl), and in which matter the suit is pending in Berlin. Meanwhile the interest thereon would come to about 5100 Rthl a year; these would have to be deposited at once with the protestation that the suit concerning the Jew Oppenheimer's claim and his false cession is pending in Berlin, so that the interest might not be handed over to him. The statement of Honrichs and Cabanis gives 13610 Rthl, the annual interest 5100; after these 8510 would remain. This matter now lies before the Justice of the Peace Count Kwilecki; I have ordered payment of the 5100 Rthl [Anweisung] and insist upon the immediate lifting of the sequestration, or of what is now called the administration, the more so as of late the general lifting of sequestration has been conceded, provided only that the running interest is settled.

On the 28th of this month the hearing in the matter is set down; I hope for the best outcome, and note most obediently that the arrears, in so far as such come in little by little, will cover everything. For the rest, however, it will be necessary to cultivate the two judges, both at Konin and at Kalisz, as much as possible and to keep them in their good disposition, for which purpose I must come very close upon my own means. Stenger I have had to let go. He was too costly for me; before he would set a pen to paper he wanted 2000 Rthl in advance; I have now wholly abandoned him and engaged a certain Raszkowski who promises to work all of it for 300 Rthl - with the exceedingly good disposition of the Justice of the Peace and of the Tribunal Presidents von Kwilecki and Kiełczewski there is now simply no need of so damnably costly an advocate, who besides the advance of 2000 Rthl also wished to be bathed in Hungarian wine and champagne. The Dąbska has swung herself into possession of the estates without any decrees or judgments whatever, by the help of two wretched fellows, a certain Scholrech and Lipelli. The two aforenamed judges Kur[illegible] and [illegible]geti have [illegible] caused a summons to sue and promise the very promptest satisfaction - I am almost convinced of the most desirable outcome, as of my own [outcome] - I have seized with favour the opportunity offered to me by God in these men, in order not only to save my princely friend's property speedily amid these never before anticipated happy events, but if possible to increase it. Indeed, with the vindication of the Trąbczyn estates I join at once the Kaemen ones (which would have caused the worthy Prince much grief), as also thereafter the rescue of the Brzyz estates, which must in the end be regarded as an equivalent for the damage inflicted upon the Prince by the Dąbska, because otherwise there would be no [uncertain: fund] available to obtain an indemnification from the notorious lady. This is indeed the advice of Count Kwilecki, who has it said to me that as soon as the decree or judgment concerning

Trąbczyn is there, I too should press so firmly for compensation for damages and should not be bashful in setting the costs - I think some 60000 Rthl will be neither too little nor yet so very much. So much on the subject, or on the vindication of the Trąbczyn and Kaemen estates. Concerning the lamentations of Grotowski, Lichnowski, Zerboni and Eysenhardt, who have [uncertain: moneys] upon Trąbczyn and Szetlewek [text lost], there is nothing to be said at present - the vindication of the estates also removes the worry for the future. I believe that if the Prince gets the estates back, 15000 Rthl are at once to be had from the tenant Smid, for he will not willingly go out and leave in the lurch the rent he has paid in advance to the Dąbska; with the aforesaid 15000 Rthl of rent one pays the four men their interest and they will gladly be quiet - Enough: everything depends upon the swiftest vindication of these estates; if this follows as desired and as it is now set in train, then the greatest worry is removed and the credit restored at the same time. For the rest, that tableau which I gave His Serene Highness three years ago remains always the basis of what the estates are, once Trąbczyn is regained, and what they will be in the end. The war will cause a shortfall, but meanwhile much interest that has not been raised has accrued, and this in the end covers the shortfall, so that the sum will always remain the same. The dismemberment stands as it is set out in that tableau - especially once Trąbczyn shall first have been rescued again, it must not suffer the least alteration. For neither Honrichs, Hawich, Hecker, nor the Karzunki had any right to appropriate to themselves a single Morgen of land, and not a single one shall remain in possession with my consent; rather, the lands must be given back and, according to the plan I once made and which was itself approved by the former Chamber for heavy money, be made known through colonists and those be established thereon. For the reassurance of the best of Princes I will note only this further here concerning the Trąbczyn affair: that the Dąbska, with the request to have the Trąbczyn estates entered in the mortgage book in her name, has been refused three times, and the last time in particular by the Tribunal President v. Kiełczewski with the words - that she could by no means be regarded as proprietress of the Trąbczyn estates, she possessed them against right, and until she had come to an arrangement with the Prince Hohenlohe and shown her capacity to possess, her Tit: post: could not be allowed her. This is surely the [uncertain: clearest] proof that the Trąbczyn estates must absolutely be rescued. The gain therein is not so insignificant as short-sighted people may perhaps believe. The revenues therefrom will in the sequel be over 16000 Rthl; the entry-moneys which have still to come in will be sufficient to pay off the debts charged upon them, and then the revenue is not to be despised. Moreover, when the Prince gets the estates back, it

will give a good complexion to his affairs and help restore his credit; the vindication of the lordship of Trąbczyn also has for its aim the recovery of the Kaemen estates, and thereby the Prince escapes a vexation which might yet draw upon him unspeakable annoyance and lawsuits for a series of years. As to my coming to Schlawenschitz, this will certainly follow as soon as I have handed over my estates; until now it was a pure impossibility. It was not want of money alone that held me back from it; it was the total loss of my estates, which I had to be at pains to set aside and to forestall all mischief. Now, through the friendly intercessions of His Excellency the worthy General v. Köckritz, I am so far advanced that the sequestration here must give way; to that end I have found 2 [uncertain: solid] tenants who will take over the estates at once; indeed I have settled everything, and, to win calm, to have a secure establishment, so as to be able to live free and not to have to want in future. As to the Collonmasche affair, this is only now [uncertain: an effort], a gnawing worm at the heart; I think on it day and night; it is once begun, and must now be brought honourably to an end. Gladly would I have flown from one pole to the other on that account, to discover the golden fleece, but the loss of my estates, the misery of my poor children, were an indescribable hindrance and a barrier which I was able to burst or remove by my own strength.

Now that God sends help from all sides, I shall soon be placed in a position to act again as a free man. My first business, or my first journey, will be to Schlawenschitz, in order then to pursue this affair. I foresee that here I shall have to fight again with a hundred-headed hydra, the more so as the Grand Chancellor B - appears to be prejudiced against it; but that shall not cripple my courage, rather the more inflame my zeal, so as to bring this business too to the desired [uncertain: end].
