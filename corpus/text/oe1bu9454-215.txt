--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld bestätigt 1000 Rthl über Bally. Hawich und Hecker sollen entlassen werden. Er drängt nach Anweisung von 5100 Rthl Zinsen auf Aufhebung der Sequestration von Zagórów. Die Dąbska habe Trąbczyn ohne Urteil in Besitz genommen, ihre Eintragung ins Hypothekenbuch sei abgewiesen; er betreibt die Vindikation von Trąbczyn und Kamionna. Von Honrichs, Hawich und Hecker angeeignete Gründe sollen zurück und mit Colonisten besetzt werden; den Colonisten der Herrschaft Zagórów soll dieses Jahr ein Drittel der Zinsen erlassen werden.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld confirms 1000 Rthl through Bally. He says Hawich and Hecker are to be dismissed. After the payment of 5100 Rthl in interest he presses for the lifting of the sequestration of Zagórów. He says Dąbska has taken possession of Trąbczyn without a judgment and that her entry in the mortgage book has been rejected; he is pursuing the recovery of Trąbczyn and Kamionna. Land appropriated by Honrichs, Hawich and Hecker is to be returned and settled with colonists; the colonists of the lordship of Zagórów are to be remitted a third of their interest this year.

Pro Memoria.

ad 1. The 1000 Rthl were duly delivered to me by the master of the horse Bally against my own and Honrichs's receipt; of these, 600 Rthl were sent in cash that very hour to [text lost: garbled] and with the remainder various costs were satisfied, in cases where I was under execution.

ad 3. So Honrichs, Hawich and Cabanis have been here. Honrichs's affair has been cleared up and he was not found to be a scoundrel, whereas Hawich has been unmasked as the very worst of fellows. Hecker will now be rewarded according to his dealings, his paper security returned to him, and he will be dismissed. Hawich is to be entirely dismissed, and Count Kwilecki has already thrown him out of the courtroom himself as a slanderer and pettifogging hole-and-corner scribbler and forbidden him all access — as to

ad 4. what concerns the situation of the Zagórów and Trąbczyn estates, the sequestration must now absolutely be lifted in the case of Zagórów. To that end I have had a schedule drawn up by Honrichs and Cabanis of what can be collected in cash at once. The sum stated by the Jew Mendel Oppenheimer would amount to 102000 Rthl / which, however, is more than 20000 Rthl again /, and in which case the suit is pending in Berlin.

The statement of Honrichs and Cabanis gives 13610 Rthl, the annual interest 5100 Rthl.

After these, 8510 would remain — this matter now lies before the justice of the peace Count Kwilecki; I have given instruction for the 5100 Rthl and insist upon the instant lifting of the sequestration, or of what is now called the administration, all the more since of late the general lifting of sequestration has been conceded, provided only that the running interest is settled.

On the 28th of this month the hearing in the matter is set down; I hope for the best outcome and observe most obediently that the arrears, should such come in by degrees, will cover everything.

For the rest, however, it will be necessary to cultivate the two judges, both at Konin and at Kalisz, as far as possible, and to keep them in their good disposition, since to that end I must encroach very closely upon my own means — Stenger I have had to let go. On account of Witów I have a lawsuit on my hands through the Schlieffen woman — She has still 6000 Rthl of purchase money to claim; at the time I would not let her see the contract with Meierowitz, in order to secure to His Serene Highness the 500 Rthl of hereditary quit-rent — I gave the lady a written undertaking that I would stand surety to her for the [uncertain: balance] of the purchase price — In the course of the war Meierowitz has paid nothing, and now the woman has sued me. For the rest, Meierowitz owes the 6000 Rthl of purchase money to the Schlieffen woman, as also the annual interest for the last five years to the Prince, amounting to 2500 Rthl — the matter will be prosecuted at Kalisz, and a separate tribunal advocate must be engaged for it. Meanwhile such stuff oppresses me greatly; it causes much cost and diminishes my credit. Nevertheless I shall bear this, and the Grattenau, Deter and Epstein affairs, in patience for the sake of the worthy good Prince's Serene Highness until God helps. Things must one day be otherwise and also better; for the reassurance of the best of Princes I will only note here this further point on the Trąbczyn affair:

"that the Dąbska, with her petition to have the Trąbczyn estates entered in the mortgage book in her name, has been refused three times, and the last time in particular by the tribunal president von Kiełczewski in these words — that she can by no means be regarded as proprietress of the Trąbczyn estates, that she holds them unlawfully, and that until she has come to terms with the Prince of Hohenlohe and proved her capacity to possess, her title of possession cannot be allowed her." This surely is the [uncertain: most telling] proof that the Trąbczyn estates must absolutely be rescued. The gain in it is not so trifling as short-sighted persons may perhaps believe. The revenues from it will hereafter be over 16000 Rthl; the entry moneys which must still come in will be sufficient to extinguish the debts charged upon it, and then the revenue is not to be despised. Besides, when the Prince recovers the estates, it will

give a good complexion to his affair and help restore his credit; also the vindication of the lordship of Trąbczyn has as its object the recovery of the Kamionna estates, whereby the Prince escapes a vexation which could yet have drawn upon him unspeakable annoyance and lawsuits for a series of years.

As concerns my coming to Sławięcice, this will certainly follow as soon as I have handed over my estates; until now it has been a pure impossibility. It was not want of money at all that held me back from it; it was the total loss of my estates, which I had to be at pains to clear aside and to forestall all mischief. Now, through the friendly intercessions of His Excellency the worthy General von Köckritz, I am so far advanced that the sequestration here must give way; to that end I have found two solid lessees who will take the estates over at once; indeed I have settled everything, and, to gain calm, to have a secure establishment so as to be able to live free and not to have to suffer want in future.

As concerns the Collonna affair, this is only now my endeavour, a gnawing worm at the heart; I think on it day and night; it has once been begun, and must now be honourably

brought to an end.

Gladly would I have flown from one pole to the other in the matter to discover the golden [uncertain: fleece], but the loss of my estates, the misery of my poor children, were an indescribable hindrance and a barrier which I was able to burst or to clear away by my own strength.

Now that God sends help from all sides, I shall soon be put in a position to act again as a free man. My first business, or my first journey, will be to Sławięcice, in order then to pursue this matter. I foresee that here I shall have to fight again with a hundred-headed hydra, the more so as the Grand Chancellor Beyme seems to be prejudiced against it; but that shall not cripple my courage, rather [illegible: garbled] my zeal, so as to bring this history too to the wished-for goal.

Beyme says that in the matter it is the form that is wanting, and that if the Queen could thereby obtain a hundred times a hundred thousand Rthl, he would not vote for it if the form had been defective.

He also conceded that Reibnitz and the commissioners had proceeded somewhat very hastily in the matter, but that they had not violated the form; indeed they had acted therein according to his will, because he was no friend of [uncertain: delay] — All the rest seemed to him gossip — These utterances alone prove how cautiously one will have to go, so as to shape the matter at once in such a way that neither Mr Beyme nor the devil will be able to object to it. The Queen wishes that the proofs be

found here, and that the matter might then soon be so conducted that one could act without the concurrence of Mr Beyme — As regards the sale of the South Prussian estates to the sub-prefect von Brzechffa, I have so ordered the matter that Honrichs concedes nothing and says nothing that binds — Brzechffa is at present powerful; he can do harm and help, and therefore I keep him in good faith; but because he himself said to Honrichs that things in Poland would soon be otherwise and that the Prince might have the estates back sooner than the tribunal's judgment would be there, in which case he too might no longer be in place, I let it hang thus; for since I have known Brzechffa's circumstances from those earlier times, his means do not suffice to buy the estates once they are together again — unless indeed he has gained so much in the course of the war, or unless the estates were to be [uncertain: squandered] from the [illegible: garbled] side. In my opinion, the purchase of these estates calls for a man who can come forward at once with at least 3 to 400000 Rthl, and Brzechffa has not so much. To send anyone further to Poland now is not needed, for everything is now arranged. Serene Highness cannot, to my feeling, go there in person; the good Prince would not be much respected there at present, nor is there any dwelling in which to be lodged. As soon as Trąbczyn is won, I shall myself go there at once
