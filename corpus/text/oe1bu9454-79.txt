--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Awaiting Minister of State von Voss's report on the proposed exchange of estates; Count Schulemburg raises no obstacle since the monies go to the Disposition Treasury, now his after Minister Werner's death, Count Hoym, summoned to Potsdam, has pledged support and would give 1000 Rthl for a swift conclusion, and Struensee and Cabinet Councillor Beyhm are favourable. Lists bribes: the Schulze daughter 2500 Rthl, Klewis 3000 Rthl, Court Fiscal Michaelis 300 ducats. Reports Berlin opinion for and against war.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

With the most imaginable longing I now await the report of His Excellency the Minister of State von Voss. May Heaven but govern this misanthrope's heart, that in this matter which lies above all else upon my heart he should draw no line athwart it. Minister Count Schulemburg has declared himself very willing, and, as I firmly believe, from that quarter no obstacle would stand in the way of the good cause. In the end it comes into his hands, for the monies flow to the Disposition Treasury, which after the decease of the former Minister Werner was transferred to Count von Schulemburg. Minister Count Hoym was summoned today to Potsdam, whither he departed at 8 o'clock in the morning – I have therefore [uncertain: informed] him of the whole affair, since it might easily happen that His Royal Majesty [uncertain: would put to] him concerning those estates which he had for so long a time under his disposition. He has given me his word

might bear.

to do everything in that regard, and I believe he is in earnest – for he was of the opinion that he would gladly see it for this reason, because then his vexation would be lifted, that he for Your Serene Highness

put Your Serene Highness's estates forward in a proposal in which he had not previously been instructed by cabinet order. He was of the opinion that Mr. von Vorh. had already taken formal exception on that account, and that he would gladly give 1000 Rthl if the matter came to a conclusion soon. Minister Struensee has also had [uncertain: notice] of the matter; he will truly not be opposed – the Cabinet Councillor Beyhm told him himself, and, to speak candidly, this gives me the greatest courage – for if the King did not mean the matter quite seriously, one would not be speaking of it; for once the matter comes among men, then it absolutely must happen. And at bottom this is very much the case already. In any event I hope besides that the matter will go in the very best way. And the fellows must after all do something for their 12 Rthl. The distribution of this sum I now know approximately: the Schulze daughter, who has a great ascendancy over her father, receives 2500 Rthl, Klewis 3000 Rthl, the Court Fiscal Michaelis 300 ducats – the remainder is shared between the X spider and Treskow. Michaelis, however, is a [uncertain: petty] fellow; he had the assurance already and

yet dissembled so – let him do, however, as it pleases him. If Your Serene Highness obtains all that I wish, at Potsdam everything will certainly go well – I have there a cabinet secretary who is with Beyhm, engaged along with Brandhorst; these will report not only now but in future everything of importance that occurs.

But it costs

200 fr. [illegible]

Now something of political matters – the King resists the war mightily.

Mr. von Köckritz and von Zastrow are likewise against it; here Minister von Struensee contends against it above all. Mr. Haugwitz seems to be in a mighty embarrassment; he has already asked me once what I heard from South Prussia, whether no certain news of the march of the Russians was to be heard — as I perceive, this seems to be the chief signal – Count Schulemburg, however, absolutely wants war. He flatters Minister Hoym on that account, and according to the latter's utterances today he will in the finest manner advise war so far as possible – for he reckons in that way to be rid of Minister Schulemburg – this last is much incensed against Struensee, since he advises against the war and always puts it off, that there is no money there – indeed he is said to have said outright, in which Goldbeck also concurs – the Count Schulemburg led the late blessed Lord into that

unhappy war, and thereby placed our House in the most sorrowful situation; he will now do it more clumsily than before – But this might now draw after it in the course of the week that Count Schulemburg persuades the King that the whole Customs, Excise and Salt Directorate is not necessary, that the people's [uncertain: widows'] costs which the King might save, that he ought to subordinate this business, as it formerly was under the old provincial colleges, which must attend to it ex officio – Count Hoym concurs in this and would gladly help overthrow Struensee. I have begged him today to go about the work cautiously in the loosing off of other men's arrows – since he might thereby be placed in a very ugly light, the more so as Colonel von Köckritz is now taking Stru[ensee]'s part – Otherwise Minister Hoym now stands very well in credit, and if he conducts himself well he can play the fullest part. I shall remain here so long until the Prot. affair is here brought into order – for I see well that when one wishes to know that something has been accomplished, one must stay upon the spot oneself. Moreover I am negotiating from the Widows' Fund on my own [uncertain: guarantee], so that, if it be required, money shall not be wanting in the arrangement of the exchange of estates – the 120 Rthl – Michaelis is justiciary at this institution – it depends on him, and as he offered me his good services, I will profit from them in that way – Concerning the blank forms I repeat my request – and I remain, until death, in deep submission Your Most Serene Highness's

Berlin, the 8th of October

1798.

most humble and faithful servant

v. Triebenfeld
