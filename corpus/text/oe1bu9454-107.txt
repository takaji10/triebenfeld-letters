da Ewr Hochfürstliche Durchlaucht hier wohl um

bis zu höchst dero Ankunft an mich behalten
der Frau Fürstin Sacken durch habe ich zwar
aufwarten wollen bin aber nicht angenommen und
dieser Hinsicht habe ich nur Ewr Hochfürstl
Durchlaucht Schreiben der Frau Fürsten
behändigen laßen. Jezt eben sende ich Ihr annoch

nes Schreiben, aus welchen die Frau Fürstin
sich überzeugen soll, daß Ewr Durchlaucht
bald eintreffen können.
Der gute Monarch und der ganze Haft nimt an
der Ereignis den lebhaftesten Anteil und alles
ist auf Ewr Durchlaucht Seite —
Die Fürsten Sa: hatt den Profes: Selle wahr
scheinlich an einigen Orten der Sache derge¬

allen bemittleiden solle

gewisser Hoym und der Professor Selle —
Ubrigens man[?] in Publicum fast alles Haare klein
und hier werden den die Noten beigefügt —
Mann was durch Hein die Fürsten habe erklärt
Sie wolle nie zu Ewr Durchlaucht zurück.
Sie wolle frey leben wo es Ihr gefiele

schreiben um daher hatte sie den Schrit
aus Liebe zu Ihren Kindern gewählt, im

Dies dem müſi das Gewaſſche anderer Seelen
Ew. Hochfürstliche Durchlaucht Freunde wünschen jezt

seit vorgestern ist hies selbst bey Hoffe die Nachricht

daher voller Erwartung — der würdige Obrist

--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld leitet ein Schreiben des Obristen von Zastrow weiter und will das königliche Cabinets Schreiben bis zur baldigen Ankunft des Fürsten behalten. Die Fürstin Sacken empfing ihn nicht. Er ließ ihr das Schreiben des Fürsten übergeben. Sie soll erklärt haben, nie zum Fürsten zurückzukehren, frei leben zu wollen und mit 2/m Rthl Appanage zufrieden zu sein. Sie sei aus Abneigung und aus Liebe zu ihren Kindern gegangen. Freunde raten dem Fürsten zu Festigkeit.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld forwards a letter from Colonel von Zastrow and intends to keep the royal cabinet letter until the Prince's expected arrival. Princess Sacken did not receive him. He had the Prince's letter delivered to her. He says she declared that she would never return to the Prince, that she wished to live freely and would be content with an allowance of 2/m Rthl. He says she left out of dislike and out of love for her children. Friends advise the Prince to stand firm.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

The accompanying letter has been sent to me by Colonel von Zastrow by way of a huntsman, that I should most humbly transmit the same to Your Most Serene Highness. The royal cabinet letter I shall not receive until today or tomorrow. Since Your Most Serene Highness will probably be arriving here at once, I shall keep it by me until your most high arrival. Upon the Princess Sacken I did indeed wish to wait, but I was not received, and in this regard I have only had Your Most Serene Highness's letter delivered to the Princess

be delivered. Just now I am sending her a further letter, obtained from Colonel von Zastrow to von K[öckritz], from which the Princess is to be satisfied that Your Serene Highness can arrive shortly.

The good monarch and the whole [uncertain: custody] take the liveliest interest in the affair, and everything is on Your Serene Highness's side — the Princess Sa: has probably had Professor Selle lay the matter out in certain quarters in such a fashion that she alone is to be pitied by all. To the Princess there come, as I hear, the lady von Rietesel, the advocate Cosmar, the minister Heinitz, a certain Hoym, and Professor Selle —

For the rest [illegible] in public almost everything [uncertain: is picked apart to the last hair], and here the notes are appended to it — One [text lost] the Princess has declared through Hein that she will never return to Your Serene Highness. She wishes to live freely wherever it pleases her; she would be content with 2/m rt appanage. She, the Princess, was pure as an angel, had no connexion with any other man, and had gone away from me, 1. because her personal aversion to Your Serene Highness was too great, and

2. Because she would not help bring her children's whole fortune to ruin, for she has already countersigned unspeakably many debts, but more she could not countersign; on that account she had chosen this step out of love for her children, to be rather dead in civil law than to be willing to squander the entire fortune. To this must [uncertain: the prattle] of other souls

Your Most Serene Highness's friends now wish of me that you most highly show yourself towards the Princess Sacken with firmness, and settle everything briefly; gladly will everything be approved on [uncertain: every] side. Since the day before yesterday there is here, even at court, the news that Suvarow has now likewise been beaten, and he himself taken prisoner; one is therefore full of expectation over the various reports — but the worthy Colonel

von K[öckritz] rejoices that he judged rightly, and even the warlike parties now say: God, how good it is that our monarch has meddled in nothing.

In deepest submission I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's

Berlin, the 21st of October

1799

faithful servant

v Triebenfeld
