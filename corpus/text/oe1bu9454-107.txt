--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Forwards a letter from Colonel von Zastrow, brought by a huntsman, and holds back an expected royal cabinet letter until the Prince's own arrival in Berlin. Reports on the Prince's marital dispute: Princess Sacken refused to receive Triebenfeld, declares she will never return, wants to live where she pleases on 2,000 Rthl allowance, and blames personal aversion and the Prince's debts. Names her supporters, including Professor Selle and the advocate Cosmar, urges firmness, and passes on court rumour that Suvorov has been beaten and captured.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

The accompanying letter has been sent to me by Colonel von Zastrow by way of a huntsman, so that I should most humbly transmit the same to Your Most Serene Highness. The Royal Cabinet letter I shall not receive until today or tomorrow. Since Your Most Serene Highness will probably be arriving here at once, I shall keep it by me until Your Highness's most exalted arrival. Upon the Princess Sacken I did indeed wish to wait, but I was not received, and in this regard I have only had Your Most Serene Highness's letter delivered into the Princess's

hands. Just now I am sending her yet a further letter received from Colonel von Zastrow to von K., from which the Princess is to satisfy herself that Your Serene Highness may soon arrive.

The good Monarch and the whole [uncertain: court] take the liveliest interest in the affair, and all are on Your Serene Highness's side. The Princess Sa: has probably had Professor Selle lay the matter out in certain quarters in such a fashion that she is to be pitied by everyone. Among the Princess's party there come to my notice the [illegible] von Rietesel, the advocate Cosmar, the Minister Heinel, a certain Hoym, and Professor Selle.

For the rest, in public almost everything is [uncertain: split into hairs], and here the notes are appended. What through Hein[?] the Princess has declared: she will never return to Your Serene Highness. She wishes to live free wherever it may please her. She would be content with 2000 Rthl of appanage. She, the Princess, would be pure as an angel, had no connexion with any other, and had left me [uncertain: on account of],

1. because her personal aversion to Your Serene Highness is too great, and

2. because she does not wish to help squander everything belonging to her children, for she has already countersigned unspeakably many debts, but more she cannot countersign; and therefore she had chosen this step out of love for her children, rather to be civilly dead than to be willing to squander the whole estate. This, then, must be the chatter of other souls.

Your Most Serene Highness's friends now wish only that Your Highness show himself with firmness towards the Princess Sacken, and in short settle everything; everything will gladly be approved on all[?] sides. Since the day before yesterday the news is here, even at court, that Suvarow has now likewise been beaten and himself taken prisoner. One is therefore full of expectation over the various reports — but the worthy Colonel

von K. rejoices that he judged rightly, and even the warlike parties now say: God, how good it is that our Monarch has meddled in nothing.

In deepest submission I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's

Berlin, the 21st of October

1799

faithful servant

von Triebenfeld
