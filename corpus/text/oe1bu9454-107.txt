--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Forwards an enclosed letter from Colonel von Zastrow, sent on by a huntsman, and reports that the Royal Cabinet letter will be kept until the Prince's expected arrival in Berlin. Princess Sacken refused to receive him, though she has been given the Prince's letter; her visitors include von Rietesel, the advocate Cosmar, a Minister, a certain Hoym and Professor Selle. Reports her declaration never to return, wanting freedom and 2000 Rthl appanage, and rumours that Suvarow has been beaten and captured.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Lord! From the Colonel von Zastrow the enclosed letter has been sent to me by a huntsman, so that I should most humbly transmit the same to Your Most Serene Highness. The Royal Cabinet letter I shall not receive until today or tomorrow. Since Your Most Serene Highness will no doubt be arriving here presently, I shall keep it by me until Your Highness's arrival. Upon the Princess Sacken I did indeed wish to wait, but I was not received, and in this regard I have only had Your Most Serene Highness's letter delivered into the Princess's hands

Just now I am sending her a further letter received from Colonel von Zastrow to von K., from which the Princess is to satisfy herself that Your Serene Highness may soon arrive. The good Monarch and the whole court take the liveliest interest in the affair, and everything is on Your Serene Highness's side. The Princess [uncertain: Sa: — Sacken?] has probably had Professor Selle lay the matter out in several quarters in such a way that she is only to be pitied by everyone. To the Princess there come, so far as I can tell, the [uncertain: E] von Rietesel, the advocate Cosmar, the Minister [uncertain: Heinel — Heinitz?], a certain Hoym, and Professor Selle.

For the rest, in public almost everything is [uncertain: split into hairs / picked to pieces], and here then the notes are appended. [text lost] what through Hein the Princess has declared: she will never return to Your Serene Highness. She wishes to live free where it pleases her. She would be content with 2000 Rthl of appanage. She, the Princess, was pure as an angel, had no connexion with another, and had gone away from me on two accounts: 1. because her personal aversion to Your Serene Highness was too great, and 2. because she would not help squander everything belonging to her children, for she has already countersigned unspeakably many debts, but more she could not subscribe to; and therefore she had chosen this step out of love for her children, preferring to be civilly dead than to be party to squandering the whole estate. Such then is the prattle of other souls.

Your Most Serene Highness's friends now wish that Your Highness show yourself with firmness toward the Princess Sacken, and in short determine everything; everything will readily be approved on all[illegible] sides. Since the day before yesterday the news is current here, even at court, that Suvarow has now likewise been beaten, and he himself taken prisoner; one is therefore full of expectation on account of the various reports — but the worthy Colonel v. K. rejoices that he judged rightly, and even the warlike parties now say: God, how good it is that our Monarch has meddled in nothing. In deepest submission I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's
Berlin, the 21st October 1799
most faithful servant
v Triebenfeld
