--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld waits at Kontop for the iron and cannot leave for Königsberg, having no travelling money and being unable to satisfy Michaelis, newly arrived from Berlin; he begs that the 1000 Centner be delivered to Beyndel quickly. He relays war news: live cartridges issued in Berlin, the "Carl and Hoff" watchword angering the French envoy, Russian troops massing near Brzesc under Grand Duke Konstantin, silver deliveries, and the King's letter to General von Zastrow. A postscript details carriage and toll charges of some 240 and 200 Rthl, Lahr's 2200 Rthl claim, and the 217 still lacking of the 1000 of iron for von der Lahn.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Even now I still wait in vain for the arrival of the iron, and on that account I delay my journey, for until it is here I cannot depart; I have neither travelling money, still less can I satisfy Michaelis, who has just now arrived here from Berlin. — The greatest misfortune is that I must sit here so long and cannot depart for Königsberg, where at present so much good might be established. Let Your Most Serene Highness show me the single grace of ordering that the 1000 hundredweight of iron be delivered here in Beyndel very soon indeed, so that I may make all arrangements and set off. Michaelis brings the news that our troops in Berlin have already received live cartridges, and that a declaration of war is looked for at any moment. One matter has caused a great sensation in Berlin; it is this: when the news arrived in Berlin that the Archduke Carl had his headquarters at Hoff, General Astog gave out as the watchword "Carl and Hoff"; the French envoy is said to have been greatly incensed at this and reported it at once by courier to Napoleon. Everything is full of expectation of the things that are to come. The Russian troops are beginning to set themselves in motion on the Polish frontiers — the Grand Duke Konstantin has departed from Petersburg in order to manoeuvre with the corps of 75000 men which stands in and about Bržesc. The delivering-in of silver proceeds very well; in Berlin the press is so great that a larger staff has had to be engaged to expedite the stamping, because the people from the province must in part wait eight days and longer before they can be dispatched. At present many men and horses are being driven together here

in order to bring together a strong corps and to mobilise quickly. — It seems certain that our House will again have to take part in the business.

To the Lord General von Zastrow the King has written a letter in his own hand, exceedingly obliging, and has referred him to patience for only a short time yet.

I burn with [uncertain: eagerness] to learn everything in detail at Königsberg soon, so as to be able to give Your Most Serene Highness definite information upon everything, or to deliver the same to Your Highness in person. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's

Kontop, the 20th of April

1809.

most faithful and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld

May Your Most Serene Highness most graciously pardon this postscript. At the very moment when I was about to send off this letter, my [uncertain: high] steward brings the enclosed letters; in these it is reported how much iron has gone off from Oppeln. Much as this would have gladdened me, so much does it cast down my courage that I am to pay the freight charge — I have as yet no travelling money — whence am I to take the freight costs?

Michaelis receives 600 Rthl of iron; these costs, over and above 240 Rthl freight and toll duty from here to Berlin — Lahr would receive for interest, costs and capital 400 [uncertain: Cantz] at 5 Rthl 12 Groschen, which is 2200 Rthl; there again I must lay out some 200 Rthl freight, toll and duty from here, and even so I shall not manage, if the costs should amount to more.

I have already tried everything to raise travelling money, and have not yet succeeded; meanwhile I have a promise of receiving 500 Rthl on Sunday; if that succeeds, then I shall set off at once for Königsberg — but the freight I cannot pay; may Your Most Serene Highness deign most graciously to have this defrayed, and command that the 217 [uncertain: Leuth] of iron still wanting to make up the 1000 be sent hither with all possible speed, so that the same may be dispatched from here at once to Berlin to von der Lahn — otherwise I travel [uncertain: full of care] and cannot work on with a quiet heart and an open brow.
