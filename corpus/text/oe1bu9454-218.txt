--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Proposes, with Herr von Heuthausen and Master of the Horse Bally, exchanging Schurgast (bought for 207000 Rthl, 92000 in debts) with General Count Wartensleben, valuing the Zagorow estates at 560000 Rthl against 237375 owing, leaving the Prince a clear mortgage of 207625; Stöckel and Weigel must be induced to take Schurgast and the mortgage. Offers his own Blizanow and a 30000 mortgage on Kontop; urges secrecy and haste against Medical Councillor Gumperts. Reports arrears at Betschen, uncollectable Olesnica debts of 35000 fl, the Wrąbczyn contract quashed, lawsuits with Olemia and Weyberin, and his own tenfold arrest and destitution.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

As to what has been done here on Your Serene Highness's estates, and what is to be expected hereafter, Herr von Heuthausen will be able to lay the reports and results before Your Serene Highness.

I have drafted a valuation and had it drawn up for him [uncertain: habnnis] — the externals of it are brilliant and serve to conclude an advantageous baptismal transaction, so that the 128000 Rthl might be regarded by me as a life-annuity-people.

In the event that Your Serene Highness should deign to approve a plan drawn up to that end with Herr von Heuthausen and Herr Bally, the Master of the Horse, it would depend upon Herr Weigel and Herr von Stöck entering into the matter according to our wishes.

The plan is properly to be made with General Count von Wartensleben regarding Schurgart; Schurgent was bought for 207000 Rthl; there were 92000 Rthl of debts upon it, and there remained clear 115000. If, on the other hand, the Zagorowo estates were to be taken at a value of 560000 Rthl / for something must be deducted on account of the setting-up expenses of the demesne farms / then, after deduction of the 237375 tℓ. owing that stood upon Zagorowo, there would remain none

322,625 Rthl, from which the 115000 Rthl which remain on Schurgard would have to be deducted, and Your Serene Highness would retain a clear mortgage of 207625 Rthl upon the Zagorowo estates. According to our plan,

then Stössel and Herr Weigel should take over Schurgast and the mortgage here, whereby Your Serene Highness would be freed of a burden of debt to the amount of 322625 Rthl. To dispose Stöckel and Weigel so that they take Schurgart and the mortgage is the principal matter. With Count von Wartensleben I would settle the business. But with Stöchte I will have nothing to do — should, however, the mass of debts of Stöchel and Weigel be still very much higher than 322625 Rthl, then I should undertake to deal with the eldest son of Count von Wastenleben concerning my Blizanow, and gladly and willingly make over to Your Serene Highness this estate, which I acquired there, together with a mortgage of 30000 upon Kontop, so that all might be set aside and Koschenten be cleared of the half. Your Serene Highness may then, with every right, demand back the Bewer from the estate of the Prince von Sacken, that portion destined for the purchase of an entailed estate, and with this arrange your whole affair of debts and keep money in hand [uncertain: Witwecke], and moreover come forward with every right as lord of an entailed estate. If the plan pleases Your Serene Highness, then deign, most gracious service, only to let me know your most high opinion by a returning messenger [uncertain: Schlaconein], and then at once to Schlawenschitz, and I shall go immediately to the old [uncertain: Wackung] to conclude the matter. Only this I remark, that for the present no one is to learn a word of this plan, and the matter must also be pressed forward very quickly, because Wartenleben has for some time already been in negotiation with the

Medical Councillor Gumperts.

For the rest, I should strive with glowing zeal to win the two lawsuits with Olemia and Weyberin before the handing over of the Zagorowo estates, so that Your Serene Highness might yet draw some 20 to 30000 Rthl from them. What will come out of the accounts of the various officials and sequestrators time will teach; that meanwhile this matter is being prepared irreproachably, Herr von Heuthausen can detail to Your Serene Highness more exactly. The sum of the arrears will, as I can assure with certainty, be more than considerable, but very many items will have to be regarded as uncollectable; such, for example, is the story with Olesnica — the three [uncertain: Keels], against whom I then so honestly warned Your Serene Highness, owe 35000 fl. — of these 3 creatures you, most gracious Lord, will never get a Groschen, for what can a smith, a journeyman linen-weaver and a village tavern-keeper have. With Wrąbczyn it is the same case; fortunately the contract has now already been quashed in one instance — Prince, that was a reminder of the conversation which nearly drove me to madness — O my poor children. I have counted the windows in the manner of your writing — now back to the matter. In Betsche too there is much to be done; there some 20000 Rthl stand in arrears which are due to Your Serene Highness — I cannot go there any further, for I have chiefly been with Councillor of Justice Künsel in Posen, and have enlightened the affairs. Now a man of head and heart must pursue the business himself on the spot

— Heuthausen, as I have come to know him, is a man of head and heart, possesses the knowledge of the language required here, has the proper savoir-vivre [uncertain: Lavoir Vivri] to get through among the Poles; give him, most gracious Prince, travelling money for expenses and for his living, and let him attend to the business, for nothing is to be got here as yet. How often I suffer, I show you, Serene Highness, [uncertain: figura-] — Schwimmern is, on account of the Invalids' interest, laid under tenfold arrest, with 9700 on account of 27000 Rthl bank interest, on account of 4000 Rthl Epstien claims, on account of 3500 old costs which I have paid and guaranteed here — on account of 4000 Rthl which the Schliefen still has to demand — nothing comes in here, I cannot save myself, my children suffer with me; in short, I suffer and [uncertain: Felde] — For what grieves me most is that my fine Kontop is gone to the devil — I have no one who takes any interest; whatever I engage, that is to do the work, turns rogue, because people do not believe that a prince should not spend and live at the devil's own rate — everyone wants to live as well at once — In spite of all this I remain, with the deepest reverence, the most faithful attachment and the deepest esteem,

Your Most Serene Highness's

the star out of the North is shining

the rest by word of mouth

Blizanow

the 21st of September 1811

faithful, humble and obedient servant

von Triebenfeld
