rungen auch nicht den mindesten Vorteil zu erringen, hoffen darf
Es ist eine Spizbuberey ohne gleichen - das Hof Rescript wegen
der Deposition war hier - demohngeachtet würde der nieder
trächtige Vergleich geſchloßen - Glenck reichte ſolchen den
6 ten zur Approbation ein und bath um die möglichste Beschleu¬
nigung selbigen zu vollziehen und für beide Theile ausfertigen
zu laßen. Ewr Hochfürstl durchl. Schreiben war den 8ten hier
worin Sie gegen den Vergleich protestirten.
Man legte dies schnell bey Seite Approbirte und expedirte mit Kläger
Schnelligkeit, den verfluchten Vergleich, und schickte ihm bey Hofe ein¬
Was ſoll ich nun thun? Dagegen klagen nach Berlin gehen und Lerm
machen – Das hilft alles nichts, man macht sich ridicule.
Gute Menschen deren es hier kaum 2 giebt, Beklagen Ewr Durchlaucht
der große Haufen aber zu Bett und verlauft jeden der Theil an
dieser Sache nimmt. O ich möchte rasend werden.

das Niederträchtigste ist nun, daß Glenck nicht komt – Er hat ohnerachtet
er meine Briefe hier erhalten nicht warten wollen, sondern ist nach Betsch
gegangen. Ich habe an ihm Extaffetten und Bothin geschickt – er kommt
nicht, antwortet auch keine Sylbe. Hier nage ſizze ich hier ver-
geblich – Gott es ist schrecklich was sich diese Menschen erlauben.
Das Volleste ist, das keiner an diesen Buben stück schuld sein will.
Metzer ſchimpft, das Wünſter den Vergleich nicht beßer gemacht hat.
und verdammt Glenck bis in Ewigkeit, daß er nicht sub speirati¬
abgeschloßen hat - Er sagt er habe wollen den Vergleich machen,
und sagen wollen ich habe zwar keine Vollmacht, werde sie aber
nachbringen / den hätte man Spielraum genug gehabt, dies aber
habe Glenck nicht acceptirt. Daher habe Glenck ganz alleine

Leugnen könne er nicht, daß wan die Deposition erfolgt wäre.
würde der Erfolg für den Fürsten sehr gut gewesen sein,

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports bitterly that the disputed settlement with Broniewski has been approved and expedited in Posen despite the court rescript on the deposition and the Prince's written protest, which arrived on the 8th and was set aside; Glenck submitted it for approbation on the 6th, admitting he had seen the decree, while Metzer blames Wünster and estimates six to eight years for the liquidation suit. Triebenfeld plans to go to Betsch, then return to dislodge Bornstädt and check Braniewehr's security.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Lord! My presentiment that here all endeavours to undo the notorious settlement would be in vain is, alas, confirmed. The villains have hedged everything about with such clauses that, even with the greatest sacrifices, one may not hope to gain the least advantage. It is a piece of knavery without equal — the court rescript concerning the deposition was here — and notwithstanding this the base settlement was concluded. Glenck submitted the same on the 6th for approbation and begged for the utmost dispatch in executing it and having it drawn up for both parties. Your Most Serene Highness's letter, in which You protested against the settlement, was here on the 8th. This was quickly laid aside; they approved and expedited the accursed settlement with the plaintiff's own speed, and sent it in at court. What am I now to do? To complain against it, go to Berlin and make an outcry — none of that helps at all, one only makes oneself ridiculous. Good people, of whom there are scarcely 2 here, pity Your Serene Highness, but the great crowd [uncertain: to bed] and [uncertain: runs down/sells off] everyone who takes part in this affair. Oh, I could go mad.

The basest thing of all is now that Glenck does not come. Although he received my letters here, he would not wait, but has gone to Betsch. I have sent express couriers and a female messenger after him — he does not come, nor does he answer a syllable. Here I [uncertain: gnaw] sit here in vain — God, it is dreadful what these people permit themselves. The [uncertain: fullest] thing is that nobody will own to any share in this rascals' business. Metzer rails that Wünster did not make the settlement better, and damns Glenck to all eternity for not having concluded it sub sp[e] [illegible] — He says he had meant to make the settlement and to say: I have indeed no power of attorney, but shall supply it afterwards / by which one would have had room enough to manoeuvre; but this Glenck did not accept. Hence Glenck concluded quite alone, without his foreknowledge. He could not deny that, if the deposition had taken place, the outcome would have been very good for the Prince, since Broniewski himself had already declared that he would drown himself or shoot himself in the head, and leave his wife and children behind to the Prince. For the rest, Metzer says Wunster made the settlement and arranged everything so — in short, each puts it off upon the

other — but as I see it, they are all equally [uncertain: culpable]. Here I care to stay no longer; rather, if Glenck does not come, I shall set off towards evening for Betsch, so as to let nothing be neglected there — for with Bornstädt there will be a great deal of noise; they have proceeded with these just as perversely as with the Trąbcziners, that is to say, they sued for eviction but neither had a liquid sum established, still less applied for execution — everything here lies in the dark, and it will cost untold trouble to bring this quickly to [uncertain: the clear]. From Betsch I must go back here once more, in order to dislodge Bornstädt and to see what security Braniewehr puts up. This very moment Glenck has arrived — He knows neither what to say in his own excuse, still less anything to propose for the redress of the matter. In short, the man feels nothing, though I could vex myself to death. He has begged Metzer to [uncertain: throw everything over] and to uphold the settlement. — This I know for certain. Glenck also let slip that he had seen the court decree before he submitted the settlement for approbation.

— But Metzer, he said, had declared that — although it was not stated therein that the causae of the arrest were to be produced, it nevertheless went without saying that they would have to be supplied afterwards; with that, however, he would never concern himself. One might well [uncertain: become] at this [uncertain: washing/chatter] — for [uncertain: yesterday/lately] [uncertain: Weltzer/Metzer] did admit that had one been able to make the deposit even for only 6 days, Broniewski was lost, and would have taken whatever one gave him out of grace. In short, it is enough to vex one to death, when one has long to do with this low, rapacious rabble. To the question how long the liquidation suit would last, Metzer opines very cold-bloodedly: 6 to 8 years. This is precisely the reason why the rascals have twisted the affair so, in order to keep Your Serene Highness always in their hands and be able to empty Your pockets. I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's Posen, the 22nd of July 1804, faithful humble and obedient servant v Triebenfeld
