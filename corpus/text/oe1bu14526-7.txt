--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Hereditary-lease contract before the Trąbczyn patrimonial court by which Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, General Bevollmächtigter of the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, grants Gottlieb (Bogumił) Sarre of the Peyser Hauland one and a half Magdeburg Hufen of waste forest land near Trąbczyn in perpetuity for 150 Reichsthaler purchase-money, already receipted, and 45 Reichsthaler annual canon after three free years; with redemption, milling and drink compulsion, Laudemium and no-remission clauses. Certified in Polish under the Zagurów court seal at Pyzdry.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
[Polish:] The undersigned Court hereby makes known to whomsoever it may concern, that between the plenipotentiary of His Serene Highness the Prince Hohenlohe Ingelfingen, the Honourable Councillor of War and Forests Triebenfeld, and the Hauländer Bogumił Sarre the following contract has been concluded.

Actum Trąbczyn, the eighth of July, one thousand eight hundred and six. There appeared the persons well known to the undersigned Justitiarius, namely as

1. the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, as General Bevollmächtigter [authorised representative] of His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe Ingelfingen, hereditary lord and lord of the court of this place, legitimated by the power of attorney annexed hereto, on his part.

and

2. the Hausländer farmer Gottlieb Sarre from the Peyser Hauland on the other part. The comparents are found to be in the condition to execute judicial acts validly, and declare, freely and after due deliberation, the following hereditary-lease contract to the protocol

§ I. His Most Serene Highness grants to Gottlieb Sarre, in the forest situated near Trąbczyn, 1 1/2 Hufen — written out, one and a half Hufen Magdeburg measure, the Hufe reckoned at thirty Morgen and each Morgen at one hundred and eighty square rods — of waste land, from Midsummer [Johanni] of this present year onward, in hereditary lease for perpetual times.

§ II. Gottlieb Sarre pays for the Hufe Magdeburg measure 100 rt. — written out, one hundred Reichsthaler — hence for one and a half Hufen 150 r. — written out, one hundred and fifty Reichsthaler — as purchase-money; and since this purchase-money has already been paid in full, Gottlieb Sarre is hereby given receipt for it without further reservation.

§ III. Gottlieb Sarre hereby binds himself, for himself and his successors in the possession of this holding, to pay from each Hufe as annual hereditary canon 30 rtl. — written out, thirty Reichsthaler — hence for one and a half Hufen 45 rtll.

45 rt. — written out, forty-five Reichsthaler — to the Dominium of this place; and this hereditary rent is to be discharged yearly in two instalments, namely with the one half at Christmas and with the [uncertain: other] half at Midsummer [Johannis], each time in cash and in Courant to the manorial treasury.

§ IV. For the wholly unbroken arable the hereditary lessee receives three free years, which begin at Midsummer [Johanni] of the present year and end at Midsummer [Johannis] 1809, so that the first half-yearly hereditary rent must be paid at Midsummer [Johanni] 1809 in the sum of 22 rt 12 gg — written out, twenty-two Reichsthaler, twelve good Groschen.

That land which at the measuring-out was handed over to Gottlieb Sarre already as broken ground

— be the same much or little — bears interest from Midsummer [Johanni] of this year, inasmuch as the free years extend only to that part which must first be brought under cultivation; and since the handing over of these [uncertain: sonderthalb] Hufen Magdeburg measure has already taken place to the hereditary lessee, he renounces all claims that might be made on this account.

§ V. The hereditary lessee is at liberty to redeem the stipulated hereditary rent of thirty Reichsthaler per Hufe down to one half, that is to say, he may according to his means pay off to the ground-lordship one, two, up to three hundred Reichsthaler of capital, whereupon for every hundred Reichsthaler five Reichsthaler shall be [uncertain: abated] yearly from the hereditary rent

The capital so paid off remains the property of the ground-lordship, and this redemption of the hereditary rent takes place only in respect of the first half; the second half, of fifteen Reichsthaler per Hufe, on the other hand remains irredeemable, and can never be raised or diminished.

§ VI. Besides the hereditary rent in cash, the hereditary lessee undertakes, for now and for future times in perpetuity, to discharge and render the [uncertain: gehauenten] royal and other public dues falling upon these one and a half Hufen, consist they in what they may, without any exception and without deduction from the hereditary rent or any other reimbursement.

§ VII. The hereditary lessee must build upon it at his own cost, without his

receiving, either now or in the future, timber or any other building assistance for this purpose. However, the whole of the timber found upon his one and a half Hufen belongs to him in property without exception.

Likewise the hereditary lessee, and his successors in the possession of this holding, must effect all future repairs and new buildings out of their own means, without subsidy or assistance.

§ VIII. Outside his own holding the hereditary lessee has no right to firewood or pasturage in the manorial forests and fields.

§ IX. Drink the hereditary lessee must always take from the manorial brewery and distillery or from the tavern

If foreign drink be brought in by him or by his people, the hereditary lessee shall each time incur a penalty of five Reichsthaler for every barrel of beer and [uncertain: seinen] Reichsthaler for every Garniec of brandy, which penalty is to be paid to the manorial lordship.

§ X. To the milling compulsion the hereditary lessee is subject, and may grind his grain only where the manorial lordship directs him.

§ XI. From all and every manorial service and deliveries in kind without distinction the hereditary lessee is wholly and forever exempt; he nevertheless remains subject to the jurisdiction of the Dominium of this place.

§ XII. The hereditary lessee and his successors in possession

in possession is entitled to alienate his holding to another in any manner he pleases; but at every sale or exchange [uncertain: a sum] of money must be paid to the ground-lordship as a Laudemium, and the consent of the same must be obtained for every alienation, inasmuch as the ground-lordship hereby reserves to itself once and for all the right of pre-emption.

§ XIII. The hereditary lessee, for himself and his successors in possession, wholly renounces, for now and for the future, all and every remission in cases of misfortune — be the same by fire from heaven, arson by evil persons, hailstorm, crop failure, cattle plague, inundation, war and pestilence, or in whatsoever manner it may [uncertain: mir] arise — and in the event of such cases there shall be neither a bonification nor a re-

-mission of the hereditary rent take place.

§ XIV. All costs pertaining to this contract the hereditary lessee undertakes to discharge.

§ XV. His Most Serene Highness consents that the hereditary lessee may, upon the ground of this contract, have his title of possession registered for himself.

§ XVI. So long as the hereditary lessee and his successors in possession shall fulfil all the obligations contained in this contract, His Most Serene Highness promises to maintain them in their rights

and to defend them against every man's real claim. Should, however, the hereditary lessee or his successors remain in arrears with the annual hereditary rent longer than two years, or should they lay waste this holding and thereby diminish the revenues of the ground-lordship, then the ground-lordship shall be entitled to let out this holding elsewhere, and the hereditary lessee shall forfeit his whole right and the purchase-money he has paid in.

Both parties promise to keep this hereditary-lease contract firmly and inviolably; having been judicially instructed as to the consequences, they renounce all

subterfuges available against it, in particular the plea of an injury suffered above or below the half [laesio enormis], of cunning persuasion, fear, coercion, of matters otherwise agreed than written down or not rightly understood; they [uncertain: apply] for this contract to be judicially executed, and, after prior verbal reading aloud, have subscribed below in their own hands as a token of approval.
