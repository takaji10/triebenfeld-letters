--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that private debts and the Bayonne sums look likely to fall under a 41¾ year moratorium, leaving some 200,000 Rthl on the estates, including a Weigel debt of 50,000 Rthl, effectively converted into an annuity. Presses the Prince to say by return of the express messenger whether he means to keep or exchange the estates, since keeping them would require judicial removal of the Hauländer. Asks for a power of attorney for the advocate Schmid at Kalisz and a letter to Hecker, who is inciting the subjects.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

By all appearances, and especially according to the assurances of several magnates, every private debt, as well as the so-called Bayonne sums — that is, Band, Jevatiden and wards' gulden — will be treated alike and made subject to the 41 3/4 year moratorium. Concerning the Bank and /Invalids' sum, Your Most Serene Highness already has the documents in hand, and perhaps I shall be so fortunate as shortly to lay at the feet of the same a like document concerning the Weigel 50000 Rthl. In this manner there rest upon the estates together 200000 Rthl, which in 41¾ years at 6 per cent would be amortised as to both capital and interest. The estates are now worth a great deal, and the debt resting upon them has been converted into a life annuity. — It is now urgently necessary that Your Serene Highness tell me much, whether you wish to keep the estates or to exchange them. If you wish to keep the estates, then, in order to obtain secure revenues, I must have the Hollanders or colonists for the greater part driven out by judicial decrees, and form pure demesne farms. But if you can or will exchange the estates to advantage, then this can be spared, and one states the revenues according to the judicial contracts, which serve as the standard for the interest receipts; only in that case a warranty of title can be given upon a sale. —

May Your Serene Highness deign to let me know your first intention as to your will by this express messenger: whether you wish the estates to be preserved for yourself and your descendants alone.

Should this be the case, then I press — since the present laws are more favourable to the estate proprietor than the Prussian ones are — for the annulment of the Hollanders or colonists. This can take at most 2 months, and then I have the judgment; and in the meantime the matter concerning the Olesnica swindlers and concerning the Wrąbczyn people must also be decided. Concerning the arrears a great number of lawsuits are pending, but I doubt that much will flow into Your Serene Highness's treasury. Everyone declares himself insolvent. — At bottom the distress is very great, and the want of money general. Most of what I now have to deal with is with Hecker and this whole brood. Hecker continually incites the subjects, together with Hawich; wherefore I most humbly beg that a judicial power of attorney be issued for the advocate of the justice-of-the-peace court, Schmid, at Kalisz, according to the enclosed form, so that he may at last make a clean sweep of the rabble. This is very urgently necessary, so that by the swiftest [uncertain: Fatharium] — which, however, must be done through a third party — I may the more quickly reach the goal of my wishes. From a review of the Honrichs and Hecker accounts, and of all of them, everything will come to light. May Your Serene Highness deign to write to Hecker with your own hand according to the enclosed draft. For I, standing as I do in litigation with the villain,

cannot now issue any orders touching the service, and certainly not to B.; at least he would not act upon them. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's — Blizanow

the 29th of July 1811.

Let Your Serene Highness have the messenger paid; he receives 6 gg per mile. 1 Rthl I have given him for the road. — most humble and obedient servant v Triebenfeld
