--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld erwartet, dass Privatschulden und Bayonner Summen (Bank-, Invaliden- und Pupillengelder) einem Moratorium von 41 3/4 Jahren unterworfen werden; 200000 Rthl auf den Gütern würden so mit 6 pCt. amortisiert. Er verlangt dringend Entscheidung, ob der Fürst die Güter behält oder vertauscht; beim Behalten will er die Hauländer gerichtlich vertreiben und Vorwerke bilden. Er bittet um Vollmacht für Advocat Schmid in Kalisz gegen Hecker, der mit Hawich die Unterthanen aufwiegelt, und um einen Brief an Hecker.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld expects that private debts and the Bayonne sums (bank, invalid and wards' moneys) will be made subject to a moratorium of 41 3/4 years; 200,000 Rthl charged on the estates would thus be paid off at 6 per cent. He urgently presses for a decision whether the Prince will keep the estates or exchange them; if he keeps them, Triebenfeld means to drive out the Hollander by legal action and form demesne farms. He asks for a power of attorney for the lawyer Schmid in Kalisz against Hecker, who with Hawich is stirring up the subjects, and for a letter to Hecker.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
216

Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

In all probability, and particularly according to the assurances of several magnates, every private debt, as well as the so-called Bayonne sums (that is to say, bank, invalid and pupil moneys), will be dealt with and made subject to the 41 3/4-year moratorium. Concerning the bank and invalid sum Your Most Serene Highness already has the documents in hand, and perhaps I shall be so fortunate as to lay before Your Highness's feet, shortly, a like document concerning the Weigel 50/m Rthl. In this manner there rests upon the whole of the estates 200,000 Rthl, which in 41¾ years, at 6 per cent, would be amortised as to both capital and interest. The estates are now much [uncertain: worth], and the debt resting upon them is converted into a life annuity — Now it is urgently necessary that Your Serene Highness tell me much, whether you wish to keep the estates or to exchange them? Should you wish to keep the estates, then, in order to obtain secure revenues, I must have the Hollanders or colonists for the greater part chased to the d— by judicial decisions, and form pure demesne farms. But if you can or will exchange the estates to advantage, then this may be spared, and one gives out the revenues according to the judicial contracts, which serve as the standard for the rental receipts; only, in a bargain, a warranty of title can be given upon them —

May it please Your Serene Highness to let me know by this express courier your first intention of will: whether Your Highness wishes the estates to be preserved only for yourself and your descendants.

Should this be the case, then I press — since the present laws are more favourable to the estate owner than the Prussian ones are — for the annulment of the Hollanders or colonists. This can take at most 2 months, and then I have the judgment; and meanwhile the matter concerning the Oleśnica swindlers and that concerning the Wrąbczyn people must also be decided — Concerning the arrears a great number of lawsuits are pending, but I doubt that much will flow into Your Serene Highness's treasury. Every one of them declares himself insolvent — At bottom the distress is very great, and the want of money general — Most of what I now have to contend with is with Hecker and this whole brood. Hecker continually stirs up the subjects together with Hawich — I therefore most humbly beg that a judicial power of attorney be issued for the peace-court advocate Schmid at Kalisz, according to the enclosed form, so that he may make a wholly clean sweep with the rabble. This is very urgently necessary, so that by the swiftest [illegible] (which, however, must be done through a third party) I may the more quickly attain the goal of my wishes — by a review of the Honrichs and Hecker accounts, and of all of them, everything will come out. To Hecker, may it please Your Serene Highness to write in your own most high hand according to the enclosed draft. For since I stand in a lawsuit with the scoundrel, I

being in a lawsuit with the villain, can now issue no orders touching the service, not to B. — at least he would not [uncertain: heed] them. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's. Let Your Serene Highness have the messenger paid; he receives 6 Groschen per mile; 1 Rthl. I have given him for the road —

Blizanow

29th July 1811.

most humble and obedient servant
v Triebenfeld
