--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that private debts and the Bayonne sums (Bank, Jesuit and Pupillen gulden) will likely fall under the 41¾-year moratorium, leaving some 200000 Rthl on the estates, amortisable at 6 pCt., with a document on the Weigel 50000 Rthl expected. Presses the Fürst to declare by express whether he will keep or exchange the estates, since keeping them requires judicial annulment of the Hauländer to form demesne farms. Requests a power of attorney for advocate Schmid at Kalisz against Hecker, notes the Olesnice and Wrąbczyner cases, widespread insolvency, and payment of the messenger.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! By all probability, and especially according to the assurances of several magnates, every private debt, as also the so-called Bayonne sums — that is to say, Bank, Jesuit and Pupillen gulden — will be treated [alike], and be subjected to the 41 3/4 years' moratorium. Concerning the Bank and /Invalid sum Your Most Serene Highness has the documents already in hand, and perhaps I shall be so fortunate as shortly to lay at the feet of the same a like document concerning the Weigel 50000 Rthl. In this manner there rest upon the estates altogether 200000 Rthl, which in 41¾ years, at 6 pCt., would be amortised as to capital as well as interest. The estates are now much [uncertain: waiting] and the debt resting upon them is converted into a life-annuity — Now it is urgently necessary that Your Serene Highness tell me much, whether you wish to keep the estates or to exchange them. Should you wish to keep the estates, then, in order to secure certain revenues, I must have the Hauländer or colonists for the most part [uncertain: driven out — T—] by judicial decrees, and form pure demesne farms. But if you can or will exchange the estates to advantage, then this can be spared, and one declares the revenues according to the judicial contracts, which serve as the norm of the interest receipts; only that upon these, in a transaction, a warranty of title can be given —

May it please Your Serene Highness to let me know your first declaration of will as by this express; whether it is your highest wish to have the estates preserved only for yourself and your descendants. Should this be the case, then — since the present laws are more favourable to the proprietor of an estate than the Prussian ones are — I press for the annulment of the Hauländer or colonists. This can take at most 2 months, and then I have the judgment; and in the meantime the matter of the Olesnice swindlers and of the Wrąbczyner people must also be decided. As regards the arrears, a great number of lawsuits are pending, but I doubt that much will flow into Your Serene Highness's treasury. Everyone declares himself insolvent — At bottom the distress is very great, and the want of money general. The most I have to deal with now is with Hecker and this whole brood. Hecker continually stirs up the subjects with the Hawich; wherefore I most humbly beg that a judicial power of attorney be issued for the advocate of the Court of Peace, Schmid at Kalisz, according to the accompanying form, so that he may make a clean sweep of the rabble once and for all. This is very urgently necessary, so that by the swiftest [uncertain: Fatharium] — which, however, must be done through a third party — I may the more quickly reach the goal of my wishes. From the revision of the Honrichsen, Hecker and all the accounts everything will appear. To Hecker may Your Serene Highness deign to write in your own most high hand according to the accompanying draft. For I cannot, being in litigation with the scoundrel,

issue any orders now which concern the service, certainly not to B.; at least he would not act upon them. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's — Blizanow, the 29th of July 1811. Let Your Serene Highness have the messenger paid; he receives 6 gg per Meile; 1 Rthl I have given him for the road. — most humble and obedient servant v Triebenfeld
