--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that private debts, like the Bayonne sums, will probably fall under the 41¾-year moratorium, leaving some 200000 Rthl on the estates amortisable at 6 per cent, with documents expected on the Weigel 50000 Rthl. Presses the Prince to decide whether to keep or exchange the estates, since retention would require evicting the Hollander colonists to form demesne farms, and requests a judicial power of attorney for the advocate Schmid at Kalisz against Hecker, plus a letter to Hecker in the Prince's own hand; mentions the Oleśnicki and Wrąbczyn suits, general insolvency, and the messenger's 6 Groschen per mile.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

According to all probability, and especially in accordance with the assurances of several magnates, every private debt, like the so-called Bayonne sums — that is to say, bond, [uncertain: Jevatiden] and wards' gulden — will be dealt with and made subject to the 41 3/4-year moratorium. Concerning the bank and /Invalids' sum Your Most Serene Highness already has the documents in hand, and perhaps I shall be so fortunate as shortly to lay at the feet of the same a like document concerning the Weigel 50000 Rthl. In this manner there rest upon the whole of the estates 200000 Rthl, which in 41¾ years would be amortised at 6 per cent, both as to capital and as to interest. The estates are now worth much, and the debt resting upon them has been converted into a life-annuity. — It is now urgently necessary that Your Serene Highness tell me much, whether you wish to keep the estates or to exchange them. If you wish to keep the estates, then, in order to secure certain revenues, I must for the most part have the Hollanders or colonists driven off by judicial decrees to the [uncertain: T], and form pure demesne farms. But if you can or will exchange the estates to advantage, then this can be spared, and one states the revenues according to the judicial contracts which serve as the norm for the interest receipts; only, in the event of a bargain, a warranty of title can be given upon them. —

May Your Serene Highness deign to let me know your first will and intention by this express: whether you wish the estates to be conserved only for yourself and your descendants.

Should this be the case, then, since the present laws are more favourable to the estate-owner than the Prussian ones are, I press for the annulment of the [uncertain: Bauländer] or [uncertain: ColloMiler]. This can take two months at most, and then I shall have the judgment; and in the meantime the matter of the Oleśnicki swindlers and of the Wrąbczyn people must also be decided. As to the arrears, a mass of lawsuits is pending, but I doubt that much will flow into Your Serene Highness's treasury. Every one of them declares himself insolvent. — At bottom the distress is very great and the want of money general. I have most to do at present with Hecker and this whole brood. Hecker is continually inciting the subjects with the [uncertain: Hawich]; I therefore most humbly beg that a judicial power of attorney be issued for the advocate of the court of the peace Schmid at Kalisz, according to the enclosed form, so that he may at last make a clean sweep of the rabble. This is very urgently necessary, so that by the swiftest [uncertain: Fatharium] — which, however, must be done through a third party — I may the more quickly reach the goal of my wishes. From the revision of the Honrichs, Hecker and all the accounts, everything will appear. To Hecker may Your Serene Highness deign to write in your own hand according to the enclosed draft. For since I stand in litigation with the villain, I

cannot now issue any orders touching the service, certainly not to B.; at least he would not act rightly upon them. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's Blizanow

the 29th of July 1811.

May Your Serene Highness have the messenger paid; he receives 6 gg per mile. I gave him 1 Rthl for the road. most humble and obedient servant v. Triebenfeld
