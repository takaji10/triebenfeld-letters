--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld legt ein soeben erhaltenes Schreiben des in großer Not lebenden Fürsten von Hohenlohe vor und bittet, sich seiner zu erbarmen. Nur der Vorschuss von 112000 Rthl könne ihn retten: Die Curländischen Erben würden befriedigt, Cosmars Gewalt ende, Sr Majestät erhalte die 112000 Rthl aus den Fonds der Majoratsgüter zurück. Zu den in jenem Schreiben erwähnten Trąbczyner Gütern im Kreis Konin erbittet er Weisung, ob er jetzt die Urkunden und Dokumente einreichen darf.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld submits a letter he has just received from the Prince of Hohenlohe, who is living in great distress, and asks that mercy be shown him. He says only the advance of 112000 Rthl can save him: the Courland heirs would be satisfied, Cosmar's power would end, and His Majesty would get the 112000 Rthl back from the funds of the entailed estates. Regarding the Trąbczyn estates in the district of Konin mentioned in that letter, he asks for instructions whether he may now submit the deeds and documents.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

I [uncertain: rise to] Your Most Serene Highness's most gracious pardon, that I [uncertain: watchman/warden] a letter just now received from the so sorely pressed Prince of Hohenlohe, to lay at the feet of the same, Most High, [uncertain: Lebensest] — of the boundless distress of this old [uncertain: both/upright] man no design of a picture can be made; he suffers unspeakably — Have mercy, Your Most Serene Highness, and [uncertain: soften/lands] the [uncertain: Blad] of the poor Prince, by the advance of 112000 Rthl. By that alone is the good Prince, languishing in the utmost need, saved. The Courland heirs (one for whose sake Cosmar has forced his way into the entailed estates alone, are at once entirely satisfied; Cosmar's power has an end, and even His Majesty comes out ex nexa and will soon receive back, out of the great funds of the entailed estates, the advance of 112000 R; every creditor receives his own bit by bit, and the Prince need hunger no more. As concerns the Trąbczyn estates in the Konin district, of which the Prince makes mention in his letter, thereupon I beg Your Serene Highness's most gracious instruction — whether I may now submit the deeds and other documents.

Your Most Serene Highness's

humble and obedient servant

Vienna, the 12th of February

1815
