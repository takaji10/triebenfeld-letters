--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld reports on Napoleon's return and the threat of war; he says little can be achieved amid the confusion, that Hardenberg promises everything and that Stägemann acts as he pleases. Since Prusimska has had the Polish estates restored to her, he submitted the enclosed memorandum: it calculates the losses on Kamionna and Trąbczyn (assessed at 316000 Rthl, net value 240000 Rthl) and demands 390100 Rthl in cash or in estates. He asks for the swiftest possible reply and for blank forms; Beguelin would like a letter from the Prince.

Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord!

The present epoch will surely remain the most remarkable in the annals of the world, and whatever may also come to pass will only ever be a consequence of it — What I fear is that the sovereigns will everywhere come too late. For Napoleon stands today almost at the Rhine, and the Austrians, [uncertain: Russians] etc., are only now beginning to negotiate travel monies — Wellington departed only yesterday, and will draw together the English or Hanoverian troops — Only our troops are being pushed forward, and were these to be beaten, then God help us, for the parvenu has already over 200000 men assembled — It is [uncertain: shameful] that such a thing could happen under the eyes of all the sovereigns, and it is grievous that one still holds one's peace of so little worth — On the 7th of April the Russian troops pass through Prague — thither the monarchs will go and then return here — but when would [uncertain: we] come to the Rhine? The war is therefore as good as fully declared. — Napoleon is away from Paris, hastening toward the Rhine; Ney has gone with a strong corps toward Switzerland, and at present one corps after another is being formed by Napoleon, so that one will not know whither one should first turn — that amid all these confusions little can be effected here, Your Serene Highness may easily judge — Prince Hardenberg promises henceforth to do everything — but Mr von Stägemann thinks and acts as it pleases him — Something will nevertheless happen before one departs from here and goes to the army. And since the two [uncertain: gentlemen] have given the Polish estates back to the Prusimska, I have submitted the accompanying Pro Memoria / which meanwhile is well worked out / — upon which I await the decision — Dismissed, Your Serene Highness cannot [uncertain: would] be with it. General Amstetten is the only one who has good will — and he assures that you must be compensated by estates — For the rest, everything seems to wish to [uncertain: win] another shape, for nothing is more changeable than the fortune of war — the snail-shaped course of all business through 7 months, and the butter-soft pliancy and most wrongly applied

generosity have made everything thick-skinned and spoiled much — and one doubts, too, whether people, after the previous treatment, will go at it again with so cheerful a spirit. Pro Memoria. In 1796 the late King of blessed memory granted to the Prince of Hohenlohe the Kamionna and Trąbczyn estates, which had [text lost] to the Treasury after the fled elder Prusimski. But Prusimski had pledged the Kamionna estates to a certain von Broniewski for 80000 rt, and the Trąbczyn estates to von Chełmski for 36000 ducats. Any maturing of possession of these estates, on the Prince's part, was thus as [uncertain: Frörmig] as the drawing of any revenues was thinkable — By way of law everything had first to be sought out and [uncertain: decided] — The suit thereon was not concluded for me in 1804; the Prince found himself obliged, in order to be able to call the estates his own at last, to conclude a settlement with Broniewski and to pay him 55000 rt in composition. The costs of the suit amounted to 6000 rt. This amounts to — 61000 rt.

From Midsummer 1805 to Midsummer 1807 the Prince thus possessed the estates only, during which time he sold the same to the bank director Leixner. In 1807, however, the Polish Treasury took the estates from him by force, and that 14 weeks after the Peace of Tilsit. During these 8 years, while Prusimski's daughter possessed the estates, there were due to the Prince the interest on the foregoing 65000 rt, and this amounts to 24400 rt. The Prince's claim in cash from the Kamionna estates accordingly amounts to 85400 rt. During the Prince's time of possession he had the estates judicially assessed; the assessment came out at — 180000 rt. Deducting from this the 55000 rt brought into account above, there remains an excess value of 125000 rt.

This excess value, however, now belongs, together with the 8 years' yield of the estates, to the bank director Leixner as purchaser of the same. The 85400 rt of so-called pledge money belong to the Prince alone and solely — the Jew Meyer Bernhard [illegible] with him the gulden; the Prince gave for it a mortgage on Wieniec etc., which estates the said Meyer Bernhard has until now held in pledge. As regards the Trąbczyn estates, these too were assessed at 316000 rt — upon which the Prince contracted debts

1. from Lichnowski 70000 rt.

2. from Weigel 30000 rt.

3. from Grotowski 20000 rt.

4. from Zerboni 10000 rt.

5. [uncertain: Mann Ge. Seher Thos] 8000 rt. On these the Prince paid to Lichnowski 32000 rt, to Weigel 30000 rt.

There thus remains owing, to be brought into deduction, 76000 rt. The clean true value of these estates accordingly amounts to 240000 rt. The Prince had to [text lost] these estates at 12100 rt; this did from 1806 to 1815 — 108900 rt. Hereupon[?], whether the interest on 76000 rt at 34200 rt so amount, the 9 years' revenues withheld from the Prince 74700 rt. Total 390100 rt.

which are now to be made good to the Prince of Hohenlohe either in cash or by estates.

The 1st of April. Now the French history begins to become very [uncertain: culpable] — Napoleon has been proclaimed Emperor by the whole country, indeed by the [uncertain: people] — the English declare that they will not meddle in the internal French affairs — So Napoleon will probably remain Emperor — the Russians are indeed drawing nearer, but — Crosswise wage-marches are being made for me — let them only eat themselves out in Germany and lay everything waste — [uncertain: Heirs] it goes likewise with the O___ troops; these move very ponderously, for there everything is wanting — the English appear to have set the whole affair in train and remain standing quite at a distance — Saxony is as little to be trusted as Poland — in the end the Prussians, with the Dutch and Hessians and the small ones etc., will be left standing quite alone, and the two Emperors will demand that these should first fetch the chestnuts out of the fire so that they may be able to eat their fill. Napoleon will put himself in [uncertain: lower] communication with Murat or Italy and, so far as possible, take the combined forces in the flank and in the rear; these must now move against him, while he sends his accomplices in haste with several bands toward the Netherlands — and whence are men to come in haste there, to ward off disasters —?
