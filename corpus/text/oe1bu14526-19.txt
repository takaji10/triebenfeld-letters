--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Hereditary-lease contract of 28 and 29 July 1804 by which Prince Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, through his general attorney, building councillor Johann Wilhelm Glenck, conveys the demesnes, rents, propination and other rights of the lordship of Betsche, including the Obra farm, to the burgesses of Betsche and the villages of Stalluhn, Schartzig, Schwiechodezin, Stocki, Lowin and Kulligowo, represented by Matheus Młynkiewicz and Johann Chmara, for 180,000 Rthl plus 1,000 Rthl yearly ground-rent; 137,000 Rthl is taken over in intabulated mortgage debts, 35,000 Rthl paid at once, 8,000 Rthl at Midsummer. Jurisdiction, hunt, castle and 10 per cent transfer fee reserved; pending suits and the Bornstädt eviction pass to the communities. Certified engrossment under the Betsche court office seal.

§ 1. His Most Serene Highness the reigning Prince Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, Governor of Breslau, Inspector General of Infantry and Knight of the Royal Prussian Great Orders, as hereditary lord of the lordship of Betsche situated in the district of Meseritz, makes over to the burgesses of the town of Betsche and the inhabitants of the villages of Kulligowo, Stalluhn, Schartzig, Stocki, Schwiechodezin, Lowin all the demesne farms, fields, gardens, meadows, marsh, lakes, ponds, pond-sites, rivers, pastures, heaths, waste grounds, forests, woods, coppices, thickets belonging to them, money and produce rents, the propination rights, brewing

and distilling rights, brick and lime kilns, the Obra demesne farm, in short each and every profitable right and prerogative which His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe has hitherto used and enjoyed as proprietor, and may, can and shall enjoy, in Pausch und Bogen, by whatever name they may be called, and in particular also the right of sale over the village mayors' holdings and demesne farms granted under hereditary quit-rent tenure, excepting the jurisdiction and the transfer fees (Laudemium), and the hunt, which His Most Serene Highness, as superior proprietor and permanent ground-lord of Betsche, reserves for Himself and for His most high successors — as their true hereditary property, in such wise and manner that the said town commune of Betsche and the mentioned places may, can and shall deal therewith as with their true property, may sell the same, [uncertain: sell it back], make gift of it, for a

§ 2. well agreed purchase and hereditary-tenure sum of one hundred and eighty thousand Reichsthaler

in Royal Prussian Courant according to the coinage standard of 1764, and an annual ground-rent of one thousand Reichsthaler in Courant according to the same coinage standard.

3. With the payment of the purchase and hereditary-tenure sum it shall be held in this manner. The town and the remaining village communities take over, ad rationes pretii, the mortgage debts named hereafter, intabulated upon the lordship of Betsche in the mortgage book of the Royal Government at Posen, namely

1, 48000 Rthl entered for the building councillor Glenck, of which sum 36000 Rthl are ceded to the banker Salomon Nathan junior at Berlin and 12000 Rthl to the banker Meyer Bernhard at Frankfurt an der Oder.

48000 Rthl

2, 2000 Rthl which are entered for the merchant Abraham Moses Hirschel at Breslau.

3, 5000 Rthl which are intabulated for the Baron von der Marwitz of Budnik and ceded to the banker Meyer Bernhard at Frankfurt an der Oder.

4, 5000 Rthl which are intabulated for the widowed senior district councillor's wife Christiana Dorothea Michaelis, née Eichborn.

5, 5000 Rthl which are intabulated for the building councillor Johann Wilhelm Glenck, and have been ceded by him to the banker Meyer Bernhard at Frankfurt, which items from No. 2 to 5 have already been called in by the creditors and are payable at Christmas of the current year.

Whereby it is noted that the interest on the 48000 Rthl is settled up to the 2nd of July of the current year, but that on the remaining capitals up to the 26th of July.

65000 Rthl

6., 72000 Rthl which are already intabulated for the banker Meyer Bernhard at Frankfurt an der Oder, and the interest thereon at 5 per cent is settled up to the 26th of July

of this year, as with the other capitals; and they promise to discharge these sums to the creditors and to come to agreement with them thereon.

7, 137,000 Rthl. The deputies stipulate to remit forthwith, out of the remainder of the purchase and hereditary-tenure sum, thirty-five thousand Reichsthaler to His Most Serene Highness at Breslau, carriage free, but the remainder of 8000 Rthl at the term of Midsummer next year, together with 5 per cent interest, without fail, if it be called in at the proper time.

The aforementioned 35000 Rthl shall be paid in this manner: tomorrow, the 29th of July, 7000 Rthl, and a week from tomorrow the remaining 28000 Rthl shall be paid, and indeed the 7000 Rthl shall be sent carriage free to Breslau at the cost of the payers.

If among the moneys to be paid there are heavy Dutch milled ducats, these shall be accepted at 4 Groschen agio per ducat. Should the communities not pay the 28000 Rthl a week from tomorrow, that is on the 5th of August, then the whole contract concluded this day ceases, and the communities are bound to pay the costs occasioned, but shall receive the 7000 Rthl back again, however without interest.

§ 4. The handing over follows as soon as the 28000 Rthl which are to be paid on the 5th of August are paid; since however the former tenant von Bornstädt is still in possession of the demesne farm here, and a suit is pending over his eviction, the communities do not indeed insist that possession of the demesne farm be given up to them forthwith, but are rather content that he remain therein until the judgment of eviction is legally in force, and that he be evicted only when the judgment of eviction is legally in force; but the communities shall for this be given by the Prince, His Serene Highness, a fair and appropriate compensation suited to the matter.

As regards on the other hand the forests, these shall be closed forthwith, just as

they already are now, and the timber felled therein, in so far as it has not been felled for the husbandry, remains the property of His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe.

§ 5. All the manorial dwelling, farm, forest and other buildings are likewise made over to the communities heritably and in property; the communities are however bound to leave the foresters their dwelling in one room in the foresters' houses until Michaelmas of the current year, as also the use of their gardens. His Serene Highness also reserves to himself the castle as the seat of the court, the dwelling of the court servant, and a dwelling for his most high self or his commissioners, and also a stable for 2 horses, which they shall themselves maintain in fit structural condition. In the case of building works upon this edifice the communities are bound to let His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen and his successors have the bricks at cost price, and to furnish the timber for the construction free of charge from the heath. The communities are also bound to furnish, free of charge from the heath, the wood needed for heating

the court room and the court servant's, and to cart it thither. In like manner the communities are also bound, when His Most Serene Highness or his commissioners are present here, to furnish the necessary firewood free of charge and to cart it thither.

§ 6. As regards the timber purchase contracts concluded between His Most Serene Highness and certain merchants, His most high self promises to satisfy the timber purchasers, without the communities contributing anything thereto.

§ 7. The town and village communities mentioned at the outset bind themselves, upon alienation of their plots of ground, to pay 10 per cent transfer fee (Laudemium) upon the purchase sum to His Most Serene Highness and his successors in possession. From this transfer fee are however excepted those holdings which the

communities now wish to lay out anew; but if these be sold in future, the transfer fee shall likewise be paid thereon at 10 per cent.

§ 8. Further His Most Serene Highness reserves to himself.

That, if He himself or his successors in possession should come hither, or should send their commissioners hither, the communities are bound to furnish the relay carriages up to a distance of 4 miles, out and back.

§ 9. Further His Serene Highness reserves to himself, should he obtain the right of patronage over the church at Betsche, the right of patronage; the town and village communities shall however have the right to choose the clergyman and to put him forward in nomination, which nominated clergyman His Most Serene Highness promises to present to the episcopal consistory.

§ 10. The suits between the community of Stocki and the Prince of Hohenlohe concerning ownership of the Stocki marsh, between the

Prince of Hohenlohe and the possessor of Zie[uncertain: lomipat], concerning the right of redemption, and between the district administrator von Schmaling as possessor of the demesne farm in Głazewo and His Serene Highness the Lord of Hohenlohe concerning confirmation of the privilege, as also the suit between the Prince of Hohenlohe and Hertzig in Lowino concerning restitution of the [illegible], the communities take over at their own risk and cost, and whatever they may win at law or obtain by way of compromise falls to the communities, without His Serene Highness having the least claim thereto.

§ 11. The competence taxes and other public and further dues and burdens hitherto paid by His Serene Highness as manorial lordship (Dominium) of Betsche, by whatever name they be called, among which belong also the sick-transport carriages to Schillner and Punctner Hauland, as also the carriages for the fetching and departure of the court, and by whatever other names they be called, the said town and village communities take over; and should these dues be raised in future, the latter can demand no compensation from the Prince

of Hohenlohe, His Serene Highness. The communities also take over the contribution of fifty Reichsthaler which His Most Serene Highness promised to render to the town school here for 1 year.

§ 12. Both contracting parties bind themselves to have this contract published before the competent authorities and to seek confirmation of it; and His Most Serene Highness herewith makes himself answerable to exert himself as best he may that this contract be confirmed by the competent authorities, and that the consent be granted to the communities for the further dismemberment of the plots of ground made over to them.

§ 13. As soon as the delivery of possession of the demesne farm here and of the Obra demesne farm has taken place, there shall at the same time be handed over to the town and village communities that livestock and farm inventory which is sold together under the purchase and hereditary-tenure sum, in the same number as the tenant von Bornstädt received the same in the year 1800 at the handing over.

§ 14.

§ 14. This contract shall be binding for all time upon His Most Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen for himself and his most high heirs and successors, as also on the part of the town and village communities. And the canon or ground-rent of 1000 Rthl payable yearly shall be paid in half-yearly instalments of 500 Rthl, namely each time at the term of Christmas and the term of St John the Baptist, yearly, sub paena executiones, and sent to Breslau carriage free.

§ 15. So long as the communities do not alienate or dismember the plots of ground and rights made over to them, the status passivae remains in its present condition. But as soon as the demesne farm is dismembered, the hereditary purchasers are bound to have the intabulated items transferred onto their plots of ground and struck out in the name of His Most Serene Highness.

§ 16. For the stipulated canon of 1000 Rthl yearly all the communities are liable in solidum, and they consent to the entry thereof in the mortgage book upon their plots of ground, just as on the other hand His Most Serene Highness consents that this present contract be entered in the mortgage book of the manorial lordship (Dominium) of Betsche and its appurtenances.

§ 17. Finally the contracting parties renounce each and every objection, by whatever name they may be called; in particular the town and village communities renounce the plea of injury beyond the half, which was explained to them to this effect:

that if the agreed purchase and hereditary-tenure sum exceeded the true value of the plots of ground and rights made over by double, they would thereby be

free to withdraw from the contract.

§ 18. His Most Serene Highness reserves to himself the superior oversight over the forests, which he will commit to the Royal Chief Forester Feller, and the communities bind themselves to compensate him for his pains and to come to agreement with him thereon. Herewith, since nothing further was to be brought forward, the proceedings were closed, the protocol read out, interpreted by the sworn interpreter Echaust to the deputies of the town and village communities who are not perfectly versed in the German language, and who had forbidden the keeping of a separate side-protocol in the Polish language, approved and signed; whereupon it was further noted by the contracting parties that the costs of the drawing up,

expedition, publication and confirmation shall be borne in halves by both parties. Read out, approved and signed; read by myself and approved: Johann Wilhelm Glenck, Princely Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen general attorney. Peter Friederich August von Triebenfeld. Jan Chmara. Registrar. Actum Betsche, the 29th of July 1804. This day there appeared

1, the Princely Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen general attorney, the building councillor Johann Wilhelm Glenck, likewise the special

attorney of the burgesses, Matheus Młynkiewicz, and the authorised representative Chmara of the communities of Kulligowo, Stalluhn, Schartzig, Stocki, Schwiechodezin, Lowin, and they bring forward the following in addition to the contract drawn up yesterday.

§ 1. The Prince of Hohenlohe, His Serene Highness, assures the communities that apart from the mortgage capitals already called in, none shall be called in within half a year.

§ 2. The town and village communities bind themselves, in criminal investigations that may occur, to bear for ever the costs of the investigation, in so far as the remaining communities belonging to the Betsche key are not themselves bound to bear the same.

Herewith the proceedings were closed, the protocol read out, interpreted by the sworn interpreter Echaust to the deputies of the community who are not wholly versed in the German language and who had forbidden the keeping of a protocol in the Polish language, approved, and signed.
