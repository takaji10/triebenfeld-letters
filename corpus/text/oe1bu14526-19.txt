--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Engrossed hereditary-lease and purchase contract by which Friedrich Ludwig zu Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, through General Plenipotentiary Baurath Johann Wilhelm Glenk, makes over the Lordship of Betsche — demesne farms, Obra farm, forests, propination, brewing and distilling rights — to the town of Betsche and the villages Stalluhn, Kulligowo, Schartzig, Stocki, Schwiechodezin and Lowin, represented by Matheus Młynkiewicz and Jan Chmara, for 180,000 Rthl plus 1,000 Rthl annual canon: 137,000 Rthl in assumed mortgages (Glenk, Meyer Bernhard, Hirschel, von der Marwitz, Michaelis), 35,000 Rthl cash, 8,000 Rthl at St John. Jurisdiction, Laudemien and hunt reserved; interpreted by Echaust; supplement of 29 July appended.

§ 1. His Most Serene Highness the reigning Prince Friedrich Ludwig zu Hohenlohe Ingelfingen, Governor of Breslau, General Inspector of the Infantry and Knight of the Royal Prussian Great Orders, as hereditary lord of the Lordship of Betsche situated in the Meseritz district, makes over to the burghership of the town of Betsche and the inhabitants of the villages Kulligowo, Stalluhn, Schartzig, Stocki, Schwiechodezin, Lowin all the demesne farms belonging to them, fields, gardens, meadows, marsh, lakes, ponds, pond-sites, rivers, pastures, heaths, waste grounds (Lehden), forests, woods, thickets, brushwood, money and payments in kind, the propination rights, the brewing

and distilling rights, brick and lime kilns, the Obra demesne farm — in short, each and every profitable right and privilege which His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe has hitherto used and enjoyed as owner, and may, can and shall enjoy — in Pausch und Bogen, by whatever names they may be called, and in particular also the right of sale over the village-mayoralties (Scholtiseyen) and demesne farms granted out on hereditary quit-rent terms; excepting the jurisdiction and the Laudemien, and the hunt, which His Most Serene Highness reserves to Himself as supreme owner and perpetual ground-lord of Betsche, and to His successors — as their true hereditary property and possession, in such manner and so that the said town commune of Betsche and the mentioned localities may deal therewith as with their true property, may, can and shall sell, [uncertain: rettauflichen] and give the same away; for a

§ 2. duly agreed purchase and hereditary-tenure sum of one hundred and eighty thousand Reichsthaler

in Royal Prussian Courant according to the coinage standard of 1764, and an annual ground-rent of one thousand Reichsthaler in Courant according to the same coinage standard.

3. With the payment of the purchase and hereditary-tenure sum it shall be held in this manner. The town and the remaining village communes take over, ad Rationes Pretii, the mortgage debts named below, intabulated upon the Lordship of Betsche in the mortgage book of the Royal Government at Posen, namely:

1. 48000 Rthl entered for Baurath Glenk, of which sum 36000 Rthl have been ceded to the banker Salomon Nathan junior at Berlin and 12000 Rthl to the banker Meyer Bernhard at Frankfurth a/O.

48000 Rthl

2. 2000 Rthl, which are entered for the merchant Abraham Moses Hirschel at Breslau.

3. 5000 Rthl, which are intabulated for the Freiherr von der Marwitz at Budnik and have been ceded to the banker Meyer Bernhard at Frankfurth a/O.

4. 5000 Rthl, which are intabulated for the widowed Ober-Amtsräthin Christiana Dorothea Michaelis, née Eichborn.

5. 5000 Rthl, which are intabulated for Baurath Johann Wilhelm Glenk, and have been ceded by him to the banker Meyer Bernhard at Frankfurth; all which items from No. 2 to 5 have already been called in by the creditors and are payable at Christmas of the current year.

Whereby it is noted that the interest on the 48000 Rthl has been settled up to the 2nd of July of this year, but that on the remaining capitals up to the 26th of July.

65000 Rthl

6. 72000 Rthl, which are already intabulated for the banker Meyer Bernhard at Frankfurth a/O, and the interest at 5 per cent settled up to the 26th of July of this year, as with the remaining capitals; and they promise to settle these sums with the creditors and to come to agreement with them thereon.

7. 137,000 Rthl. The deputies stipulate to settle, out of the remainder of the purchase and hereditary-tenure sum, thirty-five thousand Reichsthaler forthwith to His Most Serene Highness at Breslau, carriage-free, but the remainder of 8000 Rthl at the term of St John of next year, together with 5 per cent interest, without fail, if it be called in at the proper time.

The above-mentioned 35000 Rthl shall be paid in this manner: tomorrow, the 29th of July, 7000 Rthl, and a week from tomorrow the remaining 28000 Rthl shall be paid; and indeed the 7000 Rthl shall be sent carriage-free to Breslau at the cost of the payers.

Should the communes not pay the 28000 Rthl a week from tomorrow, that is on the 5th of August, then the whole contract concluded today ceases, and the communes are bound to pay the costs occasioned, but shall receive the 7000 Rthl back again, however without interest.

§ 4. The transfer shall follow as soon as the 28000 Rthl, which are to be paid on the 5th of August, are paid; since, however, the former tenant von Bornstaedt is still in possession of the local demesne farm, and a suit is pending over his eviction, the communes do not indeed insist that possession of the demesne farm be surrendered to them forthwith, but will rather be content that, until the eviction judgment has force of law, he remain therein, and that only when the eviction judgment has force of law shall he be evicted; yet the communes shall for this be given a fair and appropriate indemnification suited to the matter by His Serene Highness the Prince.

As regards the forests, on the other hand, these shall be closed forthwith, just as

they now already are, and the timber felled therein, in so far as it has not been felled for the estate economy, remains the property of His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe.

§ 5. All the manorial dwelling, farm, forest and other buildings are likewise made over to the communes hereditarily and in property; yet the communes are bound to leave the foresters their dwelling in one room in the forester's houses until Michaelmas of the current year, as also the use of their gardens. His Serene Highness likewise reserves the castle as the seat of the court, the dwelling of the court servant, and a dwelling for Himself or His commissioners, and also a stabling for 2 horses, which they shall themselves maintain in good structural repair. In the case of building works upon this edifice, the communes are bound to let His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe Ingelfingen and his successors have the bricks at cost price, and to furnish the timber for the construction free of charge from the heath. The communes are likewise bound to furnish, free of charge from the heath, the wood needed for the heating

of the court room and of the court servant, and to cart it thither. In like manner the communes are also bound, when His Most Serene Highness or His commissioners are present here, to furnish the necessary firewood free of charge and to cart it thither.

§ 6. As regards the timber-purchase contracts concluded between His Most Serene Highness and certain merchants, He promises to settle with the timber purchasers, without the communes contributing anything thereto.

§ 7. The town and village communes mentioned at the outset bind themselves, upon alienations of their parcels of land, to render 10 per cent Laudemium out of the purchase sum to His Most Serene Highness and His successors in possession. From this Laudemium, however, those holdings are excepted which the

communes now wish to lay out anew; but if these be sold in future, then the Laudemium of 10 per cent shall likewise be rendered thereof.

§ 8. Further, His Most Serene Highness reserves to Himself:

that if He Himself, or His successors in possession, should come hither, or should send their commissioners hither, the communes are bound to render the relay-cartage (Vorspannfuhren) up to a distance of 4 miles, out and back.

§ 9. Further, His Serene Highness reserves to Himself, should He obtain the right of patronage over the church of Betsche, that right of patronage; yet the town and village communes shall have the right to elect the clergyman and to put him forward in nomination, which nominated clergyman His Most Serene Highness promises to present to the episcopal Consistory.

§ 10. The suits between the commune of Stocki and the Prince of Hohenlohe concerning ownership of the Stocki marsh; between the

Prince of Hohenlohe and the possessor of Zie[uncertain: lomipat] concerning the right of redemption; and between the Landrath von Schmaling as possessor of the demesne farm in Głazewo and His Serene Highness of Hohenlohe concerning confirmation of the privilege; as also the suit between the Prince of Hohenlohe and Hertzig in Lowino concerning the restitution of the [uncertain: Schales tro] — these the communes take over at their own risk and cost, and whatever they may win in litigation or obtain by way of settlement falls to the communes, without His Serene Highness having the least claim thereto.

§ 11. The Competenz taxes and other public and further dues and burdens hitherto rendered by His Serene Highness as Dominium of Betsche, by whatever names they may be called — among which belong also the sick-transport cartage into the Schillner and Punctner Hauland, as also the cartage for fetching and conveying away the court, and by whatever other names they may be called — the said town and village communes take over; and the latter cannot, should these dues be increased in future, demand any compensation from the Prince

of Hohenlohe, His Serene Highness. The communes also take over the contribution of fifty Reichsthaler which His Most Serene Highness promised to render to the town school here for 1 year.

§ 12. Both contracting parties bind themselves to have this contract published before the competent authorities and to seek confirmation thereof; and His Most Serene Highness herewith makes Himself liable to exert Himself to the best of His ability that this contract be confirmed by the competent authorities, and that the consent to the further dismemberment of the parcels of land made over to them be granted to the communes.

§ 13. As soon as the tradition of the local demesne farm and of the Obra demesne farm has taken place, there shall at the same time be handed over to the town and village communes that livestock and farm inventory which is sold along with the purchase and hereditary-tenure sum, in the number in which the tenant von Bornstaet received the same in the year 1800 upon the transfer.

§ 14.

§ 14. This contract shall be binding for His Most Serene Highness the Prince zu Hohenloh-Ingelfingen for Himself and for His heirs and successors, as also on the part of the town and village communes, for all time. And the canon or ground-rent of 1000 Rthl to be paid annually shall be rendered in half-yearly instalments of 500 Rthl, namely each time at the term of Christmas and the term of St John the Baptist, yearly, sub paena executiones, and sent to Breslau carriage-free.

§ 15. So long as the communes do not alienate or dismember the parcels of land and rights made over to them, the Status Passivorum remains in its present condition. But as soon as the demesne farm is dismembered, the hereditary purchasers are bound to have the intabulated items transferred onto their own parcels of land, and to have them cancelled in the name of His Most Serene Highness.

§ 16. For the stipulated canon of 1000 Rthl yearly all the communes are liable in solidum, and they consent to its entry in the mortgage book against their parcels of land; just as, on the other hand, His Most Serene Highness also consents that this present contract be entered in the mortgage book of the Dominium of Betsche and its appurtenances.

§ 17. Finally, the contracting parties renounce each and every objection, by whatever names they may be called; in particular the town and village communes renounce the plea of laesio enormis (injury beyond the half), which was explained to them to this effect:

that if the agreed purchase and hereditary-tenure sum should exceed the true value of the parcels of land and rights made over by double, then they would by that means

the withdrawal from the contract be free to them.

§ 18. His Most Serene Highness reserves to Himself the supreme supervision over the forests, which he will entrust to the Royal Chief Forester Feller; and the communes bind themselves to indemnify him for his exertions, and to come to agreement with him thereon. Therewith, as nothing further was to be brought forward, the proceedings were closed, the protocol read out, interpreted by the sworn interpreter Echaust to the deputies of the town and village communes, who are not fully conversant with the German language, and who had forbidden the keeping of a separate side-protocol in the Polish language, approved and signed; whereupon it was further noted by the contracting parties that the costs of the taking down,

expedition, publication and confirmation shall be borne in half by both parties. Read out, approved and signed; read by myself and approved. Actuarius. Actum Betsche, the 29th of July 1804. Today there appeared

1. the Princely Hohenloh-Ingelfingen General Plenipotentiary, Baurath Johann Wilhelm Glenk; likewise the special

plenipotentiary of the burghership, Matheus Młynkiewicz, and the plenipotentiary Chmara of the communes Kulligowo, Stalluhn, Schartzig, Stocki, Schwiechodezin, Lowin, and they bring the following to the contract taken down yesterday.

§ 1. His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe assures the communes that, apart from the mortgage capitals already called in, none shall be called in within half a year.

§ 2. The town and village communes bind themselves, in the event of criminal investigations arising, to bear the costs of investigation for ever, in so far as the remaining communes belonging to the Betsche Schlüssel are not themselves bound to bear the same.

Thereupon the proceedings were closed, the protocol read out, interpreted by the sworn interpreter Echaust to the deputies of the commune, who are not fully conversant with the German language, and who had forbidden the keeping of a protocol in the Polish language, approved and signed.
