--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Engrossment of the hereditary-lease contract of 28-29 July 1804, made before the Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen court at Betsche between Baurath Johann Wilhelm Glenk, General Bevollmächtigter of Fürst Friedrich Ludwig, and the deputies Matheus Młynkiewicz and Johann Chmara for the town of Betsche and the villages Stalluhn, Kulligowo, Schartzig, Stocki, Schwiechodezin and Lowin: all demesnes, forests, waters and propination rights pass in Pausch und Bogen for 180,000 Rthlr plus 1,000 Rthlr yearly canon, chiefly by assuming 137,000 Rthlr of intabulated debts (Glenk, Meyer Bernhard, Salomon Nathan), with jurisdiction, Laudemien, hunt, castle and forest supervision reserved.

§. 1. His Most Serene Highness the reigning Prince Friedrich Ludwig zu Hohenlohe Ingelfingen, Governor of Breslau, General Inspector of Infantry and Knight of the Royal Prussian great Orders, as hereditary lord of the lordship of Betsche situated in the Meseritz district, does make over to the burghership of the town of Betsche and the inhabitants of the villages of Kulligowo, Stalluhn, Schartzig, Stocki, Schwiechodezin, Lowin, all the demesne farms, arable fields, gardens, meadows, marsh, lakes, ponds, pond-sites, rivers, pastures, heaths, [uncertain: Löden], forests, woods, thickets, brushwood, money and produce rents belonging to him, the propination rights, brewing

and distilling rights, brick and lime kilns, the Obra demesne farm - in short each and every profitable right and prerogative which the Prince of Hohenlohe, His Serene Highness, has hitherto as proprietor used and enjoyed, and also can, may and shall enjoy - in Pausch und Bogen, by whatever name they may be called, and in particular also the right of sale over the Scholtiseyen and demesne farms granted out at hereditary-rent tenure; excepting the jurisdiction and Laudemien and the hunt, which His Most Serene Highness, as superior proprietor and permanent ground-lord of Betsche, reserves for Himself and for His Most High successors - as their true hereditary property, in such wise and manner that the said town commune of Betsche and the said localities may deal with it as with their true property, may sell it, [uncertain: rettauflichen], and give it away, can, may and shall do so, for a

§ 2. duly agreed purchase and hereditary-tenure sum of one hundred and eighty thousand Reichsthaler

in Royal Prussian Courant according to the coinage standard of 1764, and an annual ground-rent of one thousand Reichsthaler in Courant according to the same coinage standard.

3. With regard to the payment of the purchase and hereditary-tenure sum, the matter is to be held thus: the town and the remaining village communities take over, ad Rationes Practii, the following named mortgage debts intabulated against the lordship of Betsche in the mortgage book of the Royal Government at Posen, namely:

1. 48000 rtlr entered for Baurath Glenk, of which sum 36000 rttl have been ceded to the banker Salomon Nathan junior at Berlin and 12000 rttl to the banker Meyer Bernhard at Frankfurth a/O.

48000 Rthl

2. 2000 rtl. which are entered for the merchant Abraham Moses Hirschel at Breslau.

3. 5000 rttllr which are intabulated for the Freiherr von der Marwitz of Budnik and have been ceded to the banker Meyer Bernhard at Frankfurth a/O.

4. 5000 rthl which are intabulated for the widowed Ober-Amts-Räthin Christiana Dorothea Michaelis, née Eichborn.

5. 5000 rthl which are intabulated for Baurath Johann Wilhelm Glenk, and have been ceded by him to the banker Meyer Bernhard at Frankfurth; all which items from No. 2 to 5 have already been called in by the creditors and are payable at Christmas of the current year.

Whereby it is noted that the interest on the 48000 rttlr has been settled up to the 2nd of July of this year, but that on the remaining capitals up to the 26th of July.

65000 Rthl

6. 72000 rthl which are already intabulated for the banker Meyer Bernhard at Frankfurth a/O, and the interest thereon at 5 per cent settled up to the 26th of July of this year, as with the remaining capitals; and they promise to satisfy these sums to the creditors and to come to agreement with them thereon.

7. 137,000 Rthl. The deputies stipulate to remit, out of the remainder of the purchase and hereditary-tenure sum, thirty-five thousand Reichsthaler forthwith to His Most Serene Highness at Breslau, carriage free; but the remainder of 8000 rthlr at the term of St John's of the coming year, together with 5 per cent interest, without fail, if it be called in at the proper time.

The aforesaid 35000 rthlr are to be paid in this manner: tomorrow, the 29th of July, 7000 rthlr, and a week from tomorrow the remaining 28000 rthlr shall be paid; and the 7000 rthlr are to be sent carriage free to Breslau at the cost of the payers.

Should the communities not pay the 28000 rtlr a week from tomorrow, that is on the 5th of August, then the whole contract concluded today shall cease, and the communities are bound to pay the costs occasioned thereby, but shall receive the 7000 rttlr back again, though without interest.

§. 4. The handing over shall follow as soon as the 28000 rthlr, which are to be paid on the 5th of August, have been paid. Since however the former tenant von Bornstaedt is still in possession of the demesne farm here, and a suit is pending over his eviction, the communities do not indeed insist that possession of the demesne farm be at once given up to them, but will rather be content that he remain therein so long as the eviction judgment is not final in law, and that he be evicted only when the eviction judgment is final in law; yet there shall be given to the communities for this an equitable and appropriate compensation befitting the matter by His Serene Highness the Prince.

As regards the forests, on the other hand, they shall be closed forthwith, just as

they now already are, and the timber felled therein, in so far as it has not been felled for the use of the farm, remains the property of His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe.

§. 5. All the manorial dwelling-houses, farm buildings, forest buildings and other buildings are likewise made over to the communities in hereditary property; yet the communities are bound to leave the foresters their dwelling in one room in the forester's houses until Michaelmas of the current year, as also the use of their gardens. His Serene Highness likewise reserves the castle as the seat of the court, as the dwelling of the court servant, and as a dwelling for His Most High self or His commissioners, and also a stabling for 2 horses, which they shall themselves maintain in repair. In the case of building work on this edifice, the communities are bound to let His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe Ingelfingen and his successors have the bricks at cost price, and to deliver the timber for the construction from the heath free of charge. The communities are also bound to deliver free of charge from the heath the wood needed for the heating

of the court room and of the court servant, and to cart it thither. In like manner the communities are also bound, when His Most Serene Highness or His commissioners are present here, to furnish the necessary firewood free of charge and to cart it thither.

§. 6. As regards the timber purchase contracts concluded between His Most Serene Highness and certain merchants, His Most High self promises to satisfy the timber purchasers, without the communities making any contribution thereto.

§ 7. The town and village communities mentioned at the outset bind themselves, upon alienations of their parcels of land, to render 10 per cent Laudemium on the purchase sum to His Most Serene Highness and his successors in possession. From this Laudemium, however, are excepted those holdings which the

communities now intend newly to lay out; but if these should in future be sold, then the Laudemium of 10 per cent shall likewise be rendered thereon.

§. 8. Further, His Most Serene Highness reserves to himself:

That, if He Most High himself, or his successors in possession, should come hither, or should send their commissioners hither, the communities are obliged to furnish the relay-teams for a distance of 4 miles, out and back.

§. 9. Further, His Serene Highness reserves, should he obtain the right of patronage over the church at Betsche, the right of patronage to himself; yet the town and village communities shall have the right to choose the clergyman and to put him forward in nomination, which nominated clergyman His Most Serene Highness promises to present to the episcopal Consistory.

§. 10. The suits between the community of Stocki and the Prince of Hohenlohe concerning the ownership of the Stocki marsh; between the

Prince of Hohenlohe and the possessor of Zielomi[uncertain: pat] concerning the right of redemption; and between the Landrath von Schmaling as possessor of the demesne farm in Głazewo and the Prince of Hohenlohe, His Serene Highness, concerning the confirmation of the privilege; as also the suit between the Prince of Hohenlohe and Hertzig in Lowino concerning the restitution of the [uncertain: Schales tro] - these the communities take over at their own risk and cost, and whatever they may win at law or obtain by way of composition falls to the communities, without His Serene Highness having the least claim thereto.

§. 11. The competence taxes and other public and further dues and burdens hitherto rendered by His Serene Highness as Dominium of Betsche, by whatever name they be called - among which belong also the sick-transport carriages into the Schillner and Punctner Hauland, as also the carriages for the fetching and the departure of the court, and by whatever other name they be called - the said town and village communities take over; and the latter cannot, should these dues be increased in future, demand any compensation from the Prince

of Hohenlohe, His Serene Highness. The communities also take over the contribution of fifty Reichsthaler which His Most Serene Highness promised to render to the town school here for 1 year.

§. 12. Both contracting parties bind themselves to have this contract publicly recorded before the competent authorities and to seek confirmation of it; and His Most Serene Highness herewith makes himself liable to exert himself as best he may that this contract be confirmed by the competent authorities, and that the consent to the further dismemberment of the parcels of land made over to them be granted to the communities.

§. 13. As soon as the tradition of the demesne farm here and of the Obra demesne farm takes place, there shall at the same time be handed over to the town and village communities that livestock and farm inventory which is included in the sale under the purchase and hereditary-tenure sum, in the same number as the tenant von Bornstaet received the same in the year 1800 upon the handing over.

§. 14.

§ 14. This contract shall be binding, for His Most Serene Highness the Prince zu Hohenloh-Ingelfingen for himself and his Most High heirs and successors, as also on the part of the town and village communities, for all time. And the canon or ground-rent of 1000 rttlr to be paid annually shall be rendered in half-yearly instalments of 500 rthlr, namely each time at the term of Christmas and the term of John the Baptist, yearly, sub paena executiones, and sent to Breslau carriage free.

§. 15. So long as the communities do not alienate or dismember the parcels of land and rights made over to them, the Status Passivat remains in its present condition. But as soon as the demesne farm is dismembered, the hereditary purchasers are obliged to have the intabulated items transferred onto their parcels of land and struck out in the name of His Most Serene Highness.

§. 16. For the stipulated canon of the 1000 rthlr yearly all the communities are liable in solidum, and they consent to the entry thereof in the mortgage book against their parcels of land; just as, on the other hand, His Most Serene Highness likewise consents that this present contract be entered in the mortgage book of the Dominium of Betsche and appurtenances.

§. 17. Finally the contracting parties renounce each and every objection, by whatever name they may be called; in particular the town and village communities renounce the objection of injury beyond the half, which was explained to them to this effect:

that if the agreed purchase and hereditary-tenure sum exceeded the true value of the parcels of land and rights made over by twice as much, they would thereby have

the withdrawal from the contract free to them.

§ 18. His Most Serene Highness reserves to himself the superior supervision over the forests, which he will entrust to the Royal Chief Forester Feller; and the communities bind themselves to indemnify him for his pains and to come to agreement with him thereupon.

With that, since nothing further was to be brought forward, the proceedings were closed, the protocol read out and - for the deputies of the town and village communities, who are not fully conversant with the German tongue and who had forbidden the keeping of a separate side-protocol in the Polish language - interpreted by the sworn interpreter Echaust, approved and signed; whereupon it was further noted by the contracting parties that the costs of the taking down,

expedition, public recording and confirmation shall be borne in halves by both parties. Read out, approved and signed. Read myself and approved. Actum Betsche, the 29th of July 1804. Today there appeared

1. the Princely Hohenloh-Ingelfingen General Bevollmächtigter, Baurath Johann Wilhelm Glenk; likewise the special

Bevollmächtigter of the burghership, Matheus Młynkiewicz, and the Bevollmächtigter Chmara of the communities of Kulligowo, Stalluhn, Schartzig, Stocki, Schwiechodezin, Lowin, and they add the following to the contract taken down yesterday.

§. 1. The Prince of Hohenlohe, His Serene Highness, assures the communities that, apart from the mortgage capitals already called in, none shall be called in within the space of half a year.

§. 2. The town and village communities bind themselves, in criminal investigations that may occur, to bear for ever the costs of the investigation, in so far as the remaining communities belonging to the Betsche Schlüssel are not themselves obliged to bear the same.

With that the proceedings were closed, the protocol read out and - for the deputies of the community, who are not fully conversant with the German tongue and who had forbidden the keeping of a protocol in the Polish language - interpreted by the sworn interpreter Echaust, approved, and signed.
