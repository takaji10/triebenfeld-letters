--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Word from Colonel von Zastrow that the King, now tolerably recovered, would be pleased to see the Prince in Berlin for a couple of days; Triebenfeld urges him to come and to raise the question of a stay in the Empire himself, doubting a six-month absence from Silesia would be well received. The estate exchange is to be examined so that neither state nor Prince loses, then confirmed by cabinet order, despite Minister von Voss's furious opposition. Advises silence on the secularisation schemes.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

His Majesty the King wishes that Your Most Serene Highness would take your route by way of Berlin and tarry here a couple of days. Colonel von Zastrow has [text follows] the monarch

asked outright whether Your Serene Highness might take Your Most High tour by way of Dresden, whereupon the King answered him: it would give Him pleasure to see Your Serene Highness here for a couple of days.

Concerning Your Most High sojourn in the Empire I have therefore, out of delicacy, mentioned nothing further, since Your Serene Highness can now touch this chord here in person alone. To speak candidly, I do not know whether it might be looked upon kindly if Your Serene Highness were absent from Silesia for 6 months.

The King is now tolerably restored again, and I hear that they wish to settle the exchange matter before Your Most Serene Highness's arrival. Colonel von Zastrow told me only yesterday – the good monarch has declared

that in consideration of both estates everything should be examined, so that neither the State nor Your Serene Highness lose by it; then the exchange might take place - this is now only to be confirmed by a public cabinet order, and this is also the only thing to be wished for, for then all the rest will settle itself. Minister von Voss has done everything in his power against it and still seethes with fury and vengeance. They have summoned [uncertain: Zimme] and Hell to move the good monarch - or rather B and K - to refuse the exchange outright. Only these two men stood firm as rock, and insisted upon a decision consistent with honour — Concerning the secularisation affairs, my faithful and humble opinions by word of mouth — now my truly honest counsel, that for the present not a word of it be mentioned, for these are, said between us, mouse-traps. Glenck, whom I could not make mobile as promptly as I had thought, in about 24 hours, so that Your Serene Highness may dispose of all the rest - but I await Your Most High Self here – for I shall not depart hence before the Prot. matter is in order – in deepest submission I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Berlin, the [illegible]th of January

1799.

most humble and faithful servant

von Triebenfeld
