--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that the King wishes the Prince to travel via Berlin and stay some days, Colonel v. Zastrow having sounded him out about a route through Dresden; doubts a six-month absence from Silesia would be well received. The King, now recovered, has agreed that both estates be examined so that neither State nor Prince loses, so the exchange may proceed once confirmed by Cabinet Order, despite Minister v. Voss's furious opposition. Advises silence for now on the secularisation business.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Lord! His Majesty the King desires that Your Most Serene Highness take your route by way of Berlin and tarry here a few days. Colonel v. Zastrow asked the Monarch outright whether Your Serene Highness might take Your Most High route by way of Dresden, whereupon the King answered him: it would give him pleasure to see Your Serene Highness here for a few days. Concerning Your Most High sojourn in the Reich I have therefore, out of delicacy, said nothing further, since Your Serene Highness can now touch this chord here in person alone. To speak candidly, I do not know whether it might be looked upon with favour if Your Serene Highness were absent from Silesia for 6 months. The King is now tolerably restored again, and I hear that the exchange affair is to be settled even before Your Most Serene Highness's arrival. Colonel von Zastrow told me only yesterday — the good Monarch had declared

that everything should be examined in respect of both estates concerned, so that neither the State nor Your Serene Highness should be the loser; then the exchange might take place — this is now only to be confirmed by a public Cabinet Order, and this is indeed the only thing to be wished for, for then all the rest will settle itself. Minister v. Voss has mustered everything against it and still seethes with fury and revenge. Heaven and hell have been mustered to move the good Monarchess — or rather B and K — to refuse the exchange outright. Only these two men stood firm as a rock, and insisted upon a decision consistent with honour. — Regarding the secularisation business, my faithful and humble opinions by word of mouth — for now my truly honest counsel is that not a word of it be mentioned for the present, for these are, said in confidence, mousetraps [uncertain: Glenck] which I could not make mobile as promptly as I had thought, in about 24 hours, so that Your Serene Highness may dispose of everything further — but I await Your Most High Self here, for I shall not depart hence any sooner than when the [uncertain: Prot.] affair is in order — In deepest submission I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's, Berlin, the [illegible]th of January 1799. most humble and faithful servant v Triebenfeld
