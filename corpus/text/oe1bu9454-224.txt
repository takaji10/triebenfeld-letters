--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Der Schreiber verhandelt über den Verkauf der Zagórówer Güter an General Schimmelpfennig, der sie für 200/m Rthl erhalten will. Dessen Schwiegersöhne Rittmeister von Kotzow und v. Johnston sollen die Güter mit Lieutenant Sommer besichtigen. Zahlungsplan: Güter Seliz und Modzewo für 110/m, das Gut Goła Wieś, 66000 Rthl in Pfandbriefen al pari, Rest bar oder in schlesischen Pfandbriefen. Der Verkauf an Weigel und Stössel ist laut Rittmeister v Stössel auf Rusinowice gescheitert. Der Adressat soll die Prozesse beenden.

Your Right Honourable,

Unfortunately I have had to keep Your Honour's messenger waiting until today; I have paid him the requisite daily allowance and his wages. I was put off from one day to the next before I obtained closer and somewhat definite information on the matter mentioned to you in my last lengthy letter. Through General Schimmelpfennig I received the news today that, at his advanced age and in the present weather, he could not himself view the Zagórów estates, but that he had resolved to commission his two sons-in-law, the cavalry captain von Kotzow and Mr von Johnston, to do so, and that he therefore wishes to see Lieutenant Sommer with him after the holidays, so that he might make this journey there with them, after having first given them closer information on several matters concerning the estates. What I have now provisionally learned today, with some reliability, of the General's opinion, which here to Your Right Honourable — as to which much may yet be turned or directed otherwise — in the meantime it may be necessary to enter into his ideas and to arrange the counter-calculations accordingly. He, the General, hopes to obtain my estates at 200/m rt, and his plan of payment for it, if it were to go according to his wish, is to be as follows:

1. he would gladly bring his estates Seliz and Modzewo into the bargain for 110/m,

2. then he would like to include the small estate of Goła wieś, which

Mr von Johnson now possesses, and in which he has 6000 rt,

3. further he wishes to have a capital belonging to the General's wife, amounting to 66000 rt, but consisting in West Prussian mortgage bonds, accepted al pari; some 12000 rt he would also mostly [wish?] still in the like papers

and the remainder in cash or in Silesian mortgage bonds; if indeed more should be needed, he thought that at a pinch he could add a further 20000 rt by the provision of good secure mortgages upon Silesian possessions.

It was further intimated that if, contrary to old Schimmelpfennig's expectation, more funds should be required for this requisition, he wishes that this amount might remain entered upon the estates, or else that I should retain for myself, as my own property, the arrears and the claims outstanding in the estates, in the collection of which he would then gladly assist. To Berlin he already dispatched his two sons on Friday a week ago, and to make the maternal capital of the Prussian mortgage bonds liquid. These now stand in Berlin at a rate of 52 1/2 per cent; for the estates he wishes to put forward, according to [uncertain: leading] and reliable statement, some 95000 rtl in ready money would probably be obtainable in a sale; hence, according to the plan he has devised for himself, he would in all, even if he gives 220000 and properly reckons in Courant besides, receive about 170000 rt. If anything is to come of the bargain, he must be convinced with some probability that he gains considerably by it; and since my situation absolutely requires that this prospect be made use of, I trust to Your Right Honourable's prudence that you will make use of these hints of mine and will bring this intended business happily to a conclusion, to my thanks and satisfaction. Lieutenant Sommer I have likewise instructed to that effect, and he will with full vigour, upon

his presence in Sel[u?]ze endeavour to work to the end that General Schimmelpfennig [illegible] and that, as Your Right Honourable, in the speediest manner possible make known. This is what I am able to say upon this matter, and I add only the request that [uncertain: Your Right Honourable] may apply everything so that this prospect that has been opened may soon be realised. My welfare and my woe truly depend upon it. My gratitude towards you will match my friendly sentiments. — One further circumstance, which has arisen since yesterday, makes me wish the more to make use of this prospect, since I now know for certain that the project of selling our estates to Weigel and Stössel is not [uncertain: inclined]. The cavalry captain von Stössel of Rusinowice arrived here yesterday from Berlin by way of Wrocław, where his father gave him a commission to me, and travelled back to Rusinowice today. He had also spoken with Weigel confidentially and at length. He assures me that the latter will not enter into this bargain, that his father, as is quite true, cannot do it alone, and that Rusinowice was to be drawn in seemed to astonish him. In short, for the realisation of this plan there remains not the least appearance. He made me some proposals from his father, which the latter only wishes to repeat, for which purpose he has thought to visit me about the 6th of January. Perhaps some way out may yet be found for his reassurance. It would be useless to say anything further about this plan, which I must regard as wholly failed, than that it is for that reason the more necessary to pursue the threads taken up with Schimmelpfennig. Since help is indispensably necessary to me, I conjure you to exert everything there to bring the lawsuits to an end

whereby funds may soon come to hand. I have sent an express courier to Öhringen to press for my son's coming here. It must have arrived there today, and I hope soon to receive an answer thereupon after the [uncertain: present] year, or else to see them here soon myself. In several respects I now find myself sorely harassed, and I have my hands quite full in order to avert several evils. [uncertain: Exert] yourself now, my better friend, to everything that can further our intended business of the estate sale and bring it quickly to a conclusion. Lieutenant [Sommer], when he comes, will report everything to you faithfully and will certainly not be wanting in his exertions. I hope, if all goes happily, to see you again in good cheer, and I look forward to your [text lost] do not leave me without news, especially when you have anything serviceable to report. Your intended journey to Nysa will probably not have taken place, since I heard nothing further about it. The other matter, upon which you wished to make disclosure to me by word of mouth, lies very much at my heart, and I could wish to receive from you at least some provisional hints upon it. To Lieutenant Sommer I further charge to lay before you certain matters which it might perhaps be necessary to put in order before the arrival of the Johnston sons-in-law, and I most submissively beg that you will be pleased to give favourable consideration to this.

Be pleased to remain ever assured of my true attachment and of the assurance of that esteem [text lost]
