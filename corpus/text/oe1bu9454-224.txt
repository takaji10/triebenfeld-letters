--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports at length on General Schimmelpfennig's terms for acquiring the Zagorow estates at 23000: he would bring in Seliz and Modkewo for 110000, Johnston's Goła wies (6000 Rthl), and 66000 Rthl of East Prussian mortgage bonds al pari, with further sums in cash or Silesian bonds; his sons-in-law Rittmeister von Kotzow and v. Johnston are to inspect after the holidays with Lieut. Sommer. The rival sale to Weigel and Stössel has collapsed, as Rittmeister v. Stössel of Ruzynowice confirms. Urges speedy conclusion of the lawsuits, money being indispensable, and mentions an express courier to Öhringen summoning his son.

Your Right Honourable.

Unfortunately I have had to keep Your Right Honourable's [uncertain: dead man] waiting until today, to whom I have paid the requisite daily allowance and his wages. I was put off from one day to the next before I obtained closer and somewhat definite information concerning the subject mentioned to the same in my last lengthy letter. Through General Schimmelpfennig I received today the news that he could not himself, at his advanced age and in the present weather, [uncertain: inspect] the Zagorowo estates, but had resolved to commission his two sons-in-law, the cavalry captain von Kotzow and Mr. von Johnston, to do so, and for that reason wishes to see Lieutenant Sommer with him after the holidays, so that he might make this journey with them thither after being given closer information beforehand about several matters concerning the estates. What I have now provisionally learned today with some reliance concerning the General's opinion, which I here to Your Right Honourable, about which much may yet turn or be directed otherwise, in the meantime it might be necessary to enter into his ideas and to govern oneself accordingly in the counter-accounts. He, the General, hopes to obtain my estates at 23000 v/d, and his plan of payment for it, if things were to go according to his wish, is to be the following:

1. he would gladly bring his estates Seliz and Modkewo into the transaction for 110000,

2. then he would wish to include the small estate Goła wies, which at present

Mr. von Johnston possesses, and in which he has 6000 Rthl,

3. then he wishes a capital belonging to the General's lady, which amounts to 66000 Rthl, but which consists in East Prussian mortgage bonds, to be accepted at par; to the amount of 12000 Rthl he would likewise mostly in such papers still [uncertain: wollen]

and the remainder in cash or in Silesian mortgage bonds; if indeed more should be needed, he thought he could at all events add a further 20000 Rthl by the grant of good secure mortgages upon Silesian possessions.

In this connexion it was further intimated that if, contrary to old Schimmelpfennig's expectation, more funds should be required for this requisition, he would wish that this quantum might remain entered upon the estates, or that I should retain as my property the arrears and the debts outstanding in the estates, toward the recovery of which he would then gladly co-operate. To Berlin he already dispatched his two sons on Friday a week ago, also to make liquid the maternal capital of the Prussian mortgage bonds. These now stand in Berlin at the rate of 52 1/2 per cent; for the estates which he wishes to be brought in, there would according to [uncertain: führen] and reliable statement well be 95000 Rthl in cash money to be obtained in a sale; hence, according to the plan he has devised, he would in all, even if he gives 220000 and reckons it properly still in Courant, receive 170000 Rthl. If anything is to come of the transaction, he must be convinced with probability that he gains considerably by it; and since my situation absolutely requires that this prospect be made use of, I trust to Your Right Honourable's prudence that you will make use of this my [uncertain: Wicke], and to my thanks and satisfaction bring this intended business happily to a conclusion. Lieutenant Sommer I have likewise instructed to that effect, and he will with full vigour, at

his presence in Seloze endeavour to work to the end that General Schimmelpfennig [uncertain: Cathen] and that, as Your Right Honourable, in the most expeditious manner possible. This is what I am able to say on this subject, and I add only the request that you may apply every means that this prospect now opened may soon be realised. My weal and woe truly rest upon it. My gratitude towards you will match my friendly sentiments. Yet one more circumstance, which has arisen since yesterday, makes me wish all the more for the use of this prospect, since I now know for certain that the project for the sale of our estates to Weigel and Stössel has not [uncertain: geleigt]. The cavalry captain von Stössel of Ruzynowice arrived here yesterday from Berlin by way of Breslau, where his father gave him commissions to me, and travelled back to Ruzynowice today. He had also spoken confidentially and at length with Weigel. He assures me that the latter would not enter into this transaction, that his father, as is quite true, could not do it alone, and that Ruzynowice was to be brought into it seemed to astonish him. In short, for the realisation of this plan there remains not the least appearance. He made me several proposals from his father, which the latter wishes only to repeat, for which purpose he has thought to visit me about the 6th of January. Perhaps some way out may yet be found for his reassurance. It would be useless to say anything further about this plan, which I must regard as wholly failed, than that it is on that account all the more necessary to pursue the paths entered upon with Schimmelpfennig. Since help is indispensably necessary to me, I conjure you to exert everything there to bring the lawsuits to an end

whereby funds may soon come to hand. I have sent an express courier to Öhringen to urge on my son's coming. He must have arrived there today, and I hope soon after the new year to receive an answer thereupon, or to see them here myself soon. In several respects I now find myself much harassed, and I have my hands full to avert several evils. Prepare yourself, my best friend, now for everything that can advance our intended business of the estate transaction and lead it swiftly to conclusion. Lieutenant [text lost] will report everything to you faithfully and will certainly not be wanting in his exertions. I hope, if all goes happily, to see you again with joy, and I rejoice in your [illegible]. Do not leave me without news, especially when you have anything pertinent to report. Your intended journey to Neisse will probably not have taken place, since I heard nothing further about it. The other matter, upon which you wished to make disclosure to me by word of mouth, lies very much at my heart, and I could wish to receive from the same only some preliminary hints upon it. To Lieutenant Sommer I further commit the laying to heart of certain matters which might perhaps be necessary to be set in order before the arrival of the Johnston sons-in-law, and I most submissively beg that you will be pleased to take favourable regard thereof.

May you be pleased to be ever assured of my true attachment and the assurance of that esteem [text lost]
