Gnädigſter Fürſt und Herr!
Herr Weigel ist, nachdem ich ihm zuförderst garantie geleistet, eifrig bemüht die
Sache völlig zu Applaniren. Ehe nun aber nicht von dem v. Grotowski jura cessa
gegeben worden, wird die Banque nicht discontinu¬

und ihm die Versicherung geben solle, daß die 30000 Rthl und als auch die Zinsen
bey ihm lägen. Daß aber H. v. Grotowski ehe die Gelder gezahlt werden könten,
das Cessions Instrument über die 30000 und dorten eiligst gerichtlich ausstellen müße,
nach welchen den die Gelder sogleich an ihm abgeführt werden solle.
Um hier nun nichts zu verfehlen, was zur schnellsten herbeiführung der Sache
Dienlich ist bin ich zum Hoffrath Ebersbach gefahren, und habe diesen das Instru-
ment, wie es o Grotowski ausstellen muß entwerfen laßen, welches Herr Weigel
in beigehenden Briefe von dem v. Grotowski, zur schleunigen Gerichtlichen Vollziehung
entsendet.
Ewr Hochfürstliche Durchlaucht eile ich dies Schreiben anliegend derstest durch der

gleich an Hln. v. Grotowski pr. Expressen zu schicken damit er das Cessions Instru¬

der schnelle Todesfall des redlichen Hauptman v Müllern hatt mich tief erſchüttert

Er war würklich ein guter Mensch und ein wahrhaft treuer Anhänger
von Ewr Hochfürstliche Durchlaucht.
Geruhen aber Ewr Durchlaucht diesen schnellen Todt zu beherzigen - denken
Sie bey diesen kritischen Zeiten auf Höchst Ihre Erhaltung, und stürmen

daß der Siderung Smith Vliesingen und auch die dort gewesene kleine

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports progress with Herr Weigel on the 30000 Rthl: the bank will not discount until v. Grotowski grants the jura cessa, so Court Councillor Ebersbach has drafted the instrument of cession for Grotowski to execute in court, and the Prince is asked to forward Weigel's enclosed letter by express. Urges Grotowski also to write to his mandatary at Kalisz that no subhastation is now in question. Notes the deaths of Captain v. Müllern and Morgenbeßer, urges care for the Prince's health, relays Nelson's action off Toulon and Sidney Smith at Vliesingen, and Bernhardi's letter to M. Stösseln.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! Herr Weigel, after I had first of all furnished him with a guarantee, is zealously endeavouring to smooth the matter over entirely. But until the jura cessa have been given by v. Grotowski, the bank will not discount — I have therefore [uncertain: urged] Herr Weigel that he should write at once to H. v. Grotowski and give him the assurance that the 30000 Rthl and likewise the interest are lying ready with him; but that, before the monies can be paid, H. v. Grotowski must most speedily execute in court the instrument of cession for the 30000 and there, after which the monies shall be remitted to him at once. Now, in order that nothing be neglected here which may serve to bring the matter about most swiftly, I have driven to Court Councillor Ebersbach and have had him draft the instrument as v. Grotowski must execute it, which Herr Weigel dispatches in the accompanying letter from v. Grotowski, for speedy execution in court. I hasten to convey this letter, enclosed [uncertain: herewith], to Your Most Serene Highness by the orderly just now departing. May Your Most Serene Highness be pleased only to send it on at once to Herr v. Grotowski by express, so that he may execute the instrument of cession, whereupon I shall then finalise the matter forthwith. The sudden death of the honest Captain v. Müllern has shaken me deeply.

He was truly a good man and a genuinely faithful adherent of Your Most Serene Highness. But may Your Serene Highness be pleased to take this sudden death to heart — think, in these critical times, upon Your own most high preservation, and do not press so hard upon Your health [text lost] — such rapid marches make even the most iron-firm man falter. — Morgenbeßer too died yesterday evening at 7 o'clock. — Now, Most gracious Prince and Lord, I appeal to Your heart. That, for the rest, the gallant Nelson caught the French as they meant to slip away from Toulon and unite with the Brest fleet, took ships of the line of 74 cannon along with many boats, and sank several, Your Most Serene Highness will surely know already. In Vienna this news has made a quite extraordinary sensation; the people cheered aloud, and it has everywhere been let to be seen all too plainly that the Court and the nation take England's part. That Sidney Smith burns Vliesingen and also the small fleet that was there is indeed reported from Vienna; meanwhile it is said withal that the official notices on this are still lacking. With Bernhardi I have arranged everything according to Your Most Serene Highness's will — he wrote by yesterday's post to M. Stösseln, and is honestly endeavouring from now on to lead the water onto the [uncertain: mill-race], so that the departments are set in motion, whereby the good cause will be quickly brought to its goal. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's Breslau, 29. Febr. 1804. faithful and most obedient servant v Triebenfeld

P. S. I have further [uncertain: prevailed upon] Weigel — that he must approach Grotowski, so that Grotowski at once sends along a letter to his mandatary at Kalisz — in which he informs him that he is satisfied, and that the latter is to notify this immediately to the Government and to tell it that there is now no further question of any subhastation. If Grotowski sends this letter along with the other, I shall dispatch it at once by express to Kalisz, so that everything is [uncertain: settled] before the 22nd of this month. In order to convince Your Most Serene Highness that everything has been arranged in the best way, Weigel's letter follows under flying seal. May Your Most High Self be pleased to [uncertain: close] it and to send it in haste to Grotowski, so that he may quickly execute the cession and send the instrument here. Until Weigel gave this letter and engaged himself therein that the monies already lay ready with him, I did not hand over the money — only now do I let it be conveyed to him.
