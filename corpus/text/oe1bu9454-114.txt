--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A short, badly preserved and partly illegible note, apparently to Triebenfeld rather than from him. The writer abuses an unnamed opponent who has caused the Fürst much difficulty and, now that nothing further can be extracted, wishes to settle; he hopes for a quick conclusion, mentions a greeting from v. Schlabrendorff, reports his own affair with "L. Asch" as neither good nor bad, promises documents and declarations within days, and notes his health improving.

Your Right Honourable, Most gracious Lord Councillor of War! — he is indeed a rhymeless [uncertain: rascal]! — He has made His Serene Highness a great deal of difficulty in order now to arrive at the desired end, and now that he [unparseable: rihm mag] that there is nothing more to be squeezed out, now he wants to conclude the whole matter. — Would that Heaven granted but once a favourable moment, such as the present one is; the fellows should get the snow-distress, who now [unparseable: den geüdigete würde], so press. But if it were now soon settled, so that Your Right Honourable should not be aggrieved any longer, and then came into the [uncertain: Jusend], on account of the greeting of v. Schlabrendorff too the strings would be strung in the manner which Your Right Honourable has already often had the graciousness to express. With my own affair matters do not stand well, but neither do they stand ill. — The L. Asch [uncertain: jen] means to [unparseable: zullen],

but it shall not succeed for him, and therefore I have given notice in [unparseable: bring], whence I still expect back this day the [uncertain: Bethe], together with the declaration. Within a few days it must be [unparseable: alleinl.] done, — upon that Your Right Honourable may certainly rely, and likewise that which you [uncertain: in großen Wechseln — in large bills of exchange], and that you will soon get the [uncertain: Jaunken] back; the [uncertain: Schöpfe] fetched again on Tuesday, at the latest Wednesday; — until then I most humbly beg that they may still be left standing. With my health it grows better hourly, — tomorrow I shall go out. For the gracious sympathy I give most humble thanks; I commend myself to your continuing grace, and have the honour to sign myself, with high esteem, as Your Right Honourable's Berlin[?], the 2nd of May 1802. humble and obedient servant Frig[?]
