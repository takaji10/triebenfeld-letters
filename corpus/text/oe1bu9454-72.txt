--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Written to Triebenfeld by his employer, who has waited in vain for the promised visit with Herr v. Brzechsta, assumes the leasing project with him has collapsed, and reports that he abandoned the exchange scheme with General v. Schimmelpfennig accordingly. Pressed by creditors and by arrangements made with his son and sons-in-law, he has admitted a new exchange project and allowed inspection of the Zagorow estates, encloses the original deed of donation concerning his claims on the Trappyner estates, asks for news of Brzechsta's intercession in his Warsaw suit, and urgently requests 300 Rthl for Lieutenant Sommer.

In consequence of the reports which Your Right Honourable was last so kind as to communicate to me, I have, ever since receiving them, looked forward from week to week — I might almost say from day to day — to Your Right Honourable's so ardently wished-for arrival together with Mr. von Brzechsta, and for a long time counted upon it with such certainty that on that account I have given up the whole exchange project with

General von [uncertain: lohimmelfennig] in accordance with Your Right Honourable's expressed will. I am persuaded that only the weightiest grounds can have held Your Right Honourable back from your intention, just as, in view of Your Right Honourable's complete silence concerning the matter, I can believe nothing other than that the leasing project with Mr. von Brzechsta — which Your Right Honourable assured me had been concluded on condition of my subsequent approval — must through supervening circumstances have fallen through again. These conjectures, which force themselves upon me, together with the need, ever more pressing with me, of at last coming to a decisive resolution regarding my Polish possessions, are what have moved me to give a hearing to an exchange project only lately proposed anew, and to grant my permission for the inspection of the Zagorowo estates.
