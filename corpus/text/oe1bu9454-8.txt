--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A desperate appeal, written on receipt of three letters and news of his destitute children, for the Prince to send Lüder 120 Rthl at once and 60 Rthl monthly while Triebenfeld remains away; he says he lives on borrowed money and sacrifices his family for the Prince's business. He also asks for written assurance from the Curz attorney that the matter will be settled at 112,000, and reports having had von Kirchen treated as an interested party.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
The moment I sat down to write to Your Serene Highness I received all at once 3 letters – one of the 8th, one of the 25th and the 27th of November. – The enclosed, which brought [uncertain: to be placed under guardianship] my poor children, nearly drove me to madness, and it is with a trembling hand that I write – Have mercy and send the Lüder 120 Rthl, and if I remain here yet a long while, again 60 Rthl per month – the [uncertain: keepers] must [uncertain: to hang] – I sacrifice myself here for you, I live solely on borrowed money, I put my own welfare and my children's out of sight for your sake, and you are so unmerciful as to let the children suffer the utmost want in spite of all my entreaties – Your affair now stands heavenly well, even those can now already have credit, so do not let my children go hungry there – Such [illegible] are only the laments of the abandoned children in my heart – Sinews and limbs would fail me if I must leave the cry of misery, and yet I am to work for you, my Prince, to work. Now one thing more — would it then not be possible to obtain from the Curz attorney a small written assurance that the matter is to be settled with 112000 dH. The Prince believes me, but we have powerful enemies – One sweats mightily these weeks over what has been done against the aforesaid opinion, and had I not, through the aforesaid B. – let it be known in the proper quarter that the said [uncertain: z. M.] von Kirchen is himself to be regarded as a party, because he parades in the matter as chief guardian, then everything would have been sent to Becke. Hahn will be very glad; I shall write to him myself. Only do you provide, most gracious Lord, for my children there – I shall provide well enough here. Papers I do not need here at present, for they are only needed there. Apart from a few words from the Courlandish attorney
