--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Contract of sale by which Friedrich Ludwig, Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, conveys the South Prussian estates Kollno and Kaemen (Kamionna), granted him by Friedrich Wilhelm II in 1796, to Royal Banco-Director George Conrad Leixner for 142,000 Reichsthaler, already paid (53,139 rthl to von Broniewsky, 88,860 rthl in cash); sold in Pausch und Bogen, with church capitals of 100 Floren and 250 rthl assumed, warranties on survey and dues, the disputed Krobilaw timber right, and the cancelled 100,000 rthl von Triebenfeld mortgage. Signed for Leixner by Meyer Bernhard, with notarial attestations at Breslau and Frankfurt an der Oder.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Between the Most Serene Prince and Lord, Lord Friedrich Ludewig, Reigning Prince [Fürster] of Hohenlohe Ingelfingen, Royal General of Infantry and Governor at Breslau, and the Royal Banco-Director at Frankfurth an der Oder, Herr George Conrad Leixner, the following contract of sale has been concluded:

§. 1. His Most Serene Highness sells to Herr Banco Director Leixner the estates Kollno and Kaemen or Kamionna, situate in South Prussia in the Pösen Department and its Me sevitz district, acquired in full property by virtue of the deed of donation of His Majesty the late King Friedrich Wilhelm II of the 9th of August 1796, together with all appurtenances, mills, inhabitants, fields, woodlands, lakes, waters, services, hunting, propination rights, and generally with all rights and prerogatives, burdens and encumbrances, as his true property, for a purchase price of 142,000 rthl., one hundred and forty-two thousand Reichsthaler, according to the coinage standard of 1764.

§. 2. The purchase money of the 142000 rthl has already been fully paid.

His Most Serene Highness gives receipt therefor, and acknowledges generally that from this transaction of sale He has no further demand against the purchaser.

It is noted here historically that the payment of the purchase moneys took place in the following manner:

A. 53139 Rthl 13 g 8/5 d. the purchaser has paid to the former Polish Rittmeister von Broniewsky, who as an immitted creditor was in natural possession of the estates, as the sum adjudged to him at law, upon the instruction [Anweisung] of His Most Serene Highness.

b, 88860 rttl 10 g. 3 1/5 d. have been paid in cash by the purchaser to His Most Serene Highness Himself and upon His most high instructions [Anweisungen].

i. e. 142,000 Rthl

§. 3. Besides the purchase price the purchaser assumes the following items entered in the mortgage books upon Kollno and Kaemen:

a., a capital of 100 Floren, one hundred Polish Floren, for the church at Kaemen, the interest whereon is fixed at four Polish Mark yearly.

b., for the same church a capital of

250. rthl., two hundred and fifty Reichsthaler, or 1500, fifteen hundred Polish Floren, which bear interest yearly at 3 1/2, three and one half per cent. The interest of these two items the purchaser assumes from Midsummer [Johannis] 1804.

§. 4. The present sale is made without valuation,

in Pausch und Bogen.

His Most Serene Highness however gives warranty for this:

a., that the surface content of arable, meadows, heaths and lakes set out in the survey registers, which together with the maps have been extradited to the purchaser, is really present. His Most Serene Highness herewith assures that to Him no claim by anyone whatsoever against the parcels of ground set out in these survey registers has hitherto been known. The obligation of His Most Serene Highness to answer for the correctness of the survey register in the above manner is limited to three years.

b., that in public dues no more is charged upon these estates than yearly

for Kaemen.

153 rt. 22 g. 8 pf.

for Kolno

125. rt. 21. g. 2. pf. and in ecclesiastical

dues for

Kaemen

16 rt. 16 pf.

§. 5. The natural delivery of the estates to the purchaser has already taken place in the month of September 1804, and the judicial proceedings taken down thereupon shall stand as they are.

With a view to the warranty the following principles have been

adopted between the contracting gentlemen:

a., that the usufruct of the estates from Midsummer [Johannis] 1804 is due to the purchaser, from which time the purchaser has also to bear the burdens.

Any older arrears are to be answered for by His Most Serene Highness.

His Most Serene Highness also bears the salary of the Justitiariat up to Michaelmas of last year.

b., that the estates shall be delivered to the purchaser as they stood and lay on the 15th of June of last year.

c., that the purchaser may however demand only such stock, in field cattle and farming inventory, as von Broniewsky received upon his immission in the year 1786, according to the instrument handed over to the purchaser in the German language, and was bound to render back.

§. 6. Besides the church capitals noted in §. 2., there stands entered upon the estates a mortgage capital of 100,000 rthl., one hundred thousand Reichsthaler, arising from a bond of indebtedness of His Most Serene Highness of the 1st of June 1804 in favour of the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, who has ceded this mortgage to the purchaser. This debt, however, has already been fully discharged,

and the purchaser herewith expressly renounces all claims on that account, both against His Most Serene Highness and against the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld. It is likewise solely the affair of the purchaser to procure the cancellation of this nexus in the land and mortgage books. The costs of cancellation, however, shall be borne one half by His Most Serene Highness and one half by the purchaser.

§. 7. The costs of the drawing up and confirmation of this sale, of the natural and civil tradition, and generally all costs of the sale by whatever name, shall likewise be borne by both contracting gentlemen in halves.

§. 8. Passive servitudes are not known to the vendor's Serene Highness. On the other hand, there belongs to these estates a right of timber in the Krobilaw woods. In a possessory suit against von Kwilecki this was adjudged to His Serene Highness, but in petitorio it was disallowed in the first instance.

The purchaser assumes this rem litigiosam without demanding any warranty from the vendor's Serene Highness.

§. 9. All documents and files belonging to the estates the purchaser's Serene Highness promises, so far as it has not yet been done,

faithfully to make over to the purchaser; likewise also to have those delivered to the purchaser which are still for the time being required in the accounting suit of His Serene Highness against von Broniewsky, after the conclusion of that suit. Yet it remains free to the purchaser, as goes without saying, to have those papers not yet extradited inspected as often as he finds it necessary.

§. 10. The contracting gentlemen bind themselves to hold fast to this contract in all points and clauses, renounce all objections thereto, especially the objection of laesio enormis [Verlegung über die Hälfte] and whatever others they might be called, and have executed this instrument by their own hand-written signatures and appended seals.

Breslau, the 16th of January 1805.

Read and approved by myself. Friedrich Ludwig Prince of Hohenlohe, as having read and approved this instrument.

Meyer Bernhard, in the name and subject to the approval of Herr Banco Director Leixner.
