--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A purchase contract selling the Kolno and Kaemen (Kamionna) estates in the Meseritz district, granted to Friedrich Ludwig, Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, by Friedrich Wilhelm II in 1796, to the Frankfurt an der Oder bank director George Conrad Leixner for 142,000 Rthl, already paid: 53,139 Rthl to the creditor in possession von Broniewsky and 88,860 Rthl in cash. Sold without valuation; the buyer takes over two church capitals, clears a 100,000 Rthl mortgage, and assumes a disputed timber right in the Krobilaw forests against von Kwilecki.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Between the Most Serene Prince and Lord, Lord Friedrich Ludewig, Reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, Royal General of Infantry and Governor at Breslau, and the Royal bank director at Frankfurt an der Oder, Mr. George Conrad Leixner, the following purchase contract has been concluded:

§ 1. His Most Serene Highness sells to the bank director Mr. Leixner the estates of Kollno and Kaemen or Kamionna, situated in South Prussia in the Department of Posen and in the Meseritz district thereof, acquired in full ownership by virtue of the deed of donation of His Majesty the late King Friedrich Wilhelm II of 9 August 1796, with all appurtenances, mills, residents, fields, forests, lakes, waters, services, hunting, propination rights, and in general with all rights and privileges, burdens and encumbrances, as his true property, for a purchase price of 142,000 Rthl, one hundred and forty-two thousand Reichsthaler, according to the coinage standard of 1764.

§ 2. The purchase money of the 142000 Rthl has already been paid in full.

His Most Serene Highness gives receipt therefor, and acknowledges in general that He has no further claim upon the purchaser arising out of this transaction of sale.

It is noted here as a matter of record that the payment of the purchase moneys was effected in the following manner:

A. 53139 Rthl 13 g 8/5 d the purchaser has paid, upon the instruction of His Most Serene Highness, to the former Polish cavalry captain von Broniewsky, who, as a creditor placed in possession, was in actual physical possession of the estates, as the sum legally adjudged to him.

b, 88860 Rthl 10 g. 3 1/5 d have been paid in cash by the purchaser to His Most Serene Highness Himself and upon His most high instructions.

i. e. 142,000 Rthl

§ 3. Besides the purchase price, the purchaser assumes the following items entered in the mortgage books upon Kollno and Kaemen:

a., a capital of 100 florins, one hundred Polish florins, for the church at Kaemen, the interest whereon is fixed at four Polish marks yearly.

b., for that same church a capital of

250 Rthl, two hundred and fifty Reichsthaler, or 1500, fifteen hundred Polish florins, which bear interest at 3 1/2, three and one half per cent yearly. The interest on these two items the purchaser assumes from Midsummer 1804.

§ 4. The present sale is made without valuation,

in Pausch und Bogen.

However, His Most Serene Highness gives warranty for the following:

a., that the surface content of arable, meadows, heaths and lakes set out in the survey registers, which together with the maps have been delivered over to the purchaser, is actually present. His Most Serene Highness assures herewith that no claim by anyone whatsoever to the parcels set out in these survey registers has hitherto been known to Him. The obligation of His Most Serene Highness to answer for the correctness of the survey register in the manner above is limited to three years.

b., that no more is charged upon these estates in public dues than yearly

for Kaemen

153 Rthl 22 g. 8 pf.

for Kolno

125 Rthl 21 g. 2 pf. and in ecclesiastical

dues for

Kaemen

16 Rthl 16 pf.

§ 5. The physical delivery of possession of the estates to the purchaser has already taken place in the month of September 1804, and the judicial proceedings recorded thereupon shall stand as they are.

With regard to the warranty, the following principles have been

adopted between the contracting parties.

a., that the usufruct of the estates is due to the purchaser from Midsummer 1804, from which time the purchaser also has to bear the burdens.

Any older arrears are to be answered for by His Most Serene Highness.

His Most Serene Highness likewise bears the justiciary's salary up to Michaelmas of last year.

b., that the estates shall be handed over to the purchaser as they stood and lay on the 15th of June of last year.

c., that, however, the purchaser may demand only that appurtenant stock, in field, cattle and farm inventory, which von Broniewsky received at his being placed in possession in the year 1786, according to the instrument handed over to the purchaser in the German language, and which he was obliged to render back.

§ 6. Besides the church capitals noted in § 3, there stands upon the estates a mortgage capital of 100,000 Rthl, one hundred thousand Reichsthaler, entered by virtue of a bond of indebtedness of His Most Serene Highness of 1 June 1804 in favour of the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, who has ceded this mortgage to the purchaser. This debt, however, has already been fully settled,

and the purchaser herewith expressly renounces all claims in that regard, both against His Most Serene Highness and against the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld. It is likewise solely the purchaser's own affair to procure the cancellation of this encumbrance in the land and mortgage books. The costs of cancellation, however, shall be borne one half by His Most Serene Highness and one half by the purchaser.

§ 7. The costs of the drawing-up and confirmation of this sale, of the physical and civil delivery of possession, and in general all costs of the sale by whatever name, shall likewise be borne in halves by both contracting parties.

§ 8. Passive servitudes are not known to His Serene Highness the seller. On the other hand, there belongs to these estates a right of timber-taking in the Krobilaw forests. In a possessory suit against von Kwilecki this was adjudged to His Serene Highness, but in petitorio it was denied Him in the first instance.

The purchaser assumes this rem litigiosam without demanding any warranty from His Serene Highness the seller.

§ 9. His Serene Highness the seller promises faithfully to deliver over to the purchaser all documents and files belonging to the estates, so far as it has not yet been done,

and likewise to have those delivered to the purchaser which are still at present required in His Serene Highness's accounting suit against von Broniewsky, after the conclusion of that suit. It shall, however, as goes without saying, also be free to the purchaser to have these papers not yet delivered over inspected as often as he finds it necessary.

§ 10. The contracting parties bind themselves to hold fast to this contract in all its points and clauses, renounce all objections against it, particularly the objection of injury beyond the half and whatever others they might bear the name of, and have executed this instrument by their own-hand signatures of their names and the seals appended.
