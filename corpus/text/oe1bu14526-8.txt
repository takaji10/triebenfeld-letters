--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Hereditary-lease contract by which Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, general attorney of the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, grants Christian Friedenberger of Szymanowicer Hauland one Magdeburg Hufe in the Trąbczyn forest for 100 Rthl entry-money and 30 Rthl annual rent, half redeemable, with three free years, tavern and milling compulsion, Laudemium of the tenth Groschen, and no remission for misfortune. Filed with it: Triebenfeld's general power of attorney of 1805 and the Kalisz Chamber's 1806 consent to dismembering Zagorowo and Trąbczyn.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
The Princely Hohenlohe Patrimonial Court hereby gives to be known that between the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld as general attorney of His Serene Highness as seller of the one part, and the resident Christian Friedenberger as purchaser of the other part, the following hereditary-lease contract, over one Magdeburg Hufe of land situated in the Trąbczyn forest, has been concluded and judicially declared before us.

Actum Trąbczyn, the 25th of March 1806. There appeared the persons well known to the undersigned justiciary, namely

1., the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld as general attorney of His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, hereditary lord and lord of the court of this place, legitimated by the enclosed power of attorney, of the one part, and

2., the resident Christian Friedenberger of Szymanowicer Hauland, of the other part.

The appearers find themselves in the condition to execute judicial acts validly, and declare freely and de-

liberately and with due deliberation the following hereditary-lease contract to the protocol.

§. I. His Most Serene Highness namely grants in fee to Christian Friedenberger one Hufe, in words One Magdeburg Hufe, reckoned at thirty Morgen to the Hufe and each Morgen at one hundred and eighty square rods, situated in the Trąbczyn forest, from now on for perpetual times

in hereditary lease.

§. II. Christian Friedenberger pays for the one Magdeburg Hufe 100 rt., in words One hundred Reichsthaler, as entry-money, and since this entry-money is already fully paid, Christian Friedenberger is hereby receipted for the same without further reservation.

§. III. Christian Friedenberger

hereby binds himself, for himself and his successors in possession of this holding, to pay from this one Hufe an annual hereditary canon of thirty Reichsthaler, in words thirty Reichsthaler, to the manorial lordship (Dominium) of this place; and this hereditary rent shall be discharged yearly in two instalments, namely one half at Christmas and the other half at Midsummer, each time in cash and in Courant, to the lordship's treasury.

§. IV. For the wholly uncultivated arable the hereditary lessee receives three free years, which begin from Martinmas of the past year and end at Midsummer 1809, so that the first half-yearly hereditary rent must be paid at Christmas 1809 with 15 rt., in words fifteen Reichsthaler

Reichsthaler must be paid.

That land which at the measuring-out is handed over to Christian Friedenberger as already under cultivation, be it much or little, shall bear rent from Christmas of this year, inasmuch as the free years extend only to that part which must first be brought under cultivation.

§. V. It stands free to the hereditary lessee to redeem the fixed hereditary rent of the thirty [uncertain: Reichsthaler] up to one half, that is to say, to pay off to the ground lordship, according to his means, one, two, up to three hundred [illegible] of capital, whereupon for every hundred Reichsthaler five Reichsthaler shall be

taken off the hereditary rent yearly; the capital so paid off remains the property of the ground lordship, and this redemption of the hereditary rent takes place only in respect of the first half; the second half, at fifteen Reichsthaler per Hufe, on the other hand remains irredeemable and can never be raised or diminished.

§. VI. Besides the [uncertain: aforesaid] hereditary rent, the hereditary lessee takes upon himself, for the present and for future times perpetually, to discharge and render all royal and other public dues whatsoever falling upon this one Hufe, consist in what they may, without any exception and without deduction from the hereditary rent or other reimbursement, and to

render.

§. VII. The hereditary lessee must [uncertain: build] at his own cost, without receiving for this purpose, either now or in the future, timber or other building assistance; nevertheless all the timber standing upon his Hufe belongs to him in property, without exception.

Likewise the hereditary lessee and his successors in possession of this holding must effect all future repairs and new buildings out of their own means, without subsidy or assistance.

§. VIII. Outside his own possession the hereditary lessee has no right to firewood or to [uncertain: pasture] in the lordship's forests and fields.

§. IX. His drink the hereditary lessee must always take from

the lordship's brewery and distillery or from the tavern; if foreign drink be brought in by him or by his people, the hereditary lessee incurs each time a penalty of 5 rt. for every barrel of beer and one Reichsthaler for every garniec of brandy, which penalty is paid to the ground lordship.

§. X. The hereditary lessee is subject to the milling compulsion and may grind his corn only where the estate lordship directs him. §. XI.

From all and every manorial service and deliveries in kind, without distinction, the hereditary lessee is for ever wholly exempt;

he nevertheless remains subject to the jurisdiction of the lordship of this place.

§. XII.

The hereditary lessee and his successors in possession are entitled to alienate his holding to another in any manner whatsoever; but on every sale or exchange the tenth Groschen of the purchase or exchange money must be paid to the ground lordship as a transfer fee (Laudemium), and its consent must be obtained to every alienation, inasmuch as the ground lordship hereby reserves to itself once and for all the right of pre-emption.

§. XIII. The hereditary lessee renounces, for himself and his successors in possession, all and every remission upon mis-

fortunes, whether these arise through fire from heaven or by the incendiarism of evil persons, hailstorm, crop failure, cattle plague, flood, war and pestilence, or in whatsoever manner it may be, for the present and for the future entirely, and upon such cases occurring neither compensation nor abatement of the hereditary rent shall take place.

§. XIV. All the costs attending this contract the hereditary lessee takes upon himself to discharge.

§. XV. His Most Serene Highness consents that the hereditary lessee may, upon the ground of this contract, have his title of possession settled for himself.

§. XVI. So long as the hereditary lessee and his successors in possession shall fulfil all the obligations contained in this contract, His Most Serene Highness promises to maintain them in their rights and to defend them against any man's real claim.

Should, however, the hereditary lessee or his successors remain in arrears with the annual hereditary rent for longer than two years, or else lay waste this holding and thereby diminish the revenues of the ground lordship, then the ground lordship shall be entitled to dispose of this holding otherwise, and the hereditary lessee forfeits his whole

right and the entry-money paid in.

Both parties promise to keep this hereditary-lease contract firmly and inviolably; being judicially instructed as to the consequences, they renounce all evasions available against it, in particular the objection of an alleged lesion above and below the half, of cunning persuasion, fear, compulsion, of matters otherwise agreed than written down or not rightly understood; they moved that this contract be judicially engrossed, and have, after previous reading aloud word for word, in token of their approval,

signed and subscribed the same below in their own hands.

von Triebenfeld, Your Right Honourable, of Schweinern etc., to conduct in my name all and every affair touching the estates of Zagorowo, Witów and Trąbczyn belonging to me and situated in South Prussia in the Kalisz department, whether they concern a dismemberment, or purchase and sale, leasing or pledging, or by whatever name they may be called and whatever bearing and influence they may have, both judicially and extrajudicially to man-

age; to petition, respectively to notify, all approbations or instructions necessary in that regard, as well as all other requirements that may seem to him necessary, before the Most Laudable Chamber as also before the Most Laudable Government at Kalisz and before all other requisite offices and authorities, and to attend to all other necessary business for me; to settle accounts with the lessees and conclude agreements, to pay out moneys, to receive the like and give receipts for them; to issue to my officials and servants there such orders as are conducive to the purpose, to examine their conduct and performance of duty, and, should he not find such conformable to his insight and prejudicial to me, to suspend them if need be, and in general un-

restrictedly and freely to act in all matters and before all authorities as he shall find good and most conformable to my interest.

together with the lordship's plots at Marianton, amounting in all to seven hundred and eighty-one Morgen and 77 square rods, and likewise four [uncertain: thousand] nine hundred and forty-seven Morgen of forest, to dismember the said lordships together with the remaining and larger part of the forests, namely

A.) the demesne farms belonging to the lordship of Zagorowo, Drzewce, Kopojno, Swiątniki, Swięcia and Broniki, and likewise the demesne farms belonging to the lordship of Trąbczyn, Nowawies, Przybislaw, Stara Hutta and one part of the demesne farm Trąbczyn;

B. to grant in hereditary lease the demesne farm and village Olesnice, and likewise the demesne farm and village Szetlewek;

and

22nd of October and 7th of December of last year, resolved to accede to this his petition and to approve the same in such manner that the following conditions shall obtain, namely

1., that the compensation to be granted to the subjects for their hitherto pasture and timber rights be more closely determined, and that in the hereditary-rent deeds of each individual the extent of the area assigned to him rent-free on that account be
