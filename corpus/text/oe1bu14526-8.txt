--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A hereditary-lease contract in sixteen paragraphs granting Christian Friedenberger of Szymanowicer Hauland one Hufe Magdeburg measure in the Trąbczyn forest in perpetuity for 100 Rthl paid, with an annual rent of 30 Rthl, three rent-free years on unbroken ground, half the rent redeemable at 100 Rthl per 5 Rthl abated, milling and drink compulsion, no building timber, and a tenth-Groschen transfer fee on sale. Filed with it: the Prince's general power of attorney for von Triebenfeld, and royal consent to dismembering the Zagorowo and Trąbczyn lordships.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
The Princely Hohenlohe Patrimonial Court hereby makes known that between the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, as general attorney of His Serene Highness, as seller on the one part, and the resident Christian Friedenberger, as purchaser on the other part, the following hereditary-lease contract, concerning one Hufe Magdeburg measure of land situated in the Trąbczyn forest, has been concluded and judicially declared before us.

Actum Trąbczyn, the 25th of March 1806. There appeared the persons well known to the undersigned justiciary, namely

1. The Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, as general attorney of His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, hereditary lord and lord of the court of this place, legitimated by the power of attorney annexed hereto, on the one part, and

2. the resident Christian Friedenberger from Szymanowicer Hauland, on the other part.

The parties appearing are in a condition to execute judicial acts validly, and declare freely and vol[untarily]

untarily and after due deliberation the following hereditary-lease contract to the protocol.

§ I. His Most Serene Highness grants in hereditary lease to Christian Friedenberger one Hufe, in words One Hufe Magdeburg measure, reckoned at thirty Morgen to the Hufe and each Morgen at one hundred and eighty square rods, situated in the Trąbczyn forest, from now on for all perpetual time

in hereditary lease.

§ II. Christian Friedenberger pays for the one Hufe Magdeburg measure 100 rt., in words One hundred Rthaler, as purchase money; and since this purchase money is already fully paid, Christian Friedenberger is hereby receipted for it without further reservation.

§ III. Christian Friedenberger

hereby binds himself, for himself and his successors in possession of this holding, to pay to the manorial lordship (Dominium) of this place an annual hereditary canon of thirty Rthaler, in words thirty Rthaler, from this one Hufe; and this hereditary rent shall be settled yearly in two instalments, namely with the one half at Christmas and with the other half at Midsummer, on each occasion in cash and in Courant, to the manorial treasury.

§ IV. For the wholly unbroken arable the hereditary lessee receives three free years, which begin from Martinmas of the past year and end at Midsummer 1809, so that the first half-yearly hereditary rent must be paid at Christmas 1809 with 15 rt., in words fifteen Rthaler

Rthaler.

That land which, at the measuring out, is handed over to Christian Friedenberger already as broken ground, be it much or little, bears rent from Christmas of this year, since the free years extend only to that part which has first to be made arable.

§ V. It stands free to the hereditary lessee to redeem the fixed hereditary rent of the thirty [uncertain: Rthaler] up to one half, that is to say, to pay off to the ground lordship, according to his means, one, two, up to three hundred [uncertain: thaler] of capital, whereupon for every hundred Rthaler five Rthaler shall be

abated yearly from the hereditary rent; the capital so paid off remains the property of the ground lordship, and this redemption of the hereditary rent takes place only in respect of the first half; the second half, of fifteen Rthaler per Hufe, on the other hand remains irredeemable and can never be raised or reduced.

§ VI. Besides the [uncertain: inner] hereditary rent, the hereditary lessee undertakes, for the present and for future times in perpetuity, to settle and to render all the royal and other public dues whatsoever falling upon this one Hufe, in whatever they may consist, without any exception and without deduction from the hereditary rent or any other reimbursement, and to

render.

§ VII. The hereditary lessee must build at his own cost, without receiving for this, either now or in future, timber or other building assistance; however, all the timber standing on his Hufe belongs to him in property without exception.

Likewise the hereditary lessee and his successors in possession of this holding must effect all future repairs and new buildings out of their own means, without subsidy or assistance.

§ VIII. Outside his own possession the hereditary lessee has no right to firewood or to [uncertain: pasturage] in the manorial forests and fields.

§ IX. His drink the hereditary lessee must always take from

the manorial brewery and distillery, or from the tavern; if foreign drink is brought in by him or through his people, the hereditary lessee forfeits on each occasion a penalty of 5 rt. for every barrel of beer and one Rthaler for every garniec of brandy, which penalty is to be paid to the ground lordship.

§ X. The hereditary lessee is subject to the milling compulsion and may grind his grain only where the estate lordship directs him.

§ XI. From all and every manorial service and deliveries in kind, without distinction, the hereditary lessee is for ever wholly exempt;

he remains, however, subject to the jurisdiction of the lordship of this place.

§ XII.

The hereditary lessee and his successors in possession are entitled to alienate their holding to another in any manner they please; but on each sale or exchange the tenth Groschen of the purchase or exchange money must be paid to the ground lordship as a transfer fee (Laudemium), and its consent obtained to every alienation, the ground lordship hereby reserving to itself once and for all the right of pre-emption.

§ XIII. The hereditary lessee, for himself and his successors in possession, renounces all and every remission in cases of mis[fortune]

fortune, whether they arise through fire from heaven or the incendiarism of evil persons, hailstorm, crop failure, cattle plague, inundation, war and pestilence, or in whatever manner it may be, for the present and for the future, entirely; and in cases arising neither a compensation nor a remission of the hereditary rent shall take place.

§ XIV. All costs pertaining to this contract the hereditary lessee undertakes to settle.

§ XV. His Most Serene Highness consents that the hereditary lessee may, on the ground of this contract, have his title of possession settled in his own name.

§ XVI. So long as the hereditary lessee and his successors in possession fulfil all the obligations contained in this contract, His Most Serene Highness promises to maintain them in their rights and to defend them against every man's claim in rem.

Should, however, the hereditary lessee or his successors remain in arrears with the annual hereditary rent longer than two years, or else lay waste this holding and thereby diminish the revenues of the ground lordship, then the ground lordship shall be entitled to dispose of this holding otherwise, and the hereditary lessee forfeits his whole

right and the purchase money paid in.

Both parties promise to hold to this hereditary-lease contract firmly and inviolably; having been judicially instructed as to the consequences, they renounce all subterfuges available against it, in particular the plea of injury beyond and below the half, of cunning persuasion, fear, coercion, of matters otherwise agreed than written down, or not rightly understood; they applied for this contract to be made out judicially, and, after it had first been read aloud to them word for word, have, in token of their approval,

subscribed and signed the same below in their own hands.

General power of attorney. I, the undersigned, make known and acknowledge hereby that I do expressly empower the Royal Councillor of War and Forests Peter Friedrich August von Triebenfeld, Your Honour, of Schweinern etc., hereby to manage, both judicially and extrajudicially, in my name all and every affair concerning the Zagorowo, Witów and Trąbczyn estates belonging to me, situated in South Prussia in the Kalisz Department, whether they concern a dismemberment, or a purchase and sale, a leasing or a pledging, or by whatever name they may be called and upon which they may bear and have influence;

to petition for, respectively to notify, all approbations or instructions necessary in that regard, as well as all other requirements appearing to him necessary, both with the most laudable Chamber and with the most laudable Government at Kalisz and with all other requisite offices and authorities, and to manage all other requisite business for me; to settle accounts and conclude agreements with the lessees, to pay moneys, to receive the like and to give receipts for them; to issue to my officials and servants there the orders appropriate to them, to examine their conduct and performance of duty, and, should he not find the same, by his own judgement, suitable and [find it] prejudicial to me, to suspend them if need be, and generally un[limitedly]

and freely to act in all matters and before all authorities as he shall find good and most conformable to my interest.

Whatever, then, my said attorney shall do by virtue of this commission received, I hereby promise to approve, and to hold to it as inviolably as if I had done it myself. At the same time I also bind myself to indemnify my attorney in every respect on account of this commission, and to reimburse him all [uncertain: costs] and outlays, on the pledge of my present and future property, so far as may be required for this. In witness whereof I have subscribed this general power of attorney in my own hand and

confirmed it by the impression of my arms. Thus done at the Guhewitz Court of Justice near Breslau, the 19th of February 1805. (L. S.) read by myself and approved, Friedrich Ludwig, Prince of Hohenlohe.

together with the manorial parcels at Marianton, amounting in all to Seven hundred and Eighty-one Morgen and 77 square rods, likewise four [uncertain: thousand] Nine hundred and Forty-seven Morgen of forest, to dismember the said lordships together with the remaining and greater part of the forests, namely

A.) the demesne farms belonging to the lordship of Zagorowo, Drzewce, Kopojno, Swiątniki, Swięcia and Broniki, likewise the demesne farms belonging to the lordship of Trąbczyn, Nowawies, Przybislaw, Stara Hutta and a part of the demesne farm of Trąbczyn;

B. to let out in hereditary lease the demesne farm and village of Olesnica, likewise the demesne farm and village of Szetlewek;

and

C. to be permitted to convert the services and prestations of all the subjects into a fixed money rent — we have therefore, in respect of

the utility of this design, and of the profitable services rendered and still to be rendered to Us and Our Royal House by Your Lordship the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, Our General of Infantry and Governor of Breslau, by Cabinet Orders of the

22nd of October and 7th of December of last year, resolved to grant this his petition in such manner and to approve the same, provided that the following conditions shall obtain, namely

1. that the compensation to be granted to the subjects for their hitherto existing rights of pasturage and of taking wood be more nearly determined, and that in the hereditary-rent deeds of each individual the area assigned to him rent-free for that purpose be

exactly noted as to its content; as also that herein generally the provisions contained in § 12 of the Edict issued for the province of Silesia on the 1st of May 1804 must be duly observed, on the basis of a special and corrected survey;

2. that none of the present peasant holdings may be allowed to lapse, but that if, upon a fire damage or other misfortune, no one is found who will restore such a holding out of his own means, the manorial lordship (Dominium) must re-erect the buildings at its own cost;

4. that the rights of taking wood belonging to the monastery and provostry of Ląd be likewise entered in the mortgage [book] upon the totality of the [uncertain: forests] reserved from the dismemberment, and be secured in that manner; in return whereof we approve that if hereafter the [uncertain: forests] excepted from the dismemberment should be found insufficient for the rights of taking wood belonging to the Treasury in respect of the domain office of Ratyn,
