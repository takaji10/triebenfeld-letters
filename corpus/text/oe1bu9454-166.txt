--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that Minister v. Voss and the Finance Department had the dismemberment inhibited and sent a Chamber commission to investigate; Triebenfeld defended his conduct on six points, the inhibition was withdrawn, and clear annual revenues of 31000 Rthl acknowledged, on which 3–400000 Rthl might be raised, with 180850 Rthl still to come in and freedom from debt within nine years. Complains that purchasers fled at news of the inhibition, and of his own losses of 19500 Rthl through lawsuits, hail at Schweinen and an insolvent tenant at Kontop, obliging him to leave Schenck to collect monies.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord!

Soon I shall have to let the name [uncertain: laßen] the cabals of the Finance Department yet succumb to them, so many difficulties as Minister von Voss makes in the matters of dismemberment — it is unheard of. Several proprietors who already dismembered two years ago have to restore everything again in statu quo, and are thereby brought to the beggar's staff. Here he has had everything inhibited against me, and to that end has ordered the Chamber to send a commission hither which is to examine whether everything has indeed already been dismembered — whether it might not be possible to thwart this business; in particular it is now to investigate precisely whether in any way there has been action contrary to the principles; whether all the money has not been drawn off, and therefore the public charges (onera publica), as at Betsche, were no longer covered.

On the presumption that this last would certainly be the case, everything was to be inhibited at once, and in that event the strictest measures taken. The commission departed today; I have indeed prevailed,

but money and health have been spent on it. I have meanwhile shown that I have done nothing without the prior knowledge of the provincial authority,

2. that I have acted strictly in accordance with the prescription,

3. that everything is sold, so that without a complete disruption of the whole no annulment or reversal of the dismemberment could take place.

4. that the most real revenues of the manorial lordship (Dominium) would now amount to 8 times as much as the revenues hitherto, whereby the public charges (onera publica) would be covered tenfold.

5. That His Serene Highness the Prince applied the entry monies to nothing else than the discharge of the debts which encumber the estates, in order to clear the estates, and that

6. these estates were to remain an entailed estate of the princely family, which last His Majesty had already himself most highly sanctioned.

The commission has found all this verified, and has withdrawn the inhibition, the more so as everything has also already been concluded judicially with the old peasant, and as here too it was not possible even for envy to find matter for the founding of new cabals. — The clear annual revenues acknowledged by the commission are 31000 Rthl. It had to ascertain this precisely in order to cover the public charges (onera publica). This the Chamber will now confirm, and the

Finance Department will grant us the concession for it. — As soon as I receive this instrument, neither an assessment nor anything else is needed, but one can quickly obtain 3 to 400000 Rthl upon it at any treasury, and then all distress will be at an end.

The 180850 Rthl still to come in can then be taken for the extinction of all household and other debts. The only fatal thing is the accursed procrastination, for in the first moment all the purchasers ran off upon the publication of that cursed inhibition, and I must now strike very soft notes so that I may lure the people back again. Enough: Your Serene Highness comes happily through with the dismemberment and will in years be free of debt here — for if for some years you assign the revenues from the estates here to the absorption of the debts, the estates will in 9 years be entirely clear, will yield you a revenue of 40000 Rthl, and you will besides draw out of the estates by degrees a capital of 220000 Rthl. — This I believe I can now assure Your Serene Highness of firmly and sacredly. But now, most gracious Lord, I am worn down — I have worked with truly glowing zeal, and every man of expertise marvels at what has been brought so far. Now I can hold out here no longer — both my health and my fortune are being consumed by it, and I shall leave beggars behind. Also, for some time now one misfortune after another has struck.

Thus in the last couple of months I have lost 2 lawsuits which cost me some 6000 Rthl.

In another I am held in contumacy, and shall have difficulty in putting it right and saving a further 6000 Rthl. Today I receive the news that the hail has beaten down everything on my Schweinersche estates; there I shall again have some 5000 Rthl of damage. At Kontop, as I have just heard, a tenant has become insolvent, whereby I again suffer 2500 Rthl of loss — these come in a short time to 19500 Rthl. It is as though all blessing had departed from there and had followed me hither. Now I must return in order to save what is there, for otherwise I go to ruin.

I tremble, to be sure, when I go away from here and let the guiding thread out of my hands, lest everything come to a standstill again — but necessity presses me — I must hasten for some time to Schweinen and Kontop, for otherwise I risk a new and still greater loss.

I shall, as everything here [uncertain: versteht], instruct Schenck and a few other persons to collect the monies. But I cannot answer for it whether the people will hold to the execution of my orders with the same zeal, because every brute would far too gladly see that the affairs continued in confusion for a whole generation, as has otherwise happened. In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's

Trąbczyn, the 29th of July

1805.

humble and obedient servant

von Triebenfeld
