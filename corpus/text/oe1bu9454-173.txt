--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld bittet den Fürsten, Glenck zur Beschaffung der Punctation zwischen Rochlitz und Honrichs über Witów samt Rochlitz' Vollmacht anzuweisen. Im Trąbczyner Archiv fand sich nichts, sonst drohe der Verlust von 14000 Rthl. Mit einem Kammerrat will er in Trąbczyn das Locale nach den Dismembrationsplänen revidieren; insgesamt erhofft er 350000 Rthl. Szetlewek will er diese Woche erledigen. Für 8 neue Dörfer erbittet er Namen. Den beiliegenden Vertrag mit Schenck soll der Fürst wegen 5 Huben Land und des Actuarius beanstanden.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld asks the Prince to instruct Glenck to obtain the preliminary agreement between Rochlitz and Honrichs concerning Witów, together with Rochlitz's power of attorney. Nothing was found in the Trąbczyn archive, and otherwise, he says, 14,000 Rthl would be lost. With a councillor of the chamber he intends to inspect the site at Trąbczyn according to the dismemberment plans; in all he hopes for 350,000 Rthl. He means to settle Szetlewek this week. He asks for names for 8 new villages. He says the Prince should object to the enclosed contract with Schenck on account of 5 Hufen of land and the clerk of the court.

Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord!

The building councillor Glenck has assured the criminal-court councillor Grimm that the punctation concluded between Rochlitz and Honrichs concerning Witów, together with the beginning, around the power of attorney given to Rochlitz, lies in the Trąbczyn archive, of which I am said to have taken the key into my keeping.

I at once gave the old Titz and the justice-burgomaster Schenck the commission to look through all the papers present there, to see whether the mentioned punctation and power of attorney were really to be found in that archive. Unhappily, however, not a syllable of these papers has been discovered, and for that reason I cannot move a step forward with this affair, so finely set in train — Let Your Serene Highness have but the one grace of commanding Glenck that he absolutely and with the utmost dispatch procure these pieces from his Rochlitz — for otherwise you, most gracious Lord, risk losing for ever the advantage gained of 14,000 Rthl, and moreover, since Honrichs pays neither capital nor interest, you might have much vexation with Weigel and the Schleifen.

From Amelang I have letters today — he writes that I should only have the proper justification given to the valuations, and then everything would fall into place

A good general, he says, always thinks of a fortunate retreat, and more of the like. Although the windbag says nothing more substantial, I nevertheless believe that people will be content with the district councillor's review, his attestation and his guarantee; for this was my own proposal, in order to lay it upon those men's hearts — So I must now drain the cup of suffering and do the business with the wretched Blomberg. But the base fellow will not touch a pen unless I pay him 1,000 Rthl in advance — for the reports he has submitted, and for his minutes at the dismemberment plan, I have already had to pay him 900 Rthl in cash, and if everything is to come out clean, then I must find means and fill the fellow's [uncertain: gullet]. The cashier Deter here is so good as to advance me the money. Next Monday I go with a councillor from the Chamber to Trąbczyn; he reviews the locality according to the dismemberment plans submitted by the district councillor, and on the strength of these I obtain the concession without having to approach the King.

If only everything succeeds, as it now has the appearance of doing, then we gain

1. the surplus last reported from Oleśnica, Szetlewek, Witów etc. of 50,000 Rthl.

2. through the dismemberment, in cash, 200,000 Rthl.

3. the loan still to be hoped for, some 100,000 Rthl, making 350,000 Rthl.

With this modest little sum many a bear may yet be redeemed, and, as I reckon,

everything be [uncertain: smoothed over]. To be sure, I shall have many a sleepless night yet and many an hour full of care before I can reach the goal; meanwhile, if my health in any way permits it, I shall labour on untiringly until I have attained the goal.

As to Szetlewek, I would conclude everything this very week, and thereby clear a great stone of stumbling out of the way. With Oleśnica everything is set in train, and I hope to be finished with the matter very quickly and to be able to secure for Your Serene Highness the profit last reported to him.

For the propination rights I have many tenants; I shall choose the one who offers most, and press for the greatest possible security. According to the dismemberment plan submitted by the district councillor, 8 new villages are to be established — May Your Serene Highness deign to send me the names for them by this messenger, for I must submit this to the Chamber. I had at first a mind to offer the enclosed designations; but since there was still some time, I leave this to Your Serene Highness's discretion. For the rest, the contract with Schenck follows herewith. I need him much and must grease the man's mouth; I have therefore drawn up what he absolutely demanded — Two points, however, are burdensome to me.

1. 5 Hufen of land is too much.

2. that he wishes to appoint the actuary or keeper of the minutes. May Your Serene Highness deign to send me the contract back,

and write in your own hand that you must take exception to ratifying the contract (NB my power of attorney is not sufficient) — that 5 Hufen of land, with so considerable a salary, would be too much — More than 60 Morgen you could by no means concede.

Further, that you reserve to yourself in future to appoint the keepers of the minutes, or the actuaries, yourself; the present man commended to you, by the name of Brauer, you would gladly approve — but you could not concede the point in it, because otherwise all opportunity would be taken from you of providing for the men who have served you.

I shall then so direct everything that Monsieur Schein is set at ease about it. As soon as I am done with Schenck, Grimm will be dismissed, for the man is sinfully idle and always ailing. At present Schenck has 200 Rthl.
