--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that the heads of agreement between Rochlitz and Honrichs over Witów, and Nochli's power of attorney, cannot be found in the Trąbczyn archive despite searches by old Tiel and justice-burgomaster Schenck, and urges the Prince to order Glenck to produce them or risk losing 14000 Rthl; describes paying Blomberg 900 and a further 1000 Rthl demanded, projects gains of 350000 Rthl from Olesnica, Szetlewek, Witów, dismemberment and a loan, requests names for new villages, and encloses Schenck's contract (950 Rthl) objecting to 5 Hufe and his appointment of the actuary.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord!

The building councillor Gleich has assured the criminal-court councillor Grimm that the memorandum of heads of agreement concluded between Rochlitz and Honrichs concerning Witów, together with its opening portion, on account of the power of attorney given to Nochli, lay in the Trąbczyn archive, of which I had taken the key into my own keeping.

I at once charged old Tiel and the justice-burgomaster Schenck to go through all the papers held there, to see whether the said memorandum of heads of agreement and power of attorney were really present in that archive. Unhappily, however, not a syllable of these papers has been found, and for that reason I cannot budge from the spot with this business, which had been set in train so well. Will Your Serene Highness yet do me the one grace of commanding Glenck that he, with his Rochlitz, absolutely and without the least delay procure these documents — for otherwise, most gracious Lord, you risk losing for ever the advantage gained of 14000 Rthl, and moreover, since Honrichs pays neither capital nor interest, Your Highness might well have much vexation with Weigel and the Schleifen.

From Amelang I have letters today — he writes that I need only have the proper justification given for the valuations, and then everything would sort itself out

A good general, after all, always gives thought to a fortunate retreat, and more of the same kind. Although the windbag says nothing more substantial, I nevertheless believe that people will be content with the district administrator's review, his certificate and his guarantee; for this was my proposal, in order to lay it upon those people's hearts — So now I must drain the cup of suffering and do the business with the wretched Blomberg. But the base fellow will not put pen to paper unless I pay him 1000 Rthl in advance as well — for the reports he submitted, and for his minutes on the dismemberment plan, I have already had to pay him 900 rtum in cash, and if everything is to come to nothing, then I must find some expedient and stop the fellow's maw. The cashier Peter here is good enough to advance me the money. Next Monday I go with a councillor from the Chamber to Trąbczyn; he reviews the locality according to the dismemberment plans submitted by the district administrator, and on the strength of these I obtain the concession without having to apply to the King.

If only everything succeeds as it now has the appearance of doing, we gain: 1. the surplus last reported of Olesnica, Szetlewek, Witów etc. 50000 Rthl

2. through the dismemberment, in cash 200000 Rthl

3. the loan still to be hoped for, some 100000 Rthl

makes 350000 Rthl. With this sum many a burden can indeed be discharged and, as I reckon,

everything be put right. To be sure, I shall have many a sleepless night yet and many an hour full of care before I can reach the goal; meanwhile, if my health at all permits it, I shall labour on untiringly until I have attained the goal.

As regards Szetlewek I should finish everything this very week, and thereby clear a great stumbling-block out of the way. With Olesnica everything is set in train, and I hope to be done with the business very soon and to be able to procure for Your Serene Highness the profit last reported to him.

For the propination rights I have many tenants; I shall choose the one who offers the most and press for the greatest possible security. According to the dismemberment plan submitted by the district administrator, S. new villages are to be established — May Your Serene Highness deign to send me the names for them by this messenger, for I must submit this to the Chamber. I had at first a mind to offer the accompanying designations; since, however, there was still some time, I leave this to Your Serene Highness's discretion. For the rest, there follows herewith the contract with Schenck.
