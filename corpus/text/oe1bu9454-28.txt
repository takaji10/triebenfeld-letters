--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that on the 3rd the Trąbczyn estates were taken over by Appellate Judge v Lipski, commissioner of the Kalisz Court of Appeal, and handed to Canonicus v Jezierski, mandatary of Madame v Dąbska née v Prusimska, allegedly by order of the governing Commission at Warsaw; a remonstrance was lodged invoking the Peace of Tilsit and reserving all rights on account of debts paid, but no copy of the ordinance was granted. Notes two new suits: Provost Iwanski and Kuchary claiming the Swięciacie tithe, and Martin v. Otocki with the miller Ekstaedt claiming circa 7000 Rthl over the 1798 Trąbczyn lease; asks that the Breslau composition be sent and a power of attorney issued. Signed Hawich.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Lord! I hasten to communicate to Your Most Serene Highness the disagreeable news that the Trąbczyn estates were on the 3rd of this month taken over by a Commissarius of the Court of Appeal at Kalisz, namely the Appellate Judge v Lipski, and made over to the General Mandatarius of Madame v Dąbska née v Prusimska, the Canonicus v Jezierski. In the decree issued it is stated that this taking-over is effected by ordinance of the governing Commission. In this matter I have been able to do nothing further than that I protested against the taking-over by a submitted remonstrance, and also invoked therein that this act runs contrary to the plain content of the Peace of Tilsit,

according to which all obligations of His Majesty the King of Prussia must be upheld; likewise I requested a Copy of the ordinance of the governing Commission by which the taking-over was directed, in order to ascertain the legal grounds, but this has not to this hour been granted me. Lastly I have, by a protestation, reserved to Your Serene Highness all remaining rights in these estates, particularly on account of the debts paid. Your Most Serene Highness will best know what is now to be done, for with the governing Commission at Warsaw nothing is likely to be achieved, since estates have been confiscated from several of its members, and thus their own interest guides them. Next, Your Most Serene Highness has again been involved in D. lawsuits. In the first, the Provost Iwanski and Kuchary demand the tithe from Swięciacie, which suit he lost in the first instance in Prussian times, as

I have informed myself from the Manual Acts. In the second suit the lessee Martin v. Otocki, as Cessionarius of Constantin Orczykowski at Zagorowo, and the miller Ekstaedt at Wrąbczyn, demand, out of a 9-year lease contract concluded in the year 1798. over Trąbczyn, circa 7000 Rthl in compensation, because the same has not been fulfilled. So far as I have been able to learn, a judicial composition was concluded in this matter at Breslau, according to which the lessees renounced all their rights arising from this lease contract; if Your Most Serene Highness would have the grace and deign to command that this document be sought out at Breslau and sent hither, so that it might be used in refutation. It would also be necessary that Your Most Serene Highness should deign to grant a power of attorney to someone in these matters; I would in that case assist the Mandatarius. Your Most Serene Highness's interest—

is my greatest concern, and it is grievous to me that I am perhaps the first bearer of these tidings. I remain, until death, in deepest reverence, as Your Most Serene Highness's Peisern, 5th October 1807. most humble and obedient [servant]. Hawich
