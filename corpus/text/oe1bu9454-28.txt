--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that the Trąbczyn estates were seized on the 3rd by the Kalisz Court of Appeal commissioner, the appellate judge von Lipski, and handed to canon von Jezierski, attorney of Madame von Dąbska née von Prusimska, on the Warsaw governing Commission's decree; protests were lodged citing the Peace of Tilsit, a copy of the decree refused, and the Prince's rights reserved. Warns of two new suits, including a 7,000 Rthl claim on a 1798 lease, and asks for the Breslau settlement and a power of attorney.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

I hasten to convey to Your Most Serene Highness the unwelcome news that the Trąbczyn estates were taken away on the 3rd of this month by a commissioner of the Court of Appeal at Kalisz, namely the appellate judge von Lipski, and handed over to the general attorney of Madame von Dąbska, née von Prusimska, the canon von Jezierski.

In the order that was issued it is stated that this taking away is done by decree of the governing Commission. In this matter I could do nothing further than protest against the taking away by a submitted representation, and also invoke the point that this action runs counter to the plain content of the Peace of Tilsit,

according to which all obligations of His Majesty the King of Prussia must be upheld; likewise I requested a Copy of the decree of the governing Commission by which the taking away had been ordered, so as to see the legal grounds, but this has not to this day been granted me. Finally, by a protestation, I have reserved to Your Serene Highness all remaining rights in these estates, particularly on account of the debts paid.

Your Most Serene Highness will know best what is now to be done, for with the governing Commission at Warsaw there is likely nothing to be achieved, since some of its members have had estates confiscated, and thus their own interest guides them. Next, Your Most Serene Highness has again been drawn into D. lawsuits. In the first, the provost Jwanski and Kuchary demand the tithe from Swięciacie, which lawsuit he lost in the first instance in Prussian times, as

I have informed myself from the manual records. In the second lawsuit the lessee Martin von Otocki, as cessionary of Constantin Orczykowski at Zagorowo, and the miller Ekstaedt at Wrąbczyn, demand, out of a nine-year lease contract concluded in the year 1798 over Trąbczyn, some 7000 Rthl in compensation, because the said contract was not fulfilled. So far as I have been able to learn, a judicial compromise was concluded in this matter at Breslau, under which the lessees renounced all their rights arising from this lease contract. If Your Most Serene Highness would have the grace and deign to command that this document be sought out at Breslau and sent hither, so that it might be used in refutation; also it would be necessary that Your Most Serene Highness deign to grant a power of attorney to someone in these matters, and I would in any case assist the attorney. Your Most Serene Highness's inter[est]—

is my greatest concern, and it grieves me that I am perhaps the first to bring these tidings. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's Pyzdry, the 5th of October

1807.

most humble and obedient

Hawich
