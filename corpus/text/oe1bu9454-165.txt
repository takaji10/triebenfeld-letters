Hochwohlgebohrner Herr
Hochzuverehrender Herr Kriegs und ForstRath!
Ich habe die Ehre Euer Hochwohlgebohren noch einiges
was mit heutiger reutenden Post von dem e. H. Glenck
eingegangen ist mitzutheilen, das fürstliche Schreiben
hatte ich bereits zur Post gegeben, als ich von des
Fürsten Durchlaucht den Befehl erhielte im Nach¬
trag das zu sagen, was ich hier im Auszug abschrifft-
lich anlege. Sr Durchlaucht glauben, daß es denen¬
selben von Nutzen seyn dürfte. Höchstdieselben
befinden sich heute besser als gestern, in dem der
Anfall aus der rechten Hand in den linken Fuß
gezogen war, und gestern dort heftig schmerz¬
te, jedoch hörte dieser Schmerz diese Nacht auf
und war sogar beym ausdehnen der Sehnen

nicht mehr fühlbar, mir hoffen alle das besten.
Herr Burgrath Bernhardi iſt für angekommen, jedoch
wie ich gestern selbst sahe - nicht ganz geſund - Sr
Durchlaucht schikten mich, um Ihn darüber zu beru¬
higen, weil Er wegen seiner fortdauernden Unpä¬
lichkeit noch nicht seine Aufwartung machen konnte.
Euer Hochwohlgebohren erlauben mir noch schlieslich
den herzlichsten Wunsch Ihrer Wiederstellung
und vollkommensten Fortdauernden Geſundheit
anzuhängen, und zugleich mich deren ferneren un¬
schäzbaren Gewogenheit empfehlen zu dürfen
Stets in vollkommenster Verehrung.
Euer Hochwohlgebohren,
Breslau d 8ten Mertz
1805.
unterthäniger Diener
A. Hahn

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Forwards further items arrived by that day's mounted post from Glenck, together with an extract in copy of a postscript ordered by His Serene Highness the Prince after the princely letter had already been posted, thought to be of use. Reports on the Prince's health: the attack has moved from the right hand to the left foot, painful the previous day but eased overnight. Notes Bergrath Bernhardi's arrival at Breslau in poor health, and wishes Triebenfeld recovery.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Right Honourable Sir, Highly Honoured Sir Councillor of War and Forests! I have the honour of communicating to Your Right Honourable a few further matters which have come in with today's mounted post from the [uncertain: e. H.] Glenck. I had already put the princely letter into the post when I received the command of His Serene Highness the Prince to add by way of postscript that which I here append in an extract, in copy. His Serene Highness believes that it might be of use to the same. His Highest Self finds himself better today than yesterday, in that the attack had drawn from the right hand into the left foot, and yesterday pained him severely there; yet this pain ceased during the night and was not even, upon the stretching of the tendons,

any longer perceptible. We all hope for the best. Herr Bergrath Bernhardi has arrived here; yet, as I myself saw yesterday, not quite in health. His Serene Highness sent me to set him at ease on that account, since on account of his continuing indisposition he was not yet able to pay his respects. Your Right Honourable will permit me, finally, to append the most heartfelt wish for your recovery and for the most perfect and continuing health, and at the same time to commend myself to your further and inestimable favour. Ever in the most perfect veneration, Your Right Honourable's, Breslau, the 8th of March 1805, humble and obedient servant A. Hahn
