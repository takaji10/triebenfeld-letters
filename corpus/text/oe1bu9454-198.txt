--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports on his return from Poland: Trąbczyn will be recovered by Michaelmas, the sequestration is lifted, and the Calculator Schneider is auditing the accounts of Honrichs and Frankiewitz, guaranteed by pod prefect Brzechsta, with 11700 Rthl embezzled at issue; Hecker is conceded as administrator and liable. The tenant Smidkowski pays several thousand to stay; Dąbska's petition to the King over Tropiren failed. Relays Köckritz's, Tismar's and Rhaden's remarks on the Colonna papers, Hardenberg's return and the 35000 Rthl.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! Your Serene Highness's affairs in Poland, whence I returned the day before yesterday, are ordered in the best way. 1. Trąbczyn Your Serene Highness will have back by Michaelmas at the latest. 2. The sequestration is entirely lifted. 3. The accounts of Honrichs and of Frankiewitz, for both of whom the pod prefect Brzechsta has stood guarantor, are now being audited by the Calculator Schneider appointed by the Prefecture. Hecker must prove his statements together with Hawich concerning the 11700 Rthl embezzled, and when Honrichs, Frankiewicz / this is an official of Brzechsta whom I have never seen / together with Badinski are convicted, then Brzechsta must pay, if the property of those men does not suffice. — Meanwhile I have 4. conceded that Hecker may be appointed as administrator. — He is liable for everything — and will also furnish that guarantee to Weigel. The commission of inquiry takes its beginning on the 12th of this month and will likely last several days, so that Hecker, when he is required for the Weigel matter, can appear at once. Trąbczyn offers glad prospects, for the tenant Smidkowski is paying several thousand at once, merely in order to remain in the estates.

Smidkowski was in Warsaw with Kramplitz — the Dąbska threw herself at the feet of the King, who was there at the time, on account of Tropiren; but she was refused in her petition. The arrow, then, has likewise been shot without reaching its mark. Kramplitz, who returned 4 days ago, goes about very sorrowfully with this tenant Smid. — The King's resolution is in a threatening style. I hope my two spirits Lubinski and Woyda have brought this about. 6. My own affairs are arranged — Richter arrived here from Berlin only the day before yesterday. Kösteritz has conducted himself exceedingly worthily and obligingly, likewise Tismar and everyone — I am wholly content. — Köckritz writes that he would himself forward all the Colonna papers to Your Serene Highness — he wishes heartily that something at least might be effected. Concerning Your Serene Highness's own affairs he is entirely silent. Only to Richter, with whom he chatted a full 2 hours, notwithstanding that some 20 officers and other persons were waiting upon him — to him he said: if only the devil would carry off the old business — then things would go doubly well with the Prince. To the [uncertain: Guhl] Rath Sanen[?] he wrote at once that he should be easy concerning the Invalids' interest — he promises to report the effect to me at the earliest. He said to Richter in these words: Your master has many an honest joy and much envy, but the King knows that he is a man. — Tismar writes / a certain man also wished to have something in hand [uncertain], the King answered nothing — I suspect Reibnitz

or Nüchel, Reibnitz's protector. — Thereupon the King said: people are always pecking at Triebenfeld, he is surely an honest fellow. Köckritz answered: of that I am convinced; I believe that if the man were satisfied and set in the right place, he would fetch us the devil out of hell. — This the Baron v. Rhaden writes to me also — likewise a man of the old stamp and grain. — In the so-called wind-butchery there must after all be not so much truth [uncertain: sentence lacks a verb]. Be it, however, as it may, and let each man permit himself to say, for his amusement, what he will; I am content, and wish nothing further than once to make the acquaintance of such a wind-hound, whom my pistols shall rouse most terribly indeed. — For on account of the Colonna business I must pass through hellish stirrup-leathers. Köckritz himself writes to Tismar: I pity the honest soul and wish heartily that something at least may come of it, for the daredevil has poked into a hornets' nest and provoked the B. against himself — God grant him fortune. Hardenberg is coming, as Köckritz writes, into full activity, and will arrange the business of the debts. Tismar writes this likewise and names him as Prime Minister in the same quality as he was in Bartenstein; this is said to be with Bonaparte's approval / O God /. Of Zastrow nothing will now come; to Hardenberg his death is peace. Friedr. Massow would already be called Minister, only Dohna [Dąmbska?] has hitherto prevented it. From von Rhoden Your Serene Highness will doubtless already have full news concerning the 35000 Rthl, and be reassured. Grevenitz must have written to Your Serene Highness also. He will surely be vigilant and mindful of Your Highness's advantage. The old lady was, as Kochry writes to me, very ill, but has recovered again; yet her length of life is doubted. — In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's Kantwicz[?] 8th June 1810. Two divisions are marching from us against the Turks — who will command them? Tomorrow I hasten to Kontop; on the 12th I go on to Streiten and from there to Pohl[?] — most faithful servant v. Triebenfeld
