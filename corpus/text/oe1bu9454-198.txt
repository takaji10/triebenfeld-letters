--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports on returning from Poland: Trąbczyn will revert to the Prince by Michaelmas, the sequestration is lifted, and the accounts of Honrichs and Frankiewitz, guaranteed by sub-prefect Brzechsta, are under audit over 11,700 Rthl said to have been defrauded; Hecker is conceded as administrator. The tenant Smidkowski pays several thousand to stay; Dąbska's petition to the King failed. Court gossip from Köckritz, Tismar and Richter on Hardenberg's return, the debt settlement, the Colonna papers and 35,000 Rthl.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Your Serene Highness's affairs in Poland, whence I returned the day before yesterday, are ordered in the best way.

1. Your Serene Highness will get Trąbczyn back by Michaelmas at the latest

2. The sequestration is entirely lifted

3. The accounts of Honrichs and of Frankiewitz, for both of whom the sub-prefect Brzechsta has stood guarantor, are now being audited by the calculator Schneider appointed by the Prefecture. Hecker must prove his statements together with Hawich regarding the 11700 Rthl defrauded, and if Honrichs, Frankiewicz / this is an official of Brzechsta whom I have never seen / together with Badinski are convicted, then Brzechsta must pay, should the property of those men not suffice — Meanwhile I have

4. Conceded that Hecker may be appointed as administrator — He is liable for everything — will also furnish that guarantee to Weigel — the commission of inquiry takes its beginning on the 12th of this month and will probably last several days, for Hecker can appear at once when he is required for the Weigel matter.

Trąbczyn offers glad prospects, for the tenant Smidkowski is paying several thousand at once merely in order to remain in the estates.

Smidkowski was in Warsaw with Kramplitz — the Dąbska threw herself at the feet of the King, who was there at the time, on account of Tropiren, but she was dismissed with her petition. So that arrow too has been loosed without reaching its mark. Kramplitz, who came back 4 days ago, goes about very sorrowfully with this tenant Smid — The King's resolution is in a threatening style. I hope my two spirits Lubinski and Woyda have brought this about.

6. My own affairs are arranged — Richter arrived here from Berlin only the day before yesterday. Kösteritz has conducted himself exceedingly well and courteously, so too Tismar and everyone — I am entirely satisfied — Köckritz writes that he would himself forward all the Colonna papers to Your Serene Highness — He heartily wishes that something at least might be effected.

Concerning Your Serene Highness's own affairs he keeps entirely silent. Only to Richter, with whom he chatted a full 2 hours, notwithstanding that some 20 officers and other people were waiting on him — to him he said: if only the devil would carry off the old business — with the Prince it would then be doubly well. He wrote at once to the councillor Sanen[?] that he should be easy concerning the Invalids' interest — he promises to report the effect to me shortly. He said to Richter in these words — Your master has much honest joy and many enviers on good ground, but the King knows what a man he is — Tismar writes / that a certain man also wanted one for himself alone, the King answered nothing — I suspect Reibnitz

or Rüchel, Reibnitz's protector — Upon this the King said: people are forever pecking at Triebenfeld, he is surely an honest fellow. Köckritz answered — of that I am convinced; I believe that if a man were satisfied and set in the right place, he would carry the devil out of hell for us — this the Baron von Rhaden writes to me also — likewise a man of the old stamp and grain — So in the so-called wind-butchery there must after all not be anything true. Be it however as it may, and let each man allow himself to say whatever he pleases in the way of amusement, I am content and wish nothing further than to make the acquaintance of such a windbag one day; my pistols shall wake him truly frightfully — for on account of the Colonna matter I must pass through hellish stirrup-leathers. Köckritz himself writes to Tismar: I pity the honest soul and heartily wish that something at least may come of it, for the daredevil has poked into a hornets' nest and provoked the B against himself — God grant him good fortune. Hardenberg is coming, as Köckritz writes, into full activity and will arrange the business of the debts. Tismar writes this too and names him as prime minister in the same quality as he was at Bartenstein; this is said to be with Bonaparte's approval /O God/. Of Zastrow nothing will now come, for Hardenberg is his death. Fried Massow would already be called minister, only Dohna [Dąmbska?] has hitherto prevented it. From Rhoden Your Serene Highness will doubtless already have full news regarding the 35000 Rthl, and be set at ease.

Grevenitz must have written to Your Serene Highness as well. He will surely be vigilant and mindful of Your Most High advantage. The old lady was, as Köckritz writes to me, very ill, but has recovered again; yet her length of life is doubted — In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Kantwicz[?]

8th June 1810.

Two divisions are marching from us against the

Turks — who will command them?

Tomorrow I hasten to Kontop; on the 12th I go on to Streiten and from there to Pohl[?] — most faithful servant v Triebenfeld
