--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A furious rebuttal of talk, traced to the Prince's sons in Berlin and repeated by General Kleist to the prefect Count Garczynski at Kalisz, that Triebenfeld pockets the estate revenues; he is having the komornik von Chomanowski investigate, blames gossip from Schlawenschitz and from Sommer, who is spreading reports of 1,700,000 Rthl of family debt, and cites rent arrears of some 30,000 Rthl. He presses for answers on Sobottendorff, Weigel and the 50,000 Rthl Invalids' Fund capital.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
P[ro]. M[emoria].

26 June 1812. General Kleist, during his stay in Kalisz, asked the prefect Count Garczynski how matters stood here with the Hohenlohe estates, and whether they were estates of consequence – the prefect answered him that the estates were considerable but that during the war period, and through the ruin of the rent-paying people, they were in great disorder, so that even dues were still in arrears – whereupon Mr. von Kleist said: The Prince's sons told me in Berlin, Our father has never yet drawn a Groschen out of the estates – the councillor of war von Triebenfeld sits in the estates, puts all the revenues in his own pocket and lets his favourites devour the rest. I am not [uncertain: lassen halten] but Cabanis, Hecker, Honrichs and the sequestrators, it was so before – the latter [uncertain: works up] Schenck, there everything must come to light, and Cabanis is now, in the presence of the komornik von Chomanowski, laying out his account, which he is to bring to you himself, my Prince? Neither I nor my sons could pass the matter over in silence; it has here been brought into public notice, and therefore I am having it investigated by the komornik von Chomanowski.

I never counted on thanks and gain, but as God lives I will not let myself be branded as an incendiary for all the cares and sufferings I have endured, nor that you, my Prince, [uncertain: hunt]; and that for 6 years now you have drawn nothing out of the estates is true, but one must look here to where it pinches: the people are in part stiff-necked and do not pay much, but they are also already impoverished, and the houses stand empty; the rent arrears altogether amount, without the Oller nizer, Wrgbitzer and peasant holdings, to some 30000 Rthl, all in all certainly to 80 or 90; and are then the capitals, such as the Invalids', Weigel's and the Bayonne ones, together with what stands upon Träpiren and what Your Serene Highness have drawn, to be reckoned as nothing? – If after all this there still remains to you a revenue of 14 to 1500 Rthl a year, and the Bayonne sum amortises itself, then you and your descendants can truly not complain. That I enrich myself – that is truly a cursed thought,

I have [uncertain: myself before God], what is [uncertain: concealed] there, been tormented for it with lawsuits, I suffer want with my children, am worn down by sickness in misery – and such young gentlemen say in Berlin that I enrich myself here and put the money ad Sakham. In general, a nasty gossip must be coming from Schlawenschitz and out of that quarter – I seek to preserve everything and strive to save, I often beg that Your Serene Highness should not give way to these people and should not trust them too much, but I am not heard. So now Sommer has again trumpeted it about here, that you now have nothing at all left and God knows what courses you take to obtain money; in particular, that upon the Prince's [uncertain: theuer] in the burgher period they had contracted debts of 1,700000 Rthl, that you were thereby excluded from all inheritance, and that only 25000 Rthl a year were set aside for his maintenance, but that these, on account of the debts here, which likewise amounted to several hundred thousand, had all already been placed under arrest.

Should it be true, then it is ill done to spread this abroad among people here; I seek not one thing only [uncertain: constantly to guard] your honour, but also to defend and preserve your property. God will give me back my health; but may Heaven also grant many a mercy. Here you will not do me the harm of bringing Sommer back again, for otherwise the whole thing ends tragically.

Now I beg of you a swift answer concerning Sobottendorff, concerning Weigel and concerning the Invalids' sum; if these are so, then they are out of all embarrassment, and one can thereafter count with confidence upon a secure revenue; on the contrary, however, if with the Invalids' Fund this cannot be effected, all may be said to be lost.

With Weigel and Sobottendorff it will be arranged through me, if you so wish and if Sobottendorff only comes here soon, to get the 50000 Rthl for the Invalids' Fund out of the [uncertain: Wahl] there – for it is a matter of great worth. As to the instruction upon the decree of the French [uncertain: Grünval] Department, and especially the decree itself and the original receipt enclosed, I beg that Your Serene Highness will send the same, as well as the answer in this matter, as speedily as possible [uncertain: Unzucht] then on account of writing, I lie, truly, [uncertain: Landdauer] von Triebenfeld
