--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports gossip in Kalisz: General Kleist, told by Prefect Count Garczynski that the Hohenlohe estates were considerable but ruined by war and arrears, repeated the Prince's sons' claim in Berlin that Triebenfeld pockets the revenues; Triebenfeld protests his poverty, illness and lawsuits, is having Chomanowski investigate, and blames Sommer and Schlawenschitz for rumours of 1,700000 Rthl of debts. Cites rent arrears of some 30000 Rthl and presses for answers on Sobottendorff, Weigel and the 50000 Rthl Invalids' Fund, and for the French department's decree and receipt.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
P[ro]. M[emoria]. 26 June 1812

General Kleist, during his stay in Kalisz, asked the Prefect Count Garczynski how matters stood here with the Hohenlohe estates, and whether they were estates of consequence – the Prefect answered him that the estates were considerable, but that during the period of the war, and through the ruin of the rent-paying people, they were in great confusion, so that even dues were still in arrears – whereupon Herr v. Kleist said: The Prince's sons told me in Berlin, Our father has never yet drawn a single Groschen out of the estates – the Councillor of War v. Triebenfeld sits in the estates, stuffs all the revenues into his own pocket and lets his favourites devour the rest. I am not to be held back [uncertain: I am not letting it rest], but Cabanis, Hecker, Honrichs and the sequestrators, it was so before – the latter Schenck works up, there everything must come to light, and Cabanis is now, in the presence of the komornik v. Chomanowski, laying out his accounts, which he is himself to bring to you, my Prince? Neither I nor my sons could pass the matter over in silence; it has here been brought to publicity, and therefore I am having it investigated by the komornik v. Chomanowski.

I never counted on thanks and profit, but I will not, as God is true, let myself be branded an incendiary for all the cares and sufferings I have endured; nor is it the case that you, my Prince, hunt [uncertain] – that for 6 years now you have drawn nothing out of the estates is true, but one must look here to where the trouble lies: the people are in part stiff-necked and pay little, but are also already impoverished, and the houses stand empty. The arrears of rent altogether amount, apart from the Ollernizer, Wrgbitzer and peasant estates, to some 30000 Rthl, all in all certainly 80 to 90 [text lost] – and are then the capitals, such as the Invalids' [Fund], Weigel and the Bayonne ones, together with what stands upon Träpiren, and what Your Serene Highness has drawn, to be reckoned as nothing? – If after all this there still remains to you a revenue of 14 to 1500 Rthl a year, and the Bayonne sum amortises itself, then truly you and your descendants cannot complain. That I should be reckoned against – that is truly an accursed

thought. I have, God knows [uncertain: I have myself, God, what is hidden anywhere], been tormented with lawsuits for it, suffer want together with my children, am worn down by sickness in misery – and such young gentlemen say in Berlin that I enrich myself here and put the money ad Sakham [uncertain: ad Saccum]. Above all, from Schlawenschitz and out of that quarter there must come some nasty gossip. – I seek to preserve everything and strive to save; I often beg that Your Serene Highness should not give yourself over to these people and trust them too far, but I am not heeded. Thus Sommer has now again trumpeted it about here, that you no longer have anything at all and God knows what courses you are taking to obtain money; in particular, that upon the Princes' [uncertain] they had all in the citizen period contracted debts of 1,700000 Rthl, that you were thereby excluded from the whole inheritance, and that only 25000 Rthl a year had been settled on him for maintenance, but that these too had all already been laid under arrest for the debts here, which likewise amounted to several hundred thousand.

Should it be true, it is base to spread it here among the people; I seek not [uncertain] always to guard your honour, and also to defend and preserve your property. God will give me my health again; but may Heaven also [uncertain: grant some mercy]. Here you will not restore Sommer to me again; the matter will otherwise end tragically. Now I beg a speedy answer concerning Sobottendorff, concerning Weigel, and concerning the Invalids' sum – if these are so, then out of all embarras one may thereafter count with confidence upon a secure revenue; on the contrary, however, if with the Invalids' Fund this cannot be effected, all may be said to be lost. With Weigel and Sobottendorff it will, if you so wish and if Sobottendorff only comes here soon, be seen to through me: to procure the 50000 Rthl for the Invalids' Fund and get it out of the way [uncertain]. – For that is a great and weighty business. As to the order of payment upon the decree of the French [uncertain: Grünval] Department, and above all the decree itself and the original receipt annexed thereto, I beg that Your Serene Highness will send the same, as well as the answer in this matter, as speedily as possible. [illegible] as to writing, I remain

v. Triebenfeld
