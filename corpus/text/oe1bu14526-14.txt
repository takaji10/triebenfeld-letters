--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A hereditary quit-rent contract enacted before the patrimonial court, by which War and Forests Councillor Peter Friedrich August von Triebenfeld, general attorney of the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, grants forest inspector Carl Titz five Hufen Magdeburg measure from the Trąbczyn demesne, between the Podbiele road and the mill ditch, with the orchard by the stables. Titz pays 1500 Rthl in two Midsummer instalments, plus 75 Rthl yearly quit-rent, all taxes, the Ląd monastery allowance, and Laudemium on sale, building at his own cost.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Actum Trąbczyn, the 4th of May 1805. Before the Patrimonial Court here there appeared in person:

1. the Herr Councillor of War and Forests v. Triebenfeld, as the known General Bevollmächtigter [general authorised representative] of His Serene Highness the reigning Prince zu Hohenlohe Ingelfingen, hereditary and judicial lord of the estates here,

on the one part;

2. the Princely Forest Inspector here, Carl Titz, on the other part.

The comparents are personally known to the Subforestis and are found to be in a condition to execute judicial transactions validly.

They declare, freely and after due deliberation, the following, concluded between themselves, in the

irrevocable hereditary quit-rent contract, in no point to be altered, to the protocol:

§ I. Namely, His Most Serene Highness grants in hereditary quit-rent to the Forest Inspector Carl Titz, out of the arable of the demesne farm here at Trąbczyn, 5 Hufen, in words five Hufen Magdeburg [measure], and that [uncertain: below] the demesne-farm buildings, from the road which leads to Podbiele as far as the mill-ditch, including the orchard lying beside the stables, from now on for perpetual times in hereditary quit-rent.

§ II. The Forest Inspector's hold-

ing pays as purchase-money for each Hufe 300 rt, in words three hundred Rthaler, hence for five Hufen Magdeburg 1500 rt, in words one thousand five hundred Rthaler, and that with the one half at 750 rt, in words seven hundred and fifty Rthaler, at Johanni of this year, and with the other half at 750 rt, in words seven hundred and fifty Rthaler, at Johanni of the coming year 1806, in cash and in good Prussian Courant.

§ III. The Forest Inspector Titz binds himself, for the acquired holding, from Johanni of this year onward, to a perpet-

ual annual hereditary quit-rent, which may neither be raised nor diminished, per Hufe Magdeburg of 15 rt, in words fifteen Rthaler, hence for five Hufen 75 rt, in words seventy-five Rthaler, in Courant, and that yearly in two instalments, at Christmas and at Johanni, each time of 37 rt 12 gg, in words thirty-seven Rthaler 12 gg, to be paid in cash post numerando.

§ IV. Of the second half of the purchase-money, [uncertain: the 75 ortum], five per cent shall be paid up to the day of payment.

§ V.

§ V.

He must effect the establishment himself at his own cost, without receiving for this purpose, either now or in future, timber or any other building assistance.

§ VI. Besides this bare hereditary quit-rent, the hereditary lessee binds himself, for himself and his successors in possession of this acquired holding, to discharge from these five Hufen each and every Royal tax, as well as the assessment for the Ląd monastery and the dues to the [uncertain: county / lordship], chimney-money, forage deliveries and whatever else may arise, without any exception, without on this account

to make or to obtain any remission of the hereditary quit-rent.

§ VII. The hereditary lessee must take the beer and brandy for his own consumption from the lordship's [uncertain: brewery or distillery] and [illegible], and in the case of contravention must pay to the ground-lordship as penalty, for every Garniec of foreign brandy brought in, one Rthaler, and for every keg of foreign beer, five Rthaler.

§ VIII. The hereditary lessee and his successors in possession of this holding are subject to the jurisdiction of the court here, however [text lost] all and

every deliveries in kind and Hufe-services entirely and for perpetual times exempt.

§ IX. The hereditary lessee and his successors in possession are at liberty to alienate this acquired [uncertain: word] [uncertain: word] to others by sale or exchange; however, such must be done with the consent of the ground-lordship, and upon each such [uncertain: reduction of possession] the [uncertain: tenth] Groschen of the purchase-money or exchange sum shall be paid to the ground-lordship as a [uncertain: Laudemium]; whereas upon inheritances to children and collateral relatives, on the other hand,

no Laudemium is paid.

§ X. The hereditary lessee, for himself and his successors in possession, wholly renounces each and every remission in cases of misfortune arising through fire from heaven, through arson by evil persons, hail-storm, crop failure, cattle plague, war and pestilence, or in any other manner whatsoever; and on this account no compensation from the ground-lordship, nor any remission of the hereditary quit-rent, shall ever accrue to them.

§ XI. The dwelling and farm buildings must the hereditary lessee

and his successors in possession build themselves at their own [uncertain: cost], and keep the same always in good repair; and he shall receive for this purpose from the ground-lordship neither timber nor any other building assistance.

§ XII. [uncertain: The costs] of this contract and of its engrossment the hereditary lessee shall pay.

§ XIII. So long as the hereditary lessee and his successors in possession of this holding shall fulfil all the above obligations, the ground-lordship promises to protect them in their rights everywhere, and

to defend them against every man's real claims.

Should, however, the hereditary lessee [uncertain: or] his successors lay waste this holding, or remain in arrears with the annual hereditary quit-rent for longer than two years, then this hereditary-purchase contract shall cease, and the ground-lordship shall be entitled to dispose of this holding otherwise.

Both parties promise to keep the foregoing hereditary quit-rent contract firmly and inviolably, and renounce all legal exceptions to be found therein,

in particular the objection of cunning persuasion, of over-hastiness, of an injury sustained above or below the half, of matters agreed otherwise than as set down in writing; and they have now been duly instructed by the court as to the consequences of this renunciation. His Most Serene Highness expressly consents that the hereditary lessee may, on the ground of this contract, have the title of possession of this acquired holding entered in his own name.
