--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A hereditary quit-rent contract enacted before the patrimonial court, by which War and Forests Councillor Peter Friedrich August von Triebenfeld, general attorney of the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, grants forest inspector Carl Titz five Hufen Magdeburg measure from the Trąbczyn demesne, between the Podbiele road and the mill ditch, with the orchard by the stables. Titz pays 1500 Rthl in two Midsummer instalments, plus 75 Rthl yearly quit-rent, all taxes, the Ląd monastery allowance, and Laudemium on sale, building at his own cost.

Actum Trąbczyn, the 4th of May 1805. Before the local patrimonial court there appeared in person,

1st, the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld as the known general attorney of His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, [uncertain: Erkund] lord of the court of the local estates

of the one part

2nd, the local princely forest inspector Carl Titz of the other part.

The appearing parties are personally known to the [uncertain: Subforestis] and are found to be in a condition to execute judicial transactions validly.

The same declare, freely and after due deliberation, the following hereditary quit-rent contract concluded between them, in the

irrevocable hereditary quit-rent contract, in nothing to be altered, into the protocol:

§ I. His Most Serene Highness namely grants in hereditary quit-rent to the forest inspector Carl Titz, out of the arable of the local Trąbczyn demesne farm, 5 Hufen — say five Hufen Magdeburg measure — namely below the demesne buildings, from the road which leads to Podbiele as far as the mill ditch, including the orchard lying beside the stables, from now on for perpetual times in hereditary quit-rent.

§ II. The forest inspector, for the

holding, pays as purchase money for each Hufe 300 rt, written out three hundred Rthaler, hence for five Hufen Magdeburg measure 1500 rt, written out one thousand five hundred Rthaler, namely with the one half at 750 rt, written out seven hundred and fifty Rthaler, at Midsummer of this year, and with the other half at 750 rt, written out seven hundred and fifty Rthaler, at Midsummer of the coming year 1806, in cash and in good Prussian Courant.

§ III. The forest inspector Titz binds himself, for the [uncertain: staaquirirte] holding, from Midsummer of this year onward to a perpetual

annual hereditary quit-rent, which may neither be increased nor diminished, per Hufe Magdeburg measure of 15 rt, written out fifteen Rthaler, hence for five Hufen 75 rt, written out seventy-five Rthaler, in Courant, and this yearly in two instalments at Christmas and Midsummer, each time 37 rt 12 gg, written out thirty-seven Rthaler 12 gg, to be paid in cash post numerando.

§ IV. On the second half of the purchase money [illegible] 75 [uncertain: ortum], five per cent shall be paid up to the day of payment.

§ V.

§ V.

Titz must effect the establishment himself at his own cost, without receiving for this purpose, either now or in the future, timber or any other building assistance.

§ VI. Besides this quit-rent in cash, the hereditary lessee binds himself, for himself and his successors in possession of this acquired holding, to discharge from these five Hufen

all and every royal tax, likewise the allowance for the monastery of Ląd and the dues to the [uncertain: Gräßlichkeit], chimney money, forage deliveries, and whatever else may arise, without any exception, without for this obtaining or being granted

any remission of the hereditary quit-rent.

§ VII. The hereditary lessee must take the beer and brandy he drinks from the lordship's [uncertain: frage] or brewing and [uncertain: Vormundthännte], and in case of contravention must pay to the manorial lordship, for every garniec of foreign brandy brought in, one Rthaler, and for every cask of foreign beer five Rthaler in penalty.

§ VIII. The hereditary lessee and his successors in possession of this holding are subject to the jurisdiction of the local [uncertain: konnen], yet entirely and

every delivery in kind and Hufe-services, for perpetual times, free.

§ IX. The hereditary lessee and his successors in possession are at liberty to alienate this acquired [uncertain: word] [uncertain: word] to others by sale or exchange; yet such must be done with the consent of the manorial lordship, and on every such [uncertain: reduction] of possession the [uncertain: gehüte] Groschen shall be paid out of the purchase money or the exchange sum as a transfer fee (Laudemium) to the manorial lordship; whereas on inheritance to children and collateral relations

no transfer fee is paid.

§ X. The hereditary lessee, for himself and his successors in possession, wholly renounces all and every remission in cases of misfortune arising from fire from heaven, from arson by evil people, hailstorm, crop failure, cattle plague, war and pestilence, or in any other manner whatsoever, and on this account no compensation from the manorial lordship or remission of the hereditary quit-rent shall ever accrue to them.

§ XI. The dwelling and farm buildings the hereditary lessee

and his successors in possession must build themselves at their own cost and must always keep the same in good repair, and he receives for this from the manorial lordship neither timber nor any other assistance.

§ XII. The hereditary lessee pays [uncertain: the costs] of this contract and of its engrossment.

§ XIII. So long as the hereditary lessee and his successors in possession of this holding fulfil all the above obligations,

the manorial lordship promises to protect them in their rights

everywhere, and

to defend them against any man's real claims.

Should, however, the hereditary lessee or his successors lay this holding waste, or remain in arrears with the annual hereditary quit-rent for longer than two years, then this hereditary purchase contract shall cease, and the manorial lordship shall be entitled to dispose of this holding otherwise.

Both parts promise to keep the foregoing hereditary quit-rent contract firmly and inviolably, and renounce all legal evasions that might have place therein,

particularly the objection of cunning persuasion, of over-haste, of a supposed injury above or below the half, of matters agreed otherwise than as written down; and they have now been duly instructed by the court as to the consequences of this renunciation. His Most Serene Highness expressly consents thereto, that the hereditary lessee may, on the ground of this contract, have the title of possession of this acquired holding registered in his own name.
