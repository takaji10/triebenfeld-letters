--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A hereditary quit-rent contract made before the patrimonial court at Trąbczyn between Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, acting for the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, and the princely forest inspector Carl Titz, granting Titz five Hufen Magdeburg measure of the Trąbczyn demesne arable, from the Podbiele road to the mill ditch, with the orchard by the stables. Purchase money 1,500 Rthl in two instalments, plus 75 Rthl yearly quit-rent, royal taxes, dues to Ląd monastery, compulsory lordship beer and brandy, and a tenth-Groschen fee on sale.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Actum Trąbczyn, the 4th of May 1805. Before the local patrimonial court there appeared in person,

1st, the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, as the known general attorney of His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, [uncertain: Erkund] court lord of the local estates,

of the one part;

2nd, the local princely forest inspector Carl Titz, of the other part.

The parties appearing are personally known to the undersigned, and are found to be in a condition to execute judicial acts validly.

The same declare, freely and after mature deliberation, the following, concluded between them, in the

irrevocable hereditary quit-rent contract, in no part to be altered, for the record:

§ I. His Most Serene Highness grants, namely, out of the local Trąbczyn demesne farm's arable, to the forest inspector Carl Titz 5 Hufen, say five Hufen Magdeburg measure, and this beneath the demesne farm buildings, from the road which leads to Podbiele as far as the mill ditch, including the orchard lying beside the stables, from now on for perpetual time in hereditary quit-rent.

§ II. The forest inspector [uncertain: Seite]

pays as purchase money for each Hufe 300 rt, written three hundred Rthaler, thus for five Hufen Magdeburg measure 1500 rt, written one thousand five hundred Rthaler, and this with the one half at 750 rt, written seven hundred and fifty Rthaler, at Midsummer of this year, and with the other half at 750 rt, written seven hundred and fifty Rthaler, at Midsummer of the coming year 1806, in cash and in good Prussian Courant.

§ III. The forest inspector Titz binds himself, for the [uncertain: acquired] holding, from Midsummer of this year onward, to a perpetual

annual hereditary quit-rent, which may neither be increased nor diminished, per Hufe Magdeburg measure of 15 rt, written fifteen Rthaler, thus for five Hufen 75 rt, written seventy-five Rthaler in Courant, and this yearly in two instalments, at Christmas and Midsummer, each time with 37 rt 12 gg, written thirty-seven Rthaler 12 gg, to be paid in cash post numerando.

§ IV. On the second half of the purchase money, the 75 [illegible], five per cent is to be paid up to the day of payment.

§ V.

§ V.

Titz must bring about this establishment himself at his own cost, without receiving on that account, either now or in the future, timber or any other building assistance.

§ VI. Besides this quit-rent in cash, the hereditary lessee binds himself, for himself and his successors in possession of this acquired holding, to discharge from these five Hufen

all and every royal tax, as well as the dues owing to the monastery of Ląd and the payments to the [uncertain: Gräßlichkeit] — chimney money, forage delivery and whatever else may arise — without any exception, without on that account

obtaining or making any remission of the hereditary quit-rent.

§ VII. The hereditary lessee must take the beer and brandy he drinks from the lordship [uncertain: frage] or brewery and [uncertain: Vormundthännte], and in case of contravention shall pay to the ground lordship as a penalty one Rthaler for every garniec of foreign brandy brought in, and five Rthaler for every cask of foreign beer.

§ VIII. The hereditary lessee and his successors in possession of this holding are subject to the jurisdiction of the local [uncertain: court], yet of all and

every delivery in kind and Hufe services entirely and for perpetual time exempted.

§ IX. The hereditary lessee and his successors in possession are at liberty to alienate this acquired [illegible] [illegible] to others by sale or exchange; yet such must be done with the consent of the ground lordship, and upon each such [uncertain: diminution] the tenth Groschen of the purchase money or of the exchange amount is to be paid to the ground lordship as a transfer fee (Laudemium); whereas upon inheritance by children and collateral kin, on the other hand,

no transfer fee is paid.

§ X. The hereditary lessee renounces entirely, for himself and his successors in possession, all and every remission in cases of misfortune arising from fire from heaven, from arson by evil persons, hailstorm, crop failure, cattle plague, war and pestilence, or in any other manner whatsoever; and on that account no compensation from the ground lordship, nor any remission of the hereditary quit-rent, shall ever accrue to them.

§ XI. The dwelling and farm buildings must the hereditary lessee

and his successors in possession build themselves at their own cost, and always keep them in good repair, and they receive for this from the ground lordship neither timber nor any other building assistance.

§ XII. The costs of this contract and of its official copy the hereditary lessee pays.

§ XIII. So long as the hereditary lessee and his successors in possession of this holding shall comply with all the above obligations,

the ground lordship promises to protect them in their rights everywhere, and

to defend them against any man's real claims.

Should, however, the hereditary lessee or his successors lay this holding waste, or remain in arrears with the annual hereditary quit-rent for longer than two years, then this hereditary purchase contract shall cease, and the ground lordship shall be entitled to dispose of this holding otherwise.

Both parties promise to keep the foregoing hereditary quit-rent contract firmly and inviolably, and renounce all legal exceptions available therein,

particularly the objection of cunning persuasion, of precipitance, of an injury suffered above or below the half, of matters otherwise agreed than set down in writing; and they have now been duly instructed by the court as to the consequences of this renunciation. His Most Serene Highness expressly consents that the hereditary lessee may, on the ground of this contract, have the title of possession of this acquired holding entered in his own name. [uncertain: wen]

Further the contracting parties had nothing to adduce, and after this protocol had been actually read out to them, they approved the same throughout and signed below in their own hands.
