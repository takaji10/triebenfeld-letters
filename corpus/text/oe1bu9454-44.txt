--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Pleads to be released from Berlin, where the Lahr lawsuit keeps him: a judgement awards Lahr 3,000 Rthl in paper plus interest, with 1,500 Rthl of costs needed to redeem his own pledged bonds. He has repaid Coste's 9,000 Rthl with 450 Rthl interest, and asks urgently for 2,068 Rthl, and 932 Rthl cash for Oppenheimer suit costs and for feeding the French gendarmerie. Michaelis offers to settle if given 600 hundredweight of iron at 4 Rthl, delivered free to Oppeln.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
My longing to be able to lay myself at Your Most Serene Highness's feet in person as soon as possible is without bounds — I could rage with impatience.

The Lahr lawsuit still holds me back, and I must see how the matter is to be managed.

Lahr is still to receive 3000 Rthl according to the judgement just now made known to me by Amelang, together with the interest. He must take paper securities, and these would, at the present rate of exchange, amount with the interest to about 2000 Rthl. Now there lie pledged, belonging to me, 2 bonds of 1000 each against 800 Rthl

2 of 300 for 360

1 of 400 Rthl

for 340; these are 3000 Rthl in paper for Lahr, but the costs now amount to 1500 Rthl, which I must have in order to be able to redeem them at once. As soon as I have this, I am at least free. With Coste I yesterday happily [dealt with] the 9000 Rthl which at that time had to be deposited to cover the 5000 Rthl which von der Lahr gave upon Zastrow, mine and the Grevenitz bill of exchange; these I repaid yesterday, and paid him 450 Rthl in interest. Also gave his bookkeeper and the [uncertain: Constier] Oldekopp 118 Rthl

Likewise I beg for the costs of execution and the others which the French gendarmerie — whom I have had to feed and pay here — have caused; these amount to 411 Rthl. These are 932 Rthl Courant, and until I receive them I am as good as under arrest and may not stir from the spot. For the very swiftest disposition of this matter, and particularly for the 932 Rthl in cash, and for the assurance of the 600 hundredweight of iron, I beg Your Serene Highness as urgently as most heartily, and I protest that I shall continue with the most glowing zeal to help bring all Your Highness's affairs into the best course.

answered according to wish.

The 3rd of February 1809. to Berlin. von Triebenfeld
