--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports fleeing Berlin to avoid personal arrest over a 3,000 Rthl bill claimed by von der Lahn, a first-instance decree Amelang has appealed while petitioning the Grand Chancellor to correct a misread Cabinet Order; the merchant Michaelis advanced 2,400 Rthl to pay Mathieu. Thanks the Prince for promising 600 hundredweight of bar and hoop iron, contracted to Michaelis at 4 Rthl delivered to Berlin by 1 May, and begs 100 ducats to buy state paper as security.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Your Most Serene Highness's most gracious letter of the 8th of this month I received only today, here in Kontop; the second one, however, which according to Your Serene Highness's promise was to arrive at the Post Office in Berlin on the Friday following, will unfortunately miss me, because I had to leave Berlin in haste (so as not to be entirely imprisoned by the Vandertahr). The decree of the first instance ordered the said Lahr to pay the 3000 Rthl in cash forthwith, or else to pay against me according to the law of bills of exchange — the former I could not do, and the latter I had to evade in order to gain time, particularly since one has it with confidence that the said Beyme will endeavour to withdraw all those confused dispositions, which are quite contrary to the common law and the statutes, and that the suit over the 7 years will be won in the second instance, the more so as the letter of the law is entirely on our side, and that thereby 1500 Rthl might be obtained. The honest merchant Michaelis gave me 2400 Rthl so that I might at least pay Mathieu and settle a few trifles, and in that way I escaped the claws of the said Lahr — Amelang has appealed against the first judgment, will write to the Grand Chancellor on that account and beg for an explanation of the Cabinet Order which so grievously injures the laws. Your Serene Highness's name does not appear in it at all, and even mine is not mentioned, but the inquiry is made only in general terms, and the misinterpretation which the judge has given to the Cabinet Order is set in such a light on Amelang's part that the Court must duly explain the point at issue and remove the error.

In the meantime, however, Amelang advises me to be on my guard and to have at least 3000 Rthl in state paper in readiness, which in case of need — should von der Lahn pursue me here with the execution — I could deposit. But where in the wilderness [is one to get bread] Meanwhile I do not lose courage — If I obtain the 100 [uncertain: f dor] to take from?

and the hundred ducats which Your Serene Highness need only most graciously grant, then I shall know how to find my way to them.

For the assurance of the 600 hundredweight of iron may God bless you, most gracious Prince; they shall also bring abundant fruits to you, most gracious Prince and Lord. By it I am set free, and both your credit and mine will gain greatly thereby. I have concluded the contract with Michaelis and have further promised him the 600 hundredweight of bar and hoop iron, of which the first sort, four-edged, must be I believe 5/4 of an inch square, and the hoop iron of such length, breadth and thickness that it serves for shoeing the hind stand / to be delivered by the 1st of May of this year, free to the Oberbaum before Berlin, at 4 Rthl in Courant the hundredweight. With a trembling hand I signed the contract, and until I had Your Serene Highness's assurance in the matter I could neither rest nor be still. God reward you, most gracious Prince, that you have lifted this care from me.

If it is now in any way possible for Your Serene Highness, then do not send the 600 hundredweight of bar and hoop iron here to Kontop at the beginning of April; then I shall send it on from here to Berlin and the night is lifted.

Necessity compelled me, in order to escape personal arrest, to conclude the contract at 4 Rthl, so as to obtain money, which is now beyond all measure [scarce]. But if Your Serene Highness will hereafter adopt my plan for the discharge of the debts — send iron and buy paper with it — then I shall certainly procure more than 8 Rthl Courant per hundredweight

and if paper is bought with it at half or a third of its value at suitable times, then one pays the debts with it, and the [uncertain: cashier] comes to 12 to 15 Rthl, the debts are discharged and Your Serene Highness free of cares.

Just now I receive letters from Amelang. He writes among other things that he met with Cosmar in person at the Chamber Court; the latter was so distracted that he could not await his own appointed hearings. One of his confidants asked him the reason — the answer was, God, the [illegible] Sacken has fallen very ill, the woman is old, she is very weak and may die suddenly — Her death would grieve me greatly. More on this by the next post, concerning the Prince's [uncertain: affairs] etc. Amelang. God will perhaps help in every way — if only I first have the 100 [uncertain: fr] and the 100 ducats to buy or redeem the paper, so that I am secured against the claws of Lahn. As to the first, may Your Serene Highness only be pleased to have it despatched as I have said above, and to see to it that I can still get it away in April, for thereby our mutual credit [text lost] Eternally shall I be, with the most unalterable fidelity and the most unbounded gratitude,

Your Most Serene Highness's — Today is a fortunate day; just now the justice-burgomaster Hawich and Hecker are come from Zagorowo — They bring good news, but the post is about to depart; by the next post I shall render the report most humbly humble and faithful servant

to convey.

v. Triebenfeld

Kontop

the 18th of February 1809
