--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Erbpachtvertrag: Triebenfeld als Bevollmächtigter des Fürsten zu Hohenlohe Ingelfingen verleiht dem Einsassen Christoph Erbet aus dem Hauland von Trąbczyn eine Magdeburger Hufe im Trąbczyner Wald gegen 100 Rthl Einkaufsgeld und 30 Rthl jährlichen Erbzins. Nach drei Freijahren sind zu Weihnachten 1809 15 Rthl fällig. Beigefügt sind die Generalvollmacht vom 19. Februar 1805 und die Ausfertigung vom 23. Mai 1806. Hawich übergibt 1811 die Akten dem Friedensgericht des Kreises Konin und bittet, 9 Rthl 18 dgl Kosten von Erbet einzutreiben.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A hereditary-lease contract: von Triebenfeld, as the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen's authorised representative, grants the settler Christoph Erbet of the Trąbczyn Hauland one Hufe Magdeburg measure in the Trąbczyn forest for 100 Rthl entry money and 30 Rthl hereditary rent a year. After three rent-free years, 15 Rthl fall due at Christmas 1809. Filed with it: the general power of attorney of 19 February 1805 and the engrossment of 23 May 1806. In 1811 Hawich hands the file to the peace court of the Konin district and asks that 9 Rthl 18 dgl of costs be recovered from Erbet.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Actum Mariantów, the thirty-first of March One Thousand Eight Hundred and Six. There appeared the persons well known to the undersigned justiciary, namely

1. Mr von Triebenfeld, Councillor of War and Forests, as general and authorised representative of His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, hereditary lord and lord of the court of this place, legitimated by the power of attorney annexed, of the one part

and

2. the resident Christoph Erbet of Trąbczyner hauland, of the other part.

The parties appearing are in a condition validly to execute judicial acts, and freely and after due deliberation declare to the protocol the following hereditary-lease contract:

§. I. His Most Serene [continued]

Highness lets to Christoph Erbet one Hufe, in words One Hufe Magdeburg, at thirty Morgen to the Hufe, and each Morgen reckoned at One Hundred and Eighty square rods, situate in the Trąbczyn forest, from now on for everlasting time in hereditary lease.

§. II. Christoph Erbet pays for the one Hufe Magdeburg 100 Rthl., in words One Hundred Reichsthaler, as entry money, already, and since this entry money is already fully paid, Christoph Erbet is hereby receipted for it without further reservation.

§. III. Christoph Erbet hereby binds himself, for himself and his successors in the possession of this holding, to pay from this one Hufe an annual [continued]

hereditary canon of Thirty Thaler, in words thirty Rthaler, to the manorial lordship (Dominium) here, and this hereditary quit-rent shall be discharged yearly in two instalments, namely one half at Christmas and the other half at Midsummer, each time in cash and in Courant, into the lordship's treasury.

§. IV. For the wholly uncultivated arable the hereditary lessee receives three free years, which begin from Martinmas of the past year and end at Midsummer 1809, so that the first half-yearly hereditary quit-rent must be paid at Christmas 1809 with 15 r., in words Fifteen Rthaler.

Such land as is already handed over to Christoph Erbet as cultivated at the measuring-out, be it much or little

in amount, bears interest from Christmas of this year, since the free years extend only to that part which has first to be brought under cultivation.

§. V. The hereditary lessee is at liberty to redeem the fixed hereditary quit-rent of Thirty Rthaler up to one half; that is, he may according to his means pay off to the ground lordship one, two, up to three Hundred Reichsthaler of capital, whereupon for every hundred Rthaler five Rthaler shall be taken off the hereditary quit-rent yearly; the capital so paid off remains the property of the ground lordship, and this redemption of the hereditary quit-rent takes place only in respect of the first half; the second half, at

Fifteen Reichsthaler per Hufe, on the other hand remains irredeemable, and can never be raised or lowered.

§. VI. Besides the cash hereditary quit-rent the hereditary lessee undertakes, for now and for future everlasting times, to discharge and render all the [uncertain: assessed] [uncertain: pänglichen] and other [uncertain: gleitlichen] dues falling upon this one Hufe, consist in what they may, without any exception and without deduction from the hereditary quit-rent or any other reimbursement.

§. VII. The hereditary lessee must build at his own cost, without receiving for this, either now or in the future, timber or any other building assistance [illegible] In da

However, all the timber standing on his Hufen belongs to him in property without exception. Likewise the hereditary lessee and his successors in the possession of this holding must effect all future repairs and new buildings out of their own means, without subsidy or assistance.

§. VIII. Outside his own allotment the hereditary lessee has no right to firewood or [uncertain: pasturage] in the lordship's forests and fields.

§. IX. The hereditary lessee must always take his drink from the lordship's brewery and distillery or from the tavern. If foreign drink be brought in by him or his people, the hereditary lessee shall each time incur a penalty of five Reichsthaler for every barrel of beer and one Rthaler

for every garniec of brandy, which penalty shall be paid to the ground lordship.

§. X. The hereditary lessee is subject to the mill compulsion, and may grind his grain only where the estate lordship directs him.

§. XI. From each and every manorial service and delivery in kind without distinction the hereditary lessee is wholly and for ever exempt; he remains, however, subject to the jurisdiction of the manorial lordship here.

§. XII. The hereditary lessee and his successors in possession are entitled to alienate his holding to another in any manner he pleases, but on every sale

or exchange the tenth Groschen of the purchase or exchange money must be paid to the ground lordship as a transfer fee (Laudemium), and the consent of the same must be obtained for every alienation, the ground lordship hereby reserving to itself once and for all the right of pre-emption.

§. XIII. The hereditary lessee renounces, for himself and his successors in possession, wholly and for now and for the future, each and every remission in cases of misfortune, whether the same arise through fire from heaven, arson by evil persons, hailstorm, failure of crops, cattle plague, flood, war and pestilence, or in whatever manner it may be, and upon the occurrence of such [continued]

cases neither compensation nor remission of the hereditary quit-rent shall take place.

§. XIV. The hereditary lessee undertakes to discharge all the costs attending this contract.

§. XV. His Most Serene Highness consents that the hereditary lessee may, on the ground of this contract, have his title of possession settled for himself.

§. XVI. So long as the hereditary lessee and his successors in possession shall fulfil all the obligations contained in this contract, His Most Serene Highness promises to maintain them in their rights

and to defend them against every man's claim in rem.

Should, however, the hereditary lessee or his successors remain in arrears with the annual hereditary quit-rent longer than two years, or lay this holding waste and thereby diminish the revenues of the ground lordship, the ground lordship shall be entitled to dispose of this holding otherwise, and the hereditary lessee shall forfeit his whole right and the entry money paid in.

Both parties promise to keep this hereditary-lease contract firmly and inviolably; having been instructed by the court as to the consequences, they renounce

General Power of Attorney. I, the undersigned, make known and acknowledge hereby that I do hereby expressly empower the Royal Councillor of War and Forests Peter Friedrich August von Triebenfeld, Your Right Honourable, of Świniary etc., to attend in my name to each and every matter concerning the Zagórów, Witów and Trąbczyn estates belonging to me, situate in South Prussia in the Kalisz department, be they matters of a dismemberment or of purchase and sale, of leasing or of pledging, or by whatever other name they may go and whatever bearing and influence they may have, both judicially and extrajudicially; to petition for and respectively to report all approvals or orders of conduct necessary in that behalf, as well as all other requisites appearing to him necessary, before the Most Praiseworthy Chamber as well as before the Most Praiseworthy Government at Kalisz and before all other authorities; and to attend to all other requisite business for me; to settle accounts with the lessees and to conclude compositions; to pay moneys, to receive the like, and to give receipts for them; to issue to my officials and servants there the orders serviceable to his purpose, to examine their conduct and performance of duty, and if he should find such not agreeable to his judgement and prejudicial to me, in case of need to

suspend them, and in general to act unrestrictedly and freely before all authorities and in all matters as he shall find good and most agreeable to my interest. Whatever my said authorised representative shall do by virtue of this commission received, I promise hereby to approve and to hold thereto as inviolably as if I had done it myself. At the same time I also bind myself to indemnify my authorised representative in every respect on account of this commission, and to reimburse him for all costs and outlays, on pledge of my present and future property, so much thereof as shall be required for this. In witness whereof I have subscribed the general power of attorney in my own hand and confirmed it by the affixing of my arms. Thus done in the Górzyce justice office near Wrocław, the 19th of February 1805.
