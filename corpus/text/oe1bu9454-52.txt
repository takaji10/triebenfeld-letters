--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports delivering the Prince's letter and enclosures to Her Majesty the Queen, who received them with pleasure and passed the matter to the Grand Chancellor, thence to Cabinet and Kammergericht Councillor Allbrecht, with whom Triebenfeld is now working; Herr J M v. Beym's insistence on correct form threatens delay. Political news is unreportable, rumours of French and Austrian victories alternating daily, and the King has forbidden discussion of the Franco-Austrian war. Lieutenant General v. Köchn sends his respects; the length of the stay remains uncertain.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Your Most Serene Highness's letter I have, together with the enclosures, presented to Her Majesty the Queen, which the same, in the Highest degree, received with satisfaction. For the rest, the matter was at once handed over by the Queen's Majesty to His Excellency the Grand Chancellor, who forthwith passed it on again to the Cabinet and Supreme Court Councillor Allbrecht, with whom I am now working on the affair; His Excellency J. M. von Beym appears [uncertain: and] to look very much to the form of things, and therefore I believe the matter will become tedious. The Cabinet Councillor Allbrecht is meanwhile the most upright and noblest man whom I have ever known, and who will certainly [uncertain: employ] everything, and to reach the goal.

Of political news nothing whatever may be spoken here; the reports are so various that even the most clear-sighted head must fall silent as to the possible events of the future. It is a veritable ebb and flood; every 24 hours the barometer sings differently: one day the French have beaten everything, the next day the Austrians; these so contradictory reports have moved His Majesty to issue along with the watchword the command that in no manner shall there be any reasoning upon political subjects, particularly such as concern the war between France and Austria. The June- I cannot yet determine my stay here; the purposes are various, I must go daily to several places, many lack the time on account of the overburdened business, and therefore I often go in vain – His Excellency the Lieutenant-General von Köchn. commends himself to Your Serene Highness's grace – but I, in deepest reverence, remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's

Königsberg

the 3rd of May 1809.

faithful and most obedient servant

von Triebenfeld
