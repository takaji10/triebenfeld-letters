--- SUMMARY (German) ---
v. Triebenfeld hat das Schreiben des Fürsten samt Beilagen der Königin überreicht, die es wohlwollend aufnahm. Sie übergab die Sache dem Groß Kanzler, der sie an Cabinets und Cammergerichts Rath Allbrecht weiterleitete, mit dem Triebenfeld nun daran arbeitet. Weil Beyme sehr auf die Form zu achten scheint, fürchtet er eine langwierige Sache. Der König hat verboten, über Politik und besonders über den Krieg zwischen Frankreich und Österreich zu räsonieren, da die Nachrichten sich widersprechen. Köckritz lässt sich empfehlen.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Your Most Serene Highness's letter I have presented, together with the enclosures, to Her Majesty the Queen, which the same, Most High, received with pleasure. For the rest, the matter was at once handed over by Her Majesty the Queen to His Excellency the Lord Grand Chancellor, who forthwith transferred it again to the Lord Cabinet and Superior Court Councillor Allbrecht, with whom I am now working on the matter. His Excellency Mr J M von Beyme appear[s] and it [is] to look very much to the form, and therefore I believe the matter will become tedious. The Lord Cabinet Councillor Allbrecht is meanwhile the most upright and noble man with whom I have [uncertain: been acquainted], and who will certainly employ every means and to attain the goal.

Of political news nothing whatever may be spoken here; the reports are so various that even the most clear-sighted head must fall silent as to the possible events in the future. It is a true ebb and flow. Every 24 hours the barometer shows differently: the one day the French have beaten everything, the next day the Austrians; these so contradictory reports have moved His Majesty to issue, at the parole, the order along with it, that in no manner shall there be any discoursing upon political subjects, particularly such as concern the war between France and Austria. The Juni- My stay here I cannot yet determine; the purposes are various, I must go to several places daily, many lack the time on account of their overburdened business, and therefore I often go in vain — His Excellency Lieutenant General von Köckritz commends himself to Your Serene Highness's favour — but I, in deepest reverence, I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's

Königsberg

the 3rd of May 1809.

faithful and most obedient servant

v Triebenfeld
