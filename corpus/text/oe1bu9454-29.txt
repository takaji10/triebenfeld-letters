Sehr glücklich, wann Ew. Hochwohlgeboren ich die Ehre haben

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A short request for a meeting, recalling an earlier encounter with Court Councillor Hahn at Lohsen near Brieg and a promised rendezvous after the return from Memel; the writer asks when he may wait upon the addressee in Breslau, having been questioned about it by his master, the Prince. He adds that French troops are expected to be quartered at Schlawenschitz, which may tie him to the spot, force him to clear his stables and seek other lodging.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Right Honourable Sir,

Most highly to be revered Councillor of War!

When I had the honour of meeting Your Right Honourable with the Court Councillor Hahn at Lohsen near Brieg, you were so gracious as to promise a rendezvous, only after the return from Memel. Since this has not yet taken place, and I surmise that Your Most Honoured Self will already be in Breslau again, I take the liberty herewith of asking most obediently whether I may have hope, and when, of waiting upon you in Breslau, since I have been asked about it by my most gracious Lord, His Serene Highness

10 February 1808

It would make me very happy if I could have the honour of speaking with Your Right Honourable; only at this very moment I hear that we too are to receive French quartering here: then I could not stir from the spot, and it would be harder still if I were obliged to clear out the stables, and [I] shall seek another place of abode. Notwithstanding this, I hope soon to be able to have the honour in person of assuring you that in deepest reverence I have died [uncertain: erstorben] ad

Your Right Honourable

Schlawenschitz

faithfully and most obediently

the 10th of February Bal[illegible]

1808
