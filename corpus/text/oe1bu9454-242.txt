Gnädigster Fürst und Herr!
Nach einer mühseeligen Reise, worauf ich 3 Tage und 2 Nächte zugebracht bin ich in¬
Wien angelangt - die exorbitanteste Theuerung fand ich hier an der Tagesordnung
das Liebe und manchmal sehr zerlumpte Papier, was wie bekannt die Stelle des
baaren Geldes vertritt, führt den Fremde am Stande des Verderbens
weil es der Im- und Ausländer nicht richtet. Doch darüber einen Floro der
ehrliche Mann darf nicht ohne Calcul leben
Mein Text mit dem ich hierher ging war würken und schaffen.
das erste was ich bey meinen Eintritt in der berühmten Kayserstatt hörte, war
die erſchätternde Nachricht, daß der Congress bis zum ersten Novbr ausgeſezt ſey,
daß die Souverains nach Ofen und Post auf mehrere Tage reisen daselbst große
monveniren halten und gegen Ende d M zurückkehren würden.
Ferner daß Frankreich sich sehr gegen unsere Sächßiſche und Pohlnische aequi¬
sition sträube. Mein altes Patriotisches Herz hätte über diese Neuweke
bersten mögen. – Und es hoffe ich daß dies blos eine forzon de parlez, oder
eigentlich um fein de paille, ist - der Fürst Hardenberg dem ich gesund um staru
gefunden haben, steht feſten Sinnes und wird ſich von ſeinem Rechte nichts
nehmen laßen – so viel inzwiſchen über allgemeine Dinge
Jezt über die verschiedene Geschäfte

In Betrift der G[K?]ülm und Kaehminer so wie auch der Trąbczyner Sache Land

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports arrival in Vienna after a three-day journey, complaining of extreme dearness and the ragged paper money that ruins strangers; the Congress is postponed to 1 November, the sovereigns travelling to Ofen and Pest, and France opposes the Saxon and Polish acquisitions, though Hardenberg stands firm. Württemberg affairs go well, the King preparing a constitution; dealings with Gärtner, Goesch, B. and A. on the Schlaw: business; Koschentin, Külm, Kaehmin and Trąbczyn must await Poland's fate, the Moscinska née Prusimska having powerful friends.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! After a toilsome journey, upon which I spent 3 days and 2 nights, I have arrived in Vienna — the most exorbitant dearness I found here to be the order of the day; the dear and sometimes very ragged paper, which as is known takes the place of ready money, brings the stranger to the verge of ruin, because it does not [uncertain: judge/set right] the native and the foreigner alike. But of that a [uncertain: Floro] — the honest man may not live without calculation. My text, with which I came hither, was to work and to accomplish. The first thing I heard upon my entry into the famous imperial city was the shattering news that the Congress had been postponed to the first of November; that the sovereigns would travel to Ofen and [uncertain: Post/Pest] for several days, would there hold great [uncertain: monveniren], and would return towards the end of this month. Further, that France sets itself very much against our Saxon and Polish acquisition. My old patriotic heart could have burst over these novelties. — And I hope that this is merely a façon de parler, or rather a feu de paille — Prince Hardenberg, whom I have found in good health and strong, stands firm of mind and will let nothing be taken from him of his right — so much, meanwhile, upon general matters. Now to the several affairs. The Württemberg affairs go well — the King of W., who is at bottom very ill-content here, will yield much; He will himself draft a constitution and submit the same to the allied monarchies for examination and confirmation

to lay before them — the whole body of Estates presses in upon the good gentleman, and finds a [uncertain: satisfied] hearing — Prince Mett: would be the principal opponent of this regent — but the marriage is held for certain, and since the title of Royal Highness is to be conferred, this may well bring about a change in the sentiments of Prince M. I have asked the Privy Councillor v. Gärtner to dine with me today, when someone else from the Cabinet will also be with me — He appears to be a man à la [uncertain: Fandel], though of a more settled sort — the Privy Councillor Goesch is a fine, courteous and good-natured man, who, through the Princess — who for the present, together with her consort, commends herself most cordially to Your Serene Highness — is more effective than the former — Herr v. Gärtner is an author, and there circulate here many writings against Württemberg, Baden and Nassau; by the first post I shall transmit several of them. With B. and A. I have spoken very fully concerning the Schlaw: affairs, and have taken their counsel before I submitted the matter to the Lord State Chancellor Prince Hardenberg — My plan has been very much approved, and its success is to be guaranteed. Concerning Koschentin I can as yet bring nothing forward — the funds required in the Kurp. matter appeared already a very very great capital — this business therefore comes on only when the Curian one has succeeded. In respect of the G[uncertain: K]ülm and Kaehmin as well as of the Trąbczyn matter, [uncertain: nothing can sooner be determined] than until Poland's fate is decided — this much, however, I know, that the Moscinska née Prusimska has great friends, and that even Count Nesſi has been drawn into their game by Landskog. It is a sad thing that all our couriers go to Berlin by way of Prague and Dresden. With the frequent traffic I could have sent Your Serene Highness many a thing already — yet I shall neglect nothing — In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Serene Highness's N.S. This very moment I learn that all princes who cannot furnish 12000 men are to be mediatised for ever; in that case one will also not have [uncertain: olden], unless it be that it [text lost] Vienna, the 12th of 8br 1814, most faithful and most humble and obedient servant v Triebenfeld
