--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports Minister v. Voss expected at Krotoszyn in three weeks to examine the business, and urges that Herr p. Prittwitz attend in person; Chelmshi has lost at second instance, and a settlement with Broniewski of a few thousand awaits the Krotoszyn outcome before dismemberment and exchange proceed. Glenck's survey of Kaemen and Kulm yields 6000 Rthl in interest. Advises petitioning the Grand Chancellor for a five-month extension over Brzyze, the Prusimski daughter's claim and Hoym's suppressed confiscation files; notes the Winieces forest dispute, Schlabrendorff, Wildeganst and disorder in South Prussia.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Lord! According to several reports, His Excellency the Minister v Voss is to arrive in Krotoszyn in some 3 weeks, in order to examine the business in question. God grant that the matter be concluded as we wish. If only Herr p. Prittwitz would then take the trouble and go there himself, the matter would proceed very swiftly. Chelmshi has lost in the 2nd instance as well, and now I only await what he will do further. The matter will soon be concluded. With Broniewski I can come to a close at any hour; yet I shall not do so any sooner than until I see how things turn out with Krotoszyn — I have now had everything dissected once more at Posen, and everyone now finds that not a Groschen ought to come out of it for the Patri either. In the meantime, however, so as to

neglect nothing, the dismemberment there is being drafted complete and firm; to that end the exchange is being effected, and so I shall compound with Broniewski for a few thousand [uncertain: Wir] and shall order the dismemberment — After the survey of Kaemen and Kulm, Glenck is already bringing out as much as 6000 Rthl in interest, and in this manner it will go a proportion for every estate — Only on account of Brzyze have I had much clamour at Posen; this they wished to collect at once on behalf of the Prusimski daughter, and with every trouble conceivable I have been able to obtain a term of 6 weeks — My humble counsel is that a letter now be written to the Grand Chancellor, and an extension of the term of 5 months be sought. This I shall attend to, for I do indeed still have blank forms. Thereafter the matter might turn out singularly — for the Treasury has applied to Minister Hoym to have the confiscation decree published against the Prussiemski woman as well. The Minister, however, has ordered the files to be secretly laid aside, so that something might be turned to the girl's

advantage — since Your Serene Highness would otherwise scarcely be able to agree to anything — Peculiar in its kind is this stroke — for at first he did after all report these estates to the King as confiscated, and proposed them for Your Serene Highness — by rights he must answer for this shortfall — He is altogether a singular man. For the rest, it may be that yet other means of rescue will be found; I shall assuredly leave nothing untried. On account of the Winieces forest the Chamber is making much spectacle; I shall nevertheless shortly go there myself and sift the Poles — As soon as I am finished with Prince George's affairs, which should come to pass in 5 weeks, I shall hasten to Berlin in order to prepare everything for the return of v. Voss. The letter to Herr [uncertain: Prittswig] Wildeganst will deliver today; the one to Kuch shall likewise be handed over. Everything is also very well said; nevertheless I do not believe that Weigel is to blame for it — With Schlabrendorff I shall settle the matter in one way or another. In the end he shall himself lend me the money for the payment of the

interest — Wildeganst is taking every trouble conceivable to conduct this in a delicate manner. The news concerning the war is truly sad; yet I hope that matters will go better there than is believed. With the Russians it looks ill, for there one [uncertain: schmarcht — perhaps "languishes"] very much, and as I have observed all South Prussia will [text lost] thither — It is a true misfortune that no one in South Prussia keeps watch over the people. Everything already looks quite like a horde of robbers. May God only preserve Your Serene Highness in health. In deepest submission I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's Breslau, the 6th of March 1799. faithful, most humble and obedient servant v Triebenfeld
