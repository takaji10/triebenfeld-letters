--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Minister von Voss is expected at Krotoszyn in about three weeks to examine the business there, and von Prittwitz should attend in person; Chelmshi has lost at second instance. Reports that nothing is likely to be recovered at Posen, that dismemberment and an exchange with Broniewski will go ahead, and that Glenck's survey of Kaemen and Kulm yields some 6000 Rthl in interest. Advises applying to the Grand Chancellor for a five month extension over Brzyze, where the Prusimski daughter's claim and a threatened confiscation, quietly shelved by Minister Hoym, press. Notes the Winieces forest dispute and unrest in South Prussia.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

According to several reports, His Excellency Minister von Voss is to arrive at Krotoszyn in about 3 weeks, in order to examine the business in question. God grant that the matter be concluded as we wish. If only

Mr. von Prittwitz would then take the trouble and go there himself, the matter would proceed very quickly. Chelmshi has lost in the second instance as well, and now I await only what he will do further. The matter will soon be concluded.

With Broniewski I can conclude at any hour; only I shall not do so before I see how things turn out with Krotoszyn — I have now had everything at Posen analysed once again, and everyone now finds that not a Groschen is likely to come out of it for the Patri. Meanwhile, in order

not to neglect anything, the dismemberment there will be drawn up complete and firm; to that end the exchange takes place, and so I shall come to terms with Broniewski over some thousands of We[?], and give the order for the dismemberment — After the survey of Kaemen and Kulm, Glenck is already bringing out about 6000 Rthl in interest, and in that manner it will go proportionately with every estate — Only on account of Brzyze have I had much clamour at Posen; they wished to collect this at once for the Prusimski daughter, and with every conceivable effort I have been able to obtain a term of 6 weeks — My humble counsel is that a letter now be written to the Grand Chancellor and an extension of the term by 5 months be applied for. This I shall see to, for I still have blank forms. Thereafter the matter may turn out oddly — for the fisc has approached Minister Hoym, to have the confiscation decree published against the Prussiemski woman as well. The Minister has, however, ordered the files to be secretly laid aside, so that something might be

turned to the girl's advantage — since otherwise Your Serene Highness would hardly be willing to agree to anything — Peculiar in its way is this move — for first he did after all report these estates to the King as confiscated, and proposed them for Your Serene Highness — in law he must answer for this shortfall — This is altogether a singular man. For the rest, it may be that still other means of rescue will be found; I shall certainly leave nothing untried. On account of the Winieces forest the Chamber is making much spectacle; I shall meanwhile shortly go there myself and sift out the Poles — As soon as I am done with Prince George's affairs, which should happen within 5 weeks, I shall hasten to Berlin in order to prepare everything for von Voss's return. The letter to Mr. [uncertain: Pritswig] Wildeganst will deliver today; that to Kuch shall likewise be handed over. Everything is also very well said; meanwhile I do not believe that Weigel is to blame for it — With Schlabrendorff I shall settle the matter one way or the other. In the end he shall himself lend me the money for the payment of the

interest — Wildeganst is taking every conceivable trouble to direct this in a delicate manner. The news concerning the war is truly sad; yet I hope that matters will go better there than is believed. With the Russians it looks bad, for there one languishes greatly, and as I have observed, all of South Prussia is turning that way — It is a true misfortune that no one in South Prussia keeps watch over the people. Everything already looks quite like a band of robbers.

May God only preserve Your Serene Highness in health. In deepest submission I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Breslau, the 6th of March 1799.

most faithful and humble servant

von Triebenfeld
