--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Copy of a plea on behalf of Prince August, forwarding a file of documents including the exchange with Minister von Kircheisen which the court refused to enter in its protocol. Citing medical councillor Cossnar's letter of 22 June 1810, Triebenfeld charges Cosmar with placing everything under arrest over the Kurz heirs' claim of 300,000 Rthl, which could be settled for 112,000 Rthl, only 12,000 in cash plus an instruction for 100,000; he pledges his property and liberty as security and urges administration of the entailed estates.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Copy. Most Serene Prince etc. In accordance with the commission of the Prince of Hohenlohe I am to present most humbly to Your Most Serene Highness the accompanying document. It contains the correspondence with His Excellency Herr M. v. Kircheisen, which the [uncertain: bringen] Q. L. Court would not admit into the protocol as taken, because it had not been charged therewith. Since, however, from the medical councillor Cosmar's letter of the 22nd of June 1810 sub S: the most unmistakable [uncertain: tyranny] against the poor good Prince emerges, especially as Cosmar himself admits that he has, on account of the claim of the Kurz heirs for 300000 Rthl, which the Prince alone undertook to contest, stripped the Prince of everything and laid it under arrest, I hasten most humbly to lay the documents at Your Most Serene Highness's feet. How highly necessary it is that the Curian heirs be satisfied, Your Most Serene Highness will be pleased to gather herefrom. The heirs are quite certainly, according to the proofs in hand, to be satisfied with 112000 Rthl; indeed one demands only 12000 Rthl at once in cash, and for the 100000 Rthl a secure order to pay. — Should Your Most Serene Highness approve these petitions and cause the sum to be advanced to the upright old Prince, and the whole of the entailed estates be forthwith administered for the treasury before they are given over, then an honest Prince is at once out of the claws of Cosmar and, through Your Most Serene Highness's grace, upon which alone he counts, rescued. I bind myself in return, upon the forfeiture of my whole goods and of my liberty, to deliver up the debt [uncertain: Intravante] and above all the guarantee of His Majesty at once into Your Serene Highness's hands. The one thing that remains to be wished, however, is that the Monarch, if today or tomorrow the K. heirs should again approach His Majesty concerning their satisfaction, should disavow himself of the matter, as has already been done, whereby all is quickly [uncertain: attained], and the King's word remains sacred. Cosmar lies, especially if he be held to the most expeditious rendering of accounts, unmasked and trodden in the dust, the more so as Prince August now demands back the power of attorney given to him. Funds for the discharge of the 112000 Rthl as well as of the 35000 are present in superfluity; indeed even the 18000 Rthl which Prince August is to draw annually, and of which some 54000 Rthl already lie in store, can be [uncertain: verweint] applied out of hand thereto, and thus the debt of 147/ be discharged in a very short time. Should Your Most Serene Highness [uncertain: grant] this most humble petition, then the good Prince, who all guiltless has already through 9 years endured the [illegible] sufferings, is rescued through Your most high grace, and every creditor set at rest. — In unbounded reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's v Triebenfeld. Vienna, the 24th of January 1815.
