v. Triebenfeld erklärt, ohne Vergleich mit den Prinzessinnen lasse sich in Wien nichts mehr erreichen, da man dem Fürsten Kanzler einen Vergleich über 162/m Rthl vorgespiegelt habe. Er drängt, Eberhard mit einem Pass herzuschicken. Er beklagt, dass Fürst August die Präsidentenstelle angenommen hat. Ein Schreiben fordert, ihm die 100/m Rthl bar auszuzahlen. 282

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

This very moment, and indeed some 2 hours before the post goes out, I receive Your Serene Highness's two letters of the 18th with the enclosure from Eberhard. I can do nothing further in the matter than to lament Your Serene Highness's fate — Your Highness, most gracious Prince and Lord, will surely see for yourself that without a settlement with the kurz princesses nothing more can be said here, the more so as the Prince Chancellor has already been lied to in this, in that it was asserted with confidence that the Weiler and 162/m rt. were willing to settle — and if the matter cannot now be brought about, at a time when the ladies are in want of money, then, best of Princes, they are deraugt 300/m rt. the poorer — Mr Eberhard is right that his coming here would be most necessary — only we can beg neither a pass for him here, still less money — But if he does come here, then everything shall be settled for him thus and he shall be placed beyond all responsibility. He needs only a pass from Your Serene Highness and from the magistrate there of some border town, and if he travelled with his own horses then the [text lost] devil would ask him anything — The matter must now be settled in one way or another, and I cannot and will not take that upon myself alone — For the rest, it may yet be that I, with my friends, shall before long have Cosmar's swan-song and the panegyric of the obliging spirits made public — if it be possible in the world, and if I am not led astray from other quarters, then by God I keep my word and press on toward the goal until I reach it — and if this, which I already see in the Güste, is even in some measure attained, then surely every scoundrel will want to become a knight in the cause and will boast of having done Your Serene Highness God knows what services in the world — But I stake my honour upon it that not one of them has troubled himself over the wheels on Your Serene Highness's account, still less once gone to bed this year or watched through sleepless nights for you, good Prince — Meanwhile I beg that a Hahn, a Paille, or a Lombardini not be counted among those [uncertain: Wolte], for they too may well sometimes have had the sun rather than tomorrow's bread in the house for themselves and their children — Do, noble and exalted Prince, think therein of these faithful people — I know they suffer no tutoring, but hear the counsel of an honest fellow who hates flattery and not, as

my old judge would have you believe — enough of this; God will and can help — That Prince August has accepted the presidency which all men of honour have declined is heart-shattering — for now they will hold him here no longer for weak but for — mad — The Congress has forbidden the King of Württemberg everything and has referred them to a general constitution — even every power of attorney of all the estates and persons assembled there was comprehended in the protestation — How does the man come / when I myself warned Your Serene Highness against it / to blow now in the King's horn, and to assume the character which everyone else thrust away from himself — what will Prince Langenburg, what will Prince Kirchberg and others say of this young man, that he accepts the painted office which every one of them declined with steadfastness and magnanimity — O, that is a wise father of the land — Since yesterday a letter has gone off to Prince August, and one to Berlin, to the effect that the 100/m rt. are to be paid over to him, but absolutely in cash / for perhaps Cosmar means to give paper / — You did indeed will it so, and I still doubt that he will withdraw the power of attorney — To all the rest I say nothing further than that I refer to the letters sent off these 4 days past — Barbe's servant Your Serene Highness will indeed have dispatched, as I have asked — he takes my horses, and you will have a firm wicker travelling-body given him there, with a tilt. Then send also the two-horse cart to Prague to fetch the glass — lastly may you be pleased to send the Tambour and Wawrzyn back to Blizanow by post and with a little white money — According to today's news Napoleon presses ever more toward Paris — the King is posting an army near Melun, which was done at Grenoble and Lyon too — but Napoleon is so cunning that where danger threatens him he goes round it — and since no one may be inclined to earn the premium of 12 millions of francs set upon his head, he might well succeed in paying his visit to Paris — Judgements on this differ very widely — and the confusion is great. Meinand is better off here in the matter than the stock-jobbers — O God, were it only possible to send Eberhard here — Respectfully I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's

Glenck I have found today; he commends himself to your favour, and will at once [uncertain: schasten] Your Serene Highness 200000 rt. in cash in [uncertain: scheer] money at Op[text lost]urg —

Vienna, the 26th of March

1815.

most faithful servant

v Triebenfeld

P.S. Tomorrow I shall make one further attempt, whether Mr Humboldt will not conclude the Courland settlement and earn what[?] the advocates want to have — I think Eberhard will not be able to do the whole of it — since the first declaration was not true, I do not trust the second at all, and as it seems to me, quite other levers belong to this business afterwards; should it succeed, I shall send an express courier on that account, and then Mr C. will not be needed — should no express courier come within 4 days, then Eberhard must absolutely come here —
