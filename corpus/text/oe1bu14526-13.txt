--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Hereditary-lease contract in nineteen paragraphs by which von Triebenfeld, General Plenipotentiary of the Fürst zu Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, cedes 20 Magdeburg Hufen (560 Morgen arable, 40 meadow) of Trąbczyn land on the Kopoyner side to Amtmann Johann Gottlieb Tschachmann for 6,000 Rthaler, 200 paid down and 5,800 due 22 June, plus 15 Rthaler yearly rent per Hufe, unredeemable, with Laudemium of the tenth Groschen on resale, tavern obligation, free lodging in the cooper's house to Midsummer 1806, no pasture or timber rights, and forfeiture after two years' arrears; witnessed by Cabanis and Richter.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Between the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, General Plenipotentiary of His Serene Highness the Reigning Fürst zu Hohenlohe Ingelfingen, hereditary lord and lord of jurisdiction of the estates here, on the one part, and the Amtmann Herr Johann Gottlieb Tschachmann on the other part, the following contract was this day agreed and concluded.

§ I. The Councillor of War von TriebenFeld cedes to Herr Tschachmann, out of the lands belonging to Trąbczyn, consisting of fields and meadows, the surface area of 20 Hufen, say Twenty Hufen Magdeburg measure, the Hufe reckoned at 30 Morgen and the Morgen at 180 rods, for perpetual times, and this on the Kopoyner side, next to the lands chosen by the Princely Inspector Titz.

§ II. Herr Tschachmann is at liberty to cede these acquired Hufen in several separate portions to others, and to come to such agreement with the new hereditary acquirers as he can to his own advantage.

§ III. As purchase money the Herr hereditary taker pays for each Magdeburg Hufe 300 rttl., say Three hundred Rthaler Royal Prussian Courant, hence for 20 Magdeburg Hufen six Thousand Reichsthaler.

§ IV.

§ IV. The Herr hereditary taker and his successors in possession shall pay from each Magdeburg Hufe yearly a clear money rent of 15 rttl., say Fifteen Rthaler in Courant, to the manorial treasury, and this in half-yearly instalments at Midsummer (Johanni) and Christmas, each time Seven rttlr Twelve good Groschen.

§ V. For the further establishment of this contract the Herr hereditary lessee has this day duly paid 200 rt., say two hundred Reichsthaler, for which he is hereby given receipt, and he promises to lay down the remainder of the whole sum, being 5800 rt., say five Thousand Eight hundred Reichsthaler, on the 22nd of June of this year here in Trąbczyn in cash.

§ VI. These Twenty Hufen taken in hereditary lease shall be measured out to the Herr hereditary taker as soon as possible and delivered into his possession.

§ VII. The Twenty Hufen of land shall consist of Five hundred and sixty Morgen of arable and of Forty Morgen of meadow growth.

§ VIII. The Herr hereditary lessee and his subsequent purchasers shall pay for all the sowings upon those Hufen which they take over, in such manner that they compensate the manorial lordship at market prices for as many bushels (Scheffel) as have been sown upon their lands, and this shall be faithfully demonstrated.

§ IX.

§ IX. To the Herr hereditary lessee there is granted the house in which the cooper in Trąbczyn dwells, as free lodging for one year from Midsummer (Johanni); but from Midsummer 1806 he must either have his own house finished or procure himself another dwelling.

§ X. Besides the yearly hereditary rents, the hereditary lessee undertakes, for himself and his descendants, to render all Royal and other dues charged upon these estates, by whatever name they may be called, and such shall be laid upon the Hufen and duly apportioned.

§ XI. That all the lands are dismembered and let out against rents; therefore hereditary lessees may in no wise lay claim to any right of pasture or of timber, but each must graze his cattle upon his own lands alone, and either grow his timber himself or buy it.

§ XII. To the present hereditary lessee, as to the other takers, it is free to alienate their holdings to others in any manner they please; but upon every change of possession, whether by sale or by exchange, the tenth Groschen of the purchase money, or of the amount of the exchange, must be laid down to the ground lordship as a Laudemium, and the same shall always hold itself to

to the new takers, whose business it remains not to pay out the purchase money to the seller until the Laudemium has been settled.

§ XIII. No redemption of the hereditary rent shall take place; the fifteen Rthaler per Hufe once fixed remain unredeemable for everlasting times.

§ XIV. The hereditary lessee, as also his subsequent purchasers, remain for ever entirely free of all hereditary subjection, and are also not to render the ground lordship the least manorial service, nor to deliver any payments in kind. They are, however, without exception subject to the jurisdiction of the Dominium for ever and perpetual times.

§ XV. The hereditary lessees are bound to lay out and build up the new villages according to prescription in two, or in one even line; wherefore Herr Tschachmann with his subsequent purchasers must likewise submit himself to this.

§ XVI. The Herr hereditary lessee as well as his successors in possession must always take their drink from the manorial brew- and brandy-house or tavern; and for every quart of brandy brought in from foreign estates

shall lay down One Thaler to the ground lordship, and for every barrel of beer five Rthaler.

§ XVII. The Herr hereditary lessee renounces, for himself and his successors, each and every remission in cases of misfortune, by whatever name they may ever be called, and no bonification or abatement of the hereditary rent can ever accrue to them.

§ XVIII. The costs of the contract, of its confirmation and of its engrossment, the hereditary lessee and his subsequent purchasers undertake to settle.

§ XIX. So long as the hereditary lessee and his successors in possession shall fulfil these obligations described above, they shall be protected by the ground lordship in their rights in every respect, and be defended and held harmless against every man's real claims. Should they, however, remain in arrears with the yearly hereditary canon for longer than two years, they shall thereupon forfeit this hereditary lease, and the ground lordship is entitled to resume the same without contradiction and to let it out otherwise. Both parties promised to keep the foregoing hereditary-rent contract firmly and inviolably, and renounce

all legal evasions available against it, in particular the objection of cunning persuasion, of over-haste, of an injury suffered above or below half the value, and of matters otherwise agreed than as set down in writing; and they have signed with their own hands, together with the witnesses, as follows. Trąbczyn, the 7th of May 1805. Peter Friedrich August v Triebenfeld, Royal Councillor of War and Forests, also General Plenipotentiary of His Serene Highness the Fürst zu Hohenlohe Ingelfingen.

Read and approved

Johann Gottlieb Tschachmann. Johan Fried. Cabanis as witness. Carl. Willhelm Richter

as witness.
