--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A hereditary-lease contract in nineteen clauses by which von Triebenfeld, general attorney of the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, makes over 20 Magdeburg Hufen of Trąbczyn field and meadow on the Kopojno side to district administrator Johann Gottlieb Tschachmann for 6,000 Rthl, 200 Rthl paid down and 5,800 Rthl due that June, plus an irredeemable yearly rent of 15 Rthl per Hufe. Tschachmann may resell in parcels; takers owe a tenth-Groschen transfer fee, must buy drink from the lordship, get no pasture or timber, and forfeit for two years' arrears.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Between the councillor of war and forests von Triebenfeld, general attorney of His Serene Highness the Reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, hereditary and judicial lord of the estates here, on the one part, and the district administrator Mr Johann Gottlieb Tschachmann on the other part, the following contract was this day agreed and concluded.

§ I. The councillor of war von Triebenfeld makes over to Mr Tschachmann, out of the lands belonging to Trąbczyn, consisting of fields and meadows, the surface area of 20 Hufen, say twenty Hufen of Magdeburg measure, the Hufe reckoned at 30 Morgen and the Morgen at 180 rods, for all time, and namely on the Kopojno side, next to the lands chosen by the princely inspector Titz.

§ II. Mr Tschachmann is at liberty to make over these Hufen so acquired to others in several separate parcels, and to come to such terms with the new hereditary acquirers as he best can to his own advantage.

§ III. By way of purchase money the hereditary taker pays for each Magdeburg Hufe 300 Rthl, say three hundred Reichsthaler Royal Prussian Courant, hence for 20 Magdeburg Hufen six thousand Reichsthaler.

§ IV.

§ IV. The hereditary taker and his successors in possession shall pay yearly from each Magdeburg Hufe a clear money rent of 15 Rthl, say fifteen Reichsthaler in Courant, into the lordship's treasury, and that in half-yearly instalments at Midsummer and Christmas, each time seven Rthl twelve good Groschen.

§ V. For the further establishment of this contract the hereditary lessee has this day duly paid 200 Rthl, say two hundred Reichsthaler, for which he is hereby given receipt, and he promises to lay down the remainder of the whole sum, 5800 Rthl, say five thousand eight hundred Reichsthaler, in cash here in Trąbczyn on the 22nd of June of this year.

§ VI. These twenty Hufen taken in hereditary lease are to be measured out to the hereditary taker as soon as possible and delivered into his possession.

§ VII. The twenty Hufen of land shall consist of five hundred and sixty Morgen of arable and of forty Morgen of meadow growth.

§ VIII. The hereditary lessee and his subsequent purchasers shall pay for all the seed-corn upon the Hufen which they take over, in such manner that they compensate the lordship, at market prices, for as many bushels as have been sown upon their lands, and this is to be faithfully accounted for.

§ IX.

§ IX. The hereditary lessee is granted free habitation in the house where the cooper in Trąbczyn dwells, for one year from Midsummer; but from Midsummer 1806 he must either have his own house finished or procure himself another dwelling.

§ X. Besides the yearly hereditary rents, the hereditary lessee undertakes, for himself and his descendants, to render all royal and other dues charged upon these estates, by whatever name they may be called, and such shall be laid upon the Hufen and duly apportioned.

§ XI. Since all the lands are dismembered and let out against rents, the hereditary lessees may make no claim to any right of pasture or of timber, but each must graze his cattle upon his own lands alone, and either grow his timber himself or buy it.

§ XII. The present hereditary lessee, as also the other takers, are at liberty to alienate their holdings to others in any manner they please; but upon every change of possession, whether by sale or by exchange, the tenth Groschen of the purchase money, or of the exchange value, must be laid down to the ground lordship as a transfer fee (Laudemium), and the said lordship holds always to

to the new takers, whose business it remains not to pay out the purchase money to the seller until the transfer fee has been discharged.

§ XIII. No redemption of the hereditary rent shall take place; the fifteen Reichsthaler per Hufe once fixed remain irredeemable for everlasting times.

§ XIV. The hereditary lessee, as also his subsequent purchasers, remain for ever entirely free of all hereditary subjection, and they shall not be obliged to render the least manorial service to the ground lordship, nor to deliver any payment in kind. They are, however, without exception subject to the jurisdiction of the manorial lordship (Dominium) for ever and all time.

§ XV. The hereditary lessees are bound to lay out and build up the new villages as prescribed, in two lines or in one even line, wherefore Mr Tschachmann together with his subsequent purchasers must likewise submit himself to this.

§ XVI. The hereditary lessee, as also his successors in possession, must always take their drink from the lordship's brewhouse and distillery or tavern; and for every quart of brandy brought in from other estates

they shall lay down to the ground lordship one thaler, and for every tun of beer five Reichsthaler.

§ XVII. The hereditary lessee, for himself and his successors, renounces each and every remission in cases of misfortune, by whatever name they may be called, and no allowance or abatement of the hereditary rent can ever accrue to them.

§ XVIII. The costs of this contract, of its confirmation and of its execution, the hereditary lessee and his subsequent purchasers undertake to discharge.

§ XIX. So long as the hereditary lessee and his successors in possession shall fulfil the obligations described above, they shall be protected by the ground lordship in their rights in every respect, and be defended and held harmless against any man's real claims. Should they, however, remain in arrears with the yearly hereditary canon for longer than two years, they then forfeit this hereditary lease, and the ground lordship is entitled to take the same back without contradiction and to let it out otherwise. Both parties promised to keep the foregoing hereditary-rent contract firmly and inviolably, and they renounce

all legal subterfuges available against it, in particular the objection of cunning persuasion, of over-hastiness, of an occurring injury above or below the half, and of matters agreed otherwise than as written down; and they have signed with their own hands together with the witnesses as follows. Trąbczyn, the 7th of May 1805. Peter Friedrich August von Triebenfeld, royal councillor of war and forests, also general attorney of His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen.
