--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Entwurf eines Schreibens an Minister von Schuckmann: Die Erben des verstorbenen Fürsten von Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen und Graf Bethusy ließen sich bei der russischen Regierung durch Prinz Eugen von Württemberg vertreten; nach der Note des russischen Gesandten hat der Kaiser beide Gesuche abgelehnt. Für die Erben könne, als in einer bloßen Gnadensache, keine Verwendung stattfinden. Bethusys Antrag sei unzulässig, da seine Besitzungen nicht von der neuen Grenze durchschnitten werden. Die weitere Verfügung wird dem Minister anheimgestellt.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Draft of a letter to Minister von Schuckmann: the heirs of the late Prince of Hohenlohe and Count Bethusy had themselves represented before the Russian government by Prince Eugen of Württemberg; according to the Russian envoy's note, the Emperor has rejected both requests. For the heirs, as this is a mere matter of grace, no intervention can take place. Bethusy's request is said to be inadmissible, since his properties are not cut through by the new border. Further action is left to the Minister.

Berlin, 13 May 1820.

To the Royal Privy Minister of State and Minister of the Interior, Mr von Schuckmann, c[illegible] 716 B.

The heirs of the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, who died at Sławięcice in Silesia, and Count von Bethusy in Silesia have had themselves represented before the Imperial Russian Government by Prince Eugen of Württemberg — the former on account of a compensation applied for in respect of the estates of Kamionna and Trąbczyn in Poland which were taken from their testator, the latter [lit. "the last-mentioned (neuter)"] on account of the free traffic desired between his possessions in Silesia and Poland, inferred from the treaty of between Prussia and Russia of 21 April/3 May 1815 — and upon this occasion there has reached me that note of the Imperial Russian envoy here which I have the honour most obediently to communicate to Your Excellency herewith in copy, entire, and according to which His Majesty the Emperor of Russia has refused the petition of both petitioners. In the matter of the heirs of the Prince of Hohenlohe no intercession can take place, it being a mere matter of grace; moreover, the [uncertain: support/recommendation] of his petition which was made during the Prince's lifetime through the Royal envoy at Petersburg likewise remained unregarded.

and have not been separated at all in the first place by the said new frontier. The application of Count Bethusy, however, is wholly inadmissible, since under article 18 of the treaty of 3 May 1815 the privilege applied for is to fall to those landowners whose possessions are cut through by the new frontier laid down by the said treaty, whereas the petitioner's possessions lie far apart from one another, in different provinces.

Since, if the Imperial Russian Government had [uncertain: nonetheless] granted the petition of Count von Bethusy, the government on this side would thereby have been brought into the unpleasant position of having to lodge objection against it, the impropriety of his application ought therefore not [to be left unmade known] to Count von Bethusy not my [text lost] [illegible] Your Excellency; I therefore believe I may, the more so as only the whereabouts of the petitioners, whose possessions lie in the district of the government at Opole, is not more precisely known, most obediently leave the further disposition entirely[?] to you.
