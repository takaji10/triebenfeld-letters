--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that after seven months Cosmar is finally ordered to pay the three years' withheld monies into the Brieg High Provincial Court, of which 12000 Rthl yearly is assigned to creditors; complains that Stägemann obstructs matters as defender of Cosmar and adviser of the Curian heiresses, that the Prince Chancellor promises but does nothing, while Minister Stein pursues compensation through the office of Sokolnick. Promises a Pro Memoria on the kurz affair and the military salary, recounts Adolph's awkward conduct before the King, and urgently requests 100 ducats by express courier.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

After 7 months the order is now at last given that Cosmar shall pay in, with the very greatest despatch, to the Brieg High Provincial Court the 3 years' monies he has been holding back, which Your Serene Highness is to draw annually, and out of which you have assigned 12000 Rthl yearly in favour of your creditors — he is at the same time instructed to keep order and to render prompt account. That Stägemann has the worst will shows itself at every turn; he now plays the defender of Cosmar and the counsellor of the Curian heiresses. The Prince Chancellor, on the other hand, promises everything — but reads, sees and hears nothing further once he has promised it. Minister Stein, by contrast, works honestly and is at pains to procure for Your Serene Highness the proposed compensation by way of the office of Sokolnick. He has already drawn the Chancellor into the game, so that the two of them may lay the matter before the Emperor together. Tomorrow I shall submit to the Prince again a somewhat more emphatic memorandum concerning the [uncertain: kurz] affair as well as concerning your military salary — I shall carry the matters through, even were I obliged to follow him as far as the Rhine. It must once for all be brought clear. Tomorrow morning I have a conference with Stägemann, and consequently more on Tuesday. This very moment I receive Your Serene Highness's letter of the 9th; upon that too I shall write by the next post. In reverence I remain Your Serene Highness's

As Colonel von T. tells me, Adolph conducted himself

very awkwardly indeed before the King. The King asked him what his father was doing, how he was; the answer was — I do not know, I have not seen him for a long time. turn over [text lost] Vienna, the 20th of May 1815 — most obedient, the von Triebenfeld

Both I and Barbe and Beugelin had instructed him what he should say — he could have done much for his father at that moment. In short, your sons will act, [but] never take after [you]. Humboldt and also Nesselrode wished to present him to their monarch; to that he could not be brought, and hastened away from there. The Countess Fricis and several others say there is not a drop of his father's blood in him.

Do be sure, Your Serene Highness, to send me the 100 ducats, for I mostly do not know whether I shall not have to go straight on to Berlin in the matter — and here too I still have much to pay. I have asked that it be sent by express courier. It would be very [uncertain: schlagen] if you were not to do so.
