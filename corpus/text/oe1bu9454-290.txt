--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Nach 7 Monaten ist Cosmar angewiesen, die Gelder aus 3 Jahren, von denen jährlich 12/m Rthl den Gläubigern zugewiesen sind, schleunigst beim Oberlandesgericht Brzeg einzuzahlen und Rechnung zu legen. Stägemann verteidigt Cosmar und berät die Curischen Erbinnen. Minister Stein betreibt die Entschädigung durch das Amt Sokolnik und hat den Kanzler hinzugezogen, damit beide sie dem Kaiser vorschlagen. Triebenfeld will ein Memoire zur Curischen Sache und zum Militärgehalt einreichen. Triebenfeld bittet dringend um 100 # per Estaffette.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
After seven months Cosmar has been instructed to pay the money from three years, of which 12/m Rthl a year is assigned to the creditors, into the High Court at Brzeg as quickly as possible and to render account. Stägemann defends Cosmar and advises the Curische heiresses. Minister Stein is pursuing compensation through the office of Sokolnik and has brought in the Chancellor, so that both may propose it to the Emperor. Triebenfeld intends to submit a memorandum on the Curische affair and on his military salary. Triebenfeld urgently asks for 100 # by express courier.

Most Serene Prince

Gracious Lord!

After 7 months the order is now at last given that Cosmar shall pay in, with the utmost despatch, to the Wrocław [uncertain: Brzeg] Higher Provincial Court the 3 years' monies he has behind [uncertain: behind] him, which Your Serene Highness is to draw annually, and of which You have assigned 12/m Rthl yearly in favour of Your creditors — He is at the same time instructed to keep order and to render prompt account — That Stägemann bears the worst will is shown at every turn; he now plays the defender of Cosmar and the adviser of the Curish heiresses — The Prince Chancellor, on the other hand, promises everything — but reads, sees and hears nothing further of it once he has promised it — Minister Stein, by contrast, works honestly and takes care to procure for Your Serene Highness the proposed compensation by way of the office of Sokolnik. He has already drawn the Chancellor into the affair, so that the two of them may lay the matter before the Emperor together — Tomorrow I shall again submit to the Prince a somewhat forceful memorandum concerning the Curish affair, as well as concerning Your military salary — I shall push these matters through, even should I have to follow him as far as the Rhine — It must once for all be brought to clarity — Tomorrow morning I have a conference with Stägemann, consequently more on Tuesday. This very moment I receive Your Serene Highness's letter of the 9th; on that too I shall write by the first post. In reverence I remain Your Serene Highness's. As Lieutenant-Colonel v. F. tells me, Adolph conducted himself very awkwardly before the King; the King [uncertain: asked] him what his father was doing, how he was faring, and the answer was — I do not know, I have not seen him for a long time.
