--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A wounded point-by-point defence against the Prince's reproaches: General Schüler and Count Nessetrock will not act, so help must come through the State Chancellor and Minister Humboldt; two French blank forms are wanted for a fresh appeal to the Emperor. Triebenfeld denies fault for the contradiction between the Prince's own figure of 79,000 Rthl in estate revenue and the officials' 36,000, Hahn valuing the estates at 2 millions; he rejects any secret deal with the Courland heirs over the 112,000 Rthl advance, asks to be replaced, and names Hahn as administrator.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Your Serene Highness's letter of the 7th of this month I received this very hour. Although it is true that I wrote to Schlawenschitz only the day before yesterday, the content of your letter is nonetheless so important that I hasten to bring certain matters into the light which threaten danger even to my honour, the more so [uncertain: as] in three letters already received from Your Serene Highness a very cold tone prevails. I therefore give myself the honour of answering first the latter point, point by point. As concerns then, firstly, Mr. General Schüler, he will not involve himself in the matter, and that in order not to make enemies of the Poles etc. — His last answer was: Should the Emperor ask me, well then I shall see what can be done. — Even Count Nessetrock shrugs his shoulders. I have therefore no other help in this matter to expect than through the Prince Chancellor and through Mr. Minister Humboldt. I have asked Your Serene Highness for 2 French blank forms, in order, should the Prince command it, to write once more to the Emperor. Mr. Court Councillor Barbe will draw it up, but he advises that the Prince's commands be complied with punctually. As concerns, secondly, the error regarding the yield of the estates, this — as I have already written to Mr. Court Councillor Stahn — will probably have no further consequences than that it marks an ugly contradiction, for which neither the Court Councillor nor I am at fault. Your Serene Highness yourself gives me the revenues as 79000 Rthl; this I took as the standard in the first submission. — But the officials now set these down as low as 36000 Rthl, and I should very much have wished not to have had this extract appended to the record, because the difference is too striking. That for the rest the officials have either stated it incorrectly or managed badly can only be the case. — I am entirely at ease with the statements of Mr. Court Councillor Hahn, all of which lie before His Serene Highness the Prince Chancellor from A to Z. He gives the value of the estates as 2 millions and their yield as 60000 Rthl, which in better times, however, must turn out much higher. Since even the Privy

Councillor of State von Stägemann himself holds him to be an expert man, no further disadvantage is to be feared. — That I should gladly have seen Your Serene Highness's statement of the revenue of 79000 Rthl supported in the record by at least 70000 is surely not to be censured. Given the distance and the tavern-like course of the posts I could not know that Mr. Court Councillor Stahn had already departed for Brieg, for otherwise I should not have written at that time at all. I am content, and am only too gladly convinced that the estates have funds enough to discharge the 112000 Rthl in 5 or 6 years — and so I fall reverently silent upon this passage, the more so as the aforementioned [uncertain: error] will now never have consequences and everything has already been forestalled. It does however strike me as remarkable why so much fuss is made over this matter. Did I state the revenues? — One would make a beast of burden of me, that I am to bear the blame for it; did I know the yield of the estates? Your Serene Highness gave it to me thus — your word was sacred to me, upon this foundation I raised the building. — Had I shown Hahn Your Serene Highness's statement at that time, he would perhaps have refuted it, and he would, as an upright man and as a faithful [uncertain: beginner], certainly have advised me faithfully and truly. In short, neither he nor I am at fault in the said error, in which meanwhile I, no more than Court Councillor Hahn, wish to be left exposed. As concerns the advance of the 112000 Rthl, it will be given, unless all of it be lies — thus I have lain with it from grace to grace — only this no one can lie about or impute to me, that I meant to settle the affair with the Courland heirs in secret without the knowledge of the mandatories. It is outrageous to me that for all my exertions I am now taken besides

for a rogue or a total blockhead.

At bottom I do not know whether it were so great an honour to boast of such a settlement; I have therefore advised that the King himself should never have done this, but rather that Your Serene Highness should have the advance of the 11th of this month furnished for the purpose.

I had to lay down my pen. — Your Serene Highness has hurt me very much by this letter; I believe I have now had occasion to read into your heart, and what I may promise myself of the future. Unjust suspicion brands the best of men, and still more, too often, the best of friends. — Reproach and humiliation can drive him to fury. Your Serene Highness appears to wish to burden me with it — this I cannot and am not able to bear. You have moreover let this be made known to me through a stranger's hand — this is against my sense of honour and humiliates me too much. I have said and done nothing but what stands in the rules given and sent to me. I have exerted everything to bring the affair to the desired goal. Much has already succeeded, and the rest would have been achieved too; that His Serene Highness the Prince State Chancellor lays everything aside and does not answer at once what I submit, for that I cannot be held to account. — I hardly see him even once, which fate thousands share with me; it has been enough for me hitherto that everything Barbe hands to him in Your Serene Highness's affairs he has received with goodwill and gracious assurances, and has at once passed it on in writing to the reporting official. The funds said to lie in readiness, which I mentioned in my submissions, are named for this reason, that one should form a good idea of the value of the entailed estate's properties. This, however, leads to nothing and is a figure of speech. — Meanwhile they are often mentioned in the pieces only handed in. — Yet I never intended that any other than the lawful use should be made of them, which was that your creditors should draw the 54500 Rthl, or whatever it amounts to, in accordance with your instruction. Still less did I intend to impair F. August's 100000 Rthl or his quiet life. Whether for the rest the settlements and codicils will be permitted to stand I do not know. — Your Serene Highness appears not to have noticed that I have not censured the same in any of my submissions since the first.

my concern was to prove that Your Serene Highness had undertaken to satisfy the Courland heirs alone — this was found at last in the Cosmarian declaration which only Mr. Court Councillor von Hahn sent hither. For the rest I repeat once more that I cannot compel His Serene Highness the Prince State Chancellor to any swift decision — and that I must leave it to my accursed ill fortune whether Your Serene Highness will [uncertain: obtain] the money for the payment of the debts or not. — I have nothing further to do here than to attend to your affairs daily and hourly. — Be pleased therefore to send someone here with the utmost speed who will take over the business so well set in train; until then I shall continue to work faithfully and so far as lies in my powers. — Forgive it, Your Serene Highness, if I recall that scene where you sent Hoathausen to Kalisz, had the mortgage books revised, and where the fellow (perhaps in his cups) had spoken of having me arrested — this is nearly the second instance; against the third I should gladly secure myself. With a bleeding heart I shall remember the time lost. I have nothing and do not know here what I am to live on — but my pride, most gracious Lord, you shall not break with your reproach; I have acted too honestly towards you and sacrificed everything — never did I dream of one-sided arrangements with the [uncertain: Künstl] heirs, and I regret that you were willing to bestow your confidence on me if you believed me to be the [illegible]. Finally I must still note here that when I was asked yesterday whom you might wish for as administrator, I named Court Councillor Hahn. — I am to obtain instructions from Your Serene Highness on this point also. With the utmost possible esteem and respect I am Your Serene Highness's

Vienna, the 15th of February

1815.

wholly obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld
