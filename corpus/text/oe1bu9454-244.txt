--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A report from the Congress of Vienna: the great review of troops, the King of Württemberg's absence from the saddle and his withheld constitution, the warmth between the two Emperors and the Prussian King, a deputation of Princes and Estates to Prince Metternich seeking the Emperor of Austria as their head, Saxony, and Holland's new royal title. On Poland nothing is settled, though Baron von Humboldt is helping the Prince's cause; Privy Councillor von Gärtner is ill. Triebenfeld urges pressing the Brieg High Provincial Court to annul an 1811 settlement and awaits instructions.

Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

The monarchs have remained here and the great manoeuvre has taken place here in Vienna; I was not able to take part in it or to see it, yet it is said to have surpassed everything in elegance, the Emperor as our King. Grand Duke Constantin etc. have received regiments in the Austrian [uncertain: Armina] — each of whom also led them past at the manoeuvre. Only the King of Württemberg was not on horseback and drove (as I myself saw en passant) in a carriage drawn by 8 horses, and indeed in an Oriental turn-out; the truly allied Sovereigns being on horseback, he was likewise eliminated from the manoeuvre. The cordiality between the two Emperors and our King, which was shown at every turn, has surpassed all belief. Our King, after the various toasts, stepped out onto the balcony, had a filled glass of champagne handed to him, and drank aloud to the whole body of all brave soldiers — a many-thousandfold thanks, accompanied by many millions of foreign tears, was rendered to the King for this even by the oldest bearded warriors, and it seemed [uncertain: on some] all haste between Austria and Prussia, which formerly was imbibed with the mother's milk, to have been released in one day and vanished forever; in unison they [illegible] the 4 might to the welfare of mankind, and only the ill-thinking man will [illegible] youths and many a child [uncertain: restored] look toward the future, which the well-thinking man today faces so hopefully and gladly. The King of Württemberg is said still not to come forth with the constitution which he means to lay before the allied powers for examination; it has [text lost]

the appearance as though he would miss the crossing. Several Princes of the Empire and all 4 Estates have agreed among themselves to send a deputation to Prince Metternich and to petition him that the Emperor of Austria should again come forward as their head, to whom they would willingly and gladly submit themselves if their ancient rights were restored; they would then form a combined power which could scarcely have an equal [uncertain: in the world] — Prince Metternich has referred the Estates to patience in this matter and has informed them that for the present it is not yet advisable to come forward with it.

Concerning Saxony much is spoken — but it will probably remain with the resolutions long since taken.

Concerning Poland nothing is yet determined; meanwhile the [uncertain: most upright] man assures me that, in so far as the matter concerns Your Serene Highness, they [illegible] you the best. The minister Baron von Hum. is working here to best effect, because the matter interests him himself. That moreover Your Serene Highness will yet be able to lead a quiet, happy life at Schlawenschitz, and that you will shortly see your chief enemy there trodden into the dust — for that, most gracious Prince, I can vouch to you. Only the greatest discretion has been enjoined upon me, because there is yet many a stone of offence to be cleared from the way — but the fairest morning is already dawning. The good Privy Councillor von Gärtner, who lays himself most respectfully at Your Serene Highness's feet, has been very ill for several days; as soon as he is [uncertain: recovered], I shall [uncertain: obtain] from him much essential news. With [illegible] everything is [illegible]; on account of the many affairs I must be very [uncertain: watchful]. With impatience I await a letter from Your Serene Highness, so that I may take my measures accordingly — unwillingly would I leave lying the work now brought into the best train, and only the greatest necessity could bring me to it; now still this, which, when the Chancellor of State, Mr. Hardenberg, [makes] a report concerning Cosmar

one [may reckon] on the Brieg High Provincial Court, particularly on account of the settlement of 1811 which is to be annulled, with especial confidence, and would it not be advisable, in due time, to send someone thither who might guide and press the matter quietly? I shall be most necessary, as I am instructed what ought first to happen, [and shall] give Your Serene Highness notice of every outcome, so that one may also [act] there. In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's — The Sovereign Prince of Holland is assuming the title of King — Vienna, the 18th of October

1814.
