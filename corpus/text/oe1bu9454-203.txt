--- SUMMARY (German) ---
v. Triebenfeld übersendet das Weigel übergebene Exposée zum geplanten Tausch der Güter eines v. Knobelsdorff gegen Pohlnische Güter. Laut Fischbach bleiben den Knobelsdorffschen Gütern netto 310000 Rthl; den Zagorower Gütern (Wert etwa 540000 Rthl, Schulden 247000 Rthl) bleiben 293000 Rthl. Fischbach will den Tausch gegen 1 1/2 Prozent vermitteln. Vielleicht übernimmt Knobelsdorff auch die Trąbczyner Güter samt Schulden und führt den Prozess selbst zu Ende. Triebenfeld bittet um Befehl, ob beide nach Sławięcice kommen sollen.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Von Triebenfeld sends the statement given to Weigel concerning the planned exchange of the estates of a von Knobelsdorff for Polish estates. According to Fischbach, the Knobelsdorff estates are left with a net 310,000 Rthl; the Zagórów estates (worth about 540,000 Rthl, debts 247,000 Rthl) are left with 293,000 Rthl. Fischbach is willing to arrange the exchange for 1 1/2 percent. Knobelsdorff may perhaps also take over the Trąbczyn estates together with their debts and see the lawsuit through himself. Triebenfeld asks for orders on whether both of them should come to Sławięcice.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

To Your Most Serene Highness I transmit herewith the exposé which I gave to Mr Weigel; he lives and breathes in this affair, and declares that he means the Knobelsdorff estates. I shall now set the further steps in train with Hainen[?] as well, so that he too may be brought round to give his fiat to everything — I believe, however, that Weigel will this time act without him; meanwhile it is well when every stone of offence has been cleared out of the way. My endeavour now goes only to scraping together 500 Rthl, in order to depart on the 11th of this month for Zagórów and there to put everything in such order as to be able to [uncertain: lay] the matter open and unimpeachable before Mr von Knobelsdorff.

Concerning Świniary I am in negotiation with the merchant Wiener as also with the merchant Schiller — Tesmar writes from Berlin very mystically — He says one would surely be satisfied — at bottom that is nothing but empty noise — May God only grant blessing and prosperity to my plans, for then Your Serene Highness may laugh at all the fools along with me. My deepest reverence to your children. Ever, however, Your Most Serene Highness's

Świniary, the 2nd of August.

1810

faithful and most humble and obedient servant

v Triebenfeld

As Mr Eberhard assures me, there is likewise not one true little word in the estate exchange with Strachwitz that has been so Jewishly cried up. Strachwitz too does not know a syllable of it.

Some time ago Fischbach gave me the accompanying valuations of estates belonging to a certain von Knobelsdorff, who wishes to exchange them against Polish estates — The large valuations appear to have been made up by himself, but the small ones to have been extracted by Fischbach from the Landschaft valuation. Fischbach says Knobelsdorff will let the estates stand with 70,000 Rthl of debts, thereon 255,000 Rthl in mortgage bonds, 135,000 Rthl in private debts; this amounts to = 390,000 Rthl, there remains to the good = 310,000 Rthl. The Zagórów estates are said to yield about 27,000 Rthl, which would be at a value of 540,000 Rthl.

thereon stand debts

1. the Invalids' Fund 50,000 Rthl

2. the Oppenheimer claim together with arrears of interest 115,000 Rthl.

3. Weigelt 50,000 Rthl.

4. Seher Thos 8,000 Rthl.

5. Onera perpetua about 1,200 Rthl. Good capital — 24,000 Rthl. makes capital 247,000 Rthl.

remains to the good in value 293,000 Rthl.

The Knobelsdorff estates would thus [uncertain: have] a higher value by 17,000 Rthl. Fischbach offers to bring this exchange of estates about, if it is conducive to Your Serene Highness's interest. For his trouble he demands 1 1/2 per cent. May it please Your Serene Highness to issue your commands by this express, whether Fischbach is to come with Knobelsdorff to Sławięcice. I further remark overleaf

v Triebenfeld

the 17,000 Rthl by which the Knobelsdorff estates are of greater worth will be covered by the outstanding arrears, as also by the [uncertain: possible] shortfalls in the 27,000 Rthl of revenues which in these bad times may be expected to occur — Perhaps Knobelsdorff will then also take the Trąbczyn estates, with the debts standing upon them as well as the other debts, and will himself carry the lawsuit through to the end.

But may it please Your Serene Highness to state your opinion upon everything thoroughly, so that I may [uncertain: early] give the people their answer and direct them to Sławięcice — Otherwise I might again be saddled by the most highly trusted one with new honorary titles.
