--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports sending Weigel an exposé and working on Hainen's consent, and needing 500 Rthl to travel to Zagorowo on the 11th. Negotiations over Schweinern with the merchants Wiener and Schiller continue; rumours about the Lausch and Strachnitz estates are baseless. Fischbach proposes exchanging von Knobelsdorff's estates for the Zagorowo estates, with valuations and debts set out (247,000 Rthl against Zagorowo, 390,000 Rthl against Knobelsdorff's), for 1.5 per cent commission; asks whether Fischbach and Knobelsdorff should come to Schlawenschitz.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

To Your Most Serene Highness I transmit herewith the exposé which I have given to Mr. Weigel; he lives and breathes in this affair, and declares that he means the Knobelsdorff estates. I shall now set the further steps in motion with Hainen as well, so that he too may be brought round to give his fiat to everything — I believe, however, that Weigel will this time act without him; in the meanwhile it is well if every stone of offence is cleared out of the way. My endeavour is now directed only to scraping together 500 Rthl, in order to set out on the 11th of this month for Zagorowo and there to arrange everything so that the affair may be laid open and blameless before [uncertain: mitem] at Mr. von Knobelse's place.

Concerning Schweinern I am in negotiation with the merchant Wiener as also with the merchant Schiller — Tismar writes very mystically from Berlin – He says one would certainly be satisfied — at bottom this is nothing but empty noise. May God only grant blessing and prosperity to my plans, and then Your Serene Highness may laugh at all the fools along with me. My deepest reverence to your children. Ever, however, Your Most Serene Highness's

Schweinern, the 2nd of August.

1810

faithful most humble and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld

As Mr. von Eberhardt assures me, there is likewise not one true little word in the Jewishly praised estates Lausch with Strachnitz. Strachard too does not know a syllable of it. Some time ago Fischbach gave me the enclosed valuations of estates belonging to a certain von Knobelsdorff, who wishes to exchange them against Polish estates. The large figures appear to have been made up by himself, the small ones however to have been extracted by Fischbach from the Landschaft valuation.
