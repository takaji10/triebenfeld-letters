--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Heads of purchase for the noble estate of Witów in the Warta district of the Kalisz department, sold by forest inspector Christian Friedrich Rochlitz for the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen to Martin Honrichs and Walęty von Kwasiborski for 16,000 Rthl in three instalments at 5 per cent, with no warranty for 1016 składy claimed by the burgesses of Warta, a fallback one-year lease at 800 Rthl, and a 1000 Rthl penalty; appended is the Prince's cession of 8,000 Rthl to the Breslau banker Carl Ferdinand Weigel, attested by the St Vincent foundation justice office.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
E. W. von Rösch. Between the special authorised representative of His Most Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, the forest inspector Rochlitz, as seller on the one part, and Martin Honrichs and Valentin von Kwasieborski, as purchasers on the other part, there has, for the purpose of a formal judicial contract of purchase and sale to be concluded hereafter concerning the noble estate of Witów, been well-advisedly agreed and concluded under today's date the following heads of agreement.

The said forest inspector Rochlitz sells, in the name of His Most Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, the aforesaid his principal, the noble estate situated in the Royal South Prussian Kalisz Government Department and in its Warta district, namely the village and the village mayor's holding of Witów with all appurtenant rights and privileges, nothing excepted therefrom, as the same was, pursuant to the Most High deed of donation of the most blessed King Frie-

drich Wilhelm the Second, conferred upon Colonel von Schlieben, and was conveyed by his widow and heirs, pursuant to the judicial contract of sale of 22 October 1799, to His Most Serene Highness; to the said Martin Honrichs and Valentin von Kwasieborski, and that for a purchase price well-advisedly agreed and determined between the contracting parties of 16000 rt, written out sixteen thousand Reichsthaler Prussian Courant. 2: This purchase price the purchasers bind themselves to pay in solidum, that is to say, the one for both and both for the one, to His Most Serene Highness in the following instalments, namely at St John the Baptist of the current year 4000 Rt, say four thousand Reichsthaler, at Christmas of the current year 6000 rtl., say six thousand Reichsthaler, and at Midsummer 1804 the remainder with 6000 rt., say six thousand Reichsthaler, but until the full payment of the arrears to pay interest on the same at 5 per cent.

3. His Most Serene Highness grants the purchasers warranty of title only for those parcels actually held in possession and to be handed over to them at the

delivery of possession; with regard to those 1016 składy of arable land which may be recovered at law by the burgesses of Warta and which are not in the possession of His Most Serene Highness, no warranty of title is granted, but it remains the purchasers' business alone to vindicate the arable land in question wholly at their own risk and cost, against which, however, His Most Serene Highness thereupon relinquishes all claims against the purchasers, if they should actually win these składy of arable land from the burgesses of Warta either wholly or in part.

4. The handing over of this sold estate to the purchasers shall take place 14 days after Midsummer of the current year, at which time also, simultaneously and reciprocally, the first instalment of the 4000 rtll. must be paid, because before this is done the handing over cannot take place.

5. At this handing over there shall likewise be handed over the [uncertain: ground to be lost] and the cattle and implements which are found on the estate

and are specified in the judicial recess of transfer on the part of the tenant Knoblauch to von Bloch of Blotniz of the 10th and 11th of July of the past year, exclusive of the surplus inventory in horses and oxen taken away at that time by Knoblauch pursuant to the recess of 11 July, which for that very reason are no longer to be found on the estate, and for that very reason cannot be handed over as well.

6. The seller promises to procure the consent to the purchasers' acquisition of this estate if possible at the latest by St John of the current year; should it, however, not be forthcoming, then the sale in question falls through.

7. Should the final resolution concerning the said consent be delayed longer than 14 days beyond St John of the current year, in such wise that the handing over should already have taken place and the first instalment of the purchase price should already have been paid, but the resolution in question should then turn out

in the negative, then the contracting parties have agreed, to avoid all [uncertain: disputes] concerning the revenues raised, that the purchasers shall retain this estate in possession for one year, namely until 14 days after St John's Day 1804, by way of lease, but shall on the other hand pay for this year a lease sum of 800 rtl., say eight hundred Reichsthaler, to the seller, and shall also discharge all public dues concerning this estate, by whatever name they may be called, upon their own responsibility, without being permitted to bring anything to account against the seller for them; and in this last case the purchasers renounce each and every other remission and other demands, by whatever name they may be called, but bind themselves, under the readiest execution, to vacate the estate of Witów 14 days after St John 1804 in good husbandry condition, and submit themselves in this

regard to the process of execution with all its legal consequences; yet it is of course understood that in this case, upon the vacating of the estate, the 4000 rtl. paid must be repaid to them by the seller, after deduction of the stipulated lease rent of 800 rt and of such public dues and costs as they could not show to have been paid at their departure.

8. The estate shall be handed over to the purchasers with the winter and summer sowing fully put in, and the purchasers herewith declare that they accept the winter sowing as duly put in, and only await the like with regard to the summer crop.

9. The documents concerning this estate shall be handed out to the purchasers immediately upon the arrival of the consent to their acquisition [uncertain: Frühige].

10.

10. Finally, the contracting parties renounce all objections and evasions running counter to these heads of purchase, by whatever name they may be called, in particular the purchasers renounce the objection of injury in and beyond the half, and in the event of non-performance or [uncertain: non-fulfilment] of this contract they set a vadium or conventional penalty of 1000 rtll, say one thousand Reichsthaler, which the contravening party shall be bound to pay to the other, and nevertheless to perform the contract.

11. The costs of this contract both parties assume by halves; those of the settlement of the titulus possessionis in the purchasers' names, the latter alone.

Both parties have, after they themselves had read through these heads of purchase, which were explained to the co-purchaser von Kwasiborski by his partner Honrichs in the Polish tongue, upon prior

Done as above. Christian Friedrich Rochlitz. Martin Honrichs.
