--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Acknowledges receipt by courier of an instruction to pay 200 M, three French Blanquette and various letters, but pleads weakness and headache and defers a fuller reply. Renews the request that Eberhard and Hahn come to Vienna; reports Stägemann still without audience, the high estates' affair handed on with its documents, a letter written to Zerboni on Beugelin's advice, and Cosmar's ruin. Notes warnings from Bastenstein, Göschel and Wintzingerode against August's power of attorney, Pourtalès's silence amid the Neuchâtel deputations, Napoleon's landing at Antibes and march on Toulon, Masséna's discontent, Murat, and the King of Saxony's refusal to sign; encloses papers on the King of Würtemberg and the Hohenlohe.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord. Your Serene Highness's most gracious letter of the 6th I received by express courier about an [text lost — "vor etwan einer": a noun is missing] ago; as I had just begun a letter to Your Most High Self, I set that aside and begin this one. The instruction to pay 200 M I received first of all; then there lay enclosed 3 French Blanquette, the 2 letters from Your Serene Highness, one from Herr [uncertain: H]trath Hahn, and 5 servants' [letters], to Russia, from Poland, to [uncertain: Breiten], and from Frankfurt on the Main. I think of it most heartily. But I can write little today — for I am very weak and have a great headache, and it is also scarcely 2 hours until the departure of the post — I have therefore not even been able to read everything, and shall write the greater part by the next post. — My wish I only renew, that Herr Eberhard and Herr Hahn might come here. Much would certainly be accomplished with the 3 [uncertain: Darnte]; I also continue to believe that the business with P[rince] Adolph is going equally well. Stägemann has still had no audience, and one might well perish of vexation over it. The high estates' affair I have handed over once more to the Prince H[?] Durch with all the documents in the case; I also brought myself, on the advice of the Privy Councillor of State v. Beugelin, to write to Zerboni; the ["Anwert": reading uncertain — answer? application?] I enclose herewith. By the next post I shall send the Copy of all the poor ["Armen"] petitions — the Prince has taken it well, but it has also already passed the Journal; but where it will again come to rest, I do not yet know. — The people are all here, save the single Prince alone, as though they should only trouble themselves, and the sufferer comes ["komt ſchien um": corrupt] — Meanwhile all stands well, for Cosmar is, as everyone says, quite as good as annihilated by savage enemies on account of my candid letter — I have made myself some [enemies], but what do I care for that, so long as You alone are saved. — August must only demand the power of attorney in the prescribed manner; I have set him a spur in the side in that matter, whereby he will be mightily assisted. Yesterday not only E. Bastenstein, who commends himself to Your Serene Highness, but also Göschel wrote to the Prince of Langenburg that in the Hardz house they had heard him speak very harshly of August, because he had joined himself with a most intriguing advocate against

his old good father, and had given the advocate an unlimited power of attorney, by virtue of which he must lay everything of the old good Prince's under arrest and diminish it — one does not know whether this be from want of prudence or from malice and favourable times. Wintzingerode does the same, and warns them to recall the power of attorney, not to ruin themselves, and to turn away from themselves the judgement of the first men of Europe. I think this must work strongly; the poor gentleman is held to be very weak. — Pourtalès has not yet answered. It is believed that he will arrive here at the earliest. For the sureties and receivers of Neuchâtel have sent a deputation with grave complaints against their Councillor of State to the King — the Councillor of State is now also to send a deputation here on his side — and since Pourtalès is known both in this council and in Heylen [uncertain reading], guests as a serving officer, he will probably appear in person as well. He so needs support. — Now the great and important matter — that Napoleon has escaped, Your Serene Highness knows. — According to today's news he has landed at Antibes and is turning towards Toulon. — At Toulon stands Masséna, who is very much displeased that he was not appointed a Pair — then there is also there the General v. Bertherand and Gen. Molis, all adherents of Napoleon. — In Switzerland everything is in ferment; that will make a favourable [uncertain: Märte] , and Murat is, I fear, blustering frightfully — in order to press through from that side by way of Italy. — It is already said that N. has much future there. — The King of Saxony still refuses to sign. — Wellington and Talleyrand are still with him at Pressburg. — Meanwhile it is believed that God will accordingly prevent the war. — What the King of Würtemberg has issued to the Hohenlohe, what has been answered him from all sides, what in that case has been arranged here at the Congress, all lies here enclosed. From the Congress a strong Mercuriale has been given to the King; that Your Serene Highness shall receive hereafter. — In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Serene Highness's Vienna, the 11th of March 1815, most faithful and most obedient servant v Triebenfeld
