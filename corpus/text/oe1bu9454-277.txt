--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A hurried report from the Congress, written with a headache two hours before the post: acknowledges an order to pay 200 M, French blank forms and enclosed letters; hopes Eberhard and Hahn will come; Stägemann still has no audience, the estates business has gone back to the Prince with its papers, and Zerboni's reply is enclosed. Urges August to demand a power of attorney in proper form, reporting criticism of his lawyer's arrest of his father's property. Adds news of Napoleon's landing near Antibes and march on Toulon.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord, I received Your Serene Highness's most gracious letter of the 6th by express courier about one [text lost] ago; as I had just begun a letter to Your Most High Self, I laid that aside and began this one. The order to pay 200 M I received first; then there lay in it 3 French blank forms, the 2nd letter from Your Serene Highness, one from Mr. [uncertain: Court] Councillor Hahn, and 5 [uncertain: servants], to Russia, from Poland, to [uncertain: Breiten], and from Frankfurt an der Oder and Main. I thank you most heartily for it. But I can write little today - for I am very weary and have a great headache; also it is only some 2 hours yet until the post goes out - I have therefore not even been able to read everything, and shall write the greater part by the next post - I only renew my wish that Mr. Eberhard and Mr. Hahn might come here. Much would certainly be accomplished with the 3 [uncertain: Darnte]; also I continue to believe that the business with Prince Adolph will go forward at once. Stägemann has still had no audience for his report, and one might perish of vexation over it. The high estates affair I have once more handed over to the Prince, His Serene Highness, with all the documents in the file; also, on the advice of the Privy Councillor of State von Beugelin, I brought myself to write to Zerboni; the answer I enclose herewith. By the next post I shall send the copy of all the poor petitions - the Prince has received it well, and it has also already passed the register, but where it will come to lie idle again I do not yet know - The people are all here except for the single Prince, as though they would only trouble themselves, and the one who suffers seems to be [illegible] - Meanwhile all stands well, for Cosmar is, as everyone says, as good as annihilated - on account of my candid [uncertain: Switzerland] fierce enemies.

I have made a few [enemies] for myself, but what do I care for that, so long as you are only saved - August must only demand the power of attorney in the prescribed form; I have set him a spur in the side for that, in which he will be mightily assisted. Yesterday not only E. Bastenstein, who commends himself to Your Serene Highness, but also Göschel wrote to the Prince of Langenburg, that in the Hardz house they have heard him spoken of very harshly on August's account, because he has joined himself with a most intriguing advocate against</p>

his old good father, and has given the advocate an unlimited power of attorney, by virtue of which he must place everything belonging to the old good Prince under arrest and diminish it - one does not know whether this comes from a want of prudence or from malice and favourable times. Winzingerode does the like and warns them to withdraw the power of attorney, not to bring themselves to ruin and not to set aside the judgement of the first men of Europe. I think that should work strongly; the poor gentleman is held to be very weak. Poustales has not yet answered. It is credibly reported that they will arrive here very shortly, for the burgesses and collectors of Neucheten have sent a deputation with great complaints against their councillor of state to the King - the councillor of state is now also to send a deputation here on his own part - and since Poartelles is known in this council and [uncertain: Heylen], as a guest and as a serving officer, he too will probably appear in person. He needs support so much.

Now the great and important matter - that Napoleon has escaped, Your Serene Highness knows - According to today's news he has landed at Antiber and is turning towards Toulon - In Toulon stands Massen, who is much displeased that he was not made a peer — then there is also the General von Bertherand and General Molis, all adherents of Napoleon - In Switzerland everything is in ferment, that will become a favourable [uncertain: market], and Murat is said to be arming himself dreadfully – in order to press through from that side by way of Italy - It is already said that N. has much future there — the King of Saxony still refuses to sign - Wellington and Talleyrand are still with him in Preiberg - Meanwhile it is believed that God will accordingly prevent the war - What the King of Württemberg has issued to the Hohenlohes, what has been answered to him from all sides, what has in that case been set up here at the Congress, all lies here; from the Congress a strong reprimand has been given to the King, which Your Serene Highness shall receive in future — In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's

Vienna, the 11th of March

1815

most faithful and most obedient servant
v. Triebenfeld
