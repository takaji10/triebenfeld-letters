Durchlauchtigster Fürst
Gnädigster Fürst und Herr!
Noch habe ich die Reise nach Pehlen nicht antreten können, der Mangel an Gelde legt mich
dermalen noch die Einzige Hindernis im Wege. Ich hoffe diese, obgleich kostspielig,
dennoch in Kurzem zu beheben und den reise ich so gleich. Die Auszählen sind nicht
übel ja ich hoffe die Sache soll glänzender enden, als es Anfangs schien. Ein Dritten,
außer Knobelsdorff und Strachwitz, am Mann dem Ewr Durchlaucht sehr genau kennen, und
auf den Sie nie rathen würden, will auch täuschen. Lieste der Himmel dies geschehen
den wäre das Glück über groß —
Übrigens muß ich in Ewr Durchlaucht Angelegenheiten oft den Leidens Kelch bis auf die Hoofen
leeren. So solte ich dieser Tage wegen die Schlieben ausgepfändet werden. Ich mußte
mir gefallen laßen, daß man Arrest auf meine Schwännersche Pacht Gelder, die mir nach
den Zinsen bleiben, schlug, bis die Sache, nach dem Versprechen des General v. Köckritz, von
Potsdam aus wiederöfen wird - ich kan inzwischen aus meine Güter nicht einen denien
ziehen, demohngeachtet schickte man nur Execution um 48 Rthl 6 g. Kosten und wolte mich
auspfänden laßen, ich hatte kaum einen d Münze im Hause,  ich wies in der Angst
dem Executor Schock an dem p Lombardini, dieser jagte den Menschen mit dem
Ausdruck fort.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Lack of money still prevents the journey to Pehlen, though prospects in the affair look better than expected, with a third deceiver besides Knobelsdorff and Strachwitz. Reports distraint over the Schlieben matter, arrest of his Schwänner lease-monies pending reopening from Potsdam on General v. Köckritz's promise, and Lombardini's refusal to pay the executor Schoch 48 Rthl 6 g; begs the Prince to order payment. Adds news of royal manoeuvres near Potsdam and Köpnick, Kuralien, Labaye's Paris contribution offer, jägers along the Elbe, confiscations at Hamburg, arrests, and Kalckreuth's declining standing.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

I have still not been able to set out on the journey to Pehlen; the want of money lays before me at present the sole remaining obstacle in the way. I hope, costly as it is, nevertheless to remove this shortly, and then I shall travel at once. The [uncertain: reckonings] are not bad; indeed I hope the matter will end more brilliantly than it seemed at first. A third party, besides Knobelsdorff and Strachwitz, a man whom Your Serene Highness knows very well, and upon whom you would never guess, also wishes to [uncertain: deceive / take part]. Were Heaven to let this come to pass, then the good fortune would be over-great. — For the rest, in Your Serene Highness's affairs I must often drain the cup of suffering down to the dregs. Thus these days I was to be distrained upon on account of the Schlieben business. I had to submit to their laying an attachment upon my Schwänner lease monies, which remain to me after the interest, until the matter is reopened from Potsdam, according to the promise of General von Köckritz — meanwhile I cannot draw a single [uncertain: penny] from my estates, and notwithstanding this they sent execution only for 48 Rthl 6 g of costs and wished to have me distrained upon; I had scarcely a d of coin in the house, and in my distress I directed the bailiff Schock to Mr Lombardini, who drove the man off with the declaration:

that he had not the least thing to pay, either on his own account or on mine, still less upon my order, and would never hand over a heller; and, notwithstanding that I had asked Lombardini in writing to appease Schoch with but 3 Rthl., he showed the man the door and back to me.

This has pained me very much, and I beg Your Serene Highness to instruct Lombardini that he now absolutely pay over the 48 Rthl 6 g to the bailiff without any delay — I want and wish for nothing, only this one thing, that I not be branded.

I endure more than I am able to bear; only treatment of this kind I cannot endure. There is news here of various kinds, but weighty events are lurking in the very near distance. The King is now manoeuvring in and about Potsdam; often the manoeuvre is in the town itself, accompanied by a tremendous cannonade, whereby the glaziers are put in good sustenance. From Tuesday until the 6th the manoeuvres continue between Berlin and Köpnick — then the King [uncertain: builds/goes] again to Charlottenbourg, where he will stay some time and will daily visit the [uncertain: manoeuvre] which the King [illegible]. Kuralien has been staying in Berlin since his return from Paris and gives great [uncertain: entertainments] — not without reason. Labaye, who in Paris offered linen and iron for the contribution still to be paid, has not advanced a single step with it — whether he will negotiate anything good remains to be seen.

From Dessau onward the Elbe is occupied by riflemen, likewise from Magdeburg to Hamburg, where all ships are stopped and all colonial goods confiscated. According to Breslau gossip, 60000 French troops are to pass hurriedly through Berlin; whither is still unknown. — Some 30 persons have been arrested in [uncertain: Stärme] at the requisition of the French envoy, because they had spread the [uncertain: report] that it would come to a breach between France and Russia.

For the rest, matters are beginning to limp with Field Marshal Kalckreuth. One is growing lukewarm. It is believed that much will again change shortly. In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

most humble and faithful servant

von Triebenfeld

Breslau

the 5th of October

1810
