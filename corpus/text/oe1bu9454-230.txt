--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Der Fürst beklagt, dass Triebenfeld den selbst gesetzten Termin des 8. versäumt hat, mit Brzechffa zu ihm zu kommen. Da er aus den Gütern nichts bezieht, will er das Kaufinteresse des Generals von Schimmelpfennig an Zagórów nutzen, der Anfang März besichtigen will. Er verlangt bestimmte Antwort und Geldhilfe. Gegen die Verpachtung an Brzechffa hat er nichts, drängt aber auf Eile, da er beim König eine Kommission zur Regelung seiner Schulden erbeten hat.

Right Honourable Sir, Highly Honoured Sir, Councillor of War and Forests! It is today 12 days past the term which Your Right Honourable set for yourself, namely the 8th of this month, when you intended to arrive at my residence together with Mr von Brzechffa, without my having been informed by so much as a word as to the cause of this postponement. I waited for you from one day to the next in vain, and yet it is so urgent to take a course of action concerning my estates there, for which the imminent presence of my son with me — who will be here in a few days — is a new and pressing occasion. I cannot subsist any longer if I can continually draw nothing from these estates, and since I do not even know what I have to count upon in that regard, I must seek out the means which may yet afford me some repose. I therefore may not let the renewed eagerness of General von Schimmelpfennig for the acquisition of Zagórów grow cold. This gentleman intends to betake himself thither at the beginning of March to view it, and then soon, if we can come to an agreement, to [uncertain: settle] the business with me. I [uncertain: it seemed] to him today that I forewarned Your Right Honourable of this, [uncertain: awaited] your answer, and would then give him more particular word

The groom who brings you this will probably be with Your Right Honourable on the evening of the 22nd, and I expect him back at the latest on the 26th. By empty hopes and indefinite prospects I cannot let myself be held back from acting now with effect. I count in this upon your friendly co-operation, so that we may there proceed hand in hand in every undertaking. As soon as I receive your answer I shall take my further measures, and shall report them to Your Right Honourable again at once. I beg for a quite definite answer and news concerning your prospects and intentions, which must, however, lead to something speedily. If you can procure me considerable pecuniary assistance without delay I shall be most grateful, as I stand in the most pressing need of it. It is all the more necessary that I come soon to a resolution, upon which point you will kindly instruct me, since my son's stay will not be able to be of very long duration with me.

For the Parobek accept my thanks; he bore himself bravely at the first trial, but also [uncertain: turned pale], which however did him no harm. I still flatter myself that I shall see Your Right Honourable with me very soon, and it would pain me greatly

Your [Right Honourable] will therefore be pleased to [uncertain: convince] yourself from this state of affairs how necessary it is for me to possess exact and thorough knowledge of the true present condition of all my estates, and at the same time also to make those provisions in them which are needful, in order to be able to stand with honour before the said Commission as a truthful and upright man. These are roughly the grounds which have moved me to send Your Right Honourable the lieutenant [uncertain: binnen], although[text lost] I know that his presence may perhaps not be so agreeable to you as that of any other; however, since a man acquainted with the situation of those estates is absolutely required for this commission, I flatter myself Your Right Honourable will convince yourself of the [uncertain: Strügung] of my [illegible], at least I should not wish that this choice of mine should bring our good cause any [illegible] disadvantage, since I have received from the said Sommer the reliable assurance that in his commission he will act with the setting aside of every other consideration than that aimed solely at our common welfare. I therefore request Your [Right Honourable], as [uncertain: urgently] as most devotedly, to confer with him quite without reserve and candidly upon the matter, or else, which would be still more agreeable to me, to come hither with him, so that here, at greater leisure, we might take the matter into consideration. Awaiting with impatience a satisfactory result of your mutual deliberations, I have the honour to remain with the most perfect esteem.

Your Right Honourable has left my letter of the 19th of last month so entirely, or at least its principal subjects, unanswered and unfulfilled, that I cannot forbear to repeat it herewith, and once again urgently to beg both for the transmission of the Trapergner deed of gift and for notification of what sound project [uncertain: H.] and my Zagórów estates you intend, or what plan you propose to follow in their further administration, so that not only may these estates be preserved, but that I too may at last come to some enjoyment of revenues. Hitherto accustomed to yield unconditionally to every proposition made by Your Right Honourable, I am even now far from raising any objection whatever to the intended leasing of my estates to Mr von Brzechffa; only I must herewith report to Your Right Honourable without concealment that the state of affairs here with me has so far altered that so distant a postponement of the matter as you had the goodness to report to me might very easily bring about a complete cancellation of this business, and that consequently, if anything can be done at all, precisely now is still the decisive moment. For, since I, having been so often and so [uncertain: hastily] deceived even in my most just expectations, was no longer in a position to stem the impetuous pressing of my creditors and to alter my critical situation by my own powers, I have seen myself compelled to solicit the assistance of His Majesty the King, and I await within [uncertain: Körpern] the Commission petitioned for the regulation of my debt affairs

if my hopes in that regard should remain unfulfilled. Be pleased to accept the assurances of the esteem and attachment with which I shall ever remain.
