--- SUMMARY (English) ---
The Fürst complains that Triebenfeld has failed by twelve days to appear with H. v. Brzechsta as promised, and presses for a wholly definite answer before his son's imminent visit ends. General von Schimmelpfennig has renewed his interest in acquiring Zagorowo and will inspect it in early March; immediate pecuniary assistance is begged. Further sections, apparently from related drafts, demand the Trapergner deed of donation, a workable plan for the Zagorowo estates, and cooperation with the Lieutenant ahead of the royal debt-regulation Commission.

Right Honourable Sir, Highly Honoured Councillor of War and Forests! It is today 12 days past the term which Your Right Honourable set for yourself, namely the 8th of this month, when you intended to arrive at my house together with H. v. Brzechsta, without my having been informed by so much as a word as to the cause of this postponement. I waited for you in vain from one day to the next, and yet it is so pressing to take a course of action with regard to my estates there, to which the imminent presence of my son with me — who will be here in a few days — is a new and pressing occasion. I cannot subsist any longer if I can continue to draw nothing from these estates, and since I do not even know what I have to count upon in the matter, I must seek out the means which may yet afford me some repose. I therefore dare not let the renewed eagerness of Herr General von Schimmelpfennig for the acquisition of Zagorowo grow cold. This gentleman wishes to betake himself thither at the beginning of March to view the property, and then, if we can come to terms, soon to settle the business with me. It seemed to him today that I should apprise Your Right Honourable of this, await your answer, and then give him more particular word.

The groom who brings you this will in all likelihood be with Your Right Honourable on the evening of the 22nd, and I expect him back at the latest on the 26th. By empty hopes and indefinite prospects I cannot let myself be held back from acting now with vigour. I count in this upon your friendly co-operation, so that we may there go to work hand in hand in every undertaking. As soon as I receive your answer I shall take my further measures and report them again to Your Right Honourable at once. I beg for a quite definite answer and [uncertain: report] concerning your prospects and intentions, which however must lead to something speedily. If you can procure me considerable pecuniary assistance without delay I shall be much obliged, as I stand in the most pressing need of it. It is all the more necessary that I come soon to a resolution, upon which you will kindly instruct me, since my son's stay with me will not be able to last very much longer. For the Parobek accept my thanks; he bore himself bravely at the first trial, but was also [uncertain: bleached / bled], which however did him no harm. Still I flatter myself that I shall see Your Right Honourable with me quite soon, and it would grieve me greatly

Your [Right Honourable] will therefore be pleased, from this state of things, to [uncertain: persuade] yourself how necessary it is for me to obtain exact and thorough knowledge of the true present condition of all my estates, and at the same time also to take those precautions in them which are needful, in order to be able to stand with honour, as a truthful and upright man, before the aforesaid Commission. These, roughly, are the grounds which have moved me to send the Lieutenant to Your Honour [uncertain: binnen], although I know that his presence may perhaps not be so agreeable to you as that of some other; yet, since for this commission a man acquainted with the situation of those estates is absolutely required, I flatter myself that Your Honour will yourself be convinced of the [uncertain: urgency] of my [illegible], at least I should not wish that this choice of mine should bring any [uncertain: prejudice] to our good cause, since I have received from the said [uncertain: z Sommer] the reliable assurance that in his commission he will act setting aside every other consideration than that which aims solely at our common welfare. I therefore request Your Honour, as urgently as most humbly, to confer with him quite without reserve and candidly upon the matter, or rather — which would be still more welcome to me — to come hither with him, so that we might here, at greater leisure, take the matter into consideration. Awaiting with impatience a satisfactory result of your mutual deliberations, I have the honour to remain with the most perfect esteem.

Your Honour has left my letter of the 19th of last month so wholly, or at least its principal subjects, unanswered and unfulfilled, that I cannot forbear to repeat it herewith, and once more urgently to beg both for the transmission of the Trapergner deed of donation and for information as to what sound project you intend for H. and my Zagorowo estates, or what plan you propose to follow in their further administration, so that not merely may these estates be preserved, but that I too may at last come to some enjoyment of revenues. Accustomed hitherto to yield unconditionally to every proposition made by Your Honour, I am even now far from raising any objection to the intended leasing of my estates to Herr v. Brzechsta; only I must herewith report to Your Honour without concealment that the state of things here has so altered that so distant a postponement of the affair, as you had the goodness to report to me, could very easily bring about a complete dissolution of this business, and that consequently, if anything can be done at all, precisely now is still the decisive moment.
