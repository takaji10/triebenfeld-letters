--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Three sharp reminders from the Prince to Triebenfeld, who has failed to arrive with Mr von Brzechsta on the 8th and has left an earlier letter unanswered. The Prince, unable to live on estates that yield him nothing, demands the Trąbczyn deed of gift, a definite plan for Zagorowo's administration or lease to von Brechso, and swift money. He reports General von Schimmelpfennig's renewed interest in buying Zagorowo, a visit due in March, and a royal commission on his debts, for which Lieutenant Sommer is sent to inspect.

Right Honourable Sir, Highly Honoured Councillor of War and Forests! It is today 12 days past the term which Your Right Honourable set for yourself, namely the 8th of this month, when you intended to arrive here together with Mr. von Brzechsta, without my having been informed by so much as a word of the cause of this postponement. I waited for you from one day to the next in vain, and yet it is so pressing to take some course with regard to my estates there, to which the imminent presence of my son with me, who will be here in a few days, is a new and pressing occasion. I cannot subsist any longer if I can continue to draw nothing from these estates, and since I do not even know what I have to reckon upon in this matter, I must seek out the means which may yet afford me some repose. I may not therefore allow the renewed eagerness of General von Schimmelpfennig for the acquisition of Zagorowo to grow cold. This gentleman means to travel thither at the beginning of March to view it, and then, if we can come to terms, soon to settle the business with me. [uncertain: I] seemed to him today that I should forewarn Your Right Honourable of this, await your answer, and then give him further word.

The groom who brings you this will in all likelihood be with Your Right Honourable on the evening of the 22nd, and I expect him back at the latest on the 26th; by empty hopes and indefinite prospects I cannot let myself be held back from acting now with vigour. I count in this upon your friendly co-operation, so that we may there go to work hand in hand in every undertaking. As soon as I receive your answer I shall take my further measures, and report the same at once to Your Right Honourable. I beg for a quite definite answer and [uncertain: high] intelligence concerning your prospects and intentions, which however must lead to something swiftly. If you can procure me considerable pecuniary assistance speedily I shall be most grateful, since I stand in the most pressing need of it. It is all the more necessary that I come soon to a resolution, upon which you will kindly instruct me, since my son's stay with me will not be able to be of any very long duration. For the Parobek accept my thanks; he bore himself bravely at the first trial, but also turned pale, which however did him no harm. Still I flatter myself that I shall see Your Right Honourable quite soon with me, and it would grieve me greatly

You will therefore from this state of affairs deign to [uncertain: pass over] for yourself how necessary it is for me to obtain exact and thorough knowledge of the true present condition of all my estates, and at the same time also to make in them those provisions which are needful, in order to be able to stand with honour before the said commission as a truthful and upright man. These, approximately, are the grounds which have moved me to send Your Honour the lieutenant, although I know that his presence may perhaps not be so agreeable to you as that of any other; since, however, a man acquainted with the situation of those estates is absolutely required for this commission, I flatter myself that Your Honour will convince yourself of the [uncertain: urgency] of my [illegible], at least I should not wish that this choice of mine might bring our good cause any [uncertain: detriment], as I have received from the said [illegible] Sommer the reliable assurance that in his commission he will act setting aside every other consideration than that which aims solely at our common welfare. I therefore request Your Honour as urgently as most devotedly to confer with him quite without reserve and candidly upon the matter, or rather, which would be still more welcome to me, to come here with him, so that we might here, at greater leisure, take the matter into deliberation. Awaiting with impatience a satisfactory result of your mutual deliberations, I have the honour to remain with the most perfect esteem.

Your Honour has left my letter of the 19th of last month so wholly, or at least its principal subjects, unanswered and unfulfilled, that I cannot forbear to repeat the same herewith, and once again urgently to request both the transmission of the Trąbczyn deed of gift and a communication as to what sound project Your Honour intends with respect to my Zagorowo estates, or what plan you mean to follow in their further administration, so that not only may these estates be preserved, but that I too might at last come to some enjoyment of revenues. Accustomed hitherto to have yielded unconditionally to every proposal made by Your Honour, I am even now far from raising any objection to the intended leasing of my estates to Mr. von Brechso; only I must report to Your Honour herewith without concealment that the state of affairs here has so far altered that so long a deferment of the matter as you had the goodness to report to me could very easily bring about a complete annulment of this business, and that consequently, if anything can be done at all, precisely now is still the decisive moment.
