--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Proposes three schemes to extinguish some 90000 Rthl of debt: leasing Drzewce to v. Sobottendorff for 18 years at 2000 Rthl yearly (36000 Rthl) plus four vacated colonists' holdings in Swixce at 1000 Rthl each for his sons, against return of the receipted bond; advising Weigel to lease Kopoyno with propination rights for nine years at 6000 Rthl; and having Prince August redeem the 50000 Rthl owed at the Invalids' Fund. Notes delays in the Meierowitz suit and Cabanis's accounting before Chomanowski.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! Herr v. Sommer lately told me that a certain v. Sobottendorff, with his 2 sons, was staying with Your Serene Highness, to whom you owed 40000 Rthl, and who was in the highest degree very burdensome to him on that account. Despite my illness I have considered how one might set this man at ease and at once discharge him with respect to his whole claim. My advice is to lease Drzewce to v. Sobottendorff for 18 years; the rent is taken at 2000 Rthl yearly, which comes for 18 years to 36000 Rthl. In Swixce 4 colonists' holdings are vacant, since the holders declared themselves insolvent, and before the Court of Peace the matter was settled thus, that if the two of them were given 200 Rthl and the one 100 Rthl, they would vacate the holdings at once. The one with 100 Rthl has already been settled with, and the 200 Rthl have still to be procured. These 4 holdings one gives, each reckoned at 1000 Rthl, to the two young Sobottendorffs; that would amount to 40000 d_, and thus the debt would be extinguished. Sobottendorff gives back to His Serene Highness our debt instrument, receipted, and Your Serene Highness gives him a receipt for the 18 years'

rent. The interest from the commuted services, from the old peasants and from the [uncertain: Nybbel] nevertheless remains Your Serene Highness's, and no creditor can oppose himself on that account. — Should Sobottendorff object that he loses the interest on the rent paid in advance, he may be told that he must make this good for himself by good husbandry, and that by any other route he will still have long to wait for his satisfaction. In case Your Serene Highness approves this proposal and Herr v. Sobottendorff will enter into it, he must come to me at Blizanow with the utmost dispatch, where I shall then, when I am able to travel, go with him to Drzewce, conclude the matter and send it in to Your Serene Highness for execution. Next to this, Weigel has written a very [uncertain: kriegenden] letter to me and asks my advice as to how he might come at the 50000 M and his interest. Were it Your Serene Highness's pleasure, I had here a corresponding plan. I should write to Weigel and advise him to lease the demesne farm Kopoyno together with the propination rights for 9 years at 6000 Rthl annually; this amounts for 9 years to 54000 Rthl. — Of the 4000 he must at once hand over 2000 Rthl in cash to the treasury, which are said to be still outstanding from 1805 through the sequestrator Badynski; as for the interest on the rent advanced, Herr Weigel may see how he brings it out by good management — I shall tell him

that he said that, standing as he does behind the Bayonne sum, he would probably never have anything to reckon upon. Should this succeed for me, then Your Serene Highness would again be rid of 50000 Rthl, [uncertain: without] altering the substance of the estates. My 3rd plan would then be that Prince August should pay off the 50000 Rthl coming to me at the Invalids' Fund; the Invalids' Fund would in return have to cede it to me, and by this means all danger and execution would be removed for now and for ever. I should never seek execution, and the Bayonne interest is covered 3-fold by the remaining revenues. They amount yearly to 6000 Rthl, for the interest still outstanding must be extinguished by the arrears of interest as now [uncertain: shown]; just so the matter now stands, that all interest-payers must pro futuro pay the canon and the dues according to their contracts. Of the interest there would remain, at the highest, after deduction of the Bayonne interest, over 4000 Rthl in cash to Your Serene Highness. As soon as the Olesnice affair is concluded, these 2000 Rthl come in addition; from Wrąbczyn a further 1000 Rthl a year might also fall due. Then you might count on 7000 Rthl, and would have extinguished 90000 Rthl of debts

and after 9 years the revenues of Kopojno and the propination rights come in addition, whereby you would draw an eternally secure revenue of circa 14 to 15000 Rthl. What may be obtainable in cash at Olesnice and Wrąbczyn, when both suits shall have been won, I cannot determine exactly in these times — meanwhile I count on at least 20000 Rthl in cash — that is, without the revenues [uncertain: standing over]. It is sad that everything must be [uncertain: vindicated] by lawsuits, and these are always dragged out beyond half an eternity — thus the Meierowitz affair stands in the Vocanda No. 6, that is, that 5 suits still stand before it, on which account I have already lain 4 weeks [uncertain: waiting]. — Nothing can be forced. For the rest, Cabanis is now rendering account of everything that has been received, what has been paid out in advance, and what stands outstanding in arrears, in the presence of the Burgrave or komornik v. Chomanowski; I insist upon it, and Cabanis will bring the accounts to you himself at Schlawenschitz, so that you may convince yourself before all the world of what has been done and left undone here. — I remain, until death, Your Serene Highness's Blizanow, the 26th of June 1812, most obedient servant v Triebenfeld
