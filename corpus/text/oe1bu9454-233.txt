--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Proposes three schemes to clear debts: leasing Drzewce to von Sobottendorff for 18 years at 2000 Rthl yearly (36000 Rthl) plus four vacant colonists' holdings at Swixce valued at 1000 Rthl each to his two sons, discharging the 40000 Rthl bond; leasing the Kopojno demesne with propination rights to Weigel for nine years at 6000 Rthl against his 50000; and having Prince August redeem the 50000 Rthl owed the Invalids' Fund. Notes the delayed Meierowitz suit and Cabanis's accounting before the burgrave, to be brought to Schlawenschitz.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Mr. p. Sommer told me lately that a certain von Sobottendorff, with his 2 sons, was staying with Your Serene Highness, to whom You owed 40000 Rthl, and who was on that account most exceedingly troublesome to You. Despite my illness I have given thought to how this man might be quieted and at once settled with in respect of his whole claim.

My counsel is to lease Drzewce to von Sobottendorff for 18 years; the rent is reckoned yearly at 2000 Rthl, which amounts over 18 years to 36000 Rthl. In Swixce 4 colonists' holdings are vacant, for the possessors declared themselves insolvent, and before the court of conciliation the matter was arranged, that if the 2 from Berlin were given 200 Rthl and the one 100 Rthl, they would vacate the holdings at once. The one with 100 Rthl has already been settled with, and the 200 Rthl have still to be found. These 4 holdings, each reckoned at 1000 Rthl, one gives to the two young Sobottendorffs; that would amount to 40000 d_, and the debt would be discharged. Sobottendorff returns to Your Serene Highness the bond of debt receipted, and Your Serene Highness gives him a receipt for the 18 years'

rent. The interest from the [uncertain: recherten] services, from the old peasants and from the Nybbel nevertheless remains to Your Serene Highness, and no creditor can oppose himself on that account. — Should Sobottendorff object that he would lose the interest on the rent paid in advance, it may be said to him that he must recover the same by good husbandry, and that by any other way he would still have long to wait for his satisfaction.

In case Your Serene Highness approves this proposal and Mr. von Sobottendorff will enter upon it, he must come to me at Blizanow with all possible speed, where I then, when I shall be able to travel, will go with him to Drzewce, conclude, and send the matter to Your Serene Highness for execution. Next to this, Weigel has written me a very [uncertain: pressing] letter and asks my counsel how he might come at the 50000 M and his interest. Were it that Your Serene Highness should be pleased, I had here a like plan.

I would write to Weigel and advise him to lease the demesne farm Kopojno together with the propination rights for 9 years at 6000 Rthl yearly; this amounts for 9 years to 54000 Rthl. — Of the 4000 he must at once pay over 2000 Rthl in cash to the treasury, which are said to have remained outstanding from 1805 through the sequestrator Badynski; as to the interest on the rent advanced, Mr. Weigel may see how he brings the same out by good management — I shall tell him

that he said that, standing behind the Bayonne sum, he would likely never have anything to reckon on. Should this succeed for me, Your Serene Highness would again be quit of 50000 Rthl without impairing the substance of the estates. My 3rd plan would then be that Prince August should pay off the 50000 Rthl coming to me at the Invalids' Fund; the Invalids' Fund would have to cede to me in return, and hereby every danger and execution would be removed for now and always. I would never seek execution, and the Bayonne interest is in 3 respects covered by the remaining revenues. They amount yearly to 6000 Rthl, for the remaining interest must be discharged by the interest arrears now still [uncertain: assigned]; thus the matter now stands, that all interest-payers must for the future pay the canon and dues according to their contracts.

Of the interest there would then at most remain to Your Serene Highness, after deduction of the Bayonne interest, over 4000 Rthl in cash. As soon as the Oleśnica matter is concluded these 2000 Rthl come in addition; from Wrąbczyn too some 1000 Rthl yearly might further accrue. Then You could count on 7000 Rthl, would have 90000 Rthl of debts

discharged, and after 9 years the incomes from Kopojno and the [uncertain: propriation] fall in, whereby one would draw a perpetually secure revenue of circa 14 to 15000 Rthl. What may be obtainable in cash at Oleśnica and Wrąbczyn, when both suits shall have been won, I cannot determine exactly in these times — meanwhile I count at least on 20000 Rthl in cash — that is, apart from the revenues set out overleaf. It is a sad thing that everything must be vindicated by lawsuits, and these are forever dragged out beyond half an eternity — thus the Meierowitz suit stands in the [uncertain: Vocande] No. 6, that is, there are still 5 suits standing before it, and I have already been lying in wait for that for 4 weeks. Nothing can be forced. For the rest, Cabanis is now rendering account of everything that has been taken in, what has been paid out in advance, and what stands outstanding in arrears, in the presence of the burgrave or of the komornik von Chomanowski; I insist upon it. Cabanis will bring the accounts himself to Schlawenschitz, so that You may be convinced before all the world of what has here been done and left undone. — I remain, until death, Your Serene Highness's

Blizanow, the 26th of June

1812

most obedient servant

von Triebenfeld
