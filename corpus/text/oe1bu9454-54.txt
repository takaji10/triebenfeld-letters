--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld fleht den Fürsten an, die 600 Centner Eisen (300 Stab- und Reifeneisen, 300 Schieneisen) bis zum 23. April nach Bojadła statt Ende Mai nach Opole zu liefern. Michaelis sei Lieferung am 1. Mai in Berlin zugesagt, bei 500 Rthl Strafe und einem Wechsel über 2400 Rthl. Von der Lahr biete 5 1/2 Rthl pro Centner, solle 6 Rthl annehmen. Triebenfeld will die in Zagórów assignierten 4000 Rthl beschaffen. Gegen eine angebliche Drohung Weigels schütze das Generalmoratorium bis Johanni 1810.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld begs the Prince to deliver the 600 Centner of iron (300 of bar and hoop iron, 300 of rail iron) to Bojadła by 23 April instead of to Opole at the end of May. He says delivery in Berlin on 1 May was promised to Michaelis, under a penalty of 500 Rthl and a bill of exchange for 2,400 Rthl. Von der Lahr, he says, offers 5 1/2 Rthl per Centner and should accept 6 Rthl. Triebenfeld intends to raise the 4,000 Rthl assigned at Zagórów. Against an alleged threat by Weigel, he says the general moratorium until Midsummer 1810 gives protection.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

The 100 Friedrich d'or most graciously sent me by Your Most Serene Highness have for the greater part likewise come to nothing for me, inasmuch as the Chief Post Office had these monies withheld so long until I had paid off a small sum of accumulated postal charges and 480 Rthl Courant monies which I had incurred in the King's service. This has brought about a long delay, so that only yesterday evening did I receive Your Most Serene Highness's [uncertain: Highness] gracious letter of the 3rd of February. Its content, that the 600 Centner of iron are to be delivered as far as Opole only at the end of May, has utterly crushed me. I have promised to deliver the iron in Berlin at the Oberbaum already on the 1st of May. I have given a bill of exchange upon the 2,400 Rthl paid by Michaelis, payable on the 1st of May, and there is a contract, in the event the iron does not come in punctually, with a penalty of 500 Rthl Cour[ant] besides, fixed over and above it — I had to enter into these terms of fact merely in order to be released from the Berlin arrest sooner, before the 2nd judgment in the matter of Lahr came out.

Your Most Serene Highness I beg, for God's sake, to make it possible just this once that I have the 600 Centner of iron / namely 300 Rthl of bar and hoop iron and 300 Centner of rail iron / at Bojadła by the 23rd of April, so that I may load it at once and dispatch it to Berlin. I should be, if Your Most Serene Highness should not be pleased to grant this my heartfelt and urgent request,

utterly unhappy; indeed I should have to flee like a rogue, leave everything in the lurch, and should lose my entire credit, which I now think to re-establish in such a fashion that I may again be effective for Your Serene Highness in every way. Have mercy, Most gracious Prince, and do not let me become a liar this time. The contract, as well as the bill of exchange, has been concluded judicially with Michaelis; I should be very wretched and worse than badly placed if I did not keep my word; I have resources [uncertain: resources] afterwards and can bring much about — Only this time honour and all hangs upon the fulfilment of the contract — Thereupon I [uncertain: lift] the execution and sequestration, make, once I have my estates again, a timber contract upon the Konotop timber, go, when this is settled, to Vienna and fetch the contracted arms, which is the King's own hobby-horse, bring these to Koźle and then go again to Königsberg or wherever the King is; I can then bring about very much good — Concerning van der Lahr I have just now also received these enclosures; he offers already 5 1/2 Rthl per Centner of iron — but he must absolutely take the iron at 6 Rthl. I shall write to Amelang; may Your Most Serene Highness also be pleased to approach Amelang on that account once more, and have van der Lahr assured of this. I mean meanwhile to procure Your Serene Highness the 4,000 Rthl in Zagórów which are assigned, and then you will have your whole iron paid for again — Only be pleased to send the 400 Centner of iron, soon after the 600 Centner which Michaelis must have first, likewise to Bojadła; I shall then already direct all the matters. Court Councillor Hahn writes that Weigel is threatening — Who then can now compel Your Serene Highness to pay old debts.

the general moratorium protects you indeed until Midsummer 1810. Of old debts one need only settle the interest; newly contracted ones, taken up after the granting of the general moratorium, must under the newer edicts issued in favour of the Jews and usurers be paid by force, and this is precisely what touches me now.

Once more I beseech Your Serene Highness, do send me the 600 Centner of iron by the 23rd of April at the latest, that I may this once be saved — if you do not do this, then my whole civil existence is annihilated and my estates are put up for compulsory auction. The 600 Centner of iron are a trifle for Your Serene Highness, and you lose nothing thereby but at the most receive the money again by Midsummer, when all the interest comes in; the same is the case with the 400 Centner for van der Lahr: the debt is thereby now extinguished, and you receive the 4,000 Rthl assigned to me by the French General Government, for then you have the money for 1,000 Centner of iron again. Only be pleased to have it shipped as far as Bojadła near Zielona Góra [uncertain: instead of] to Opole. There I will have it loaded and send it to Berlin; this too costs another 400 Rthl or so; meanwhile I will indeed defray these costs, only show me this one grace and send the iron only as far as Bojadła.

In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

The answer from van der Lahr has remained with Amelang's letter in Konotop; I shall most humbly transmit the same by the first post.

Głogów, the 21st of March 1809.

most faithful and obedient servant

v Triebenfeld
