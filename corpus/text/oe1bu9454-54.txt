--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that the 100 Fr. sent him were largely absorbed by the Chief Post Office against arrears and 480 Rthl of Concur. monies, delaying receipt of the Prince's letter of 3 February. Desperately begs that 600 Centner of iron (bar, hoop and rail) reach Boyadel by 23 April, since a court-registered contract and bill of exchange with Michaelis for 2400 Rthl, with a 500 Rthl penalty, require delivery in Berlin by 1 May. Also urges pressure on Amelang over von der Lahr's iron at 6 Rthl, promises 4000 Rthl assigned in Zagorowo, and notes Hahn's warning of Weigel's threats and the general moratorium's protection until Midsummer 1810.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! The 100 Fr. most graciously sent to me by Your Most Serene Highness have for the greater part likewise been made water of, inasmuch as the Chief Post Office had these monies withheld so long until I had paid off a small sum of accrued postal Gülcher and 480 Rthl of Concur. monies which I had incurred in the King's service. This has brought on a long delay, so that it was only yesterday evening that I received Your Most Serene Highness's gracious letter of the 3rd of February. Its content, that the 600 Centner of iron are not to be delivered as far as Oppeln until the end of May, has utterly crushed me. I have promised to deliver the iron in Berlin at the Ober Baum as early as the 1st of May. I have given a bill of exchange upon the 2400 Rthl paid by Michaelis, payable the 1st of May, and there is a contract, if the iron does not come in punctually, a penalty of 500 Rthl was moreover fixed over and above it — I had to enter into these facta conditions merely in order to be released the sooner from the Berlin arrest, before the 2nd judgment concerning Lahr came out. Your Most Serene Highness I beg for God's sake to make it possible just this once that I have the 600 Centl. of iron (namely 300 Rthl of bar and hoop iron and 300 Leute of rail iron) at Boyadell by the 23rd of April, so that I may load it at once and dispatch it to Berlin. Were Your Most Serene Highness not to be pleased to grant this my heartfelt and pressing request, I should be

exceedingly unfortunate; indeed I should have to flee like a scoundrel, abandon everything and lose my whole credit, which I now think to re-establish in such a manner that I may in every way be of effect again for Your Serene Highness. Have mercy, Most gracious Prince, and do not let me be made a liar this time. The contract, as well as the bill of exchange, was concluded before the court with Michaelis; I should be very wretched and worse than ill off were I not to keep my word; I have resources afterwards and can effect much. — Only this once, chief honour and everything depends upon the fulfilment of the contract — Thereupon I outrun the execution and sequestration, make, once I have my estates again, a timber contract upon the Kontopper woods, go, when this is settled, to Vienna and fetch the contracted arms, which is the King's own hobby-horse, bring these to Cosel and go then again to Königsberg or wherever the King is; I can then effect very much good. — Concerning the Lahn I have just now also received these enclosures; he offers as much as 5 1/2 Rthl per Centner of iron — but he must absolutely accept the iron at 6 Rthl. I shall write to Amelang; may Your Most Serene Highness also be pleased to approach Amelang once again in the matter, and to have this assured to von der Lahr. I think meanwhile to procure for Your Serene Highness the 4000 Rthl in Zagorowo which are assigned, and then you will have your whole iron paid for again — May you only be pleased to send the 400 Leut. of iron soon, after the 600 Leute which Michaelis must have first, also to Bogadie; I shall then already direct all the matters. Court Councillor Hahn writes that Weigel is threatening — Who can now compel Your Serene Highness to pay old debts.

the general moratorium protects you indeed until Midsummer 1810. Of old debts one need only settle the interest; newly contracted ones, taken up after the granting of the Gen. Morat., must, under the more recent edicts which have been issued in favour of the Jews and usurers, be paid par force, and this is precisely what strikes me now. Once more I beg Your Serene Highness beseechingly, do send me the 600 Centner of iron by the 23rd of April at the very latest, that I may this once be saved — if you do not do this, then my whole civil existence is destroyed and they will sell my estates over my head. The 600 Centner of iron are a trifle for Your Serene Highness, and you lose nothing by it, but rather receive the money back at latest by Midsummer, when all the interest comes in; just the same is the case with the 400 Leute for von der Lahr — the debt is thereby now extinguished, and you receive the 4000 Rthl assigned to me by the French General Government; then you have the money for 1000 Lentl of iron again. May you only be pleased to have it shipped as far as Bogadel near Grüneberg, to be sent on by water to Oppeln. There I shall have it loaded and send it to Berlin; this costs a further 400 Rthl again, yet I will indeed bear these costs myself; only show me this single grace and send the iron only as far as Boyadel. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's — The answer from v. d. Lahr has been left behind with Amelang's Bruch in Kontop; I shall most humbly forward the same by the first post. Glogau, the 21st of March 1809. faithful and most obedient servant v Triebenfeld
