--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that through General v. Köckritz's mediation the Invalids' Department will advance 150000 Rthl without royal application: 50000 Rthl at once on mortgage and Triebenfeld's bill, the remaining 100000 Rthl after the Chamber revises the assessments, repayable over 50 years. Notes that the Depositorium, Widows' Fund, Bank and Seehandlung lend nothing into South Prussia, and bankers demand 10–12 per cent proxenetica. Promises the demanded 10000 Rthl, settles the Tirard and Hauk debt, advises against the proxenetica suit, defends himself over the Schlabrendorff affair, dismisses Wöllenhaupt on dismemberment, and proposes remitting 15000 Rthl to the Reich.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord: At last, through the mediation of the venerable General von Köckritz, it has been brought about that the Invalids' Department has now declared itself willing to furnish 150000 Rthl, without my having to seek permission for it from His Majesty the King, which is otherwise the course of business. The Department gives, for the present, 50000 Rthl upon the mortgage and my bill of exchange; then the Chamber must still revise the assessments, and after these the Department pays the 100000 Rthl, which, provided the interest is duly paid, may stand for 50 years.

The [uncertain: populle] deposit fund, the Widows' Fund still less, nor the Bank or the Maritime Trading Company, pay a single heller more into South Prussia, but are rather at pains to call everything in — this causes a frightful stagnation in all business and spreads such lamentation and [uncertain: Ob.] among the people that one's hair stands on end. I too should have obtained nothing, had not the [uncertain: Braun] General taken up my cause in the most active manner. The

26 Jan 1805.

bankers here now themselves give, merely to keep themselves afloat, 10 to 12 per cent brokerage on 5 to 6 per cent interest; and besides, Liep: Meyer Wolff each sets his own terms — he alone still has money, and thinks of those seeking money whatever is agreeable to him. Whoever now falls into the hands of men of this sort is irretrievably lost. God and that upright friend be heartily thanked that he here allotted a better and truly the most desirable lot of all. As soon as I now receive the money I shall pay the 10000 Rthl that have been demanded, and send Your Serene Highness in all haste the remaining sum after deduction of costs.

With the Tirard and Hauk affair I am finished; tomorrow I pay the arrears of interest out of my own money, and then someone takes over the debt, which can stand quietly for 4 years. The suit concerning the brokerage taken I do not find well advised — the thing damages Your Serene Highness's credit anew, and frightens off all those so-called men of business who are hand in glove with one another. This was indeed already cast up at me in the course of this very transaction, and appeal was made to it that Seebald should bring suit and demand the brokerage back;

The Biron affair has struck fear and terror into all — the moral lesson which Your Serene Highness writes to me solely on account of the Schlabrendorff affair I did not deserve. Every breach of faith is foreign to my heart, and so it shall remain to my last breath. I do not indeed know what I wrote in that agonising moment; in every case I must have expressed myself erroneously, in giving an account according to Your Serene Highness's letter. Honour remains in all my dealings the first spring of action; to it I sacrifice toil, means, and health. And the truth which I then stated in the Pro Memoria concerning the whole Schlabrendorff affair is, according to my [uncertain: demart], no knavery. I should curse all my dealings, and withdraw — a thing to which I am much inclined — into the last corner of the earth, far from all men, if in the end I were to be judged so.

The Mennonites I shall answer definitely according to Your Serene Highness's command. Wöllenhaupt's judgement concerning the dismemberment of the estates is too inconsiderable for a further word to be said about it. Let Your Serene Highness be pleased to give him the commission and promise him a few thousand, and he assures you he will dismember half the world — I have the samples

still before me — [uncertain: Rochtet] matters I shall attend to on my return to Unruhstad. The free pass for the wines must by now already be there. What the subjects of Betsche want I do not understand — there must again be some store of trouble lurking in the background. Meanwhile the date of this letter is of the month of June, and indeed earlier than that of the contract.

Next Wednesday I go back from here, in order to direct in Kalisz the revision of the assessment, so as to obtain the 100000 Rthl the sooner, and then to be able to dismember in peace. The Eysenhardt affair is proceeding [uncertain: et melang] wise and well, so that Your Serene Highness will have no vexation on that account. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's — As to the moneys to be sent out into the Empire, about half of the 30000 Rthl could now be got together — I thought Your Serene Highness might send that sum now, and promise to remit the other 15000 Rthl soon after.

Berlin

the 26th of January

1805
