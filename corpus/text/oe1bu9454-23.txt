Durchlauchtigster Fürst
Gnädigſter Fürſt und Herr!
Eur Durchlaucht, bat ich legt hin so flehentlich an meine arme Töchter einige hundert und
Münze zu ſchicken. Sie haben es nicht gethan. Sie müßte es wohl nicht möglich,
machen können. Entschuldigte Ewr Durchlaucht mich, daß ich wie ein Bettelh und
Ihnen lästig fiel die Noth drängte mich bis zur Verzweifelung — Meine Kinder
arbeiten für Geld, leiden Noth — und ich hungern und daß darum weil
ich alles opferte.

--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld klagt, der Fürst habe seinen Töchtern kein Geld geschickt; er selbst habe über die erhaltenen 4300 Rthl hinaus große Kosten getragen, erleide Execution und sei gichtkrank. Criminal Rath Stenger soll in Zagórów, Trąbczyn, Pszczew und Witów alles ordnen, verspricht Trąbczyn und Kamionna zurückzubringen und schlägt einen Actuarius Seifert für 250 bis 300 Rthl vor; Triebenfeld erbittet die Genehmigung. Honrichs soll sich ausweisen, sonst lässt Stenger ihn verhaften. Es heißt, die Russen würden Polen besetzen.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Your Serene Highness, I begged [uncertain: last] time so imploringly to send my poor daughters a few hundred and coin. You have not done it. You must surely not have been able to make it possible. Your Serene Highness will excuse me that I, like a beggar, became burdensome to you; need drove me to the point of despair — My children work for money, suffer want — and I go hungry, and that because I sacrificed everything.

Your Serene Highness at that time, when I was under arrest in Berlin, gave 3000 rt, likewise 1000 rt and 300 rt. That makes = 4300 rt. By the taking up of the 3125 rt and 813 [illegible] and interest upon interest I had to take from

v. d. Lahr 5140 rt and from Coste 9000 rt in papers; adding to this the costs at 3400, these together = 17500 rt. To the above 4300 rt comes the iron at circa 4000 rthl, which is = 8300 rt; there remain = 9200 rt. I have satisfied Coste entirely; I have paid Lahr all but 1600 rt; I have settled all brokerage fees, expenses and court dues — Meanwhile I am still to satisfy the 1600 rt to v. d. Lahr and 3500 rt to Blochsdorff and Holzapfel, who gave me the papers for the payment of Coste — since the French General Government has awarded me 4800 rt, these people, who have already sued me and laid an execution upon me, [uncertain: fund], might well be paid out of the latter sum when it comes in. So that [uncertain: 1/Your] Serene Highness need never again be burdened, there still remain in the meantime, when these = 5100 rt

are paid, = 4100 rt which

I have settled, and which I would only demand one day when Your Serene Highness has abundance. Doubly true it is to me, meanwhile, when I must [uncertain: hear] that Your Serene Highness's people say behind my back that His Highness gave me so very much, that I plunder you. God in heaven — I plunder you or burden you — I suffer execution, am distrained for the sake of your affairs, my children work for money — must, like common folk, carry and haul water, my estates are sequestrated — I soon everything — I begged only for a few hundred in coin for my poor children, and for that this is still said of me — Gracious Lord, that tears my heart, and now I swear to God: if He helps me, then He shall never see me turn here again — Köckritz has done something for me; I have also been called upon to come to Berlin, but in this sad situation I shall merely thank him and stay away, for I have nothing and go hungry with my children — I have indeed leased out my estates, but until Midsummer nothing is left over; the arrears of interest consume everything. I must see how I hunger my way through until then, and in order not to suffer a relapse I shall live on alone and shall not step onto the stage again, where for my good will I am so vilely hissed off.

Now I am moreover very ill, am crippled in my hands by the gout, so that I write this with the greatest difficulty. And for that reason I beg a gracious excuse for my wretched scrawl.

For the rest, the criminal-court councillor Stenger, here from Kargowa, will set everything in order in Zagórów and Trąbczyn, Pszczew and Witów and render to Your Serene Highness a humble report. He promises quickly to [uncertain: vindicate] Trąbczyn

and also to bring Kamionna back, so that Leixner may not raise an outcry. Up to now I have raised all these costs, paid and fed the people, paid execution and court dues; a further 185 rt in court dues remain to be paid, on account of which the executor of the High Provincial Court of Głogów, Allexius, was to distrain upon me yesterday — but since he found nothing, he had to be [text lost] with his fees, which with mileage money amount to 25 rt Courant. Until now I have borne everything in silence and have not wished to write unpleasant [uncertain: days] to Your Serene Highness. But now I am brought entirely to a stand, and have nothing more, but go hungry with my children.

I am to conduct the lawsuit with Oppenheimer that is pending in Berlin and in Poland.

2. with that ungrateful woman, the Schlieffen woman in Wrocław

3. with the [uncertain: etwas] fund in Wrocław, over the 800 rt which Hagen advanced to [uncertain: Gratsnau].

4. With v. d. Lahr, who at that time gave the 5140 rt in papers, received iron money in boards, which he afterwards reckoned at only half the value, the iron at 3 rt, and so still has 1600 rt to receive.

5. With Blochsdorff and Holzapfel, who gave 3500 rt in paper money in the utmost distress, to satisfy costs.

6. Now to vindicate Trąbczyn and Kamionna.

7. To free all the estates from the Polish watchers and to restore Your Serene Highness to the revenues and the unspeakably great arrears.

All this costs a furious deal of money. Nobody will serve without payment. Thus far I have given what I could raise. Now I have nothing more, but go hungry with my children myself — If the matter now lies still, it is not my fault — I lie wretched with the gout and cannot travel either. 591 rthl. 18 g. Courant have I borrowed of late and so paid out. God knows how this is to be done.

God grant it.

It is said the Russians will occupy the whole of Poland. In deepest respect I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Konotop, the 16th of February

1810

Your [uncertain: Krierenae] Councillor Stenger

true humble and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld

since they do not know [uncertain: Hakern] there; he has done nothing but denounce and cause damage. I shall hand over to Stenger the Hecker paper security; he may deal with him as he deserves and let him travel. But he wants an actuary there, and for that he proposes a certain Seifert — I do not yet know the man — He says Seifert would serve for less than the half and would be very well content with 250 to 300 rt. He would, once he has the estates free and back again, seek to save Your Serene Highness tenfold, in transfer fees (Laudemium) and court dues, what Seifert would cost; for he must have a man there who would keep the archive in order and whom he could send with confidence. May it please Your Serene Highness to tell me whether you approve of this. Honrichs is to give an account of himself at once; if not, Stenger will have him arrested and will immediately take in the three demesne farms he has appropriated to himself, with cattle and everything. Honrichs has not written for several weeks, notwithstanding that I have sent messenger after messenger to him. Even about the sale of the estates he has not said one word more. It is a pity about my 6 [uncertain: resting] swine and other game meat which I sent there — I stinted myself and mine of it and gave it to the man at his own proposal, in order to win the coals.

Stenger stands in great repute. He is much employed — He promises quickly to vindicate everything, but he wants to be well paid, and I now have no money — 591 rt I have paid and 185 rt are still to be paid quickly — the former I borrowed here from the dyer when the execution came; the latter I do not know how to raise; it is urgently necessary that both sums be paid — I should then, and he will find counsel, be able to give Stenger at least 2 to 300 rt for the journeys — otherwise the man will not stir from the spot any more and everything is lost. Unless the [uncertain: Klüßen] take Poland.

Lombardini has not given the 150 rt to my poor children. O how I should have thanked Your Serene Highness had you sent 500 rt [uncertain: perhaps] to my daughter; I should have escaped a great disgrace —
