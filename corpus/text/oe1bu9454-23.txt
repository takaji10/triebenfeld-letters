--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A desperate appeal for money: Triebenfeld, crippled by gout and under distraint, itemises what he has spent on the Prince's affairs, setting 4,300 Rthl received against borrowings from v. d. Lahr (5,140 Rthl, 1,600 still owing), Cote (9,000) and Blochsräff and Holzapfel (3,500), with 591 Rthl 18 g. paid and 185 Rthl of court dues due. He lists seven pending lawsuits, reports engaging criminal-court councillor Stenger to recover Trąbczyn, Zagorowo, Betsche and Witów, proposes Seifert as clerk, and threatens Honrichs with arrest.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Your Serene Highness, did I not lately beg so imploringly to send my poor daughter a few hundred and coin? You have not done it. She [uncertain: it] probably could not make it possible. Your Serene Highness must excuse me that I, like a beggar, became burdensome to you; want pressed me to the point of despair – My children, and because they work for money on that account, suffer want, and I hunger, having sacrificed everything.

Your Serene Highness gave, at the time when I was under arrest in Berlin, 3000 Rthl, likewise 1000 Rthl and 300 Rthl, which makes

God in Heaven – that I should prattle to you or fall burdensome to you — I suffer execution, am distrained for the sake of your affair, my children work for money, must carry and haul water like common people, my estates are put under sequestration – I [lose] soon everything — I asked only for a few hundred in coin for my poor children, and for that I am reproached with it besides – Gracious Lord, this tears my heart, and I swear to God that if He helps me, then He shall never see me turn here again. Köckritz has done something for me; I have also been called upon to come to Berlin, but in this sad situation I shall merely thank him and remain behind, for I have nothing and hunger with my children. I have indeed leased out my estates, but until Midsummer Day nothing is left over; the arrears of interest consume everything. I must see how I hunger my way through until then, and in order not to suffer a relapse I shall live on alone and not appear again upon the stage, where for my good will I am so odiously hissed off.

Just now I am very ill with it, am crippled in my hands by the gout, so that I write this with the greatest difficulty. And that is why I beg a gracious excuse on account of my wretched scrawl.

For the rest, the criminal-court councillor Stenger here from Karge will put everything in order in Zagorowo and Trąbczyn, Betsche and Witów, and will render a humble report to Your Serene Highness. He promises to vindicate Trąbczyn quickly

and also to recover Kaemen, so that Leixner may not be able to raise an outcry. I have hitherto raised all these costs, paid the people, fed them, paid execution and court dues; there are still 185 Rthl of court dues to pay, on account of which the Glogau Superior Provincial Court executor Allexius was to have distrained me yesterday – but since he found nothing, he had to be paid off with his fees, which together with mileage money amount to 25 Rthl Courant. I have hitherto borne everything in silence and have not wished to write Your Serene Highness disagreeable things. But now I have been brought entirely to a standstill and have nothing more, but rather hunger with my children. I am to conduct the lawsuit with Opernheimer, which is pending in Berlin and Poland.
