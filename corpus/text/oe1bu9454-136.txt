--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Congratulating the Prince on the sale of Kolno and Kaemen, Triebenfeld reports that buyers will pay the 76,000 Rthl in hard coin, to go to the Foundry Department, and that a fresh judicial valuation secured this; he awaits Glenck's power of attorney. Also: a Zagorowo mortgage and Lichnowski's offer behind 130,000 Rthl, the reckoning with Radzimski at Szetlewek, eviction of the Floricke tenants, plans to dismember Trąbczyn and settle Mennonites on some 400 Hufen, Minister von Voss's 1 July deadline, and a bitter denunciation of Glenck.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord, On the sale of Kolno and Kaemen I offer my most hearty congratulations, insofar as thereby Your Most Serene Highness's peace of mind has been secured, and my stout brother-in-law also satisfied. For the rest, I have done everything possible in order, according to Your Serene Highness's command, to be here on the 12th and to finalise everything. In the meantime neither Mr Gleich nor Mr Wünster is here, nor does either let anything be heard of himself, and I have neither papers nor power of attorney in hand to be able to settle anything real. This much, however, I have brought about: that the 76000 Rthl shall be paid here by the prospective purchasers. So that no pressure need first be applied in Breslau and no brokerage fees given, and also so that these moneys may be paid at once in Berlin to the Foundry Department according to Bernhardi's instruction. In this way everything concerning Koschentin is now disposed of, and it is only necessary that the people here be put at ease

and that the Broniszewski matter be pursued — in this last affair I shall seek to exert influence in Berlin — for in Posen it is said, as only Bernhard says, to stand badly again.

It was a piece of good fortune that I had the Otto valuation of Kaemen etc. drawn up afresh and judicially [uncertain: attested]; for the gentlemen here wanted the assessment, and the one spoiled in Berlin for 25 [uncertain: Fider] could hardly be shown to the people; so I saw to it in Käbisch that I obtained the same in probative form, and now the purchasing gentlemen are glad, and have therefore not only promised anew to make the payment of the 76000 Rthl, but they now wish to give hard coin here. I have written this to Bernhardi by today's post, and have also pressed that Meyer Bernhard must write this to him, so that he may be entirely at ease and may act according to his plan.

Concerning Kölzig I have brought up a new battery, which I hope will work well. I have dispatched to Berlin the Unruh so often named to Your Most Serene [Highness] and the like. I offered Blomberg a wager of 10 Friedrichs d'or, and Blomberg, who likewise found himself too nearly touched by my expressions, accepted the wager, in order to give the matter a merrier turn again; and I laid down the Friedrichs d'or. The horses were already standing

harnessed, with which I was to depart for Kalisz, and to be here for certain on the 12th — I therefore waited until a messenger came back from Wroberin from Meyer, who is a secret friend of Flöre's, and brought back not only the confirmation of what Blomberg had said, but also a note from Glenck, who has now revealed himself — Blomberg swept up my 10 Friedrichs d'or with the warning: in future not to hold the bad man for the worst, nor the honest man for the most honest. By great God! it was not the loss of the ten Friedrichs d'or that vexed me, but the shame of being so nastily caught out pressed me almost to the ground. Abashed, I begged pardon of Blomberg and also of Flöriche, and seated myself silently in my carriage, after I had first sent on to Glenck the papers forwarded after him by express courier — and drove off to Kalisz in grief and care. I have still not seen the fine gentleman, and am here already waiting again 1 1/2 days for him in vain — I shall, since Your Serene Highness expressly told me to treat him solidly,

also say not a word to him. But Your Serene Highness must not take it ill of me either, if from now on I hold this Glenck to be a truly vindictive, evil and really irreparably envious man.

Harm he shall not do, nor shall he be an obstacle in any of my plans, which I make for Your Serene Highness's best, for my dealings lie open, are bound up with no self-interested gain, and are therefore supported and furthered by good men; and it is often granted me even to win over the bad and to better them; therefore I do not fear him. But this I swear, that he is the most frivolous and most wicked man, who can never be bettered either by [uncertain: harshness] or by good words.

I tremble only lest he fritter away the time, and not go to Winnir and see to the handing over to Dąbrowski. The handing over is to be on the 24th of June. If he is not there at the appointed time, then Dambrouphi has an evasion and does not pay the 20500 Rthl, for he wishes to lease out elsewhere again, in order thereby to bring the sum

together. I shall indeed press it upon his heart, the more so as it is now most necessary that he not knock about here any longer. Whether, however, he will listen to my representation, time must teach.

For my part, once the contract is concluded here, I shall hasten to Berlin, see to the loan and bring the money to Lombar dine; from there I hasten to Trąbczyn and there settle everything as aforesaid. When I am finished there I shall return to Breslau, attend to my business, and then go to Betsche, Kulm and Kaemen, so that everything may be arranged there too and these people here be satisfied.

With boundless fidelity I remain, until death, respectfully, Your Most Serene Highness's

Frankfurt an der Oder

13th June 1804

faithful, humble and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld
