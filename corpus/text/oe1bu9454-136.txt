Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

On the sale of Kolno and Kamionna I offer my most hearty congratulations, insofar as by it Your Most Serene Highness's peace of mind is secured, and my stout brother-in-law has likewise been satisfied. For the rest, I have made every effort, in accordance with Your Serene Highness's command, to be here on the 12th and to finalise everything. Meanwhile neither Mr Glenck nor Mr Wunster is here, nor does either let anything be heard from him, and I have neither papers nor power of attorney in hand to be able to settle anything of substance. This much, however, I have brought about: that the 76/m rt. shall be paid here by the prospective purchasers. So that in the first place no pressure need be exerted at Wrocław and no brokerage fees given, and also so that these monies may be paid at once in Berlin to the Ironworks Department according to Bernhardi's instruction. In this way everything concerning Koszęcin is now disposed of, and it is only necessary that the people here be put at ease in their minds

and that the Broniewski matter be pursued — in this last affair I shall seek to have effect in Berlin — for in Poznań it is said, as only Bernhard says, to stand badly once again.

It was a piece of luck that I had the Otto valuation of Kamionna etc. drawn up afresh and judicially attested; for the gentlemen here wanted the valuation, and the one spoiled in Berlin for 25 fr. d'or could hardly be shown to the people, so I saw to it at Kalisz that I obtained the same in probative form, and now the purchasing gentlemen are glad, and have therefore not only promised anew to make the payment of the 76/m rt., but they will now give hard coin here. I have written this to Bernhardi by today's post, and have also pressed that Meyer Bernhard must write the same to him, so that he may be entirely easy and may act according to his plan.

Regarding Kölzig I have brought up a new battery, which I hope will have good effect. I have despatched to Berlin the Unruh so often named to Your Most Serene [Highness] and the like — he lodges with Mr von Köckritz: and is, as is known, his all-in-all; he stays there 3 weeks and is constantly to remind him of Kölzig

so that it not be overlooked.

The Grand Chancellor has ordered the government at Głogów to place all monies and all documents under seizure, and has also made known to it that on the 2nd of this month a letter was written to Luckesine — that, even if no document of insinuation, at least a certificate be sent in, to the effect that he has caused the sentence of confiscation, already become res judicata, to be delivered to Count Schlabrendorff at Paris, according to which, once the certificate of Luckedini[?] comes in, the estates are to be confiscated at once, and thereafter otherwise disposed of by His Majesty. Friend Unruh[?] is now to await this, and diligently to let his battery play — Whether Amelang will accomplish anything with Beyme — time will tell — I confess that I count little on this assistance.

The mortgage on Zagórów I have received duly made out. Also from Lichnowski the assurance that he, as I should require it, is willing to place himself behind 130/m rt. I shall now see how much they will give me, and what I obtain Your Serene Highness will find in store in the chest — if I can obtain the money without Lichnowski's help, that is preferable to me, for in that case his security [uncertain: he sees he must little] Weigel, the 27/m rt. has at once to revert upon Trąbczyn and will thereafter let himself be dealt with better. The

Kalisz man conceded this to me already. With Radzinski in Szetlewek I have got so far that he is to sit out his year, and in return has had to let all claims lapse. The reckoning with him is roughly this:

He has to claim 1500 Rthl

from 2 years' interest 112. rt.

Outlays according to Glenck's instructions for building costs 487 rt 4 gg  Sum: 2099 rt. 4 gg.

He is to pay rent from Midsummer 1803/4 = 1300 rt. likewise interest thereon 56 rt.

Rent from Midsummer 1804/5 = 1300 rt.

Which is 2656 rt.

Consequently Your Serene Highness would have to receive up to 1805 = 557 rt. Now he wants 306 rt. of legal costs allowed him after all, which leaves only 25[5?] rt. remaining. That then is the revenue of a whole year — but it must be cleared up. For the rest there are 2 gentlemen here who wish to take Szetlewek. They give 1 rt. ground rent per Morgen, which together with the peasants' arable and other things will come to some 2000 rt. yearly, and the propination rights and jurisdiction are besides this also to be reckoned at several hundred more.

Against the tenant Floricke and the others I have at last obtained a decree of payment — but I have applied at once for execution, and mean to wear them down. They are already very humble now — they still demand 4 Hufen, made ready, and 8 Hufen of waste land, but they already offer 20 rt. ground rent per Hufe — I shall do the beggarly lads no harm — but there will certainly be nothing to be got either.

NB the legal costs I think to tear from Radzinski's soul at my next coming there.

My plan with Trąbczyn, if Your Serene Highness approves the same, is now this: once I have the decree of execution in my hands I should throw the fellows about and make an honourable end with them — Thereupon I lease Trąbczyn, together with[?] the whole propination rights, for 1 year to Otocki, on the condition that I may dismember straight away. In that manner not a Groschen is lost, but rather I gain several thousand rt. more, for Otocki is to receive at Midsummer 6000 Rt. Floricke gives 6000 rt. rent yearly, and that for 15 years, with propination rights and all revenues.

I demand for 1 year, including the brewery at Kopojno etc. = 9000 rt. In that manner Your Serene Highness need not pay the above 6000 rt. and still receive in cash into the treasury = 3000 rt.

Notwithstanding this, the dismemberment is to take place not a moment later, nor is any ground to be let out a shilling cheaper — for this I stand surety to Your Serene Highness. The remainder of Swięcia consists of 9 Hufen of poor land and 5 Hufen of marsh amid sand hills, which are in part overgrown with timber. For these grounds the colonists will give little or nothing. Meanwhile Mr von Biskupski of Kuchar, who adjoins there, has applied to me and wishes to take these grounds at the fixed perpetual ground rent of 30 rt. per Hufe, for the reason that he has already had much quarrelling with the colo-

nists. For this strip is to remain lying as rough pasture between Swięcia and Kuchar — When the Germans now see that a stranger means to take it up, they will in the end give just as much themselves, and then my purpose is achieved here too. With Drzewce and Grądzyń I must still manoeuvre on account of the priests — for these demand free building and firewood; the judgement of the second instance will be published in the next few days. The Treasury has lost again. It now depends on whether it will seek a review — if it does not seek a review and lets the judgement become res judicata, then here too the dismemberment can be proceeded with at once. There is no lack of people who wish to buy grounds — Only if I press it too much, the people think there is distress and they bid far below the rate once accepted, from which however I shall never depart, but rather hope to obtain more than less — therefore the matter must be handled solidly, for now no distress presses any longer. With Kopojno, Skokum, Swiątniki, the Oleśnica marsh and the Wrąbczyn Warthe grounds, I think to provide for the Mennonites. These grounds amount to some 400 Hufen

the people are to arrive for certain between the 14th and 18th of this month. I have left Blomberg the instruction that he is not to let the Hufe go on average below 600 rt.; in addition to this they are to pay 10 rt. yearly rent. That then is a capital of about 240000 rt. and 4000 rt. in rent — With this capital all debts are paid, and whatever comes in for all the other places is delivered into the treasury. Meanwhile this will amount to a capital of some 150000 rt. — The brewery I would advise establishing at Trąbczyn, and to retain for it a wood of about 3000 Morgen. The brewing and brandy rent-roll would then, once everything was in order, certainly bring in some 6 to 8/m rt. yearly, and Your Serene Highness can in this way take in about 400000 rt. in cash for the grounds to be dismembered, and also draw from your South Prussian estates a yearly rent, subsisting for all time, of 48 to 50000 rt. That then, by God, is no inconsiderable entailed estate — But Your Serene Highness would have to have a house built upon the estates, for the higher barracks at Trąbczyn is after all no place for a ruling lord, and the nest so much praised at Mariantów is too small and is already about to fall in, together with the new [uncertain: Sprichten]

at Trąbczyn; therefore thought will gradually have to be given to the erection of a manor house in its place. Only one cannot make a beginning with it before 2-3 years. Minister von Voss has treated me truly handsomely and, in the presence of 26 persons, delivered a particular speech in my praise — He also sent greetings through Blomberg and said — that I should by all means conclude the dismemberments of all the princely estates, even if only pro forma, so that he might approve them. For from 1 July of this year he would forbid all further dismemberment. He has already made this known to the Chamber as well. For these reasons too I am here as if on coals, and await Glenck's arrival; for unhappily I can conclude nothing without a power of attorney — God knows meanwhile where the man is drifting about, for when I was at Trąbczyn on the 8th an express courier came from Poznań by way of Pyzdry to the justice-burgomaster Schenck with all the letters written to Glenck these several times past, and sought him out — among them were several from Your Serene Highness — namely 2 bulky ones and 3 smaller — How can anything sensible come of such drifting about, and what costs does the sending of express couriers not cause, and for a [uncertain: Dummesbrunter]. So I did not know where I should send the letters. At last Blomberg came and said, do you

know where Glenck is? Answer: whether he be with the devil or in heaven — is unknown to me. Neither there nor there — he says he has for a couple of days been sitting quietly in the deepest [uncertain: Gehau] at Wrąbczyn, and is seeking, through Meyer, Tappert and a couple of other fellows, to scare off the people who wish to buy here — Flöricke confessed this to me. In the heat of it I said — that only such a scoundrel as Flöricke is could put such a thing about. Glenck is no marauder — still less so black a villain as should work against or destroy the plans of his brave master, who pays him honestly — Flöricke, who was standing in the hall, heard this and found himself much offended, but engaged, for the rescue of his honour, to furnish the proof. I offered Blomberg a wager of 10 fr. d'or — Blomberg, who likewise found himself too nearly touched by my expressions, accepted the wager, so as to give the matter a merrier turn, and I laid down the fr. d'or. The horses were already standing

harnessed, with which I was to depart for Kalisz, and meant to be here for certain on the 12th — so I waited until a messenger came back from Wrąbczyn, from Meyer, who is a secret friend of Flöricke's, and brought not only the confirmation of what Blomberg had said, but a note from Glenck, who has now disclosed himself — Blomberg swept up my 10 fr. d'or with the warning — in future not to take the bad man for the worst, nor the honest man for the most honest. By [uncertain: Beyme] Great God! it was not the loss of the ten fr. d'or that vexed me, but the shame of being so nastily caught out pressed me well nigh to the ground. Ashamed, I begged pardon of Blomberg and of Flöricke too, and sat down quietly in my carriage, after I had first sent on to Glenck the papers forwarded after him by express courier — and drove off to Kalisz in grief and care. I have not yet seen the fine gentleman, and am already waiting here again 1 1/2 days for him in vain — I shall, since Your Serene Highness has expressly said so, treat him solidly

and say not a word to him. But let Your Serene Highness not think ill of me for it now either, if from now on I hold this Glenck to be a truly vindictive, evil and really irreparably envious man.

Harm he shall not do, nor be an obstacle in any of my plans, which I make for Your Serene Highness's best, for my dealings lie open, are bound up with no self-interested gain, and are therefore supported and furthered by good people, and it often even lies with me to win over the bad and to better them; therefore I do not fear him. But this I swear, that he is the most frivolous and most evil man, who can never be bettered either by hard words or by good.

I only tremble lest he fritter away the time and not go to Wieniec[?] and see to the handover to Dąbrowski. The handover is to be on the 24th of June. If he is not there at the appointed time, then Dąbrowski has a pretext and does not pay the 20500 rt., for he intends to lease out elsewhere again, so as thereby to bring the sum

together. I shall indeed press it upon his heart, the more so as it is now most necessary that he drift about here no longer. Whether however he will listen to my representation, time must teach.

For my part I shall hasten, once the contract is concluded here, to Berlin, see to the loan and bring the money to Lombardini; from there I hasten to Trąbczyn and settle everything there as aforesaid. Once I am finished there I return to Wrocław, see to my own business and then go to Pszczew, Kulm and Kamionna, so that everything may be arranged there too and these people here satisfied.

With boundless fidelity I remain, until death, reverentially, Your Most Serene Highness's

Frankfurt an der Oder

13 June 1804

faithful, humble and obedient servant

v Triebenfeld
