Von Gottes Gnaden Friedrich Wilhelm König von Preußen
Unsere Freundschaft, Königliche Gnade und guten Willen zuvor
Hochgebohrner Fürst, besonders Lieber Freund und Lieber
Getreuer! Im Verfolg der Ewr. Liebden, auf das unterm
31sten December v. J. angebrachte Gesuch um die Erlaubniß
zur Verabpachtung der Herrschaft Betsche an die dortige
Bürgerschaft und die übrigen ländlichen Einseßen, am
5ten Februar d. J. gewordenen Bekanntmachung, laßen
Wir Ewr. Liebden mit Rückgabe des eingereichten Erbpachts
Contracts auf den Grund des in Gemäßheit einer Aller¬
höchsten Cabinets-Resolution vom 11ten v. M. an Unsere
Cammer ergangenen Rescripts Unsers SüdPreußl. Finanz
Departement vom 15ten v. M. hierdurch zum Bescheide
geben, daß die in Rede stehende Vererbpachtung ganz
unzuläßig befunden worden ist, und Ewr. Liebden der
dazu nachgesuchte Consens nicht ertheilt werden kann,
denn
1tens würden, wenn Ewr. Liebden dieser Consens nicht ver¬
sagt worden wäre, mehrere Grundherrschaften
ähnliche Anträge wagen, und dadurch die Besitzungen
des in hiesiger Provinz so zahlreichen Adels auf deßen Con¬
servation Bedacht genommen werden muß, vermindert
und demselben die Gelegenheiten zu Güter Acquisitionen
benommen werden. Falls indeß auch dieses erhebliche
Hinderniß in Ewr. Liebden Vorhaben nicht schon entgegen stünde,
so würde die mehrerwähnte Verabpachtung nicht genehmiget
werden können, weil
2tens die zweckmäßige Bewirthschaftung der Betscher
Forst, welche, da diese Forst die einzige beträchtliche
in der dortigen Gegend ist, alle Rücksicht verdient, nicht
statt finden könnte, indem, wenn den Erbpächtern d.
freye Benutzung überlaßen wird, auch die beste Aufsicht
die Devastation nicht ganz verhütten könnte,
3tens ist hierbey auch noch der Umstand zu erwägen
daß wenn Ewr. Liebden Antrage deferirt würde
die von den Einsaßen in Erbpacht zu nehmenden
Ländereyen nicht einmal ordnungsmäßig bewirth

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Refuses the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen permission to let the lordship of Betsche in hereditary lease to the burgesses and rural residents, returning his submitted hereditary-lease contract. Three grounds are given: other landed lordships would follow suit and shrink the province's noble estates; the Betsche forest, the only substantial one in the district, would be devastated once lessees had free use of it; and the residents already hold enough land, lack the means, and the burgesses should pursue urban trades instead.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
By the Grace of God, Friedrich Wilhelm, King of Prussia. Our friendship, royal grace and good will beforehand, High-born Prince, particularly dear Friend and dear Faithful Subject! In pursuance of the notification issued on the 5th of February of this year upon Your Lordship's petition submitted on the 31st of December of last year for permission to let out the lordship of Betsche on lease to the burgesses there and the remaining rural residents, We hereby give Your Lordship, with the return of the hereditary-lease contract submitted, upon the ground of the rescript of Our South Prussian Finance Department of the 15th of last month issued to Our Chamber in conformity with a Most High Cabinet Resolution of the 11th of last month, the following decision: that the hereditary letting in question has been found wholly inadmissible, and that the consent sought thereto cannot be granted to Your Lordship; for, firstly, if this consent had not been refused Your Lordship, several landed lordships would venture similar applications, and thereby the possessions of the nobility, which is so numerous in this province and upon whose conservation regard must be had, would be diminished, and the opportunities for the acquisition of estates would be taken from it. Even if, however, this considerable obstacle did not already stand in the way of Your Lordship's design, the aforementioned letting could not be approved

because, secondly, the appropriate management of the Betsche forest — which, since this forest is the only considerable one in that district, merits every regard — could not take place, inasmuch as, if free use of it were left to the hereditary lessees, even the best supervision could not wholly prevent its devastation; thirdly, there is also this circumstance to be weighed, that if Your Lordship's application were deferred to, the lands to be taken in hereditary lease by the residents could not even be managed in an orderly fashion, since every resident, and likewise every burgess of the town of Betsche, possesses as much land as he requires for a proper husbandry; and moreover, as regards the burgesses, care is to be taken that they apply themselves rather to the pursuit of urban trades than to that of agriculture, most of the residents being besides too lacking in means to be able to raise the sums of money required for the management. In which We remain well disposed toward Your Lordship with friendship, royal grace and good will.

Posen, the 18th of May 1805. Royal South Prussian War and Domains Chamber [uncertain: G] v. Köller

To
