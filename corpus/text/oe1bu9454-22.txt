--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Refuses, on grounds of honour and illness (gout), to be named co-defendant or to travel to Poland, having already four suits on his neck as the Prince's surety: over the Invalids' Fund, with the Jew Oppenheimer, and with Colonel von Schlieffen's widow, whose 6,000 Rthl purchase money for Witów he guaranteed to secure the Prince 500 Rthl yearly hereditary quit-rent, now four years in arrears. Details 259 Rthl paid out, 500 Rthl in costs, the agent Stieger's fees, and Lombardini's failure to pay 150 Rthl to his destitute children.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Now that, by a middling leasing-out and by the real assistance of General Köckritz, I may hope to set aside my afflictions - the gout seizes me again - For some days now I have been laid low in a hard way, yet I hope soon to be restored.

However painful my illness may be in the meantime, I will nonetheless not delay the answer demanded of me, in accordance with Your Serene Highness's commands. Your Serene Highness deigns to say that I should be cited as co-defendant in Your affairs.

Most gracious Prince and Lord! I have already for a considerable time had 4 lawsuits about my neck in Your affair, which will yet cost much money - the one on account of the Invalids' Fund, the one with the Jew Oppenheimer, and now the one with the Colonel's lady von Schlieffen, are considerable - and they vex me frightfully, because I alone must suffer for it that I made myself surety for Your Serene Highness.

Out of faithful attachment I did not wish to tell You the [uncertain: Crediosa], but carried the matter forward quietly, and I beg in this much - Believe that these lawsuits already cost me a very great deal of money — only 7 days ago I had to pay the executor Alexius 132 Rthl, and the Chamber Court further demands 85 and 42 Rthl for 127, which makes

makes together 259 Rthl Courant. Empich conducts the Oppenheimer suit; he has already received much, and he demands, if he succeeds, still much more - the Schlieffen suit is being conducted against me in Breslau, and in Poland I am pursuing it against Meierowitz. This fellow still owes the Schlieffen lady 6000 Rthl of purchase money for Witów — I gave the Schlieffen lady a written undertaking in which I made myself good for the sum; I did this in order to obtain for Your Serene Highness the 500 Rthl yearly hereditary quit-rent from Witów, which Mucerowitz has now been in arrears with for 4 years and more — Out of gratitude that I helped the lady to all that she has got, she has brought suit against me – I have now been compelled of necessity to bring suit against Meierowitz, in order to help the old crone to her 6000 Rthl and Your Serene Highness to the 2500 Rthl of arrears of hereditary quit-rent. The suit already costs, in Breslau and Poland, some 500 Rthl. I have borne it, endured it, paid it, and said nothing to Your Serene Highness, for I spare You too gladly, because You have afflictions enough already. The matter with the [uncertain: sequestrated] Fund makes me altogether [uncertain: careless]; it already costs me many thousand thalers, honour, and health — It is the ground-stuff of all the afflictions I have endured hitherto and of nameless grief. I hoped God would help at last, and that the matter would end by a death - It does not happen, and I can do nothing but seek to drag the matter out.

Against going to Poland my sense of honour rebels – this is not unknown to You, and therefore You will no doubt be willing to wait until matters change. I work on and hope to succeed; I have engaged a certain Stieger who now pursues the matter with

vigour – He costs me much but will certainly bring everything into the clear — dullards cannot possibly be employed now; one is laughed at there and matters immediately grow worse — Men must absolutely come forward who understand the language, since everything is transacted in the language of the country - But these men let themselves be paid dearly, – I raise it and give it, without always being burdensome to Your Serene Highness. For Your complaints rend my heart quite enough as it is; I love [uncertain: You] too much. The money that Lombardini has given, and is now to give, is not the 10 Rthl part of what I have already given — Whimper and beg I cannot; I endure and bear, for my most gracious Prince, what no one else in the world would do further.

Here are 3 decrees again; I do what is possible - But by force I can now compel nothing. Had I 50 men to command, I would lay all the treasures of Poland at Your feet – as it is, however, I must endure and wait.

Honrichs, and Honrichs's arrival, I look for here daily. Stenger is investigating everything, and until then I suspend my judgement — So much is certain, that Hawich is the greatest rogue - [uncertain: If] Honrichs is too, then woe to him – Only he has not received money; rather a certain Radenski, who is appointed by the government, draws everything in. I have therefore not seen a Groschen from thence. Nor, upon my honour, do I take anything from thence, so as not to fall into confusion. Lombardini has not given the 150 Rthl - I had made them over by order of payment to my son, who was here and wanted money, because he was requesting

to be driven out of the regiment; for I could give him nothing, because the executor had just taken away the last Groschen, and 100 Rthl had to be sent to Berlin - Lombardini has given nothing, and my son lives, as do I and my daughters, in the greatest want - My grief, through these tangled affairs, is before God without bounds — Consider now, my Prince, the shame: my daughters work for money at millinery and embroidery. They have judicially renounced me, only in order to save the furniture and to bring into safe keeping what they have from their mother – I can give them nothing until I have my estates free again. I assigned by order of payment 100 Rthl to my son and 50 Rthl to my daughters - but Lombardini was not so obliging, as I hear within the hour, as to pay the money - It outrages one's inmost being - for this I have not deserved - could I in haste raise 500 Rthl in coin, to send it to the children speedily, I would gladly give up on [uncertain: John's day], when the lease monies come in, 1000 Rthl for it in return_ This news makes me well nigh furious. – I have given up everything gladly and willingly - I have never drawn a heller from Your Serene Highness's estates, and I am left to endure this language – As soon as I am well I shall lay the account before Your Serene Highness. - I also beg You to [uncertain: call for] the information to be gathered - whether I have drawn a Groschen from there. O most gracious Prince, that hurts too sorely. O had I but soon 500 Rthl for my poor children. No, this exceeds all bounds. God preserve Your Serene Highness and let it go better with You than with me; this blow strikes me too hard.

[illegible] I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's

Kontop, 27th January

1810

humble and obedient servant
