Der Krieges und Forst Rath von Triebenfeld zu Kontop bey Gruneberg in Schlesien empfing auf sein Ansehen
an 1sten Febr. 1805 von der Gem. Jnvaliden Casse hieselbst ein Darlehn von 50000 Rthl Cour nach dem Münzfuße de 1764.
in damals ohne Verlust sogleich in baares Geld umzusezzenden Behandlungs Obligationen gegen 5 pct in halbjährigen
Theilen zu bezahlenden Zinsen, und Zurückbezahlung des Jahrlehns nach einer beiden Theilen freystehenden 6 Monatlichen
Kündigung. Über dieses Darlehn hat derselbe unterm 28ten Januar 1805 der Gen. Invaliden Casse eine gerichtliche
Schuldverschreibung ausgestelt, und darin zur Sicherheit dieses Darlehns der gedachten Casse nicht allein sein sämtliches
Vermögen zum Unterstande eingesezt, sondern ihr auch außerdem eine Obligation so durchl. des Fürsten zu Hohenlohe
Ingelfingen de dato Brulau d. 1ten Juny 1804 an ihm den von Triebenfeld über die Summa von 250000⁠ Hautgestellt
und auf Sr. Durchl. Zagorower Güter eingetragen, verpfändet und event. cedirt, auch derselben im Ansehung
ihres Darlehns die Prioritaet vor der ihm in lezterer Obligation noch verbleibenden 200000 Rthl eingeräumt. Von
dem erwähnten darhöhe der 50000 Rthl ist der p v Triebenfeld, die Zinsen a 5 pC. seit dem 1ten Novbr. 1807 bis
Ende July 1871, also für 3 3/4 Jahr mit 9375 s. Cour: schuldig verblieben. Alle Mittel von demselben diesen Zinsrest be¬
zahlt zu erhalten sind fruchtlos gewesen, und es hat daher die Gen: Invaliden Casse sich genötiget gesehen, sich der ihr sowohl
gegen den von Triebenfeld, als auch gegen die ihr verpfändeten Zagorower Güter Sr. Durchl. des Fürsten zu Hohenlohe aus dem
oben erwähnten Schuld und verpfändungsInstrumente zustehende Rechte in Ansehung dieses Darlehns und der davon restierenden Zinsen

Sr Durchlaucht der Fürst zu Hohenlohe Ingelfingen aus dem Nachlaße der hieselbst verstorbenen Fürsten Selchen die Summen

welchem hier, da Gefahr im Verzuge vorhanden ist, die Rede
ist, ist weiter nichts erforderlich, als
1. daß der Implorant oder Arrest sicher seine Forderung, und worin sie bestehe anzeige.
2. Deß Umstände woraus eine gegenwärtige Gefahr keine Verzüge sich entnehmen läßt, nachweise, oder bestimmt angebe, und

3 daß er sofort eine anehmlich Caution sowohl zur Dekung des Richters als des Arrestandi wegen das demselben aus der
Verkümmerung etwa entstehenden Nachtheils bestelle.

gabe des § 33 Part. 1. Titel 29 der Gerichts Ordnung befreit. Es kömt mithin auf das da sein die andern Erforderniße eines

cret der Kallischen Praefectur dargethan, daß die von ihm der Gen. Invaliden Casse zur Sicherheit der Quit: 50000 Rthl um Zinsen

eintragen zu laßen. Auf diese Weise wird die Gen. Invaliden Casse um die ganze Sicherheit ihrer Forderung ge¬
bracht, und da notorisch die Vermögens Umstände des v Triebenfeld sehr verwikkelt und müßlich sind, so ist sie

der 250000 Rthl, gegen deren Verpfändung und eventuellen Cession dem v. Triebenfeld von der Gen: Invochen Caße 50000 m

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Copy of a fiscal petition by Seelmann to the Kammergericht seeking urgent arrest, danger being in delay, of the 80000 Rthl bequeathed to the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen from the estate of the deceased Princess von Sacken, to cover the General Invalids' Fund's loan of 50000 Rthl Courant made to Triebenfeld in February 1805 and 9375 Rthl of interest unpaid since November 1807. Cites the pledged 250000 Rthl Zagorow obligation of 1 June 1804, now reckoned to the Bayonne sums, the encumbered Kontop and Schwammern estates, Advocate Mitschke's suit at Kalisz, and asks that Justice Minister von Kircheisen as executor be ordered accordingly.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Copia. Pro Fisco. Summum periculum in mora. The Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld at Kontop near Gruneberg in Silesia received, upon his own standing, on the 1st of February 1805, from the General Invalids' Fund here, a loan of 50000 Rthl Courant according to the coinage standard of 1764, in Behandlungs-Obligationen [treatment obligations] then convertible into ready money at once without loss, against 5 per cent interest payable in half-yearly instalments, and repayment of the annual loan upon a six months' notice open to either party. Concerning this loan he issued, under the 28th of January 1805, a judicial bond to the General Invalids' Fund, and therein, for the security of this loan, not only pledged to the said Fund his entire property as surety, but besides that also pledged and conditionally ceded to it an obligation of His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe Ingelfingen, of date Brulau the 1st of June 1804, made out to him, von Triebenfeld, for the sum of 250000 [uncertain: made out] and entered upon His Serene Highness's Zagorow estates, and likewise granted to it, in respect of its loan, priority before the 200000 Rthl still remaining to him under the latter obligation. Of the said sum of the 50000 Rthl, the said von Triebenfeld has remained owing the interest at 5 per cent from the 1st of November 1807 until the end of July 1811, that is for 3 3/4 years, amounting to 9375 [illegible] Courant. All means of obtaining payment of this arrear of interest from him have been fruitless, and the General Invalids' Fund has therefore found itself compelled to make use of the rights belonging to it, both against von Triebenfeld and against the Zagorow estates of His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe pledged to it, arising out of the abovementioned instrument of debt and pledge, in respect of this loan and of the interest remaining due thereon; and to this end it has judicially empowered the Royal Saxon and Ducal Warsaw Tribunal Advocate Mitschke at Kalisz, transmitting to him the original documents concerning that loan, to sue in its name before the competent Ducal Warsaw Tribunal, for the present against the possessor of the Zagorow estates, for the interest outstanding on the capital of the 50000 Rthl, and if necessary to seek the sequestration of these estates. Apart from the fact that, according to information gathered, the Zagorow estates are very heavily encumbered, there arises here also the inconvenience that, according to the enclosed letter of von Triebenfeld of the 5th of this month to the General Invalids' Fund, submitted with the request for its return, and the resolution of the Kalisz Prefecture attached to that letter in an authenticated copy, the capitals belonging to the General Invalids' Fund in the Duchy of Warsaw and mortgaged upon estates there have, together with the interest outstanding, likewise been reckoned to the [uncertain: Bayonne sums]. In these circumstances, and since, as appears from the mortgage certificate communicated to the Invalids' Fund by the Plogan Superior Provincial Court by letter of the 21st of September 1810, and here likewise enclosed sub petito retraditionis, the estate Kontop near Grüneburg in Silesia belonging to von Triebenfeld is encumbered almost to the height of its purchase price in the year 1800; further, since according to the tenor of this letter of the Glogan Superior Provincial Court the estate Schwammern, still belonging to the said von Triebenfeld and situated in the department of the Brech Superior Provincial Court, is likewise burdened with considerable debts: the General Invalids' Fund is without any security whatever for the capital of 50000 Rthl which it has to demand and for the interest outstanding thereon, which, as already mentioned, already amounts by the last day of July of this year to 9375 Rthl Courant. This Fund has meanwhile learnt that His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe Ingelfingen has received from the estate of the Princess von Sacken, deceased here, the sum of 80000 Rthl as a bequest, and will pay the same over again to the said von Triebenfeld in satisfaction of the sums of money owing to him; and I have been charged by this Fund, by the letter of the 29th hujus enclosed in the original, which rests upon the order of the Royal Privy Councillor of State and Chief of the General Police Department in the Ministry of the Interior of the 28th of this month, enclosed in copy, to lay an arrest with all speed upon the aforesaid [uncertain: 8000] Rthl before a Most Laudable Superior Court of Appeal [Kammergericht], for the covering of the loan of the 50000 Rthl Courant and of the interest outstanding thereon from the 1st of November 1807 to the end of [uncertain: Ehrt.] 1811 amounting to 9375 Rthl Courant, and likewise of the interest running from then onward. This arrest is well founded in every respect. For the grounding of an arrest in urgent and pressing cases, of which there is [illegible] here, since there is danger in delay, nothing further is required than: 1. that the implorant or party seeking arrest state his claim with certainty, and in what it consists. 2. that he prove, or definitely state, the circumstances from which a present danger admitting of no delay may be gathered, and indicate the means of proof thereof. 3. that he at once furnish an acceptable security both for the covering of the judge and of the arrestandus, on account of the disadvantage that may arise to the latter from the impounding.

From the third requisite, concerning the furnishing of security, the fiscus, and therefore also the General Invalids' Fund, which has the Jura Fisci, is exempt according to the measure of § 33 Part 1 Title 29 of the Judicial Ordinance. It therefore depends only upon the presence of the other requirements of a petition for arrest. The claim of the General Invalids' Fund against von Triebenfeld for 50000 Rthl of capital and the interest outstanding thereon is, even though the original bond concerning it is not at present at hand, nevertheless certified by the files of the Military Department which concern this loan transaction and which are enclosed sub petito retraditionis, whereby the requisite under 1 is satisfied. It is further shown by the autograph letter of von Triebenfeld of the 3rd of July, and likewise by the decree of the Kalisz Prefecture transmitted in authenticated copy with that letter, that the mortgage upon the Zagorow estates, pledged and conditionally ceded by him to the General Invalids' Fund as security for the said 50000 Rthl and interest, has been reckoned to the Bayonne sums, and that the possessor is to be compelled by execution to have the sum owing, together with the interest in arrear, entered anew judicially. In this way the General Invalids' Fund is deprived of the whole security of its claim, and since the property circumstances of von Triebenfeld are notoriously very entangled and precarious, it is exposed to the danger of coming short of its capital of the 50000 Rthl and the interest outstanding thereon, and of being left with nothing but the empty looking-on, if the aforesaid [uncertain: 8]0000 Rthl are not laid under arrest; in such wise the 2nd requirement of an arrest is likewise present here. The circumstance that von Triebenfeld is the original debtor of the 50000 and of the said interest, and that the 80000 Rthl in question are bequeathed to the Prince of Hohenlohe Ingelfingen, cannot be prejudicial here. For the Prince is, in respect of the 250000 Rthl, against the pledging and conditional cession of which the 50000 Rthl were lent to von Triebenfeld by the General Invalids' Fund, a debtor of von Triebenfeld; and the General Invalids' Fund is, by virtue of the cession granted to it, entitled to exercise all rights against the Prince which belong to its cedent, the said von Triebenfeld, for by virtue of its cession it has stepped into the place of the latter. The arrest upon these 50000 Rthl is founded in every respect, whether the Prince be still owner of them, or have already made them over to von Triebenfeld for his satisfaction. Both the Prince and von Triebenfeld are bound to the General Invalids' Fund, and both are now no longer safe for it. Von Triebenfeld does indeed possess estates in these lands, but they are so encumbered that they afford no security, and therefore, according to § 10 Part 1 Title 29 of the Judicial Ordinance, the arrest nonetheless takes place, although he is resident in the Royal lands. I most obediently beg a Most Laudable Superior Court of Appeal to grant this petition for arrest most graciously, and, since the General Invalids' Fund has learnt that the Minister of Justice von Kircheisen, His Excellency, is said to be executor of the will of the late blessed Lady Princess von Sacken, not only to cause the requisite order of arrest to be issued to him citissime, but at the same time to declare the said Fund, per decretum, put into possession of these 80000 Rthl in respect of the capital of the 50000 Rthl which it has to demand together with the interest thereon. Berlin, the 30th of July 1811. Seelmann
