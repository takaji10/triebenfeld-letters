--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Recovering from severe illness at Swiątniki, Triebenfeld reports that no one pays: 31 lawsuits pend, execution and eviction are sought against the Oleśnica colonists, Niedzwiedzki of Kopojno and Hawich of Drzewce, and three demesne farms are to be re-established. Outstanding are 8657 Rthl of taxes, 7500 Rthl interest to the Invalids' Fund, and interest on 27000 Rthl. Napoleon's new remission edicts have unsettled the colonists; the Trąbczyn suit is referred upward after Prince Poniatowski's intervention; Zagorowo is valued at 22140 Rthl yearly, leaving 7140 Rthl.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord!

A severe illness, such as I have never yet had, has hindered me from writing to Your Serene Highness sooner – now I am so far recovered that I can think and act again. The dreadfully great vexation which I must swallow here daily might well hinder me from ever growing well again. Still no one here pays; 31 lawsuits are now pending on that account, and now I hope that some will be compelled to pay by execution and distraint, and also that the Olesnicer people will be thrown out. Likewise Niedzwiedzki from Kopojno and Hawich from Drzewce are to be cast out, and then 3 demesne farms are to be established again, because the Germans not only pay nothing at all but are going to the devil. — There remain to be paid some 8657 Rthl 20 [uncertain: dathen] or taxes and dues, 7500 Rthl interest to the Invalids' Fund, and on 27000 Rthl other interest and costs — [uncertain: kann] shall I take in enough to [uncertain: seinen] this.

According to the new edicts every colonist brought into the country is to receive 6 free years; upon this the people now rest themselves — Meanwhile all this must be between now and NB the land-taxes were otherwise discharged in order to preserve the estates, but for the interest which I have guaranteed to pay they sequestrate all my estates - how am I to live on that, and where are 34000 M

I have promised the colonists that if for 3 years [uncertain: prangt] they pay the half, I would procure them a remission of the interest; but the [uncertain: Böserichter] still refuse. Your Serene Highness gained after 1 1/2 years, and yet money would come in. Everything was already well disposed, but the more recent declaration of King Napoleon, that the people are to have so many free years that they shall pay the sums owed to Prussia over 40 years in such manner that of the capital only 3½ [uncertain: lerle] interest and 2½ [uncertain: post] towards amortisation of the capital need be paid, has confounded all heads afresh, so that for the present nothing good is to be hoped for — even the Trąbczyn affair lies still. The Prince Poniatowski has written in secret to the Tribunal not to adjudicate in the matter at all, but rather, if the Tribunal may not award the estates to the Dąbska woman, to refer the matter to the higher magistracy — this last the Tribunal has done, after the most bitter debates. The higher magistracy is now the King of and Napoleon - the estates are, by the Peace of Tilsit,

[uncertain: fardurchlaucht] [text lost: antrißen], and must absolutely be restored to you. [uncertain: Salz] is now working the matter out. The subjects all wish to have Your Serene Highness as their lord; the old Hollanders offer at once to lay down 40000 Rthl upon the contract concluded with them; therefore I am chiefly seeking every means to get the estates back. The valuation of the Zagorowo estates I shall transmit to Your Serene Highness as soon as I can work again. It comes out at 22140 Rthl annual yield; but that can only be reckoned from Midsummer, and when all arrears are paid and all interference of the government is lifted.
