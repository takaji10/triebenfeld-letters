--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports from the Congress of Vienna on the Hohenlohe family's entailed estates: Minister von Humboldt has promised particular support, and the State Chancellor thinks the money a trifle but expects trouble from the Justice department over annulling a settlement the old Prince approved. Argues that Cosmar forced the dying Princess Sacken into a forged settlement loading it with debts, and that his guardianship and her life-interest have lapsed. Satisfying the Courland heirs would cost 112,000 Rthl; a damaged schedule totals 79,500 and 147,000 Rthl.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Poland's fate too has been decided, and a more favourable lot has fallen to us than had been believed. For Your Serene Highness there is now nothing that need be begged for on the intervening side — the affairs of the Empire will doubtless soon come up for hunting [uncertain: Jagt], of which what the Privy [uncertain: Ir] Goerch submitted on behalf of the whole House of Hohenlohe follows here in copy – His Excellency the Minister von Humboldt has promised me to support Your Serene Highness's cause particularly. Upon the paper submitted before me through the Prince State Chancellor's office, of which I sent the copy by the last post, His Serene Highness was pleased most graciously to express himself at the first report in these words: The money is a trifle, that shall be given, since so much can be gained thereby; but with the Justice department we shall doubtless get much noise over the annulment of the settlement, the more so as the old Prince himself approved it. Meanwhile it is remarkable that the deceased Prince's Sacken affairs disposed of the revenues of the entailed estates and appointed an executor of the testament; the estates never belonged to her at all. I have once more set out everything properly, and in particular that Cosmar drove the old [uncertain: Kering] Princess, on account of the Courland loan which the former Princes of Hohenlohe co-signed, to despair, and brought her to the point that, on the edge of the grave, she subscribed the false settlement forged by Cosmar, and allowed it to happen that so many debts were entered therein, whereby all other creditors were to be pushed back.

I maintain that the old Princess after her death could not have the least share in

the revenues of the entailed estate, still less after her death [text lost] because with her death her right of life-interest was to lapse, and [text lost] Cosmar having been declared guardian of the minor, this ceases when [text lost] Princes Adolph and Alexander were of age, and since the former did not [text lost] receive estates, but was the sole heir of the estate of the Princes Sacken [text lost] Cosmar has nothing whatever to do with the entailed estates and would have to be [text lost] dismissed here — Meanwhile Prince Adolph would be, within a year and a day — all [text lost] of age in 2 to 3 years, whereupon his whole interference likewise ceases, the [text lost] would receive merely, as a higher [uncertain: höhe], an appanage of 1200 Rthl out of the entailed estates. All the anxieties of Princess Sacken, which had induced her and even the Prince to sign the notorious settlement swindled through by Cosmar in fraudem tertii, would cease at once if the Courland heirs were satisfied. And that can now be done with 112000 Rthl. Not only on this account had, as was said in the earlier submission, an agreement already been reached with the attorney of the heirs of the estate, but the other creditors had also been so far brought round that they would be content with 12000 Rthl yearly for as long as the Prince lives — To the question — but will the estates really yield so much that the 112000 Rthl and everything that the deed of foundation states, and also the 12000 Rthl to the other creditors, can be paid? Answer: Certainly – I have laid at His Serene Highness's feet the proof of the yield and of the sums to be paid —

Well! my friend, it turns then upon your [uncertain: Oe] surety. The two Emperors and the King with [uncertain: Pfar] Wilhelm returned last night to Hungary. The King of Bavaria of [uncertain: Deresemberg] meanwhile remained here with their hereditary princes, as did the King of Denmark. Grand Duke Constantine had meanwhile been viewing places abroad — Respectfully I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's

Vienna, the 28th of October

1814.

humble and obedient servant
