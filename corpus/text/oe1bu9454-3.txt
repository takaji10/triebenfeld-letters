--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Desperate appeal for money: only 932 Rthl received in five months against 1400 Rthl reckoned for four weeks, leaving 170 th owed to the Prince and Barbe, and no 12 fl. for a hired coach to visit Minister Nesselrode and Privy Councillor Schröder over the Pohl affair; begs 200 ff be sent at once. Advises caution in nominating an administrator of the entailed estates in the King's name — a devoted man able to give cash security, with economic and ironworks knowledge — and urges a Pro Memoria be sent quickly for the Prince Chancellor's approval.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Two letters, of the 19th and the 28th of January, I have received — no more. Against that I have sent 7 pieces from here since the 13th — in all of them I have begged for help; in the one of the 19th Your Serene Highness gives hope, in the one of the 28th you say not a word about it. On what, most gracious Lord, do you suppose I am to live here amid this frightful dearness? I reckoned at the outset 1400 Rthl for 4 weeks — as yet I have received no more than 932 Rthl, and that over 5 months. It is a hell, a torment, that I owe the Prince and Barbe 170 th; I am almost ashamed to death — life in Tobolcki's deserts is truly not so full of torment as mine — I would gladly work with all my soul, but with wholly empty hands — O it is an accursed life. — Tomorrow B means to drive with me to Minister Nesselrode and to the [uncertain: comforting] Privy [illegible] Councillor Schröder, so as to neglect nothing in the Pohl affair, but rather to prepare every avenue. But I truly do not know where I am to get the 12 fl. to pay the hired coach — In short, if I do not soon receive help, and if you do not very, very quickly find counsel, then everything goes to the devil; I can help myself no further, but must remove myself from here in secret, laden with shame and mockery. I have solicited much here on your behalf, and wished besides to bring it about that the Prince should visit you on his return journey, for he does not return by way of Dresden. — But I cannot decently show myself before him — My plan was then to give your military affairs the most fortunate turn and to bring this to the desired end. But how am I to come forward when I cannot for shame raise my eyes — I therefore beg you once more, for God's sake, send the 200 ff with all haste, that I may come out of debt. — Now this further point. — When you wish that, as administrator in the King's name, the entailed estates should be administered, here you must proceed very cautiously — such a man must be capable of giving security. König has, it is true, much money, but that is a [uncertain: Bectie] — I advise taking none of all your old people, and how easily may not one or the other

be called to account together with Cosmar — for naturally one will demand to know why no more was drawn from the estates, and there [uncertain: Bäte] a valuation might well take place — after which one would then ask where the revenues have gone. The most advisable thing is [text lost] a Pro Memoria of its own here — and to propose outright somebody who is to administer the whole entailed estates for the Royal account — but this must be a man who, 1st, is truly attached and devoted to you, most gracious Prince, so that the last fraud may not prove worse than the first — for such a man can prepare for you either heaven or hell. 2. And he must be capable of giving security, the security to be in cash money, not [uncertain: aprin] or in landed property — 3rdly he must possess economic knowledge as well as having learning otherwise. 4thly he must not be wholly without knowledge of the ironworks branch — but if he cleaves faithfully to Your Serene Highness, then he will be able to be instructed therein by you; and he must go to work without brutality, be prudent of course, so that the old bloodsuckers do not make an X out of a U for him — If you propose, particularly with these qualities, the Prince Chancellor will certainly approve. But these things I must have here very quickly, for I hope within a few days to obtain the Prince's resolution, both as to the advance and as to the Polish estates, and then I shall not remain here a moment longer, especially if I am put in a position to pay off the debts — also because I confidently hope to move the Prince to go somewhat by way of Schlawe. I should think you might send somebody here with my horses, either riding or driving — in 4 days the man can be here; then the money is received securely and the papers quickly — Take it to heart that your Pro Memoria must absolutely, even if the money should already be on the way, nevertheless be had very quickly and certainly in the matter censured above.
