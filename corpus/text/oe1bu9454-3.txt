3 Two letters, of the 19th and 28th of January, I have received, and no more. Against that, since the 13th I have sent 7 pieces from here — in all of them I have begged for help; in the one of the 19th Your Serene Highness gives hope, in the one of the 28th you say not a word about it. On what, gracious Lord, do you suppose I am to live here, with the frightful dearness of everything? I reckoned at the outset on 1400 rt for 4 weeks — as yet I have received no more than 932 rt, and that over 5 months — It is a hellish torment that I owe the Prince and Barbe 170 #; I am almost dying of shame — life in Tobolcki's wastes is truly not so full of torment as mine — I would gladly be [uncertain: Seelen] active, but with wholly empty hands — Oh, it is an accursed life — Tomorrow B[arbe] means to drive with me to Minister Nesselrode and to the [uncertain: for comfort] privy hope-councillor Schröder, so as to neglect nothing in the Polish matter, but rather to [uncertain: forestall] all the routes. But I truly do not know where I am to get the 12 fl. to pay the hired coach — in short, if I do not soon receive help, and if you do not very, very quickly provide a remedy, everything will go to the devil; I cannot help myself further, but must take myself off from here secretly, burdened with disgrace and derision.

I have brought much about here for you, and wished just now to bring this about too, that the Prince should visit you on his return journey, for he is not returning by way of Dresden. — But I cannot decently show myself before him — My plan was then to give your military affairs the happiest turn and to bring this to the desired end. But how am I to come forward, when for shame I cannot raise my eyes —

Of the Prince State Chancellor's journey Your Serene Highness must as yet say not a word to any living soul — It is only according to my and B[arbe]'s plan.

I therefore beg you once again, for God's sake, send the 200 # with all speed, that I may get clear of the debt — Now this besides — [uncertain: Wenn] do you wish, as administrator in the King's name, to administer the entailed estates, here you must go to work very cautiously — such a man must be capable of giving security. König has money enough, to be sure, but he is a beast — I advise you to take none of all your old people, and how easily may not one or the

other be called to account together with Cosmar — for naturally they will want to know why no more was drawn from the estates, and there a valuation [uncertain: Bäte] well take place — after which one would then ask, where have the [uncertain: Recenzen] gone?
