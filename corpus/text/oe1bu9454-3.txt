--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A desperate plea for money: Triebenfeld has had only 932 Rthl in five months against the 1400 Rthl he reckoned for four weeks, is 170 Rthl in debt to the Prince and to Barben, cannot even find 12 fl. for a hired coach, and threatens to leave in secret. He wants 200 ff at once, reports planned calls on Minister Nesselrode and Privy Councillor Schröder over the Polish matter, and advises sending a separate memorandum naming a loyal, solvent, economically and ironworks-trained administrator for the entailed estates on the King's account, not one of the old servants.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Two letters, of the 19th and the 28th of January, I have received, and no more. Against that I have sent 7 pieces from here since the 13th — in all of them I have begged for help; in the one of the 19th Your Serene Highness gives hope, in the one of the 28th you say not a word about it. On what, most gracious Lord, do you suppose I am to live here amid this frightful dearness? I reckoned at the outset on 1400 Rthl for 4 weeks — as yet I have received no more than 932 Rthl, and that over 5 months. It is a hell and a torment that I am 170 th in debt to the Prince and to Barben; I am almost dying of shame — life in Tobolcki's wastes is truly not so full of torment as mine is — I would gladly work with all my soul, but with wholly empty hands — O, it is an accursed life. Tomorrow B. means to drive with me to Minister Nesselrode and, for consolation, to the Privy [uncertain: hoffen] Councillor Schröder, so as to neglect nothing in the Polish matter, but rather to prepare every avenue. But I truly do not know where I am to get the 12 fl. to pay the hired coach — In short, if I do not receive help soon and you do not very, very quickly find a remedy, then everything goes to the devil; I can help myself no further, but must remove myself from here in secret, laden with disgrace and mockery.

I have solicited much here on your behalf, and wished just now to bring it about that the Prince should visit you on his return journey, for he does not return by way of Dresden. — But I cannot decently show myself before him — My plan was then to give your military affairs the happiest turn and to bring this to the desired goal. But how am I to come forward when I cannot for shame raise my eyes — I beg you therefore once again, for God's sake, send the 200 ff with all haste, that I may get out of debt — Now this besides — When you wish that as administrator in the name of the King he should administer the entailed estates, here you must go to work very cautiously — such a man should be capable of giving security. König has indeed much money, but that is a [illegible] — I advise you to take none of all your old people, and how easily might not one or the other

other be called to account together with Cosmar — for naturally it will be desired to know why no more was drawn out of the estates, and there [uncertain: Bäte] a valuation would then take place — after which one would then ask where the arrears have gone. The most advisable course is [that they] send a separate Pro Memoria here — and propose outright someone who is to administer the whole of the entailed estates for the Royal account — but this must be a man who is truly attached and devoted to [uncertain: 1 taus] you, most gracious Prince, so that the last fraud does not turn out worse than the first — for such a person can make you a heaven or a hell.

2. And he must be capable of giving security, the securities to be given in cash money, not [uncertain: aprin] or in landed property —

3) Thirdly, he must possess economic knowledge as well as have learning otherwise. Fourthly, he must not be wholly without knowledge of the ironworks trade — provided, however, that he is faithfully attached to Your Serene Highness, he will be able to be instructed in it by you; and what is more, he must go to work without brutality, be naturally shrewd, so that the old blood-leeches do not make an X out of a U for him — If you propose, particularly with these qualities, the Prince Chancellor will certainly approve. But I must have these matters here very quickly, for I hope within a few days to receive the resolution of the Prince, both as to the advance and as to the Polish estates, and then I shall not remain here a moment longer once I am in particular put in a position to pay off the debts — also because I confidently hope to induce the Prince to go somewhat by way of Schlawen. I should think you would send someone here with my horses, either riding or driving — in 4 days the man can be here, so that the money is received securely and the papers quickly — Take it to heart that your Pro Memoria must absolutely, even if the money should already be on its way, nevertheless very quickly and certainly be here in the aforementioned matter.
