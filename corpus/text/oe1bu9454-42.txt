--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A desperate appeal for 2,068 Rthl to secure release from arrest in Berlin: French gendarmes seized Triebenfeld as the Prince's representative over 3,521 Rthl of interest arrears owed to the Invalids' Fund, and he has since juggled pledges, bills and depreciated state paper with von Zastrow, the merchant Lahr, the dyer Coste and Mathieu. He reports his own estate at Kontop sequestrated, six months' illness, protests lodged against the Zagorowo sequestration, a fiscal suit against Oppenheimer over an inflated 105,000 Rthl claim, and a scheme to clear 122,000 Rthl of debt cheaply through paper.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

If ever a human creature has been pressed down by want and tribulation and scourged through every class of suffering, then I am he now. It is now over a year since I was sent hither to effect this and that here, and then to return from here by way of Vienna to Königsberg.

Just as I was on the point of departing from here, I was seized by French gendarmerie on the order of the General Government and for that reason placed under strict arrest, namely that I, as Your Serene Highness's authorised representative, should at once pay to the Invalids' Fund the arrears of interest amounting to 3521 Rthl. The affair excited a terror by which my honour and credit suffered. Against surety given by the Chamber Court Councillor von Trevinitz and the innkeeper Mathien I was released from my arrest for 4 weeks, with the observation that within that time I should procure the 352) and pay the same together with costs to the French intendant Maluce in cash, in hard Courant, failing which I, together with my [uncertain: Coventin], should be taken into arrest anew.

During these 4 weeks, then, the money had to be procured

To obtain this sum in cash was a work of impossibility; I had to take paper at its nominal value, and in return promise to repay cash Courant after 3 months. The honest Minister von Zastrow gave a pledge and no joint bill of exchange, which [illegible] and I signed with him. The time ran out quickly and the merchant von der Lahr demanded 5000 Rthl together with interest. For the sake of Mr. von Zastrow and Grevenister I had to find counsel quickly — I therefore took from the dyer Coste 9000 Rthl in state paper at 6 per cent interest, which was deposited as cover, and with it the Zastrow pledge and the bill of exchange were redeemed. Coste had given me the state paper on the condition that I should return the same to him in precisely such paper. The paper stood very low at that time; I could not sell it, but pawned it, and that again cost heavy percentages and percentage charges. Meanwhile the 3000 Rthl from Your Most Serene Highness came in; of this I at once paid 2153 Rthl in cash to Lahn and bought paper for the remainder. Hecker gave 4000 Rthl in paper as surety; this I pawned again for 1000 Rthl and paid interest and costs. In order meanwhile, like all bank

bankers, to profit from the speculation in paper and to get out of the affair, I bought and sold paper to advantage, so that at present I have the whole 9000 Rthl for Coste together again, and nothing further is wanting but the cash interest of 340 Rthl. Once I have that, this entire sum is discharged and wholly settled. Meanwhile Lahr has sued me over the 3000 Rthl still coming to him, and is hindering my departure. I have deposited paper for it, but I may not leave until the matter is [uncertain: cleared]. Under the provincial law he must now be content with paper. Meanwhile I must pay the supplement, and this amounts, first, on the Coste paper, as said above, to 340 Rthl

2. further interest, court and other costs 568

3. For two bonds on which I still owe the whole bill of exchange.

360

4. For 2 bonds lying in deposit 800

coming to 2068. These 2068 Rthl must be procured and paid; then this exceedingly vexatious business is lifted and settled, and I can travel where I will.

If it is in any way in the world possible for Your Serene Highness, then drive

such together over there and send it with all haste to Kontop; otherwise I cannot stir from the spot — and yet it would be very good if I might wait upon Your Serene Highness in person, for I myself do not know how to help myself. Through this business of my having been arrested here I lost credit and health; my creditors awoke on every side and to this day are sequestrating Kontop upon me. I cannot go there myself, for I am under arrest here on Your Serene Highness's account. What pains me most is that Mathieu means in the end still to hold back the remainder, since I still owe him 1500 Rthl in coin. 2083 Rthl I have already paid him, and 1500 Rthl are still coming to him; on that account the man will not let me travel.

For nearly 6 months, on top of all this vexation, I lay very wretchedly ill, and this business too costs me some 300 Rthl here, since I have had to pay 170 Rthl to the doctor and to the apothecary. Never has misfortune pursued me as it has this year here. Yet this too will be got over, if only I receive the 2068 Rthl so that I can bring the Lahr matter entirely clear.

Find counsel for this, Your Serene Highness, I beg you heartily and urgently; otherwise I cannot get

away from here, and must sit here until help comes. From my estates I now have nothing; the taxes are too oppressive — I still do not know how I am to be rid of the sequestrator in Kontop, and the longer I sit here the greater is my loss!

The damage I suffer is dreadful. Your Serene Highness knows that I never complain, but now the water is at my throat. I beg you, most gracious Lord, have mercy and see to it that I now soon receive the 2068 Rthl, so that I may come into freedom. From the French General Government the order has been sent to the Ministry of Justice at Warsaw that the sequestration on account of the Invalids' Fund is to be lifted, and also that the interest extorted from [uncertain: one] is to be repaid out of the estates — these Your Serene Highness can collect yourself against the original dispositions, and in that way you lose nothing, and I may well be indemnified for the cover of the costs I have had here. As regards the sequestration otherwise imposed at Zagorowo, I have had the proper protestations entered, and against the Jew Oppenheimer, who to the

[uncertain: Bau] has ceded his claim, I have here framed a suit in which I am having him prosecuted by the fiscal authority — for he reckons up to himself [uncertain: prorenetica] and God knows what besides. He pretends to have 105000 to demand, which at bottom is a lie — He has 72000 Rthl to demand of me, and this, once the suit is decided, will be deposited here in Berlin in paper, and then everything there is in order.

With the Invalids' Fund and all the debts, 122000 Rthl would come out.

I have here a merchant who will take iron and other produce too, and will give money; if one were in a position to raise 60000 Rthl, one would have 120000 Rthl, as the paper now stands, and could offer everything [uncertain: head for head] — Were the paper to fall again to 30 per cent, then one could settle everything for at most 40000 Rthl — the plan has long since been drawn up by me; were I only first out of here, then everything should soon be brought clear.

Lay my petition to heart, Your Serene Highness, and see to it that I am rescued and set at liberty. I will willingly endure every hardship, travel day and night, only of my

liberty I will not and cannot be deprived. I am paralysed in everything; the very thought strikes down all my courage and makes me incapable of anything.

From Königsberg I have had letters of the 8th of this month; something consoling is written with regard to Your Serene Highness, and in particular Mr. G. von K. is said to have taken up Your Serene Highness's affairs with great [uncertain: earnestness]. After the return of the post from Petersburg a good outcome is looked for — Whether Const. will indeed effect anything good remains to be seen. He promised much — Only I no longer put much trust nowadays in such gentlemen's promises. I have been deceived too much and too often; I have also suffered too greatly, physically and morally, so that I still fear I shall become the worst of misanthropes. The Princess Sacken is said to be quite well. They have quartered General Decke upon her, with which she is very dissatisfied. Help speedily, Your Serene Highness, that I may get free from here, for then I shall fly to lay myself at your feet. In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Berlin, the 16th of January

1809.

faithful and most obedient servant

v Triebenfeld
