--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Returns letters from the Count of S., praising his straightforwardness but lamenting the lost chance to gain the Prince some 40,000-50,000 Rthl. Reinhardt reports from Bonn that the hoped-for treasury assistance has not materialised. Conflicting news on the South Prussian ministry: Minister von Goldbeck writes that Count von Hoym keeps it and has been given the Ansbach and Bayreuth provinces, while Haugwitz's confidant Priebich claims Haugwitz will have it. Chełmski has not yet appealed; a form of power of attorney awaits Metzer's return.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
To Your Most Serene Highness I most humbly return, with a kiss, the letters of the Count of S. most graciously communicated to me. It pleases me that the Count conducts himself so straightforwardly in the whole affair, and in the end perceives his own advantage. Only, on the other hand, I grieve all too much that in this matter I have once again had to experience a trait dishonouring to humanity, and further that the opportunity is taken from me of rendering Your Most Serene Highness a truly [uncertain: substantial] service and of gaining for Your Highness some 40 to 50000 Rthl. From Bonn, Reinhardt writes that

nothing has yet come about concerning the assistance of the treasury. God! how pitiable are the men [uncertain: people] whose words one may give credence to. From Minister von Goldbeck in Berlin I received a letter yesterday, in which he says: Up to now many rumours have been in circulation in the Residence concerning the South Prussian Department. A few days ago, however, I heard from a reliable man that His Excellency Count von Hoym retains it.

He writes that His Majesty had, in addition to his otherwise very extensive Justice Department, been pleased also to give him the Ansbach and Bayreuth provinces, and had ordered him to draft some new laws. Heaven knows what there is in all this. For about 3 days ago a confidant of Minister Haugwitz, a certain Priebich, passed through here; he firmly assured

Councillor Krüger that Minister Haugwitz was to be South Prussian Minister. Chełmski has not yet appealed, and perhaps the estates may also be leased out along with the rest at Midsummer without obstacle. Metzer, as the returning express brings the news from Grävenitz, was not at home, but is coming back in 2 days; then the form for the power of attorney, as also the other reports, will follow. In deepest reverence I remain, Your Most Serene Highness's, Breslau, the 1st of April

1798.

faithful and most humble servant
von Triebenfeld
