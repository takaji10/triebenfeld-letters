--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A distraught report from the Congress that seven earlier letters have gone unanswered, with desperate repeated pleas for 200 ducats: 100 Rthl are owed to the State Chancellor and 70 Rthl to Barben, and Triebenfeld says he is going hungry and losing credit. He reports Stägemann, Hardenberg and Humboldt favourable, money and an administrator expected, Kalisz and Saxony feared lost, Wellington's arrival, and Count Pourt's wish to buy though only 150 Rthl cash is available. He urges Prince August to revoke Cosmar's power of attorney publicly.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Your Serene Highness's letter of the 28th of last month I received half an hour ago, together with the reply of Herr Falkenhausen. Since I have long known the content of the report of the Bruger [uncertain: OC] court — these three weeks past — I pass over this in silence. — But now the shock that Your Serene Highness is said to have received no letters from me since the 13th of last month paralyses my senses and my understanding; I have in that time sent 7 letters from here, in which I reported many and important things.

I informed Your Serene Highness that I had written to Mr. Schöler on one of the [uncertain: Paranquetter] concerning Trąbczyn, — I reported that I had in like manner given a letter of thanks to the privy councillor of state Stägemann. I informed you that I had submitted the correspondence of Mr. Kircheisen etc., which had been sent to me, to the Prince, and had sent my own letter with it in copy. I have faithfully written to you what has happened in this time and what has been said here about Your Serene Highness's affair. I informed you that even Kalisz is lost to Prussia, and that those districts in which the estates lie fall to [uncertain: Russia] — and that it will now cost much trouble to hold the same back, because there everything is done by money and petticoats.

I further informed Your Serene Highness that now Saxony too — at least the finest part, that is from Weissenfeld onward over Dresden and Leipzig as far as Lauban — the devil is carrying off. I wrote to you by whom the greatest [uncertain: cabals] are being forged. In my last letter I reported that Wellington has arrived, and that perhaps one may hope from that.

In all the letters I set out my boundless distress, but above all [uncertain: more skilfully] and urgently begged that 200 ducats be sent, because I must hand over 100 Rthl to the Prince Chancellor and 70 Rthl to Barben — and this may well be the reason why no letters are said to have arrived there. — Hard, more than hard, indeed unjust it is that Your Serene Highness lets me suffer so dreadfully. — I go about here a beggar's dog, and hunger again — Have you then no mercy at all upon me — do you then wish that I should become an utter laughing-stock? — I am to be effective and to act with deliberation — Only send someone here who will try the thing too with empty hands, indeed with an empty stomach. Long I went about like a [uncertain: Laupenhänd] — now I have clothing on my body, to be sure, but in exchange a hollow stomach — I am to live off one and another of the men who labour honestly — what am I to say to that? B. is beginning to look askance. Only yesterday evening he said to me, this does look curious — with those people words are apparently no gospel either — I am still dying of shame, and that from [uncertain: Schreitwillen]. And now first of all the Prince: already Jordan has mockingly dunned me for the 100 Thl; the thing may end tragically — And I swear before God that I shall not [uncertain: go under unblamed] — for here everything is at stake — [uncertain: Hunt] therefore upon your letter of today. — The papers all lie with the privy councillor of state Stägemann; had he not so frightfully much work, everything would already be settled. The Prince Chancellor wishes you well, as does Minister Humboldt, and so does the privy councillor of state Stägemann together with the whole bureau — There are two secret adversaries here who would gladly do harm. But they are not likely to succeed; the money will be paid and an administrator appointed.

The other matters concerning Your Serene Highness resolve themselves. In the [uncertain: Ko:] affair Count Pourt: has already replied — he wishes to buy — [uncertain: but] at the moment he can dispose of no more than 150 Rthl in cash — In a few weeks, however, considerable sums are coming in to him from France and England — He has further required information about [uncertain: Eris] and the other matter, which has been given him in the most conclusive terms, since I have the files here. He does not wish to be taken in, for he says he is the father of 7 children.

We have copied out everything, sent him the assessments and set him [uncertain: weill Anfait] — for B. is a thoroughly honest fellow and goes very straight — He has asked you to inform him soon when he should come to Schlawenschitz, so that preparations can be made.

Of the end of the Congress there is as yet nothing to be heard. And the reports given to Your Serene Highness concerning Saxony and Poland are not true — rather the contrary still stands firm at present — One will now see what Wellington. The [uncertain: Näße] Empress departs from here on the 18th or 20th for Carlsruhe to her mother, will declare — Should the Congress be at an end, the Emperor goes there too, and both then come to [uncertain: Revhir]. For the rest, there is continual talk here of dreadful new wars — and even men who formerly contested these rumours zealously now shrug their shoulders. Two ladies and two ministers are playing [uncertain: verstatt]. Only our upright Prince Hardenberg alone stands firm as a rock — Would to God that Wellington's instructions were in accord with his sentiments — We live in a dreadful period. — May God only turn everything to the best — The King of Württemberg grows ever more subdued — He receives no other answer upon his projected constitution than that he is referred, provisionally, to the general constitution now in progress.

That Prince August has sent the written undertaking is very good, if only he would now also, with the utmost speed, revoke the power of attorney for Cosmar and cancel it entirely — Cosmar will twist and turn before he gives it up, and may very well yet play mad tricks. Here it is necessary that the [uncertain: J. Aequit] write at once to the Ministry of Justice, to the Chamber Court, to the Bruger Superior Provincial Court, and into Saxony to the judicial authority under which Oppurg lies, and write quickly in every place without attracting notice, that he has herewith wholly revoked and annulled the power of attorney given to the said Cosmar under such-and-such a date, wherefore he declares whatever Cosmar may wish or may do in his name to be null and void — indeed the young Prince could have this made public in the Berlin and Silesian newspapers besides — If this is done, then the fellow is at once flung to the antipodes — pray press this, Your Serene Highness, with burning zeal. Grev: writes that Madame C. stands in still higher favour with the Minister of Justice than [text lost] one must therefore arm oneself with their weapons

her husband

and bar to him, the said C., every way out.

Only for God's sake send also, as quickly as possible, the 200 [uncertain: ducats]; I am in the utmost distress and despair, and thereby unable to act with the requisite force.

Most reverently I remain

Your Serene Highness's

P.S. The subsequently submitted correspondence

with the Ministers of Justice has, with my petition,

produced a very good effect

Vienna, the 9th of February 1815

most faithful and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld
