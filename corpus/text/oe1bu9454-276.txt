--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports on enquiries made for Minister v. Humboldt through Justice Commissioner Eberhard at Brieg, whose enclosed letter he suspects betrays a treachery originating in Brieg, since nothing has been ordered and the matter still rests unreported with Privy Councillor of State Stägemann. Fürst Hardenberg has again promised help, with Fürst Bartenstein to remind him. Urges that Eberhard be sent to Vienna to settle with the Princesses; insists on the 112000 Rthl advance. Notes the King's reply as a political formula drafted by Humboldt, the King of Saxony's refusal at Preßburg, imperial travel plans, and the Polish estates submission awaiting money for translations.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! The Herr Minister v Humboldt had entrusted a matter to me, in which I, to see clearly in it, [was] to write to Brieg — I applied in this matter to the Herr Justice Commissioner Eberhard, and upon his answer received yesterday I obtained the enclosed Copy of a letter. I am [uncertain: bewildered/admonished] over it, and I, like everyone else, do not know what one is to think of it. Nothing whatever has been ordered here, for everything still lies with the Privy Councillor of State Stägemann, who has as yet had no report made to him on the matter. If, then, anything has been written to Berlin, it is a treachery which can have had its origin only in Brieg itself. A few days ago Fürst Hardenberg once again declared most solemnly that he would certainly help Your Serene Highness, and tomorrow Fürst Bartenstein goes to him again with a reminder. Everyone is in expectation of the happiest outcome. Only now the Eberhard news paralyses my tongue and hand. I have always demanded only the 112000 mk, with which the Curian heirs were to be satisfied, and have begged that under Your Serene Highness's [uncertain: aegis] the estates might be administered for the discharge of the advance of 112000 Rthl. Everything else then follows of itself — this is the view of every man. The Herr C. Eberhard says in his letter that if he could be here, he would certainly have concluded and brought about the settlement with the Princesses — the Princesses are indeed here and remain here also — were it therefore in any way possible in the world, let Your Serene Highness nevertheless send him here, for even should they pitch their strings higher, one could at once make a report thereof to the Fürst Chancellor and direct everything for the best. The answer of the King mentioned in Eberhardt's letter was given to the Princesses 8 days after the arrival of the Monarch here in Vienna, where the 3 ladies pressed him very hard / which I also wrote to Your Serene Highness at once — the King said therein that he could not concern himself with the matter here, that it must wait until his return to Berlin. But the King, so far as I know, is not in the least minded to concern himself with it.

Nor has he himself any need of it under the Landrecht. For according to this one may proceed against the guarantor only when every means has been tried and every fund exhausted. But so far as I know for certain, and may confide to Your Serene Highness under the seal of secrecy, that Royal answer is only a political turn of phrase, which the Herr Minister v Humboldt, His Excellency, drafted — one did not wish to affront the niece of Fürst Talleyrand, the present great friend of Fürst Metternich, [and] therefore gave the 3 ladies that exceedingly civil letter with the assurance that the King, after his return to Berlin, would have the matter laid before him – of composition or satisfaction there stands not a word therein. If now Herr Eberhard could come here with all speed, I would see to it that the ladies were told, in the event, in so good a manner, that it is doubted whether the King would concern himself with the matter, and [that he] would rather remain true to the declaration once given by him. They would then take it up again from other sides, and would quickly come to a composition. For the rest, matters look here very [uncertain: baut]. Discretion must wrap itself in the thickest veil over things which happen day and night — [text lost] to stand not in a grey distance. Very thunder-pregnant clouds. The King of Saxony has arrived in Preßburg and refuses to accept the portion of Saxony conceded to him — He insists upon the whole of Saxony — It is believed, however, that he will in the end accommodate himself. For if he did not, Saxony would be strongly occupied throughout by Prussia. The [uncertain: Russian] Empress departs hence in a few days for Baden – thither the Emperor follows her on the 16th; the King travels 10 days after, straight to Berlin; there the Russian too comes, and the Emperor of this place, and they will remain together for some 12 days. The submission concerning the Polish estates lies ready; I await only the command of the Fürst Chancellor and money in order to be able to redeem the translations. The Eberhard letter I [uncertain: enclose] in the original; the writing comes very [uncertain: hard] to me. Afterwards I beg to have it back. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Serene Highness's Vienna, the 4th of March 1865. most obedient servant v Triebenfeld
