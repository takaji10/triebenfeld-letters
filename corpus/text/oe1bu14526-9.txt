--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A six-year lease of the demesne farm of Kopojno, from Midsummer 1805 to Midsummer 1811, made before the Hohenlohe patrimonial court at Trąbczyn between the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, through his general authorised representative the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, and Maciey von Niedzwiedzki: 1,500 Rthl a year, payable in advance, in Pausch und Bogen without valuation, brewery, distillery and mill rents excluded, with terms on labour services, firewood, repairs and crop rotation. A supplement adds 13 Hufen at 625 Rthl, raising the rent to 2,125 Rthl.

The Princely Hohenlohe Patrimonial Court hereby makes known that before it, between His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, through his general mandatory the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, as lessor on the one part, and Mr Maciey von Niedzwiedzki as lessee on the other part, the following lease contract concerning the estate of Kopojno has been declared ad Protocollum, which reads word for word as follows: Actum Trąbczyn, the 17th of August 1805. Before the undersigned justiciary of this place there appeared in person, 1st, the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld as the known general authorised representative of His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, hereditary lord and lord of the court of this place, and

2nd, the local holder of the propination rights lease, Mr Maciey von Niedzwiedzki. Com-

The appearing parties are personally known to the undersigned, and there exists no doubt as to their capacity validly to perform judicial acts. They declare freely and after due deliberation the following lease contract concluded between them, to be entered in the judicial protocol.

§ I. That is to say, the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, by virtue of the power of attorney held from His Most Serene Highness, leases out the demesne farm of Kopojno, just as the same is now divided and arranged, together with the winter and summer sowing now standing, the fishery in the waters and in the Warthe stream, and all the remaining usufructs, yet with the exclusion of the brewery and distillery, as being separately leased, and without any livestock or field inventory, to Mr Maciey Niedzwiedzki for

six consecutive years, namely from Midsummer Day of the present year 1805 until Midsummer Day 1811, for an annual rent of 1500 Rthl, in writing one thousand five hundred Rthl Courant.

§ II. This rent money shall be paid annually in advance, namely each time on Midsummer Day, that is the 24th of June, in good Prussian convention money; and since the rent for the first year, amounting to 1500 Rthl, in writing one thousand five hundred, [uncertain: not good] has already been paid in cash, receipt is hereby given for the due receipt thereof.

§ III. This lease is concluded in Pausch und Bogen without a valuation, and therefore no subsequent reckoning in respect of the yield takes place.

§ IV. The lessee must effect the tillage of the fields with his own draught teams

and there shall be made over to him, for hand-services only, the whole of the komorniks and cottagers present in Kopojno with their services, and these shall receive from the lessor, namely

a., each komornik, ten in number, four Morgen, and

b., each cottager, whose number is undetermined, two Morgen of Magdeburg measure, free of rent. As to the working, the lessee must come to terms with these people according to his own convenience.

§ V. Free grinding of the bread grain is likewise granted to the lessee at the mills at Tarzewo and Kopojno; the cash rent from these mills, on the other hand, as also from the rent-paying peasants in Kopojno, remains to the lessor.

§ VI. Besides the cash rent, the lessee assumes the settlement of all Royal dues in taxes,

forage deliveries and the like, as also the allowance due to the monastery of Ląd, without any compensation or abatement of the rent.

If in future these dues come to be apportioned over the entire surface area of the estates, then any resulting decrease shall accrue to the lessee's benefit.

§ VII. The lessee received for his own need 50 annually, in writing fifty cords of pine firewood, from the lordship's forest, which wood he must cart himself, and likewise have felled at his own cost, or else pay the felling-wage for it to the princely office. He also receives two cartloads of hard timber every year.

§ VIII. All dwelling and farm buildings and gardens situated in Kopojno the lessee receives for his use; he must however maintain the buildings in roof and frame at his own cost. The larger repairs, on the other hand, fall to the lessor,

as also all new buildings.

§ IX. Since the lessee for the present does not receive the fields with a determined sowing, the appearing parties have agreed between themselves that the lessee binds himself, at the expiry of the lease term, to leave behind the one field sown with winter grain, the second duly manured with summer grain, and the third field as fallow worked up in husbandmanlike fashion, without either party making any demands upon the other on account of surplus or deficient sowing. The fields shall be divided into three wholly equal parts.

§ X. This lease begins from Midsummer Day of this year and the lessee renounces all subsequent reckonings in respect of the usufructs of the various heads of husbandry hitherto foregone.

§ XI. The clearing of the field ditches and the upkeep of the bridges falls to the lessee; yet for the latter he shall receive the necessary timber free of charge, and the village people must render assistance in this.

§ XII. The lessee may make over the estate to another in sub-lease only with the foreknowledge and express consent of the lessor; in the contrary case such a sub-lease is invalid and the lessee is answerable to the lessor for all damage or detriment arising therefrom.

§ XIII. In the event of misfortunes occurring, such as fire from heaven, or arson by evil-disposed persons, total hail-strike and war, the lessee shall receive a remission such as is granted to the lessees on the Royal domains according to the principles of the Chamber.

Upon all other misfortunes, whether they arise from crop failure or in whatever manner it may be, the lessee expressly renounces claim; he also hereby binds himself expressly to make every indemnification which is caused by his own fault or that of his people.

§ XIV. The lessee must maintain the fields in good manured condition, and may not sell any straw or hay off the estate, unless he had the same absolutely in surplus.

§ XV. At the expiry of the years of lease no particular notice of termination is required from either the one side or the other; rather the lessee is bound to vacate at Midsummer Day 1811; yet there shall be allowed him the

vacating period customary in this region, up to 14 days after Midsummer Day; but he must see to the necessary carting-out himself.

§ XVI. The lessee shall have free pasture in that portion of grazing-ground and woodland which remains over for the manorial lordship (Dominium) and is not dismembered.

§ XVII. The costs of this contract and of the future re-delivery both parties shall bear by halves.

§ XVIII. For the fences the lessee shall receive the requisite brushwood, and the same shall be assigned to him upon his application. Both parties promise to hold firmly to this contract and, having been judicially instructed as to the consequences, renounce all legal subterfuges available against it, in particular the objection of an injury suffered above or below the half, of a cunning persuasion, of overhaste,

of seduction, fear, coercion, of matters otherwise agreed than written down, or not rightly understood. They moved that this contract be drawn up, and, after it had been read out and explained in the Polish language, have in token of their approval signed below in their own hands, whereby it is noted that Mr von Niedzwiedzki has expressly waived the keeping of a Polish protocol.

Read and approved, Peter Friedrich August von Triebenfeld.

Actum Trąbczyn, the 24th of August 1805. As a supplement to the lease contract concluded on the

17th of this month concerning the demesne farm of Kopojno, the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld for his part and Mr Maciey von Niedzwiedzki on the other part this day desire the following agreement.

§ I. Mr von Niedzwiedzki receives, in addition to the twenty-six Hufen already taken in lease, a further 13 Hufen, in writing thirteen Hufen Magdeburg measure, of arable and meadow for the six years of lease, and pays for this an annual rent of 625 Rthl, in writing six hundred and twenty-five thaler Courant; so that henceforth the whole rent of Kopojno amounts to 2125 Rthl, in writing two thousand one hundred and twenty-five Rthl.

§ 2

This additional rent of six hundred and twenty-five thaler is for the present year, from Midsummer 1805 to the same term 1806, already paid forthwith in advance, and the councillor of war von Triebenfeld hereby gives receipt for the due receipt thereof in the most legally binding manner. In future it shall be settled each time together with the principal sum praenumerando.

§ 3. These thirteen Hufen are incorporated into the demesne farm, and the lessee must in future return the third part of the arable sown with winter grain and a third with summer grain, and pay all dues thereon as is determined in the principal contract. The appearing parties request that the foregoing agreement be carried over into the principal contract to be drawn up, and, after it had been read out and explained,

have in token of their approval signed below in their own hands.
