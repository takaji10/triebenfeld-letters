--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports from the Congress that the Prince State Chancellor Hardenberg has been confined to bed for five days, alarming because he has drafted the Prince's submissions himself and means to help; Minister Humboldt hints the case is already won, and Court Councillor Hahn's opinion supports it. The military claims lie dormant until Berlin. Everything turns on ending the Courland business, defeating Cosmar and securing possession of the entailed estates. Announces his arrival in person on the 11th.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

His Serene Highness the Prince State Chancellor has today already kept to his room and bed for the 5th day. If anyone quietly prays to God for the preservation of this man, then truly I do so. [uncertain: Not only that] the whole machine would come to a standstill, but Your Serene Highness's affairs would be left lying half-way. At present everything still stands very well – Prince H. wishes Your Serene Highness to be helped; may God preserve him for that – the gentleman's labours are exorbitant, and notwithstanding this he has written up Your Serene Highness's matters, so far as I submitted them, entirely himself. A true proof that he means to help. The Prince has required a further report, but from where I do not know; only Minister Humboldt seems to know more, yet says nothing further than that we already had a won game – Court Councillor Hahn's opinion has had its effect besides, because everything I have said is confirmed by it. I hope, with firm trust in God, that Your Serene Highness, through the help of the exalted Prince Hard:, will now yet enjoy a very peaceful and happy old age. Of Your Serene Highness's military affairs there is at present complete silence; B., however, says this is kept in reserve and laid aside until Berlin – which is well too, for there the helpers are more numerous. For the present everything depends solely upon the Courland matter being brought to an end, so that Your Serene Highness may be rid of this dreadful business, that Cosmar may be overthrown, and that you may come into possession of the estates as actual lord of the entailed estate, and no longer have to live there on the mercy of your servants. Everything else will then follow of itself. – Just now B. was able to, and brings me the news, that His Majesty the King

and everything that is Prussian, but the D[?] departs from here for Berlin – the Emperor; all the rest follows with everything belonging to his court on the 10th to that place, and at the same time all the other sovereigns and other high personages; then too the worthy Emperor Franz sets out on his journey thither, and presumably this will last through the Carnival. To Your Serene Highness I shall now write no further. May you also be pleased to send no further letters to me, for on the 11th of [uncertain: M] I intend to lay myself personally at your most exalted feet. – In deepest reverence I remain

Your Serene Highness's

Vienna, the 30th of November

1814.

faithful and most obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld
