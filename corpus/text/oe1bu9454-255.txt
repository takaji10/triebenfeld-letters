--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports from Vienna that State Chancellor Hardenberg has kept to his room and bed for five days, and fears the Prince's business would be left half finished; Hardenberg has drafted the submissions himself and clearly means to help, Minister Humboldt hints the game is already won, and Court Councillor Hahn's opinion supports Triebenfeld's account. Military affairs are deferred to Berlin. All now depends on ending the Curian matter, overthrowing Cosmar, and securing possession of the entailed estate. Notes the courts' removal to Berlin on the 10th, and announces his own arrival on the 11th.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! His Serene Highness the Prince State Chancellor has today already for the 5th day been keeping to his room and bed. If anyone prays to God in silence for the preservation of this man, then truly I [uncertain: am] he. Not only that the whole machine would come to a standstill, but Your Serene Highness's affairs would be left lying half-way. At present everything still stands very well – the Prince H. wishes Your Serene Highness to be helped; may God indeed preserve him – the labours of the gentleman are exorbitant, and notwithstanding this he has written up Your Serene Highness's matters, so far as I submitted them, entirely by himself. A true proof that he means to help. The Prince has further required a report, but from whence I do not know; only Minister Humboldt seems to know more, yet says nothing further than that we had already [uncertain: won our] game – Court Councillor Hahn's opinion has also had effect, because everything I have said is confirmed thereby. I hope with [uncertain: firmest] trust in God that Your Serene Highness, through the help of the exalted Prince Hardenberg, will yet enjoy a very tranquil and happy old age. Concerning Your Serene Highness's military affairs, one is now entirely silent; B. however says this is reserved and laid aside until Berlin – well and good, for there the helpers are more numerous. For the present everything depends solely upon this, that the Curian matter be brought to an end, that Your Serene Highness be rid of this hideous business, that Cosmar be overthrown, and that you attain as actual lord of the entailed estate to the possession of the properties, and need no longer live there upon the mercy of your servants. Everything else will then follow of itself. Just now B. could [text lost] and brings me the news that His Majesty the King

and everything that is Prussian, but the [uncertain: Dt.] departs from here to Berlin – the Emperor. All the rest follows with everything belonging to his court thither on the 10th, and at the same time all the other sovereigns and other high personages; then the worthy Emperor Franz too sets out on his way thither, and presumably this will last through the Carnival. To Your Serene Highness I shall now write no further. May you also be pleased to send no further letters to me, for on the 11th of M[arch?] I think to lay myself personally at your most exalted feet. In deepest reverence I remain Your Serene Highness's Vienna, the 30th of November 1814. most faithfully obedient servant v Triebenfeld
