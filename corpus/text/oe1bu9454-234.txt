--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports on negotiations with Weigel, for whom an express messenger has been sent to Breslau; if the plan succeeds a debt of some 54,000 Rthl will be cleared and the estates free in nine or ten years, some 90,000-odd Rthl of debts discharged without loss of substance. The Invalids' Fund, owed some 9,000 Rthl arrears of interest, has laid arrest on everything; proposes that 50,000 Rthl from the Sacken bequest pay it off, with Prince August's and the King's intervention. Urges dispatch of the instrument, judicially attested or signed by two witnesses. Written from his bed.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Beginning 2. How matters stand between me and Weigel, Your Serene Highness will see from the enclosure. Since that time he has already written twice more to the Jewish [uncertain: Bang] a Redlich and enquired when, where and how he might find me; I have already written to him and today am sending an express messenger to Breslau to him again, that he is to come with all speed. — If my plan succeeds, of which I have no doubt, then you are rid of a debt item of some 54000 Rthl, and after 9, at most 10 years you will have the estates clear on that side. — It is much if some 90000 Rthl of debts are extinguished without losing a Groschen of the substance of the estates. Now everything in this matter depends upon the most speedy arrival of Herr Weigel — if he presents himself, then the matter is assuredly agreeable to him, and the plan I shall lay before him so evident that he will certainly enter into it and be satisfied.

The Invalids' Fund is and remains for me now the one stone of stumbling — it has some 9000 Rthl of arrears of interest to demand, and has therefore laid an attachment upon everything, upon old as well as new interest. Were it now possible that out of the Sacken estate or bequest the capital of 50 000 Rthl could be paid to the Invalids' Fund, and that I could assign the 50000 Rthl to that purpose, then you would be master, for I should certainly not press. — This is the only means of obtaining an entirely free hand. My counsel is that Prince August lend a hand here in the work, that he for his own person insist upon it — [uncertain: ſchöffen] the spectacle. I am writing on that account to the King, that he should command it, for he permitted at that time that the money should and must be paid to me upon the mortgage. The King can also now press for it that it be paid out of the great

bequest — the only thing would be that instead of to the Invalids' Fund one should thereafter (when the old woman has pasted everything over far too devilishly and has tied even Prince August's hands) pay interest yearly there. You would then be rid of the accursed execution, and could, once everything is arranged, draw yearly revenues of 6 to 8000 Rthl.

The matter must go through by way of me. I write to the King and say that I received the monies at that time with Your Majesty's consent out of the Invalids' Fund. The [uncertain: Zwalden] presses me at the same time as the Prince. — Is the Prince owing me that, or a [uncertain: könnigstiger] capital? I should call it in in order to satisfy the Invalids out of it — the Princes of Hohenlohe, father and son, wish this too; the guardians (or whoever it now may be) forbid this. Command, Your Royal Majesty, that the money which I absolutely must receive be taken out of the Sacken bequest, and the Invalids' Fund paid

which must in return be bound to give me [uncertain: Jura lehre] — I concede if need be that the Sacken estate should for the present draw the interest out of the estates of Zagorowo in my stead. Only I wish thereby to be covered for the future, and to see the Prince freed from the most hideous pressure. If we do it so, the matter is assuredly settled, and if the King himself commands it, one will not wish to [text lost]. Your Serene Highness, consider it all well, and write to me about everything without any foreign interference whatever, with the utmost speed. By God, all will go very well. Only send the instrument, and should it be quite impossible for it to be authenticated [uncertain: quickly in court], then write it yourself and have it signed by 2 witnesses — but it is better done before a court; very, very urgent for me. The writing has cost me blood and sweat. God grant that you can read it better than I am able to write it. Lying in bed one does not write well. Ever

Your Serene Highness's

Blizanow, the 6th July 1812

faithful servant

v. Triebenfeld
