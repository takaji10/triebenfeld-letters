--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld hat einen Eilboten nach Wrocław geschickt, damit Weigel schnell kommt. Gelingt sein Plan mit Weigel, sei der Fürst eine Schuldpost von etwa 54000 Rt los. Hindernis ist die Invaliden Casse: Sie fordert rund 9000 Rt rückständige Zinsen und hat Arrest auf alle Zinsen gelegt. Triebenfeld will beim König erwirken, dass das Kapital von 50000 Rt aus dem Sackenschen Nachlass an die Casse gezahlt wird, und empfiehlt dafür die Mithilfe von Fürst August.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld has sent an express messenger to Wrocław so that Weigel will come quickly. If his plan with Weigel succeeds, he says the Prince will be rid of a debt of about 54,000 Rthl. The obstacle is the Invalids' Fund: it is demanding some 9,000 Rthl in arrears of interest and has placed an attachment on all interest. Triebenfeld wants to obtain from the King that the capital of 50,000 Rthl from the Sacken estate be paid to the Fund, and he recommends the help of Prince August for this.

Enclosure 2. How I stand with Weigel, Your Serene Highness will see from the appendix. Since that time he has already written twice more to the Jewish, Bang a Redlich, and enquired when, where and how he might find me. I have already written to him and am today sending an express messenger to Wrocław, again to him, that he is to come with all speed. — If my plan succeeds, which I do not doubt, then you are rid of a debt item of some 54,000 Rthl., and after 9 or at most 10 years you will have the estates clear on that side. — It is much if you extinguish some 90,000 Rthl of debts without losing a Groschen of the substance of the estates. Now everything in this matter depends on the most speedy arrival of Mr Weigel — if he presents himself, then the matter is certainly agreeable to him, and the plan I shall lay before him so evident that he will certainly agree to it and be satisfied.

The Invalids' Fund is and remains for me at present the only stone of stumbling — it has some 9,000 Rthl of arrears of interest to demand, and has therefore laid an arrest upon everything, namely upon both old and new interest. Were it now possible that out of the Sacken estate or bequest the capital of 50,000 Rthl could be paid to the Invalids' Fund, and that I could make over the 50,000 Rthl for that purpose, then you would be master, for I should certainly not press. This is the only means of obtaining an entirely free hand. My counsel is that Prince August lend a hand here in the work, that he insist upon it for his own person — [uncertain: create] the spectacle; I am writing on that account to the King, that he should command it, since he permitted at that time that the money should and must be paid to me upon the mortgage. The King can also now press for it, that it be paid out of the great

bequest — the only thing would be that, instead of to the Invalids' Fund, one thereafter pays annual interest to that quarter / when the old woman has [uncertain: pasted over] everything most devilishly and has even tied Prince August's hands /. You would after all be rid of the accursed execution, and could, once everything is arranged, draw annual revenues of 6 to 8,000 Rthl.

To me the matter must go through. I am writing to the King and say that I received the moneys at that time out of the Invalids' Fund with Your Royal Majesty's consent. The Invalids press me at the same time as the Prince. — The Prince owes me this or a [illegible] capital. I [uncertain: announce] it in order to satisfy the Invalids out of it — the Princes of Hohenlohe, father and son, also wish this; to me the guardians / or whoever it now is / forbid this. Command, Your Royal Majesty, that the money which I must absolutely receive be taken out of the Sacken bequest and the Invalids' Fund be paid,

which must in return be bound to give me the rights ceded to it (jura cessa). — I concede if need be that the Sacken estate shall for the present draw the interest from the estates of Zagórów in my stead. Only I wish thereby to be covered for the future and to see the Prince freed from the most hideous pressure. If we do it so, then the matter [uncertain: succeeds] for certain, and if the King himself commands it, one will not [uncertain: care to] take it to heart. Your Serene Highness, [consider] everything well and write to me about everything, without any foreign interference whatever, with the very greatest speed. By God, everything will go very well. Only send the instrument; and should it be quite impossible for it to be [uncertain: drawn up in court] quickly, then write it yourself and have it signed by 2 witnesses — but it is better done judicially. To me it is very, very urgent. This writing has cost me blood and sweat; God grant that they read it better than I am able to write it. Lying in bed one does not write well. Ever

Your Serene Highness's

Blizanow, the 6th of July 1812

faithful servant

v Triebenfeld
