--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports on negotiations with Weigel, for whom an express messenger has been sent to Breslau; if the plan succeeds a debt of some 54,000 Rthl will be cleared and the estates free in nine or ten years, some 90,000-odd Rthl of debts discharged without loss of substance. The Invalids' Fund, owed some 9,000 Rthl arrears of interest, has laid arrest on everything; proposes that 50,000 Rthl from the Sacken bequest pay it off, with Prince August's and the King's intervention. Urges dispatch of the instrument, judicially attested or signed by two witnesses. Written from his bed.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Beginning 2. How I stand with Weigel Your Serene Highness will see from the enclosure. Since that time he has already written twice more to the Jewish [uncertain: Bang eine Redlich] and enquired when, where and how he might find me. I have already written to him and [uncertain: send/harm] today an express messenger to Breslau still after him, that he is to come with all speed. — Should my plan succeed, of which I have no doubt, then you are rid of a debt item of some 54000 Rthl, and after 9, at most 10 years you will have the estates clear on that side. — It is much if some 90000-odd Rthl of debts can be discharged without losing a Groschen of the substance of the estates. Now everything in this matter depends on the most speedy arrival of Herr Weigel — if he presents himself, then the business is surely agreeable to him, and the plan which I shall lay before him so plain to see, that he will certainly enter into it and be satisfied.

The Invalids' Fund is and remains for me now the sole stone of offence — it has some 9000 Rthl of arrears of interest to demand, and has therefore laid arrest upon everything, upon old as well as new interest. Were it now possible that out of the Sacken estate or bequest the capital of 50 000 Rthl could be paid to the Invalids' Fund, and that I could assign the 50000 Rthl to that purpose, then you would be master, for I should certainly not press. — This is the only means of obtaining an entirely free hand. My counsel is that Prince August lend a hand to the work here, that he insist upon it for his own person — [uncertain: ſchöffen] the spectacle. I am writing on that account to the King, that he should command it, for he permitted at that time that the money should and must be paid to me upon the mortgage. The King can also now press for it, that it be paid out of the great

bequest — the only thing would be that instead, to the Invalids' Fund thereafter (when the old woman has pasted everything over too devilishly and has even tied Prince August's hands), interest be paid yearly. — You would nevertheless be free of the accursed execution, and could, once everything is arranged, draw yearly revenues of 6 to 8000 Rthl. The matter must go through by my hand. I am writing to the King and say: I received the moneys at that time out of the Invalids' Fund with Your Royal Majesty's consent. The [uncertain: Zwalden] presses me at the same time together with the Prince. — The Prince owes me this or a [uncertain: könnigstiger] capital? I [uncertain: künfte] it in order to satisfy the Invalids out of it — the Princes Hohenlohe, father and son, desire this too; but the guardians (or whoever it now may be) resist it. Command, Your Royal Majesty, that the money which I must absolutely receive be taken out of the Sacken bequest and the Invalids' Fund be paid,

which must in return be bound to grant me [uncertain: Jura lehre]. — I concede at all events that the Sacken estate shall for the present draw the interest from the estates of Zagorowo in my stead. Only I wish thereby to be covered for the future, and to see the Prince freed from the most horrible pressure. If we do it thus, then the matter is [uncertain: laid/settled] for certain; and when the King himself commands it, one will not care to [uncertain: take it to heart]. Your Serene Highness, consider everything well, and write to me about everything, without any foreign interference whatever, with the very greatest speed. By God, everything will go very well. Only send the instrument; and should it prove altogether impossible to have it [uncertain: authenticated] quickly before a court, then write it out yourself and have it signed by 2 witnesses — but better it is done judicially. Very, very urgent for me. This writing has cost me blood and sweat; God grant that they read it better than I am able to write it. Lying in bed one does not write well. Eternally Your Serene Highness's
Blizanow, the 6th of July 1812
faithful servant
v Triebenfeld
