Gnädigster Fürst und Herr
Mit blutenden Herzen muß ich Ewr Hochfürstliche Durchlaucht nun doch mein überaus
großes Unglück melden. Mann hat in Schweiner alles versiegelt und um
Beschlag genommen um die Güter zu sequentiren.
Der Wucherische von der Lahr hatte dies nachgesucht weil die ihm gesandte
Producte als Eysen und Bretter bey den inodern Waßer an 6 Wochen unter
wegens waren. Jezt hat der Hund seine Bezahlung aber ich bin unglücklich
weil die andern Gläubiger nicht ehender abgehen, als bis sie ihre Zinßen
haben. Da dort um hier alles in Beschlag genommen ist, so habe ich nun
kleinen Jord und weis mich durchaus nicht zu retten. Mein Unglück ist
jezt gränzenlos, und meine Kinder werden mit mich Bettler den Durch¬
der Sequestration kömt nichts heraus, alles wird aufgerieben, die Zinsen
häufen sich und die Subhactation erfolgt werden Güter die 200000 Rthl
werth sind, im diesem Zeiten für Baum 1/3 teil verschleudert werden
Nie hätte ich geglaubt, daß es mit mir dahin kommen könte - ich konte
mich drauf unter den wohlhabendesten Männern des Staates zählen
jezt schon muß ich mich als Bettler betrachten, und warum? Blos weil
ich zu gut war nur dem König und andere Männer helfen wolte, weil
ich nie auf mein sondern fremdes Intresse sehr
Ich scheine mich unter Wenschen zu gehen ich lebe wie ein Wilder in
einsamen Winkeln und Bezammere einen und meiner Kinder Schicksahl
was ich bey Gott nicht verdiene Schläfl ist mich fremd und Menschen
die mich nur verspotten muß ich fliehen - am mehrsten kränkt mich

das Jammern und Gewinsel meiner Kinder für die ich mich zwar verberge¬

verfolgen. Ich kan sie nichts geben, den ich habe so wahr Gott ist selbst
nichts und meine Töchter arbeiten für Geld und laße es im Geheim ver¬

Ich selbst erträge Hunger dürf und jede Fahrlichkeit des Lebens, ich habe wenig bedürfniß

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports his ruin: everything at Schweiner and Kontop sealed and placed under attachment towards sequestration, obtained by the usurer von der Lahr over iron and boards delayed six weeks by low water; estates worth 200000 Rthl face subhastation at a third of value. Grävenitz has sent von der Lahr's receipt to J. W. v. Loen at Strelan, but 1860 Rthl of old and new interest must be paid first; Loen has sold goods worth 1000 Rthl for 460 Rthl. Attempts to raise money on Zagorow under French decrees failed, Honrichs being arrested and threatened with shooting. Begs 1800 Rthl be sent to Professor Schade, laments his daughters working for money, and pleads inability to obey Lombardini's summons to attend the Prince.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord, With a bleeding heart I must now after all report to Your Most Serene Highness my exceedingly great misfortune. They have sealed up everything in Schweiner and placed it under attachment, in order to sequester the estates.

The usurious von der Lahr had petitioned for this because the products sent to him, such as iron and boards, were some 6 weeks on the way owing to the [uncertain: low] water. Now the dog has his payment, but I am ruined, because the other creditors will not withdraw any sooner than until they have their interest. Since everything there and here has been placed under attachment, I now have small [uncertain: Jord] and know of absolutely no way to save myself. My misfortune is now without bounds, and my children will become beggars with me; for out of the sequestration nothing comes, everything is consumed, the interest accumulates and the compulsory auction follows: estates which are worth 200000 Rthl will in these times be squandered for a bare 1/3 part. Never would I have believed that things could come to this pass with me — I could once count myself among the most well-to-do men of the state; now already I must regard myself as a beggar, and why? Merely because I was too good, because I wished only to help the King and other men, because I never looked to my own but to others' interest. I seem to myself to go about among [uncertain: Wenschen]; I live like a savage in lonely corners and bewail my own and my children's fate, which before God I do not deserve. [uncertain: Schläfl] is strange to me, and men who only mock me I must flee — what grieves me most is

the lamenting and whimpering of my children, from whom I indeed hide myself but who nevertheless pursue me by letter with the most dreadful songs of lamentation. I can give them nothing, for — as God is true — I myself have nothing, and my daughters work for money and let it be sold in secret so as not to grieve me. This alone tears my heart the most. I myself endure hunger, thirst and every peril of life; I have few needs and am neither a gambler nor a wencher; I would take no account of all these calamities but rather (if I had to bear them alone) endure them without [uncertain: complaint], but the reproach that I must see my children working for money, this deranges my senses and altogether blunts my feelings. With 1860 Rthl I could be saved, but where am I to get them, since here and in Schweinern the sequestration

has been [uncertain: imposed] — My credit is now quite gone, I cannot obtain 100 Rthl and must go without. I meant to raise money out of Zagorow upon the decrees of the French General Government. To take 1800 Rthl of it for myself and to send Your Serene Highness whatever more came in — I offered, since ready money is not let out of the country, to accept horses, sheep and spirits; the poor Honrichs entered into it and wished to see to this. But they have not only forbidden it, they have arrested Honrichs on the spot and threaten to shoot him — I now have my hands quite full in trying to save the man, who is the father of 1 children. So here too, instead of help, I have expense, grief and care.

I should have timber, boards and produce with which to obtain money, but they let me touch nothing and have laid an attachment on everything — I am absolutely first to pay the Lahr matter, the 1860 Rthl thereon; then they will raise the sequestration and release to me my property together with all the produce. From the blood

blood-leech von der Lahr, Grävenitz sends us at this very moment a receipt and a letter to J. W. von Loen, to the effect that he is satisfied save for a few hundred d of costs and interest, but that he consents to the raising of the sequestration and execution. This I am sending this very hour to [uncertain: Strelan] to Loen; but through the noise the sequestration has now also been occasioned on account of the old and new interest of 1860 Rthl, and until I pay these they will not raise the sequestration after all. My fine [uncertain: crops], which as between brothers were worth 1000 Rthl, Loen has already had sold for 460 Rthl, and reckoned off one item of interest against it — O, I could go mad. I ought to have murdered before I sacrificed myself for King, country and all and gave away sums — I should then perhaps, as now, have been able to [uncertain: end] tragically, but my children ought not to have had to work for money. Had [uncertain: Vierchitz] lived, I should have had a friend who would not have let me sink, but he is no more — To Mutius I wrote — but he means to starve himself.

Your Serene Highness is still the only one I now have on this round earth to whom I can pour out my heart. But whether you can help, God knows — It is 1800 Rthl by which I can be saved; then I am indeed still indebted to the Kockeitzsch family, but that I can take at once out of produce which lies here under attachment, and satisfy everyone.

Were it possible for Your Serene Highness to raise the 1800 Rthl there, I would beg that this sum be sent at once to Professor Schade, so that he might immediately pay the interest and press for the raising of the sequestration — I should thank Your Serene Highness for it eternally. This very hour I receive a letter from Lombardini — it is of the 29th of July and already 7 days old, saying that I am to come to Your Serene Highness

Gladly as I would obey Your Serene Highness's commands, I cannot do so now. I cannot stir from the spot until I am saved and have the estates and the produce free. Then I can travel day and night. Gladly will I do it too, even if I had to go down into hell itself; only just now I am wholly unable to travel 3 miles. My horses are gone to the devil and money I have none. If Your Serene Highness can find counsel, may you deign to do it soon. But command most graciously that everything be sent by an express. The posts go too slowly, as the letters which I receive from there prove. Most reverently I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's

humble and obedient servant

v Triebenfeld

Kontop

the 4th of August

1809.
