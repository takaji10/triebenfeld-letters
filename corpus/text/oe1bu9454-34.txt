Durchlauchtigster Fürst
Gnädigster Fürst und Herr!
Ew. hochfürstliche Durchlaucht höchstverehrtes Schreiben vom 7 ten der erhalte ich mit dem
Beilagen so eben, und erwiedere darauf ganz gehorsamst daß des Honrichs Anbringen ein
Granzenloser Unsinn ist, der aber von Euer Durchlaucht gleich so beantwortet worden, als es
der Kerl verdient
Ich hatte 21 Briefe an den Buben theils durch reitende und Fußboten geschrieben, nie aber auf
wortete mich der Mensch - ich ward hier und da gedrängt müste Kosten allerley Art durch dem
Profeßor Rhoda bezahlen laßen, von Honrichs aber erhielt ich weder Geld, Nachweis oder ir¬
gend eine Sylbe Rechnung – da Schmid todt war und aller Nachfragen nun umsonst wäre
entschloß ich mich andere Menschen dorten anzustellen, die treu waren und zusammen genommen
weniger Kosten solten als Schanck allen - ich that dies umsamme da sich die französische
Behörde zu Gunsten meiner dorten verwenden und die Reste zum Theil beitreiben laßen wolte.
dazu würde nun die Rechnungen und Gerichtlich attestirte Nachweisungen erfordert. Etwas

Ewr Durchlaucht decidirtes Schreiben wird dem Honrichs nun wohl den Muth benehmen

Sorgen Ewr Durchlaucht nur daß Sie dorten wie ich in allen meinen 6 Briefen geschrieben habe.
das Geld erhalten. Die erste Nachricht darüber wird mich neue Kräfte geben, um nach
Gringen zu kommen, und dan soll Ihnen vieles helle werden.

Mein größter Gram und Sorge ist, daß ich erst die geborgte Gelder für die Invaliden Caſſe abgezahlen,

Die Briefe in Amelang und Kopp habe ich immer noch hinter mir weil besonders lezterer

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Rejects Honrich's complaint as nonsense: despite 21 letters by mounted and foot messenger, Honrichs has sent neither money nor accounts, while costs had to be met through Professor Rhoda; since Schmid's death Triebenfeld has installed cheaper men, Hester at 600 Rthl and allowances, with French authority ready to help collect arrears. Urges the Prince to secure money there, laments his illness and the Invalids' Fund debt, and promises to come and cede the 250 Rthl mortgage and repay von Zastrow.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! Your Most Serene Highness's most highly esteemed letter of the 7th of this month I have just received with the enclosures, and reply to it most obediently that Honrich's representation is a boundless nonsense, which however has been answered by Your Serene Highness at once just as the fellow deserves. I had written 21 letters to the lad, partly by mounted and partly by foot messengers, but never did the man answer me — I was pressed here and there, had to have costs of every kind paid through Professor Rhoda, but from Honrichs I received neither money, nor account, nor a single syllable of a reckoning — since Schmid was dead and all further enquiry would now be in vain, I resolved to appoint other men there, who were faithful and who taken together should cost less than Schanck alone — I did this all the more since the French authority was willing to intervene there in my favour and to have the arrears in part collected. For that the accounts and judicially attested statements were now required. Something, and to that end I engaged Hester, who receives 600 Rthl yearly and some allowance in kind as Tietz does; but otherwise not a farthing more — how can such a lad say that the man will cost 10000 a year — the Bösmacht has cost them thus far, and now he is vexed that Hester stands in his way. Something Hester has already sent in by way of statements of the advances. Your Serene Highness's decided letter will now doubtless take from Honrichs the courage to withhold the papers any longer, and once I have the statements I shall summon men here who will indeed take effect — from our own authorities one may by no means promise oneself any help at all; that looks a sorry business — Be that as it may, that friend will shortly still do something for the [uncertain: Gmacher]; let Your Serene Highness only take care that you receive the money there, as I have written in all my 6 letters. The first news of it will give me new strength to come to Gringen, and then much shall be made clear to you.

My greatest grief and care is that I may first pay off the borrowed monies for the Invalids' Fund, and return to Herr von Zastrow his bill of exchange and the pledged mortgage. Shame and disgrace would press me to the ground if this were left undone. Everything must be summoned up so that this is set right. The letters to Amelang and Kopp I still have lying behind me, because the latter in particular is not yet back — but I very much doubt his help — Still I lie wretchedly ill; grief and sorrow all but wear me away. The Invalids' affair has given my health a great blow. It may be that the setting right of it will contribute much to my recovery. — Still more, however, if I may see Your Serene Highness in person and so make my reckoning. There all shall soon be [uncertain: settled] for him. In short, if Your Serene Highness can obtain money there, then I shall come thither, cede the mortgage of 250 Rthl, and both your sorrow and mine will at once be wholly lifted. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's — I do not know whether I address my letters rightly; it always takes so long before I am graciously favoured with an answer to them. I have sent the estimates and other judicially certified documents — I have in three, this is the [uncertain: fourth] letter — described everything as it stands and what may be done to Your Serene Highness's advantage — but to all of these I have as yet had not a single answer. Most faithful and most obedient servant, Breslau, the 18th of April 1808. v Triebenfeld
