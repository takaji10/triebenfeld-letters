--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Hereditary quit-rent contract before the Hohenlohe patrimonial court at Trąbczyn: von Triebenfeld, general attorney of the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, grants building inspector Johann Wilhelm Wollenhaupt 18 Hufen of arable at Drzewce and 2 Hufen of meadow at Wrąbczyn, with dwelling house and brewhouse, for 6000 Rthl (300 Rthl per Hufe) and 300 Rthl annual quit-rent, to be divided into ten peasant holdings. A second protocol annuls the contract, Wollenhaupt receiving 600 Rthl in full settlement and vacating the Drzewce house for the lessees Tietz and Schatzmann.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
The Princely Hohenlohe Court Office at Trąbczyn hereby makes known that before it, between His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe, through his general attorney, the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, on the one part, and the building inspector Wollenhaupt on the other part, the following contract and respectively issuance of receipt has been declared.

Actum Trąbczyn, the 11th of May 1805.

Before the undersigned patrimonial court there appeared

1. the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, as the well-known general attorney of His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, hereditary lord and lord of the court of the estates here, on the one part, and

2. the building inspector Mr. Johann

Wilhelm Wollenhaupt on the other part. The parties appearing are personally known to the undersigned and are subject to no doubt as to their capacity to execute judicial acts. They declare, freely and after due deliberation, to the protocol the following hereditary quit-rent contract concluded between them, irrevocable and in no point to be altered.

§. I. His Most Serene Highness grants, namely, from the demesne farm Drzewce

18 Hufen, written out eighteen Hufen, of clear arable land, and at Wrąbczyn

2 Hufen, written out two Hufen, of Magdeburg meadows, together therefore 20 Hufen, written out twenty Hufen Magdeburg measure, to the building inspector Johann Wilhelm

Wollenhaupt, from now on for everlasting time in hereditary quit-rent, and there are herewith at the same time also

1. the dwelling house and

2. the brewhouse in Drzewce transferred to him in ownership.

§. II. The said Wollenhaupt pays as purchase money for the twenty Hufen received in grant, per Hufe 300 Rthl, written out three hundred Rthl, amounting in all to 6000 Rthl, written out six thousand Rthl, in cash and in good Prussian Courant of treasury standard.

The buildings mentioned in §. 1 Wollenhaupt receives without payment, and their value is already comprehended within the purchase.

§. III. On account of the purchase money there have already been paid 400 Rthl,

written out four hundred Rthl, in cash, for which receipt is hereby given. At Midsummer of the present year 3000 Rthl, written out three thousand Rthl, and the remainder of 2600 Rthl, written out two thousand six hundred Rthl, at Christmas of this year, shall be delivered in cash and in Courant.

§. IV. The hereditary leaseholder binds himself, for these acquired twenty Hufen, from Midsummer of this year onward, to a perpetual hereditary quit-rent which may never be raised or lowered, per Hufe Magdeburg measure of 15 Rthl, written out fifteen Rthl, and thus for 20 Hufen 300 Rthl, written out three hundred Rthl, in Courant in cash, and this yearly in two

instalments, at Christmas and at Midsummer, each time of

150 Rthl, written out one hundred and fifty Rthl, yet not in advance but after the lapse of each half year, and consequently to be paid for the first time at Christmas.

§. V. The said Wollenhaupt undertakes the obligation to dismember these acquired twenty Hufen into ten separate peasant holdings, yet in such a manner that none of them shall amount to less than one Hufe Magdeburg measure.

§. VI. The establishments the hereditary leaseholder and the other takers must effect at their own cost, without any timber or other building assistance being given thereto by the ground lordship, either now or in future.

Likewise neither they nor the present acquirer receive any right of pasturage or of timber-cutting on the remaining seigneurial territory.

§. VII. The twenty Hufen acquired shall be measured out to Wollenhaupt as soon as possible, and he binds himself to pay in cash to the ground lordship, for as much of the winter and summer field as he shall receive already sown, the contribution for the seed corn according to the price current at the Midsummer market in Zagorowo.

§. VIII. Besides the hereditary quit-rent in cash, the hereditary leaseholder obliges himself, for himself and his descendants in possession of these twenty Hufen, to discharge all and every royal tax, as also the dues to the monastery of Ląd and the charges to

to the clergy, chimney money, delivery of forage, and whatever else may arise, without any exception whatsoever, without making or obtaining any remission of the hereditary quit-rent on that account.

§. IX. The hereditary leaseholder must take his drink, in beer and brandy, from the seigneurial tavern or brew- and distilling-house, and in cases of contravention must pay to the ground lordship a penalty of one Rthl for every garniec of foreign brandy brought in and five Rthl for every barrel of foreign beer.

§. X. The hereditary leaseholder and his successors in possession are subject to the jurisdiction of the manorial lordship (Dominium) here, yet are entirely and for everlasting

time exempted from all and every delivery in kind and manorial labour service.

§. XI. The hereditary leaseholder and his successors in possession are at liberty to alienate these acquired 20 Hufen to others by sale or by exchange, yet such must be done with the consent of the ground lordship, and at every such change of possession the tenth Groschen of the purchase money or of the amount exchanged shall be rendered to the ground lordship as a transfer fee (Laudemium). Upon inheritance by children and collateral relatives, on the contrary, no transfer fee is paid, and likewise the first takers at the dismemberment are also exempt, but in no wise upon any further sale thereafter.

§. XII. The hereditary leaseholder, for himself and his successors in possession, renounces all and every remission in cases of misfortune

which arise through fire from heaven, through the instigation of evil persons, crop failure, hailstorm, inundation, cattle plague, war and pestilence, or in any other manner whatsoever, and on that account no indemnity from the ground lordship nor any remission of the hereditary quit-rent shall ever accrue to them.

§. XIII. The costs of this contract and of its engrossment the hereditary leaseholder pays.

§. XIV. So long as the hereditary leaseholder and his successors in possession shall fulfil all the above obligations, the ground lordship promises to protect them everywhere in their rights and to answer for all real claims of any person.

Should, however, the hereditary leaseholder and his successors lay this holding waste, or

remain in arrears with the annual hereditary quit-rent for longer than two years, then this hereditary lease contract ceases and the ground lordship shall be entitled to dispose of this holding otherwise.

Both parties promise to keep the foregoing hereditary quit-rent contract firmly and inviolably, and renounce all legal evasions that might be raised against it, in particular the objection of cunning persuasion, of precipitancy, of an injury suffered above or below the half, and of matters agreed otherwise than as written down; and they have been duly instructed in this renunciation by the court. His Most Serene Highness expressly consents that the hereditary leaseholder may, on

the ground of this contract, have the title of possession of these acquired 20 Hufen entered in his own name.

Further the contracting parties had nothing to adduce, and after this protocol had been read out to them word for word, they approved it throughout and signed it below in their own hand.

Peter Friedrich August von Triebenfeld.

F. W. Wollenhaupt, approved and read.

Brauer. Actum Trąbczyn, the 25th of August 1805.

Before the undersigned there appeared

1. the Councillor of War and

Forests von Triebenfeld, as the known general mandatary of His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen,

2. the building inspector Johann Wilhelm Wollenhaupt. Both gentlemen appearing declare that, on account of a change of circumstances that has intervened, they hereby annul the foregoing contract and wish to renounce and forgo all rights and obligations contained therein, wholly and without reservation; which renunciation was then also mutually accepted and received.

On the ground of this complete annulment and nullification of this contract, the Councillor of War and Forests paid to the

building inspector Wollenhaupt the sum of six hundred Rthl in Prussian Courant, which he received, gave receipt for without reservation, and thereupon declared that by this sum he was fully satisfied in respect of all his demands arising from his conduct of office, administration, and generally in respect of all business conducted for His Most Serene Highness up to the present day, and that he therefore wished hereby to give a general and special receipt, with surrender of all further claim; which declaration is accepted in the best form by Mr. von Triebenfeld in the name of his princely lord and principal. Since

Since the building inspector Wollenhaupt inhabits the dwelling house on the demesne farm Drzewce with his family, but this building is designated as the future dwelling of the present demesne farm lessees Tietz and Schatzmann, Mr. Wollenhaupt promises to vacate this dwelling house by Michaelmas of the current year at the latest, and until then also to come to terms about it with the said lessees in every way possible to him.

Against the lessees he reserves to himself an improvement expended upon the buildings of

43 Rthl 1 silver Groschen, and the compensation for the laying out of a garden. The parties appearing have

signed the foregoing protocol in their own hand, facta prael. et ratibus. Read and approved.

von Triebenfeld    Johann Wilhelm Wollenhaupt
