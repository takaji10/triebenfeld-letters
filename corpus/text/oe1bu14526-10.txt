--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A hereditary quit-rent contract made before the Hohenlohe patrimonial court at Trąbczyn granting building inspector Johann Wilhelm Wollenhaupt 18 Hufen of arable at the Drzewce demesne farm and 2 Hufen of meadow near Wrąbczyn, with dwelling house and brewhouse, for 6,000 Rthl and 300 Rthl yearly rent, on condition he divide the land into ten peasant holdings. Filed with it is its cancellation three months later, von Triebenfeld paying Wollenhaupt 600 Rthl in full settlement of all claims from his service, with Wollenhaupt to quit the Drzewce house by Michaelmas.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
The Princely Hohenlohe Court Office at Trąbczyn hereby makes known that before it, between His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe, through his general attorney, the councillor of war and Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, on the one part, and the building inspector Wollenhaupt on the other part, the following contract and respectively the giving of a receipt has been declared.

Actum Trąbczyn, the 11th of May 1805.

Before the undersigned patrimonial court there appeared

1. the councillor of war and Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, as the known general attorney of His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, hereditary lord and lord of the court of the estates here, on the one part, and

2. the building inspector Mr Johann

Wilhelm Wollenhaupt on the other part. The appearing parties are personally known to the undersigned and are subject to no doubt as to their capacity to perform judicial acts. They freely and with full deliberation declare to the protocol the following hereditary quit-rent contract, concluded between them, irrevocable and in nothing to be altered.

§. I. His Most Serene Highness grants, namely, from the demesne farm Drzewce

18 Hufen, in words eighteen Hufen, of clear arable land, and near Wrąbczyn

2 Hufen, in words two Hufen, of Magdeburg meadows, together therefore 20 Hufen, in words twenty Hufen Magdeburg measure, to the building inspector Johann Wilhelm

Wollenhaupt, from now on for perpetual times in hereditary quit-rent, and there are at the same time herewith also

1. the dwelling house and

2. the brewhouse in Drzewce transferred to him in property.

§. II. The said Wollenhaupt pays as purchase money for the twenty Hufen granted to him, per Hufe 300 Rthl, in words three hundred Reichsthaler, making in all 6000 Rthl, in words six thousand Reichsthaler, in cash and in good Prussian treasury-standard Courant.

The buildings mentioned in §. 1 Wollenhaupt receives free of charge, and their value is already comprehended within the purchase.

§. III. On account of the purchase money there have already been paid 400 Rthl,

in words four hundred Reichsthaler, in cash, for which receipt is hereby given. At Midsummer of the present year 3000 Rthl, in words three thousand Reichsthaler, and the remainder with 2600 Rthl, in words two thousand six hundred Reichsthaler, at Christmas of this year, will be paid over in cash and in Courant.

§. IV. The hereditary lessee binds himself, for these twenty Hufen acquired, from Midsummer of this year onward, to a perpetual hereditary quit-rent, which may never be raised or reduced, per Hufe Magdeburg measure of 15 Rthl, in words fifteen Reichsthaler, thus for 20 Hufen 300 Rthl, in words three hundred Reichsthaler, in Courant cash, and that yearly in two

instalments, at Christmas and at Midsummer, each time with

150 Rthl, in words one hundred and fifty Reichsthaler, yet not in advance but after the elapsing of each half year, and consequently to be paid at Christmas for the first time.

§. V. The said Wollenhaupt undertakes the obligation to dismember these twenty Hufen acquired into ten separate peasant holdings, yet in such manner that none of them shall amount to less than one Hufe Magdeburg measure.

§. VI. The establishments the hereditary lessee and the other takers must effect at their own cost, without any timber or other building assistance being given thereto by the manorial lordship, now or in the future.

Likewise neither they nor the present acquirer receive any right of pasturage or of timber-taking on the remaining territory of the lordship.

§. VII. The twenty Hufen acquired shall be measured out to Wollenhaupt as soon as possible, and he binds himself, for as much as he shall take over sown in winter and summer field, to pay the contribution for the seed-corn in cash to the manorial lordship at the price current at the Midsummer market in Zagorowo.

§. VIII. Besides the cash hereditary quit-rent the hereditary lessee obliges himself, for himself and his successors in possession of these twenty Hufen, to discharge all and every royal tax, as also the dues for the monastery of Ląd and the levies to

to the clergy, chimney money, forage deliveries and whatever else may occur, without any exception, without making or obtaining any remission of the hereditary quit-rent on that account.

§. IX. The hereditary lessee must take his drink in beer and brandy from the lordship's tavern or brew- and distilling-house, and in cases of contravention must pay to the manorial lordship as penalty one Reichsthaler for every garniec of foreign brandy brought in and five Reichsthaler for every barrel of foreign beer.

§. X. The hereditary lessee and his successors in possession are subject to the jurisdiction of the manorial lordship (Dominium) here, yet are wholly and for perpetual

times free of all and every delivery in kind and manorial labour service.

§. XI. The hereditary lessee and his successors in possession are at liberty to alienate these 20 Hufen acquired to others by sale or exchange, yet such must be done with the consent of the manorial lordship, and upon every such change of possession the tenth Groschen of the purchase money or of the amount exchanged is to be paid to the manorial lordship as a transfer fee (Laudemium). Upon inheritance to children and collateral relatives, on the other hand, no transfer fee is paid, and likewise the first takers at the dismemberment are also exempt, but by no means upon any further sale onward.

§. XII. The hereditary lessee, for himself and his successors in possession, renounces all and every remission in cases of misfortune

which arise through fire from heaven, through the instigation of evil people, crop failure, hail, inundation, cattle plague, war and pestilence, or in any other way whatsoever, entirely and utterly, and on that account no indemnification from the manorial lordship nor remission of the hereditary quit-rent shall ever accrue to them.

§. XIII. The costs of this contract and of its official copy the hereditary lessee pays.

§. XIV. So long as the hereditary lessee and his successors in possession shall fulfil all the above obligations, the manorial lordship promises to protect them in their rights everywhere and to answer for all real claims of any person.

Should, however, the hereditary lessee and his successors lay this holding waste or

remain in arrears with the annual hereditary quit-rent for longer than two years, then this hereditary lease contract ceases and the manorial lordship shall be entitled to let out this holding elsewhere.

Both parties promise to keep the foregoing hereditary quit-rent contract firmly and inviolably, and renounce all legal evasions available against it, particularly the objection of cunning persuasion, of precipitancy, of an injury incurred above or below the half, of matters agreed otherwise than as set down in writing, and they have been duly instructed in this renunciation on the part of the court. His Most Serene Highness expressly consents that the hereditary lessee, on

the ground of this contract, may have the title of possession of these 20 Hufen acquired entered in his own name.

Further the contracting parties had nothing to adduce, and after this protocol had been read over to them word for word, they approved it throughout and subscribed it below in their own hands.

Peter Friedrich August von Triebenfeld.

F. W. Wollenhaupt, approved and read.

Brauer Actum Trąbczyn, the 25th of August 1805.

Before the undersigned there appeared

1. the councillor of war and

Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, as the known general mandatory of His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen,

2. the building inspector Johann Wilhelm Wollenhaupt. Both the appearing gentlemen declare that, on account of a change of circumstances having intervened, they hereby annul the foregoing contract, and wish to renounce and disclaim all rights and obligations contained therein, wholly and without reservation, which renunciation was then also mutually accepted and received.

On the ground of this complete annulment and nullification of this contract, the councillor of war and Councillor of War and Forests paid to Mr

building inspector Wollenhaupt the sum of six hundred Reichsthaler in Prussian Courant, which he received, gave receipt for without reservation, and thereupon declared that by this sum he is fully satisfied in respect of all his demands arising out of his conduct of office, his administration, and generally in respect of all business conducted for His Most Serene Highness up to the present day, and that he therefore wishes hereby to give a general and special receipt, with surrender of all further demand, which declaration is fully accepted by Mr von Triebenfeld in the name of his princely lord and mandator. Since

Since Mr building inspector Wollenhaupt inhabits the dwelling house on the demesne farm Drzewce with his family, but this building is designated for the future dwelling of the present demesne farm tenants Tietz and Schatzmann, Mr Wollenhaupt promises to vacate this dwelling house by Michaelmas of the current year at the latest, and also until then to come to terms concerning it with the said tenants in every way possible to him.

Against the tenants he reserves to himself an improvement expended upon the buildings of

43 Rthl 1 silver Groschen, and the compensation for the laying out of a garden. The appearing parties have, of the fore-

going protocol, facta prael. et ratibus subscribed in their own hands. Read and approved.

von Triebenfeld  Johann Wilhelm Wollenhaupt
