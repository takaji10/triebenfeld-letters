--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Hereditary quit-rent contract recorded before the Hohenlohe patrimonial court at Trąbczyn: von Triebenfeld, general plenipotentiary of the Fürst zu Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, grants Building Inspector Johann Wilhelm Wollenhaupt 18 Hufen of arable at Drzewce and 2 Hufen of meadow at Wrąbczyn, with dwelling and brew house, for 6000 Rthaler and 300 Rthaler yearly quit-rent, Wollenhaupt to divide the land into ten peasant holdings. A second protocol annuls the whole contract, pays him 600 Rthaler in full settlement, and obliges him to vacate the Drzewce house for the lessees Tietz and Schatzmann, reserving 43 rttl 1 Silber-gr of melioration.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
The Princely Hohenlohe Court Office at Trąbczyn hereby gives to be known that before it, between His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe, through his general plenipotentiary, the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, of the one part, and the Building Inspector Wollenhaupt of the other part, the following contract and respectively the giving of a receipt has been declared and put on record.

Actum Trąbczyn, the 11th of May 1805.

Before the undersigned Patrimonial Court there appeared

1. Herr Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, as the known general plenipotentiary of His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, hereditary lord and lord of the court of the estates here, of the one part, and

2. the Building Inspector Herr Johann

Willhelm Wollenhaupt of the other part. The comparents are personally known to the undersigned and are subject to no doubt as to their capacity to execute judicial acts. The same do freely and after mature deliberation declare to the protocol the following hereditary quit-rent contract concluded between them, irrevocable and in nothing to be altered.

§. I. His Most Serene Highness does, namely, grant out of the demesne farm Drzewce

18 Hufen, in writing eighteen Hufen, of clear arable land, and at Wrąbczyn

2 Hufen, in writing two Hufen, of Magdeburg measure of meadow, together therefore 20 Hufen, in writing twenty Hufen Magdeburg measure, to the Building Inspector Johann Willhelm

Wollenhaupt, from now on for everlasting times in hereditary quit-rent; and there are herewith at the same time also

1. the dwelling house and

2. the brew house at Drzewce made over to him in property.

§. II. The said Wollenhaupt pays as purchase money for the twenty Hufen received in grant, per Hufe 300 rt, in writing three hundred Rthaler, making altogether 6000 rt, in writing six thousand Rthaler, in cash and in good Prussian Courant of the treasury standard.

The buildings which are mentioned in §. 1 Wollenhaupt receives free of charge, and their value is already comprehended within the purchase.

§. III. On account of the purchase money there have already been paid 400 rttl,

in writing four hundred Rthaler, paid in cash, for which receipt is hereby given. At St John's of the present year 3000 rttl, in writing three thousand Rthaler, and the remainder with 2600 rtl, in writing two thousand six hundred Rthaler, at Christmas of this year, will be paid off in cash and in Courant.

§. II. The hereditary lessee binds himself, for these twenty Hufen acquired, from St John's of this year onward, to a perpetual hereditary quit-rent, which may [uncertain: never] be raised or diminished, per Hufe Magdeburg measure at 15 rttl, in writing fifteen Rthaler, thus for 20 Hufen 300 rtl, in writing three hundred Rthaler, in Courant in cash, and that yearly in two

instalments, at Christmas and at St John's, each time with

150 rttr, in writing one hundred and fifty Rthaler, yet not in advance but after the elapse of each half year, and consequently to be paid for the first time at Christmas.

§. V. The said Wollenhaupt undertakes the obligation to dismember these twenty Hufen acquired into ten separate peasant holdings, yet in such manner that none of them shall amount to less than one Hufe Magdeburg measure.

§. VI. The establishments the hereditary lessee and the other takers must effect at their own cost, without either now or in the future any timber or other building assistance being given by the ground lordship.

Likewise neither they nor the present acquirer receive any right of pasturage or of timber-taking, also in the remaining lordly territory.

§. VII. The twenty Hufen acquired shall be measured out to Wollenhaupt as soon as possible, and he binds himself, for so much as he shall receive sown in winter and summer field, to pay the contribution for the seed-corn in cash to the ground lordship at the price current at the St John's market in Zagorowo.

§. VIII. Besides the cash hereditary quit-rent the hereditary lessee obliges himself, for himself and his descendants in possession of these twenty Hufen, to discharge all and every royal tax, likewise the competency for the monastery of Ląd and the dues to

to the clergy, chimney-sweeping money, delivery of forage, and whatever else may arise, without any exception, without making or obtaining any remission of the hereditary quit-rent on that account.

§. IX. The hereditary lessee must take his beverages of beer and brandy from the lordly tavern or brew and distilling house, and in cases of contravention must pay to the ground lordship as a penalty one Rthaler for every Garnez of foreign brandy brought in, and five Rthaler for every barrel of foreign beer.

§. X. The hereditary lessee and his successors in possession are subject to the jurisdiction of the Dominium here, yet are wholly and for everlasting

times freed from all and every delivery in kind and [uncertain: manorial] services.

§. XI. The hereditary lessee and his successors in possession are at liberty to alienate these acquired

20 Hufen to others by sale or exchange, yet such must be done with the consent of the ground lordship, and at every such change of possession the tenth Groschen of the purchase money or of the exchange amount shall be paid over to the ground lordship as a Laudemium. Upon inheritance to children and collateral relations, on the other hand, no Laudemium is paid, and likewise the first takers at the dismemberment are also [exempt], but by no means exempt upon further sale hereafter.

§. XII. The hereditary lessee renounces, for himself and his successors in possession, all and every remission in cases of misfortune

which arise through fire from heaven, through the [uncertain: setting on] by evil people, crop failure, hailstorm, inundation, cattle plague, war and pestilence, or in any other manner whatsoever, and no compensation from the ground lordship nor remission of the hereditary quit-rent shall on that account accrue to them.

§. XIII. The costs of this contract and of its engrossment the hereditary lessee pays.

§. XIV. So long as the hereditary lessee and his successors in possession shall fulfil all the above obligations, the ground lordship promises to protect them everywhere in their rights and to answer for any man's real claims.

Should, however, the hereditary lessee and his successors lay this holding waste, or

remain in arrears with the yearly hereditary quit-rent for longer than two years, then this hereditary-lease contract ceases and the ground lordship shall be entitled to put out this holding otherwise.

Both parties promise to keep the foregoing hereditary quit-rent contract firmly and inviolably, and renounce all legal subterfuges available against it, especially the objection of cunning persuasion, of over-hastiness, of an occurring lesion above or below the half, of matters agreed otherwise than as written down; and they have been duly instructed in this renunciation on the part of the court. His Most Serene Highness expressly consents thereto, that the hereditary lessee may, upon

the ground of this contract, have the title of possession of these acquired 20 Hufen entered in his own name.

Further the contracting parties had nothing to adduce, and after this protocol had been read over to them word for word, they approved the same throughout and subscribed it below in their own hands.

Peter Friedrich August v. Triebenfeld.

F. W. Wollenhaupt, approved and read.
