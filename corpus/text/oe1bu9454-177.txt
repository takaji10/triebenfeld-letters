--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Business in Berlin is stalled by the army's mobilisation: Klewiz has left for South Prussia and Minister v. Voss, summoned home from psalm-singing at Havelberg, is expected back, whereupon Privy Councillor Reinbeck promises to lay the Zagorowo/Trąbczyn dismemberment before the King for approbation, on which all borrowing from the Invalids' Fund and Wards' Collegium depends. Includes a schedule of fifteen new colonies (21,407 Morgen, 578 householders) and demesne and woodland remainders; only 7,130 Rthl received, with 68,000 Rthl owed to the Prusimska, Weigel and the Schlabrendorff woman; 2,000 Rthl paid to Colonel v. Schlieben; war news of mobilisation, generals' postings and retirements.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord, There is at present little to be settled here, for everything is now in confusion on account of the mobilisation of the army. Klewiz had to hasten yesterday to South Prussia and [uncertain: to] drive the grain together, Minister Voss sits in Havelberg and sings psalms, and therefore there is, in this [illegible: Eachschlagt], nothing to be done for the time being. The approbation is being pressed forward by Privy Councillor Reinbeck, who intends to see to it and have it executed as soon as Voss returns. Upon the approbation of the dismemberment the money business depends; as soon as I have this, the revenues stand [uncertain: reiste], and then I hope to obtain from the Invalids' Fund and from the Wards' Collegium the sum of money required for the payment of all debts. All this has been set in train; meanwhile I cannot wait upon the thing, but must hasten again to Tropizin in order to work there. With Betsche the matter must and will also go forward. Reinbeck is Amelang's bosom and boyhood friend; through him everything will be capable of being pressed forward. Should anyone be at all refractory, I have the promise of the most powerful protection from the Court.

To Mr. Minister von Voss there went out yesterday from Potsdam an order that he should suspend the psalm-singing until another time and come home, inasmuch as there were more important affairs to be dispatched here. He is to arrive this evening. Reinbech has just now given Amelang the [uncertain: Verführung / instruction]

that tomorrow the dismemberment matter will be laid before the Minister and the day after tomorrow the report will go off to the King and the highest approbation be obtained. According to the state of the files he would answer for it that the same could not [uncertain: entstehen / be refused] but must absolutely be granted. As regards Betsche, on Reinb.'s advice I am to submit nothing until I have that approbation — then I am to submit the matter to the Minister myself, so that he may turn it all to the best advantage. In respect of the war business it remains the case that the whole army is being mobilised.

The Emperor has now proposed an interview with our King. The King has answered him par Concir that he would be content with that. From his resolve to mobilise his whole army, however, he would not depart — for he would not let the [uncertain: Buße / penance] be drawn through his land; that would be worse than if he lost a whole campaign — In general he would now meddle no further in anything; whoever, however, should seek to compel him to anything, him he would meet with his whole force, and he would sooner be buried with his last man beneath the ruins than depart from this resolve.

General Ruchel departs tomorrow for East Prussia; likewise General Relitirente /who is very dissatisfied and wishes to demand his discharge/ is to depart these days for Pomerania. As General Köckritz says, it is wished that Your Serene Highness might also soon be in South Prussia. Seven generals are being placed in the retired state, among whom General Dolfs was included when I heard them named. Field Marshal Mollendorf is to [text lost] house
