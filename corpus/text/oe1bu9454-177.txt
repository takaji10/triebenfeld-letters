--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Wegen der Mobilmachung ist in Berlin wenig zu erreichen. Die Approbation der Dismembration bei Zagórów und Trąbczyn betreibt Geheimer Rath Reinbeck; davon hängt das erhoffte Geld von Invaliden Casse und Pupillen Collegium ab. Beigefügt ist ein Verzeichnis der neuen Kolonien (21407 Morgen, 578 Wirthe; dem Dominium bleiben 4575 Morgen). Radzinski soll 6000 Rthl erlegen. Zerstreute Colonisten versprachen etliche Tausend Rthl. Zu zahlen bleiben Prusimska 20000, Weigel 23000, Schlabrendorff 25000 Rthl.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord, there is little to be settled here at present, since everything is now in confusion on account of the mobilisation of the army. Klewitz had to hurry off yesterday to South Prussia to drive grain together there; Minister von Voss sits in Havelberg and sings psalms, and therefore in whatever falls into this department there is nothing to be done for the moment. The approval is being pressed forward by Privy Councillor Reinbeck, who intends to attend to it and have it executed immediately upon Voss's return. Upon the approval of the dismemberment the money business depends; as soon as I have this, the revenues stand [uncertain: travelled], and then I hope to obtain from the Invalids' Fund and from the Board of Wards the sum of money needed for the payment of all debts. All this has been set in train — meanwhile I cannot wait upon the thing, but must hasten back to Trąbczyn to work there. With Pszczew the matter will, and must, likewise go forward. Reinbeck is Amelang's bosom and boyhood friend, and through him everything will be capable of being pressed. Should anyone be in any way refractory, I have the promise of the most powerful protection from the Court.

To Minister von Voss there went out yesterday from Potsdam an order that he should suspend the psalm-singing until another time and come home, since there were more important affairs to be dispatched here. He is to arrive this evening. Reinbeck has just now given Amelang the [uncertain: seduction]

that tomorrow the dismemberment matter will be laid before the Minister, and the day after tomorrow the report will go off to the King and the highest approval be obtained. According to the state of the files he would vouch for it that the same cannot [uncertain: arise] but must absolutely be granted. As to Pszczew, upon Reinbeck's [uncertain: Reinb] advice I am to submit nothing before I have that approval — then I am to submit the matter to the Minister myself, so that he may turn the same to everyone's best advantage. As regards the war business, it remains the case that the whole army is being mobilised.

The Emperor has now proposed a meeting between himself and our King. The King has answered him by courier that he was content with that. From his intention of mobilising his whole army, however, he would not depart — for he would not let the [uncertain: penance] march through his country, that would be worse than if he lost a whole campaign — in general he wished now to meddle in nothing further, but whoever should try to force him to anything, him he would meet with his whole might, and he would sooner be buried with his last man beneath the ruins than depart from this intention.

General Rüchel departs tomorrow for East Prussia, and likewise General Kalckreuth / who is very discontented and wishes to demand his discharge / is to depart these days for Pomerania. As General Köckritz says, it is wished that Your Serene Highness might also soon be in South Prussia. Seven generals are being put into retirement, among whom General Dolfe was included as I heard them named. Field Marshal Mollendorf is to [text lost] house
