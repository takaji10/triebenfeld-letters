--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Business in Berlin is stalled by the army's mobilisation, though Privy Councillor Reinbeck promises to lay the division of the estates before Minister von Voss and obtain the King's approval within days; on that approval depends raising money from the Invalids' Fund and Wards' Board. Enclosed is a register of fifteen new colonies at Zagorowo and Trąbczyn, 21,407 Morgen and 578 farmers, with land left to the lordship. Only 7,130 Rthl has come in; Radzinski owes 6,000; Prusimska, Weigel and Schlabrendorff claim 68,000 Rthl. War news follows.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord, There is at present little to be settled here, for everything is now in confusion on account of the mobilisation of the army. Klewiz had to hasten to South Prussia yesterday and drive the [uncertain: the] grain together; Minister Voss sits in Havelberg and sings psalms, and therefore there is, as regards what falls within this [illegible], nothing to be done for the time being. The approbation is being pressed forward by Privy Councillor Reinbeck, who intends to see to it and have it executed as soon as Voss returns. Upon the approbation of the dismemberment the money business depends; as soon as I have this the revenues stand [uncertain: ready], and then I hope to obtain from the Invalids' Fund and from the Wards' Board the sum of money needed for the payment of all debts. All this has been set in train; meanwhile I cannot wait upon the thing, but must hasten back to Trąbczyn in order to work there. With Betsche the matter must go too. Reinbeck is Amelang's bosom and boyhood friend, and through him everything will be capable of being pushed forward. Should anyone prove in some measure refractory, I have the assurance of the most powerful protection from the court.

To Minister von Voss there went out from Potsdam yesterday an order that he should suspend the psalm-singing until another time and come home, inasmuch as there were more important matters to be dispatched here. He is to arrive this evening. Reinbeck has just now given Amelang the [uncertain: guidance]

that tomorrow the dismemberment matter will be laid before the Minister, and the day after that the report will go off to the King and the highest approbation will be procured. According to the state of the files he would answer for it that the same cannot [uncertain: fail to be granted] but must absolutely be granted. As regards Betsche, upon Reinbeck's advice I am to submit nothing until I have that approbation — then I am to submit the matter to the Minister myself, so that he may turn the whole of it to the best advantage. As respects the war business, it remains the case that the whole army is being mobilised.

The Emperor has now proposed an interview between [uncertain: himself and] our King. The King has answered him par Courier that he would be content with that. From his resolve to mobilise his whole army he would not depart, however — for he would not let the [uncertain: penalty] be drawn through his country; that would be worse than if he lost a whole campaign — In general he would now meddle no further in anything, but whoever should seek to force him to something, him he would meet with his whole might, and he would sooner be buried beneath the ruins with his last man than depart from this resolve.

General Ruchel goes off tomorrow to East Prussia; likewise General Relitirente /who is very dissatisfied and means to demand his discharge/ is to go off these days to Pomerania. As General Köckritz says, it is wished that Your Serene Highness might also soon be in South Prussia.
