--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that the prince's letter, left lying by the tenant at Schweinere, reached him three weeks late; he sold his wool at Oxtrow for travel money and rode to Count Wartensleben at Neisse, only to find the Schurguet estates already sold to a bankrupt Kulins merchant for 135000 Rthl. Proposes buying young Wartensleben's Constadt estates (asking 200000 Rthl, 120000 in debts), contingent on Weigel and Stössel assuming the Zagorow mortgage of 270000 Rthl and receipting 350000 Rthl, with Koschentin recovered as an entail for Prince Alexander; asks for instructions by express to Blizanow and the return of the valuation and arrears schedule in original.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! Your Most Serene Highness's most safely-kept letter was sent to Schweinere, so that it might be forwarded to me as occasion offered. There the tenant let it lie for a considerable time, and for this reason I received it 3 weeks [uncertain: still/after] its dispatch. Immediately upon receiving it I went to Oxtrow, sold my wool in order to have travelling money, and then hastened to Count Wartensleben at Neisse. Unhappily, however, I came too late, for Wartenleben had already concluded with a bankrupt merchant from Kulins, wherein he takes on estates for Schurguet for 135000 Rthl, for which I would not give 40000 Rthl; only, the matter is past. Meanwhile the young Wartensleben, who is now in Austrian service, has the Constadt estates — He has given circa 150000 Rthl for them. He has sunk into them his wife's fortune — she being a born Reuhenbauernst — and has made very many improvements. He now demands 200000 Rthl for these estates. Upon the estates there stand 120000 Rthl of debts. There would thus remain a further 80000 Rthl. Upon these one is willing to bargain. Should Your Serene Highness wish to negotiate here, then write to me in all haste by an express to Blizanow, and also come, then at once to Schlawenseins to arrange everything, and thereafter to Wartensleben at Neisse to conclude. Everything, however, depends upon Weigel and Stössel taking back the Constadt estates, as also the mortgage upon the Zagorow estates, and granting Your Serene Highness a receipt against them. Am I to negotiate with Weigel to that end, or has Your Serene Highness someone for it who understands it better than I?

The matter would then stand as follows — at Zagorow there would remain, according to the valuation, apart from the arrears, 35000 Rthl; at Konstad there would remain 80000. Consequently there would remain upon the Zagorow estates a mortgage of 270000 Rthl; this Weigel and Stössel would have to take on together with Constadt, and grant Your Serene Highness a receipt for 350000 Rthl. So also, if meanwhile Weigel and Stössel were willing, without Constadt, to take on the Zagorow estates outright for 350000 Rthl and to grant Your Serene Highness a receipt, so that you might get Koschentin back, then it would be, according to the said [text lost] Koschenten could then be taken as an entailed estate for Prince Alexander. Prince August cannot act against his father and [illegible] — He would have to press for this, that Your Serene Highness be given out this sum together with the 200000 Rthl already paid upon Koschentin, which amounts to 550000 Rthl without those arrears, and so Your Serene Highness could defy hell itself. — Let Your Serene Highness consider this, and only write your opinion by a swift messenger to Blizanow. — I should very gladly have come at once now to Schlawenschütz, but I must absolutely go [uncertain: to] — too much besides, since the most important court terms are also waiting. The valuation, together with the schedule of arrears, I beg to have back only in the original; it will be required in aplicii at the term. For the rest, my Wartensleben as yet knows not a word as to what sort of estates those are which I offered him in the exchange dealing — he guessed at the Bavarian ones, after Prince George — I bade him leave off the game of questions, since he could learn nothing now until I myself am first instructed. At bottom the old greybeard moves me to pity — His fate is a sad one. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's faithful, humble and obedient servant v Triebenfeld Brieg, the 31st of 8br 1811.
