--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports a wasted journey to Count Wartensleben at Neisse: the Schurguet deal was already closed with a bankrupt Kulins merchant at 135,000 Rthl, a sum Triebenfeld thinks wildly inflated. The younger Wartensleben will sell the Constadt estates, worth 200,000 Rthl against 120,000 Rthl of debt. Triebenfeld sketches a scheme by which Weigel and Stössel take over Constadt and the Zagorowo mortgage, receipting some 350,000 Rthl and freeing Koschentin, and asks for instructions by express to Blizanow.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Your Most Serene Highness's most carefully secured letter was sent to Schweinere, so that it might be forwarded to me as occasion offered. There the tenant let it lie for some considerable time, and for this reason I received it 3 weeks after it had been dispatched. Immediately upon receiving it I went to Oxtrow, sold my wool in order to have travelling money, and then hurried to Count Wasserleben at Neisse. Unhappily, however, I came too late, for Wartenleben had already concluded with a bankrupt merchant from Kulins, whereby for Schurguet he accepts estates at 135000 Rthl for which I would not give 40000 Rthl; only, the matter is past. — Meanwhile the younger Wartensleben, who is now in Austrian service, holds the Constadt estates. — He gave for them about 150000 Rthl. He has sunk into them his wife's fortune, she being a born Reuhenbauernst, and has made very many improvements. He now demands 200000 Rthl for these estates. On the estates there stand 120000 Rthl of debts. There would thus remain a further 80000 Rthl. On this sum one is willing to bargain. Should Your Serene Highness wish to treat here, then write to me in all haste by an express courier to Blizanow, and come as well, then at once to Schlawenseins to arrange everything, and from there on to Wartensleben at Neisse in order to conclude. Everything, however, depends upon Weigel and Stössel taking over the Constadt estates again, as also the mortgage upon the Zagorowo estates, and giving Your Serene Highness a receipt in return. Am I to negotiate with Weigel on this account, or has Your Serene Highness someone for it who understands it better than I?

The matter would then stand as follows: — with Zagorowo there would remain, according to the valuation, apart from the arrears, 35000 Rthl

with Konstad there would remain

80000. Consequently there would remain upon the Zagorowo estates a mortgage of 270000 Rthl; this Weigel and Stössel would have to take over together with Constadt, and give Your Serene Highness a receipt for 350000 Rthl; so also, if Weigel and Stössel would meanwhile, without Constad, take over the Zagorowo estates outright for 350000 Rthl and give Your Serene Highness a receipt, so that you get Koschentin back, then it would be according to the said Koschentin could then be taken as an entailed estate for Prince Alexander. Prince August cannot act against his father and are payable — He would have to press upon it that this sum, together with the 200000 Rthl already paid upon Koschentin, be handed over to Your Serene Highness, which comes to some 550000 Rthl without those arrears, and so Your Serene Highness could defy hell itself. — Let Your Serene Highness weigh this and write your opinion to Blizanow, only by a swift messenger. — I would very gladly have come to Schlawenschitz at once, just now, but I must absolutely go [uncertain: elsewhere], there being too much besides, and the most important court terms are waiting. The valuation, together with the designation of the arrears, I request back only in the original. It will be required in copy at the term. For the rest, my Wartensleben [says] not a word as yet as to what sort of estates those are which I offered him in the exchange-dealing — he guessed at the Bavarian ones, after Prince George — I bade him leave off the game of questioning, since he could now learn nothing until I myself had first been informed.
