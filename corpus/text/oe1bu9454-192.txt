--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Bedridden in a fifth week and unable to travel to Berlin, where 360,000 Rthl are promised, Triebenfeld reports pacifying rebellious peasants, dismissing the tenants, and standing surety that Weigel and Lichnowski will be paid from the judicial fund on ceding their mortgages. He accuses Glenck of allying with Olesnica men, circulating a lampoon among prospective colonist buyers, and details sums already received from Swięcia, Olesnica and Szetlewek, plus a judgement favouring Giese's hereditary lease.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
My dear friend!

There lie in Berlin 360000 Rthl that have been promised to me. Only I lie here and have been keeping to my bed now in the 5th week; I have quite ruined myself here with vexation. Meanwhile I have now put everything here in order, and have entirely pacified the peasants who were stirred up by arch-rogues, and have also driven the tenants to the devil. When God or devils and hell-brands of men play me crosswise tricks, so that I must all but perish of vexation [uncertain: Argus], then all splendour comes to an end. Glenck was here and has joined together with the Olesnicer hell-brands, and for that has engaged a certain Kramplich, Bergemann and Rybbeh; the [illegible] pack of hell incites the people and turns the buyers away; it is too mad how it goes with me. If meanwhile my constitution helps itself and I am well again, then may the devil fetch all the knaves. The tenants are, as said before, all gone to the devil, and from Christmas and St. John's onward everything must be so understood that monies alone may be raised, and that nothing more must arise for expenditure. Mr. Weigel will be paid, for that I stand surety, for I obtain from the judicial fund the money for Weigel and Lichnowski; these must cede their mortgages, and get their money; I have already said this to the witnesses at Weigel's too; for me, when I lie here in misery, I cannot act in Berlin; as soon as I recover somewhat I shall come to Breslau and go at once with my daughters to Berlin

From here I can as yet draw nothing, but from Michaelmas onward much money should come in little by little. It would have been easy to obtain money here if I had been willing to give away the parcels of land for a third of the price, but from that may God preserve me. — Now the Prince still receives 190000 Rthl in cash and 40000 in perpetuity, that being the annual revenues. The Prince has already received 10000 Rthl from Swięcia, 10000 Rthl from Olesnica, 12000 Rthl from Szetlewek, and 9000 Rthl have been given for grounds, all of which has gone upon the costs and interest and also upon old debts. The peasants' revolution and the Plenck knaveries have had their use — I have indeed had to give much back, but now it is all the better and more amply made out. Enough: everything will go well — if only I am well again first — the writing comes very hard to me. So only this further, that I am unceasingly yours,

v Triebenfeld

Trąbczyn

the 12th of Aug

1806 Upon the whole Prochenkamr affair I must suspend my judgement, for I do not know it — As it seems to me, what is lacking is a man who knows the locality thoroughly, and a good deal of money — the Stöz: praise is suspect to me, well evaded — the Pro Memoria of the Koſchenkin officials give sufficient [illegible] true friend and serve guidance.

the enclosed lampoon Glenck has made upon me and has had it distributed among the colonists who are minded to buy; the original I have sent to His Serene Highness — It is drafted and dealt out by Glenck's own high hand.

To the Olesnicer [uncertain: Schluzbuben] he has indeed given certificates and helped to support their cause, and for that they have promised him to sell the bailiwick. Yesterday the judgement came down upon the fellows: 1. the said Giese may not evict the fellows;

2. he is to discharge the debts encumbering Olesnica;

3. he is to apply for the consent.

4. When that is given, to have the hereditary lease entered in the mortgage book; but 6. Giese is refused, in that the Prince may not hand over the estates to him before the consent has hitherto been granted. That is a sentence at which the dogs might well howl. I shall now at once apply to the authority for the consent; there the resolution will be that such curials may not possess noble estates, and the Chamber will throw them out — then all feuding will have an end.
