--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports from Vienna on postal delays and Congress business: the monarchs have left for Hungary, the Congress will open on their return, Saxony is to pass wholly to Prussia, while Poland remains undecided, with Austria resisting and Talleyrand intriguing; a Thorn–Kalisz–Kempen line would leave Silesia and Prussia unconnected and indefensible. Notes his and Gärtner's illness, hopes from the State Chancellor, delay in the Koschentiner affair, silence enjoined on the Trąbczyner estates until Poland's fate is settled, Zerboni's Red Eagle 3rd class, and the imperial estates' deputation to Emperor Franz; encloses a memorial and Gärtner's brochure.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! Your Serene Highness's most highly esteemed letter of the 9th of this month I received only on the 23rd; if the letters which I send to Your Serene Highness take a like snail-shaped course, then things look sad indeed. — Here it is now somewhat quiet; the monarchical bears [uncertain: uf], as they have nevertheless resolved to go to Hungary, and have in fact departed thither today. After their return the Congress will be opened. The ministers have meanwhile remained here and continue working with the greatest activity. Saxony's fate is now already decided for ever: Prussia obtains the whole of it — Weimar receives a small increment; opinions, Gotha etc. remain as they are, yet all of them are subordinated to the [uncertain: Laug]. Concerning Poland nothing has yet been decided. Austria will by no means lay itself down to the mark, and Talleyrand too seems to be stirring things up underhand, hence the favourable delay. However, from the continuing friendship between the Russian Emperor and our King one hopes the best — for the line from Thoren close past Kalisz towards Kempen would be too slight an equivalent for Prussia's exertions, and this line could never be regarded as a military frontier. Silesia and Prussia would always remain without connexion, and in the face of Russia's great power would have to be regarded as lost provinces, because there are no fortresses or rivers of consequence there. Of the affairs of the Empire I can say little today, for Herr v. Gärtner lay very ill for several days, and I too have had to keep to my room for 4 days on account of gouty attacks and a swollen head. But by the next post I shall be able to write much.

From the assistance of His Serene Highness the State Chancellor I confidently hope a very great deal of good for Your Serene Highness. — The accompanying piece, with all its enclosures, I have had to submit after altering it twice. The first time I had not adduced Your Serene Highness's letter, which must properly be regarded as a power of attorney. In the Koschentiner affair nothing essential has yet been done, because B. and A. do not find it advisable to lay so much before them at once. In the matter of the Polish estates I am to keep quiet until Poland's lot has fallen; for if indeed the district in which the Trąbczyner estates lie should fall to Russia, the Prince Chancellor will certainly at once procure everything with His Majesty the [uncertain: Westen] Emperor for the best advantage of Your Serene Highness. Zerboni received the Order of the Red Eagle, 3rd class, today, and goes to Warsaw. Heaven still bestows its gifts as strangely as in 1793. Neuwied, Erbach, Leiningen and several others have gone as deputies to the Emperor Franz on behalf of the imperial estates. The Emperor has most graciously given them a hearing and promised to do what is possible — whereupon these gentlemen also requested an audience with our King; this the King refused, and merely had them told that they might submit their request in writing. These matters seem to be drawing out very far, and in the end the mountain might bring forth a mouse. For the rest, Herr Privy Councillor v. Gärtner (truly one of the foremost heads and a much esteemed man) bombards them frightfully, and, as I believe, not without effect. — The accompanying writing or little volume I see in many places, of which he is the author. Four more of the like exist, and each of equal force. The K[ing] of Würz[burg] is furious about it. I send Your Serene Highness this brochure for this reason, that they may see that everything which can be said has already been said, in order to bring the matter back into the old track. — I await the command of Herr W. as to what is still to be said in Your Serene Highness's name. — I do not shoot into the blue, [uncertain: Spannen] and [illegible] excites a great sensation — God knows what will come of it? It is in unbounded veneration that I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's — Vienna, the 25th of October 1814 — faithful and most obedient servant v Triebenfeld
