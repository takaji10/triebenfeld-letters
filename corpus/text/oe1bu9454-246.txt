--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports from Vienna on postal delays and Congress business: the monarchs have left for Hungary, the Congress will open on their return, Saxony is to pass wholly to Prussia, while Poland remains undecided, with Austria resisting and Talleyrand intriguing; a Thorn–Kalisz–Kempen line would leave Silesia and Prussia unconnected and indefensible. Notes his and Gärtner's illness, hopes from the State Chancellor, delay in the Koschentiner affair, silence enjoined on the Trąbczyner estates until Poland's fate is settled, Zerboni's Red Eagle 3rd class, and the imperial estates' deputation to Emperor Franz; encloses a memorial and Gärtner's brochure.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Your Serene Highness's most highly honoured letter of the 9th of this month I received only on the 23rd; if the letters which I send to Your Serene Highness travel at an equally snail-like pace, then things look sad indeed. – Here it is somewhat quiet just now; the monarchical bears [uncertain: uf], how they have nevertheless resolved to go to Hungary, and in fact departed thither today. After their return the Congress will be opened. The ministers have meanwhile remained here and are working on with the greatest activity. Saxony's fate is now already decided for good: Prussia gets the whole of it – Weimar receives a small increment; Meiningen, Gotha etc. remain as they are, yet all of them are subordinated to the [uncertain: Laug]. As regards Poland nothing is yet decided. Austria absolutely will not lay itself down to the mark, and Talleyrand too seems to be stirring things up under the hand, whence the favourable delay. However, one hopes the best from the continuing friendship between the [uncertain: Wüßen] Emperor and our King – for the strip from Thorn close past Kalisz towards Kempen would be too slight an equivalent for Prussia's exertions, and this line could never be regarded as a military frontier. Silesia and Prussia would always remain without connexion, and given Russia's great power would have to be considered lost provinces, because there are no fortresses or rivers of consequence there.

Of the affairs of the Empire I can say little today, for Herr von Gärtner lay very ill for several days, and I too have had to keep to my room for 4 days on account of gouty attacks and a swollen head. But with the next post I shall be able to write a great deal.

From the aid of His Serene Highness the State Chancellor I hope with confidence for very much good for Your Serene Highness — the accompanying piece with all its enclosures I have had to submit after altering it twice. The first time I had not adduced Your Serene Highness's letter, which really must be regarded as a power of attorney.

In the Koschentin matter nothing essential has yet happened, because B. and A. do not find it advisable to bring forward so much at one time.

In the business of the Polish estates I am to keep quiet until Poland's lot has fallen; for if the district in which the Trąbczyn estates lie should indeed fall to Russia, the Prince Chancellor will certainly at once procure everything with His Majesty the [uncertain: Western] Emperor for the best advantage of Your Serene Highness.

Zerboni has today received the Order of the Red Eagle, 3rd class, and is going to Warsaw. Heaven still dispenses its gifts just as strangely as in 1793. Neuwied, Erbach, Leiningen and several others have gone as deputies to the Emperor Franz on behalf of the [uncertain: aufsstände]. The Emperor has most graciously [uncertain: received] them and promised to do what is possible — whereupon these gentlemen also begged an audience with our King; this the King refused them, and had them merely told that they might submit their request in writing. These matters seem to be drawing out very far, and in the

end the mountain may well bring forth a mouse. For the rest, Herr Privy Councillor von Gärtner (truly one of the foremost heads and a much respected man) bombards frightfully, and, as I believe, not without effect — the accompanying writing or little volume I see in many places, of which he is the author. Four more of the same exist, and each of equal force. The [uncertain: K.] of Würz: is furious about it. I send Your Serene Highness this pamphlet so that they may see that everything that can be said has already been said, in order to bring the matter back into the old track – I await from

H. W. the command as to what is still to be said in Your Serene Highness's name – I do not shoot into the blue [illegible] and [illegible] excites a great sensation – God knows what will come of it? It is in boundless veneration that I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Vienna, the 25th of October
1814

most faithful and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld
