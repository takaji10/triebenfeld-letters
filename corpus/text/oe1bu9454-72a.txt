--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports a lease contract for the Zagorowo estates concluded with sub-prefect von Brzechsta subject to ratification; the planned journey to Schlawenschitz failed when Brzechsta and prefect Count Garczynski were called to Warsaw, but Brzechsta will come on 11 or 12 March bringing 2–3000 Rthl. The suit against the Wrobeziner peasants was won in the Tribunal Solenne, allowing eviction and execution for arrears. The Trąbczyn restitution is to go before the Council of State; the estates, worth 300000 Rthl against 80000 Rthl of debts, could be cleared. Complains of ruinous war levies and deliveries in kind to Warsaw.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

That a lease contract has been concluded with the sub-prefect concerning the Zagorowo estates sub spe rati, I have most obediently reported to Your Serene Highness; at the same time I notified the same, most high, that I intended to arrive with Brzechsta at Schlawenschitz on the 8th of this month. Since, however, the prefect Garczynski as well as the sub-prefect von Brzechsta were summoned to Warsaw by express courier, the plan of being at Schlawenschitz on the 8th [uncertain: failed].

In the meantime the sub-prefect will quite certainly be here again on the 11th or 12th of March, and then the journey to Schlawenschitz will be set out upon at once. Brzechsta will bring 2 to 3000 Rthl in cash with him immediately, and then it will depend upon Your Serene Highness whether the same, most high, will ratify the contract

themselves or dispose otherwise of the estates. According to our plan, Brzechsta intends, upon his return, to summon all the arrears-debtors in [uncertain: Kone] at once before the lower land court, and to have himself compelled by that judge — who is his kinsman and bosom friend — to proceed to collection by the strictest execution, which hitherto has always been forbidden.

For the rest, the suit with the Wrobeziner peasants was won for Your Serene Highness a couple of days ago in the Tribunal Solenne. The contract is wholly annulled, the peasants are ordered to pay the arrears, and they can be evicted and the holdings sold to others — the judgment was handed to the peasants yesterday by the [uncertain: town crier] of Jarnowski. I must now await the term laid down in the Code Napoléon after publication of the judgment, in order then to proceed with the harshest execution. Perhaps I shall succeed meanwhile in coming to terms with some of the fellows and obtaining a few thousand Rthl, which I would bring along with Brzechsta.

The Olernizer matter will be won in this instance just as [uncertain: readily] as the Wochenziner one, and doubtless the 3 scoundrels, who are here set checkmate, will not press the matter further but must seek their salvation in flight. The Trąbczyn matter Brzechsta is now looking into with the prefect Count Garczynski in Warsaw. It must be brought before the Rada Stanu, and there these two friends of mine will [uncertain: sound out] most of the members as to whether one may lay the matter before that body and hope for a swift and good outcome. In the judgment of every thinking man, Your Serene Highness must have the estates back. For the Commission Rządząca took them from Your Serene Highness 4 months after the Peace of Tilsit, notwithstanding that everything the King had done during the time of possession was to remain in its force, which was likewise guaranteed by Napoleon, the Russian Emperor, and the King of Saxony.

Upon the estates there stand, as far as I recollect, 80000 Rthl of debts; but if the Altz-Hauländer would gladly give 40000 Rthl for the forest as soon as Your Serene Highness has the estates again, and would settle upon

settle upon it, and if the Dąbska woman must make good the damage and surrender the 6 years' revenue, then the estates — which are worth 300000 Rthl throughout — are clear of debts, and it is then once again a fine whole. Upon my arrival Brzechsta will lay all this before Your Serene Highness himself. I should long since have gone to Warsaw myself in this matter, had it ever been possible to raise the costs; for all that could come in hitherto has barely sufficed to meet the frightfully heavy levies. Thus now all deliveries to Warsaw, since most of the troops have been drawn thither, must be rendered in kind at once, and sums paid for their clothing besides. The distress is on that account general and has here already reached its utmost height; I remark only so much, that the demands of the people in Warsaw on account of the Trąbczyn matter would cost nothing, but that these two honest men would set it in train and see to it out of true friendship, in quiet. So much, however, is certain, and everyone from here must assure Your Serene Highness of it, that the estates are preserved and saved for you; the how and the where will show itself in its own time. In deepest reverence I remain

Your Serene Highness's

Kopojno

the 23rd of February 1812.

humble and obedient servant

von Triebenfeld
