--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports a lease contract for the Zagorowo estates concluded with sub-prefect von Brzechsta subject to ratification; the planned journey to Schlawenschitz failed when Brzechsta and prefect Count Garczynski were called to Warsaw, but Brzechsta will come on 11 or 12 March bringing 2–3000 Rthl. The suit against the Wrobeziner peasants was won in the Tribunal Solenne, allowing eviction and execution for arrears. The Trąbczyn restitution is to go before the Council of State; the estates, worth 300000 Rthl against 80000 Rthl of debts, could be cleared. Complains of ruinous war levies and deliveries in kind to Warsaw.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! That a lease contract has been concluded with the Herr sub-prefect concerning the Zagorow estates sub spe rati, I have most obediently reported to Your Serene Highness; at the same time I informed the same, most highly, that I intended to arrive with Herr von Brzechsta at Schlawenschitz on the 8th of this month. When, however, in the meantime both the Prefect Garczynski and likewise the sub-prefect v. Brzechsta were summoned to Warsaw by express courier, the plan of being at Schlawenschitz on the 8th came to grief. In the meantime the sub-prefect will quite reliably be back here on the 11th or 12th [uncertain: Wurtz — probably "März", March], and then the journey to Schlawinschitz will be set out upon at once. 2 to 3000 Rthl in money Herr v. Brzechsta will bring with him immediately, and then it will depend upon Your Serene Highness whether the same, most highly, will the contract

himself ratify or otherwise dispose of the estates. According to our plan, Herr v. Brzechsta means, upon his return, at once to summon all the arrears-debtors at Kone before the Land Lower Court, and to have himself compelled by this judge, who is his kinsman and bosom friend, to payment by the strictest execution, which hitherto has always been forbidden. For the rest, a few days ago the suit with the Wrobezin peasants was won by Your Serene Highness in the Tribunal Solenne. The contract is totally annulled, the peasants are directed to pay the arrears and may be evicted, and their holdings sold to others — the judgment was delivered to the peasants yesterday by the [uncertain: Burgruf] of Jarnowski. I must now await the term laid down in the Code Napoleon after publication of the judgment, in order then to proceed with the harshest execution. Perhaps it will succeed that in the meantime I may already come to terms with a few of the fellows and obtain some thousand rt, which I should bring along with Herr v. Brzechsta.

The Olernitz affair will be won in this instance just as [uncertain: weines] as the Wochenzin one, and doubtless the 3 scoundrels, who are here set checkmate, will not press the matter further, but will have to seek their salvation in flight. The Trąbczin affair Herr v. Brzechsta is now reconnoitring with the Prefect Count Garczynski in Warsaw. It must be brought before the Rada Stanu, and since these two friends of mine [uncertain: sondern] most of the members, [it must be considered] whether one may lay this matter before them there and hope for a swift and good outcome. In the judgment of every thinking man, Your Serene Highness must have the estates back. For the Commission Rządząca took them from Your Serene Highness 4 months after the Peace of Tilsit, notwithstanding that everything the King had done during the time of possession was to remain in its [uncertain: Schlähre — force?], which was also guaranteed by Napoleon, the Russian Emperor and the King of Saxony. Upon the estates there stand, so far as I recollect, 80000 Rthl of debts; but if the Alt-Hauländer, as soon as Your Serene Highness has the estates again, would gladly give 40000 Rthl for the forest and would settle

themselves there, and if the Dąbska woman must make good the damage and give up the 6 years' revenue, then the estates, which are always worth 300000 Rthl, are clear of debt — and it is then again a fine whole. Upon my arrival, Herr v. Brzechsta will lay all this before Your Serene Highness himself. I should long since have gone to Warsaw myself in this matter, had it ever been possible to raise the costs, for all that could be taken in hitherto has barely sufficed to meet the frightfully heavy taxes. Thus now all deliveries must go to Warsaw, because most of the troops have been drawn thither, rendered at once in kind, and sums paid for their clothing besides. The distress is on that account universal and has here already reached its highest pitch; so much only do I remark, that the demand of the people in Warsaw on account of the Trąbczin affair will cost nothing, but that these two upright men would set it in train and see to it out of true friendship, in silence — So much, however, is certain, and everyone here will have to assure Your Serene Highness of it, that the estates are preserved and saved for you; the how and the where will show itself in its own time. In deepest reverence I remain Your Serene Highness's Kopoyno, the 23rd of February 1812. humble and obedient servant v Triebenfeld
