--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports exorbitant grain and straw deliveries demanded of the Zagorow estates, the Ober-Prefecture at Kalisch refusing execution against the peasants, and a renewed sequestration averted only when the Siedern sub-prefect Brzechsta applied 2000 Rthl and took receipts as rent; the sequestrator has been recalled and is under observation. Describes constant quartering (62 persons victualled), murdered noblemen, subjects fled to the woods, no revenue, courts at rest, appeals pending over Werbizer and Olleinue, Honrichs's embezzlement, and his own bilious fever and podagra.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Several weeks ago, when I was on the point of travelling to Schlawenschitz with Mr. D. Bræckl., word was sent to us at Blizanow that an exorbitant delivery of grain and straw had been announced upon the Zagorowo estates, under frightful threats. I [uncertain: applied] against this to the Upper Prefecture at Kalisz and received a refusal in answer; I begged that execution be laid upon the peasants etc. and that a part of the arrears of rent be collected in, in order to pay for the delivery as well as the dues; this was again refused with the observation that the inhabitants must be spared, because they likewise have to deliver much, to furnish much carting-service, and to feed the foreign troops passing through. Scarcely had I received this resolution when I received by express the news that the estates had again been taken under sequestration. I hastened here at once to save the estates out of the claws of the sequestrator, for the fellow would have reckoned ten times as much again for the victualling of the troops, where here on many a day

30, 40, or more officers, not counting their men, have to be fed by the lordship for nothing, and would have delivered up no supply at all. The [uncertain: Siedern] sub-prefect had 2000 Rthl lying by, which he wished to [text lost] bring along through

to Schlawenschitz with him. I begged of him to settle the deliveries out of it and to hand over the receipts for them as rent, so that we might only get the sequestration off our necks and save the estates; the noble Brzechsta was at once ready for this — the sequestrator has these few days been recalled and placed under observation on account of various [uncertain: proceedings]. I, however, had vexed myself so frightfully over all these adverse events that I fell into a bilious fever, and lying wretchedly with it I got, a fortnight ago, the worst gout in both feet, and now lie already many weeks like a [uncertain: Erquilett] under the most raging pains. Daily since [uncertain: March] there is quartering here, often there are 20 officers with their servants to be fed; the day before yesterday, [uncertain: poor me], 62 persons to be fed. The people are coarse; if one does not give, they take everything away by force. The subjects have run off, because they themselves have nothing, the houses stand empty, and if the estate owner will not be exposed to the most terrible maltreatment, and have his cattle, horses and sheep taken from him, then he must provide richly himself. One nobleman they have murdered, others they have regulated with a hundred [uncertain: and more] strokes of the cudgel, one [uncertain: Vod] they hid [text lost] struck dead on the spot

God knows what will come of it and how long this frightful plague will yet last — Here all would be lost were it not for the honest Brzechsta. I lie wretched, have not a Groschen of money, nowhere to get any from; the subjects even on my own estates have fled and live in the woods on herbs and roots. Nothing can be brought in, since from nobleman and peasant alike they take away all drink and horses; no brandy is sold, no one has a Groschen more to drink away, and what can be distilled and brewed scarcely suffices for the troops — in short, the distress has reached its highest pitch. This plague of the land is to last until the first of June — then I shall treat with the sub-prefect. Nor can anything be [uncertain: done] until then, for all the courts of justice are at a standstill. The Werbizer peasants have gone to [uncertain: Warenham] to the court of cassation; I must await the decree. Likewise I am awaiting the decision concerning [uncertain: Olleinue]. Once this is decided, Brzechsta will receive his money against Johann, which is to be paid out to him with 10000 Rthl, and then at Midsummer I shall come with him to Your Serene Highness

we shall bring what ready money is possible; concerning the matter Your Serene Highness will learn nothing further. More to write of I am very [uncertain: loth] — the distress is boundless. I suffer the greatest of it with my children. I have had to employ every means to save the estates for myself, for they would have been lost for ever had the worthy sequestrator maintained himself in them. As soon as this plague of the land ceases and I am restored to health I shall [uncertain: act], and I hope to God that, however dreadful everything looks, I shall nevertheless preserve all and remove the distress. It is in the deepest reverence that I, respectfully, remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's — Honrichs will most likely be arrested one of these days. He was patented far beyond the estates here — but has embezzled dues and committed other frauds. Forgive, Your Serene Highness,

my scrawl

I have had to spend some 4 hours

in getting it down.

Kopojno

22nd May 1812  most humble and obedient servant v. Triebenfeld
