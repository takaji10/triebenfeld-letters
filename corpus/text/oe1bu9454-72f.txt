--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld berichtet, dass den Zagórówer Gütern eine übermäßige Getreide- und Strohlieferung auferlegt und sie erneut sequestriert wurden; die Ober Prefectur in Kalisz verweigerte die Zinsbeitreibung bei den Bauern. Brzechffa war auf seine Bitte bereit, die Lieferungen zu begleichen; der Sequestor wurde abgerufen. Triebenfeld liegt mit Podagra, klagt über Einquartierung, geflohene Untertanen und Geldnot. Honrichs soll wegen unterschlagener Abgaben verhaftet werden.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld reports that an excessive delivery of grain and straw has been imposed on the Zagórów estates and that they have again been placed under sequestration; he says the Upper Prefecture in Kalisz refused to collect the interest from the peasants. He says Brzechffa was willing, at his request, to settle the deliveries, and that the sequestrator was recalled. Triebenfeld is laid up with gout and complains of the billeting of troops, of subjects who have fled and of lack of money. He says Honrichs is to be arrested for embezzling dues.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Several weeks ago, when I was on the point of travelling to Sławięcice with Mr von Brzechffa, word was brought to us at Blizanow that an [uncertain: exorbitant] delivery of grain and straw had been announced to the Zagórów estates under dreadful threats. I [uncertain: turned] against this at the Upper Prefecture at Kalisz and received a refusing answer; I begged that execution be laid upon the peasants etc. and that a part of the arrears of interest be levied, in order to pay both the delivery and the dues; this was again refused with the observation that the inhabitants must be spared, because they likewise have much to deliver, must furnish much team-service, and must feed the foreign troops passing through. Scarcely had I received this resolution when I received by express the news that the estates had again been taken under sequestration; I hurried here at once to rescue the estates from the sequestrator's claws, for the fellow would have reckoned ten times as much again for the victualling of the troops — where many a day here

30, 40, or more officers, apart from their men, must be fed gratis by the lordship — and would have carried off no delivery at all. The worthy sub-prefect had 2,000 Rthl lying ready which he [text lost] through

wished to bring with him to Sławięcice; I begged him to settle the deliveries out of it and to hand over the receipts for them as lease-money, so that we might only be rid of the sequestration and save the estates; the noble Brzechffa was at once ready to do so — the sequestrator was recalled some days since and, on account of various offences, taken under observation. But I had vexed myself so dreadfully over all these adverse events that I fell into a bilious fever; lying wretched in it, a fortnight ago I got the worst podagra in both feet, and now for many weeks I have lain like a [uncertain: skeleton] under the most raging pains. Daily since 8[illegible] there has been billeting here; often there are 20 officers with their servants to be fed; the day before yesterday there were a poor 62 persons to be fed. The people are coarse; if one does not give, they take everything away by force. The subjects have run off, because they themselves have nothing; the houses stand empty, and if the proprietor of the estate will not be exposed to the most frightful maltreatment, and have his cattle, horses and sheep taken from him, he must himself provide richly. Some noblemen have been murdered, others settled with a hundred [uncertain: Arspiegel], a sub-prefect beaten dead on the spot

God knows what will come of it and how long this dreadful plague will yet last — Here all would be lost were it not for the honest Brzechffa. I lie wretched, have not a Groschen of money and nowhere to take any from; the subjects even on my own estates have fled and [uncertain: sip] live in the woods on herbs and roots. [uncertain: Nothing can be brought in] since they take away everything to drink and all horses from nobleman and peasant alike; [text lost] and brandy is not sold, no one has any longer a Groschen to drink away, and what can be distilled and brewed scarcely suffices for the troops — In short, the distress has reached its highest pitch. Until the first of [uncertain: June] this scourge of the land is to last — then I shall treat with the sub-prefect. Nor can anything be done until then, since all the courts of justice are at rest. The [uncertain: Werbizer] peasants have gone to Warsaw to the court of cassation; I must await the decree. Likewise I am awaiting the decision concerning [uncertain: Olleinue]. If that is decided, [uncertain: ſorrechſta] will have his money by Midsummer, which is to be paid out to him in the sum of 10,000 Rthl, and so I shall come at Midsummer with him to Your Serene Highness

we shall bring what ready money is possible; concerning the [illegible] matter Your Serene Highness will learn the rest. More particulars I do not much care [illegible] — the distress is boundless — I suffer the greatest of it with my children — I had to employ every means to save the estates for myself, for they would have been lost for good had the worthy sequestrator kept his hold in them. As soon as this scourge of the land here ceases and I am restored, I shall act, and I hope to God that, however frightful everything looks, I shall nevertheless preserve it all and remove the distress. It is in the deepest reverence that, full of respect, I remain, until death,
