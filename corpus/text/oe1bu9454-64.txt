--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports the Queen's death at Strelitz on the 19th and the King's grave illness, urges recovery of the 100000 Rthl and asks whether Köckritz has returned the papers, and complains of harassment over interest on the Invalids' capital and of Roßem's threatened sequestration of Triebenfeld's estates. Appended: a copy valuing the Knobelsdorff (Bischdorff, Mollna) estates against Zagoraa, with the Weigel and Stössel claims of 190000 and 180000 Rthl; and an October postscript on Hardenberg's promised help over the 112000 Rthl, with repeated pleas for 300 Rthl in gold.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
This very moment I receive from Friend F in Berlin the news that the Queen died at Strelitz on the 19th, early, at 9 o'clock; also that the King is very ill, and that the most mournful consequences are feared. Although I would offer to send these matters to Exp. with all recommended haste, Your Serene Highness will no doubt have the news sooner. The Collorina affair is now a non-thing; now, gracious Lord, you must only see to it that you receive your 100000 Rthl. Have you already got the papers back through Köckritz - write me that with the greatest haste, for I am going to Berlin soon. I shall only try to raise the travelling money and hasten thither. Friend F. is pursuing his own days. The visit from Pastor Willhelm you perhaps do not reckon high enough in the valuation; it will probably bring you something in turn, that is to say, the cases must come on in the order of their consequences - previously the opposite was the case - the certain answer you will now receive, most gracious Lord, on the other side - but God grant it only after some 35 years. I must, if only I can raise the travelling money, absolutely hasten to Berlin. I am being frightfully harried over the interest on account of the Invalids' capital. I could go mad - the clumsy wretch means to sequestrate my estates and leave [uncertain: Leibhartin] - you see, I endure all this for your sake - if only one of the Ver: friends could help.

Had I money - had I at least horses - then I would fly from one place to another, but the accursed postal charges tax the purse too much, and I am still too much in arrears.

Ever, however,

Your Serene Highness's most faithful servant,

v. Triebenfeld, 6 Xbr 1809

Postscript on the 30th of October.

Because Your Serene Highness's letter of the 9th had run 11 days before I received it, I suppose the course of the post is to blame that I got no answer from Your Serene Highness to 5 letters - but now I hear from His Serene Highness the Prince Chancellor that Your Serene Highness has written to him - consequently I must believe that my exertions here are not being received with favour, and that Your Serene Highness does not care to think me worthy of an answer - were this really the case, then deign, Most Serene Prince, at least to let me know it. As for the rest, so far as Your Serene Highness's affair is concerned, the Prince State Chancellor takes it up with true earnestness - his words today were: I will help the old man. I mean that the King should pay the Lerian debt. The King guaranteed the debt and paid the interest until 1806. Moreover the Prince has indeed been [uncertain: cashiered] - the salary would have to be made over to him - [uncertain: Dräger] how much has the [uncertain: Forst] had this year in claim: 14000 Rthl, and that is just 112000 Rthl. Oh, splendid! he shall be helped.

The King comes out of the affair as the best of men, and on the debt item some 300000 Rthl are gained. A pity that the Prince has let his military affairs lie so, and that he did not press for a formal judgement - the money, or the 112000 Rthl, will most certainly be given, and the settlement of 1811 given up. Perhaps something great may yet be effected touching Your Serene Highness's honour. Tomorrow, on All Saints' Day, the Prince State Chancellor lays the matter before the King. Proud I shall be if my petition brings forth so many good effects. Here follows the last Pro Memoria, which I have obtained; it is a fatality that I have no Copy, for my hands are failing me from all the writing.
