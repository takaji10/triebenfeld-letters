--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports the Queen's death at Strelitz on the 19th and the King's grave illness, urges recovery of the 100000 Rthl and asks whether Köckritz has returned the papers, and complains of harassment over interest on the Invalids' capital and of Roßem's threatened sequestration of Triebenfeld's estates. Appended: a copy valuing the Knobelsdorff (Bischdorff, Mollna) estates against Zagoraa, with the Weigel and Stössel claims of 190000 and 180000 Rthl; and an October postscript on Hardenberg's promised help over the 112000 Rthl, with repeated pleas for 300 Rthl in gold.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
This very moment I receive from Friend F in Berlin the news that the Queen died at Strelitz on the 19th, early, at 9 o'clock; also that the King is very ill, and that the very saddest consequences are feared. Although I begged that these matters be sent to Exp with all haste, Your Serene Highness will no doubt have the news sooner. The Collorina affair is now an absurdity; now, gracious Lord, you must only see to it that you receive your 100000 Rthl. Have you already got the papers back through Köckritz - write me this most urgently, for I go soon to Berlin. - I shall try only to raise the travelling money and hasten thither. - Friend F is tracking his own trail [uncertain: Dage]. The visit from Pastor Willhelm you perhaps do not reckon high enough in the valuation; on the other hand it will presumably bring you [uncertain: seigen], that is to say, the cases must occur one after another in their sequence - previously the contrary was the case - the certain answer, most gracious Lord, you will now receive in the hereafter - but may God grant it only after some 35 years. I must, if only I can scrape up the travelling money, absolutely hasten to Berlin. - I am being frightfully harried over the interest on account of the Invalids' capital. - Roßem I should like to become [?]; the clumsy sack means to sequestrate my estates and to have [uncertain: Leibhartin] - see, I suffer all this for your sake - if only one of the V[ienna]: friends could help. Had I money - had I at least horses, I would fly from one place to another; but the accursed post-money taxes the purse too much, and I am still too much in arrears. Ever, however, Your Serene Highness's most faithful servant, v. Triebenfeld, 6 Xbr 1809

Dear upright Friend,

Enclosed I convey to you the valuations, and a Copy of the Landschaft assessment of the Biocdorff and Molna estates. The latter are, as the figure shows me, sound, so that everyone must find Herr v. Knobelsdorff's demand fair. The debts intabulated upon the estates consist in mortgage bonds and small mortgages. They amount together to 330000 Rthl, and, since only 75000 Rthl are callable after the expiry of the moratorium, they can hardly become burdensome to you, my honest friend, given your means. Everything depends on how you are in a position to handle Herr v. Stössel. He is hard pressed and, as I know, is offering his papers at 50 per cent; I believe you may buy the whole 180000 Rthl from him for 90000 Rthl, whereby he must moreover concede sound terms of payment. - To press this gentleman is no sin, for he treated the good Prince frightfully in the Köschent [uncertain: mir Handel] dealings. Meanwhile I leave that to your wisdom.

For the rest the matter now stands thus: - Bischdorff yields, according to the Landschaft assessment, annually 20462 Rthl 12 s; Mollna 10955 Rthl 18 s. The royal yield is small - the [uncertain: Schauß] taken only at 12; the 60000 Rthl can therefore very properly be conceded. Hence more by 3000 Rthl. Consequently annual revenue 34414 Rthl. The interest amounts: to the Landschaft, at 4 1/4 per cent, 10264 Rthl; to the mortgage creditors 3750; to [uncertain: Stothet], for whom I still allow 100000 capital, 5000; for Herr R. R. N. Weigel's capital of 190000 Rthl, 9500 = 28514 Rthl. And accordingly there remains to you, dear friend, already a surplus of 5900 Rthl. With your interest - which after all derives from interest upon interest and God knows what - you will, dear old friend, if you are called proprietor and lord of jurisdiction of the above-named lordships, have an annual net revenue of 16000 Rthl; and what will you, as a rich man, not further make of the ironworks? I wish you all fortune therein, victory, and yet another 20 years' span of life, in which I shall often rejoice to have been the intermediary who served you and the good Prince in so sound a manner.

With particular esteem, etc.

v. Triebenfeld

Postscript of the 30th October.

Because Your Serene Highness's letter of the 9th had been 11 days on the road before I received it, I supposed the course of the post to be at fault that I received no answer from Your Serene Highness to 5 letters - but now I hear from His Highness the Prince Chancellor that Your Serene Highness has written to him; consequently I must believe that my exertions here are not received with approval, and that Your Serene Highness does not wish to deem me worthy of an answer. - Were this really the case, then deign, Most Serene Prince, at least to let me know it. -

As for what otherwise concerns Your Serene Highness's affair, the Prince State Chancellor takes it up with true earnestness; his words today were: - I will help the old man. I mean the King to pay the Ler[?] debt. The King has guaranteed the debt and paid the interest up to 1806. Also, the Prince has indeed been [uncertain: cassirt]; his salary ought to be given him. - [illegible] how much has the Prince had this year in [uncertain: Ant:] 14000 Rthl, and it is just 112000 Rthl. O fine! he shall be helped. The King comes out of the affair as the best of men, and on the debt item some 300000 Rthl are indeed gained. A pity that the Prince let his military affairs lie so, and that he did not press for a formal judgment. - The money, or the 112000 Rthl, will most certainly be given, and the [uncertain: Transactoren] of 1811 abandoned. Perhaps something great will yet be effected touching Your Serene Highness's honour. - Tomorrow, on All Saints' Day, the Prince State Chancellor lays the matter before the King. Proud shall I be if my submission brings forth so many good effects.
