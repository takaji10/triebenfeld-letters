--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Forwards by mounted messenger an offer from sub-prefect von Brzechsta to lease the Zagorowo lordship for six years at 20,000-25,000 rl a year, restoring the three demesne farms for 3,000 Rthl and bearing all burdens, but demanding 10,000 Rthl compensation if the estates are sold; urges acceptance. Lists urgent payments of some 6,600 Rthl, warns that sequestration will resume, and asks whether to treat instead with General von Schimmelpfennig, and about Weigel, Stössel and freeing Köschentin.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Although the last messenger has not yet returned, and Your Serene Highness's enlightened resolutions are unknown to me, I would not delay in submitting to Your Serene Highness most obediently, by a mounted messenger, a proposition made to me by the sub-prefect von Brzechsta. The said Mr. v. von Brzechsta wishes to lease the Zagorowo lordship for 6 years.

He offers annually 20,000 - 25,000 rl. He will put the 3 demesne farms back into complete good order for 3000 Rthl, and bear all the burdens. He will make payment half-yearly and give security with his [uncertain: Hnabe] and estates. He demands, however, that if the estates were sold and he were put out of the lease, there should then be given or granted him a sum for withdrawal of 10,000 Rthl, which he would be entitled to recover afterwards out of the arrears, which in his opinion are not likely to be obtained from the inhabitants any time soon.

Whether Your Serene Highness wishes to make any use of this offer of Mr. von Brzechsta I leave entirely to your most high discretion, and I most obediently beg to be graciously instructed thereon. My unpresumptuous suggestion would be not to reject this plan so entirely, and to conclude a contract.

Most obediently I observe that, on account of the impending war and the threatening dreadful revolutions, it becomes absolutely necessary to come to a resolution regarding the estates as quickly as possible. With the most frightful exertion I am not in a position to bring together so much that the appallingly high taxes and the interest could be paid. I will, shall, and out of 1st. The [uncertain: Podothen] with [illegible]

- pay 1200 Rthl

2. settle the new exorbitant deliveries to Warsaw with

1240 Rthl 8 gr.

3. interest to the Invalids' Fund - to remit 1250

4. to have 1250 Rthl sent to Weigel

5. old soldiers and other costs 1730 Rthl [uncertain: bemühtiger] All the attachments have indeed been lifted out of friendship for me by the prefect sub-prefect and the president of the tribunal - but if the costs are not paid, the sequestration sets in anew, and what do I not risk with the Invalids' Fund? The prefect Mr. von Brzechsta is now so good as to take upon himself, since

the deputy judge [illegible]ukarski, his friend, has however always been against us, the pursuit of the interest and arrears - but he, like myself, doubts that, in the present dreadful want of money, much could be brought together. The people are truly not only poor but obstinate, and confounded by the utterly repugnant ordinances - A man such as the sub-prefect Brzechsta can, by degrees, with the power residing in him, bring everything back into a good course. May Your Serene Highness therefore graciously instruct me what you most highly intend to do. Whether you will accordingly treat with the general von Schimmelpfennig, or whether the lease contract is to be concluded with Brzechsta, and afterwards whether the agreement with Weigel and Stössel is then to be effected and Köschentin freed?

In the first case I would indeed wish Your Serene Highness every conceivable good fortune from the bottom of my heart, but for the rest I should have to withdraw from this affair and fall silent in reverence, the more so as I see no further advantage in this business than that Your Serene Highness would receive some thousands of [uncertain: Propriver] rl. in worthless papers, but would lose dreadfully thereby. Should Your Serene Highness approve the 2nd plan, I would come with Mr. etc. von Brzechsta

to Schlawenschitz - the contract would be concluded, and Mr. S. von Brzechsta would probably also bring a couple of thousand [illegible] with him, and then the negotiations with Weigel and Stössel could be set in train and [uncertain: beynekt], to which end I gladly throw in my Blizanow holdings, the estates which I am still [uncertain: sending in] against it. The Olesnica and Wrąbczyn matter will at last come up next week; I firmly hope that this affair will be of blessed consequences - Until, however, these matters are concluded, I cannot yet get away from here.

For the rest, the Diet in Warsaw has ended very stormily, and cruelly many oppressive new taxes have been laid upon the estate owners. The King has returned to Dresden. Strong speeches were made. For the New Year entered upon today I wish Your Serene Highness with the most joyful heart every conceivable good fortune and the most enduring health - and I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Blizanow

4 Jan.

1812

most faithful and humble and obedient servant
