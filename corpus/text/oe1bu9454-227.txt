--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld übersendet das Angebot des Unterpräfecten von Brzechffa, die Herrschaft Zagórów 6 Jahre für jährlich 20,000-25,000 Rthl zu pachten und die 3 Vorwerke für 3000 Rthl instand zu setzen. Triebenfeld rät zum Vertrag, da er Abgaben und Zinsen, darunter 1250 Rthl an die Invaliden Casse, nicht aufbringen kann und neue Sequestration droht. Er bittet um Entscheidung zwischen einem Handel mit General von Schimmelpfennig und der Pacht mit anschließendem Vergleich mit Weigel und Stössel zur Freimachung von Koszęcin.

Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Although the last messenger has not yet returned, and Your Serene Highness's enlightened resolutions are unknown to me, I would not delay in most obediently forwarding to Your Serene Highness, by a mounted messenger, a proposition made to me by the sub-prefect von Brzechffa. The said Mr von Brzechffa wishes to lease the Zagórów lordship for 6 years.

He offers annually 20,000 — 25,000 Rthl. He will put the 3 demesne farms back into complete condition for 3000 Rthl, and bear all the burdens. He will make payment half-yearly and gives his goods and estates as security. He requires, however, that should the estates be sold and he be put out of the lease, a sum of withdrawal of 10,000 Rthl should then be given or granted to him, which he should be entitled to recover afterwards out of the costs, which in his opinion could not be obtained from the inhabitants so soon.

Whether Your Serene Highness wishes to make any use of this offer of Mr von Brzechffa, I leave entirely to Your Highness's judgement, and I most obediently beg to be graciously instructed thereon. My proposal, without presuming, would be not to reject this plan altogether, and to conclude a contract.

Most obediently I observe that, on account of the impending war and the dreadful revolutions also threatening, it becomes unavoidably necessary to reach a decision concerning the estates as quickly as possible. With the most fearful exertion I am not in a position to bring together so much that the appallingly high taxes and the interest could be paid. I will, shall, and out of it — 1st. Pay the taxes with [uncertain: diein] — 1200 Rthl

2. settle the new exorbitant deliveries to Warsaw with 1240 Rthl 8 Groschen

3. send interest to the Invalids' Fund — 1250 Rthl

4. have 1250 Rthl sent to Weigel

5. old taxes[?] and other costs 1730 Rthl [uncertain: bemühtiger]. All the writs of arrest have indeed been lifted out of friendship for me by the prefect sub-prefect and the president of the tribunal — but if the costs are not paid, sequestration sets in anew, and what do I not risk with the Invalids' Fund?

The prefect Mr von Brzechffa is now so good as to take upon himself, since

the assistant judge [uncertain: Gukarski], his friend, has however always been against us, the collection of the interest and the arrears — but he, like myself, doubts that in the present dreadful shortage of money much could be brought together. The people are truly not only poor, but obstinate, and confounded by the [uncertain: seelen] contrary ordinances — A man such as the sub-prefect Brzechffa can, little by little, with the authority residing in him, alone bring everything back into good order. May Your Serene Highness therefore be pleased graciously to instruct me what Your Highness intends to do. Whether Your Highness will accordingly treat with Mr General von Schimmelpfennig, or whether the lease contract is to be concluded with Brzechffa, and afterwards whether the agreement with Weigel and Stössel is to be effected and Koszęcin set free?

In the first case I would indeed wish Your Serene Highness every conceivable good fortune from the bottom of my heart, but for the rest I should have to withdraw from this affair and fall silent in reverence, all the more since in this business I see no further advantage than that Your Serene Highness would receive some thousand [uncertain: Frey inne] Rthl in [uncertain: schlechtschenden] papers, but would lose dreadfully thereby. Should Your Serene Highness approve the 2nd plan, I would come with Mr von Brzechffa

to Sławięcice — the contract would be concluded, and Mr von Brzechffa would probably also bring a couple of thousand along, and then the negotiations with Weigel and Stössel could be set on foot and [uncertain: concluded], to which I would gladly throw in my Blizanow holdings, the estates which I still [uncertain: einsandle] against it. The Oleśnica and Wrąbczyn matter will at last come up next week; I firmly hope that this affair will be of blessed consequence — Meanwhile, until these matters are concluded, I cannot yet get away from here.

For the rest, the Reichstag in Warsaw has ended very stormily, and cruelly many oppressive new taxes have been laid upon the landowners. The King has returned to Dresden. Strong speeches have been held. For the New Year entered upon today I wish Your Serene Highness, with the most faithful heart, every conceivable good fortune and the most lasting health — and I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Blizanow

4 Jan.

1812

most faithful and humble and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld
