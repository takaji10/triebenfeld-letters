--- SUMMARY (English) ---
News, received by express courier, of the death of the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Neuenstein, who shot in the deer park on 20 July, fell ill on the 24th and died of apoplexy on the 27th. Under his testament naming Hahn's Prince sole heir, possession was taken at Öhringen and elsewhere and oaths of fealty sworn; counter-measures by the Princes of Langenburg and Kirchberg were repulsed and their patents replaced. The Prince travels there in September, leaving the South Prussian affairs to Triebenfeld.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Right Honourable Sir, Highly Honoured Sir, Councillor of War and Forests! To Your Right Honourable I hereby report most obediently that yesterday afternoon and at 6 o'clock, by express courier, the news arrived of the decease of the reigning Prince zu Hohenlohe Neuenstein, Serene Highness; the aged gentleman on the 20th of July still shot a piece of fir-wood in the game park, was seized on the 24th of the same month by the [uncertain: Rathlauf], which was an ordinary illness with him, and ended, through an apoplectic stroke that supervened, on the 27th early after 10 o'clock

his pious and praiseworthy life. At Oehringen, as in all Hohenlohe places of this branch of the line, possession was at once taken, in accordance with the testament which has named my most gracious Prince sole heir of these fine lands — and town and country joyfully rendered the oath of fealty. Attempts were made on the part of the Princes zu Langenburg and Kirchberg to exercise [uncertain: geheliche] measures, grounded upon a certain misunderstood partition treaty; but manful resistance was offered by the [uncertain: Aufaenger] of the Ingelfingen side, the troops were turned back, and the Ingelfingen patents of taking possession

were posted up, and there too, where on the opposing side they permitted themselves — to post up theirs, these were taken down again and the Ingelfingen ones affixed in their place. Thus stands the whole affair at present — and the arrival of His Serene Highness is most ardently awaited there, as now lord of the most flourishing fields of Franconia. His Highness will also set out upon the journey thither in the month of September, once the Silesian [uncertain: Reonen] are concluded; how long the stay will last is still undecided; only that His Serene Highness now believes he must leave over all the South Prussian affairs the more to Your Right Honourable's wise discernment, since His Highness will again be away for a considerable while

All this I have wished to report to the same as is my bounden duty, and permit myself only further to add the assurance of true veneration, in which I ever have the honour to be Your Right Honourable's

Breslau, the 4th of August 1805

humble and obedient servant
A. Hahn
