--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Encloses the drafted reply to Prince August, noting that withdrawal of the power of attorney follows from the Vienna resolutions, and warns of fresh blunders at Öhringen that only a personal journey could avert; requests a secret instruction and power of attorney for that extremity, and a note in the Prince's own hand about travel money. Goldstücker will be pacified over the bill of exchange. Blames Mr. v. Braun, no match for Cosmar and condemned by Princess Emilie, and proposes inspecting the estates to establish their true income and a fund for building costs, so their means no longer benefit a Berlin advocate.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
In accordance with Your Serene Highness's command of today, I send herewith the drafted reply to Prince August. The withdrawal of the power of attorney certainly flows from the consequences of the Vienna resolutions; it is, however, to be feared that new blunders will be made in Öhringen, and these could only be averted by word of mouth, since experience teaches how little has hitherto been achieved by all written negotiation. In Öhringen I have confidence that I can bring about everything that is necessary for Your Highness's peace of mind, and in the extreme case one there looks at matters oneself and conducts oneself according to the circumstances and according to the manner in which one is in the habit of conducting oneself towards Your Highness —: A secret instruction for this case, together with a power of attorney, would be to me

urgently necessary, of which I should have to make use only in this extreme case. I believe, however, that all this is not necessary, and Your Serene Highness will for that which Your Highness's son there obtains through the altered circumstances of the times – be compensated here, since it will be easy for him to renounce an enjoyment of revenues which he hitherto did not have at all and which, so far as I know the course of ideas there, he still very much calls in doubt. [uncertain: When] an answer to this letter arrives one must see more clearly into everything, and I hope that if Your Highness were still to add something in his own hand concerning the travelling money, this obstacle too would be removed. Goldstüker I shall readily set at ease by means of this journey, which he has long wished for, because he has always wanted to come to terms over the bill of exchange. The foolish

Herr von Braun is the root of all the trouble hitherto, because he was in no way a match for Cosmar, and would not at all find his way into the sphere of activity that opened up to him. Princess Emilie has here several times broken the staff over him, and has named him to me by his name; it does not appear, however, that this has harmed him in the Prince's opinion. By means of the particular inspection of the estates I hope to be able to convince Your Highness of their true and to-be-hoped-for yield for the next budget year, for from that same inspection it also follows how much is indispensably necessary for the buildings, and I shall then [uncertain: propose] to Your Highness a fund out of which these cash outlays can be met without diminishing the actual revenues. In all these matters much depends on the forms one ob-

serves.

It is high time that the great [uncertain: means] of the estates be no longer frittered away for the benefit of a Berlin advocate, but rather come to those to whom they belong. Once all is in order, I hope to have proved by the deed itself who it is that has truly been honestly disposed towards Your Highness and the most high family, even if I do not often name my honest and well-meaning sentiments in words, because I am in earnest about them
Hahn

the 15th of February 1815
