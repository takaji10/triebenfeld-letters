--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that the inheritance case awaits resolution, the papers lying with the Chamber Court's and Cabinet Councillor Albrecht's opinion before a high official whom the Queen presses for it, and that the distraint business is proceeding. Urges the Prince to write plainly to the King and answer the Commission openly, praising Major von Pirch as his ablest advocate and noting General von Köckritz, Haacke and Massenbach's parallel appeal, delayed by Scharnhorst's fever. Doubts the Consul's reports of Austrian losses and Russian war declaration; recounts Schill's stroke against French and Westphalian regiments.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! Your Most Serene Highness's two letters of the 24th and 29th of last month I received today together. Submissively I reply that in the inheritance matter no resolution has yet been given, but rather that the whole affair, with the opinion of the upright and honest Chamber Court and of the Cabinet Councillor Albrecht, lies before His Excellency the [uncertain: Gr. K. Beym], from whom the Queen's Majesty awaits it most eagerly. With regard to Your Serene Highness's domestic affairs, and especially concerning the so-called distraint business, whatever Your Highness could only for the present wish will follow. As concerns the war affairs, there Your Serene Highness is yourself at fault; your friends have long since most earnestly advised you that you should write to His Majesty in a straightforward tone and beg for the conclusion of the matter, but in doing so should go to work openly in the matter itself and pay compliments to no one. Your Serene Highness's most honest and most faithful friend here is the truly upright Major von Pirch; this is, by God, a rare, honest and thoroughly upright man, who pursues your cause better than Your Serene Highness yourself, guides it with wisdom, and judges and sees through it with the keen eye of the most expert man. His Excellency General v. Köckritz means very well, only this good gentleman cannot be as effective in the business as he might well wish. Haacke and others are well disposed, because the noble, upright Major Pirch directs them and steers them to the point. Massenbach has written to His Majesty and begged for the conclusion of his affair. He appeals to General von Scharnhorst; the latter has

unfortunately the fever, and so the whole affair hangs. For the rest, most gracious Prince and Lord, I implore you yourself: follow the counsel of truly upright friends; write to the King and beg for the swift conclusion of your affair. But answer the Commission quite openly, [uncertain: do house of another's sin], and pay compliments to no one. You must in earnest risk that the staff be broken over [text lost] — one departs on no account from the form. Reports from the [uncertain: armies] are altogether wanting here. There is in this an ever-continuing alternation, like ebb and flood. — With the greatest certainty it may be assumed that the loss of the Austrians cannot be so great as the Consul here had it trumpeted abroad a few days ago, according to which the French army was to be in Vienna within 8 days and the whole Austrian army already now totally cut to pieces. — Just so, the Russian declaration does not appear to be so grave as the Consul here had it distributed in all quarters as a true declaration of war. — Russia, according to the true sense of it, is only breaking off all diplomatic negotiations with Austria, because the latter cannot enter upon the proposed peace negotiations. — Schill, according to the reports which came in here yesterday, has already done a bold stroke: cut two regiments of French and Westphalians quite to pieces, taken 4 cannon with him, and is hastening to Cassel and from there to Hanover, where the English troops are said already to have arrived. He signs himself Major General of the first Brunswick [uncertain: Hanoverian] Division. Once more I beg Your Serene Highness heartily: write to the Monarch, spare no one in the questions, and be then assured that your wishes will come to fulfilment. Full of reverence as a servant I remain Your Most Serene Highness's Königsberg, the 13th of May 1809 truly humble and obedient servant Peter
