Durchlauchtigſter Fürſt
Gnädigster Fürst und Herr!
Euer Hochfürstliche Durchlaucht beide Schreiben vom 24ten und 29ten v. M. habe ich heute
zu sammen erhalten. Submissiß erwiedern ich, daß in der Erbschafts Ange¬
legenheit noch keine Resolution gegeben worden, sondern daß die ganze Sache mit
dem Gutachten des Biderin und redlichen Cammergerichts und Cabinets kath Albrecht

erwartet.
In Ansehung Ewr Durchlaucht Häuslichen Angelegenheiten und besonders wegen
der sogenannten Auspfändungs Geschichte, wird was Höchstdießelben nur
vor der Hand wünschen können, erfolgen.
An betref der Krieges Geschichten da ſind Ewr Durchlaucht Selbſt ſchuld
Ihre Freunde haben höchst Ihren längst gerathen daß Sie in einem Geraden
Tone an Sr Majestät schreiben und um Beendigung der Sache bitten, dabey
aber offen in der Sache selbst zu Werke gehen und mit Niemanden Compliment
machen sollen.
Ewr Durchlaucht redlichſter und treueſten Freund hier iſt der wahrhaft Bider
Major von Pirch, dies ist bey Gott ein seltener redlicher und durch
aus ehrlicher Mann der Ihre Sache beßer, wie Ewr Durchlaucht selbst be¬
treibt, mit Weißheit leitet und mit dem Scharßblick des Sachkundigsten
Mannes Beurtheilt und Durchschaut - dH. Genl v. Köckritz Excellenz meint es
ſehr gut, nur kan dieſer gute Herr dabey nicht ſo würbsam denn als er
es wohl wünscht - Haacke und andere sind gut weil Sie der edle bidere
Major Pirch lenckt und auf die Pointe hingegt.
Massenbach hat an Sr Majestät geschrieben und um Beendigung seiner Sache
gebeten - Er beruft sich auf den Herrn Gen: von Scharnhorst - dieser hat

zum Unglück das Fieber und so hängt die ganze Sache

Rath wahrhaft biderer Freunde, Schreiben Sie dem König und Bitte um schnelle
Beendigung Ihrer Sache - antworten aber der Komißion gerade offen thun
Haus fremder Sünde und machen mit Niemanden Complimente. Sie müßen
ernsten risquiren, daß der Staab über die
zubrochen wird - Mann weicht
durchaus nicht von der Form-
Stelle Neuigkeiten von den Armeen fehlen hier gänzlich. Es ist darin ein immer
währender Wechßel der Ebbe und Fluth gleichs – Mit größter Gewisheit läst
ſich annehmen, daß der Verlust der Oſterreicher nicht ſo groß ſein kan als ihn

französische Armee schon im 8 Tagen in Wien und die ganze Osterreichsche
Armee schon jezt total aufgerieben sein solte —
Eben so scheint die Muße Erklärung nicht so arg zu sein die der Er Consul

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that no decision has yet come in the inheritance matter, the papers resting with a minister after opinions from the High Court of Appeal and Cabinet Councillor Albrecht, with the Queen awaiting the outcome. Urges the Prince to write plainly to the King for a speedy conclusion of his war-related case and to answer the Commission frankly; praises Major von Pirch as his ablest advocate, alongside General von Köckritz and Haacke. Notes Massenbach's petition and war news of Schill's successes and Austrian losses.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Your Most Serene Highness's two letters of the 24th and 29th of last month I have received today together. Submissively I reply that in the inheritance matter no resolution has yet been given, but rather that the whole affair, with the opinion of the upright and honest High Court of Appeal and of the Cabinet Councillor Albrecht, lies before His Excellency [uncertain: dh. Gr. K. Beym], from whom Her Majesty the Queen most eagerly awaits it.

With regard to Your Serene Highness's domestic affairs, and especially concerning the so-called distraint business, whatever Your Highness can for the present only wish for will follow.

As concerns the war matters, there Your Serene Highness is yourself to blame; your friends have long since most earnestly advised you that you should write to His Majesty in a plain tone and ask for the conclusion of the affair, but in doing so should go to work openly in the matter itself and pay compliments to no one.

Your Serene Highness's most honest and most faithful friend here is the truly upright Major von Pirch; this is, by God, a rare, honest and thoroughly upstanding man, who pursues your cause better than Your Serene Highness yourself does, guides it with wisdom, and judges and sees through it with the keen eye of the most knowledgeable man. — His Excellency General von Köckritz means very well, only this good gentleman cannot therein be so [uncertain: würbsam] as he well wishes to be. — Haacke and others are well disposed, because the noble, upright Major Pirch steers them and directs them to the point.

Massenbach has written to His Majesty and asked for the conclusion of his affair. — He appeals to General von Scharnhorst; the latter has

unfortunately the fever, and so the whole affair hangs. For the rest, most gracious Prince and Lord, I implore you — follow the counsel of truly upright friends, write to the King and ask for a speedy conclusion of your affair — but answer the Commission quite openly, [uncertain: thun Haus fremder Sünde] and pay compliments to no one. You must in earnest run the risk that the staff be broken over [text lost]

broken — one departs

by no means from the form — place. News of the armies is entirely wanting here. There is in this a perpetual alternation, like ebb and flood. — With the greatest certainty it may be assumed that the loss of the Austrians cannot be so great as the Consul here had it trumpeted abroad a few days ago, according to which the French army was to be in Vienna within 8 days and the whole Austrian army was already now to be totally cut to pieces. — Just so, the [uncertain: Muße] declaration does not seem to be so bad as the Consul here had it distributed in all quarters as a true declaration of war. — Russia, according to the true sense, is only breaking off all diplomatic negotiations with Austria, because the latter cannot enter into the proposed peace negotiations. — Schill, according to the reports that came in here yesterday, has already done a strong piece of work: cut to pieces entirely 2 regiments of French and Westphalians, taken 4 cannon with him, and hastens to Cassel and from there to Hanover, where the English troops are said already to have arrived. — He signs himself Major General of the first Brunswick [uncertain: Hanna versahm Diescion]. Once again I beg Your Serene Highness heartily — write to the monarch, spare no one in the questions, and then be assured that your wishes will be fulfilled.

Full of dutiful respect I remain, as

Your Most Serene Highness's

Königsberg,

the 13th of May 1809

faithful, humble and obedient servant

Peter
