--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports from Vienna that the renewed war blocks progress, but sets out a claim for indemnification to be laid before the Emperor and King: 60,000 Rthl pledge-money paid to von Broniewski plus 33,000 Rthl interest, the Kaemen estates valued at 180,000 Rthl, and Trąbczyn at 316,000 Rthl less debts to Lichnowski, Grotowski and Seher, giving 366,000 Rthl in debt-free estates and 93,000 Rthl cash. Alegemann does nothing, Pourtales has not answered. Long news of Napoleon's return to Paris, Ney's defection, and a Württemberg scandal.

Most gracious Lord, I quite certainly believed I should be able this very post to report something definite and good to Your Serene Highness, but the outbreak of the war hinders everything - yet something will come to pass - Trąbczyn, and even Kaemen and Kulm, Your Serene Highness will receive back the more [uncertain: the] Herr Alexander has even prevailed upon our Most Gracious Lord to sign the matter along with him, namely that the Kohlen are to keep the estates which the S[equestration] Commission has returned to them - consequently no reckoning is to be made upon that any longer. First I only wish to know who is to indemnify Your Serene Highness - then I shall pick out what is of use and good -

Herr P. Alegemann does nothing, one may beg as one will - now, on account of the outbreak of the war, he has the more excuses - with God's assistance something will after all succeed in each case.

Say anything I still cannot. Pourtales has not yet answered - he too must be watching the outbreak of the war -- Meanwhile it is said that he will certainly come, for otherwise he would have written off. He was in Paris to call in his monies.

That is, Napoleon is now said really to be in Paris already; on the 18th, according to yesterday's courier reports, he was already at Fontainebleau. Marshal Ney has now likewise turned rogue - Napoleon has written here that his wife and his son are to be handed over to him -- and sent to him at Paris with his escort. Oh, what is going on now! The rabble now rules in France, for the adventurer has called them up and promised freedom and equality to the sansculottes -- It is a nation which at bottom one must abhor - the mob is said to be housing there worse than a herd of devils, and to mark their march, wherever they go, with cruelty and

atrocities which no man is able to describe - the King of France is said to be in Lille, and what is most lamentable is that not a shot has yet been fired by the King's troops -- the villain marches on thither with his rabble unhindered; everyone gets out of his way as from a mad dog. In the newspapers there are declamations of boundless loyalty and devotion to the King

adduced; Bonaparte's force is put down as low as 8 to 10 men, and yet the Emperor is in Paris and drives out the adored King. Here the gentlemen are listening to hear whether they will not be called upon from France's side for assistance - they [uncertain: arm themselves] and leave it; would that Heaven willed. Meanwhile the robber and the [uncertain: Cancellie] get so far that hundreds of thousands will have to be led to the shambles. O tempora, o mores - King Murat too is now said already to be raising his head, and before anyone is aware of it he will be housing in Italy and pressing on right up to the gates - The distress, or rather the dearness, here now exceeds all bounds - for what are the poor to be [uncertain: mobilised] and war to be waged? One looks forward to nothing but the most dreadful suns of terror. The Württemberg affair has, according to the reports come in this night, [uncertain: scandalously graced itself] before the fat Majesty. Let Your Serene Highness read the enclosures and be amazed. The Prince [uncertain: Stadt] and Herr Metternich also set this gross creature very sorely in [uncertain: a corner], and now the more than boundless disgrace - that his own brothers set him so [uncertain: early] in the pillory - the poor Duke Wilhelm, who after all played the part of Minister of War with him, was at once driven off by the tenderly thankful Majesty; and Eugen's son too he has, out of Most Gracious clemency, commanded to go straightway to the devil; Honrichs and Loar he has threatened to have imprisoned for ever - the business causes much amusement here. What vexes me is that the wisdom of his Diet's president is so odiously [uncertain: persiflaged]. This Illustrious Lord is now being cruelly dragged through the mire, both here and by the mediatised. Perhaps I shall be in a position to send in more consoling news than hitherto with the next post. In deepest reverence I remain, until death,
