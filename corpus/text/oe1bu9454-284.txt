--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld meldet, dass Trąbczyn, Kamionna und Kolno nicht mehr zu erwarten sind: Alexander habe erreicht, dass die Polen die ihnen zurückgegebenen Güter behalten. Er beantragt Entschädigung. Für Kamionna fordert er 93000 Rthl baar (Pfandschilling Broniewski samt Zinsen) und 120000 Rthl in Gütern. Für Trąbczyn, taxiert auf 316000 Rthl, bleiben nach Abzug der Schulden 246000 Rthl. Insgesamt 459000 Rthl. Weigels 30000 Rthl sind auf Zagórów übertragen. Stägemann tut nichts. Bittet um Blanquets und um Eberhards Kommen wegen des Vergleichs.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld reports that Trąbczyn, Kamionna and Kolno are no longer to be expected: he says Alexander has secured that the Poles keep the estates returned to them. He applies for compensation. For Kamionna he demands 93000 Rthl in cash (the Broniewski mortgage sum together with interest) and 120000 Rthl in estates. For Trąbczyn, valued at 316000 Rthl, 246000 Rthl remain after deduction of the debts. In all 459000 Rthl. Weigel's 30000 Rthl have been transferred to Zagórów. Stägemann does nothing. He asks for blank forms and for Eberhard to come because of the settlement.

Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord, I quite certainly believed I should be able to report something definite — something good — to Your Serene Highness by today's post, but the outbreak of the war impedes everything — yet something will come of it — Trąbczyn, and even Kamionna and Kulm, Your Serene Highness will never again recover — Mr Alexander has even induced our Most Gracious Lord to set his signature to the matter, namely that the Poles are to keep the estates which the commission restored to them — consequently no further reckoning is to be made upon that. Now I am submitting to the Emperor and the [uncertain: King] the just petition that Your Serene Highness be indemnified — my calculation shall not be an insignificant one —

1. Your Serene Highness had to pay von Broniewski his pledge-money of circa 60000 rt. 180 3/5. To this the interest of 15 years, 33000 rt. Total [illegible] — 93000 rt.

Here it is to be observed that although Meyer Bernhard gave the money at that time, this capital nevertheless stands, like all the others, registered out of Pszczew and the Kujavian estates, the latter of which the Jew M. Bernhard still now holds in pledge for it, wherefore this claim belongs wholly to Your Serene Highness —

2. the Kamionna estates were at that time assessed by Otto's courts at 180000 rt. Deduct therefrom the above 60000 rt.

there remain 120000 rt.

Accordingly Your Serene Highness must receive for Kamionna 213000 rt, namely 93000 rt in cash and 120000 rt in estates

3. of Trąbczyn: these estates would be, as [assessed] by me, valued at 316000 rt; upon them there still stand the debts contracted by Your Serene Highness.

1. for Lichnowski 40000 rt.

2. Grotowski 20000 rt.

3. Seher Thos 8000 rt. there remains in value, and should they come through to the good, 246000 rt, together 459000 rt.

The Emperor or the King must therefore give Your Serene Highness debt-free estates to the value of 366000 rt, and those 93000 rt you must receive in cash — upon Trąbczyn there stood a further 34000 rt for Lichnowski, but these are paid; still, one cannot demand them, because they were debts which Your Serene Highness had contracted — Weigel's 30000 rt have been transferred onto Zagórów — The [illegible] gentlemen shall grow pale enough — and first I only wish to know who is to indemnify Your Serene Highness; then I shall duly pick out what is of use and good — Mr Stägemann does nothing, beg as one may — now, with the outbreak of the war, he has more excuses — with God's help something will yet succeed. I can say nothing yet — Pourtalès has still not answered — he too must be waiting to see the outbreak of the war. Meanwhile D. says he will certainly come, since otherwise he would have written off. He was in Paris to call in his monies — Napoleon is now said really to be already in Paris; on the 18th, according to yesterday's courier reports, he was already at Fontainebleau. Monsieur Rey has now also turned rogue — Napoleon has written here that his wife and his son are to be handed over to him, and sent to him at Paris under his escort — Oh, what is now afoot! The mob now rules in France, for it is the mob this adventurer has called up, promising freedom and equality to the sans-culottes — It is a nation one must at bottom abhor — the rabble there is said to be housing worse than a herd of devils, and to mark their march, wherever they go, with cruelties and atrocities which no man is able to describe — the King of France is said to be at Lille, and what is most [uncertain: lamentable] is that not a shot has yet been fired by the King's troops — the Evil One moves on with his horde unchecked; everyone gets out of his way as out of a mad dog's — In the newspapers there are reasonings [text lost] of boundless loyalty and devotion to the King

set out; Bonaparte's force is reduced to 8 to 10 thousand men, and nevertheless the Emperor is in Paris and drives out the adored King — Here the gentlemen are listening to hear whether they will not be called upon from the French side for assistance — they [uncertain: are gratifying themselves] and let it be; would to Heaven — Meanwhile the robber and the rabble get so far that hundreds of thousands will have to be led to the shambles — O tempora, o mores — King Murat too is said already to be raising his head, and before anyone is aware of it he will be housing in Italy and pressing perhaps as far as the gates of V[ienna] — The distress, or rather the dearness, here now passes all bounds — for what is the poor militia to be furnished and the war waged? One sees nothing but the most conceivable suns of terror before one — the Württemberg affair has, according to the reports come in this night, [uncertain: turned out] scandalously for the fat Majesty — Let Your Serene Highness read the enclosures and be astonished — Prince Hardenberg and Prince Metternich too set this corpulent creature very sorely in [uncertain: a corner], and now the more than boundless disgrace — that his own brothers put him so early in the pillory — the poor Duke Wilhelm, who after all played the part of Minister of War with him, him the tenderly thanking Majesty at once drove off; the son of Eugen too he has, out of his Most Gracious clemency, ordered to go straightway to the devil; Heinrich and Loar he has threatened to have imprisoned for ever — the affair affords much amusement here — What vexes me is that the wisdom of his Diet's president is so odiously [uncertain: ridiculed]. This Illustrious Lord is now being cruelly pulled to pieces, both here and by the mediatised princes. Perhaps I shall be in a position to send in more consoling news by the next post than hitherto. In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

turn over

Your Serene Highness's
