--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that Trąbczyn and Kaemen have been seized by force by the Prusimski family, against which little can presently be done; protests have been entered to secure the sum Triebenfeld registered, though he expects dismissal. Notes that General von Sanitz's wife has petitioned Napoleon, whose reply on the Maniecki estate and Wibicki will set the norm for the Prince's case. He travels next to Memel and perhaps Petersburg, hoping to work through Grand Duke Constantine, having presented him with firearms; the Prussian King and Queen revere the Prince but cannot help.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Whether this letter will share the fate of all the preceding ones I leave undecided; out of faithful attachment to Your Most Serene Highness I cannot grow weary of working on wherever it may be done, and of reporting most obediently to Your Highness by this route the events that have lately occurred.

That Trąbczyn and Kaemen have been taken by force by the Prusimski family Your Serene Highness will doubtless already know, and that at present nothing of consequence can be done against these one-sided undertakings Your Serene Highness will be pleased to perceive. I have merely had the protests entered, in order much to secure the sum registered by me. Whether this will succeed I do not yet venture to assert; indeed I rather believe that for the present everything will be dismissed. The hope that the matter will change as a whole and that the [uncertain: violent] situation will take on a quite different shape is the only [uncertain: anchor] upon which I alone build. The wife of General von Sanitz, from whom too the estates conferred upon her husband have been taken, has herself written to the Emperor Napoleon and asked him for a declaration, since he only [commanded] all, to give back to Wibicki the estate of Maniecki, but to leave everything else in statu quo

19 October 1807

commanded; the answer in that matter will now serve as the norm in Your Serene Highness's case as well, and as soon as it comes I too shall draw up the necessary plans for further operation. — I go tomorrow again to Memel and perhaps from there with commissions to Petersburg, or wherever I shall find the Emperor Alexander. It may easily happen that through the Grand Duke Constantine, to whom I made a present of the fine set of firearms, I may effect something good in Your Serene Highness's cause. Through our own [uncertain: court] I can effect not the least thing in the affair. Both the King and the adored Queen, with all the [uncertain: attendants] now present there, revere Your Serene Highness in the highest degree — but no one can help at this time. Nor do I trouble the good monarch at all with matters of this kind, for, since he truly loves and esteems Your Serene Highness, it would only increase his sorrow. But if I am so fortunate as to [illegible] or also to come near to His Constantine, then I shall bend every effort that these should help; and I believe that it will only succeed to find means whereby such a [illegible] may be given to the matter that all the Poles shall [uncertain: be made to open] their [uncertain: sons], should they take a fancy to stretch out their claws further after Your Serene Highness's property.

May Heaven only grant that Your Serene Highness soon return. With boundless faithful attachment and the deepest respect I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

[illegible]

most faithful and obedient servant

Breslau, the 19th October

1807

Peter
