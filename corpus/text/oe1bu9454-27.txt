--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that Trąbczin and Kaemen have been seized by force by the Prusimski family, against which nothing can presently be done; protestations have been lodged to secure the sum he registered, though he expects dismissal. Notes that Frau Generalin v. Sanitz, similarly dispossessed, has petitioned Emperor Napoleon, whose reply will set the norm. Plans to travel to Memel and perhaps Petersburg to reach Emperor Alexander, hoping Grand Duke Constantin, recipient of a set of firearms, may help; Höst can achieve nothing.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! Whether this letter will meet the fate of all the preceding ones I leave undecided; out of faithful attachment to Your Most Serene Highness I cannot grow weary of continuing to work wherever it can be done, and of reporting most obediently to the Same, by this route, the events which have lately occurred. That Trąbczin and Kaemen have been taken by force by the Prusimski family, Your Serene Highness will doubtless already know, and that at present nothing of consequence can be done against these one-sided undertakings Your Serene Highness will deign to perceive. I have merely had the protestations lodged, in order very much to secure the sum registered by me. Whether this will succeed I do not yet venture to assert; indeed I rather believe that for the time being everything will be dismissed. The hope that the matter will change as a whole and that the present situation will take on quite another shape is the only anchor upon which I alone build. The Frau Generalin v. Sanitz, from whom the estates conferred upon her husband have likewise been taken, has written herself to the Emperor Napoleon and asked him for a declaration, since he only commanded that all, to Wibicki the estate Maniecki be given back, but that everything else be left in statu quo. 19 8br. 1807

commanded — the answer in that regard will now serve as the norm in Your Serene Highness's affair as well, and as soon as it arrives I too shall draw up the necessary plans for further operation. — I go tomorrow again to Memel and perhaps from there with commissions to Petersburg, or wherever I shall find the Emperor Alexander. It may easily come about that, through the Grand Duke Constantin, to whom I presented the fine set of firearms, I may effect something good in Your Serene Highness's affair. Through our Höst I can effect not the least thing in the business. Both the King and the adored Queen, with all the Alantons now present there, revere Your Serene Highness in the highest degree — but no one can help at this time; nor do I trouble the good monarch at all with matters of this kind, for it would, since he truly loves and esteems Your Serene Highness, only increase his sorrow. But if I am so fortunate as to come near Allerwürcke, or also to your Konstantin, then I shall exert everything so that these shall help; and I believe that it will only succeed in finding means whereby the affair shall be given such a turn that all the Poles shall have their sons opened up, should it please them to stretch out their claws after Your Serene Highness's property any further. May Heaven only grant that Your Serene Highness soon return. With unbounded faithful attachment and the deepest esteem I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's most humble and most faithfully obedient servant. Breslau, the 19th 8br. 1807. Peter
