--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that Count Schlabrendorff has been satisfied, the interest amounting to 11085 Rthl, with the receipt made out to the Prince, while nothing has come towards covering Triebenfeld's own 10 [thousand]. Notes three failed attempts to be received by the Princess and the sensation caused in Breslau by the writing to Prittwitz; describes the Queen's illness and recovery, the King's activity and troop drills in Berlin, and the absence of any prospect of war. Bad news from Glenck in the Prusimski lawsuit prompts him to use the blank forms entrusted to him to petition the Grand Chancellor for an extension of the term.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Lord! I have satisfied Count Schlabrendorff and have the receipt to hand — the sum of the interest came to 11085 Rthl, the receipt is made out directly to Your Most Serene Highness — I, however, have now not a syllable towards the covering of my 10. "to hand; nor do I ask anything after it, for I am too certain that Heaven will help Your Most Serene Highness back quite sound and happy, and that everything — everything will go according to wish. I could not have the good fortune of speaking with Her Serene Highness the Princess when I travelled hither — I had myself announced 3 times but was not once received. It appears as though the writing of the [one] to Prittwitz — as though I were hated by the King and by the public — had caused a sensation in Breslau as well; this, however, signifies nothing, for I

find myself here in Berlin and Pot:[uncertain: Potsdam] so very well in soul. Our good Queen was truly quite ill, but is improving, to the joy of all upright vassals. The Monarch is well, and labours not only with boundless activity for the welfare of the State but also drills the troops in such a way that one's heart laughs in one's body. On Sunday I saw the old Guard, also in white gaiters and their grenadier caps — upon the faces of the whole battalion one saw so plainly the most memorable joy to me, and by God a tear trembled in my eye, for that is the old brave Köckritz's work — the officers now receive their place at table and feel themselves as happy as the others. The worthy men K. Z[astrow]. and R. are in good health and live in the greatest harmony. Of war there is no thought, and in this matter too the Monarch already shows before all the world his rock-firm steadfastness and whole manner of thinking.

This very moment I receive from Glenck news in the Prusimski lawsuit matter which is precisely not good, for I [see] that the jurists mean to cheat us. Since I still have in my hands all the blank forms most graciously entrusted to me, I would at once submit a writing here to the Grand Chancellor and beg for a prolongation of the term. Time gained is all gained — In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's Berlin, the 30th April 1799. most faithful servant v. Triebenfeld
