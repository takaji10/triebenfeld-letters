--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld hat Graf Schlabrendorff befriedigt; die Zinsen betrugen 11085 Rthl, die Quittung lautet auf den Fürsten, eine Deckung für ihn selbst fehlt. Bei der Fürstin wurde er dreimal nicht vorgelassen; er erwähnt ein Schreiben von Prittwitz. Glenck meldet Ungünstiges im Prusimski-Prozess; Triebenfeld würde mit den ihm anvertrauten Blanquets beim Großkanzler um Verlängerung des Termins bitten. Dazu Hofnachrichten: die Königin erholt sich, an Krieg ist nicht zu denken.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld has satisfied Count Schlabrendorff; the interest amounted to 11,085 Rthl, the receipt is made out in the Prince's name, and there is no cover for Triebenfeld himself. He says he was refused admission to the Princess three times; he mentions a letter from Prittwitz. Glenck reports unfavourable news in the Prusimski lawsuit; Triebenfeld says he would use the blank forms entrusted to him to ask the Grand Chancellor for an extension of the deadline. He adds court news: the Queen is recovering, and war is out of the question.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
105

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

I have satisfied Count Schlabrendorff and have the receipt in hand — the sum of the interest came to 11,085 Rthl, and the receipt is made out precisely to Your Most Serene Highness — I, however, now have not a syllable towards the covering of my [illegible] in hand, nor do I ask anything on that score, for I am too certain that Heaven will bring Your Most Serene Highness back quite well and happy, and that everything — everything will go according to wish.

Her Serene Highness the Princess I was not able to have the good fortune of speaking with when I travelled here — I had myself announced 3 times, but was not once received. It appears as though the letter of the [uncertain: Prittwitz] — as though I were hated by the King and by the public — has also made a sensation in Wrocław — this, however, is of no consequence, for I

find myself here in Berlin and Pot[sdam] in very good spirits indeed — Our good Queen was truly quite ill, but is [uncertain: mending], to the joy of all upright vassals — The Monarch is well, and not only labours with boundless activity for the good of the State but also drills in such a way as makes one's heart laugh within one's body — on Sunday I saw the old Guard, in white gaiters too and their grenadier caps — on the faces of the whole battalion one saw the [uncertain: most memorable] joy, and by God a tear trembled in my eye, for that is the work of the brave old Köck[ritz] — the officers now receive their place at table and feel themselves as happy as the others. The worthy men K[öckritz], Z[astrow] and R. are in good health and live in the greatest harmony.

Of war there is no thought, and the Monarch already shows in this matter, before the whole world, his rock-firm steadfastness and all cast of mind

This very moment I receive news from Glenck in the Prusimski lawsuit which is just not good, for I [uncertain: note] that the jurists mean to cheat us. Since I still have in my hands all the blank sheets most graciously entrusted to me, I would straightway give a letter here to the Grand Chancellor and ask for an extension of the deadline — time gained, all gained — In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's Berlin, the 30th of April 1799.

most faithful servant

v. Triebenfeld
