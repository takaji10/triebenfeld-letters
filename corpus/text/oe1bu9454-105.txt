--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that Count Schlabrendorff has been satisfied, the interest amounting to 11085 Rthl, with the receipt made out to the Prince, while nothing has come towards covering Triebenfeld's own 10 [thousand]. Notes three failed attempts to be received by the Princess and the sensation caused in Breslau by the writing to Prittwitz; describes the Queen's illness and recovery, the King's activity and troop drills in Berlin, and the absence of any prospect of war. Bad news from Glenck in the Prusimski lawsuit prompts him to use the blank forms entrusted to him to petition the Grand Chancellor for an extension of the term.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord!

I have satisfied Count Schlabrendorff and have the receipt in hand - the sum of the interest amounted to 11085 Rthl; the receipt is made out directly to Your Most Serene Highness - I, however, now have not a syllable towards the covering of my

10. "in hand; nor do I ask after it, for I am too certain that the [uncertain: zimel] helps Your Most Serene Highness back quite healthy and happy, and that everything - everything will go according to wish.

The Princess's Serene Highness I could not have the good fortune of speaking with when I travelled hither - I had myself announced 3 times but was not once received. It appears as though the letter of von Prittwitz - to the effect that I am hated by the King and by the public - has also caused a sensation in Breslau - this, however, signifies nothing, for I

find myself here in Berlin and Pot:[Potsdam?] so very well in my soul. Our good Queen was truly quite ill, but is recovering, to the joy of all upright vassals. The Monarch is well, and not only labours with boundless activity for the good of the State but also drills the troops in such a way that one's heart laughs in one's body. On Sunday I saw the old Guard, in white gaiters too, and their grenadier caps - upon the faces of the whole battalion one saw truly the most memorable joy to me, and by God a tear trembled in my eye, for that is the old brave [uncertain: Köch:] work - the officers now receive their place at table and feel themselves as happy as the others. The worthy men K. Z[astrow]. and R. are in good health and live in the greatest harmony.

Of war there is no thought to be had, and the Monarch already shows in this matter, before the whole world, his rock-firm steadfastness and all his way of thinking

This very moment I receive from Glenck news in the Prusimski lawsuit matter which is precisely not good, for I [see] that the jurists mean to cheat us. Since I still have all the blank signed forms most graciously entrusted to me in my hands, I would at once submit a letter here to the Grand Chancellor and beg for an extension of the term. Time gained is everything gained — In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's, Berlin, the 30th of April 1799, most faithful servant

v. Triebenfeld
