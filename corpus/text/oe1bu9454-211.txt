--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports that Herr von Knobelsdorff has visited and inspected the locality to his satisfaction, and that Triebenfeld is now bringing the papers into order so the whole may be surveyed at a glance, despite great confusion and obstinate people. Expects the Trąbczyn lawsuit to end well, the term having run since the 24th, with the Tribunal's decree perhaps a fortnight off; a win would end all distress. Praises Knobelsdorff's honesty and asks to be left sole conduct of the business.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! Herr von Knobelsdorff was here and has satisfied himself as to the locality. It [uncertain: pleased/took shape for] him; I am therefore endeavouring now to bring everything into order, so as to arrange it all in such a way that one can survey the matter at a glance. The confusion is meanwhile very great, the people obstinate, who are heard from the Hawich; often I could go mad. With God I hope to settle everything. As to the lawsuit concerning Trąbczyn, it will come to a good end; the term has already stood since the 24th, the matter is still not concluded, and it may easily be another 14 days before the decree is read out by the Tribunal. If I win here, then all distress shall have an end. H. v Knobelsdorff I hold to be an exceedingly upright [text lost] man, who states his holdings only in accordance with the truth, and this shall be done on this side as well (if Your Serene Highness leave the conduct of it to me alone) to fetch — until the last breath I remain Your Serene Highness's
Klein Öhringen, the 29th of November 1810
most faithful servant
v Triebenfeld
