--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Ein Executions Decret aus Posen verlangte für die Prusimska an 22000 Rthl; in Kalisz gewann er mit Dokumenten von Rapacki Zeit. Bauern hatten angewiesene Gründe verlassen, alte besät und deutsche Colonisten verjagt; er ließ Wortführer mit dem Kantschu schlagen und die Gründe abgrenzen. Die Pachtverträge von Honrichs, v. Zglinski und dem Pächter von Kopojno wurden aufgehoben oder auf ein Jahr verkürzt; Vorwerke sollen zu 600 Rthl je Hufe dismembriert werden. Glenck rede mit einem Pasquill Käufer ab.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
An execution decree from Poznań demanded some 22000 Rthl for the Prusimska; at Kalisz he says he gained time with documents from Rapacki. Peasants had left the plots assigned to them, sown the old ones and driven off German settlers; he had the ringleaders beaten with the whip and the plots marked out. The leases of Honrichs, v. Zglinski and the tenant of Kopojno were cancelled or shortened to one year; demesne farms are to be broken up at 600 Rthl per Hufe. He says Glenck is talking buyers out of it with a libel.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord, After I have lain here 5 weeks with the present gall [fever] and have barely escaped death, my first concern is to put Your Serene Highness au fait of everything, how your affairs stand here. I must therefore, if with the weakness still pressing upon me I shall be able to recall it all, begin from Berlin. There I departed a few days before Midsummer with the assurance that I should receive so much out of the justice fund as to be able to pay Oppenheimer with 110000 Rthl. and to get the mortgage book clear. Then it was intended to pay Lichnowski the 40000 Rthl. still coming to him against jura cessa; further to hand over to Grotowski the remainder, 20000 Rthl., against the adding of his instruments.

Finally, to satisfy Haugt on the very same footing on account of his claims of 18000 Rthl., and Secher Thoss[?] with 8000 Rthl.,

likewise the Prusimska with 8000 Rthl.

To Zerboni, Eysenhardt and Seehausen, the old Mr Radzinski was to be paid with 24000 Rthl., because the villain does not render his rent but only keeps making chicanery.

It was still my trouble to raise, for the Countess Schlabrendorff, [uncertain: also] for that creature the Prusimska, 8000 Rthl., together 33000 Rthl., and in particular to gather something for Your Serene Highness. Also indeed to render the interest to the Invalids' Fund on the 50000 Rthl. promptly, so that credit might be won; this must together amount to 311000 Rthl.,

and more debts you have not here, for all the others are discharged and stand under the 110000 Rthl. In the meantime, Your Serene Highness has on the other hand, as the table states, 184000 Rthl. to take in by degrees, and there remain owing on the estates only the 40263 Rthl. which would yield annually = 127000 Rthl.

So, as said overleaf, [I went] from Berlin before Midsummer to Pszczew, to whom the Rader of the Jews, Meyer Bernhard, had brought 3 or 4 suits against Your Serene Highness, because nothing was yet known of the Royal approval concerning the Pszczew matter.

In Pszczew I communicated the news to the people, settled accounts with them, likewise paid Echaust old court costs, and after these the burgesses and residents still owed Your Serene Highness 4000 Rthl., which they must pay at Michaelmas. Meanwhile I learned, to my horror, that an execution decree had been sent from Poznań to Trąbczyn, by which (since Metzer had noticed nothing) the Prusimska was to be paid 10000 Rthl. dowry together with interest and costs, thus together some 22000 Rthl.

I therefore hastened as best I could to Kalisz and proved, by the documents procured for me by Rapacki, that Prusimski had already paid that dowry in part; by this I did indeed gain time, but the matter hangs, and a year and a day may pass again before I bring it clear. Only this is certain, that even those 16000 Rthl. entered in the tables may no longer be paid in full, let alone the 22000 Rthl. From Kalisz I went to Trąbczyn, where I now confidently believed I should find some 30000 Rthl. But, o heaven, how startled I was when instead of money I found a thousand confusions, knaveries, and consequently empty [coffers] — the earlier[?] ones all declared themselves insolvent — made out great accounts on account of the exorbitant deliveries, carting and quartering under the Russians and Prussians, so that the rents of 2 years would still have gone out on it.

The old peasants who had been put on quit-rents had ceased, and had, despite all judicial contracts, approvals by the Chamber and by His Royal Majesty himself, again abandoned the grounds assigned to them, and — certainly with the connivance of the tenants, so that these knaves might believe themselves entitled to make a so much the greater account — had again

appropriated their former grounds and sown them, and thereby almost dissolved the demesne farms — indeed in the end they even drove off the German colonists already settled, so that I could count neither on the stipulated entry moneys from these, still less on quit-rents. On top of all this Schand had kept silent and gave himself out for dead of gangrene when I wished to call him to account. I asked this Massette for advice as to what was to be done in the matter — his answer was to let the affair take its course, and to report it to the government, to throw a lawsuit round the necks of the tenants and the subjects.

Question: Have you proceeded so well in the Floricke, Rybbeck and Szczucki matter?

Ans.: No, but it cannot be done otherwise. Question: How long do you believe the lawsuit can last before the matter comes clear?

Ans.: Well, some 2 to 3 years.

Question: Does the gentleman understand Russian?

He: No!

I: Well then, I will show it to him. With me one spits such lazy scribblers in the face, lets him run, and acts alone.

Upon this I went to Trąbczyn and summoned the tenant Honrichs before me with his contract.

To the question whether he could pay the rent sum now falling due, of 2000 Rthl., and also at once render the remainder of the 800 Rthl., he answered — No. He believed, he added, that he owed nothing either, for he had over 5000 Rthl. in claims

and I believe he is a scoundrel. He pays, or within an hour he lies with his affair over the Warthe. By this first tone the fellow was put out of countenance[?] and began to plead.

Upon this I gave way, that if he at once surrendered the lease contract, let the claims fall, and paid the 2800 Rthl. at Christmas on forfeit of his whole property, he might still sit this year; yet it must be free to me to dismember as much, and when I pleased, of the demesne farm of Trąbczyn. To this he agreed; the contract for 6 years was annulled and in its place a one-year contract drawn up, with the condition that as soon as he had reaped I might dismember the demesne farm; whereupon Honrichs undertook himself to recruit purchasers who should give 600 Rthl. per Hufe / consequently 2 to 300 Rthl. more than hitherto /, in return for which I conceded to him that the grounds which should be [uncertain: Dummabredt] need not be improved again, and also promised him to place him in Your Serene Highness's service with 400 Rthl. yearly salary, if he would faithfully effect this and in particular help bring the peasants and the other tenants to reason. After this operation I at once had all the residents belonging to Trąbczyn summoned together. When the peasants saw that the tenant stood ready on my side already with the whip — then their courage too fell away, and within an hour this work was so ordered to Your Serene Highness's advantage that neither devil nor saint can have anything against it in eternity. The peasants soon paid their half-yearly quit-rent and vowed to lay down the other half at Christmas. Now I went with Honrichs to

Swiątniki. Here sat von Zglinski with his two gaping fools of sons.

There the peasants had sown a great part of the manorial fields and, in league with the tenant, had so confused everything that I myself shuddered to penetrate into this [uncertain: Labirath]. Under the contract von Zglinski was to pay 1500 Rthl. The contract ran for 12 years, and contained among its clauses the provision that, should the tenant not pay on the term day, the contract should at once be null and void, and the tenant bound to vacate immediately, but to pursue any claims he might have by course of law. Now I asked this Sarmatian whether he would pay; his answer was — I shall not only not pay, but shall sit here so long until I know my claims, which amount to 5700 Rthl., satisfied; and moreover I insist that the grounds which the peasants hold in possession, but which were previously assigned to me, be handed over to me at once.

At this [uncertain: summons] all the peasants also appeared and declared that they would not give up the grounds, even if the Prince and the King himself should come; they were born and bred on these grounds and would never vacate them. I represented to the fellows that they had settled everything judicially, that the Prince had made them a gift of their freedom, had besides given them the inventory gratis, that their quit-rent was insignificant compared with that of the colonists, that even the houses and buildings had been given them into the bargain,

whereas the colonist must first laboriously [clear] all the soil and build everything at his own cost. — Meanwhile my talking availed nothing; it was much more like oil to put out a fire. Since, however, I had marked the spokesmen and had at the same time perceived which of them drew near to von Zglinski and his sons, I had one of these step forward, threw him down and had 50 strokes of the whip counted out to him — the fellow did not endure them quite to the end, for Honrichs flogs like the best Cossack general — he begged for mercy; notwithstanding, I let it go on, but at the 43rd the beast fainted. I had him doused with water, and when he came to himself had him led aside and the next one brought forward. I threw him down too; Honrichs with every stroke tore breeches and hide apart — this took effect, and he cried for mercy and promised to report. I had them stop, and to the question what he had to say he then declared that all three communities had been set on by Zglinski, Niedźwiecki and the people of Oleśnica. The [uncertain: Schatz] and several others, particularly the people of Kopojno who had likewise come in beneath, confirmed this — they confessed themselves deserving of punishment, but begged imploringly for forgiveness and declared themselves content with the grounds assigned to them. In the meantime, however, they had to complain of the justiciary: he had always turned them away when they wished to lay a complaint before him or to ask for orders of conduct; on the other hand he had always stood together with the noblemen and lived in merriment with them. After this coup of mine, which succeeded, I at once sent to Schanden and had him bidden come to me immediately, so that in his presence the peasants' ground might be properly bounded, and separated for eternity from the manorial and colonists' grounds.

He appeared in the shape of the most crushed wretch, and the work too was begun and happily completed; only Schenck remained halfway

and substituted a certain Ogrodowitz. Perhaps the scoundrel will croak and death free us from this hypocrite.

After this too had been happily settled, I went at the tenant again by the throat, who, since he was unmasked, [uncertain: took it to heart] and [saw] the complete failure of his plans; he therefore asked for pardon, which I could not give; so he surrendered the contract and withdrew in apparent [fashion] — I had his grain reaped and sold for 260 Rthl., and [I am] now [offering] the demesne farm, which consists of 30 Hufen of land and meadow, the Hufe at 600 Rthl. entry money and 15 Rthl. yearly quit-rent — now it comes to this, that Honrichs soon gets purchasers for it, for through the tenant's infamy nothing has been lost here either, but much gained. After this the tenant of Kopojno had to step forward; he, warned by those proceedings, soon asked for fair weather — his accounts likewise came to some 6000 Rthl. He brought them down, however, to 1125, which I had to accept, since the district councillor had all but flayed the people; but in return he too had to cancel the contract and grant it only for 1 year, for which he must pay 2500 Rthl. at Christmas, and against which I may also at once have it dismembered. The peasants of Stoki, who had been incited by that gang of rogues from Oleśnica and Wrąbczyn, had likewise to lie down at the mark, after Honrichs, because they had secretly pulled apart the boundary mounds, but had luckily been caught by them, had counted out 30 strokes of the whip to each. Now on this side everything is in order, and the Germans are gradually coming in again.

The old malicious Tietz too, who has leased Drzewce, promises to render the rent; only the fellow will bring 600 Rthl. into deduction as the 2 years' pension which Your Serene Highness is said to have assured him with 300 Rthl. for his lifetime.

I did not know what there is in this; may Your Serene Highness deign to instruct me upon it. In the records here nothing is to be found except the accompanying certified copies, which are taken from Tietz's original pieces, and according to these the old [uncertain: Inachten] has nothing to demand.

All these affairs I had concluded within the space of 6 days, and I was at pains to have the Germans, who had some 40000 Rthl. to pay, sought out together in order to obtain money to pay the interest, to satisfy the [uncertain: Schluß Arendorffen], and to gather something for Your Serene Highness. But this did not succeed — many of the old ones did indeed show themselves, but always vanished again. Newer purchasers were to come, but always drew back timorously again. At this failure of my plans I so vexed and grieved myself that of a sudden a violent gall fever came upon me, which has compelled me to keep my bed now these 5 weeks. Only these 2 days is it improving, and I can now think and work more again. Meanwhile everything still looks very motley, and prospers only so far as the long time in bed, violent headache and the great weakness permit.

[uncertain: Weigens] I shall, as soon as I am certainly back, and if possible have appeased the Schlabrendorff woman, go again to Berlin — for here it might take too long before I bring together anything appreciable. The notorious Glenck, who has hatched a true conspiracy against me and Your Serene Highness, is, as I have just learned, and what came out during my illness, talking all purchasers away with his gang — he has been staying here already over 6 or 7 weeks — he has joined himself with the three rogues of Oleśnica, with a certain Kramplich, with Flöricke, Rybbeck, with a certain Bergemann and more such rabble, in order to frustrate all my plans.

To attain his purpose he has made a lampoon upon me and given such to people who wish to buy, who have taken it with them as far as the Mark, and thereby at the same time frightened back others who wished to come here

and settle themselves. His clerk by the name of Meyer, who lives here on the Wrąbczyn village bailiwick, has fallen out with him and during my illness reported him to Schmid. The latter, perhaps to ingratiate himself again, sent me the lampoon yesterday evening.

The content is as stupid and tasteless as the fellow himself is; only the rascal has attained his purpose of hindering my plans and harming the good cause. He thinks I shall tire and go off, and then he would have to come back because nobody would be there.

The wretch is offering his estate for sale and means to dismember it himself; he always draws the people who wish to come and buy here aside to himself, talks them away from here as aforesaid, and when in the end he can give no judicial contracts, against which the peasants of Wrąbczyn always protest, then the people go back and think they would only be cheated in Your Serene Highness's estates. I am urged to denounce the fellow to the fiscal authority, or at least to throw an action for defamation round his neck — I shall not do it — the villain has harmed Your Serene Highness more than me. I therefore submit this to Your Serene Highness's judgement. For the rest, deign, most gracious Lord, to tell me whether the yearly quit-rents ought not to be demanded of the fellow. He has not yet given a single kreuzer, but assures everyone that he settles accounts on that score with Your Serene Highness alone.

The outstanding quit-rents are said to amount already to over 400 Rthl., and even this is a motive for throwing the fellow out

and taking the village bailiwick back in again, particularly if his own clerk will prove that he has for some years constantly appropriated to himself all the [grass] from the Wrąbczyn meadows, amounting to several hundred Morgen, and has even taken quit-rents from the komorniks living there.

The bailiwick itself is fully paid for with 4000 Rthl.; the 300 Morgen would indeed be worth nearly as much, only these cannot be assured to him, for there are peasants' meadows lying in them at every turn, and then a costly lawsuit is unavoidable — the wretch has nastily misled Your Serene Highness in the granting of the 300 Morgen — the rogue has forgotten that he sold within the Wrąbczyn boundary some 10 Hufen to 4 Kashubians for a trifle and took the money; so, if the cursed contract with the peasants is to be kept, nothing remains. I greatly fear that Your Serene Highness will yet get a costly lawsuit out of this [uncertain: Schwedenstrei-], for it was just now said that Glenck has sold the bailiwick to the [uncertain: Schulweische] Giese in Oleśnica.

Now the fellow has so nastily prompted you that you sell him the 300 Morgen for 300 Rthl. and allow him to dismember — if Giese buys, then he will at once go judicially against Your Serene Highness — my advice at the time was good, to give the fellow 4000 and let him run.

With the Oleśnica fellows the [uncertain: man] has brought Your Serene Highness into such great misfortune, and as they say he is supposed to have secretly given the [uncertain: Bessinn] testimonies by which they will win the lawsuit. For this man's sake the matter here is most disagreeable to me

As concerns the revenues and the sums which Your Serene Highness has to receive from the estates here now and in future, you will perceive these from the accompanying table. I believe you can, when all this shall have been settled, be very well content with it.

Since, for the rest, the lazy Schenck means, as he says, to croak, the councillor of war von H[e?]llen will be so good as to come here from Kalisz once every 4 weeks and look over everything that happens here, without his demanding a Groschen. Also, since Schenck now wishes to have himself brought at once to an abbot at Poznań, I shall immediately hand over the cash-box to Honrichs and Cabanis; these two shall not be permitted to do anything one without the other, and there is therefore nothing to be feared. I think too to be here again about Michaelmas, when many have now promised to pay. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Serene Highness's. very ill —
