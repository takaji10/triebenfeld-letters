--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports on a hearing due at Trąbczyn against the Olesnicers, who protested with the district councillor to avoid paying 10,000 Rthl. Recommends closing with von Otocki, who offers 10,000 Rthl entry money and 1,500 Rthl yearly for Szetlewek in hereditary lease, plus drink-selling rights of both estates at 4,000 Rthl, and further woodland. Urges annulling Honrichs's contract in favour of Meierowitz, suing Szczucki and Ceizewski, paying old Lichnowski 1,390 Rthl, and sending plate to bribe the Chamber President.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

Your Most Serene Highness's gracious letter I have received by express courier, and yesterday had the answer delivered to the Olesnicers.

Tomorrow a hearing in this matter is set at Trąbczyn, and I hoped to see the business finalised shortly.

The rogues say that they are reluctant to litigate against Your Serene Highness, and yet the fellows, in league with the district councillor, lodged protestations here nine months ago already and wished to set the matter up in such a way that they would never have to pay over the 10000 Rthl – now, when they see that their case stands poorly, now they come crawling and try their luck by another route. From Berlin I look for the answer at any hour now – God grant that it turn out as wished.

Szetlewek the von Otocki wishes to take in hereditary lease – He offers already 10000 Rthl of entry money and 1500 Rthl of annual canon. Glenck valued the estate at 40000 Rthl, but he promised 800 Morgen of woodland from Trąbczyn – if I can get it without these 40000 Rthl, then I close with him, for in that case Your Serene Highness profits 16000 Rthl against the valuation. Further, Otocki wishes to lease the propination rights of both estates. He offers 3000 Rthl annually, but below 4000 Rthl I shall not let them go, and then only for 3 years. The proposition is valued, together with the estates, at 2700 Rthl; so here too an annual 1300 Rthl are gained. The rent he pays half-yearly in advance, and that

now shortly, which would then amount to a capital of 12000 Rthl and come in by Easter. If matters succeed with the Olesnicers, then a further 11000 Rthl come in shortly. I have charged the aforesaid with buying out the old peasants in Swincin. There are 13 of them, each of whom holds 2 1/3 Hufe; if he can dispose of the fellows with 2000 Rthl, then I shall raise money here, pay them out at once, and sell the already built-up holdings for about 1600 Rthl; in this way a further 9000 Rthl in cash would be gained here, and ready money might also be looked for here sooner than from the old subjects.

With Honrichs things look bad – the fellow does not pay — He says it stands in the contract that Your Serene Highness would have to apply for the concession on his behalf. But it does not stand there that Your Highness guaranteed to procure it for him. I shall write to the department on this account and see to it that it is refused – and then the contract must be quickly annulled. As soon as this is done, the same Meierowitz will buy it, who at the outset was so eager to take it, but was turned away by Glenck. Meierowitz gives 13000 in cash, and a canon of a thousand-odd annually. In this way Your Serene Highness would have profited 10000 Rthl on the estate. A wretched 100 ducats have deprived Your Serene Highness of this profit and plunged you into vexation. Perhaps God will grant that I redress it. Grimm too wishes to buy the estate of Witów in the name of his son-in-law; but he offers no more than

2000 Rthl, which he promises to pay out at once in cash – in which case admittedly only a bare 4000 Rthl would be saved as against the present wretched sale – I therefore refuse Grimm nothing as yet, so that he will help me swiftly to get Honrichs out of the way – afterwards I shall examine the matter and keep the best. May Your Serene Highness only be pleased to command Glenck that he absolutely bring in without delay the points of agreement concluded by von Köckritz, together with the Grimm supplement and the power of attorney given in the matter at [uncertain: Wohlch], so that action can quickly be taken against Honrichs. Szczucki and Ceizewski must be sued jointly today as the warrantors in the Przespolewski matter, for these fellows took the money to themselves under the decree of the Polish Tribunal and by receipt — they have had the use of it for 18 years already; they must either surrender it at once, or set Your Serene Highness free of the obligation. It is infamous of such a scoundrel that he lets the matter lie so long until one of the mounted enforcement officers is upon his neck. It would be necessary that one were always sitting here and driving such fellows in the ribs, so that he does not lie idle and in the end let himself be quite outflanked – With the old and the young Lichnowski I have everything settled; the latter has much respect for me and runs after me like the billy-goat after the lump of salt. The old one begs imploringly for 1390 Rthl — he writes that he is in the greatest distress – then he will wait as long as it takes until he can be paid further. For this, counsel must now be found.

If Your Serene Highness has so much there, then send it to the old fellow soon; I shall procure it again at once, be it assured upon my honour. The Chamber President here is a spiteful beast, and moreover ravenously hungry – His only company is with the chamber [official] Honrichs Peter, his wife, and the councillor of war Zerboni – the latter is his shadow and steers him as he pleases. These are again bad prospects – if only I could soon send into the fellow's house the washbasin and the three boxes from [uncertain: Rigs], which must however be so plated as though they were from [uncertain: Branzenis], then he would at once be very pliable. May Your Serene Highness only see to it that I get these pieces here soon. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's Otocki also wishes to take a further 20 to 30 Hufe of woodland, arable and meadow in hereditary lease, and to pay as the colonists do — many are already applying again for plots, and when I [illegible] with it, the same will be quickly disposed of. The bilious fever has seized me again; I can hardly drag my legs after me again — the vexation is too manifold. Kalisz, the 1st of March

1805 most humble and faithful servant v Triebenfeld
