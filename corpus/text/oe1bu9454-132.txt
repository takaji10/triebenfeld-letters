Mein Theuerſter Freund

kehren. Zum rasend werden war es für mich so lange hier zu sezzi¬
auf H. Glenck zu warten, und am Ende nichts auszurichten.
Das Resultat ist immer daß die Menschen in Trüben fischen und

10000 Rthl Jahrl haften - dies betrüge a 5 pro Cent
gerechnet ein Capital von 200000 Rthl Da man aber dem H. G.
versichert hat, daß das Ordens Capital zufrieden ist wann

ob 4 pro Cent reine Zinsen von seine Capitalien hat, so könte man
den Werth der Güter auf 250000 Rthl annehmen.
Nun aber würde der Fürst mit 180000 Rthl da er Geld braucht sehr zu
frieden sein, folgl. gewandts Cap. 70000 Rthl baar bey diesen Kauf¬
Sie könte noch dies ausreden, daß das Capitel oder die Ordens
Cammer, wan noch mehr heraus gebracht würde, eine der Sache
angemeßene tantieme geben müste.
In Fall der Herr Director Stubenrauch dies eingehen will
so habe Sie die Gewogenheit mir eine Estaffette zu schicken
ich eile den gleich wieder nach Berlin schließe ab und leiste
die Evection.
Hätte ich die Sache ehender so gekannt so wäre alles schon aufs

H. Gleich hat stets auf ein Kauf Geld von 500 Rthl pro Hube

nehmen, und der Werth der Güter ward vermindert

beherzigen Sie dies und suchen den würdigen guten Man den Cammer

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Departing for Breslau after a fruitless wait on Mr Glenck, Triebenfeld complains that speculators are profiting barbarously at the Prince's expense, citing 30,000 marks made at Betsche and worse expected at Kaemen. He urges instead that Chamber Director Stubenrauch buy the estates and find the money by Midsummer, offering to guarantee 10,000 Rthl yearly, or 12,000 if no entry money is taken; values of 180,000-300,000 Rthl are canvassed. He wants an express courier, and caution with the Cabinet Councillor over Koltzig and indemnification.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
My dearest Friend. I am just now on the point of stepping into the carriage and returning to Breslau. It was enough to drive me mad, to sit here so long [uncertain: sezziauf] waiting upon Mr. Glenck, and in the end to accomplish nothing. The result is always that people fish in troubled waters and mean to profit barbarously at the Prince's expense. At Betsche the people have in so short a time gained thirty thousand [uncertain: rung] without having to put themselves to the least trouble. The subjects merely buy themselves free and give 30000 mk more than the purchasers gave. Meanwhile the latter profit by this sum. Spoiled by this business, at Kaemen still more than this was to be gained at once, and yet God knows what screws are then to be applied to the Prince — in this I cannot intervene, and therefore I depart with nothing accomplished. — Now, my dear Friend, see to it only that [uncertain: dh Ere] Director Stubenrauch buys the estates and provides the money. — I will stand surety to the man for a clear and perpetual [uncertain: Revencie] of 10000 Rthl yearly, if I may dismember the estates — this would amount, reckoned at 5 per cent, to a capital of 200000 Rthl. But since Mr. G. has been assured that the Order's capital is content if

it has 4 per cent clear interest on its capitals, then one might set the value of the estates at 250000 Rthl. Now, however, the Prince would be very well content with 180000 Rthl, since he needs money, consequently [uncertain: gewandts] capital of 70000 Rthl in cash upon this purchase. You could further urge that the Chapter, or the Order's Chamber, should, if still more were brought out, give a percentage commensurate with the matter.

In case Director Stubenrauch will agree to this, have the kindness to send me an express courier; I shall hasten back to Berlin at once, conclude the matter and perform the [uncertain: Evection].

Had I known the business thus earlier, all would already be settled, and much could have been gained by it. But as it is, one always learns everything too late.

Mr. Gleich has always reckoned upon a purchase-money of 500 Rthl per Hufe, which the people were to pay — in this respect he [text lost] to assume a light hereditary rent of 10 to 12 [uncertain: st] per [uncertain: Hure], and the value of the estates was diminished; but if I now take no entry-money, the subject willingly pays 50 Rthl per Hufe yearly. By this means a far higher value now comes out, so that I am still able, out of the old rents and for beer and brandy excise, an everlasting

subsisting [uncertain: Vereine] of 12000 Rthl to bring out, and consequently a value at 4 per cent of 300000 Rthl; according to which the Order's Chamber would profit by 120000.

Take this to heart, and seek to move that worthy good man, Chamber Director Stubenrauch, to make the purchase and to provide the moneys for it by Midsummer Day of this year. In the matter of the Koltzig estates, or in the business of indemnification, seek to effect the best; but do go cautiously with the Cabinet Councillor, for at present I fear that man extraordinarily.

With the most faithful heart and the greatest attachment I am unceasingly

Your

Frankfurt an der Oder

the 23 May 1804.

most faithfully devoted servant v Triebenfeld
