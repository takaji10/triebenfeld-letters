--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports the failure of prolonged waiting on Mr. Glenck and departure for Breslau with the errand unaccomplished, complaining that speculators fish in troubled waters at the Prince's expense: at Betsche the subjects bought their freedom for 30000 mk above the purchasers' price, and worse is expected at Kaemen. Urges that Director Stubenrauch buy the estates by Midsummer, offering guarantees of 10000 Rthl yearly revenue through dismemberment, valuations of 250000–300000 Rthl against the Prince's acceptance of 180000, a tantième from the Order's Chamber, and caution with the Cabinets-Rath over the Koltzig indemnification.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
My dearest Friend, I am at this very moment about to step into the carriage and return to Breslau. It was enough to drive me mad, to sit here so long [uncertain: sezziauf] waiting upon Mr. Glenck, and in the end to accomplish nothing. The result is always that people fish in troubled waters and mean to make barbarous gains at the Prince's expense. At Betsche the people have, in so short a time, gained thirty thousand [uncertain: rung] without having needed to trouble themselves in the least. The subjects merely buy their freedom and give 30000 mk more than the purchasers gave. Meanwhile the latter profit by this sum. Spoiled by this business, at Kaemen there was to be gained still more than the like of this, and yet God knows what screws are then to be applied to the Prince — this I cannot intervene in, and I therefore depart with my errand unaccomplished. — Now, my dear Friend, do you only see to it that Mr. Director Stubenrauch buys the estates and provides the money. — I will stand surety to the man for a clear and perpetual revenue of 10000 Rthl yearly, if I may dismember the estates — this would amount, reckoned at 5 per cent, to a capital of 200000 Rthl. But since Mr. G. has been assured that the Order's capital is content if

it has 4 per cent clear interest from its capitals, then one might set the value of the estates at 250000 Rthl. But now the Prince, since he has need of money, would be very well content with 180000 Rthl, consequently the Chapter would gain 70000 Rthl in cash upon this purchase. You might further urge this, that the Chapter, or the Order's Chamber, should, if still more were to be brought out, give a tantième proportionate to the matter. In case Mr. Director Stubenrauch will enter into this, have the kindness to send me an express courier; I shall then hasten back at once to Berlin, conclude, and furnish the [uncertain: Evection — probably Execution, i.e. carry out the execution of the contract]. Had I known the business sooner as it stands, all would already have been in order, and much might have been gained thereby. But as it is, one always learns everything too late. Mr. Gleich has all along reckoned upon a purchase money of 500 Rthl per Hube, which the people were to pay — In this regard [text lost] he to assume a light hereditary quit-rent of 10 to 12 st per Hufe, and the value of the estates was diminished; but if I now take no entry money, then the subject willingly pays 50 Rthl per Hufe yearly. By this means there now comes out an enormously high value, so that I, besides the old interest and for beer and brandy excise, [have] a perpetual

[uncertain: standing] [uncertain: association] of 12000 Rthl, and consequently a value at 4 pl. of 300000 Rthl, am in a position to bring out; according to which the Order's Chamber would profit by 120000. Take this to heart, and seek to move the worthy good man, the Chamber Director Stubenrauch, to make the purchase and to provide the moneys for it by Midsummer of this year. In the matter of the Koltzig estates, or in the indemnification business, seek to effect the best that may be; but do go warily with the Herr Cabinets-Rath, for at present I fear this man extraordinarily. With the most faithful heart and the greatest attachment I am unceasingly your Franckfurth a d h., the 23 May 1804. most faithfully devoted servant v Triebenfeld
