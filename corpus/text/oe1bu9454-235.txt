--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports the messenger's two-day delay and his dispatch by another route, then that old Weigel has arrived, is willing to conclude the lease contract and, on Triebenfeld's advice, is not averse to buying the Zagorow estates, assuming the debts and paying a further 200000 Rthl to be set off against Koschentin. Weigel would bid as creditor at a compulsory auction, hoping to secure Koschentin for under 600000 Rthl and cede it back; secrecy from Stössel and others is urged, and the papers are wanted with all haste.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

My messenger was held up 2 days and, being a blockhead, returns only today. I am therefore sending the Demme beast by another route, and report to Your Serene Highness that old Weigel arrived here with me already today. He has not only the best will to conclude the lease contract, but he has disclosed to me that, following my advice, he is not disinclined to purchase the Zagorowo estates. He assumes the debts and pays a further 200000 Rthl on top, which he wishes to set off against Koschentia. In this matter, however, he advises that the business be kept secret, and that Koschentin be put up for compulsory auction. He offers as creditor and hopes to obtain it below the assessed value; what is thereby lost to us, Stössel

lose, who would have cheated Your Serene Highness more than too grossly. He engages himself to issue a written undertaking that he will cede Koschentin, just as he should acquire it, to Your Serene Highness, or to whomever Your Serene Highness should command. He believes that Your Serene Highness would obtain it for still under 600000 Rthl – only no person must suspect that Your Serene Highness or Prince August wished to keep Koschentin. May Your Serene Highness write to me upon this in secret Your declaration of will, for the old man does what I advise him. As to the papers, may Your Serene Highness deign to send them with the utmost haste, for I know of no expedient for meeting the costs; the instrument, however, must be drawn up just as I have prescribed it, otherwise it must come to nothing. In deepest reverence I remain

Your Serene Highness's

Blizanow

the 18th of July 1812

most humble and obedient

v. Triebenfeld
