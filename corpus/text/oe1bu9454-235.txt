--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports the messenger's two-day delay and his dispatch by another route, then that old Weigel has arrived, is willing to conclude the lease contract and, on Triebenfeld's advice, is not averse to buying the Zagorow estates, assuming the debts and paying a further 200000 Rthl to be set off against Koschentin. Weigel would bid as creditor at a compulsory auction, hoping to secure Koschentin for under 600000 Rthl and cede it back; secrecy from Stössel and others is urged, and the papers are wanted with all haste.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! My messenger was held up for 2 days and, being a blockhead, is only returning today. I am therefore sending the stupid beast by another route, and report to Your Serene Highness that old Weigel arrived here with me already today. He has not only the best will to conclude the lease contract, but he has disclosed to me that, following my advice, he is not averse to purchasing the Zagorow estates. He assumes the debts and pays a further 200000 Rthl on top, which he wishes to set off against Koschentin. In this, however, he advises that the matter be kept secret, and that Koschentin be put up for compulsory auction. He bids for it as a creditor and hopes to obtain it below the assessed value; what is thereby lost, we lose to Stössel

lose, which would have cheated Your Serene Highness far too grievously. He engages himself to issue a written undertaking that he will cede Koschentin, just as he should acquire it, to Your Serene Highness, or to whomever You should command. He believes that You would obtain it for under 600000 Rthl — only no soul must suspect that Your Serene Highness or Prince August wished to keep Koschentin. Let Your Serene Highness write me Your intention on this in secret, for the old man does what I advise him. As for the papers, may Your Serene Highness be pleased to send them with the greatest haste, for I know no way of meeting the costs; the instrument, however, must be drawn up just as I have prescribed, otherwise it must come to nothing. Reverentially I remain Your Serene Highness's
Blizanow, the 18th of July 1812
most humble and obedient
v Triebenfeld
