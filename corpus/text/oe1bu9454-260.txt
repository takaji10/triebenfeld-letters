--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports having forwarded, via Neisse and express courier, the Resolution obtained from Prince State Chancellor Hardenberg and the order to the Brieg Superior Provincial Court, and urges the Prince to have Court Councillor Hahn frame the declaration on seven heads against Cosmar and the elder Princes — fictitious settlement sums, the stolen power of attorney, the son's revolt — so the settlements and codicil conflicting with the 1799 Senate instrument be annulled and the 112000 capital treated per the Triebenfeld plans; asks for a copy, blank forms, a letter of thanks to Hardenberg, and 300 Rthl in gold, only 118 f remaining.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Yesterday I had the honour of transmitting to Your Most Serene Highness the Resolution obtained only from His Serene Highness the Prince State Chancellor, together with the order to the Brieg Superior Provincial Court, by an opportunity as far as Neisse and from there by express courier, for this reason: that Your Serene Highness should be quickly convinced of the fortunate turn of your affair, and at the same time be forewarned to be active there in your affairs. I count now [uncertain: sahr] upon the fairness and insight of the good Court Councillor Hahn, who must now in a letter give the matter such a turn that the report of the Superior Provincial Court and Your Serene Highness's declaration come out everywhere in conformity with my submissions. On this everything now depends — killing mockery and shame, or honour, fortune and peace of life. [text lost] be shown, that 5 with Your Serene Highness were everywhere induced to accede to those shameful settlements, codicils etc. in your period of suffering, when you were yourself deprived of your liberty; 2. that the sums stated in the settlement in favour of the elder Princes are for the greatest part fictitious, and are entered there in order to defraud the State and other creditors — that for this very reason 3rdly [they undertook] to pay the so-called Curian sum alone, and to set your former consort ex neom, in spite of her signature; 4. that all these matters were forged solely by Cosmar and the Princes in Berlin, and sent to you for accession under glittering promises or threats; 5. that Cosmar so conducted the matter that they wished to push you, gracious Lord, out of possession of the entailed estates and to put your son in their place, so that all creditors should be set back; 6. that Cosmar, once this had succeeded with him, contrived to obtain a power of attorney from the son by stealth, and brought this weak man to the point of himself rebelling against his father, curtailing him of every revenue and having arrest laid upon everything; 7. that when you perceived your error, you wished to address the matter and to come to terms with your creditors, or had agreed with them — but that Cosmar hindered this too; and then the proofs, as they lie there, and as the good Hahn knows all this better than I. The conclusion

must go to this, that the settlements and the codicil, which conflict with the Senate instrument of 1799, be immediately annulled; that the estates be left to them to manage as soon as possible; that they be permitted to come to terms with the creditors, or that the association already concluded may stand — and finally that, for the discharge of the principal debt, a capital of 112000 was only advanced, against which, according to the Triebenfeld plans, is to be discharged within the stated period etc. etc. Finally, let the good Hahn box Cosmar on every ground, on account of his omission of the rendering of accounts, on account of his [uncertain: instanien] administrations. You have, most gracious Prince, truth and the greatest right on your side; only make no compliments and spare nobody. Your son has not dealt honestly with you. Out of stupid [uncertain: Gritz] he has entered into a plot with your mortal enemies against you. It is an exceeding grace if the possessions in [uncertain: Mich] are left to him, all the more since much good may be hoped for there, of which, however, I unfortunately [have] a profiting [uncertain: Bau], because I have very much attacked the Württemberg Majesty — I may never with Gärtner set foot on Württemberg soil — it is a good fortune that we may not make too great [uncertain: bewegen]. For the rest, yesterday, in the highest fury, when I received the letter from my daughter, I took no Copy of the Prince Hard[enberg] Resolution before the same was delivered to me at the very moment. It is now advised that I should remain here so long, until the matter comes back from Brieg — also until things are clear regarding Poland — Let Your Serene Highness command that a Copy be sent here to me by the first post. Also send me a piece of 4 blank forms, so that I may give some letters of thanks to deserving persons. To the noble Prince Hardenberg write for the [uncertain: eeligsten] yourself and thank him meanwhile [uncertain: Toni] whereby to commend to him the further course of your affair — deign to send me this very soon [illegible], so that it may be delivered and everything further attended to. With the most perfect esteem I persist, [as] Your Serene Highness's
Vienna, the 10th of December 1814.
wholly obedient servant
v Triebenfeld
