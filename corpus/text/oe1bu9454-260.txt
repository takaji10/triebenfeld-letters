--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld hat die Resolution Hardenbergs und die Verfügung an das Brieger Oberlandesgericht per Estaffette übersandt und setzt auf Hoffrath Hahn. Er gibt die Punkte der fürstlichen Erklärung vor: in Haft zu Transacten und Codizill induziert, Summen größtenteils fingiert, alles von Cosmar betrieben, der den Sohn gegen den Vater aufgebracht habe. Ziel: Aufhebung der Transacte, Überlassung der Güter, Vorschuss von 112/m Rthl. Er bittet um Abschrift, Blanquets und einen Dankbrief an Hardenberg. Nachschrift: bittet dringend um 300 Rthl in Gold.

260

To Your Most Serene Highness I had yesterday the honour of forwarding the resolution which has only now been obtained from His Serene Highness the Prince State Chancellor, together with the order to the Brzeg Higher Provincial Court, by an opportunity as far as Nysa and from there by express courier, and this for the reason that Your Serene Highness should be quickly convinced of the fortunate turn of your affair, and at the same time be forewarned to be active there in your concerns. I now count very much upon the [uncertain: community] and discernment of the good Court Councillor Hahn; he must now, at Brzeg, give the matter such a turn that the report of the Higher Provincial Court and Your Serene Highness's declaration come out throughout in conformity with my submissions. On this everything now depends — deadly mockery and shame, or honour, fortune and peace of life. [text lost] be set forth — that firstly Your Serene Highness was throughout induced into the accession to that shameful settlement, codicil etc. during your period of suffering, when you were yourself deprived of your liberty — secondly that the sums stated in the settlement in favour of the old Prince are for the greater part fictitious, and are entered for the purpose of defrauding the State and other creditors — that for this very reason, thirdly, [it was] undertaken to pay the so-called Courland sum alone, and to place your former consort out of the legal bond, notwithstanding her signature

4. that all these things were forged by Cosmar and the Prince in Berlin alone, and were sent to you for accession under glittering promises or threats.

5. that Cosmar so directed the matter that they meant to press you, most gracious Lord, out of the possession of the entailed estates and to put your son in their place, so that all creditors might be set back —

6. that Cosmar, when this had succeeded for him, knew how to obtain a power of attorney from the son by stealth and brought this weak person to the point of rising up against his own father, of cutting off every revenue from him and having an attachment laid upon everything

7. that when you perceived your error, you wished, or had agreed, to redirect the matter and to come to terms with your creditors — but that Cosmar hindered this too — and then the proofs as they lie there, and as the good Hahn knows all this better than I do — The conclusion

must go to this, that the settlement and codicil, which conflict with the [illegible] deed of 1799, be forthwith annulled; that the estates be left to you to farm as soon as may be; that you be permitted to come to terms with the creditors, or that the association already concluded may stand — for finally, that for the discharge of the principal debt a capital of 112/m Rthl might be advanced to you — which, according to the Triebenfeld plan, is to be discharged within the named period, and so on and so forth — Lastly, let the good Hahn [have] Cosmar thoroughly at the root, on account of his omitted rendering of accounts, on account of his infamous administrations, sheets — You have, most gracious Prince, truth and the greatest right on your side — only make no compliments and spare nobody either. Your son has not dealt honestly with you. Out of stupid avarice he has entered into a plot with your mortal enemies against you. It is exceeding grace if you charge the possessions to me, the more so as much good may be hoped for there — of which, however, I can now unhappily profit, because I have attacked the Württemberg Majesty very severely — I may never with Gärtner set foot on Württemberg soil — It is a piece of luck that we need not make too great detours — For the rest, yesterday, in the highest rage, when I received the letter from my daughter, I took no copy of the resolution of Prince Hardenberg, inasmuch as the same was delivered to me in all that moment. It is now advised that I should remain here so long, until the matter comes back from Brzeg — and also until there is clarity concerning Poland — Your Serene Highness will therefore command that a copy be sent to me here by the first post — Also send me a piece, 4 blank sheets, along with it, so that I may give some letters of thanks to deserving persons — To the noble Prince Hardenberg write [uncertain: in your own hand] yourself and thank him in the most touching tone, in doing which you commend to him the further course of your affair — be pleased to send this here soon, so that it may be handed over and everything further attended to — With the most perfect respect I persist,

Your Serene Highness's

Vienna, the 10th of December

1814.

most obedient servant
