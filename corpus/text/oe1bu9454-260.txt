--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports having sent on, by express courier, a resolution obtained from Prince State Chancellor Hardenberg with an order to the Brieg Superior Provincial Court, and coaches the Prince on the line Court Councillor Hahn must take there: that the settlements and codicil extorted during the Prince's confinement, with sums largely fictitious and forged by Cosmar and the older Princes to defraud creditors, be annulled, the estates restored and creditors compounded with. Urges a thank-you letter to Hardenberg, a copy of the resolution, and 300 Rthl in gold, his funds being down to 118 florins.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Yesterday I had the honour of forwarding to Your Most Serene Highness the resolution which has only now been obtained from His Serene Highness the Prince State Chancellor, together with the order to the Brieg Superior Provincial Court, by an opportunity as far as Neisse and from there on by express courier, for this reason: that Your Serene Highness should be swiftly convinced of the happy turn of your affair, and at the same time be forewarned, so as to be active there in your concerns. I count now entirely upon the [uncertain: fairness] and discernment of the good Court Councillor Hahn; he must now in Brieg give the affair such a turn that the report of the Superior Provincial Court and Your Serene Highness's declaration turn out in every respect conformable with my submissions. On this at present everything depends — deadly mockery and shame, or honour, fortune and peace of life. [text lost] be done, that 5 with Your Serene Highness were everywhere induced to accede to those shameful settlements, codicils etc. in your period of suffering, when you yourself were deprived of your liberty,

2. that the sums stated in the settlement in favour of the older Princes are for the greater part fictitious, and are entered there in order to defraud the State and other creditors — that it is precisely for this reason, 3rdly, that the so-called Courland sum alone was undertaken to be paid, and your former consort ex neom, in spite of her signature, to be set down;

4. that all these matters were forged solely by Cosmar and the Princes in Berlin, and were sent to you for accession under glittering promises or threats.

5. that Cosmar so directed the matter that they wished to press you, most gracious Lord, out of the possession of the entailed estates and to put your son in their place, so that all creditors should be set back;

6. that Cosmar, once this had succeeded with him, knew how to obtain a power of attorney from the son by stealth, and brought this weak person so far that he himself rose up against the father, curtailed him in every revenue, and had a distraint laid upon everything;

7. that when you perceived your error, you wished to redress the matter and to come to terms with your creditors, or had agreed with them — but that Cosmar hindered this too, and then the proofs as they lie there, and as the good Hahn knows all this better than I do.

The conclusion

must go to this, that the settlements and the codicil, which are at variance with the Senate instrument of 1799, be immediately annulled; that the estates be left to them to manage as soon as may be; that they be permitted to come to terms with the creditors, or that the association already concluded may stand — for finally, that for the extinguishing of the principal debt a capital of 112000 only was advanced, [uncertain: against] which, according to the Triebenfeld plans, it is to be extinguished within the said time etc. etc.

Finally, let the good Hahn [have] the whole ground against Cosmar, on account of his omitted rendering of accounts, on account of his [uncertain: instanien] administrations — box them. You have, most gracious Prince, truth and the greatest right on your side; only make no compliments and spare nobody. Your son has not dealt honestly with you. Out of stupid [uncertain: Gritz] he has entered into a plot with your mortal enemies against you. It is an exceeding grace that he is left the possessions in [illegible], the more so as much good is to be hoped for there, from which, however, I unhappily [have] a profiting [illegible], because I have also greatly offended the Württemberg Majesty — I may never with Gärtner set foot on Württemberg soil — it is a good fortune that we are not permitted to make too great stirrings.

For the rest, yesterday, in the highest fury, when I received the letter from my daughter, I took no copy of the Prince Hard[enberg] resolution, [uncertain: since] it was delivered to me at that very moment. It is now advised that I should remain here so long until the matter comes back from Brieg — and also until things are clear with regard to Poland — Your Serene Highness will therefore order that a copy be sent here by the first post. Also send me a set of 4 blank forms, so that I may give some letters of thanks to deserving persons. To the noble Prince Hardenberg write for the [uncertain: most blessed] yourself and thank him meanwhile [uncertain: Toni], wherein commend to him the further course of your affair — this be pleased to send me very [uncertain: soon], so that it may be handed over and everything further attended to. With the most perfect respect I remain

Your Serene Highness's

Vienna, the 10th of December

1814.

entirely obedient servant
