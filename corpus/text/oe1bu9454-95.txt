dem Augen als er mir diesen Auftrag machte,
Er selbst will nicht eher schreiben als bis alles geschehen
ist — der G. C. R. versichert gerade hin, daß er hier
contre Coeur handeln müße – der Souvenin aber
wolte es haben — Jedes ist dies doch ein redlicher
Mann, den durch ihm habe ich die Breslauschen
denkart kennen lernen – Bey Gott da giebts

Bösewichter, unter denen der von oben an steht
wan man Ewr Durchlaucht von Einteilung des Depos¬
tements und von sonstigen wißen von Ihrer Sache
gesagt hatt, sind die gröbsten Lügen- Vielmehr

Plan mit 6 gemacht, daß ein hartes refus erfolgen
solte - jedes mündlich darüber den Aufschlus¬

befolgen – den ich denke stets – kan mich mancher

der wahrhaft gute Monarch
schaden

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Colonel v. Köckritz sends his compliments and word that the Quiet exchange business stands exceedingly well and may be counted as concluded, though he will not write until all is done. The G. C. R. acts contre Coeur at the Sovereign's insistence. Reports of a division of the Department are dismissed as gross lies, a hard oral refusal having been planned before departure; warns against H. v V.'s misrepresentations to the indisposed King, and dismisses the Secular business and peace rumours.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Lord! The worthy Colonel v. Köckritz has charged me to commend him most cordially to Your Most Serene Highness, and to assure the same, most [uncertain: submissively], that the Quiet exchange business stood exceedingly well, indeed might be regarded as concluded in the best manner — Joy shone from the noble man's eyes as he gave me this commission; he himself will not write until everything has been done — the G. C. R. assures me outright that he must act here contre Coeur — but the Sovereign would have it so — Each of them is nonetheless an honest man, for through him I have come to know the Breslau cast of mind — By God, there are

villains, among whom the one stands foremost from the top down. When Your Serene Highness has been told of a division of the Department and of other knowledge of your affair, these are the grossest lies — Rather, at the last moment, before the departure was made, the plan was laid with 6 that a hard refusal should follow — each of it orally, the disclosure thereof; now may Your Serene Highness be pleased to follow my counsel — for I think always: though many a wretched creature can do me no good at once, yet he can [do me] harm; the truly good Monarch is indisposed, and therefore there is no audience at present; otherwise I should be at the edge at once, but for this I must still wait — meanwhile it [uncertain: troubles] me greatly, because I tremble constantly whether H. v V. does not once again twist the matter and see to it that other

false representations occur — the King means well by all; if one lays before him so much erroneous stuff, then the gentleman must in the end act against his own interest. The Secular business is in the wide field, and seems to me to be a piece of bread which the French throw out, after which one is to snap and be pastured. People believe in a peace with the Emperor and the French. These are, however, only ideas. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's — Before this comes, Your Serene Highness will receive an express courier, and then may the same write at once concerning the leave of absence — Berlin, the 29th of Xbr 1798, faithful most humble and obedient servant v Triebenfeld
