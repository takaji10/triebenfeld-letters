--- SUMMARY (English) ---
News from the Congress: the King of Württemberg has restored the Estates but summoned a diet for 15 March forbidding any reduction of taxes raised by 50 per cent, prompting protests to the Congress, and Prince August is to be warned to concede nothing. Reports on the King of Saxony, Hesse-Darmstadt, East Friesland and Swedish Pomerania. Hopes the 112,000 Rthl advance will now be approved, and has had the deeds and process records copied, certified and translated for the Trąbczyn claim.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

The King of Württemberg, seeing limits set to his power, has restored the Estates, which is known to Your Serene Highness, and soon thereafter he has proclaimed a kind of diet, at which he has summoned all the Estates to appear on the 15th of March. Here he means to come to an agreement with his vassals, Estates and subjects — but in advance he commands and lays down as law that there may be no talk whatever of any reduction of the taxes, which have been raised by 50 per cent, but rather that both the new and the old dues must remain for everlasting time as they stand today and be paid under the strictest execution.

His [uncertain: Royal] Majesty has himself ordered this made known to the venerable old [uncertain: five] noble houses with the All-Highest wording "We Friedrich King of [uncertain: Württemberg]" command you etc.

made known and had it so served upon them. Notice of these sentiments, raised by this act of grace, has however been given to the Congress here — but nothing has been answered thereupon. The multitude of the Estates, among whom is particularly the old Metternich, have at once protested against it here before the Congress, and at the same time enquired whether they were bound to present themselves before the King of Württemberg on the 15th of March. As yet no answer has been given to this, and particularly to the latter question. Yet I have had a hint from high quarters that it does no harm if the Estates appear. — Only none of them should let himself be bound to us and outwitted. — A general constitution is being drafted, and in it all the rules between lords, vassals and subjects will be determined and fixed, in respect of the dues, and even of tolls etc., and with this everyone will be able to be content. B: and Göschel now wish that I should give Your Serene Highness the speediest news of it, so that you may most urgently warn Prince August that he, out of deep devotion for the uncle, concede nothing there to his own detriment and to the damage of the whole, or let himself be outwitted — of this last request I hereby acquit myself, whereby I also remark that Mr Goschel takes much pains, yet in five months has not once so much as spoken to the Prince Chancellor himself; a Mr von Gärtner who pressed himself forward has lost much and lies

in fact very ill these past couple of weeks. For the rest, the King of Saxony may now in fact go to Brünn, whither he has been summoned; he may not take his journey by way of Dresden, but must go through Silesia. At Brünn he is to declare that he is content and accepts the patch of ground assigned to him with the highest gratitude, and will rest satisfied therewith for evermore — provisionally this has already been done. Only the matter is to become valid, when he is no longer called a prisoner as at best, if he repeats the declaration in freedom. The Grand Duke of Hesse-Darmstadt, who made a frightful noise on account of Westphalia and even took it into his head to threaten, has been served quite honourably and civilly, and exceedingly vigorously. He now observes a humanly decent silence and now says yes willingly to everything. Thus this matter too is settled. East Friesland the English have obtained, just as it was secretly promised them before the war for their accession, and our Lord has all Swedish Pomerania for it, and thus these matters too have been disposed of to satisfaction.

Switzerland, Italy and Spain still at present excite the attention of the combined powers, but this does not so much interest our court, and a lasting peace may be reckoned on with the highest confidence.

Since I now know for certain, though only these 3 days past, that Your Serene Highness's affairs, and particularly the granting of the advance of 112000 Rthl, have been delayed so long purely on that account, I now hope for the speediest and best conclusion; for the Prince Chancellor, who must obtain the King's approval for the advance, could not come forward with it before all fear of a new war had been removed — this is now said to be the case, and now I hope for the end.

The Trąbczyn vindication will cause me much care — from the Prince Chancellor I have as yet no resolution upon it; I do not know what I should begin, for without his orders I may nowhere make a start. — A commission has been appointed in which General Amstetten presides; gladly would I have

already submitted the matters there — but I have been advised against it; so much was intimated, to hold the records and the means of proof in readiness. I have therefore had the deeds copied and certified. Likewise the process records have already been translated into German and signed by the sworn interpreter — they may be [uncertain: made out] for me and submitted wherever it shall be ordered.

In respect of Your Serene Highness's military affairs, the Prince Chancellor will doubtless take occasion to speak with His Majesty, when he makes his report to the King concerning the advance of 112000 dl.

The Congress will still last, so it is said, 6 weeks. All the settlements of accounts are to be entirely concluded here.

The Finance Minister von Bülow lies gravely ill of a burning fever; there reigns here a sickness from which few remain spared. This is what I have been able to write to Your Serene Highness today from my sickbed. Respectfully I remain as

Your Serene Highness's

Vienna

the 25th of February 1815

most obedient servant

v Triebenfeld
