--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports from Vienna that the King of Würtemberg has restored the Estates and summoned a Diet for 15 March while forbidding any reduction of the taxes raised by 50 per cent; the Estates, including old Metternich, have protested to the Congress. B: and Göschel urge that Prince August be warned to concede nothing; notes Göschel's lack of access to the Prince Chancellor and v. Gärtner's illness. Also the King of Saxony's summons to Brünn, Heßendarmstadt's compliance, East Friesland to England and Swedish Pomerania to Prussia, hopes that the 112000 Rthl advance will now be approved, anxiety over the Trąbczyner vindication and General Amstetten's commission, and von Bülow's illness.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

The King of Würtemberg, seeing limits set to his power, has [uncertain: reinstated] the Estates, which is known to Your Serene Highness, and soon thereafter he has proclaimed a kind of diet, in which he summoned all the Estates to appear on the 15th of March. Here he means to come to terms with his vassals, Estates and subjects — beforehand, however, he commands and lays down as law that there can be no question of any reduction of the taxes, which have been raised by 50 per cent., and that both the new and the old dues must remain for all perpetuity as they now stand, and be paid under the strictest execution.

His Würtemberg Majesty has himself made this known to the venerable old [uncertain: five] princely houses with the most exalted expression "We Friedrich, King of Wp, command you etc."

made known and had it thus served upon them. Of these sentiments raised in such homage, however, notice has been given to the Congress here — but nothing has been answered thereto. The great number of the Estates, among whom is in particular the elder Metternich, have at once protested against it here before the Congress, and at the same time enquired whether they were bound to present themselves before the King of Würtemberg on the 15th of March. As yet no answer has been given thereto, and particularly to the latter question. — Nevertheless I have had the hint from a high hand that it does no harm if the Estates appear. — Only none of them should let himself be bound to us and outwitted. — A general constitution is being drafted, and in it all the norms between lords, vassals and subjects with respect to the dues, even the tolls etc., will be determined and fixed, and with this everyone will be able to be content. B: and Göschel now wish that I should give Your Serene Highness the speediest news of it, so that You may warn Prince August with the utmost haste, that he there, out of deep devotion to the uncle, concede nothing to his own detriment and to the harm of the whole, nor let himself be outwitted — of this latter request I hereby discharge myself, whereby I also observe that Mr. Goschel gives himself much trouble, yet in five months has not spoken even once with the Prince Chancellor himself; a certain Mr. von Gärtner who pressed himself forward has lost much and lies

in fact very ill these past couple of weeks. For the rest, the King of Saxony may now in fact go to Brünn, whither he has been summoned; he may not take his journey by way of Dresden, but must go through Silesia. There at Brünn he is to declare that he is content and accepts with the highest gratitude the patch assigned to him, and will thereby set himself at rest for ever — provisionally this has already taken place. — Only the matter is to become more valid, when he is no longer called a prisoner in the best case, if he repeats the declaration in freedom. The Grand Duke of Hessen-Darmstadt, who made a frightful noise on account of Westphalia and even took it upon himself to threaten, is being quite honourably and most vigorously served. He now observes a humanly decent silence and now assents willingly to everything. And so this matter too is settled. East Friesland the English have obtained, as was secretly promised them before the war for their accession, and our Lord has all Swedish Pomerania for it, and thus these matters too are disposed of to satisfaction.

Switzerland, Italy and Spain still now excite the attention of the combined powers, but this probably does not so much interest our Court, and a lasting peace may be reckoned upon with the highest confidence.

Since I now know for certain, though only these 3 days past, that Your Serene Highness's affairs, particularly the granting of the advance of 112000 Rthl, have been delayed so long purely on that account, I now hope for the soonest and best end of it; for the Prince Chancellor, who must obtain the King's approval for the advance, could not come forward with it any sooner than when all fear of a new war was removed — this is now said to be the case, and now I hope for the end.

The Trąbczyn vindication will cause me much anxiety — from the Prince Chancellor I have as yet no resolution upon it; I do not know what I am to begin, for without his orders I may nowhere make a start. — A commission has been appointed in which General Amstetten presides; gladly would I have

already submitted the matters there — but I have been advised against it. Thus much was intimated, that the records and means of proof are to be held in readiness. I have therefore had the deeds copied and authenticated. Likewise the process records are already translated into German and signed by the sworn interpreter — they may be [uncertain: bought out] from me and submitted wherever it shall be ordered.

With respect to Your Serene Highness's military affairs, the Prince Chancellor will doubtless take the opportunity to speak with His Majesty when he lays before the King the report concerning the advance of 112000 dl.

The Congress will, as they say, last another 6 weeks — all the settlements of accounts are to be entirely concluded here.

Your Finance Minister von Bülow lies gravely ill with a burning fever; there prevails here a sickness from which few remain spared. This is what I have been able to write to Your Serene Highness today from my sick-bed. Respectfully I remain as

Your Serene Highness's

Vienna

the 25th of February 1815

entirely obedient servant

v Triebenfeld
