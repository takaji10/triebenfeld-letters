--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Zur Trąbczyner Vindication hat Triebenfeld noch keine Resolution des Fürsten Kanzler. Bei einer Kommission unter General Amstetten wurde ihm die Einreichung vorerst widerraten. Die Urkunden ließ er abschreiben und vidimieren, die Prozessakten beeidigt ins Deutsche übersetzen. Die Bewilligung des Vorschusses von 112000 Rthl erhofft er bald. Vom Kongress berichtet er über den württembergischen Landtag zum 15. März und den Protest der Stände. Barbe und Göschel bitten, Fürst August zu warnen, nichts einzuräumen.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

The King of Württemberg, seeing limits set to his [uncertain: power], has (re-established the Estates, as is known to Your Serene Highness, and soon thereafter he has proclaimed a sort of Landtag, where he has summoned all the Estates to appear on the 15th of March — Here he wishes to come to an agreement with his vassals, Estates and subjects — But in advance he commands and lays down as law that there can be no question of a reduction of the taxes, which have been raised by 50 per cent, since both the new and the old dues must remain in perpetuity as they stand today and be paid under the strictest enforcement — His Württemberg Majesty has himself ordered this to the venerable old princely houses with the Most High formula "We Friedrich, King of W., etc., to you, etc."

made known and had it so served upon them. Of these sentiments raised [uncertain: Huldwaller] notice was however given to the Congress here — but nothing has been answered thereto — The majority of the Estates, among whom in particular is old Metternich, have at once protested against it here at the Congress, and at the same time enquired whether they were bound to present themselves on the 15th of March before the King of Württemberg. As yet no answer has been given thereto / and particularly as to the latter question / — [uncertain: Jedest] I have had the hint from a high hand that it does no harm if the Estates appear. — Only none is to let himself be bound and outwitted with us — A general constitution is being drafted, and therein all the norms between [illegible], vassals and subjects will be determined and fixed with regard to the dues, even the tolls and so on and so forth — and with this every man will be able to be content — Barbe and Göschel now wish that I should give Your Serene Highness the speediest news of it, so that you may [uncertain: muß das eiligsten] warn Fürst August, that he there out of deep devotion for the uncle concede nothing to his own detriment and to the damage of the whole, nor let himself be outwitted — of this last request I hereby discharge myself, whereby I also remark that Mr von Göschel gives himself much trouble, yet through 5 months has not once even spoken to the Prince Chancellor himself; a Mr von Gärtner [uncertain: dar sich zudrung] has lost much and lies

in fact very ill these couple of weeks. For the rest, the King of Saxony could now in fact go to Brünn, whither he has been summoned; he may not take his journey by way of Dresden, but must go through Silesia. There at Brünn he is to declare that he is content and accepts with the highest gratitude the patch assigned to him, and will thereby be set at rest for ever — provisionally this has already happened — Only the matter is to become more valid, when he is no longer called a prisoner as in Berlin, if he repeats the declaration in freedom — The Grand Duke of Hesse-Darmstadt, who made a frightful noise on account of Westphalia and even took it into his head to threaten — has been quite honestly, civilly and exceedingly vigorously attended to. He now observes a humanly civil silence and now says willingly yes to everything. [illegible]. A. so this matter too is settled — East Frisia the English have obtained, just as it was secretly promised them before the war for their accession, and our Lord has for that the whole of Swedish Pomerania, and thus these matters too are disposed of to satisfaction — Switzerland, Italy and Spain still now excite the attention of the combined powers, but this does not so much interest our court, and a lasting peace is to be reckoned upon with the highest confidence — Since I now know for certain / though only these 3 days / that Your Serene Highness's affairs, particularly the granting of the advance of 112000 rt., have been delayed so long purely for this reason, I now hope for the soonest and best end to it, in that the Prince Chancellor, who must obtain the King's [uncertain: approval] regarding the advance, would not come forward with it before every fear of a new war had been removed — this is now said to be the case, and now I hope for the end — The Trąbczyn Vindication will give me much anxiety — from the Prince Chancellor I still have no resolution upon it, I do not know what I am to begin, for without his orders I must nowhere begin — A commission has been [uncertain: laid down], in which General Amstetten presides; gladly would I have

already submitted the matters there — but I have been advised against it — so much was [uncertain: indicated], that the files and means of proof be held in readiness. I have therefore had the deeds copied and certified — likewise the process files have already been translated into German and signed by the sworn interpreter — they may be [uncertain: bought out] from me and submitted wherever it is ordered — With regard to Your Serene Highness's military affairs, the Prince Chancellor will doubtless take occasion to speak with His Majesty, when he makes his report to the King concerning the advance of 112/m rt.

The Congress will, as they say, last 6 weeks more — all the settlements of accounts are to be entirely concluded here — The Finance Minister von Bülow lies very ill of a burning fever; there reigns here a sickness from which few remain spared — This is what I have been able to write to Your Serene Highness today from my sick-bed. Respectfully I remain as

Your Serene Highness's

Vienna

the 25th of February 1815

most obedient servant

v Triebenfeld
