--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Hahn reports that the death of the mining councillor Bernhardi has obliged him to travel to Upper Silesia to put the affairs of the lordship in order jointly with inspector Kreinig and master of forests Reichmann, both of whom he knows as sound men of business. Koschentin, he warns, is not in the best order and will give trouble. He asks leave to call on Triebenfeld at Trąbczyn before returning to the Empire, having already sought the Prince's permission, and hopes for a reply at Koschentin.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Right Honourable Sir, Highly Honoured Sir Councillor of War and Forests. I hereby report to Your Right Honourable that the death of the mining councillor Bernhardi has brought upon me the orders to travel to Upper Silesia, and to regulate the affairs of this lordship in common with the inspector Kreinig and the master of forests Reichmann. I may hope that matters will go better under the direction of these men, since both of them are known to me,

as upright and judicious men of business. Once I have finished in Koschentin, I shall return to Breslau and from there back into the Empire. To leave Silesia without having paid my respects to Your Right Honourable is something I could not forgive myself. I therefore most obediently beg the same and for permission — to visit you in Trąbczyn and there to pay my attendance. On that account I have also already sought gracious permission from His Serene Highness, which I hope to obtain.

Koschentin will give something to do,

for, as one takes the matter, everything there is not in the best order. Should Your Highly Honoured Self be pleased to gladden me with a few lines at Koßhentin, I should be very happy. Herlich, whom I have with me, commends himself to your grace. I add only the assurance of the most perfect veneration, in which I remain always: Your Right Honourable's, Klein Althammer

the 19th of July 1806.

most humble and obedient servant

A. Hahn
