--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Urges the Prince to raise 500-600 Rthl in travel money so that the writer, Eberhard, can go to Vienna, where the three princesses of the Curian heirs are present in person and a settlement is, he argues, obtainable now or never after the Ministry's repeated refusals. He sets out three obstacles: the heirs' general attorneys have told him not to interfere, he has no money, and he needs passes from the Minister of Justice and the Chancellor of State. Triebenfeld's reply will decide matters; Korb could find the funds.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Copy

Most Serene Prince etc. etc. etc.

I too received by the last post a letter from Mr. Triebenfeld, in which he urgently presses upon my heart that I should come to Vienna.

According to a plan I have thought through, which is built upon the negative resolutions hitherto issued on the part of the Ministry, it seems to me also to be possible to move the Curian heirs, who are just now in person in Vienna, to the acceptance of a settlement, and I myself might perhaps be able to work towards it, if I were there; but, in the first place, in view of the direction issued to me on the part of the general mandatories of the Curian heirs, to abstain from any interference in this matter, I cannot act contrary to it, and cannot appear in Vienna abruptly and without any occasion on the part of the heirs; second, I am wholly wanting in money, and I am in no condition to advance these travelling costs, nor to borrow them anywhere; third, I cannot travel abroad without [uncertain: passes] from the Minister of Justice and the Chancellor of State, which I should first have to apply for.

The second obstacle is now no doubt the most important; if Your Serene Highness is not able to find means, then the misfortune is great, and not to be overlooked, for only now, in Vienna, with the personal presence of the 3 princesses, is a settlement to be had with thanks, or never. Is there then no means whatever at Your Serene Highness's command to raise at least 5 to 600 Rthl? Never yet would a capital have yielded such interest as this one. The return is certainly as incalculable as the damage will be, if Your Highness does not raise these travelling costs; I, by God, am not able to.

The King's departure on the 26th, which I do not believe, since according to other reports the King is said to have extended the rents until the end of April, would do no harm, since the Prince Chancellor of State remains there, and he alone can accomplish everything. I am writing today to Mr. von Triebenfeld, and lay my plan before him for his judgement, in which 2 ways are indicated by which, for me at least, an occasion might be taken to give a motive for my journey to Vienna. Mr. von Triebenfeld

will at once find his bearings, since he knows the terrain exactly, and will know what is to be done, and will also act quickly, so that his answer, shortly to be expected, will decide everything. In the meantime I implore Your Serene Highness to procure the [uncertain: hour-money]. Korb can and certainly will find means.

In deepest reverence I sign myself,

Your Serene Highness's

Brieg

the 12th of March 1815

wholly most humble and obedient

Eberhard
