--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Eberhard bittet den Fürsten dringend, mindestens 5 bis 600 Rthl für eine Reise nach Wien aufzubringen, zu der v. Triebenfeld ihn drängt. Ein Vergleich mit den Curischen Erben sei nur jetzt möglich, solange die 3 Prinzessinnen persönlich in Wien seien. Hindernisse sind eine Weisung der General Mandatarien der Erben, sich nicht einzumischen, sein Geldmangel und nötige Pässe. Einen Plan mit 2 Wegen, die Reise zu begründen, schickt er v. Triebenfeld.

Most Serene Prince

etc. etc. etc.

I too received by the last post a letter from Mr von Triebenfeld, in which he urgently presses upon me to come to Vienna.

According to a plan I have thought through, which is built upon the refusing resolutions hitherto issued on the part of the Ministry, it seems to me likewise to be possible to move the Curish heirs, who are just now in Vienna in person, to accept a settlement, and I myself might perhaps be able to work towards it if I were there; but, in the first place, given the instruction issued to me on the part of the general mandatories of the Curish heirs to abstain from any interference in this matter, I cannot act contrary to it, and cannot appear in Vienna abruptly without any occasion for it on the part of the heirs; secondly, I am entirely without money and am in no condition either to advance the costs of this journey or to borrow them anywhere; thirdly, I cannot travel into Courland without [uncertain: passes] from the Justice [uncertain: Minister] and the State Chancellor, which I should first have to apply for

The second obstacle is now surely the most important; if Your Serene Highness is unable to find means, then the misfortune is great and not to be foreseen, for only now in Vienna, with the personal presence of the 3 princesses, is a settlement [uncertain: conceivable] — or never. Is there then no means at all at Your Serene Highness's command to raise at least 5 to 600 Rthl? Never yet would a capital have yielded such usury as this one. The return is certainly as incalculable as the damage will be, if Your Highness does not raise these travelling costs; I, by God, am not able to.

The King's departure on the 26th, which I do not believe in, since according to other reports the King is said to have extended the lodgings until the end of April, would do no harm, since the Prince State Chancellor remains there, and he alone can effect everything. I am writing today to Mr von Triebenfeld and am laying my plan before him for his judgment, in which 2 ways are set out by which occasion could at least be taken for me to give grounds for my journey to Vienna. Mr von Triebenfeld

will at once find his bearings, since he knows the ground exactly, and will know what is to be done, and will also act quickly, so that his answer, soon to be expected, will decide everything. In the meantime I implore Your Serene Highness to procure the [uncertain: journey] money. Korb kann er he will certainly find means.

In deepest reverence I sign myself.

Your Serene Highness's

Brzeg

the 12th of March 1815

wholly most humble and obedient

Eberhard
