--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Deep snow halts work, but contracts with the old peasants and their dues are now settled before the court, and talks proceed with von Otocki over Szetlewek and the tavern rights. Amelang reports well of the loan; Klewitz will allow the dismemberment if land goes for rent rather than cash. Glenck is accused of obstructing sales with Wollenhaupt and others, and claims totalling 3,011 Rthl from Winnicki's heirs, Chwalowski, Sprengel and Wollenhaupt are disputed.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

The snow lying here a foot deep hinders all my operations; I must sit here to no purpose, unable to do anything. The contracts with the old peasants and the securing of the dues I have these days concluded before the court, and have thereby secured to Your Serene Highness a considerable revenue for all time. Now I am negotiating over Szetlewek and the propination rights with von Otocki, for the old Radremil [uncertain: changes] his mind every moment and would gladly draw the matter out so as to be able to cheat in the end. From Berlin I have good news concerning the loan; Amelang promises to bring everything about, only I am not to hurry him. The Department urgently requires still the refusal of Minister

von Voss and of the Chamber concerning the revision of the assessments. This I am sending on by the first post to Amelang and hope for the best. Concerning the dismemberment, Klewitz has expressed himself that if the lands are not, as at Betsche, sold against ready money but rather for a proportionate rent, the consent ought not thereby to fail.

the latter has for the most part been done, and the consent will, since Herr von Goldbeck has written so handsomely, certainly follow before long. Already I have sent Amelang in all haste a description of the whole affair, so that he may demonstrate everything thoroughly to Herr von Klewitz and show him that the revenues of these estates are not being sold, but are through the dismemberment increased fourfold and set [uncertain: on a footing] for all time. More than grievous it is for me meanwhile that alongside so much work I must still contend against a [uncertain: pack] of villains; and were my attachment to Your Serene Highness not boundless, I should long since have grown weary in my zeal.

Herr P. Glenck makes merry here over my proceedings, makes them ridiculous before the world, and is at pains to thwart everything. Now he has joined himself with Wollenhaupt, engaged the latter as his mandatary, and promised him all the kingdoms of the world. The three Olesnica lads and a certain Meyer stand in this plot as well, and when purchasers come, all these scoundrels take pains to talk the people out of it. Altogether Glenck's conduct is grasping and vindictive, and it is all one to him whether he helps to cheat Your Serene Highness out of some thousands of Rthl, so long as he attains his purpose and hinders everything by means of the plot. Everything is in the greatest confusion and nobody can find either beginning or end –

Thus for example the

Winnicki heirs have now again brought suit for surveying costs. 1300 Rthl

Chwalowski demands

850 Sprengel, which Herr Glenck now attests

61. Wollenhaupt

800. making 3011 Rthl. These people have already received large sums, and notwithstanding there is nothing substantial to be had from Glenck, nor any survey register, but everything must be surveyed and divided anew. In the Winnicki affair, however, nothing will come of it, for I know the curator; Chwalowski will have to content himself with the half; Sprengel must have the money, since Glenck has [uncertain: attested] to him the claim of 61 Rthl for his present exertions. With Wollenhaupt, however, it will look badly. The fellow demands, as said above, 800 Rthl. I proved to him that he has for 2 years worked elsewhere and left Your Serene Highness's works lying wholly untouched; wherefore there went off

600 Rthl. Further, he has for 4 years appropriated to himself 5 Hufen of land and has [uncertain: had the use of] all of these; this amounts yearly to 150 Rthl, consequently for 4 years 600, makes

1200 Rthl. He must therefore still pay out 400 Rthl

To this Wollenhaupt replies: the building councillor Glenck had granted him leave to undertake works outside, wherever he might know of them and be able, for it would surely be an honour to the Prince if his people were sought after.

In respect of the 5 [uncertain: Huren] made use of, he likewise says that

Herr Glenck had given him leave to use them freely and to draw the revenues of Marianton (that is, rent from the inhabitants) and everything else.

The dwelling too the fellow will not vacate, for he says both Herr Glenck and Your Serene Highness had assured him of the dwelling for so long as until he should be otherwise provided for. Schenck hangs his mouth on this account, because he cannot move in, and will presumably give up his engagement and the justiciarship - I must now [uncertain: cajole] this fellow also so far as is possible — should he however grow too defiant, then he may go to the devil, for I have now made myself tolerably [uncertain: informed], and for that post we shall find a hundred for one. Enough to drive one mad it is, though, to have to fight one's way about with all these lads amid works already beyond surveying, and to keep the rogues always in view so that they do not do too much harm. Schenck insists absolutely on 4 [uncertain: Herren] of land and on the liberty of choosing an actuary himself. I shall make a verbal report to Your Serene Highness on the matter.

With boundless fidelity I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's

Trąbczyn

the 31st of March 1805

humble and obedient servant

von Triebenfeld
