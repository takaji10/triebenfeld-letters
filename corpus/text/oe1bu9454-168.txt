--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Deep snow halts operations; contracts with the old peasants and their dues have been secured before the court, while negotiations over Szettleich and the propination rights with v. Otocki drag on. Amelang reports good prospects for the Berlin loan but needs the refusals of Minister v. Voss and the Chamber; Klewitz will consent to the dismemberment if plots go for rent rather than cash. Complains that P. Glenck, Wollenhaupt, the three Olesnice lads and Meyer are deterring purchasers, and itemises claims of 3011 Rthl (Winnicki heirs 1300, Chwalowski 850, Sprengel 61, Wollenhaupt 800), against whom he counter-claims 400 Rthl; Schenck demands 4 Hufen and his own Actuarius.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

The snow lying a foot deep here hinders all my operations; I must sit here to no purpose, without being able to do anything. The contracts with the old peasants and the securing of the dues I have these last days concluded before the court, and thereby secured for Your Serene Highness a considerable revenue [uncertain: Nevenis] for all time. Now I am negotiating on account of Szettleich and the propination rights with von Otocki, for the old Radremiliändert changes his mind every moment and would gladly protract the matter so as to be able to cheat in the end. From Berlin I have good news concerning the loan; Amelan promises to bring everything about, only I am not to hurry him. The Department urgently requires still the refusal of Minister

von Voss and of the Chamber on account of the revision of the assessments. This I send off by the first post to Amelang and hope for the best. Concerning the dismemberment Klewitz has remarked that if the lands were sold not, as at Betsche, for ready money but for a proportionate rent, the consent ought not to fail.[uncertain: des Consens aus nicht entstehen solte]

the latter has for the most part been done, and the consent will, since Mr von Goldbeck has written so handsomely, certainly follow shortly. I have already sent Amelang in all haste a description of the whole affair, so that he may thoroughly demonstrate everything to Mr von Klewitz and show him that the revenues of these estates are not sold away, but by the dismemberment are increased fourfold and set on a lasting footing for all time. It is, however, more than galling to me that, amid so many labours, I must still contend against a [uncertain: Wotte] of villains; and were my attachment to Your Serene Highness not boundless, I should long since have grown weary in my zeal.

Mr P. Glenck here makes sport of my doings, makes them ridiculous before the world, and is at pains to thwart everything. Now he has come to an understanding with Wollenhaupt, has engaged him as his agent and promised him all the kingdoms of the world. The three lads of Olesnica and a certain Meyer are in this plot as well, and when purchasers come, all these scoundrels take pains to talk the people out of it. Altogether Glenck's conduct is grasping [uncertain: haab] and vindictive, and it is all one to him whether he helps to cheat Your Serene Highness of some several thousand Rthl [uncertain: 4lln], if only he attains his purpose and, with the plot, hinders everything. All is in the greatest confusion and no one can find either beginning or end –

Thus, for example, the

Winnicki heirs have now once more sued for surveying costs. 1300 Rthl

Chwalowski demands

850 Sprengel, which Mr Glenck now attests

61. Wollenhaupt

800. which make 3011 Rthl. The people have already received large sums, and notwithstanding this there is nothing substantial from Glenck, nor any surveying register, but everything must be surveyed and apportioned anew. In the Winnicki matter, however, nothing will come of it, for I know the curator; Chwalowski will have to be content with the half; Sprengel must have the money, since Glenck has attested to him the claim of 61 Rthl for his present efforts. But with Wollenhaupt it will look foolish. The fellow demands, as said above, 800 Rthl. I proved to him that for 2 years he had worked elsewhere and had let Your Serene Highness's works lie wholly idle, whence there fell away

600 Rthl. Further, that for 4 years he had appropriated to himself 5 Hufen of land and had used them all, which amounts yearly to 150 Rthl, consequently over 4 years 600, making

1200 Rthl. He must therefore still hand back 400 Rthl

To this Wollenhaupt replies: that the building councillor Glenck had granted him permission to undertake works outside wherever he knew of them and was able, for it would surely be an honour to the Prince if his people were sought after.

With regard to the 5 [uncertain: Huren] he has used, he likewise says that

Mr Glenck had given him permission to use them freely and to draw the revenues from Marianton (that is to say, rent from the inhabitants) and everything.

The fellow will not vacate the dwelling either, for he says that both Mr Glenck and also Your Serene Highness had assured him of the dwelling for so long as until he should be otherwise provided for. Schenck hangs his mouth on that account because he cannot move in, and will presumably give up his engagement and the office of justiciary — I must now also cajole [uncertain: Casuliren] this fellow so far as possible — but if he becomes too defiant, then he may go to the devil; for now I have knocked about enough, and for that post we shall find a hundred for one — enough to drive one mad, but so it is, to have to fight one's way about with all these lads amid labours already beyond surveying, and to keep the rogues always in view so that they do not do too much harm. Schenck insists absolutely on 4 [uncertain: Herren] of land and on the liberty of choosing an assistant clerk himself. I shall make Your Serene Highness a verbal report upon it.

With unbounded fidelity I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's

Trąbczyn

the 31st of March 1805

humble and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld
