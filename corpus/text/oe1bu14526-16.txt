--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Hereditary-lease contract, concluded before the princely Hohenlohe Patrimonial Court at Trąbczyn, by which the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, through his general attorney the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, grants the Althütte demesne farm of 10 Hufen 10 Morgen Magdeburg measure to residents Martin Giering, Wilhelm Daniel Schulz, Gottfried Dümmel, Michel Dümmel, Gottfried Damt and Christoph Schoenfisch for 1,500 Rthl purchase money (450 paid, 1,050 due at St George's 1805) and 225 Rthl yearly canon, with free years for grubbed land, tenth-Groschen transfer fee, tavern obligation, joint liability, and annulment of Kunkel's earlier contract; engrossed 1806.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
The principal copy has been drawn up at 10 rtl 6 g. The princely Hohenlohe Patrimonial Court at Trąbczyn hereby attests that between His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, hereditary lord of the Zagorowo and Trąbczyn estates, on the one part, and the residents Martin Giering, Wilhelm Daniel Schulz, Gottfried Dümmel, Michel Dümmel, Gottfried Damt and Christoph Schoenfisch, on the other part, concerning the demesne farm called Althütte, consisting of 10 Hufen 10 Morgen Magdeburg measure, the following hereditary-lease contract has been concluded and declared to the court protocol:

Actum Trąbczyn, the twentieth of December, One Thousand Eight Hundred and Four. Before the undersigned

Patrimonial Court

there appeared in person,

1., the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld as general attorney of His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, hereditary and judicial lord of the estates here, on the one part

and

2., The residents

1. Martin Giering, Wilhelm Daniel Schulz, Gottfried Dümmel, Michel Dümmel, Gottfried Dams, and Christoph Schoenfisch from the estates here, on the other part.

The appearing parties are personally known to the undersigned, and there is no doubt as to their capacity validly to execute judicial acts.

The same declare, freely and after due deliberation, the following hereditary-lease contract to the protocol.

§. I. Namely, His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, through his general attorney the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, makes over the demesne farm belonging to Trąbczyn called the Alte Hütte, consisting of 10 Hufen 10 Morgen, say

Ten Hufen ten Morgen Magdeburg measure, in arable, meadows, gardens and overgrown marshland and [uncertain: Götzstellen], together with all the farm buildings found on this demesne farm, excepting the new cow byre, which remains to the ground lordship, to the aforementioned residents Martin Giering, Wilhelm Daniel Schulz, Gottfried Dümmel, Michel Dämmel, Gottfried Damt and Christoph Schönfisch, in hereditary lease for perpetual and everlasting time.

Each Hufe contains thirty Morgen and each Morgen one hundred and eighty square rods Magdeburg measure.

§. II. As purchase money the hereditary lessees pay

6. hereditary lessees, one hundred and fifty Reichsthaler per Hufe, making for 10 Hufen 1500 rt, say One Thousand and thereupon Hundred Reichsthaler. Of this they have already paid down 450 rtl., say four hundred and fifty Reichsthaler, and the remaining 1050 rt, say One Thousand and Fifty Reichsthaler, they bind themselves to pay to His Princely Serene Highness at the term of St. George's day 1805, in cash and in Courant of the standard of the treasury.

As yearly hereditary canon the hereditary lessees pay, and that from Martinmas of this year onward, per Hufe 22 rthl 12 gg., say twenty-two Reichsthaler twelve good Groschen, making for 10 Hufen 225 rttl, say two hundred and twenty-five Reichsthaler, in two instalments, namely the half with 112 rt.

12 gg., say One Hundred and Twelve Reichsthaler 12 gg., at Midsummer, and the second half likewise with 112 rt. 12 gg., say One Hundred and Twelve Reichsthaler 12 gg., at Christmas, each time in arrears, in cash and in Courant into the manorial treasury; but they are wholly freed for perpetual time from each and every manorial labour service and delivery in kind to the ground lordship.

§. IV. This stipulated hereditary rent is to be paid from Martinmas of this year onward, yet only for such land and meadow as is already in fact brought under cultivation; for those parts, on the other hand, which must first be made arable by grubbing out, they receive [uncertain: four] free years, which, since they have already had the harvest cut this year,

begin with the past Midsummer of this year and end at Midsummer One Thousand Eight Hundred and Eight, so that for this uncultivated land they shall render the first rent at Christmas One Thousand Eight Hundred and Eight; and the objection that this or that one of them had not yet brought his land under cultivation shall then not be admitted.

§. V. Besides the yearly stipulated hereditary rent, however, the hereditary lessees shall further, for themselves and their descendants, bear in perpetuity each and every royal and other public tax and impost falling upon these ten Hufen, by whatever name they may be [uncertain: called].

§. VI. Since in the first § there are

ten Hufen ten Morgen conveyed to the hereditary lessees in all, it is here noted that they have received the ten Morgen for their building plots free of charge, and need pay neither purchase money nor rent for them.

§. VII. The hereditary lessees are at liberty to make over these ten Hufen so acquired to others in several separate parts, and to agree with these new takers as best they can to their own advantage.

§. VIII. Their establishments must both the present hereditary lessees and the other future takers, for now and for future times,

always effect at their own cost, and they receive for this neither remission of the hereditary rent nor any other building assistance; they may, however, use for this the timber standing on the Hufen received in hereditary lease, as belonging to them in property, all of it without any exception.

§. IX. These Hufen taken in hereditary lease shall, as soon as the weather permits, be duly measured out to the hereditary lessees and delivered into their possession, and at the same time it shall be ascertained how much the area amounts to of the land already now under cultivation, from which

the rent is paid.

§. X. The hereditary lessees renounce entirely, for themselves and their successors, any building timber, firewood, or pasture in the remaining manorial woods and fields, and they may not overstep the boundaries of their holding.

§. XI. The present hereditary lessees, as well as the other future takers, are at liberty to alienate their holdings in any manner they please; but at every change of possession, whether by sale or by exchange, the tenth Groschen of the purchase money or of the exchange value must be paid down to the ground lordship as a transfer fee (Laudemium),

and the same shall in this regard always hold to the new acquirer, whose business it remains not to pay out the purchase money to the seller before the transfer fee has been discharged. Upon inheritance, or upon cession to children and collateral relatives, no transfer fee is paid, and likewise the first takers from the present hereditary lessees are wholly exempt from it.

§. XII. The hereditary lessees are at liberty to redeem the stipulated hereditary rent by payment of the capital sum, that is to say, each of them may pay down to the ground lordship in cash fifty Reichsthaler, one hundred Reichsthaler or one hundred and fifty Reichsthaler, which capital

shall then remain the property of the ground lordship, and five per hundred of yearly rent shall be written off to them on this account; yet it is expressly laid down that this redeemable amount may in no case exceed, per Hufe, the sum of one hundred and fifty Reichsthaler, and that accordingly [uncertain: fifteen Reichsthaler] of yearly rent, say Fifteen Reichsthaler per Hufe, shall remain standing irredeemable for perpetual time.

§. XIII. The hereditary lessees and their descendants remain for ever entirely free of all hereditary subjection, but are without exception subject to the jurisdiction of the manorial lordship (Dominium).

§. XIV. Their drink in beer

and brandy the hereditary lessees and their successors in possession must always take from the manorial brew-house and distillery or tavern, and pay to the ground lordship, as a penalty, one Reichsthaler for every quantity of brandy brought in from outside estates and five Reichsthaler for every barrel of beer.

§. XV. The hereditary lessees hereby expressly renounce, for themselves and their successors, each and every remission in cases of misfortune, whether these arise by fire from heaven, by kindling through evil persons, by crop failure, hail, flood, cattle plague, war and pestilence, or in whatsoever manner, and

no compensation or remission of the hereditary rent shall ever accrue to them on that account.

§. XVI. The costs of this contract, of its confirmation and engrossment, the hereditary lessees undertake to discharge.

§. XVII. So long as the hereditary lessees and their successors in possession fulfil the obligations described above, they shall be protected in their rights by the ground lordship in all things, and defended and held harmless against any man's real claim; but should they remain in arrears with the yearly hereditary canon for longer than two years, they shall thereupon forfeit this hereditary lease, and the ground lordship shall be entitled to let it out elsewhere, in

which case only their improvements need be made good to the hereditary lessees.

§. XVIII. For the correct and prompt payment of the fixed hereditary rent the hereditary lessees and the future takers, as well as their successors in possession, are liable in perpetuity one for all and all for one, thus jointly and severally, in such wise that the ground lordship shall be entitled in this regard to hold either to the one or to the other. Both parts promised to keep the foregoing hereditary-lease contract firmly and inviolably, and renounce all legal evasions that might be raised against it, in particular the objection of an injury suffered above or below the half, of

cunning persuasion, of over-hastiness, of the impossibility of raising the rent out of the property, of matters otherwise agreed than written down or not rightly understood; and it is expressly consented by His Most Serene Highness that the hereditary lessees may, on the ground of this contract, have the title of possession of their holdings entered in their own names.

Further, on the part of His Most Serene Highness, liberty is granted to the hereditary lessees to fetch free of charge the clay for their first establishments generally from the Trąbczyn territory; yet this liberty ceases entirely as soon as they shall have built their buildings. Also

For the rest it is noted that with the said hereditary lessees a contract over these ten Hufen was already drawn up on the 26th of May of the current year by a certain Kunkel, which contract has now been given back by the hereditary lessees and is hereby, by their striking it through, entirely annulled and cancelled, in such wise that it is to be regarded as not concluded, and that neither the one nor the other part shall form or assert any rights or claims out of it.

The contracting parties had nothing further to bring forward; they asked for

the confirmation and engrossment of this contract; and after this protocol had been read out to them word for word, and they had also been instructed that the renunciation of the legal evasions and objections draws after it their complete loss, they once again renounced the same after due deliberation, and have in token of their approval subscribed and signed below.

Read by myself and approved.

Friedrich Ludwig, Prince of Hohenlohe
