--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Hereditary-lease contract, concluded before the princely Hohenlohe Patrimonial Court at Trąbczyn, by which the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, through his general attorney the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, grants the Althütte demesne farm of 10 Hufen 10 Morgen Magdeburg measure to residents Martin Giering, Wilhelm Daniel Schulz, Gottfried Dümmel, Michel Dümmel, Gottfried Damt and Christoph Schoenfisch for 1,500 Rthl purchase money (450 paid, 1,050 due at St George's 1805) and 225 Rthl yearly canon, with free years for grubbed land, tenth-Groschen transfer fee, tavern obligation, joint liability, and annulment of Kunkel's earlier contract; engrossed 1806.

The Princely Hohenlohe Patrimonial Court at Trąbczyn hereby attests that between His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe Ingelfingen, hereditary lord of the Zagorower and Trąbczyner estates, on the one part, and the resident tenants Martin Giering, Wilhelm Daniel Schulz, Gottfried Dümmel, Michel Dümmel, Gottfried Damt and Christoph Schoenfisch, on the other part, concerning the demesne farm called Althütte, consisting of 10 Hufen 10 Morgen Magdeburg measure, the following hereditary-lease contract has been concluded and declared to the court protocol:

Actum Trąbczyn, the twentieth of December One Thousand Eight Hundred and Four. Before the undersigned

Patrimonial Court

there appeared in person,

1st, the Councillor of War and Forests v. Triebenfeld, as general plenipotentiary of His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe Ingelfingen, hereditary and judicial lord of the estates here, on the one part

and

2nd, the resident tenants

1st, Martin Giering, Wilhelm Daniel Schulz, Gottfried Dümmel, Michel Dümmel, Gottfried Dams, and Christoph Schoenfisch from the estates here, on the other part.

The comparents are personally known to the undersigned, and there exists no doubt as to their capacity validly to execute judicial acts.

The same declare, freely and after due deliberation, the following hereditary-lease contract to the protocol.

§ I. Namely, His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe Ingelfingen, through his general plenipotentiary the Councillor of War and Forests v. Triebenfeld, does cede the demesne farm belonging to Trąbczyn called die Alte Hütte, consisting of 10 Hufen 10 Morgen, say

Ten Hufen ten Morgen Magdeburg measure, in arable, meadows, gardens, and overgrown marsh and [uncertain: Götzstellen], together with all the farm buildings found on this demesne farm, excepting the new cowshed, which remains to the ground lordship, to the aforesaid resident tenants Martin Giering, Wilhelm Daniel Schulz, Gottfried Dümmel, Michel Dämmel, Gottfried Damt and Christoph Schönfisch, in hereditary lease for perpetual and everlasting time.

Each Hufe contains thirty Morgen and each Morgen one hundred and eighty square rods Magdeburg measure.

§ II. As purchase-money the hereditary lessees pay

6. hereditary lessees, one hundred and fifty Reichsthaler per Hufe, making for 10 Hufen 1500 rt, say One Thousand [and] Five Hundred Reichsthaler. Of this they have already paid down 450 rtl, say four hundred and fifty Reichsthaler, and the remaining 1050 rt, say One Thousand and Fifty Reichsthaler, they bind themselves to pay off to His Princely Serene Highness in the term of St. George 1805 in cash and in Courant of the standard of the treasury.

As annual hereditary canon the hereditary lessees pay, and that from Martinmas of this year onward, per Hufe 22 rthl 12 gg., say twenty-two Reichsthaler twelve good Groschen, making for 10 Hufen 225 rttl, say two hundred and twenty-five Reichsthaler, in two instalments, namely the half with 112 rt.

12 gg., say One Hundred and Twelve Reichsthaler 12 gg., at St. John's, and the second half likewise with 112 rt. 12 gg., say One Hundred and Twelve Reichsthaler 12 gg., at Christmas, each time postnumerando, in cash and in Courant, into the lordship's treasury; but they are wholly exempted for perpetual time from all and every manorial service and delivery in kind to the ground lordship.

§ IV. This stipulated hereditary rent is to be rendered from Martinmas of this year onward, yet only from such land and meadow as is in fact already brought under cultivation; from those parcels, on the other hand, which must first be made arable by grubbing out, they receive [uncertain: four] free years, which — since they have already had the cutting this year —

begin with the past St. John's of this year and end with St. John's One Thousand Eight Hundred and Eight, so that from this uncultivated land they shall render the first rent at Christmas One Thousand Eight Hundred and Eight; and there shall then be no place for the objection that one or another of them had brought his land under cultivation only later.

§ V. Besides the annually stipulated hereditary rent, the hereditary lessees are further [uncertain: bound], for themselves and their descendants, to bear in perpetuity all and every royal and other public tax and impost falling upon these ten Hufen, by whatever name they may be called.

§ VI. Since in the first § the

hereditary lessees have in all been granted ten Hufen ten Morgen, it is here noted that they have received the ten Morgen for their building-sites free of charge, and need pay neither purchase-money nor rent for them.

§ VII. The hereditary lessees are at liberty to cede these ten Hufen thus acquired to others in several separate parcels, and to agree with these new takers as best they can to their own advantage.

§ VIII. Their establishments must both the present hereditary lessees and the remaining future takers, for now and for future times,

always effect at their own cost, and they receive for this no remission of the hereditary rent nor any building assistance; however, they may employ for this purpose the timber standing on the Hufen received in hereditary lease, as which belongs to them in its entirety and without any exception as their own property.

§ IX. These Hufen taken in hereditary lease shall, as soon as the weather permits, be duly measured out to the hereditary lessees and delivered into possession, and at the same time it shall be ascertained how great the surface content of the land already now under cultivation is, from which

the rent is paid.

§ X. The hereditary lessees, for themselves and their successors, wholly renounce any claim to building timber, firewood, or pasture in the remaining lordly woods and fields, and they may not overstep the boundaries of their holding.

§ XI. The present hereditary lessees, as also the remaining future takers, are at liberty to alienate their holdings in any manner they please; but upon every change of possession, whether by sale or by exchange, the tenth Groschen of the purchase-money or of the exchange-value must be paid to the ground lordship as a laudemium,

and the same shall in this respect always hold to the new acquirer, whose business it remains not to pay out the purchase-money to the seller before the laudemium has been settled. Upon inheritances or cessions to children and collateral relatives no laudemium is paid, and likewise the first takers from the present hereditary lessees are wholly exempt therefrom.

§ XII. The hereditary lessees are at liberty to redeem the stipulated hereditary rent by payment of the capital sum, that is to say, each of them may pay to the ground lordship in cash fifty Reichsthaler, one hundred Reichsthaler, or one hundred and fifty Reichsthaler, which capital

shall then remain the property of the ground lordship, and five per hundred shall be written off from their annual rent on that account; yet it is expressly laid down that this quantum to be paid off may in no case exceed the sum of one hundred and fifty Reichsthaler per Hufe, and that accordingly [uncertain: fifteen] Reichsthaler, say Fifteen Reichsthaler, per Hufe shall remain standing as annual rent, irredeemable for perpetual time.

§ XIII. The hereditary lessees and their descendants remain forever free of all hereditary subjection [uncertain: willingly], yet are without exception subject to the jurisdiction of the Dominium.

§ XIV. Their drink, in beer

and brandy, the hereditary lessees and their successors in possession must always take from the lordly brew- and distilling-house or tavern, and for every quantum of brandy brought in from foreign estates they must pay one Reichsthaler, and for every barrel of beer five Reichsthaler, as a penalty to the ground lordship.

§ XV. The hereditary lessees, for themselves and their successors, hereby expressly renounce all and every remission in cases of misfortune, whether these arise from fire from heaven, arson by evil persons, crop failure, hailstorm, flood, cattle plague, war, or pestilence, and in whatever manner they may arise, and

no compensation or remission of the hereditary rent shall ever accrue to them on that account.

§ XVI. The costs of this contract, of its confirmation and engrossment, the hereditary lessees undertake to settle.

§ XVII. So long as the hereditary lessees and their successors in possession shall fulfil the above-described obligations, they shall be protected everywhere by the ground lordship in their rights and shall be defended and held harmless against every man's real claim; but should they remain in arrears with the annual hereditary canon for longer than two years, they shall thereupon forfeit this hereditary lease, and the ground lordship shall be entitled to let the same out elsewhere, in

which case the hereditary lessees may be compensated only for their ameliorations.

§ XVIII. For the correct and prompt payment of the fixed hereditary rent the hereditary lessees and the future takers, as well as their successors in possession, are perpetually liable one for all and all for one, and thus jointly and severally, in such wise that the ground lordship shall be entitled in this respect to hold either to the one or to the other. Both parties promised to keep the foregoing hereditary-lease contract firmly and inviolably, and renounce all legal exceptions available against it, in particular the objection of an injury suffered above or below the half, of

cunning persuasion, of over-haste, of the impossibility of raising the rent out of the thing itself, of matters otherwise agreed than written down or not rightly understood; and it is expressly consented by His Most Serene Princely Highness that the hereditary lessees may, on the ground of this contract, have the Titulum Possessionis of their holdings entered in their own names.

For the rest it is noted that with the aforementioned hereditary lessees a contract was already drawn up on the 26th of May of the current year, through a certain Kunkel, concerning these ten Hufen, which contract has now been returned by the hereditary lessees and is hereby wholly annulled and cancelled by them by means of striking through, in such wise that the same shall be regarded as not concluded, and that neither the one nor the other party shall form or assert any rights or claims out of it.

The contracting parties had nothing further to state; they asked for

the confirmation and engrossment of this contract; and after this protocol had been read out to them word for word, and they had also been instructed that the renunciation of the legal exceptions and objections entails the entire loss of the same, they once more renounced them after due deliberation, and have subscribed and signed below in token of their approval.
