--- SUMMARY (English) ---
An eighteen-paragraph hereditary-lease contract made before the Hohenlohe patrimonial court at Trąbczyn, granting the demesne farm Althütte, 10 Hufen 10 Morgen Magdeburg measure, to six settlers, Martin Giering, Wilhelm Daniel Schulz, Gottfried Dümmel, Michel Dümmel, Gottfried Damt and Christoph Schoenfisch, for 150 Rthl purchase money per Hufe, 1,500 Rthl in all, and 225 Rthl yearly rent. Terms cover free years on land still to be cleared, joint liability, a tenth-Groschen transfer fee, compulsory manorial drink, and cancellation of an earlier contract of 26 May.

The Princely Hohenlohe Patrimonial Court at Trąbczyn hereby attests that between His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, hereditary lord of the Zagorowo and Trąbczyn estates, of the one part, and the residents Martin Giering, Wilhelm Daniel Schulz, Gottfried Dümmel, Michel Dümmel, Gottfried Damt and Christoph Schoenfisch, of the other part, concerning the demesne farm called Althütte, consisting of 10 Hufen 10 Morgen Magdeburg measure, the following hereditary-lease contract has been concluded and declared to the court protocol:

Actum Trąbczyn, the twentieth of December One Thousand Eight Hundred and Four. Before the undersigned

Patrimonial Court

there appeared in person,

1., the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld as general attorney of His Serene Highness the reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, hereditary lord and lord of the court of the estates here, of the one part

and

2., The residents

1. Martin Giering, Wilhelm Daniel Schulz, Gottfried Dümmel, Michel Dümmel, Gottfried Dams, and Christoph Schoenfisch from the estates here, of the other part.

The appearers are personally known to the undersigned, and there is no doubt as to their capacity validly to perform judicial acts.

The same declare, freely and after due deliberation, the following hereditary-lease contract to the protocol.

§. I. Namely, His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, through his general attorney the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, makes over the demesne farm belonging to Trąbczyn, called the Alte Hütte, consisting of 10 Hufen 10 Morgen, say

Ten Hufen ten Morgen Magdeburg measure, in arable, meadows, gardens and overgrown marsh-ground and [uncertain: Götzstellen], together with all the farm buildings found on this demesne farm, excepting the new cow-byre, which remains to the ground lordship, to the aforementioned residents Martin Giering, Wilhelm Daniel Schulz, Gottfried Dümmel, Michel Dämmel, Gottfried Damt and Christoph Schönfisch, for perpetual and everlasting times in hereditary lease.

Each Hufe contains thirty Morgen, and each Morgen one hundred and eighty square rods Magdeburg measure.

§. II. As purchase money the hereditary lessees pay

6. The hereditary lessees one hundred and fifty Reichsthaler per Hufe, making for 10 Hufen 1500 rt, say One Thousand and thereupon a Hundred Reichsthaler. On this they have already paid down 450 rtl., say four hundred and fifty Reichsthaler, and the remaining 1050 rt, say One Thousand and Fifty Reichsthaler, they bind themselves to pay off to His Princely Serene Highness at the term of St George 1805 in cash and in current money of the standard of the treasury.

As an annual hereditary canon the hereditary lessees pay, and that from Martinmas of this year onward, per Hufe 22 rthl 12 gg., say twenty-two Reichsthaler twelve good Groschen, making for 10 Hufen 225 rttl, say two hundred and twenty-five Reichsthaler, in two instalments, namely the half with 112 rt.

12 gg., say One Hundred and Twelve Reichsthaler 12 gg., at Midsummer, and the second half likewise with 112 rt. 12 gg., say One Hundred and Twelve Reichsthaler 12 gg., at Christmas, each time postnumerando, in cash and in Courant, into the manorial treasury; but they are wholly freed for perpetual times from all and every manorial labour service and delivery in kind to the ground lordship.

§. IV. This stipulated hereditary rent is to be paid from Martinmas of this year onward, yet only from such land and meadow as is already actually brought under cultivation; from those parts, on the other hand, which must first be made arable by grubbing, they receive [uncertain: four] free years, which, since they have already had the first cut this year,

begin with the past Midsummer of this year and end at Midsummer One Thousand Eight Hundred and Eight, so that from this uncultivated land they shall render the first rent at Christmas One Thousand Eight Hundred and Eight; and the objection that one or another of them had not yet brought his land under cultivation shall then not be admitted.

§. V. Besides the annually stipulated hereditary rent, however, the hereditary lessees shall further, for themselves and their descendants, bear in perpetuity all and every royal and other public tax and due falling upon these ten Hufen, by whatever name they may be called.

§. VI. Since in the first § there are

granted to the hereditary lessees in all ten Hufen ten Morgen, it is here noted that they have received the ten Morgen for their building plots free of charge, and need pay neither purchase money nor rent for them.

§. VII. The hereditary lessees are at liberty to make over these ten Hufen so acquired in several separate parts to others, and to come to terms with these new takers to their own advantage as best they can.

§. VIII. Their establishments must the present hereditary lessees, as well as the remaining future takers, for the present and for times to come

always carry out at their own cost, and they shall receive for this neither remission of the hereditary rent nor any other building assistance; they may, however, employ for this the timber standing on the Hufen received in hereditary lease, as being theirs in property, all of it and without any exception.

§. IX. These Hufen taken in hereditary lease shall, as soon as the weather permits, be duly measured out to the hereditary lessees and delivered into their possession, and at the same time it shall be ascertained how much the surface content amounts to of the land already now under cultivation, from which

the rent is paid.

§. X. The hereditary lessees renounce entirely, for themselves and their successors, any building timber, firewood or pasture in the remaining manorial woods and fields, and they may not overstep the boundaries of their holding.

§. XI. The present hereditary lessees, as well as the remaining future takers, are at liberty to alienate their holdings in any manner they please; but upon every change of possession, whether by sale or by exchange, the tenth Groschen of the purchase money or of the exchange sum must be paid to the ground lordship as a transfer fee (Laudemium),

and in this regard the same shall always hold to the new acquirer, whose business it remains not to pay out the purchase money to the seller before the transfer fee has been settled. On inheritances or cessions to children and collateral relatives no transfer fee is paid, and likewise the first takers from the present hereditary lessees are wholly exempt from it.

§. XII. The hereditary lessees are at liberty to redeem the stipulated hereditary rent by payment of the capital sum; that is to say, each of them may pay down to the ground lordship fifty Reichsthaler, one hundred Reichsthaler or one hundred and fifty Reichsthaler, which capital

shall then remain the property of the ground lordship, and five per hundred of it shall be written off from their annual rent; yet it is expressly laid down that this redeemable sum may in no case exceed, per Hufe, the sum of one hundred and fifty Reichsthaler, and that accordingly [uncertain: fifteen Reichsthaler] of annual rent per Hufe shall remain standing irredeemable for perpetual times.

§. XIII. The hereditary lessees and their descendants remain forever wholly free of all hereditary subjection, but are without exception subject to the jurisdiction of the manorial lordship (Dominium).

§. XIV. Their drink in beer

and brandy the hereditary lessees and their successors in possession must always take from the manorial brewhouse and distillery or from the tavern, and for every quantity of brandy brought in from other estates they shall pay one Reichsthaler, and for every barrel of beer five Reichsthaler, as a penalty to the ground lordship.

§. XV. The hereditary lessees hereby expressly renounce, for themselves and their successors, all and every remission in cases of misfortune, whether these arise from fire from heaven, arson by evil people, crop failure, hailstorm, flood, cattle plague, war, or pestilence, and in whatever manner they may arise; and

on that account no compensation or remission of the hereditary rent shall ever accrue to them.

§. XVI. The costs of this contract, of its confirmation and of the official copy, the hereditary lessees undertake to settle.

§. XVII. So long as the hereditary lessees and their successors in possession fulfil the obligations described above, they shall be protected in their rights by the ground lordship in every respect, and be defended and held harmless against any man's real claim. Should they, however, remain in arrears with the annual hereditary canon for longer than two years, they shall thereupon forfeit this hereditary lease, and the ground lordship shall be entitled to let the same out elsewhere, in

which case only their improvements need be made good to the hereditary lessees.

§. XVIII. For the correct and prompt payment of the fixed hereditary rent the hereditary lessees and the future takers, as well as their successors in possession, are liable in perpetuity, one for all and all for one, hence jointly and severally, in such wise that the ground lordship shall be entitled in this regard to hold to the one or to the other. Both parties promised to keep the foregoing hereditary-lease contract firmly and inviolably, and renounce all legal evasions available against it, in particular the objection of an injury sustained above or below the half, of

cunning persuasion, of undue haste, of the impossibility of raising the rent out of the property, of matters otherwise agreed than written down or not rightly understood; and it is expressly consented by His Most Serene Highness that the hereditary lessees may, on the ground of this contract, have the title of possession to their holdings entered in their own names.

Further, on the part of His Most Serene Highness the hereditary lessees are granted the liberty of fetching, free of charge, the clay for their whole first establishments from the Trąbczyn territory; yet this liberty ceases entirely as soon as they shall have built their buildings. Also

there shall further be delivered to them two thousand [illegible] instead of bricklayer's [uncertain: stones] from the Trąbczyn brickworks, for which they must pay the costs at three Reichsthaler per thousand. For the rest it is noted that with the said hereditary lessees a contract concerning these ten Hufen was already drawn up on the 26th of May of the current year by a certain Kunkel, which contract has now been given back by the hereditary lessees and is hereby, by striking through, wholly cancelled and annulled by them, in such wise that the same is to be regarded as not concluded, and that neither the one party nor the other shall form or assert any rights or claims out of it.

The contracting parties had nothing further to adduce; they asked for

the confirmation and official copy of this contract; and after this protocol had been read to them word for word, and they had also been instructed that the renunciation of the legal evasions and objections entails the entire loss of them, they once more, after due deliberation, renounced the same, and in token of approval have subscribed and signed below.

Read by myself and approved.

Friedrich Ludwig, Prince of Hohenlohe
