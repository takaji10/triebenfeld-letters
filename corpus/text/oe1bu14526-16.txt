--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Hereditary-lease contract, engrossed under the Trąbczyn patrimonial court seal, by which v. Triebenfeld, general plenipotentiary of Fürst Friedrich Ludwig zu Hohenlohe Ingelfingen, cedes the Trąbczyn demesne farm Althütte (10 Hufen 10 Morgen, Magdeburg measure, the new cow-byre excepted) in perpetuity to Martin Giering, Wilhelm Daniel Schulz, Gottfried Dümmel, Michel Dämmel, Gottfried Dams and Christoph Schoenfisch: entry-money 150 rtl per Hufe (1500 rtl, 450 paid, 1050 due at St. George 1805), annual canon 225 rtl, laudemium of the tenth Groschen, tavern compulsion, joint liability; an earlier Kunkel contract is cancelled.

The Princely Hohenlohe Patrimonial Court at Trąbczyn hereby attests that between His Serene Highness the reigning Prince zu Hohenlohe Ingelfingen, hereditary lord of the Zagorowo and Trąbczyn estates, of the one part, and the resident holders Martin Giering, Wilhelm Daniel Schulz, Gottfried Dümmel, Michel Dümmel, Gottfried Damt and Christoph Schoenfisch, of the other part, concerning the demesne farm called Althütte, consisting of 10 Hufen 10 Morgen Magdeburg measure, the following hereditary-lease contract has been concluded and declared to the court protocol:

Actum Trąbczyn, the twentieth of December One Thousand Eight Hundred and Four. Before the undersigned

Patrimonial Court

there appeared in person,

1., the Councillor of War and Forests v. Triebenfeld as general plenipotentiary of His Serene Highness the reigning Prince zu Hohenlohe Ingelfingen, hereditary and judicial lord of the estates here, of the one part

and

2., the resident holders

1. Martin Giering, Wilhelm Daniel Schulz, Gottfried Dümmel, Michel Dümmel, Gottfried Dams, and Christoph Schoenfisch from the estates here, of the other part.

The comparents are personally known to the undersigned, and no doubt whatever obtains as to their capacity validly to perform judicial acts.

The same declare, freely and after due deliberation, the following hereditary-lease contract to the protocol.

§. I. His Serene Highness the Prince zu Hohenlohe Ingelfingen, through his general plenipotentiary the Councillor of War and Forests v. Triebenfeld, namely cedes the demesne farm belonging to Trąbczyn called die Alte Hütte, consisting of 10 Hufen 10 Morgen, say

Ten Hufen ten Morgen Magdeburg measure, in arable, meadows, gardens and overgrown marshes and [uncertain: Götzstellen], together with all the farm buildings found on this demesne farm, excepting the new cow-byre, which remains to the ground lordship, to the aforesaid resident holders Martin Giering, Wilhelm Daniel Schulz, Gottfried Dümmel, Michel Dämmel, Gottfried Damt and Christoph Schönfisch, in hereditary lease for perpetual and everlasting time.

Each Hufe contains thirty Morgen and each Morgen one hundred and eighty square rods Magdeburg measure.

§. II. By way of entry-money the hereditary lessees pay

6. the hereditary lessees one hundred and fifty Reichsthaler p. holding, making for 10 Hufen 1500 rt, say One Thousand and thereupon Hundred Reichsthaler. §. IV. This stipulated hereditary rent is rendered from Martinmas of this year, yet only from such land and meadow as is in fact already brought under cultivation; from those parts, on the other hand, which must first be made arable by grubbing, they receive [uncertain: four] free years, which, since they have already had the [uncertain: first cutting] this year,

§. V. Besides the annually stipulated hereditary rent, however, the hereditary lessees for themselves and their descendants [must] also bear in perpetuity all and any royal and other public taxes and dues falling upon these ten Hufen, by whatever name they may be called.

§. VI. Since in the first §. to the

hereditary lessees ten Hufen ten Morgen in all are granted, it is here noted that they have received the ten Morgen for their building-plots free of charge, and need pay neither entry-money nor rent for them.

§. VIII. Their establishments must be effected, both by the present hereditary lessees and by the other future takers, for now and for times to come

always at their own cost, and they receive for this neither remission of the hereditary rent nor [uncertain: any further] building assistance; they may however use for this the timber standing upon the Hufen received in hereditary lease, as being wholly and without any exception their own property.

§. IX. These Hufen taken in hereditary lease shall, as soon as the weather permits, be duly measured out to the hereditary lessees and delivered into possession, and at the same time it shall be ascertained how much the surface content of the land already now under cultivation, from which

the rent is paid, amounts to.

§. X. The hereditary lessees, for themselves and their successors, wholly renounce any building or fire timber, or pasture, in the remaining manorial woods and fields, and they may not overstep the bounds of their holding.

§. XI. The present hereditary lessees, as also the other future takers, are at liberty to alienate their holdings in any manner they please; but upon every change of possession, whether by sale or by exchange, the tenth Groschen of the purchase-money or of the exchange amount must be paid to the ground lordship as a laudemium,

and the same shall in this regard always hold to the new acquirer, whose business it remains not to pay out the purchase-money to the seller before the laudemium has been discharged. Upon inheritance, or cession to children and collateral relatives, no laudemium is paid, and likewise the first takers from the present hereditary lessees are wholly exempt therefrom.

§. XII. The hereditary lessees are at liberty to redeem the stipulated hereditary rent by payment of the capital sum; that is, each of them may pay the ground lordship in cash fifty Reichsthaler, one hundred Reichsthaler or one hundred and fifty Reichsthaler, which capital

shall thereupon remain the property of the ground lordship, and five per hundred shall be written off from their annual [uncertain: rent] on account thereof; yet it is expressly laid down that this sum to be paid off may in no case exceed, per Hufe, the sum of one hundred and fifty Reichsthaler, and that consequently in annual rent [uncertain: fifteen] say Fifteen Reichsthaler per Hufe shall remain standing irredeemable for perpetual time.

§. XIII. The hereditary lessees and their descendants remain free for ever from all hereditary subjection, but are without exception subject to the jurisdiction of the Dominium.

§. XIV. Their drink in beer

and brandy the hereditary lessees and their successors in possession must always take from the manorial brew- and distilling-house or the tavern, and for every quantity of brandy brought in from outside estates they shall pay One Reichsthaler, and for every barrel of beer Five Reichsthaler, as a penalty to the ground lordship.

§. XV. The hereditary lessees, for themselves and their successors, hereby expressly renounce all and any remission in case of misfortune, whether it arise by fire from heaven, kindling by evil persons, crop failure, hailstorm, flood, cattle plague, war and pestilence, or in whatever manner it may, and

on that account no compensation or remission of the hereditary rent shall ever accrue to them.

§. XVI. The costs of this contract, of its confirmation and engrossment, the hereditary lessees undertake to discharge.

§. XVII. So long as the hereditary lessees and their successors in possession fulfil the obligations described above, they shall be protected everywhere by the ground lordship in their judicial [uncertain: assemblies], and be represented and held harmless against every man's real claims; but should they remain in arrears with the annual hereditary canon for longer than two years, they shall thereupon forfeit this hereditary lease, and the ground lordship is entitled to let the same out otherwise, in

which case only their meliorations may be made good to the hereditary lessees.

§. XVIII. For the correct and prompt payment of the fixed hereditary rent the hereditary lessees and the future takers, as well as their successors in possession, are liable in perpetuity one for all and all for one, hence jointly and severally, in such wise that the ground lordship shall in this regard be entitled to hold to the one or to the other. Both parties promised to keep the foregoing hereditary-lease contract firmly and inviolably, and renounce all legal evasions available against it, in particular the objection of an injury suffered above or below the half, of

cunning persuasion, of over-haste, of the impossibility of raising the rent out of the property, of matters otherwise agreed than written down or not rightly understood; and it is expressly consented by His Most Serene Highness that the hereditary lessees may, on the ground of this contract, have the Titulum Possessionis of their holdings entered in their own names.

Further, on the part of His Most Serene Highness the liberty is granted to the hereditary lessees to fetch the [uncertain: clay] from the Trąbczyn territory free of charge for their first complete establishments; but this liberty ceases entirely as soon as they shall have erected their buildings. For the rest it is noted that with the aforesaid hereditary lessees a contract concerning these ten Hufen was already drawn up on the 26th of May of the current year by a certain Kunkel, which contract is now returned by the hereditary lessees and is hereby, by means of striking through, wholly annulled and cancelled by them, in such wise that the same shall be regarded as not concluded, and that neither the one party nor the other shall frame or assert any rights or claims out of it.

The contracting parties had nothing further to adduce; they asked for

the confirmation and engrossment of this contract, and after this protocol had been read out to them word for word, and they had also been instructed that the renunciation of the legal evasions and objections entails their complete loss, they once more renounced the same after due deliberation, and in token of approval have subscribed and signed below.

Read by myself and approved.

Friedrich Ludwig Fürst zu Hohenlohe
