--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Hahn berichtet, dass der Fürst am Fuß fast geheilt und sonst wohlauf sei. Der Fuß sei nur noch wenig geschwollen und schmerze nicht. Der Andreas Orden habe Freude bereitet, mehr noch das artige Benehmen des Kaysers, von dem der Fürst bezaubert sei. Auch in Wrocław soll der Kayser schmeichelhaft vom Fürsten gesprochen haben. Das Regiment Hohenlohe sei durch Erfurt marschiert, der Fürst ritt ihm entgegen. Der Herzog von Weimar werde erwartet.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Hahn reports that the Prince's foot is almost healed and that he is otherwise well. He says the foot is only slightly swollen and causes no pain. The Order of St Andrew gave pleasure, he says, and still more the courteous behaviour of the Emperor, by whom the Prince is said to be charmed. In Wrocław too the Emperor is said to have spoken flatteringly of the Prince. The Hohenlohe regiment marched through Erfurt, he reports, and the Prince rode out to meet it. The Duke of Weimar is expected.

171

Right Honourable Sir, Highly Honoured Sir, Councillor of War and Forests. Since I know that Your Right Honourable takes an interest in news of my most gracious Lord, I take the liberty of conveying to you the assurance that His Serene Highness is almost entirely healed of the Bible in his foot, and is in other respects perfectly well; the foot is only a little swollen still, and causes no pain. His Serene Highness wishes to be most kindly commended to you. The Order of St Andrew has given pleasure, yet the courteous conduct of His Majesty the Emperor has given rise to still greater satisfaction.

The Prince is quite enchanted by this young and courteous monarch. In Wrocław too the Emperor is said to have spoken of His Serene Highness in the most flattering terms.

Today the Hohenlohe regiment marched through here; the Prince had ridden out to meet it. Tomorrow the Duke of Weimar is expected here. I commend myself to Your Right Honourable's favour, and have the honour to remain in the most boundless esteem,

Your Right Honourable's

Erfurt, 24 November

1805

humble and obedient servant

A. Hahn
