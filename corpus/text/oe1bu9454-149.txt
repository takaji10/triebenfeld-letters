--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports on the parcelling sales: buyers from the Oder and Nezzbruch are expected in numbers, waste corners cut from the Swięce parcels have been sold advantageously, and no Hufe will go under 600 Rthl; the Zagorow assessment should exceed 400000 Rthl, enabling borrowing in Berlin to satisfy Schlabrendorff, Legnowelii and other pressing creditors. Quarrels with Glenck and the Olesnice hereditary lessees brought on a three-day colic. The Wrgbin subjects, threatened with removal of their parcels, forced labour and sale with the Voigtey to von Marowecke for 15000 Rthl, have been referred to justice-burgomaster Schenck.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord!

Still I await the arrival of the people from the Oder and the Netze marshes. From Wednesday onwards they will doubtless present themselves in droves, and then I hope quickly to be at the goal of my wishes. Several parcels of ground which the Swięce people had marked out, and which lay about the boundary as waste corners, I have sold very advantageously; I not only place the soil, which was not esteemed at all, well with a purchaser, but likewise [make] the shape [of the land], so as now to be able to conclude better with the large purchasers upon regular plots. From the plan I once drew up I do not depart, and I answer for it that no Hufe shall be given away under 600 Rthl (that is to say, ground-money and interest taken together). If it has succeeded with the very worst grounds, then it will not fail me with the better ones. The assessment will probably be finished this week, and, as I hope, will come out at over 400000 Rthl from the Zagorowo estates. By this means all ill-will is defied; I shall take in

Berlin as much money as is needed for the payment of Schlabrendorff, Legnowelii and other pressing creditors, and pay them. Gradually the money for these grounds comes in, and that debt is paid off again; in this way nothing is lost through over-hastiness and the great object is attained. It goes hard, very hard, for much has been spoilt by the easy earlier sales; nevertheless my perseverance will prevail, for that I stand surety.

The recent vexation with Glenck, where the fellow tells me to my face that I mean to wrest his property from him, and the annoyance with the hereditary leaseholders stirred up by the Olesnice people, has brought on me a frightful colic which compelled me to keep to my bed for 3 days; I am still very much out of sorts.

For the rest, the subjects of Wrgbin have protested against their contract and refuse to render the rent. Their complaint is that Glenck has threatened them to take their grounds away and to give them others in their place; besides this they complain that Glenck's clerk has compelled them to serve the whole year through, and that, since he could not use all the services on the bailiwick, he has sent the subjects to Zagorowo, where they must work for the townspeople, and for which

the steward has himself paid 4 Groschen daily per person / of whom there are 18 /. The subjects find it too hard that they should be sold like slaves into service to other, strange men. The steward has moreover given the people to know that he means to draw all 18 householders wholly to the bailiwick, and that Glenck means to sell them along with it to von Marowecke, who on that footing gives 15000 Rthl for the bailiwick.

The subjects implore that Your Serene Highness might not permit this; they would gladly give a [uncertain: Rund] per Morgen from their fields, and perform everything else that lay within their powers. I have referred the people with their petitions and lamentations to the justice-burgomaster Schenck, and requested him to quiet them and to bring the matter into order, so that Glenck may not cry out again that I mean to wrest from him his well-acquired property.

In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Trąbczyn

the 2nd of December

1804

most humble and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld
