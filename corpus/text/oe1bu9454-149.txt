--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports on the parcelling sales: buyers from the Oder and Nezzbruch are expected in numbers, waste corners cut from the Swięce parcels have been sold advantageously, and no Hufe will go under 600 Rthl; the Zagorow assessment should exceed 400000 Rthl, enabling borrowing in Berlin to satisfy Schlabrendorff, Legnowelii and other pressing creditors. Quarrels with Glenck and the Olesnice hereditary lessees brought on a three-day colic. The Wrgbin subjects, threatened with removal of their parcels, forced labour and sale with the Voigtey to von Marowecke for 15000 Rthl, have been referred to justice-burgomaster Schenck.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Lord! I still await the arrival of the Oder and Nezzbruch people. From Wednesday onward they will probably present themselves in droves, and then I hope to be swiftly at the goal of my wishes. Several parcels which I would have struck out from the Swięce ones, and which lay about the boundary as waste corners, I have sold very advantageously; I not only place the soil, which was not esteemed at all, well with a buyer, but likewise [uncertain: the figure] now, so as to be able to conclude better with the large purchasers upon regular plots. From the plan I once drew up I shall not depart, and I stand surety that no Hufe is to be given away under 600 Rthl (that is to say, ground-money and interest taken together). If it has succeeded even with the very worst parcels, then it will not fail me with the better ones. The assessment will probably be completed this week and, as I hope, will come out at over 400000 Rthl for the Zagorow estates. By this means all ill-will is defied; I take in

Berlin as much money as is needful for the payment of the Schlabrendorff, Legnowelii and other pressing creditors, and pay them. Gradually the money for these parcels comes in; one pays off that debt again, and in this way nothing is lost through precipitancy and the great object is attained. It goes hard, very hard, for much has been spoiled by the easy earlier sales; nevertheless my perseverance will prevail, for that I answer. The recent vexation with Glenck, where the man tells me to my face that I wish to wrest from him his property, and the annoyance with the Olesnice hereditary lessees who have been stirred up, have brought upon me a frightful colic which compelled me to keep my bed for 3 days; I am still very much out of sorts. For the rest, the Wrgbin subjects have protested against their contract and refuse to render the interest. Their complaint is that Glenck has threatened them to take away their parcels and to give them others in their place; besides this they complain that Glenck's clerk has compelled them to serve throughout the whole year still, and that he, since he could not use all the services on the Vogtey, has sent the subjects to Zagorowo, where they must work for the townsmen, and for which

the Meyer has himself paid 4 gg. daily per person / of whom there are 18 /. The subjects find this too hard, that they should be sold like slaves into service to other, foreign persons. The Meyer has moreover made it known to the people that he intends to draw all 18 farmers wholly to the Voigtey, and that Glenck wishes to sell them along with it to the von Marowecke, who in that manner gives 15000 Rthl for the Voigtey. The subjects implore that Your Serene Highness might not permit this; they will gladly give a [uncertain: Rund] per Morgen of their fields and perform everything else that lies within their powers. I have referred the people with their petitions and lamentations to the justice-burgomaster Schenck, and requested him to quiet them and to bring the matter into order, so that Glenck may not cry out again that I wish to wrest from him his well-acquired property. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's Trąbczin, the 2nd of December 1804 most humble and obedient servant v Triebenfeld
