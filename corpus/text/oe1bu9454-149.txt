--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Awaiting settlers from the Oder and Netze marshes, Triebenfeld reports selling waste corner plots profitably and holds to his plan that no Hufe go for under 600 Rthl; a valuation of the Zagorowo estates should exceed 400,000 Rthl, letting him borrow in Berlin to pay Schlabrendorff, Legnowelii and other pressing creditors. Quarrels with Glenck and the Olesnica hereditary lessees have made him ill. The Wrgbiner subjects refuse their contract, protesting forced labour and Glenck's intended sale of them with the bailiff's holding to von Marowecke for 15,000 Rthl.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

Still I await the arrival of the Oder and Netze marshland people. From Wednesday on they will doubtless present themselves in droves, and then I hope soon to be at the goal of my wishes. Several plots which the Swięcer people would have marked out, and which lay about the boundary as waste corners, I have sold very advantageously; I not only place the soil that nobody had any regard for well with a buyer, but likewise [uncertain: shape the layout] so as now to be able to conclude better with the large purchasers for regular parcels. From the plan I once drew up I do not depart, and I stand surety that no Hufe shall be given away under 600 Rthl (that is to say, purchase money and interest taken together). If it has succeeded with the very worst plots, then it will not fail me with the better ones. The valuation will probably be finished this week, and, as I hope, will come out at over 400000 Rthl for the Zagorowo estates. By this means all ill-will is defied; I shall take up in

Berlin as much money as is necessary for the payment of the Schlabrendorff, Legnowelii and other pressing creditors, and I pay. Gradually the money for these plots comes in, and that debt is paid off again; in this way nothing is lost through precipitancy and the great aim is attained. It goes hard, very hard, for much has been spoilt by the easy earlier sales; nevertheless my perseverance will prevail, for that I answer.

The recent vexation with Glenck, where the man tells me to my face that I mean to wrest his property from him, and the annoyance with the Olesnica hereditary lessees, who have been stirred up, has brought on me a frightful colic which forced me to keep to my bed for 3 days; I am still very much out of sorts.

For the rest, the Wrgbiner subjects have protested against their contract and refuse to render the interest. Their complaint is that Glenck has threatened them to take away their plots and to give them others in their stead; besides this they complain that Glenck's clerk has compelled them to serve through the whole year still, and that he, since he could not use all the services on the bailiff's holding, has sent the subjects to Zagorowo, where they must work for the townspeople, and for which

the steward has himself paid 4 Groschen daily per person (of whom there are 18). The subjects find it too hard that they should be sold like slaves into service to other, strange people. The steward has moreover made known to the people that he intends to draw all 18 farmers entirely to the bailiff's holding, and that Glenck means to sell them along with it to von Marowecke, who in that manner gives 15000 Rthl for the bailiff's holding.

The subjects implore that Your Serene Highness might not permit this; they would gladly give a [uncertain: Rund] per Morgen from their fields, and render everything else that lay within their powers. I have referred the people with their petitions and lamentations to the justice-burgomaster Schenck, and requested him to quiet them and to bring the matter into order, so that Glenck may not cry out again that I mean to wrest from him his well-acquired property.

In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Trąbczyn

the 2nd of December

1804

most humble and obedient servant

v Triebenfeld
