--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld wartet auf die Berliner Resolution und führt die neue Taxenrevision auf Prinz George und Kalckreuth zurück. Otocki verspricht 10000 Rthl und 2000 Rthl Propinationspacht im Voraus; Meierowitz würde nach Honrichs' Abgang 20000 Rthl und 500 Rthl jährlichen Canon zahlen. Ein Kammeredikt verbot die Erbpacht an Nichtadlige ohne königlichen Consens; Glenck habe dies verschwiegen und den Contract mit den Olesnicern schließen lassen. Laut Dombrowski liegen der Prusimskische Compromiss und Quittungen über Prusimskis Zahlung im Archiv.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld is waiting for the Berlin resolution and puts the new revision of the valuation down to Prince George and Kalckreuth. He says Otocki promises 10000 Rthl and 2000 Rthl of propination rights rent in advance, and that Meierowitz would pay 20000 Rthl and an annual canon of 500 Rthl once Honrichs leaves. A chamber edict forbade hereditary leases to non-nobles without royal consent; he says Glenck kept this quiet and let the contract with the people of Oleśnica be concluded. According to Dombrowski, the Prusimski compromise and receipts for Prusimski's payment are in the archive.

Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord!

From Berlin no resolution has yet been forthcoming; I do not know what I am to think of Amelang's diligence. Men are all cut from one last — only do not always stand behind them, or clear the way oneself, or [uncertain: ober füllt] the rapacious claws so, and they let everything go at their snail's pace. If the review is made difficult for me by the Finance Department, then I shall have the district administrator review, attest and guarantee the valuations; that is indeed more than is demanded. It therefore comes down solely to this, that I be given a decision from Berlin.

The chicaneries over the review of the valuations are entirely new and arise solely from Prince George's and General Kalckreuth's impetuous conduct — neither Amelang nor still less I knew anything of it in Berlin; the [uncertain: Zand] was solely with Minister von Voss, by whom all the orders were swiftly given, and since the matter was not dealt with in collegial session, no one could know anything of it. [uncertain: Unfuhr] this pretty ordinance first in Wrocław from young Count Kalckreuth. Meanwhile so much [uncertain: Ehe] has been set aside that here too a way out must be found.

NB Amelang has himself given notice to the Jew [uncertain: Itzig] on the 18000 Rthl which he has secured elsewhere. The Jew has believed that this was done only for appearance's sake, and thought to write to Your Serene Highness with a judicial notice — but it remains with my plan[s?] — the interest I have duly paid, and hold a receipt for it, so that Your Serene Highness's honour could not be [uncertain: compromised] —

Here everything so far goes as one would wish, and I hope to bring together a heavy lump of money. With Otocki I think to conclude today. He promises 10000 Rthl within a short time, as well as 2000 Rthl as the

half-yearly propination rent in advance. If I am rid of Honrichs, as I firmly hope, then Meierowitz will pay 20000 Rthl in cash 8 days before Midsummer, and will lay down an annual canon of 500 Rthl in perpetuity.

What has happened with Swięcia, and whether my plan will succeed, I shall learn today from Nerlich.

For the purchase of the grounds which are [uncertain: me]hhn to be dismembered altogether, many persons have already come forward, and among them several noblemen, who wish to buy many Hufen and to pay in cash at once. Meanwhile I press only for the issuing of the consent, so that no impediment of any kind can be put in my way.

The Chamber had entrusted the investigation to the district administrator and prescribed the norm according to which Your Serene Highness may dismember; [illegible] this has been strictly complied with, everything set aside, and everything submitted to the Chamber, so that all chicaneries have been met and the ways to the throne made smooth, [uncertain: war] one wished to draw a new barrier across our nose, for I should lay before the King those dispositions which had been given, as well as all the contracts concluded in accordance with them, and the minutes, and should then there demand the concession with full right, without having to beg. The President v. G. is entirely of one mind with me in this. By the next post I shall send Your Serene Highness a copy of all these proceedings. With the Oleśnica fellows everything will be remedied very gradually.

There has long existed with the Chambers here (of which, however, as God lives, I have not known one jot) an edict, according to which

1. no peasant or non-noble may be granted a noble estate in hereditary lease without special royal consent

2. even to noble hereditary lessees there may not be handed over, or left to the hereditary lessees,

a. not the jurisdiction

b. not the [uncertain: Zad]

c. not the beer and brandy revenue, and

d. not the power to drive out the old subjects, or to disturb them in their livelihoods

Already under the 23rd of December 1803 the Chamber here made this known to the building councillor Glenck and to the district administrator v. Blomberg. More than punishable is it of the former, that he failed Your Serene Highness in this and permitted that a contract was nevertheless concluded with the Oleśnica fellows. But base is it of the latter, that he nevertheless wished to conclude a settlement between the 3 Oleśnica rogues and the old subjects, and to have Your Serene Highness guarantee the same. God, what sort of men are these, and in whose hands has Your Serene Highness been until now — By high disposition the whole of the Chamber files have been given to me; I am having all the fine pieces extracted, so that Your Serene Highness may for once be convinced

Now I must hold firmly and purely to the performance of the contract not now kept by the Oleśnica people, so that these scoundrels do not get through with any counter-claim, for otherwise we should have to fear a lawsuit of years' duration. More than devilish it is that Glenck said not a word of the two Chamber dispositions or resolutions of the 23rd of December 1803 and the 24th of January 1804, and allowed a contract of the 22nd of February 1804 to be concluded out of them, which runs bolt straight against the edicts and rescripts of the Chamber that had been made known to Glenck. I now count upon Schenck's good will and adroitness, which I had already called upon beforehand.

From Dombrowski I learn just now that the Prusimski compromise stands in the Kowal archive, and that the receipts are also inquired into there, that Prusimski has paid. The proceedings run to some seventy sheets; I have asked Dombrowski to have them extracted with haste, then one will indeed get on the track of the matter, for [if] the recesses and actual resignations of Brzyze had also been stolen out of the archives, those persons would yet be able to demand nothing, but would rather have to pay out, since we do not have Brzyze. [uncertain: im] Ribbeck and Floricke Your Serene Highness need not trouble yourself, for these dogs I shall chastise jointly with Lichnowski. If only my health were not so shattered that I could [uncertain: gagen] about in all places, then everything should go more briskly; as it is, I must too often keep to my room in bed, there I lack all care, and the constant vexation wears me quite away — Meanwhile I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's

Kalisz, the 4th of March 1805.

most faithful servant

v. Triebenfeld

The wash-stand has given very much pleasure — the bust of bronze, however, I am bringing back — the friend wishes only three to 4 large ones of plaster, and those coated so as though it were bronze. Echtler has perhaps such in stock. Might I have them here by the next post?
