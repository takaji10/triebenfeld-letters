--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Still no resolution from Berlin on revising the land assessments; Triebenfeld blames Amelang's slowness and obstruction traced to Prince George and the Kalckreuths, and will if need be have the district administrator attest the figures. He expects large sums: 10,000 Rthl from Otochten, 2,000 Rthl propination rent, and 20,000 Rthl plus a 500 Rthl annual payment from Meierowitz once Honrichs is settled. Noblemen are bidding for the lands to be divided. He reports Chamber edicts of December 1803 and January 1804 forbidding the Oleśnicki hereditary lease, concealed by Glenck, and notes the Prussiemski papers at Kowal and his own failing health.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Lord!

From Berlin no resolution has yet come; I do not know what I am to think of Amelang's diligence; men are all cut on one last — only do not stand always behind them, or clear the way oneself, or so fill the rapacious claws, and they let everything go at its snail's pace. If the revision is made difficult for me by the Finance Department, then I shall have the district administrator revise, attest and guarantee the assessments; that is then more than is required. So it comes only to this, that word be sent me from Berlin.

The chicaneries over the revision of the assessments are quite new and arise solely from Prince George and General Kalckreuth's wife's [uncertain: General Kalckreuth's] impetuous conduct — neither Amelang nor still less I knew anything of it in Berlin; the wrangling was solely with Minister von Voss, by whom all the orders were swiftly given, and since the matter was not transacted in collegial form, no one could know of it either. This fine ordinance first came out [uncertain: was carried] in Breslau by the young Count Kalckreuth. Meanwhile, since so much has already been set aside, a way out must be found here too.

Here everything goes so far according to wish, and I hope to bring together a heavy lump of money. With Otochten I think to be finished today. The like, 10000 Rthl in a short time, as also 2000 Rthl as the

half-yearly propination-rights rent, promises to pay in anticipation. If I am rid of Honrichs, as I firmly hope, then Meierowitz pays 20000 Rthl in cash 8 days before Midsummer, and lays down an annual canon of 500 Rthl in perpetuity.

What has happened with Swięcia, and whether my plan will succeed, I shall learn today from Nerlich.

For the purchase of the lands which are to be dismembered generally, many persons have already come forward, and among them several noblemen, who wish to buy many Hufen and to pay at once in cash. Meanwhile I press only for the issue of the consent, so that no obstacle of any kind can be laid in my way.

The Chamber had entrusted the investigation to the district administrator and prescribed the norm according to which Your Serene Highness may dismember; this has been strictly complied with, everything set aside, and everything submitted to the Chamber, so that all chicaneries have been met and the ways to the throne levelled, were one to try to draw a new barrier before our nose; for I should lay before the King those dispositions given, as also all contracts concluded thereafter, together with the protocols, and then demand there the concession with full right, without needing to beg. President v. G. is entirely of one mind with me on this. By the next post I shall send Your Serene Highness a copy of all these transactions. With the Oleśnicki fellows everything will be remedied very gradually.

For a long time already there has existed at the Chamber here (of which, however, as God lives, I knew not one jot) an edict, according to which

1. no peasant or non-noble may be granted a noble estate in hereditary lease without special Royal consent

2. even to noble hereditary lessees there may not be handed over or made over

a. not the jurisdiction

b. not the [uncertain: Zad]

c. not the beer and brandy dues, and

d. not the power to drive out the old subjects, or to displace them in their livelihoods. Already under 23rd December 1803 the Chamber here made this known to the building councillor Glenck and to the district administrator von Blomberg. More than punishable is it of the former, that he concealed such from Your Serene Highness and allowed that a contract should nevertheless be concluded with the Oleśnicki fellows. But base is it of the latter, that he nevertheless wished to conclude a settlement between the 3 Oleśnicki rogues and the old subjects, and to have Your Serene Highness guarantee the same. God, what manner of men are these, and in whose hands has Your Serene Highness been hitherto! — The whole Chamber files have been given me on high disposition; I am having all the fine pieces extracted, so that Your Serene Highness may at last be convinced

Now I must hold firm and purely to the performance of the contract not kept by the Oleśnicki people, so that these rascals may not get through with a possible counter-claim; otherwise we should have a suit of years to fear. More than devilish is it that Glenck said not a word of the two Chamber dispositions or resolutions of 23rd December 1803 and 24th January 1804, and allowed a contract to be concluded under 22 February 1804 which runs point-blank against the edicts and rescripts of the Chamber made known to Glenck. I now count upon Schenck's good will and adroitness, which I had already called upon beforehand.

From Dąbrowski I learn just now that the Prussiemski compromise lies [uncertain: stands] in the Kowal archive, and that the receipts are also inquired into there, that Prussiemski has paid. The transaction runs to some seventy sheets; I have asked Dąbrowski to have it extracted with haste; there indeed one will get on the track of the matter, for if the recesses and actual resignations of Brzyze had also been stolen from the archives, that party would still be able to demand nothing more, but would have to pay out further, since we do not have Brzyze. About Ritlach and Floriche let Your Serene Highness not trouble yourself, for these dogs I shall chastise in concert with Lichnowski. If only my health were not so shattered that I could go about in all places, then everything should go more briskly; as it is, I must too often keep the room, keep to my bed, where all nursing is wanting to me, and the constant vexation quite wears me out — Meanwhile I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's

Kalisz, 4th March 1805.

faithful servant

v. Triebenfeld
