--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Recovering from illness at Blizanow and waiting on the Prince's approval before travelling to Posen and Betsche, Triebenfeld sets out a plan for Betsche: agree terms with the destitute Leixner in Miedzeritz, validate a recovered receipt and clear the mortgage entries, and consent to annulling the contract the insolvent burgesses and peasants seek, while keeping their earnest money and claiming arrears of canon of 11,000 Rthl, ending the sequestration, and pursuing Echaust. Also reports Humboldt and the compensation case, Trzciński's plea for time, and his wish to be rid of Schenck.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

My illness has ceased; only the great weakness does not permit me to undertake long journeys. The physician forbids exposing oneself at once to the weather, wherefore I must still keep quarantine for a few days. With this in view I shall await the return of this messenger. My estate affairs in Kohlen I have arranged - there remain to us exactly 62000 Rthl - Schenck has leased out the estates to a Jew [uncertain: ruinously] and badly - yet I now obtain, by other turns of business, so much that I shall be able to travel and to meet the manifold costs; 500 Rthl are now [text lost] to me, and with that I go to Betsche and from there to Berlin. The Emperor Alexander does not come to Berlin until the end of April, about which however I expect fuller information shortly from the aforesaid Beidlung.

In Betsche my first business will be to come to an agreement with Leixner in Miedzeritz and to see that I bring the receipt that has been found into validity - If that succeeds, then I shall have the debt struck out in the mortgage book, and seek out the [uncertain: Müzzen] which is to be drawn from Betsche: The burgesses and peasants have declared themselves insolvent and press for the annulment of the contract - They prove the [uncertain: Lation] that they are injured beyond the half, since the estates were valued at only 64000 Rthl. I prove that even the highest authority of the land examined everything and approved Your Serene Highness's offer; since moreover the Chief President Zerboni wishes the annulment of the contract, I shall consent to it, yet reserving only quaevis competentia. That is to say, the burgesses and peasants forfeit the earnest money they have given.

2. They must at once pay the arrears of canon and competence with about 2000, so that the sequestration ceases at once.

3. They must at once deliver over to Your Serene Highness the arrears of canon with 11000 Rthl. 4. They must declare how they mean to discharge the arrears of interest. NB these interest payments were formerly demanded by the State, but now that will fall to the ground, if it be proved that the sums of respectively 137000 Rthl entered upon Betsche have been improperly reckoned as [uncertain: Bayon] sums, but that it is the pure private property of Your Serene Highness.

What is to be gained from this, I shall attend to.

5. The communities must themselves turn their weapons against Echaust and his confederates.

According to my reckoning there would then remain to Your Serene Highness

1. The interest on about 68000 Rthl

2. the arrears of canon of 11000

3. the 28000 Rthl paid down by the peasants in 1805

4. what can be wrested from Echaust and his confederates, with 20000 Rthl. Thus Your Serene Highness would have from Betsche altogether 127000 Rthl. NB the 28000 Rthl Your Serene Highness already has, so there would still be to receive 99000 Rthl. Now it depends upon what Betsche is still worth; the forest is totally devastated. At 64000 Rthl it was valued - Queritur, will it be worth 72000 Rthl. And how will it stand with the other 65000 Rthl charged upon it? They do indeed stand in the receipt as well, but I do not yet know whether I shall be able to move Leixner to accept these too. If that should succeed and Leixner took this sum upon Kulm and Kaemen, then all would be helped - Your Serene Highness would have to receive, as said above, 99000 Rthl

From the estates themselves I [uncertain: reckon] the

72000

from the remainder with about

20000 Lombardini, Messrs. Hahn and the good Bally would be paid. Everything however depends upon my succeeding with Leixner. The man is now destitute; I must support him and give money for the outlay of costs of his business. The Chief President Zerboni is charged from higher quarters to sustain him; Kaemen is to remain his. - I insist meanwhile that the sums of 72 and 65000 Rthl entered upon Betsche belong not upon Betsche but upon Kaemen, according to the receipt of Meyer Bernhard. God grant me further good fortune - but may Your Serene Highness deign to write to me whether this altogether has your approval. The compensation business, which through the malicious delay has come to a standstill, will nevertheless turn out very well. It is being worked diplomatically, and the Minister von Humboldt has great need of me now. Trzciński is here again. It is not this one but the Major Trzciński who was suffocated - He was with me the day before yesterday and begged for indulgence until Midsummer, when he receives 25000 Rthl for an estate sold - Go to law I cannot, that costs money; I have granted him such, but assured him that I would lay arrest not only upon the purchase moneys but upon all revenues. Of Schenck I [uncertain: see] and hear nothing - He wants to have a general power of attorney to himself alone - May Your Serene Highness deign to send me a letter

such as I requested in my last, so that I may be rid of him. With Zerboni he has fallen out, I do not know on what grounds - The Chief President also wrote lately to the district administrator von Kurnatowski that he, together with the chief forester Kräger and with the privy councillor von Triebenfeld, should for the present investigate the forest devastations in Betsche, so that one may see clearly. Schenck is offended that the government merely answered him that his submission could not be understood. He wishes, if he receives a general power of attorney and 500 Rthl together with free defrayment, to go to Posen and work upon the matter - It will be hard for me to raise 10 Rthl; I have therefore given him no decision upon it, and at bottom do not need him - My tenant's brother is house factor and favourite of Zerboni, through whose hands pass all the offerings that are brought. Had Your Serene Highness been able to [uncertain: spare] me a [uncertain: Frisling] for 7 bowls, it would have been of use. My tenant goes with me to Posen - He himself stands in very close connexion with Mr. Z. on account of deliveries - and will render only services. As soon as I receive Your Serene Highness's answer I travel to Posen and Betsche. In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's
