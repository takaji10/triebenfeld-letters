--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Forwards a letter just arrived by express courier from Kunckel and asks the Prince for instructions, urging that a settlement be reached with von Otocki, ideally amicably for 1,500 or 2,000, and leaving it to the Prince whether Kunckel should be given power to settle. Warns that Kunckel and Jentsch are working against each other, the one rash, the other spiteful, and advises keeping both in check. Glenck failed to call again, leaving the Kontop accounts behind.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Late yesterday evening I received by express courier the enclosed letter from the aforesaid Kunckel, which I herewith [uncertain: at last] submit to Your Most Serene Highness, requesting Your high instructions as to my conduct. It is indeed necessary to reach an arrangement with von Otocki, and if this could be effected by amicable settlement, so that Otocki would be content with 1500 or 2000 ℔, it would be an advantageous and desirable thing. I leave it to Your Most Serene Highness's highest discretion whether the power of attorney to settle should be given to Kunckel. That Kunckel and Jentsch moreover appear to be acting so much against one another causes me great concern – I fear that Kunckel, through his vehement bearing, will do foolish things, and Jentsch out of malice will make intrigues, which may prove prejudicial. My unpresumptuous counsel is to instruct these men well and to keep them within their bounds.

Glenck did not come to me again yesterday, and has even left the Kontop accounts here, which would have given information upon everything in this matter.

von Triebenfeld

Breslau, 23rd April 1804
