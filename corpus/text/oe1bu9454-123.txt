--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Forwards an express letter just received from Kunckel and asks the Prince's instructions. An arrangement with von Otocki is needed, and an amicable settlement in which Otocki accepted 1,500 or 2,000 would be advantageous; whether Kunckel should receive power of attorney to settle is left to the Prince. Warns that Kunckel and Jentsch are working against each other, Kunckel rashly and Jentsch by intrigue, and advises keeping both in check. Glenck failed to return and left the Kontop accounts behind.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Late yesterday evening I received by express courier the enclosed letter from the said Kunckel, which I herewith [uncertain: at last] submit to Your Most Serene Highness, and I beg for Your High instructions as to my conduct. It is indeed necessary to come to an arrangement with von Otocki, and if this could be brought about by an amicable settlement, such that Otocki would be content with 1500 or 2000 ℔, it would be an advantageous and desirable thing. I leave it to Your Most Serene Highness's highest [uncertain: consideration] — whether Kunckel is to be given the full right to settle. That, moreover, Kunckel and Jentsch appear to be acting so much at cross purposes with one another arouses my great apprehension — I fear that Kunckel, through his vehement conduct, will do foolish things, and Jentsch, out of malice, will make intrigues, which may prove prejudicial. My humble counsel, which is not to be misapprehended, is to instruct these people well and to keep them within their bounds. Glenck did not come to me again yesterday, and has even left the Kontopp accounts here, which would have given information upon everything in this matter. v Triebenfeld, Breslau, the 23rd of April 1804
