--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Forwards, on the Prince's command, a carte blanche for the reply to Herr von Rewek, to be drawn up at Triebenfeld's discretion, together with an extract from a letter of B. R. Glenck of 22 March 1806. Hahn attributes Glenck's ruin to reckless speculation, frivolity and his wife, and urges Triebenfeld to join the Prince in rescuing him by sending the schema for the consent, which would release Ribbek from the Wrąbczyn purchase and silence Glenck's weekly complaints; Glenck describes pawning goods, selling clothes, lost credit and creditors' suits, and the delayed sale of Wilhelmsruhe.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Supplementarily, I am, by command of the Prince's Serene Highness, to send Your Right Honourable the enclosed carte blanche for the reply to Herr von Rewek[uncertain: Rewek]; may the same have the kindness to have it drawn up according to your own view. I likewise enclose herewith the extract from a letter of B. R. Glenck, as His Serene Highness commanded me. The folly of this man, his ever-active but all the more senseless spirit of speculation, his frivolity and his lady wife have brought him to the brink of the abyss, on which he indeed stands. It now remains reserved both to the magnanimity of the Prince and to that of Your Right Honourable, whether you will once again extend to him the hand of rescue.

His Serene Highness is ready for this, and hereby calls upon the same to act in like manner; and I should have to be ignorant of your magnanimous and readily giving heart, were you not willing to comply with the Prince's wish in this respect, if it is in any way possible. In order to bring this about, it is left to Your Right Honourable to bring the aforesaid Glenck at last to silence by the transmission of the schema for the consent to His Serene Highness. His Serene Highness wishes it also, and for the sake of this man's perpetual lamentations, which arrive with every post-day. Should Glenck once again decline the conditions perhaps necessary

Extract from the letter of Glenck, dated the 22nd of March[uncertain: Mrz] 1806. In the straits in which the Wrąbczyn estate matter and the evasion of payment place me, I have had to take refuge in pawning my belongings, in selling clothes, and in borrowing small sums of a few thalers. My dismissed household servants have cried the noise about still further, and since the public was moreover brought to the opinion that I stood without consequence with Your Serene Highness, a complete [uncertain: Wlisd] credit followed upon me; my numerous creditors grew loud, and I had daily to be shifting about only to borrow now and to avert the judicial suits, and to seek to borrow the most necessary household requirements, since I had to give up my salary for payments of debts, properly speaking as brokerage. Thus I have saved [uncertain: nach] Wilhelmsruhe against the sub-attestation and a low sale, so that out of duty for the [uncertain: Menngen] I must delay the sale until such time as it will fetch a higher

value, inasmuch as I foresee a piece of good fortune here if I can maintain it a while longer; and to that end it belongs that, by producing the consent, I get Ribbek out of the purchase of Wrąbczyn and can leave this estate to a better man. My family needs this protection

for an arrangement, as has already happened, then there is of course nothing left but to let him go fully into the ruin which his swindling spirit has prepared for him. My boundless confidence in Your Right Honourable, and, I may say, the kindness which the same have always shown me, and which has always animated me with true pride and confidence in myself, have brought forth these utterances, and I may hope of the same that you will not construe them ill. With boundless veneration, Öhringen, the 10th of April 1806. A Hahn
