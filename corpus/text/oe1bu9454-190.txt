--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Forwards, on the Prince's command, a carte blanche for the reply to Herr von Rewek, to be drawn up at Triebenfeld's discretion, together with an extract from a letter of B. R. Glenck of 22 March 1806. Hahn attributes Glenck's ruin to reckless speculation, frivolity and his wife, and urges Triebenfeld to join the Prince in rescuing him by sending the schema for the consent, which would release Ribbek from the Wrąbczyn purchase and silence Glenck's weekly complaints; Glenck describes pawning goods, selling clothes, lost credit and creditors' suits, and the delayed sale of Wilhelmsruhe.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Subsequently I am, by command of His Serene Highness the Prince, to send Your Right Honourable the enclosed carte blanche for a reply to Mr. von Rewek[illegible]; may you have the kindness to have it drawn up according to your own view. I also enclose herewith the extract from a letter of the mining councillor Glenck, as His Serene Highness has commanded me. The folly of this man, his ever-active but all the more senseless spirit of speculation, his frivolity and his lady wife have brought him to the edge of the abyss, on which he in truth stands. It remains reserved both to the magnanimity of the Prince and to that of Your Right Honourable whether you will once more extend to him the hand of rescue.

His Serene Highness is ready for this, and hereby calls upon you to act in the same manner; and I should not know your magnanimous, freely giving heart if you were not willing to meet the Prince's wish in this respect, if it is at all possible. In order to bring this about, it is left to Your Right Honourable, by the transmission of the draft form of the consent to His Serene Highness, to bring the said Glenck at last to silence. His Serene Highness wishes it also for the sake of this man's never-ceasing songs of lamentation, which arrive with every post-day. Extract from the letter of Glenck

dated the 22nd of March[illegible] 1806.

In the straits into which the little Wrąbczyn estate and the evasion of payment have put me, I have had to take refuge in pawning my belongings, in selling clothes, and in borrowing a few thalers at a time. My dismissed servants have cried the noise about still further, and since the public too was brought to the opinion that I stood without consequence with Your Serene Highness, there followed a total [uncertain: Wlisd] credit upon me; my numerous creditors grew loud, and day by day I had to do nothing but scrape about to borrow, to avert the suits at law, and to seek to borrow the most necessary household requirements, since I had to give up my salary for payment of debts, properly speaking as brokerage. Thus I have saved Wilhelmsruhe from the attestation and from a low sale, so that out of duty toward [uncertain: the Menngen] I must delay the sale until such time as it will fetch a higher

value, since I foresee a piece of good fortune here if I can hold it for a while longer; and to that end it belongs that, by producing the consent, I get Ribbek out of the purchase of Wrąbczyn and can leave this estate to a better man. My family needs this protection

for an arrangement, as has already happened, then of course nothing remains but to let him fall into the ruin which his swindling spirit has prepared for him. My boundless confidence in Your Right Honourable, and, I may say, the kindness which you have always shown me, and which has always inspired me with true pride and assurance in myself, brought forth these expressions, and I may hope of you that you will not take them ill. With boundless veneration

Öhringen

the 10th of April

1806.

A Hahn
