--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Dictated by the Prince because of inflamed eyes, an urgent plea for ready money before Christmas and for the quick sale of the Zagorowo estates to clear his private debts. General von Schimmelpfennig and his sons have inspected maps and valuations and would exchange their debt-free Szelce and Maderzewo, once bought for 26,000 ducats and now valued near 100,000 Rthl; the General is warned to be sly and grasping. Doubts about terms with Weigel and Stössel, praise for Lieutenant Sommer, whose pay he asks be arranged, complaints of abandoned Polish lawsuits, and news of his daughter Adele's betrothal.

Right Honourable Sir, Highly Honoured Sir Councillor of War and Forests! Your Right Honourable's letter of the 20th of November I received on the 26th; but until today I have had to detain the messenger, who has been paid and whom I have also compensated for his stay, in order to acquaint Your Right Honourable exactly with several occurrences which do indeed seem to me worth being turned to account and which probably, in your judgement too, deserve preference over other plans. Once I have first answered your letter, I shall go into detail upon them so far as seems necessary, partly to portray to you the advantageousness of the newer prospect, partly also to set forth the necessity which alone makes it a law to seize the chance, so as to procure for me partly ready ringing means, partly such objects as are free of debt, and thereby, if only for the moment, to extricate myself from a press which almost crushes me, to gain time, and, after increasing consultation with my children, to create air for myself through further reasonable plans to be adopted, so as to be able to subsist in some tolerable fashion. God keep you in health; I was ailing for some days, but since yesterday it goes better; only my eyes still plague me greatly through their inflammation. Hence I make use of a stranger's pen and dictate this. I believe I have overlooked nothing, nor sinned in the matter through any want of circumstantiality. The wish to make it also quite [uncertain: clear] has led me to abuse your patience, and I therefore beg you, my esteemed friend, to forgive the length of this epistle. It is with the most particular esteem that I remain Your Right Honourable's

Schlawenschitz, the 4th of December 1811.
