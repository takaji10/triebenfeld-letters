--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Too ill to travel for instructions, Triebenfeld reports that the Poles keep using him as negotiator over the sale of the estates while no settled plan exists: rumour has it that 150,000 Rthl of debt on Trąbczyn would pass to Walichnowski, who is also to take on 56,000 Rthl and a yearly charge of 5,000 Rthl. He urges a fixed demand of 60,000-80,000 Rthl purchase money plus 4,000-5,000 Rthl annual hereditary rent on first mortgage, warns of damage to the Prince's honour, and asks that the note be kept from Wünstere.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
I would gladly have attended upon Your Most Serene Highness in person, at my own cost, and obtained your high commands concerning the sale of the estates, but I am very ill and cannot leave my room. The Poles constantly choose me as their man of business, only I do not always know what I am to say, since no [uncertain: under] plan has been drawn up, but the matter is put forward now one way, now another. Thus it is said, for example, that debts of 150000 Rthl are charged upon Trąbczyn, which Walichnowski is to take over, and that notwithstanding this he is to take on 56000 Rthl and a canon of 5000 Rthl yearly.

What there may be in the matter I cannot judge; I hold it, however, to be a misunderstood piece of gossip, and I fear only that Your Serene Highness's honour will suffer by it. For if debts are charged upon the estates, then is it not necessary that this be told to every Pole who wishes to take the

estates in hereditary lease. My unpresuming proposal is this: that it be said to Walichnowski that he must pay 60000 or 80000 Rthl of hereditary purchase money now and, in perpetuity, 4 to 5000 Rthl yearly hereditary rent, and that Your Serene Highness would give order as to when he is to pay the rent, which would remain secured upon the first mortgage. This would then be a firm word, from which on no account must there be any departure.

I know the Poles thoroughly — if one shows a firm tone and resolution, and scorns their offers, especially all bribes, then one is at once at one's goal with this brood.

I have proved this sufficiently in the case of Dombrowchen. Forgive, most gracious Prince and Lord, my frank address. It is, however, truly well meant. May Your Serene Highness deign to take to heart this further request also, and

by no means let this note be seen by Wünstere or by others — it would do harm rather than good — men are often too covetous, so that when an honest man meets them upon these paths, they let him be and do him injury. Command me by two lines in confidence as to how you would have it, and then reckon, most gracious Prince, upon my faithful zeal, which will yet be ten times greater and go still more to my heart than my truly painful sickness. Wallechnowski has in truth a real will to bring the Trąbczyn estates into his own hands.

In deepest submission I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's

Bre[uncertain: slau], the 11th of December 1803

most faithful and most humble and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld
