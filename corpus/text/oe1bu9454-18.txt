--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Written from a violent fever, rejects the instrument and power of attorney the Prince has had drawn up as needless, since a general power of attorney already exists and everything is under attachment. Recounts arrest by the French and payment made as the Prince's representative, and the French General Direction's order restoring 3121 Rthl with costs and interest. Asks instead for a declaration acknowledging 3125 Rthl owed to him, totalling 4219 Rthl, recoverable by a court officer from the arrears of Wrąbczyn, Alt Olesnica, Kopojno, Skokum, Swiątniki, Adelow, Sophienthal and Neu Swięce ahead of the Invalids' Fund; sends his forester by horse and warns all collapses if it does not arrive by the 15th or 16th.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

I was lying in the most violent fever when Your Serene Highness's letter reached me [uncertain: einigung]; the sight of Your hand cheered my [uncertain: Summe]; but how great was my astonishment when I saw the instrument that had been drawn up, together with the power of attorney. Who gave Your Serene Highness this counsel? W here what is to be recovered for Your Serene Highness, that would be done without any such instrument. I have, after all, a general power of attorney for that purpose. For you I unfortunately cannot recover anything, for at this moment everything is laid under attachment. Besides, you were indeed bound to pay the interest to the Invalids' Fund.

I was dragged about in Berlin Collé; when the French held me under arrest I had to pay on Your Serene Highness's behalf, as your authorised representative. When I had been released from arrest I demonstrated the unheard-of and unjust proceeding of the intendant

Maluce and of the Invalids' Fund before the French General Direction — which thereupon ordered that only the 3121 Rthl out of the estates, together with costs and interest, were to be returned. In accordance with this decree it is I who can demand the monies back, not Your Serene Highness, for you are the party owing — I claim the interest, the accrued interest and the costs, which together amount to 4219 Rthl, and I demand that the same be recovered for me out of the arrears and interest of the subjects, upon which the Invalids' Fund has now laid an attachment.

Now in order to facilitate this matter and not first to have to seek out lengthy judicial assistance, Your Serene Highness should, on the advice of the komornik, declare:

that, in accordance with the decree of the French General Government, you still owe to the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld the 3125 Rthl of interest taken from him by compulsion and arrest, which the Invalids' Fund received on 14 February 1808 against a receipt

in receipt, and which the French General Government ordered to be [uncertain: Remtonschen] to the said Triebenfeld out of the revenues of the Zagorowo estate; and that the said von Triebenfeld has on that account not yet been satisfied, neither in respect of the capital he advanced nor of the costs and accrued interest.

Since, then, the capital advanced, together with interest and costs, amounts to 4219 Rthl, you accordingly instruct the said Triebenfeld that he may draw this sum, to the amount of 4219. -, out of the old and new interest payments which the inhabitants of Wrąbczyn, Alt Olesnica, Kopojno, Skokum, Swiątniki, Adelow, Sophienthal and Neu Swięce have to pay, and may satisfy himself out of these funds.

To that end you concede that this above-mentioned sum of 4219 Rthl may forthwith be recovered through a komornik, and consequently by the strictest execution, and be delivered into the hands of the said von Triebenfeld. This the komornik then has the Tribunal

approve, and then sees what he can recover or get hold of out of it ahead of the Invalids' Fund.

This is the only means of rescue for obtaining any money here at present, so as to keep the machine running. I am therefore sending my forester on horseback once again, and I beg Your Serene Highness in the most pressing manner to have the instrument drawn up in the manner described above with the utmost dispatch and to convey it to me by the messenger. I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's

if I do not have the instrument here by the 15th, or at the very latest early on the 16th, then everything comes

to an end and collapses

altogether

Humble and obedient three

Blizanow

the 6th of July 1812

v Triebenfeld
