worden ausbringen mögte. Auf mein artiges Ansuchen erhalten
ich beigehendes Scopptisches Billet.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Confined to bed by illness and barely able to write, Triebenfeld reports that Bornstädt intends to leave, probably out of money, and urges that his claims be settled before he goes. He asks the Prince to order Glenck, who answered his civil request with a curt note, to hand over the lease and settlement contracts and accounts made with Bornstädt, and to offer 5,000 Rthl as a final settlement, against Braun's estimate of 7,500 Rthl plus the honorarium promised in the Betsche purchase.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord, My illness is so obstinate that henceforth I must keep to my bed or to the sofa; because of that I cannot lay myself at Your Serene Highness's feet and speak with the same most highly about the business; even writing comes very hard to me.

In the enclosed letter Bornstädt says that he wishes to depart. Presumably his wise money is all [gone]. I therefore had Glenck asked whether he might not come to me and bring out the papers, or properly speaking the various lease and settlement contracts which have been concluded with Bornstädt. In answer to my civil request I received the accompanying Scopptisch note.

To let the man travel off without settling the matters is not good; the matter must be settled, for by this means one strikes the enemy on 3 sides. My humble petition is that Your Serene Highness command Mr. Glenck to send me the contracts, settlements and accounts which have been negotiated with Bornstädt. And then that Your Serene Highness should say, in but a couple of lines, that the same most highly, out of especial grace and in order to make an end of everything, is willing to give Bornstädt 5000 Rthl.

According to Braun's opinion he must have 7500 Rthl, without the honorarium promised to him in the Betsche purchase business. If therefore one could get clear of everything with 5000 Rthl, one would be making [uncertain: non solides fude].

When Your Most Serene Highness comes to me I shall lay the pieces before the same most highly. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's Breslau, the 12th September 1804. faithful most humble and obedient servant v. Triebenfeld
