--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Confined to bed or sofa by an obstinate illness and finding even writing difficult, Triebenfeld reports Bornstädt's enclosed announcement that he intends to depart, his money presumably spent. Glenck, asked to bring out the lease and settlement contracts made with Bornstädt, sent only a curt note; Triebenfeld petitions the Prince to order Glenck to surrender the contracts, settlements and accounts, and to authorise in writing a final payment of 5000 Rthl, Braun reckoning Bornstädt's claim at 7500 Rthl besides the honorarium promised in the Betscher purchase.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord!

My illness is so obstinate that henceforward I must keep to the bed or the sofa; on that account I cannot lay myself at Your Serene Highness's feet and speak with the same most highly concerning the business; even writing comes very hard to me. In the enclosed letter Bornstadt says that he means to depart; presumably his wise money is all gone. I therefore had Glenck asked whether he might not come to me and bring out the papers, or rather the various lease and settlement contracts which have been concluded with Bornstädt. Upon my courteous request I received the accompanying Scopptian note. To let the man travel without settling the matters is not good; the matter must be settled, for by this means one strikes the enemy on 3 sides. My humble petition is that Your Serene Highness command Herr Glenck to send me the contracts, settlements and accounts which have been negotiated with Bornstädt. And then, that Your Serene Highness say, if only in a couple of lines, that the same most highly, out of particular grace and in order to make an end of everything, will give Bornstadt 5000 Rthl.

According to Braun's opinion he must have 7500 Rthl, without the honorarium promised him in the Betscher purchase business. If then one could get away with 5000 Rthl in all, one would be making non solides fude. When Your Most Serene Highness comes to me, I shall lay the pieces before the same most highly. In deepest reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's

Breslau, the 12th of 7br. 1804.

most faithful and most humble and obedient servant
v Triebenfeld
