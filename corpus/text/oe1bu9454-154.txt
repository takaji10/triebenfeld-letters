--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Reports the loan negotiation proceeding well and under examination; denies Wollenhaupt's talk of a sequestration, noting that Lichnowski has lifted the attachment on the interest for six weeks and that Grimm assures him the attachment on Magnuski's account is also lifted, though Przespolewski has no claim to 30000 Rthl and the Dębinska has newly attached payments to Magnuske. Denounces Blomberg's letter of 25 December as extortionate threats over the dismemberment, and describes the sharp rebuke sent him.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord!

The matter of the loan is going forward in the best way; God grant that no new obstacles arise. It lies for examination with a man who is otherwise sound.

Everything that Your Serene Highness may be pleased to charge me with under the 13th I shall faithfully attend to. As for what Wollenhaupt has prattled about a sequestration, that is pure [uncertain: Lage]; on the contrary, Lichnowski has lifted the attachment on account of the interest for 6 weeks. The attachment on account of Magnuski has, according to Grimm's assurance, likewise been lifted; with the next post I shall meanwhile write to him. In the meantime I do not properly understand the whole business: Przespolewski surely has no 30000 Rthl to demand, and the attachment obtained by Magnuski has surely been lifted. For the rest, the Dębinska woman has only laid an attachment again, to the effect that nothing is to be paid out to Magnuske. – This will not happen in any case. Blomberg has written a letter under the 25th of December, consequently earlier than the one which was answered to him by express,

to me, which has followed me hither through the post. This letter is of such a content – that Blomberg infamously incriminates himself and must sit at least 10 years in the fortress. No robber who throws to the ground the wanderer in the wilderness, stripped of all means of defence, could make such base demands so entirely without shame as this honour-forgetting man does. In plain words he says today besides — that if all this were not punctually given to him, then nothing should come of the dismemberment; but if he obtained what he demands, then he would himself carry the matter through — Moreover, he means to go on shooting about with me until one of the 7 [illegible] fetches. Here, however, he meets his master, for today he already receives the full [uncertain: Laage] – I have given him notice that if he should let himself presume to speak so much as one more word, or to meddle in the matter without my knowledge, I should know how to chastise him – for with a villain, which he has stamped himself to be, I would have no pity, and woe to him if he now stirred himself — He will assuredly be very meek.

in the [uncertain: leuteten] submission I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's

Berlin, the 15th of January

1805

most faithful servant

v. Triebenfeld
