# Plan: the newspapers

Status: **the section agreed with the editor 2026-10-22** ("Let's make a new
section for newspaper articles, since it doesn't really fall under archival
sources or literature"); the first item brought in, shown on the site,
**not yet published** (publishing waits for the editor's word, as always).

Newspaper items stand between the edition's two existing sections and belong
to neither:

- **not holdings.** The Sources page is for primary sources from archives;
  a clipping has no archive, no scans to show, no marks of doubt, no line by
  line review. It never counts in the edition's figures (documents, pages,
  marks of doubt), never appears in the document lists, the browse page or
  the timeline.
- **not literature.** A work of the literature is *about* the history; a
  newspaper item is *of* it, a witness of its own day. (The LITERATURE_PLAN's
  test would have made a newspaper item recording events a holding, like
  Kuryer Polski 1754; the editor ruled otherwise for this section, and the
  question whether Kuryer Polski 1754 should move here from the Sources page
  is open — NEEDS_CONFIRMATION, "The newspapers".)

The site has a third section: the menu carries **Newspapers** ("Zeitungen")
between Sources and Literature; /newspapers/ lists the items by year, each
with its own page (/de/zeitungen/ in German). The first two items are the
"Z Zagórowa" column of Dziennik Poznański, R. 79, nr 180 (7 August 1937),
and the Kuryer Polski's funeral report of 1754, moved here from the Sources
page (section 5).

## 1. Where an item lives

```
newspapers/<slug>/
  item.yml     bibliography: the newspaper, the issue, the date, the language,
               the item's title and title_en, kind, era, visibility, rights,
               the account of what the item is (EN and DE), a detailed summary
               of the contents (EN and DE), who made the English, the scan's
               source (`scan:`) where there is one
  original.md  the transcription, line by line as printed, "¬" at a word
               broken at the line's end (the Kuryer Polski convention); the
               file opens with comment lines, as corpus/literature/ does
  english.md   the English, made in a session, not checked whole
  notes.md     how it came in, how it was read, every ruling of the editor,
               what was left out, what it bears on, the rights
```

The slug is `<papertitle><year><issue>` without spaces: `kuryerpolski1754075`
if it comes here, `dziennikpoznanski19370807` as it stands. The page files
under `site/` are generated by `pipeline/newspapers/newspapers_site.py` (a
step of regenerate.py, after the literature steps); never edit them by hand.

## 2. How an item is read

No transcription is expected. The text is read from the scan and kept as
printed: the line division as printed, the print's punctuation (a period
mid-sentence stands), abbreviations left alone, **nothing tidied** — the
transcription rule (EDITORIAL_RULES.md) as the literature keeps it. What the
readings cannot settle goes to the editor on a spot sheet in the
pipeline/review/queries.py style (see `scratch/dp1937_sheet.py` for the
stand-alone form, `units/kuryerpolski1754075/intake/spot_sheet.py` for the
unit form); the rulings are recorded in the item's notes.md. The English is
Claude's, made in the session; the item's page says so.

**OCR in the project** (since 2026-10-22, at the editor's word): Tesseract
5.5.3 (winget `tesseract-ocr.tesseract`) with the Polish models in
`%LOCALAPPDATA%\Tesseract\tessdata`, wrapped by `pipeline/ocr.py` —
`read(image)` for the text, `words(image)` for word boxes with confidences.
Read a page as the **agreement of several passes** (two upscalings, two page
segmentation modes, and the standard model beside tessdata_best); a pass that
disagrees with the rest is a witness against itself, not a reading. Where the
pass disagreements stop, the spot sheet takes over. The Windows.Media.Ocr
engine (pl) is a third witness on whole words only; it is too rough to
transcribe from.

The engine reports what it cannot read with confidence; its word confidences
(`words()`) are the first sorter of what goes to the sheet.

## 3. On the site

- **The list page** (/newspapers/): each item under its newspaper's name,
  with the issue's citation as the link, its language, its account, and
  "Read the item".
- **The item's page**: the title (a description of the item, as a holding's
  title is), the issue's citation as the standfirst, the facts (newspaper,
  published, language), the account, the caveat that the English is
  Claude's and unchecked (`translation: editor` in item.yml changes the
  caveat to "the editor's own", as the Kuryer Polski's is), and a link to
  where the item comes from (`source:` in item.yml — the digitisation or,
  for a clipping, the blog or archive that published it).
- **The item's picture** (`image:` in item.yml, the file in
  `site/assets/newspapers/<slug>/`): shown above the texts, linked to the
  full file — the clipping itself (the editor, 2026-10-22: "The site
  should carry the clipping"), or the page of a digitised issue. The file
  is committed: the item is not a holding, and its picture is not part of
  the scans pipeline (`site/assets/scans/` is regenerated from `pages/`,
  which a newspaper has no part in).
- **The texts**: one at a time, the English first, by the literature's
  buttons (assets/literature.js); the original line by line as printed.
- **Kept off the site** (`visible: false`, the rule of the literature,
  LITERATURE_PLAN section 4): listed, its page holding only the detailed
  summary of its contents (`summary_en`, `summary_de` in item.yml, both
  required or the build stops); no stretch of its text anywhere on the site
  (verify_site.py holds the built site to that).

## 4. The index of people and places

The editor's word (2026-10-22): "The people/places index should run over
the items." `pipeline/newspapers/newspapers_index.py --check` (a step of
regenerate.py, after newspapers_site.py) runs the edition's patterns for
people and places (`pipeline/build/entities.py`) over the originals of the
visible items — the literature's index machinery
(`pipeline/literature/literature_index.py`), with two differences:

- an item is one page, so a mention links the item's page with no anchor;
- **an item's uid is its slug with "-1"** (`kuryerpolski1754075-1`), the
  shape a document's uid has: the register's `only_in` filters pin the
  spellings they were written for to that uid ("Pawłá Prusinskie",
  "Rozrazeskich Prusimskiey" of the Kuryer Polski), and the alias keeps
  them working for the item that left the corpus.

The rulings are in `newspapers/index.yml`, one per name and spelling
(`--sheet` writes every hit in context to review/newspapers/mentions.md;
`--check` fails on a hit with no ruling, so a new item cannot bring unread
hits to the site). Kept hits go to `corpus/newspapers/mentions.json` (for
questions) and `site/_data/newspapers_mentions.yml` — the box "In the
newspapers" on a person's entry on /people/ and an estate's on /places/,
beside the literature's, closed by default.

**A person of the newspapers can name no document** (Głowiecki: the report
of 1754 is his only witness, and it left the corpus): such a person stands
on /people/ all the same, with the newspapers' mentions beside them and no
documents shown (build_site_data, the people list; the counts line and the
document list are left out when there are no documents).

**The Dziennik Poznański clipping** comes from the blog **Zagórów w
historii** (its post of 2 October 2024, "Kolejne wycinki z prasy sprzed
lat, cz. 3"), by the editor's word, and the rights are approved by them.
Its thirteen doubtful readings were ruled on a spot sheet
(`newspapers/dziennikpoznanski19370807/notes.md` has them all).

**The Słowo notice** (2026-10-22) is the first item read from a
digitised issue rather than a clipping: the editor named the article
("Sprzedaże dóbr", Słowo R. XI, nr 2 of 4 January 1892, p. 3, the Crispa
digitisation), gave their own crop of the column (`3-DPI300.jpg`), and
ruled "only this snippet of the article is needed" — the notice's five
printed lines, two sales, one of them the edition's own estate: Trąbczyn
sold by Count Miączyński to Countess Władysławowa Wielopolska for 225,000
rs., the press witness of the transfer the wlodzimierz era's ownership
line records ("by 1892 Countess Maria Wielopolska"). Read by Tesseract
(two passes as the page is, one upscaled) with word zooms; five readings
ruled on a spot sheet — among them the editor's "150 włók" against the
draft's 159, and "peckim" against the draft's "peckiu"
(`newspapers/slowo18920104/notes.md`). The item belongs to the wlodzimierz
era; its one index hit is Trąbczyn, ruled. Open: whether the press's
"Władysławowa Wielopolska" (the wife of a Władysław) and the era's
"Countess Maria Wielopolska" name one woman (NEEDS_CONFIRMATION, "The
newspapers").
