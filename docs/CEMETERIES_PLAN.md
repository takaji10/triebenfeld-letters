# Plan: the cemeteries

Status: **built and published 2026-10-26** (the editor's word "Go ahead
and merge into main and publish"); the five gazetteer entries drafted and
waiting, the visits' exact date open. The material is the
editor's own survey of the Evangelical (Lutheran) cemeteries of the
Trąbczyn/Zagórów area and its neighbourhood, visited on the ground, each
one's condition noted and its headstones read as far as they read
(`cemeteries.md`, the editor's file, kept verbatim in
`reference/cemeteries/source/`). Ten cemeteries, about 200 headstones read,
all ten one visit, in 2023 (the editor's word; the survey's own footnote
names the year).

The section is a fourth of its own, like the newspapers (`NEWSPAPERS_PLAN`)
and the places (`PLACES_PLAN`):

- **not holdings.** No archive, no scans, no marks of doubt; it never counts
  in the edition's figures (documents, pages, marks of doubt), never appears
  in the document lists, the browse page or the timeline.
- **not literature and not newspapers.** The survey is not a printed witness
  of its day nor a work about the history; it is the editor's own field
  record — the gazetteer's kind of material, the editor's own work on the
  ground (GAZETTEER_PLAN, "Place Names.md"), brought in as data.
- **no person entries for the people the stones name** (the editor's word,
  2026-10-26): the headstone readings are the record, in tables, and stand
  as they are read.

## 1. Where a cemetery lives

```
cemeteries/<slug>/
  cemetery.yml     the heading (EN and DE), the place the cemetery belongs
                   to (section 3), the graves counted, the visit's date,
                   the account of the cemetery and of its condition
                   (EN and DE), the sources the survey cites
  headstones.yml   the headstones, one entry each, as read (section 2)
  notes.md         how the survey came in, every ruling of the editor,
                   what the cemetery bears on
```

The slug is the cemetery's own, plain: `lazinsk-pierwszy`,
`huta-lazinska`, `michalinow-trabczynski`, `wrabczynkowskie-holendry`,
`orlina-duza`, `anielewo`, `friedrichsfeld`, `michalinow-olesnicki`,
`stawiszyn`, `zbiersk-kolonia`. It lives in its own URL space
(/cemeteries/<slug>/, /de/friedhoefe/<slug>/), so it collides with no
place's slug.

The page files under `site/` are generated by
`pipeline/cemeteries/cemetery_pages.py` (a step of `regenerate.py`, beside
the places' and the newspapers' builders; never edit them by hand). It
writes `site/_data/cemeteries.yml` (committed, like the other generated
site data), `site/_cemeteries/<slug>.md` and `site/_cemeteries_de/
<slug>.md`, collection `cemeteries` / `cemeteries_de`, layout `cemetery`
built on the place layout: the eyebrow back to the list page, the heading,
the facts (graves counted, visited, the place it belongs to), the account,
the names table, and the sources cited. The list page (/cemeteries/,
/de/friedhoefe/) lists the ten by their places, the Trąbczyn area first
and the outliers after, each with its grave count and its account — the
order is the editor's eye (section 5).

**The editor's descriptions kept as written.** The condition paragraphs of
the survey are the editor's own prose, like the gazetteer's entries: their
text is kept as written (EDITORS_VOICE, the gazetteer precedent), the
German made by Claude from it. Field notes speak of "I visited" and "a
closer inspection revealed"; the page says what a reader needs and the
editor's wording stands unless they rule otherwise.

**The survey's sources.** The survey carries two footnotes. The first is
the Evangelical-Augsburg Parish in Konin's information signs of 2018,
cited after Słowiński and Połom (p. 53 — the edition's
`slowinskipolom2024`, on the site as a summary). The second stands behind
one headstone reading of Huta Łazińska and is named by the editor
(2026-10-26): "**Der evangelische Friedhof in Łazińsk Drugi** [The
Protestant cemetery in Łazińsk Drugi]", Arbeitsgemeinschaft
Ostdeutscher Familienforscher, https://agoff.de/?p=83943 — the article is
by Jürgen Frantz, 31 July 2016, and is reserved to AGOFF's registered
members (the page says so on its face; the editor believes a password is
behind it, and the fetch from the session reached the notice and not the
article). It is in `docs/MISSING_SOURCES.md`: cited, not held. The same
site carries the series the article belongs to — the headstone database's
cemetery pages — among them "Der evangelische Friedhof in Tarszewo"
(Friedrichsfeld) and "Der evangelische Friedhof in Chruściki" (Adelhof),
the two places the Friedrichsfeld survey names; whether the edition wants
them is the editor's (NEEDS_CONFIRMATION, "The cemeteries"). The citations
are given on the cemetery's page, as the place pages give theirs.

## 2. The headstone tables

Each cemetery's page carries its names as a table, the site's table code
(`unitlib.render_table`), one row per headstone:

| Name (as read) | Born | Died | Note |
|---|---|---|---|

- **Nothing tidied** (EDITORIAL_RULES, the transcription rule): the reading
  stands as the survey has it — the bracketed letters ("Sch\[mechel?\]"),
  the "\[?\]" of the unread, the "\*" of the unread first letters, the
  headstones' own spellings ("Glige", "Zelmer", "Schmeichel"), the date
  gaps ("b. \[?\].04.1926"). The names are readings of German
  inscriptions; they are not translated, and the English and German pages
  carry the table alike.
- **The quoted inscriptions** ("Er war Kantor 34 Jahre in Lazinsk",
  "unser geliebtes Söhnchen und Brüderchen") go in the note column, as
  printed in the survey.
- **Two kinds of mark in the survey are not yet explained** and are kept
  until the editor says what they are (section 5): the numbers in
  asterisks — *(267)*, *(316)*, *(604)* — and the figure references
  "(Fig. 25)" beside five readings. If the figures are photographs of the
  headstones and exist as files, the pages can carry them, as the
  newspapers carry their clippings (`image:` in cemetery.yml); the
  editor's standing "no pictures" rulings for the press items were the
  items' own, not a rule for this section.
- **The graves counted** (7 at Łazińsk Pierwszy, 83 at Michalinów
  Trąbczyński, 57 at Orlina Duża, and so on) stand above the table with
  the survey's own qualification ("plus some without nameplates"); the
  table is only what was read, and a cemetery with fewer readings than
  graves says so by its figures.
- **Unreadable stones are rows too**: "(unreadable)" where the survey
  records one, so the table's length and the grave count can be set side
  by side.
