# Plan: the place pages

Status: **the section agreed with the editor 2026-10-22** ("Let's add a new
source to a place: Zagórów. This site has a lot of information that should be
added under the place name, on a separate page for the place"); the first
place and its first source are on the site, **not yet published** (publishing
waits for the editor's word, as always).

The places so far have had no page of their own: the Places page
(/places/, /de/orte/) shows the estates in focus as cards, each with its
medieval spellings, its later official names (`renamed:` in
reference/places.yml), its documents and its "In the literature" and "In the
newspapers" boxes. The editor's ruling makes a second thing: **a page per
place** that has sources of its own, hung on the place's card and linked
from it — as the person pages hang on the People page
(LITERATURE_PLAN, section 22).

The section sits between the edition's existing sections, and the editor
put it there:

- **not holdings.** The Sources page is for primary sources from archives.
  A source brought in for a place never counts in the edition's figures
  (documents, pages, marks of doubt), never appears in the document lists,
  the browse page or the timeline.
- **not the newspapers.** A newspaper item is a witness *of* its day
  (NEWSPAPERS_PLAN, the section's own account). The first source here — the
  town's own presentation of itself on its municipality's site — is of no
  day; it is the place speaking about itself, whenever it was written.
- **not literature** in the section's sense either (works about the
  history, kept page by page for questions); the place page shows a source
  whole, as the newspapers show an item whole. The literature's and
  newspapers' rulings it follows where they fit: the transcription as
  printed, nothing tidied; the detailed summary of the contents kept even
  when the source is shown whole; a work not shown holds a summary page
  (LITERATURE_PLAN section 4).

## 1. Where a place and its sources live

```
places/<place-slug>/          one place that has its own page
  place.yml                   the page: the heading, what the place is, what
                              the page is (EN and DE)
  <source-slug>/              one source brought in for the place
    source.yml                bibliography: the site, the address and the
                              capture, the title, the language, the kind,
                              the era, visibility, the account of what the
                              source is (EN and DE), the detailed summary
                              (EN and DE), who made the English, the link
                              to where it can be seen
    original.md               the transcription, as printed, nothing tidied
    english.md                the English, made in a session, not checked
                              whole
    notes.md                  how it came in, how it was read, every ruling
                              of the editor, what it bears on, the rights
```

**The place-slug is the place's slug in reference/places.yml** — the same
slug its card on the Places page carries (`zagorowo`). The build fails
loudly if it is no estate of the edition, so a misspelt slug cannot make an
orphan page. The source-slug follows the newspapers' rule for a web item
(`<source><year><date>`, the date standing in the issue's place):
`zagorowpl20220614`.

The page files under `site/` are generated by
`pipeline/places/place_pages.py` (a step of pipeline/regenerate.py, after
build_site_data.py, beside person_pages.py); never edit them by hand. It
writes `site/_data/places_pages.yml` (committed, like the other generated
site data), `site/_places/<slug>.md` (/places/<slug>/) and
`site/_places_de/<slug>.md` (/de/orte/<slug>/), collection `places` /
`places_de`, layout `place` (site/_layouts/place.html). A source kept off
the site needs `summary_en` and `summary_de` in its `source.yml`, or the
build stops.

## 2. How a source is read

The transcription rule (EDITORIAL_RULES.md) as the newspapers keep it: the
text taken from the page as it prints, **nothing tidied**; the print's
slips stand and are recorded in the source's `notes.md`. A web page has no
printed line division; the paragraphs stand whole. The viewer's or site's
own furniture (instruction lines, navigation) is not part of the source's
text and is not kept — recorded in the notes. The photographs are not
carried (the editor, 2026-10-22, for the first source); their places are
marked "[Obraz]" / "[Picture]", the convention of the literature's figures.
The English is Claude's, made in the session; the standing caveat says so
on the page. No paid run is needed and none was made.

## 3. On the site

- **The card on the Places page** carries a line "More about the place"
  ("Mehr über den Ort") linking the place's page, where the Places page
  has one (`site/_includes/places.html`, driven by places_pages.yml).
- **The place's page** (/places/zagorowo/, /de/orte/zagorowo/): the
  eyebrow back to the Places page, the heading, what the place is
  (standfirst), the line to the place's card on the Places page, the short
  account of what the page is, and the sources — each a section with its
  title (a description of the source, as a holding's title is), its facts
  (published, language), the account of what it is, the caveat that the
  English is Claude's and unchecked, the link to where it can be seen (the
  Wayback Machine's capture), and the text: one text at a time, the English
  first, by the literature's buttons — one viewbar for the whole page, so
  every source's two texts change together.
- **Kept off the site** (`visible: false`): listed in places_pages.yml
  with `summary_only: true`, its section holding only the detailed summary
  of its contents, in both languages; no stretch of its text anywhere on
  the site (pipeline/verify_site.py holds the built site to that, as it does for the
  newspapers).

## 4. The checks

`pipeline/verify_site.py` (the places block, after the person pages'): every place
of places_pages.yml has its page in both languages; the page carries a
source section; each source shown whole has its text on the page (the
longest line of its original.md, a 60-character window, as the newspapers'
leak check works); and each language's Places page links the page.

## 7. The widening to a general index (ruled 2026-10-26, not yet built)

The editor's ruling of 2026-10-26: **the Places page transitions from
"the estates in focus" to a general index of places** — tier 1 the places
related to Trąbczyn/Zagórów and nearby, tier 2 everything else — because
eventually each place will have its own page. The cemeteries
(`docs/CEMETERIES_PLAN.md`) are the first driver: their ten places are
villages and colonies, no one of them an estate, and the section's pages
need the widened page to hang on.

What the ruling keeps and what it changes:

- **The estates in focus are not touched.** They serve the Hohenlohe-era
  letters and stand as the first tier of cards they are; the letters'
  metadata (the estates each document names, the counts, the Places
  page's present list) does not change.
- **Tier 1, tier 2.** The index has two tiers: tier 1 the places of
  Trąbczyn, Zagórów and their neighbourhood — the ground the edition's
  documents move in — tier 2 every other place the edition records
  (Berlin, Posen, Ingelfingen, and the rest of the register's far list).
  The tiers are the Places page's display order, not a new field of the
  register's entries; the register's own `tier:` (Book-confirmed,
  Text-confirmed) is the evidence grade and stays what it is.
- **Eventually, a page per place.** The widened page is built so that any
  place of the edition can take a page, the way Zagórów has one; a place
  gets its page when it has content for one (the cemeteries' ten first).
  `pipeline/places/place_pages.py` today refuses a slug that is no
  estate; that check widens to accept any place with a page's own
  content.
- **Not yet built.** The widening is recorded here as ruled and comes
  with the cemeteries' build (CEMETERIES_PLAN, section 4). The shape the
  widened page takes — the tiers as two lists, the estates' cards first —
  is Claude's to propose and the editor's eye on the built page, as the
  sections' machinery has gone so far.

## 8. The page per place and the remade Places page (ruled 2026-10-27)

The editor's ruling of 2026-10-27: **every place gets its own page**, and the
Places page is remade as a plain alphabetical index. The ruling in parts:

1. **A page per place.** Every place of the index (the 257 of
   `site/_data/places_index.yml`) takes `/places/<slug>/` and
   `/de/orte/<slug>/`, whether or not a gazetteer entry stands behind it.
   A page shows, in order:
   - the heading — **"Polish (German)"** for the Hohenlohe colonies that
     had a German name originally and later a Polish one
     ("Olechowo (Sophienthal)"), the plain name otherwise;
   - the kind of place and, where the gazetteer describes it, its
     situation (the location line);
   - the **description**: the gazetteer's `text_en` — the editor's own
     account where one exists and the citation sentences made from the
     corrected Słownik / Nazwy / Kodeks citations, with the footnotes
     resolved (a `linked` ref a link to the document, a `missing` ref
     the plain citation);
   - the **dated name-forms** (the gazetteer's `names:`, each with its
     footnote);
   - the **OpenStreetMap view** — an embedded map with a marker — for
     the places that can be identified with confidence; the rest get no
     map box;
   - the **document hits** last: the documents of the edition that name
     the place, by the same list the estates' cards have shown.
   The one authored place page so far (Zagórów, with its zagorow.pl
   source shown whole) keeps its shape and gains the gazetteer's
   description block; the authored sources hang below it.
2. **The Places page becomes a plain index.** No "Estates in Focus"
   framing, no cards, no sort buttons. Two alphabetical lists — tier 1
   the places of the Trąbczyn/Zagórów area together with every name of
   the editor's original list of 112 (the match run's places), tier 2
   everything else (the Hohenlohe estates elsewhere, the places of
   writing, the register's far list) — each item the place's name and
   its kind, linking its page.
3. **The estates' information stays in the letters.** Nothing is removed
   from the Hohenlohe-era letters' pages, where the estate a document
   concerns is part of understanding it. Only the Places page stops
   presenting it as a framing.
4. **The estate anchors move.** Document pages link
   `/places/#estate-<slug>` today; with the cards gone those links
   retarget to the place's own page `/places/<slug>/` — the better
   destination, and no anchor is lost that a reader was relying on.

### How it is built

**Phase 0 — the data (the long pole).**
- *Coordinates.* The gazetteer's `location:` gains `lat`/`lon` where the
  place can be identified with confidence: the 42 estates already carry
  coordinates in `site/_data/estates.yml`; the Zagórów-area places are
  georeferenced from the edition's own maps (Perthées 1798, Gilly 1802,
  Richter 1843, the Messtischblatt of 1940) and the Słownik's own
  distances ("N versts from Słupca"), checked against the modern map.
  A place that cannot be pinned down gets no map, not a guessed one.
  The far places (Berlin, Wien, Breslau) are trivially identified and
  get their coordinates like any other.
- *German names.* A `german:` field enters the gazetteer entry and the
  register for the colonies named in German first — the six renamings
  of 1891 recorded on theSG's t. XV (Sophienthal→Olechowo,
  Friedrichsfeld→Tarszewo, Ludwigslust→Mały Las, Adolfsberg→Zalesie,
  Adelhof→Chruściki, Ingelfingen→Anielewo) and any further Hohenlohe
  colony the review data shows named in German. The heading renders
  "Polish (German)" from the pair.
- *The tier-1 manifest.* The editor's 112, as provided, listed
  explicitly (the match run's slugs, `gradzen` reading as `gradzyn`
  since the merge of 2026-10-27), united with the places whose `area:`
  is `trabczyn-zagorow`; stored once, consumed by the site data build.

**Phase 1 — the generator.** `pipeline/places/place_pages.py` widens
from "the authored places" to "every place of the index": an authored
`places/<slug>/` keeps its sources machinery; every other place's page
is generated from the gazetteer entry, the register and the document
mentions, with no authored file required. The German/Polish heading
pair, the kinds in both languages, the map embed (the plain
openstreetmap.org embed, no JavaScript beyond the iframe, a link
beside it for printing) and the document-hit list come from the site
data the build already makes. The German pages render the name, the
kind and the index in German; the description stands in English,
marked `lang="en"`, until the editor rules on translating 120 texts.

**Phase 2 — the Places page.** `site/_includes/places.html` loses the
cards, the sort bar and the estates framing; the two tiers render
alphabetically from `places_index.yml`, each item name + kind + link.
The letters' and documents' place links retarget from
`#estate-<slug>` to the pages.

**Phase 3 — the checks and the publish.** `pipeline/verify_site.py` gains the
rule the ruling implies: every item of the index has a page in both
languages, and no built page links a dead `#estate-` anchor. The whole
chain (build_site_data, place_pages, gazetteer checker) green, then
publish as always.

**What a page without a gazetteer entry shows.** Some 137 of the
index's places have no gazetteer entry — mostly far places named in
the documents (Breslau, Warschau, and the like). Their page is the
heading, the kind, the map where identification is trivial, and the
document hits; the gazetteer can be extended to them later, one
session at a time.
