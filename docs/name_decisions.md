**`Sakkin` resolved by the biography, on your instruction to follow it.** Seidel
uses `Sacken` ×55 (as *von der Osten-Sacken*) against a single `Sachen`, which is
the ordinary word. The book also names her outright — *"Fürstin Christiane
Charlotte Sophie von der Osten-Sacken (1733–1811)"* — matching L18192's "die
Fürstin Sakkin … auch nach ihrem [Tod]" in letters written after her death. So
these three are the Fürstin, not *Sachen*.

**Update:** `Sakken` ×13 (including `Sakkensche(n)`) was subsequently merged into
`Sacken` on your instruction, as part of the §H batch.

**And the `Sachen` half of the question turned out to be real.** Surfaced while
checking letter 74d: the corpus carries **8 instances of `Sachen` preceded by a
noble title** — `Fürstin Sachen`, `der verstorbenen Fürstin von Sachen`,
`Nachlas der Fürsten von Sachen` — which are the Fürstin, not the ordinary word.
Your original instinct that the name appears as *Sachen* was correct; following
the biography simply makes `Sacken` the canonical form for all of them. Corrected
2026-09-03 with a **title-anchored** substitution, never a bare whole-word
replace: the other **116** `Sachen` in the corpus are the ordinary German word
and were left untouched. `Sacken` now stands at **25**.

## Still open — investigated, needs your eye

**`Holland` / `Holländer` / `Hauländer` — context splits three ways.**
`L216` settles the term: *"die Hauländer oder Collonisten"*. So:

| Line | Text | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 539 | "die Hessen, Hannoveraner, und Holländer" | **Dutch** — correct, leave |
| 7842 | "1 Holländer Hufe" | settler context → likely `Hauländer` |
| 7843 | "von den Besitzern zweyer Holländer acquiriret" | settler context → likely `Hauländer` |
| 14718 | "der Pol Bauren und Holländer" | settler context → likely `Hauländer` |

`Holland` the country (L31, L32, L37 — "Geld aus Holland") is correct throughout.

**`Schlawen` ×6** — used exactly as a place ("nach Schlawen", "über Schlawen").
Plausibly short for `Schlawenschitz`, but a writer may simply abbreviate, so not
proposed. Needs the scan.

**`Stege` (L180)** — "ist Pochammer nach Stege". Reads as a place, not a person.
Note `Steegemann` does not occur in the corpus at all; the name here is
`Stägemann`. Left alone.

**`Wünster` ×3** (L112, L136, L140) — "der Hofrath Wünster", "H. Wünster".
A person, spelled identically three times. That consistency is the writer's, not
a slip. Not `Münster`. Left alone; belongs in the who's-who instead.

**`Franckfurten` (L277)** — the line reads "Franckfurten u Main", i.e. *Frankfurt
am Main*. Root-only that gives `Frankfurten`, keeping the ending as project
policy requires. Flagged rather than applied.

**`Märck` (L38, corpus line 2827)** — you are checking this against the scan.
The line reads `Märck Landschafts Obligationen nicht 500 Rthl geben wolte.`
`Märdz` does not parse there, but **`Märkische Landschafts-Obligationen`** does —
a real Prussian instrument (Kurmärkische Landschaft bonds), and this letter is
about exactly that kind of paper. So `Märck` may be an abbreviation, `Märck.`
for *Märkische*, and correct as written. **Open pending your reading.**
