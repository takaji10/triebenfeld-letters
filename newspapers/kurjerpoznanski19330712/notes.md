## Provenance

The clipping comes from the blog **ZAGÓRÓW-MIEJSCE Z HISTORIĄ** ("Zagórów -
a place with history", zagorowzhistoria.com), the same blog the Dziennik
Poznański clipping of 1937 came from: its post of 24 May 2024, "To i owo,
cz. 2" (this and that, part 2), the editor's word. The blog is the item's
`source:`, shown on its page. The clipping is the column's own width; it
carries no page number and no issue number, and the citation keeps the date
only (the editor's word, 2026-10-22: the date stands in the issue's place).

The item is the newspapers' **ninth** (the plan's section 5), the fourth from
this blog, and the first witness in the press of the German colonists'
"hitleryzacja" - a year into Hitler's Germany, seven years before the
Ostdeutscher Beobachter's feature on Łazińsk of 1942 describes the same
population as a "purely German settlement".

## How it was read

No transcription existed. There was no paid run. The text was read as the
agreement of four passes over the whole clipping (`pipeline/ocr.py`:

1. Tesseract 5.5.3, `pol` from tessdata_best, the clipping upscaled ×3, psm 6;
2. the same, ×4;
3. the same, psm 4, ×4;
4. Tesseract with the standard `pol` model, ×4;

plus three passes on the first line alone, ×8, psm 7 and psm 8, best and
standard). The passes agree on almost every word; the body's lines sit about
26 pixels apart, with the bold lead line double that height.

What the passes left open was settled at the pixel level first (thresholded
ASCII renderings of the places, and ×12-×16 zooms), which fixed: the
ogonek of "Trąbczyn" (line 3; the print has it, every pass dropped it), the
ł of "nosiły" and its final y, the ogonek of "języku" and of "uczęszczające",
the 'g' with its descender loop in "ewangelik" (two passes read "selik"),
the **period** after "Niemców" (so "Zamieszkiwane" opens its sentence), the
**comma** after "Hitlera", the **period** after "przekręciło", and the
Polish quotes „…” round "dzień dobry", closed with a period.

## What it bears on

- **Emilienhain.** The print's name of the first colony is "Emilienhain"
  (the ×16 zoom: a bowl-a and a two-stem n, not the e and m of "heim"). The
  edition's documents carry the colony as **Emilienheim** (Breyer's notebook
  APP 53/968/0/-/716 on Łazińsk, the Ostdeutscher Beobachter's feature of
  1942 beside it). The item keeps its name as printed and the difference is
  flagged (NEEDS_CONFIRMATION, "The newspapers"): the documents first, the
  item transcribed as printed.
- **Ludwigslust** is matched by no pattern of the register (reference/
  places.yml has no entry); it is recorded here, not ruled.
- **Szymanowice** likewise: the article's third rural commune, named in the
  edition's table of villages of about 1797 ("Szyman.", AGAD
  1/169/0/1/1745, the editor's identification) but with no place entry of
  its own. Unmatched, recorded here (NEEDS_CONFIRMATION, "The newspapers").
- **The dismissed teacher.** The note's closing incident - the school where
  an Evangelical taught, empty of the colonists' children on the
  inspector's visit, the teacher dismissed after it - names no school and
  no teacher. The Ostdeutscher Beobachter's feature of 1942 names the
  Łazińsk teacher Mielke "seit 1925" (the editor's ruling of 2026-10-22
  makes him Breyer's Mielke and the cantor of Słowiński 2023, one man). If
  the dismissed teacher of 1933 were Mielke, the 1942 "seit 1925" would
  need a look; nothing the edition holds identifies the school, so the
  question is only asked, not answered (NEEDS_CONFIRMATION, "The
  newspapers").
- The **gminy wiejskie Oleśnica, Trąbczyn i Szymanowice** are the press
  witness of the district's rural communes as the paper of 1933 saw them:
  Oleśnica the village and commune the 1937 item moved the old people's
  home to, Trąbczyn the edition's estate.

## The index

`newspapers_index.py --check` runs over the item: **three hits**, all of
spellings ruled in `newspapers/index.yml` by the earlier items and standing
under those rulings - "Zagórowa" (the town, line 2), "Oleśnica" (the
commune, line 2; the same identification the 1937 item's ruling carries, the
editor's confirmation of it still open) and "Trąbczyn" (line 3). The names
that match no pattern - Szymanowice, Emilienhain, Ludwigslust - produce no
hit and are recorded above instead.

## The rights

Anonymous press of 1933: Polish copyright in an anonymous work runs 70
years from first publication, so it ended in 2004. Shown whole on the site
(`visible: true`), on the editor's ruling of 2026-10-22 for the 1937
clipping ("Assume the rights are approved"), the source known. The item's
English is Claude's (the standing caveat on the page), made in the session
that read the clipping.
