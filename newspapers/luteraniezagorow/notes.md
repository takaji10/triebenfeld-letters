## What the item is, and what it bears on

- The parish's own chronicle of its Zagórów filial, written by the
  congregation for the visitors of its website — the account on the item's
  page says so. Not a historian's work but a congregation speaking of
  itself, in the shape of dated entries; the kind of witness that carries
  what no history book records (the electronic clock and bells of 2008,
  the Dom Seniora's closing of 2009).
- **Werner (1904)** (`werner1904`): the page's "W latach 1720 - 1817 ...
  otoczeni opieką duszpasterską przez proboszcza Parafii ewangelickiej we
  Wrześni" is the same story Werner's entry 201 tells — the Wreschen
  (Września) parish that served the German Hauländer country around
  Peisern and Zagorowo until 1817, when the district went to Russia. The
  page's next sentence — the incumbents "z Grodźca, Konina, Żychlina" from
  1817 to 1856 — is the story of the years Werner's entry stops at.
- **Kneifel (1964)** (`kneifel1964`): the page's church — the plot of
  1857, the foundation stone of 8 May 1878, the consecration of 1882 — is
  the "Zagórów church of 1878-1896" Kneifel's excerpt carries (p. 165 f.
  of the excerpt), and its parish stands in Kneifel's diocese list ("Zagörow
  mit dem Filial Lissewo-Pyzdry", pp. 112, 123 of the excerpt).
- **Ląd**: the chronicle opens with the Reformation "prowadzony przez
  zakonników z Lądu" — the abbey of Ląd whose copy-book the edition
  carries (APP 53/1455/0/2/21). The claim is the page's own; the edition's
  documents say nothing of the sixteenth century at Ląd.
- The Rev. **Karol Świtalski** (the first service after the war, 1954) and
  Bishop **Michał Warczyński** (the Dom Seniora's consecration, 1995) are
  the page's only named persons; neither is the edition's. The page names
  no sources and no literature; nothing here asserts its agreement beyond
  what is recorded above, and the edition keeps to its documents where
  they differ (the documents-first rule). Nothing in it contradicts them.
