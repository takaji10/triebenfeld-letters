## Provenance

The article is a report on the portal **TwojaSłupca** (twojaslupca.pl), the
local news site of Słupca, published **6 July 2015** (the portal's date line
reads "6 lipca, 2015", with the comma as printed). The jubilee it reports was
the day before ("Obchody jubileuszu odbyły się wczoraj"): 5 July 2015, a
Sunday. The portal counts the village's 775 years from the document of 1240
— 1240 + 775 = 2015 — which is the arithmetic of the jubilee itself.

## What the item is, and what it bears on

- A local portal's report of a village festival, written for its readers on
  the day (the account on the item's page says so). Its history paragraph is
  the village's story told from the village's side, and its report of the
  celebration — the tablet and the obelisk, the honours, the exhibition, the
  band — is the kind of thing no historian else records.
- **The history paragraph is the edition's own documents' story.** Each
  claim it makes is matched by the KDW holding (`units/kdw/`), the edition's
  printed medieval charters:
  - the **document of 1240**, the Archbishop of Gniezno granting the
    Cistercians of Ląd tithes from several villages, Trąbczyn among them,
    is the KDW's no. 2: Archbishop Fulko's grant of the tithes of the
    chapels of St Peter and St Andrew, from Zagórów, Skokum, Kopojno,
    Trąbczyn ("Trampczino"), Szetlew, Łukom and Wrąbczyn, 30 September 1240
    (sic) — the editors mark the year "sic". The article's "a raczej
    Trampczin" is the Latin form of that document.
  - **Janek, son of Szymon** ("z nadania księcia Bolesława Pobożnego"), is
    named in the KDW's charter of 27 March 1276 (Duke Bolesław's grant of
    Trąbczyn to count Mikołaj), which recites that the duke had earlier
    given the village to comes Ianchon (Janek), son of Simon, who died
    without issue, and then to lord Mikołaj, who died without legitimate
    successors. The article's **"komes lądzki Mikołaj"** is that count
    Mikołaj, the duke's treasurer at Ląd ("thesaurarii nostri de Lenda"),
    the grantee of 1276 — the same gift the medieval era's own blurb names
    ("the gift of Trąbczyn to Count Mikołaj in 1276").
  - **"komes Jan i Przedpełk"** are the brothers Jan and Przedpełk, heirs
    of Trąbczyn ("Iohannis et Przetpelkonis fratrum, heredum de
    Trampczino"), of the KDW's two judgments of 1346.
  - The **rodu Trąbczyńskich herbu Dryja** to the end of the seventeenth
    century, and the later heirs, the article tells without its sources;
    the register carries the families under their own entries. The article's
    list of the heirs who "took an active part in the wars of national
    liberation" — Antoni Prusimski, Stanisław Count Miączyński, Aleksander
    and Włodzimierz Miączyński — is the article's own account, not judged
    here; the identifications are under **The index** below.
- The article does not name the edition or any of its works; nothing here
  asserts its agreement beyond the match of the facts recorded above, and
  the edition keeps to its documents where they differ (the documents-first
  rule). The 1240 of the jubilee is the article's own count.

## The index

The index of people and places runs over the items (the editor,
2026-10-22). What this item brings the patterns is ruled in
`newspapers/index.yml`, pair by pair, from the sheet
(`review/newspapers/mentions.md`). What was ruled, and on what ground:

- **Trąbczyn** ("Trąbczyn", "Trąbczyna", "Trąbczynie" — the headline, the
  lead, the history paragraph, the honours' name twice, the exhibition's
  title, the celebrations): the village the edition stands at; every
  spelling ruled in. The **"Trampczin"** of the history paragraph is
  matched by no pattern of the register (whether to extend the pattern
  waits on the editor, NEEDS_CONFIRMATION, "The newspapers").
- **Ląd** ("w Lądzie", the count's title "lądzki"): matched by no pattern
  either — the register's pattern takes "Ląd" bare and "Lądzk..." with the
  initial capital only, both built for the Kuryer Polski's print; the
  portal's lower-case and inflected forms slip past it (NEEDS_CONFIRMATION,
  "The newspapers", with the same question for the 1942 item's Łazinsk and
  Drommin).
- **Antoni Prusimski** ("Prusimski"): ruled in. The article names him among
  the heirs of Trąbczyn who took part in the wars of national liberation;
  the register's man is Antoni Ostroróg-Prusimski, the Starost of
  Niszczewice, owner of the Trąbczyn estate, of whom the entry's own
  identity says he took part in the uprising of 1794 and lost the estate
  for it — the article's context and the register's man agree.
- **Miączyński** ("Miączyński", twice): ruled out. The article's
  heirs-list names "Stanisław hrabia Miączyński, czy Aleksander i
  Włodzimierz Miączyński". Włodzimierz is the register's other entry
  (miaczynski-wlodzimierz, the Count of the summons of 1860), whose pattern
  is pinned to that document and cannot match here; "Stanisław hrabia" and
  "Aleksander" no ruling of the edition identifies. The register's own
  precedent keeps an unidentified later Miączyński off Stanisław's entry
  (its `not_in` for Breyer's "Graf Miączyński" of 1936), and the ruling
  follows it — ruling the bare surname in would attach Włodzimierz's
  mention to the person page of Michalina's husband. Whether the editor
  wants the mentions attached (and to whom) is open (NEEDS_CONFIRMATION,
  "The newspapers").
- **"Wolicki"** — the surname of Józef Wolicki, one of the villagers
  honoured — is caught by the register's pattern for the place Wolica
  (`Wolic\w*`): ruled out as the surname it is, the place not of the
  article.
- The rest of the article's names — the other honourees of "Zasłużony dla
  wsi Trąbczyn", and the historical Janek, Szymon, Bolesław Pobożny,
  Przedpełk and the rest of the history paragraph — are matched by no
  pattern of the register.
