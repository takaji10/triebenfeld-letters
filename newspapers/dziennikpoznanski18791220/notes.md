# Dziennik Poznański, 20 December 1879: the death notice of Włodzimierz
# hr. Miączyński — notes

A death notice (nekrolog) in the black-bordered box on p. 4 of Dziennik
Poznański, R. 21, nr 292 (20 grudnia 1879), digitised by the Wielkopolska
Digital Library (https://wbc.poznan.pl/dlibra/publication/69094/edition/84585/content).
The WBC record: the copy sygn. Cz4293 of PAN Biblioteka Kórnicka,
digitised from the collections of Biblioteka PTPN w Poznaniu, "słaba
jakość mikrofilmu" (poor microfilm quality), "domena publiczna".

## How it came in (2026-10-23)

The editor: add the image `0004_0001.jpg` of the WBC download (the page of
the issue carrying the notice) as a new newspaper source; it is the
obituary notice of Włodzimierz Miączyński; Tesseract may be used for the
OCR.

- **What is transcribed**: the death notice whole — eleven printed lines,
  from the display-capital name line to the signature. The text around the
  box on the page (a confectionery advertisement of the factory of
  G. Weese in Poznań, among other notices) is not part of the item and is
  not transcribed.
- **How it was read**: Tesseract 5.5.3, pol and pol-std, the page as it is
  and upscaled, at several segmentations, with word-level zooms of the
  doubtful spots — and, when the display capitals of the name line defeated
  every pass, the issue's own DjVu fetched again from the WBC
  (`/Content/84585/DjVu/0004_0001.djvu`, not kept in the repository) and
  rendered at 600 dpi, where the same lines read cleanly. The two images
  are different renderings of the same microfilm frame; each spot was
  settled where the passes agree, and what they do not agree on is below.
- **The doubtful readings, and how they stand** (the editor's eye wanted
  on all of them):
  - the name line's `hr.` — every 600 dpi pass reads "nr.", every human
    reading and the sense say **hr.** (hrabia); "nr." is no title.
  - `dziedzic` — secure (the passes and the sense agree; it is the word
    for the proprietor of an estate).
  - `majętności Trąbczyna` — the 600 dpi passes read the line in
    agreement ("MAJETNOSCI TRAMBCZYNA" and its variants); the case is the
    genitive, as the phrase "dziedzic majętności Trąbczyna" requires.
  - `Polskiem` — the old orthography's locative, which the passes of
    300 dpi read three times ("Polskiem") against "Polskim" once; the
    600 dpi tight crops kept suggesting "Polském" with an accent (eight
    reads), which is as likely the microfilm's speck over the e as the
    print's own mark. "Polskiem" stands; the accent question is open.
  - `Zmarł` — the first word of the body line, at the notice's left edge,
    dropped by every OCR pass; the ink profile of both images shows a real
    word standing before "po", and the word reads "Zmarł" at the zooms of
    both images. The sentence requires a verb of dying here.
  - `18go` — secure (both images, high confidence; the "go" raised, as the
    prints of the time set it).
  - `Ekspedycya` — the dispatch of the body. The 300 dpi passes gave
    "Eksportac"/"Eksekucja"-like shapes, the 600 dpi pass "Ekoportacja";
    the descender pattern at the 600 dpi zooms (three letters standing
    below the line) and the standard phrase of the notices of the time
    ("ekspedycya zwłok", the body travelling by rail; "eksekucya" would be
    no Polish collocation with "zwłok") settle **Ekspedycya**.
  - `22` — the day of the ekspedycya, high confidence in both images; the
    22nd of December 1879 was a Monday, as the line's own "w poniedziałek"
    says.
  - `23` — the day after "nazajutrz" in the funeral line. The second digit
    reads as an 8 to every pass of both images, but the English engine set
    to digits alone recognises only the "2" — the second glyph is damaged,
    and the sense (the funeral the day after a Monday the 22nd) allows
    only the 23rd. "23" stands in the transcription; the digit is the
    item's one reading the editor has not seen.
  - `czém` — the old orthography's é, which the 600 dpi pass reads with
    high confidence and the 300 dpi passes garble; kept as read.
  - `synowiec` — secure (the nephew; the 300 dpi pass reads it, the word
    is the notices' standard).
- **Line division**: as printed, eleven lines; the notice breaks none of
  its words with a printed hyphen, so the "¬" convention appears nowhere.
- **Kept as printed**: "hr." before both names; "majętności" (not the
  modern "majatek"); "18go", "rb.", "b. m.", "ss.", "czém" in the old
  orthography; "z Pawłowa" (of Pawłowa/Pawłów) as the signature's place.
- **The comma after "Polskiem"**: the low mark after the word at the 600
  dpi zooms reads as a comma, and the sentence needs one before "Zmarł";
  it stands.

## What it bears on

- **The Count's death, and the date.** The wlodzimierz era's ownership
  line has Count Włodzimierz Miączyński holding Trąbczyn to his death in
  1879, "then his nephew Władysław Miączyński" — this notice is that
  death's press witness, set in the paper two days after the event it
  reports. But the notice says the Count died on the **18th** of December,
  where the editor's ruling of 2026-10-15 (from the notebook APP
  53/968/0/-/716) puts his death on the **20th**. The question is already
  open: Żychliński (1891, p. 222) has the 18th too (NEEDS_CONFIRMATION,
  "The literature", "Włodzimierz's death"). The notice — printed in the
  paper of the 20th and reporting the death of the 18th — sides with
  Żychliński, a period witness against the records. The documents stand
  (the editor's rule, 2026-10-20); the press's 18th is kept as printed and
  the ruling is the editor's.
- **The nephew and his seat.** The notice names the heir "the grieving
  nephew, Władysław hr. Miączyński of Pawłowa" — the same nephew the
  ownership line records, and the only witness of the era that gives his
  seat: Pawłowa (Pawłów). Whether the register's places hold a Pawłów of
  this family is not settled here.
- **The index.** The item's one hit is Trąbczyn ("Trąbczyna", standing
  under the ruling already in `newspapers/index.yml`; the box "In the
  newspapers" shows it on the estate's page on /places/). The Count
  himself the register's patterns do not bring in: the Włodzimierz
  entry's pattern (`miaczynski-wlodzimierz`) is pinned by its `only_in`
  to the summons of 1860 and cannot match here, and the bare "Miączyński"
  of the name line and the signature falls under the standing "no"
  ruling of the 2015 report's heirs-list (the register's own precedent
  keeps an unidentified later Miączyński off Stanisław's entry). The
  names live in the transcription and this notes file.
