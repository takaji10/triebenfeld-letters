"On the nutrition of the people" — Dr. Konstanty Lidmanowski's study of

pp. 720-725, digitised by Crispa, the University of Warsaw's digital

## The ground: the Słupca powiat, named

The article names the edition's own geography by name: "For my research
on the nutrition of the people, I took the Słupca district, which
stretches in a narrow strip on the border of the Grand Duchy of Poznań"
— the powiat in which Trąbczyn lies — and, the borderland living in like
material conditions, extends the figures to the whole strip along the
Prussian frontier. The data collected "for over a dozen years". This is
the one text of the three that names its ground, and the account says so.

- **Słupca has no place entry** in the edition's register
  (reference/places.yml) — the index's machinery matches the edition's
  registered places, and the powiat's name ("powiat słupecki", the
  adjective form) is matched by no pattern. Recorded here; whether Słupca
  — the powiat town, the doctor's birthplace — should be a place of the
  edition is the editor's (NEEDS_CONFIRMATION, "The newspapers").

## The unit: the ciepłostka

The reckonings are in "heat units" — the Polish ciepłostka, the old
calorie: about 50 per kilogram of body weight a day for a hard-working
person, taken as 3,500 for a man of 70 kg. The term is left as the
translation has it ("heat units"); the account explains it. The print's
figures are kept exactly: breakfast 1,126, dinner 1,481, snack 1,391,
supper 1,376 for the wealthy farmer — 5,374; and 470, 792, 930 and about
1,000 for the smallholder — 3,192 against the 3,500 needed.

## The English: corrections of the session (2026-10-22)

Four corrections, each with the Polish that decided it:

- **A sentence printed twice** — "categories according to their wealth,
  and the collected data will only then tell us how the people in a given
  area nourish themselves." — the translation's own artifact, the
  Polish having it once ("kategorie, względnie do zamożności jego i
  zebrane dane będą dopiero nam mówić, jak odżywia się lud w danej
  okolicy"). The second copy struck.
- "if note bene he earned well in summer" → "if nota bene he earned well
  in summer" — the Polish has the Latin "nota bene"; the English had
  slipped its vowels.
- "a plate of lightly seasoned potatoes with sour skimmed milk" → "a
  plate of potatoes with little butter on them, with sour skimmed milk"
  — "talerza mało kraszonych kartofli z kwaśnym zbieranym mlekiem":
  kraszone is dressed with fat, not seasoned.
- "today's dirty small-town butchers" → "today's dirty little-town
  shambles" — "brudnych rzeźników małomiasteczkowych": the jatki are the
  butcher's stalls the doctor means, the sausage and blood pudding being
  bought at them.

## What it bears on

- **The era.** `twentieth-century`, like its fellows — and the figures
  are the era's own: what the people whose land and forest rights the
  wlodzimierz era's tables fixed ate twenty years on.
- **The author.** Dr. Lidmanowski (`lidmanowski` on /people/): his study,
  his data, his powiat.
- **The companion texts**: the housing survey of 1914 (the same
  observation work, the same journal); the Jutrzenka piece of 1913, which
  digests his Zdrowie article of March 1913 and so assumes a third
  article of the series not in the edition.
- **What no other source of the edition records**: the diet in figures —
  the bread, milk, potatoes and meat of the very people the edition's
  court books and registers name, and how far the smallholder's and
  day-labourer's day fell short of the working body's need.
