Volumina Legum required that they be drawn up exactly and precisely, in a solemn form, in a manner univocally pointing to the rights and the duties flowing from them. "The entries, and above all the perpetuities are to be written according to the form laid down in the statute"[^24]. The copies from the acts and the books were drawn on parchment, with the signatures of the judge and the sub-judge, with their seals on the strips of parchment, additionally with the signature of the starost as the representative of the king in the given land in which the chancellery functioned. The copies were drawn in the hand-written script, often with the ornate majuscules of the texture, furnished with the wax seals, glued with a string to the parchment. On the whole the keeping of the court books had an enormous significance, for beside the criminal rulings, which were entered in them, every entry of a civil character, such as those concerning the acquisition of land, the donations, the testating, the pledges of immovables, gained the legal force only at the moment of the making of the entry in the court book. Examples of such entries the literature cites[^25]:

– "Michał of Złotniki grants to his spouse Katarzyna, the daughter of Konrad Kunaszewski, 300 grzywnas as dowry and morning gift on ½ of the village of Złotniki";

– "Knat of Kutyszcz, the land judge of Halicz, carries out the division of the estates Łuka and Myszkowce between the brothers german of the Myszkowski family".

The entries made in the land court books as well as in the municipal ones could be grouped in a few categories:

– the acts of a private will – one-sided (the renunciations, the testaments, the plenipotencies, the acquittances, the securing of the financial claims, the sureties, the declarations of the peasants on their release or on their entering into subjection and so on), two-sided (the agreements concerning the movables and the immovables, temporal and perpetual, the life annuities, those bound with an exchange, a donation, a purchase), the securing of the dowry, the matters bound with the care and the curatorship;

– the depositions (the relations) – on the judicial acts done (e.g. the serving of the summons);

– the court views (the visitations, the obductions), the so-called "binding" into the estates (the intromission), the executions and others;

– the protests and the infringement of the public or the private goods, as well as on the crimes committed;
