# 2. The noble courts

Once the nobility had taken shape as a separate social estate, it followed as a natural course of things that the noble judiciary won a form specific and separate from the remaining courts of the Commonwealth of the time.

At the very bottom of the structure of the judiciary stood the land courts, the municipal courts and the podkomorski courts as the courts of first instance.

The first kind of courts: the land courts descended from the structures of the starosts' courts, adjudicating in the king's name. The starost judged in the name of the king, who could not be present at the court sittings in person. Hence there took root the practice of delegating the judicial powers to the office of the starost[^8]. He heard the matters over a larger territory, which resulted in the delegating of the judicial acts to judges, who had earlier attended the starosts at the court sittings. Thus gradually the function of the judging of matters passed to the judges, who began to specialise in the acts entrusted to them. The composition of the court was filled out by the representatives of the local nobility (as assessors), which was to underline not only the solemnity of the rulings and the authority of the court, but lent the legitimacy of any possible execution as well, for the organs of execution and of police did not exist yet. The execution of the rulings went through the intervention of the nobility of the given land, and so their presence on the bench had significance beyond measure[^9]. A deputy judge and a scribe were chosen for the composition. The land courts so formed covered the individual voivodeships, which already at that time were large units of the territorial division. It was necessary, then, for the composition of the land court to move between the districts and to hear there the local matters brought to its cognisance. These sittings were called the district sessions (roczki), which as a rule took place four times a year, but it happened also, that in case of need. At the end of a district session the judge proclaimed the term of the next sitting, guided by the principle that they were held again in the same place[^10]. With the development of the infrastructure the custom of the courts sitting under the open sky, in tents, dies away. The courts begin to gather in the castles, the manors, the land offices, in the monastic rooms and in the churches.
