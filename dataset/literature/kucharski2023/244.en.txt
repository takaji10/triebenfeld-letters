The courts gathered in the rooms called the audience chambers, where at the chief place stood the table called the "tribunal", with a cross set on it. In the middle of the table sat the judge in the surroundings of the assessors. The parties entered the hall after the calling of the case. The standing forth of both the parties was obligatory, and the absence without an important cause was punished with a fine, called also the non-standing. The procedure of that time, from the statute of the year 1578, allowed the possibility of the putting off of the case, which was called the dylacje or the puttings-off, at most four times, with the taking of the next term as a fatal one. The judge opened the hearing, which went orally and openly and was made up of three stages. At the first term every question of organisation was considered, the puttings-off, the further terms were planned. In the second stage, the plaintiff proclaimed his summons, described his claims and cited the rules on which he based his pretensions. The defendant answered the claims of the plaintiff, raised his own arguments and declared whether he accepts the judicial summons or contradicts it. The outline of the most important claims of the parties was written into the court book by the scribe. The plaintiff could no longer withdraw his complaint from that moment, unless with the agreement of the opposite party. Then followed the third part of the process: namely the evidentiary proceedings, during which the parties presented their findings, called the witnesses or delivered before the adjudicating bench the material proofs. The processes were not divided into civil and criminal, and so both the elements of those processes in the present meaning interwove. In the evidentiary proceedings[^33] before the courts of the time the rule found its application that it was the parties who had to gather and themselves present every proof before the adjudicating bench. "The court did not seek the objective truth, but contented itself with the proofs, which made the formal basis for the giving of the ruling"[^34]. The so-called propinquity of the proof was applied, which consisted in the determination that the right of the priority in the carrying out of the proof belonged to the party higher in estate.
