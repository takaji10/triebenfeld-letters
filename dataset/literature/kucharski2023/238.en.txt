# 3.2. The lay judges – the assessors

Beside the professional judges, in the compositions of the courts there sat the lay judges, who represented the interests of the nobility as the whole of a given land, called the assessors. They were noblemen of the given land, whom the professional judge chose for himself, so that they adjudicated together with him in the judicial composition. As a rule there were six of them in the composition; they had no legal education and rather advised the judge in the giving of the judgments. As we read in the literature, "a court assessor is a person who, either by virtue of an entitlement belonging to her, or of a calling by an empowered judicial authority, or of an office she holds, assists the judge or the tribunal in the acts of the adjudication, contentious and non-contentious, being at the same time a witness of the course of the trial"[^19]. Curiously, the assessors were standing and non-standing, and the former were recruited from among the noblemen holding the offices in the given land and appointed by the king. It is thus a second function in the judicial composition of the land court nominated by the ruler, while in the municipal courts there was no requirement of their appointment, though that practice began to fix itself[^20]. "The szlachta sat not only on their own land and municipal courts, but also on the town courts; on the other hand, on the noble courts there often sat as assessors townsmen, and not only the local officials, the burgrave, the wójt, the jurymen, but also ordinary townsmen, holding no office, and also Latin clergy and even Ruthenian. (…) This admission of the various strata and estates to a share in the court sittings was a proof of a high tolerance, and at the same time it strengthened the authority of the court and the trust in its justice, regardless of the differences of estate"[^21].

# 3.3. The court scribe

The whole of the judicial composition was closed by the scribe (of the land court or of the municipal one), who sat at the presidial table, though the question whether he took part in the giving of the rulings remains disputed. In contradistinction to the judge and the assessors, he had to possess a solid education, the ability of the handling of Latin and the knowledge of the rules and of the earlier rulings serving as the basis and the pattern
