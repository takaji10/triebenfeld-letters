"There is no collection of general laws; the constitutions and ordinances are often at variance with one another. And they are so many that with the closest study it is impossible to go through them all and grasp the meaning proper to them. What a fine excuse for the judges and assessors, unaccustomed to serious work, to trouble themselves little about these laws — what a broad field for the tricks and the litigiousness of the pleaders" (the advocates) — wrote Fryderyk Schulz, a Livonian traveller making his way through the Poland of the age of Stanisław August[^5]. The whole of the land law was chaotic and resembled rather a jungle, making an excellent field for the pettifoggers and the litigious of the courts, hard to master for the judicial practitioners. The judges indeed felt the lack of a uniform body of material laws and of a procedure, codified, uniform, that would have allowed the fixing of the adjudicative practice over the whole country, which would have strengthened the political and the social cohesion of the State. The adjudicating corps, too, to the very end of the Noble Commonwealth's existence could not show a solid legal education, for the judicial functions were conferred on the local landowning nobility, whose substantive preparation rested rather on the experience gained in chancellery work than on a university education. "Soon I learned, as usual, by heart the accessorii (the formulae for the opening of a process). It was to be an elementary book of the law. Day and night I toiled in private over reading the statutes of Herburta and the correction of the Prussian, having not the smallest notion of the spirit of the laws and of their principles. Not knowing the difference of what natural law, political, civil, criminal and the law of nations were to signify in the ground. I recited the Roman laws, the Magdeburg statutes, like a parrot, from custom, for so did my whole worthy assembly"[^6] — wrote Józef Wybicki. It was the practice to begin a career in the judiciary early, which the author of the national anthem notices, writing: "At sixteen I had already been running about various courts, where the litigants drove us. I gained practical knowledge, so that in my seventeenth year I began, as the saying went, to stand forth, to speak up, in the municipal and the land courts…"[^7].
