A nobleman who would kill another nobleman paid the punishment of 240 marks; making use of an edged weapon he was threatened with the punishment of one year and six weeks of the lower tower, while if he made use of a firearm the punishment was twice as long. The sejm constitution of the year 1588 determined the depth of the lower tower at 12 ells from the floor to the ceiling. The orderedness of the dimensions of the prison rooms the starosts were to watch over, and severely to combat every attempt at making an ease for the convicts. One may assume that the coming out alive of such a room, without windows, dark, unheated and damp-foul, where the convict slept on the ground or on hay, was practically impossible, the more so that the food had to be delivered by the family of the wronged party, which won the process. "If pity or the goodwill of the warders did not ease them this punishment, most often the death of the punished interrupted its sitting out" — as Kajetan Koźmin justly observed[^39]. The convict, after his being led to the undergoing of such a punishment, went down the ladder to the bottom, it was drawn out and the entrance was bolted. Far lighter was the punishment of the so-called upper tower, where the cells had little windows, the furnishings stood there, and the prisoner could be visited. The rule held, however, that if the prisoner put his head out of the window or crossed the threshold of the cell, he underwent the punishment from the beginning.
