The rulings given by the land courts were called the final rulings, from which, after the introduction of the institution of the appeal, one could appeal. The bringing of the means of appeal held back the execution of the punishment. The giving of the ruling itself, however, bound itself with a ceremonial, underlining the weight of the ruling. A curious description of the giving of a ruling adjudging the punishment of death is brought by the Lublin palestrant Jan Duklan Ochocki: "When the decree condemning to death was to be read, the judges covered their heads, the presiding one called louder — 'Open the doors! The decree of death!' The usher too, with his head covered, three times opened and shut the doors and shut them with a crash, calling: — 'Be silent, gentlemen, for the listening to the decree of death!' During the reading of the decree the delinquent stood before the court. After him three days were given him to prepare himself for the death, and the priest no longer left him. (…) The headsman too was constantly at the delinquent, slept with him and accustomed him to himself"[^37].

The punishments meted in the period of the Noble Commonwealth answered the conviction of the time of the necessity of the laying of the stress on the general prevention, which cannot wonder, for given the weakness of the execution of the laws, the individual prevention often proved illusory. The conviction of the exceptionality and the inviolability of the nobility caused that towards the representatives of that estate no tortures were applied during the skrutynium, and even from the heaviest misdeeds one could buy oneself out.

So Wacław Matuszewicz, after a quarrel, beat and shot with a pistol his adversary, the nobleman Parowiński, who soon died of the wounds taken. The companions of the dead man caught the perpetrator, to set him down to the castle, which guaranteed a quick administration of the punishment. The body of the dead man was delivered to the municipal court at Brześć as the chief proof in the case. Into the chancellery came the manifest of the instygator and the process moved. The perpetrator had, however, an experienced palestrant, who so tangled him in the case and taught him "how to stand in the court", that it was adjudged that Wacław Matuszewicz shall take the oath and cleanse himself of the accusation of a murder wilful. He was then set free, after the paying to the wife of the killed man 3000 zlotys and to the court 1000 zlotys[^38].
