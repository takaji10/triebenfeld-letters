To frighten the wretch, Łaziński ordered him to dig a grave in the cellar if he did not persuade the family and the other Kraków merchants to pay a ransom at the height of 12600 zlotys. The merchant was unbending and refused, at which Łaziński, fearing to keep him in his own house, carried him over to the popa at Moszczeniec. There the dashing merchant, left alone, entered into a good understanding with the churchman and, having described to him his lot, moved him to send the messages to the castle at Sanok. In reaction to the above the deputy starost arrived at once and, together with the starost's guard, took the prisoner from the hands of the kidnappers. Łaziński hid himself, reckoning that the matter would die down and he would escape the responsibility. The merchant Montelupi did not however give up, hired an armed band and a year after the event came to Siniawy, to Łaziński's manor, in the company of his son-in-law Damian Baliński. The manor was surrounded and Łaziński was caught. Montelupi did not plan at all to hand his tormentor into the hands of justice, but meted the punishment to him himself. After the binding of the robber to a post he was shot, and then the whole band, undisturbed, drove off quietly[^40].

The bad example from the elders passed to the youth, which despite the harsh upbringing and the moral and the religious rules inculcated at times demoralised itself too and entered the road of the criminal misdeed. In the historical sources there is found the description of the case of a noblewoman of Zakroczym, Katarzyna of the Kwasieborscy Fałęcka, who lodged at the municipal court of Zakroczym a protest against her unnatural son, for that he did not want to go to school, and given to a clerical seminary for the learning, soon fled. After the return home he prepared his mother a hell. He carried out the furnishings and the clothes of his siblings, which he sold. To the household trying to oppose that behaviour the youthful offender threatened with a beating, swinging at the same time a knife and an axe. The noblewoman wrote in her complaint: "my own sister, standing personally before these present acts, he beat without any cause, made the marks of bruises, and drove all the people from the manor, and tried to set the manor on fire, the windows in the manor and in the village he knocked out, and he promises certainly to beat, to kill and to burn the manor and the village. Wherefore, forestalling the evil, lest he accomplish his senseless intention, this complaint is brought into the present acts – Katarzyna Fałęcka"[^41].
