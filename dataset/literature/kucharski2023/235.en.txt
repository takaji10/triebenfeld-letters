The institution of the appeal from the rulings will be introduced only in the 16th century, by the Formula Processus. That codification of the land law, enacted by the Sejm of Piotrków in the year 1523[^15], introduced the institution of the appeal, made the execution of the rulings more effective, shortened the time of the duration of the processes, but also abolished the institution of the judge's reprimand[^16]. Before that happened, the practice of the questioning of the rulings had taken shape in another way. Namely through the so-called reprimand of the judge, an appeal of a *sui generis* kind. Given the impossibility of entering against a ruling (which would have equalled the entering against the king, who decided and spoke through the ruling), the dissatisfaction of the parties with the ruling turns against the judge adjudicating. The complaint concerns an unjust or an improper conducting of the proceedings, contrary to the rules. The party that did not agree with the ruling lodged, in the course of the hearing, a "reprimand" against the judge, which caused the necessity of the hearing of the case by a court of a higher instance, which adjudicated as to its justification and handed the case to the same court, which adjudicated already in another composition. The party entering the reprimand entered the procedural position of the plaintiff, and the judge that of the defendant. The party that would win such a "reprimand" proceeding was obliged to pay the fee called the "pamiętne". A reprimand could be brought only against the judges sitting in the land courts, the municipal ones, the podkomorski and the municipal ones. For obvious reasons there was no possibility of its being brought against the judge of the royal court, whose rulings were without appeal, for the king gave them. The bringing of a judge's reprimand held back the execution of the ruling. A reprimand should be brought after the reading of the ruling is finished, and the guarantor of its effectiveness was the settling of the court fee called the kocz.
