The criminal judiciary in the period after the unification of the Kingdom rested in the hands of the court of the oprawca, that is an official of the municipal court appointed by the starost, that is an organ coming from the royal nomination. In the period before the institution of the royal instygator had taken shape, he had the task of the pursuit of the crimes threatening the safety and the public order[^13]. An office with competences so shaped gathered in its hands the investigative and the jurisdictional acts: it established, pursued and confined the perpetrator of a crime. One cannot refuse that office an exceptional effectiveness and efficiency of action for the practice of the time in the field of the execution of the laws, as the numerous complaints of the nobility against the abuses of the oprawca point to — one who, in their belief, infringed their rights and liberties. It is a fact that the court of the oprawca had an efficient and a harsh investigative apparatus, and the cases pending were judged quickly. This did not however stop the noble brethren from the criticism of the oprawca and from the fierce combating of that judicial office, which led at the end of the 15th century to the liquidation of that institution. Reminiscences of its existence function to the present day through the pejorative meaning given to the word "oprawca" (the bailiff).

In the 15th century the wiec courts keep on further, one in each voivodeship, which were in a straight line the functional heirs of the district courts of the princes. The close of the 15th century, however, is already the decline of that kind of institution, and the competences pass to the sejm court, which adjudicates in the presence of the nobility under the presidency of the voivode. The judgments fall in the sejm courts in the king's name, who is the highest judge. The most important deeds of the high officials go precisely to the king's court, though to relieve the king, a practice takes shape that in his stead a dignitary indicated by him presided over the court. In the practice of the time of the functioning of the organs of the administration of justice it was accepted that the multiplicity of the types of court had the task of the relieving of the ruler of the personal judging of the matters, which the monarch had the right to take over always to his own cognisance. That competence engaged itself always at the moment when the ruler appeared in a given land; then all the courts stopped their activity and the judge, the sub-judge and the scribe took their place at the ruler's side, to serve him with their help in the giving of the rulings.
