# Judiciary of the nobility from the 15th to the 18th century

# Abstract

*The judiciary of the age of the feudal monarchy changed its character in a natural way into an estate one, which was bound up with the emergence of the social estates, above all the nobility, which as a result of the political processes and the social changes under way won ever greater privileges, among them judicial immunities*[^2]*. This study has sketched in general the judicial organs of the administration of justice in the Polish Commonwealth from the 15th century to the decline of the Polish state. These organs were naturally not uniform in structure or organisation, and their competences overlapped. They evolved with time, though it is a fact that these changes did not keep pace with the changes taking place in the international environment around the First Commonwealth, and were not able to answer the needs both of the reform of the state and of the social changes. At a certain stage they even became a burden on the political reforms, adding their share, as it were, to the ossification of the structures of the then Polish state and its fall at the end of the 18th century.
