2.1.7. The roofs of dwelling houses

The roofs are always pitched, with two symmetrical slopes. Most are constructions at an angle of 40⁰ to 45⁰ set on a broad-fronted building, typical of this region. Buildings with roofs thatched with straw or reed are still found; most buildings now have roofs covered with asbestos-cement sheets (eternit), sometimes with tiles or sheet metal. The rafters mostly rest on ceiling beams projecting beyond the face of the wall, more rarely on a purlin laid on the axis of the wall on the ceiling beams, set off from those ceiling beams.

The construction most often used (bound up with the single-pile plan) is the collar-beam roof. On one-and-a-half-pile and double-pile buildings the construction of the roof trusses is more complicated – mostly a rafter roof on posts (stolcowa).[^3]

The roofs are set symmetrically on the building. Their gables are mostly boarded and sometimes have extra doors into the loft.

The roofs of all types of building, both dwelling houses and farm buildings, had no dormer gables.
