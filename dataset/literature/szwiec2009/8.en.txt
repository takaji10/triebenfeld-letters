1.2. Gathering the source material, and the fieldwork

The study of rebuilt and vanished buildings was possible thanks to conservation records, archival material and other sources. For these buildings an inspection on the spot was also needed in every case.

The starting material for the report was maps, photographs of architecture, aerial photographs, sketches from nature and surveys (plans and elevations), as well as archival material and local and specialist publications of various kinds (on architecture, urban planning, ethnography and the conservation of monuments). This material was obtained in the course of the research. An analytical method was adopted. The research was carried out directly in the field, in archives and by interviews. Detailed photographic and drawn records and surveys were made. On the basis of the source, cultural and historical data gathered, a catalogue was made of valuable existing buildings with regional features. These buildings were located in the field and analysed, and the conclusions were put into graphic form. Comparative studies were made of the architectural and landscape elements typical of the “Pyzdry Forest”. The catalogue contains general and detailed maps marking where the buildings studied stand, sets of photographs, freehand sketches, drawn analyses and surveys of individual buildings. Maps, archival photographs and written archival sources were analysed and set against reality and against the photographs, drawings and surveys made by the research team.

1.3. The area of the research

The area studied is the communes of Blizanów, Chocz, Gizałki, Grodziec, Mycielin, Pyzdry, Rychwał, Rzgów, Stawiszyn and Zagórów, which lie in the “Pyzdry Forest”.

It is an area with a common cultural landscape, which coincides with the so-called “Pyzdry Forest”.
