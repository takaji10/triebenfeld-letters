2.2.2. Windows and doors in farm buildings

Because of the building’s use, the window openings in the parts for livestock were made small, and in rooms (where more light was needed) such as workshops or fodder rooms, much larger. At first, in timber buildings, these openings were open or closed only by a wooden shutter. In brick buildings small windows were used, mostly of steel or cast iron, of many panes, with one pane that tilted open, in proportions close to 2:3, straight-headed or with a segmental arch.

Of interest is the use, in buildings of bog iron ore, of window openings with a frame nailed together from boards in the shape of an equilateral triangle.

The doors of farm buildings were made of simple ledged construction, in various proportions according to the kind of room they closed. The larger openings, through which carts passed, were made in proportions close to a square; the smaller, for people only, mostly in the proportions 1:1¾ or 1:2. The door opening was straight or finished at the top with a segmental arch, and the leaves of the door had the same shape. They were made of plain boards set vertically and hung on strap hinges of steel, which sometimes had ornamental ends.
