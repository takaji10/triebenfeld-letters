# Wąsicki (1953), p. 153
# translation by Claude

Until then the main stress on the king's part had been laid on colonising South Prussia, leaving aside the other provinces formerly part of the nobles' Commonwealth. The motives for this course rested largely on the justification of the whole action laid before the Prussian king by Captain Nothardt. On the same grounds minister Schrötter came forward with a proposal to develop a livelier colonising action in New East Prussia, and mainly in the Płock department. For it formed a prolongation of South Prussia and the link between Silesia and East Prussia. This question evidently raised no doubts, and so the king agreed to extend the colonising action to this region, on the same principles on which colonisation was conducted in the other lands of the new Prussian provinces. In this matter minister Schrötter was to come to an understanding with minister Voss and Capt. Nothardt as to the allocation of colonists among the individual provinces[^59].

Schrötter, in accordance with the king's recommendation, came to an understanding with minister Voss; the principles for settling colonists, however, which were set out in the instructions sent to the chambers, raised serious reservations in his mind. In particular this concerned the day labourers, who, in Schrötter's opinion, were given the best settlement conditions of all the categories of settlers. It could therefore be supposed that the inflow of this category of settlers would be the most numerous, which did not agree with Schrötter's intentions. We have already seen that minister Voss too at first set fairly high property requirements for arriving settlers, precisely in order to stem the inflow into the new province of a poor population which, despite help from the state, was unable to set itself up on the land allotted to it. Only because of an express royal order was the property qualification lowered, and in many cases it was not applied at all when the newcomers came from territories occupied by French troops or forming a theatre of war.

Moreover, the advance of 1,000 thalers for setting up provided in the instruction stood in no proportion to the means brought by the colonist. For these reasons Schrötter considered that the principles relied on in settling colonists in South Prussia could not be applied in New East Prussia[^60]. Nor was agreement reached on the distribution of the applying colonists among the individual provinces. Voss was of the opinion that colonists should first of all be directed to South Prussia, and only when the possibility of settlement in that province ceased should they be passed on to New East Prussia. From a conversation with Captain Nothardt minister Schrötter could conclude that he would not only not deal with recruiting settlers for New East Prussia, but on the contrary would put obstacles in the way. In this situation minister Schrötter gave up agreeing and co-ordinating the whole action with minister Voss and Capt.
