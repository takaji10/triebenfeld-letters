The greatest trouble for Schrötter was the question of settling the families classed as Büdner (Büdnerfamilien). For in the colonies being created there was no room for them, because of their fairly numerous inflow despite the restrictions applied, and founding special colonies for this category of settlers seemed unjustified to Schrötter. He therefore turned his attention to the royal manor farms, where he thought there would be the best opportunity of settling them, while at the same time releasing the subjects there from the obligation of labour service (Scharwerk). Until then the obstacle to abolishing this obligation had been the lack of hands for work on the manor farm. Schrötter, wishing to remedy this, ordered the chambers to settle Büdner on the manor farms, in order to assure them a hired labour force. As in South Prussia, so in this province too the lessees of manor farms were to be obliged to settle day labourers at their own cost. In this case the principle that they must possess means of their own of 60 thalers was even departed from, since in Schrötter's opinion too they could not be sent back.

Colonisation in New East Prussia made ever more visible progress, although there was no such express command and care for this action on the part of the king and the General Directory. In 1803 a larger number of colonists was settled in New East Prussia than in the Warsaw department of South Prussia[^64].

The "Reichs-Kolonisten-Werbungs-Büreau" founded by Captain Nothardt at Ohringen near Heilbronn in Württemberg also carried on a lively activity. Frederick William III granted for 1803 a sum of 321,885 thalers for colonisation, stressing that this action should not be checked[^65]. The funds set aside for this purpose thus grew more and more. Seeing, however, the very marked progress of this action in New East Prussia, carried on moreover at lower cost, the Prussian king decided to abolish the recruiting office at Ohringen, whose upkeep involved rather high costs. Captain Nothardt was placed at the head of the Kalisz chamber with the special task of supervising the course of the colonising action in all the departments of South Prussia. The recruiting of colonists was entrusted to the Franconian chamber at Ansbach[^66]. At the same time, on the king's express order, ministers Voss and Schrötter were to agree together the principles of procedure in settling new settlers. Already before this, however, namely in May 1802, a more detailed instruction on this matter than before had been drawn up for South Prussia, differing fairly considerably from the one issued for the chambers and Capt. Nothardt[^67]. For it took account of the points on which minister Schrötter had expressed his reservations.
