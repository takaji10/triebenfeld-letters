# Wąsicki (1953), p. 173
# translation by Claude

B) The day labourers receive a dwelling, a pigsty, half a morgen of garden, 3 fathoms of wood, paying 8 thalers 16 groschen a year. Their livelihood is to be their work, for which they receive pay in money. Above all, however, they must work on the prince's manor farm[^82].

Forest waste land set aside for the settling of smallholder colonists in the district of the forest range of Lisówko. Domain office of Buk. Plan of 1799

The political situation at the time favoured the recruiting of colonists, and so many were found willing to go to the new Prussian province, although, as we shall see, the conditions of settlement on the Prince of Orange's estates proved later to be harder than on the royal estates. On 24 January 1799 the number of volunteers for leaving had already reached 150 families, that is about 600 persons[^83]. The prince obtained from Frederick William the sum of money needed for the colonising action and the exemption of the colonists from state burdens, as had been provided for the settlers on the royal domains.
