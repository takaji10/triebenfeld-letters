As follows from the above list, the largest number were hired labourers, settled directly on the manor farms, in the larger colonies or near the towns, that is where they could more easily find work. Colonists of category IV were few. Colonists assigned to categories V and VI, on the other hand, are entirely lacking. The data cited by Hoffmann, that by 1804 four colonists of groups V and VI had been settled, are therefore not confirmed[^72].

We have no figures for how this matter stood in the other departments, but it may be supposed that it did not depart from the general principles. Thus, although the procedure and principles of colonisation changed fairly substantially compared with the first instructions, and although, contrary to the king's reservations, attempts continued to be made to limit the number of settlers of the first, and even the second, category, it was precisely these colonists who flowed into South Prussia in the greatest numbers. For settling as many hired labourers as possible on the domains was in the king's interest. Minister Schrötter criticised this kind of procedure and the colonisation orders issued for South Prussia, since these categories of settlers, in the opinion — justified, moreover — of the administrator of New East Prussia, showed the strongest tendencies to move beyond the frontiers of the Prussian provinces when they were promised better settlement conditions elsewhere. Already in 1803 the chambers' reports sent to the General Directory very often contain complaints of colonists' secret flight. The cause of this frequent abandonment of both South Prussia and New East Prussia by colonists who had only just arrived was the possibility of moving to Russia[^73]. For in this period a very lively colonising action was being carried on in Russia. The appeal of Tsar Alexander I caused many families of colonists, chiefly smallholders, to begin moving to the new lands, where they were undoubtedly given better settlement conditions. The colonists secretly sold the stock allotted to them, so that the chambers had to announce a prohibition on buying these things from colonists without their consent. This announcement was made public in the Gazeta Południowo-Pruska[^74].
