On average, therefore, the cost of settling one family was 1000 thalers[^49]. An area of 5241 Magdeburg morgen was set aside for colonisation. The figures given by Holsche agree in principle with the list of colonies founded in South Prussia sent by the Poznań chamber to the General Directory in 1804[^50]. Naturally, over a period of four years the number of colonists settled there grew. The colonies of Sasinowo (Sachsenfeld), which Holsche does not mention and which had already arisen in 1798, and Ludwigsberg (Ludwikowo) at first numbered only 4 families from Baden. Its growth proceeded gradually, as settlers flowed in[^51].

From the summer of 1799 the recruiting of colonists for South Prussia was taken in hand by Captain Nothardt. From then on the centralised action gained in strength, and certain fixed principles for it had also by then been worked out. Nothardt presented his proposal and plan for conducting it to the king and the provincial authorities[^52]. As the reason for conducting colonisation more vigorously than until then precisely in South Prussia he gave its strategic and economic importance. In the following year, according to the plan drawn up, after the king had approved Nothardt's proposals, the captain was to go to Germany to direct the recruiting of colonists on the spot. At the same time Frederick William II promised to set aside funds for colonisation to the amount of 50 to 60 thousand thalers. In 1800, 445 Württemberg families were settled in South Prussia, and the king aimed to extend the settling activity still further[^53].
