Only in New East Prussia, after its organisation, did minister Schrötter try to attract colonists, recruited mainly from the Tatar population, from Lithuania and from Volhynia[^24]. The Tatars, however, were settled in New East Prussia not for economic purposes but rather in order to form from the settlers suitable units of light cavalry, which was in fact done later. The fact that the Tatar population and also Lithuanian peasants were settled in this province may best show that Germanising factors did not come into play here, but, as we have already stressed, population and fiscal factors, and in the settling of the Tatars also military ones.

Besides the Tatars and Lithuanians, colonists coming mainly from the Prussian provinces were settled here at this time, which in principle did not agree with Frederick William's intentions.

A certain revival of the colonising action can be observed only with the accession of Frederick William III and the end of the war with France. At this time, too, there was a change in the post of the administrator of the affairs of the province of South Prussia. In place of minister Hoym, one of its first co-organisers, minister Voss, was appointed again.

At the very outset of his reign Frederick William III ordered the General Directory to draw up a plan of colonisation for the individual provinces and to state what had been done in this regard until then. Unfortunately we have no information on what the plan drawn up by Voss for South Prussia looked like. Presumably the minister, while awaiting the king's remarks on and acceptance of the colonisation plan, issued an instruction to the chambers under him in which he declared expressly that this action had not yet begun[^25]. For these reasons he ordered those applying and expressing the wish to settle in South Prussia, both from the Prussian provinces and from other parts of the Reich, to wait until the announcement of the date when the admission of new settlers would begin.

Soon, however, minister Voss together with the provincial authorities was placed in a position of compulsion. In the summer of 1798 the first reports reached Berlin of a larger number of applications from colonists, mainly from Württemberg and Baden[^26]. Württemberg had already, during Frederician colonisation, been a region from which a large number of settlers were recruited. The reason for this was the considerable density of population of this part of Germany; besides, strong propaganda was carried on there, as will be discussed below. In Berlin, however, an increased inflow of colonists was expected at this time mainly from the lands lying on the left bank of the Rhine, because of the occupation of this part of Germany by French troops. Everything therefore pointed to the possibility of beginning the colonising action on a broad scale, otherwise, that is, than had been the case under the previous ruler[^27].
