For it was well understood that in the regions where recruiting was mainly to take place, that is on the left bank of the Rhine and in Württemberg, the kind and manner of farming differed from that in South Prussia. For these reasons it was stipulated that applications should not be accepted from farmers who knew only the cultivation of the vine, but only from those whose speciality was the growing of grain. Cases could be admitted where the colonist knew both kinds of cultivation. The chambers kept fairly strictly to this requirement, rejecting applications from people who did not know farming[^55].

As before, so now the instruction stressed that colonists must possess sufficient means, necessary for running a farm well. The only exception to this rule could be made for day labourers, since it seemed doubtful whether they would possess the required, relatively large, means: 100 thalers. The amount of land assigned to colonists depended solely on the candidate's means. In principle the area of land assigned could not exceed 3 łany (Hufen), although there was the possibility of creating larger farms: of five and six łany. It was adopted as a principle that a colonist settling in South Prussia received: 3 łany of land in perpetual lease, for which he paid a rent in produce and money, timber for building or its equivalent in cash, 250 thalers of non-repayable grant, an advance for setting up of 1,000 thalers, repayable over 5, 8 or 15 years, and finally they were granted years free of obligations, for a period of 3 to 6 years according to the quality and kind of land received, and lastly exemption from military service.

In order to conduct the whole action properly and to co-ordinate it with the activity of the chambers dealing directly with the settling of the arriving settlers, Captain Nothardt was to make monthly reports on his work and send them to the General Directory. In this report, besides the personal particulars and occupation of the settlers, were to be given the amount of their means, the category to which the colonist had been assigned, and finally the administrative district to which he had been handed over[^56]. We have no data on how many colonists were settled in 1801. Accepting the figures given by I. Rhode, it should be supposed that the number of colonists settled at that time ranged from 800 to 1000 persons, that is about 200 families[^57]. The area of land set aside for colonists amounted to 500 łany, and the fund set aside for this action — 200,000 thalers, thus four times the fund of the previous year[^58].
