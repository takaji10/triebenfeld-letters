If the owners would not give their consent to the chambers' proposal, then the whole matter was to be referred for decision to the ministry of justice, which would commission the appropriate Regierung to decide it.

It may give us pause that small manor farms, that is cultivated land, were also set aside for founding colonies, which really did not agree with the fundamental intentions of the colonisation carried on in South Prussia. It was stressed clearly, however, that only those manor farms could be set aside for this purpose which, because of their excessive distance from larger economic units, were unsuitable for growing grain or rearing cattle on a suitable scale.

Minister Voss was well aware that the settlers arriving from the Reich would be reluctant to settle on abandoned peasant farms among a population foreign to them. Besides, there might arise the danger that the colonists, having received a farm of this kind, would have to perform the manorial services to which the rest of the population living in the village was bound. For the earlier instructions stressed clearly that the colonists received the land in perpetual lease, for which they paid only a rent. Seeing this state of affairs, the General Directory gave an instruction that abandoned peasant farms were to be occupied by the local population, or the abandoned land joined to other peasant farms to enlarge them. It was also possible — according to the instruction — to move peasants from farms lying next to the manor farms to the abandoned farms, and to fill their places with arriving colonists classed as cottagers and smallholders, or else to incorporate the land into the manor farms. In this way an attempt was made on the one hand to create larger peasant farms and on the other to assure the royal domains of a labour force for which the main, indeed the only, means of support would be work on the manor farm.

Similar principles were to be applied in founding colonies on land near the towns. The chambers were to see to the building of rented houses in which artisans, day labourers and Lassbauer (lassyci) would find lodging.

This care to settle free hired labourers on the one hand, and then to create large farms for colonists, bore witness to the far-reaching changes already under way in rural conditions. The feudal economy had at this time begun to outlive itself and slowly, first on the royal estates and then on the nobles' manor farms, to take on the forms of a capitalist economy. The transition to new forms of cultivation, the clearing of forests and the draining of marshes required the settling of a large number of free cottagers, gardeners and smallholders, compelled to sell their labour to the owners of manor farms. And so, although in principle there was constant talk of settling colonist-farmers, at the same time especially many hired labourers were settled, so needed on the manor farms.
