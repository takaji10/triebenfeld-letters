# Wąsicki (1953), p. 157
# translation by Claude

To the second category were assigned colonists who possessed means of from 300 to 1000 Reichsgeld, or 162 thal. 12 gr. to 541 thal. 16 gr. They were to be settled on farms of 15 to 40 morgen. Besides land they were to receive travel money, 3 to 6 free years and exemption from the cantons.

The third category was formed by colonists who were to be assigned 3 łany (Hufen) of land. The means required to be assigned to this category were to be from 1000 to 2000 Reichsgeld, or 541 thal. 16 gr. to 1083 thal. 8 gr. They were also granted free of charge the necessary amount of timber for building a house and farm buildings, in kind, or cash for buying it. Besides, they received a non-repayable money grant (Gnaden-Geschenk) of 200 thal. and, for a period of 5, 8 or 15 years, an interest-free loan of 600 thal. The free period was to last 6 to 8 years. The colonists were entitled to freedom from the cantons.

The fourth category was formed by colonists whose means amounted to from 2000 to 3000 Reichsgeld, or 1083 thal. 18 gr. to 1625 thal. They were settled on four łany and granted timber for building, a money grant of 200 thal., an interest-free loan of 600 thal., 3—6 free years and freedom from the cantons.

To the fifth category were assigned colonists whose means amounted to from 3000 to 4000 Reichsgeld, or 1625 thal. to 2166 thal. 16 gr. They received 5 łany of land, and besides timber for building, 3 to 6 free years, an interest-free loan of 1400 thal., repayable, however, during the free years, and finally exemption from the cantons.

The sixth and last category of settlers was formed by those for whom an allotment of land of 6 łany was provided. The required means amounted to from 4000 Reichsgeld to 5000 Reichsgeld, or 2166 thal. 16 gr. to 2708 thal. 8 gr. The help provided for them by the state was of the same amount and kind as for the previous group of settlers.

As follows from this, then, the instruction of 1802 differed fairly considerably from the one issued earlier. Financial help was given in principle according to the category of settlers, which again was closely bound up with the amount of land received. It must be admitted that the first category, to which a fairly considerable number of newcomers was assigned — judging from the chamber's reports and the General Directory's interventions in these matters — nevertheless received fairly substantial help. Colonists assigned to the second and even the third group did not, it is true, receive a non-repayable loan for a fixed period, which was in any case closely bound up with the areas of land received, but for them too the benefits provided were fairly considerable.

From the above instruction it follows fairly clearly that the General Directory was concerned on the one hand with creating relatively large rural farms, of 1 to 6 łany, and on the other with supplying hired labour for work on the manor farms.
