What was meant, naturally, was settling them on the royal domains as hired labourers. Newcomers from Berlin, where they at first applied, were to be directed not only to South Prussia but also to New East Prussia. Frederick William thought it fitting not even to wait for settlers to apply, but to issue a public announcement encouraging settlement in the new Prussian provinces[^28].

For the unexpected newcomers from Württemberg, in accordance with the royal directions, abandoned peasant farms were to be set aside first of all. For this purpose the provincial authorities were ordered to establish their exact number. At the same time they were asked to inform the General Directory of those parts of the province where there was uncultivated land to which arriving colonists could be sent. Also when royal domains were let on lease, regard should be had, in accordance with the royal directions, to the possibility of settling colonists on them[^29]. In the particular cases cited, the General Directory did not lay down as a principle the settling of colonists on abandoned peasant farms; this was rather to be treated as an exception, with a view to finding quickly for the settlers who had arrived land on which they could be settled immediately on arrival. For the fundamental intention of the colonising action was to settle the newcomers on land not cultivated until then, requiring clearing or draining. This may be shown by the king's reply to the complaint of the Württemberg colonists to whom the Poznań chamber had assigned forest land requiring clearing for their settlement[^30]. Land of this kind was also set aside for Württemberg colonists in the neighbourhood of Mosina and Puszczykowo, near Poznań. The Poznań chamber's position in this matter met with the full approval of the General Directory[^31]. The provincial authorities, however, were not prepared to receive the first settlers. From the conduct of the Poznań chamber it may even be supposed that it wished to rid itself as quickly as possible of the unforeseen settlers who caused it nothing but trouble. Its attitude in this matter is shown by the fact that after the first settlement proposals laid before the General Directory it turned to the Białystok chamber in New East Prussia, asking whether in its district there might not be a possibility of settling the colonists who had arrived[^32].
