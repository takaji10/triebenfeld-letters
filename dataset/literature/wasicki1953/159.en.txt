It was further explained that inhabitants of South Prussia could be settled only as cottagers, who were to be entitled to the same benefits as newcomers. Requests of colonists of the first category for enlarging their buildings and building barns were to be rejected, and they were even to be allowed to sell part of the land they received[^76], and this both to colonists and to the local peasants. In this way an attempt was made to prevent them from devoting themselves exclusively to farming the piece of land they had received, which would defeat the purpose of settling newcomers in this category. Nor were the chambers permitted to enlarge the area of land of cottagers, being ordered to refuse all requests of colonists in this matter[^77]. Moreover it was pointed out that in no case should colonisation be treated as provision for invalids[^78]. Minister Voss warned the chambers not to accept as colonists newcomers to whom the recruiting offices had refused permission to settle. For he feared that otherwise too many persons would apply who did not possess the required means and who would become only a burden on the provincial authorities, weighing on the fund set aside for colonisation[^79]. Frederick William, however, as usual in these matters, did not allow them to be removed from the frontiers of South Prussia.

From the instructions and directions given to the chambers by the General Directory it was possible only to some extent to form a view of the legal position of the colonist and his relation to the land he received, and of the obligations laid on him and the rights due to him. The lack of sources to settle this question makes it necessary to turn again to the data from New East Prussia. The legal position of this category of people, however, could not differ, since it rested on the same principles.

The colonist, after arriving in the new province, received land in a place indicated and prepared by the chamber. With the chamber he concluded an agreement regulating their mutual rights and obligations. Under the provisions of the contract the colonist received the land in perpetual lease.
