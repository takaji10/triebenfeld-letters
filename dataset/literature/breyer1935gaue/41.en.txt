Of the 805 who pay church dues in the congregation of Grodziec, 101 families, or 13 in a hundred, are landless; 183 families, or 23 in a hundred, have typical dwarf holdings of 1 to 6 Morgen. In the congregations of Zagórów, Stawiszyn and Prażuchy things are by no means better.

In *house building* building with planks prevails everywhere, which is natural given the great wealth of forest. Since the shallow peaty meadows hold bog iron ore in most cases, this stone is much used for building farm buildings, even dwelling houses. Lately Jewish traders have been buying up these "iron stones" and taking them to the factories of Upper Silesia, where artificial fertiliser is made from them. In the Silesian settlements the dwelling house always stands apart. The farm buildings are often surrounded by birch groves and patches of wood. Because of the marshy, sandy ground, scattered settlement prevails with only few exceptions. The farmsteads lie off the village street, each with its own access road.

Now and then the last traces of the old folk costume are still to be met. Folk art is well kept among the Silesians. Only among them are the finely carved and tastefully decorated grave boards to be seen. Folk dance and folk song are still alive. Folk life always received new impulses from the seasonal work in Germany, though not always in a good sense. There is almost no link with the Łódź industrial area.

### History of Settlement

Around 1740 the German settler made his way with axe and spade into the tracts of the Kalisz country, remote from men and untilled since time immemorial. In 1746 the owner of the estate of Trąbczyn, Paul von Kolno-Prusiński, concluded with honest Hollanders a contract of hereditary lease (Erbpachtvertrag), by which the woods and waste lands south of the estate of Trąbczyn were given over to be brought under cultivation. The village of Łazińsk Holland arose. The castellan of Sieradz, J. Wężyk, settled four German villages on his estates at Grodziec in 1772: Borowiec, Konary, Grunde and Wielołęka. On the lands of the estate of Ceków a whole series of Holländereien arose after 1770, among them the large village of Prażuchy, which by 1799 already counted 34 farmers on 24 Hufen; in 1775 Poroże was founded; in 1775 Zbiersk Holland and Ciświca-Holland, in 1778 Lipnica and Jarantów, in 1780 Jaszczury and Zakrzyn.
