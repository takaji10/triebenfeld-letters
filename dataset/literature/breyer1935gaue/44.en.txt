# Breyer (1935a), p. 44
# translation by Claude

The town of *Kalisz* was rebuilt by the Prussian government after the fire of 1793 and settled with many Prussian officials. German craftsmen, merchants and tradesmen joined them. After 1831 many German entrepreneurs left Kalisz; even so it counted 2405 Germans in 1860, 2500 Evangelicals in 1913, and in 1921 only 1297 Evangelicals. The Kalisz cloth factory of Repphahn, founded in 1816, was known far beyond the borders of central Poland. Not far from Kalisz, in Opatówek, Fiedler founded a fine-cloth factory in 1823.

### Origin

Apart from the villages of Józefów, Wielopole and part of Białobłoty, in which Pomeranian farmers settled, the German population of the Kalisz country is of *Silesian* origin. The Silesian dialect, with certain gradations, prevails without exception. Custom and usage, folk art and folk song are of a truly Silesian stamp. In the church books of the Evangelical congregations of Grodziec, Stawiszyn and Zagórów the places of origin given for the immigrants are above all villages in the neighbourhood of: Bentschen, Neutomischl, Grätz, Wollstein, Tirschtiegel, Zduny, Wreschen, Pudewitz, Krotoschin, Kirch-Boruj, Militsch, Heinzenburg, Parchwitz, Wohlau, Brieg. All these places lie within the area of the Silesian dialect, in southern Posen and the neighbouring Lower Silesia. The settlers came from the new settlements that had arisen in the 17th and 18th centuries. In the wooded, swampy areas of the Kalisz country they found soil like the soil they were used to at home around Grätz, Neutomischl and Wollstein. The growing of hops, carried on in many Kalisz colonies until the World War, also points to the Neutomischl area.

Only a few of the stated origins point to other dialect areas. Only from the neighbouring Oder marshes did a limited number of immigrants come, from the neighbourhood of Landsberg an der Warthe, Sonnenburg and Driesen.

### Schools

A report written in 1797 by Pastor Grimm of Stawiszyn for the consistory in Posen gives German elementary schools in the following Hollander villages: Zbiersk, Prażuchy, Kosmowo, Dannowitz, Zamenty, Zakrzyń, Przespolew, Czachulec, Grojetz and Mycielin. With the schools in Obory, Łazińsk, Białobłoty, Gadowskie Hol. and Wierzchy, which also already existed then, this gives a total of 15 German elementary schools.
