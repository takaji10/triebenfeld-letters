And when such a commission stands, it can never be disturbed.

Since boundary cases and their decision are accustomed to take ^[*Constitution 1768, under the title: Union of the Crown Tribunal in Boundary Cases, fol. 220.*] the most time in the Tribunals, and justice is frequently made to suffer through inadequate and poor delineation of maps produced before the court — therefore We enact *lege præsenti* that such cases shall necessarily have their first instance before the land court, which court *non intrando in cognitionem* of that case, nor prescribing any rule, shall send it to the plot *cum præfixione Termini Condescensoralis Granicialis & optione conducendi Geometræ iurati*; and there the Sub-Chamberlain's office — whether the Sub-Chamberlain himself (who all shall be sworn by the formula in the constitution of 1588) or his Boundary Chamberlains — admitting no appeal *in præliminariis causæ*, with prior summons of the collateral heirs, having regard to the documents and boundary markers or the natural boundary, and *in defectu* of those obvious ones, with prior sworn inquiry and testimony of elderly witnesses, shall adjudicate that case *finaliter*.
