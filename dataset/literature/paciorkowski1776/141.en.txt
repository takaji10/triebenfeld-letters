As regards the boundaries of Our nobility, We have thought fit to ^[*The party who shows more probable markers shall be admitted to confirmation.*] provide as follows: that if it should happen that any litigant for boundaries should show or produce the mounds called *kopce* — at least three — or other notable markers in the open country, commonly *wiązdy*; and in forests, markers in the manner of a cross, as is the custom — then Our Sub-Chamberlain, discerning the one who has shown better and more notable markers or mounds, shall under his honour and conscience be required and bound to send him to prove his case. Where, however, both parties lack notable markers between the estates — then the plaintiff shall be admitted to prove the boundaries with witnesses as the law requires.

### *The Sub-Chamberlain shall not transpose the session of the parties, unless they consent or a cause exists.*

^[*Herburt, ibid.*] Since Sub-Chamberlains or their Boundary Chamberlains for the most part transposed the delimitations of boundaries, either out of favour or on account of the necessity of one party, the other being unwilling — and from this, through negligence, favourable delays were committed in the delimitation of boundaries — We, providing for this error, have decreed and decree to be perpetually observed: that from now on, the Sub-Chamberlains of all lands of the entire Kingdom shall not transpose boundary sessions of their own accord, against the order of the law,
