The boundary path having been concluded and determined, regarding the same corner point written above, the court proceeded to the review of the cited party's boundary path. First the cited party took the commencement of its boundary path from those same corner mounds where the plaintiff's party had determined its boundary path, from the east toward the west; and setting aside that same small river which the plaintiff's party had alleged to be the natural boundary, proceeding toward that same western quarter, he demonstrated to the court an ancient hillock resembling a mound, standing a hundred paces from the corner mounds, alleging it to be the first and true wall mound, destroyed only by cattle, standing under the very forest called *Nart*. Against which demonstration of the mound, the Right Honourable [names] protested, alleging it to be not a true mound but a hillock.
