# Paciorkowski (1776) [= Lochowski 1641], p. 21
# translation by Claude

villages, mills, ironworks, works of iron, glass, paper, and all others of every kind and description — estates, that is; and these and other features of the same kind, positions and situations of places, circumstances, adjacencies, and connexions must be taken note of and specified and expressed precisely in the boundary act. Furthermore, the erection of boundary markers and metal markers is of two kinds: either by corner mounds on both sides of the boundary wall, three, or according to the convergence of estates and boundary walls even four, which are also called particular corner mounds and corner mounds, erected and built in a triangle or quadrangle in a rounded mound shape from earth; and by several wall mounds between the estates of both parties, erected and built from earth by the same method, or marked out in the open country by a ditch; of which mounds ^[*The straight line is to be observed.*] the straight line, the orderly and just spacing of each from the next, the number — how many paces each is from the next — together with the metal markers placed in the mounds, and with the abovementioned definition of the places, shall be described in the boundary act.

It is further to be noted that, in the completion of boundaries, ^[*The natural boundary is to be observed in the absence of boundary markers.*] rivers, waterways, brooks, and torrents, their channels, courses, and beds, are of the greatest observance as natural boundaries and limits, and are assigned to estates as a perpetual boundary, in the absence of boundary markers.
