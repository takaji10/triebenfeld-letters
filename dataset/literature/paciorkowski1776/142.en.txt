# Paciorkowski (1776) [= Lochowski 1641], p. 142
# translation by Claude

under a penalty of fourteen marks to be exacted by Us from whoever shall do otherwise — except where the consent of the parties shall concur, or a legitimate cause shall intervene.

A party condemned in the land court ^[*Herburt, fol. 477.*] is not admitted to contest in the field; indeed, notwithstanding any documentary evidence whatsoever, or more apparent signs, if the defendant should produce any at that time, it is nonetheless to stand with the plaintiff's conduct and the oath with witnesses.

At that same field session, it shall not be permitted to the cited party to raise any contest before the Sub-Chamberlain or his office regarding the right of path — which he has already lost in the land court — but the plaintiff shall lead the office according to his own will and conscience; and shall make the boundaries by raising mounds and making other metal markers, notwithstanding any opposition whatsoever of the cited party, nor any documentary evidence or more apparent signs, if any he should produce at that time. ^[*The plaintiff shall confirm as seventh.*] When these things are done, beside the final and particular corner mound, the plaintiff shall swear with six witnesses of equal rank regarding the true and just delimitation: that he has taken nothing of the land and estate from the cited party, and alienated nothing from him.

Furthermore, if the condemned party should not comply even in the field, ^[*Herburt, fol. 478.*] the same applies: where the plaintiff shall have sworn the oath with witnesses, the Sub-Chamberlain decrees that the boundaries shall endure in perpetuity.
