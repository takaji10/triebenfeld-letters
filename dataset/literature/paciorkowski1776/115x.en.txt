# Paciorkowski (1776) [= Lochowski 1641], p. 115x
# translation by Claude

The nature of boundaries is in the manner of a building that has four walls and corners, otherwise known as *węgielniki*. An estate of great length and breadth, however, whose neighbouring estates meet it with their own boundary walls — in such a case one estate may have not only four but five, six, seven, or even eight boundary walls, and as many corner points, according to the model of the present diagram. [T.N. 1]

A river delimiting estates as a natural boundary.

The same river's new course, which is not reckoned as the boundary.
