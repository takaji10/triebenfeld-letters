# Paciorkowski (1776) [= Lochowski 1641], p. 22
# translation by Claude

If a river or stream naturally and spontaneously diverts itself, the ancient channel is to be treated as the boundary; ^[*The ancient course of a river, for the delimitation of estates and inheritances.*] but if it is through the deliberate effort of a man, then the new channel or course is to be taken as the boundary, while the old channel entire is to belong to the estate in which it is situated.

Moreover, parcels of an estate or ^[*It is lawful to reclaim avulsed portions, notwithstanding prescription.*] quantities of land, fields, meadows, forests, and plots avulsed in any manner from their principal part — that is to say, from the estate and possession — and occupied, may by virtue of an act of general boundary restoration be reincorporated into their principal part or head; nor shall the allegation of any prescription stand in the way, provided the plaintiff confirms the same parcel within his boundary path by oath.

In actions and cases of boundaries no prescription runs; actions of boundaries are not extinguished by prescription and discharge; and it is likewise open to the plaintiff, at such time as he wishes and is able, to bring an action for boundaries; and the adjudication of boundaries by the ordinary judge is always open, just, and equitable.

In boundary cases, for winning ^[*Three things are required to establish the corner point in law.*] and legally establishing the right of path, three things are of primary necessity: documentary evidence, from which the truth is drawn; real mounds and clear and credible markers; and the acknowledgement of both boundary walls by the collateral heirs. By which real acknowledgement the corner point is established, the parties disputing the same among themselves.
