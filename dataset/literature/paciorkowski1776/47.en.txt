# Paciorkowski (1776) [= Lochowski 1641], p. 47
# translation by Claude

evidence or sworn inquiries — favouring no party but having only GOD before its eyes — the court proceeded to the erection and raising of the mounds, first the corner mounds and then the wall mounds. The cited party — both the principal respondent and the collateral heir — moved to the Crown Tribunal courts of the Kingdom at Piotrków, not admitted. The party protests the grievance; the court, together with the plaintiff's party, counter-protests that no grievance was done to either the cited party or the collateral heir through the adjudication of the boundary path — having examined the documentary evidence and in accordance with its conscience, it found the plaintiff's party's boundary path to be more probable and adjudged it to that party — and again proceeded to the erection of the corner mounds; first raising one corner mound for the plaintiff's party, namely for the estate of the village of [name], toward the east, another for the cited party and its estate of the village of [name], toward the west — both mounds on one side of the paved road running from the town of [name] to the village of [name] — and a third on the other side of the road toward the north for the collateral estate of the village of [name]; all situated at an equal distance of five ordinary cubits; raised and built up from earth; and in each, metal markers, namely charcoal, brick, and iron slag, together with a glass bottle filled with millet, with a paper sheet placed inside it bearing a description of today's act by which the mounds were raised, so that it may be clear to posterity in the event of any difference
