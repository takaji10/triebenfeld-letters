## V

Nobility might, therefore, be relatively easy to acquire for those who desired it and who had acquired some, at least, of the trappings of nobility. Maintaining an economic position sufficient to support a noble lifestyle without recourse to plebeian pursuits was far more difficult. The nobility might be equal before the law, but it varied widely in its economic condition, from a tiny group at the top, which controlled vast estates to the landless gołota (the naked ones) at the bottom. The fact that the nobility paid extraordinary taxes voted by the Sejm makes it possible, where records have survived, to document this economic diversity. The act introducing the poll tax in 1662 divided the nobility into various groups. Possessores or posesjonaci – noble landholders in possession of allodial or leasehold estates – were to pay 3 zloties, and 1.5 for their wives and children above the age of ten. Impossessionati – nobles without land who earned their living through military service, or service at the Court or on the estate of a rich noble – were to pay 2 zloties. A noble landholder without serfs who worked his own land up to a fixed amount (about 16 hectares), paid 2 zloties for himself and 15 groszy for his wife and children. Poorer nobles – the szlachta częstkowa, who owned or leased part of a village, and the szlachta zagrodowa (smallholders) – each paid 1 zloty, and 15 groszy for their wives and children. In the palatinate of Cracow in 1662, just over half of the nobility (2275 out of 4279) were classified as possessores; 485 (11.5 per cent) were listed as impossessionati; 227 (5.3 per cent) served other nobles as estate factors or managers; 788 (18.4 per cent) served in a more humble capacity; 405 (9.5 per cent) were classified as smallholders and 99 (2.3 per cent) represented the poor nobility who farmed their land themselves.[^30] The 1673 act renewing the poll tax divided the nobility into noble landholders (szlachta osiadła),[^31] nobles in service, and nobles without serfs. In the palatinates of Poznań and Kalisz, 5133 out of 9484 nobles came into the first category (54.1 per cent), 484 were classed as factors or estate managers (5.1 per cent), 3029 (32 per cent) as nobles in service, 361 (3.8 per cent) as poor nobles – mostly szlachta częstkowa – and 477 (5 per cent) as smallholders without serfs.[^32]
