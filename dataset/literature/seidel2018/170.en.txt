## 17 Family Letters IV (Marianne to Cosmar, 1811, and to August, 1816/17)

Four letters by Marianne are presented here.[^133] For her, political events did not play so decisive a role. What stood in the foreground for her was the separation from her children and her new circumstances of life with her two small daughters. But she knew, of course, learned it from letters, that FL's own circumstances of life had also changed dramatically after the defeat at Jena and Auerstädt, including the surrender of his princely office, his captivity in Öhringen, and his banishment to Upper Silesia. Something of this is reflected here.

FL is never named directly in her extensive correspondence; "the Prince," "August's father," or "Slawentzitz" — that is, the place, or rather the castle — are the usual circumlocutions.

The following letter of 1811 was addressed to Cosmar, the Berlin Councillor of Medicine and Justice, who had attended to Princess Sacken until her death on 6 July 1811. She had appointed him executor of her will and endowed him with considerable rights.

At a meeting in Berlin with her son August, presumably also with Cosmar and her husband Sacken, there must have been a dispute, which Marianne presented as a misunderstanding. The letter is reproduced here because — almost hidden — it touches on several themes that shed light on Marianne's inner state, which must indeed be assessed as unstable, and also hints at "circumstances," by which the economic situation might be meant — or perhaps something else?
