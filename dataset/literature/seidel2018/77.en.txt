## 7 Friedrich Ludwig, the Deserted Husband

### 7.1 Separation and Divorce, the Transact (1799–1804)

The reason underlying the separation of the married couple FL and Marianne remained a mystery for a long time. That it was intended to be permanent emerges from the contract drawn up by Fürstin Sacken for her daughter, the Transact. The daughter had granted her mother power of attorney for its drafting. By 9 and 19 November 1799 respectively, everything was complete. It had gone quite quickly.

This document[^61] is an extensive text — 33 densely written pages, divided into 25 paragraphs, in which everything is addressed and settled that is regarded as requiring settlement, both immediately and in the more distant future (a judicial divorce would not follow until 1804.[^62] Its documents refer repeatedly to the Transact).

Without doubt the Transact is the work of the circumspect Fürstin Sacken, though the text does state that she discussed its contents with the daughter and the son-in-law. Paragraph 25 reads:

> *Since, in the absence of Her Serene Highness the Princess of Hohenlohe, Her Serene Highness the mother, acting on behalf of Her beloved daughter, has concluded this Transact — and indeed out of considered maternal solicitude, in order to secure for the future the happiness and honor of a beloved daughter, and the happiness and honor of a beloved princely House descended from her — Her Most Serene Highness the Princess of Sacken hereby undertakes to obtain from Her beloved daughter*
