# Seidel (2018), p. 79
# translation by Claude

> *His Most Serene Highness and His Most Serene Consort, Their Serene Highnesses, both renounce, under the present circumstances, any claim to personal reunion, and mutually consent to live apart from one another, insofar as, in the course of time, the free and joint will of both should not bring this now-necessary separation to an end or annul it.*

Paragraph Two settles the daughter's private allowance. She is to receive 7,000 reichsthalers annually, of which 400 come from the revenues of the numerous estates, and 3,000 directly from the mother.

In Paragraph Three: the daughter receives Schloss Oppurg and its appurtenances.

In the fourth paragraph the Prince leaves to his wife, "for her free disposal and ownership," her jewelry and toilette, library, engravings and materials, a team of horses, a riding horse together with a cob, and two good "closed" carriages, namely one single-seat and two double-seat carriages. Reference is made to the case of inheritance. The young Princess has not yet decided "to make Oppurg her permanent residence." Nevertheless it is stipulated "that, for the formation of her household, the Princess's two chambermaids, her Chamberlain Günther, and the remaining personnel required for service, kitchen, and stable shall depart with her."

Further paragraphs settle the financial provision for the children and grandchildren (of Fürstin Sacken), directed first at the eldest (August), who by right of primogeniture is to become his father's successor, but then also at his brothers and sisters. The costs and revenues of the Silesian and Saxon possessions (though not the South Prussian ones) are set out. Slaventzitz ("consisting of eight villages and 2 colonies, as well as 6 outlying farms"), originally the property of the daughter's father, Count von Hoym, is ceded to FL. It had "already been held in ownership by virtue of marital usufruct."
