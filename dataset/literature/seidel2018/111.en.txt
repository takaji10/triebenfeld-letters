One might have expected that Goethe, besides his Weimar Duke and the commander-in-chief, the Duke of Brunswick, would also mention other participants, perhaps FL as well. But of him there is no mention anywhere. And yet they must have met, for instance at the evening conversation circles in the respective field camps that Goethe describes. Charles Augustus probably introduced the two to one another; there is, however, no document in which either Goethe or FL reports on a meeting. Nevertheless, the two of them probably retained a memory of this time when they did indeed meet again later:

It was in 1806, in Jena, during a few days at the beginning of October. Napoleon's army was advancing. Some confusion prevailed in the Prussian army command over its plans of action. Things did not go, contrary to what he himself probably expected, according to FL's own conceptions, but he had to follow the orders of the now 72-year-old Duke of Brunswick, behind whom the King had placed himself. FL arrived in Jena on 2 October. On 3 October he at once gave the great banquet described below. On 4 October a council of war was held at headquarters in Erfurt, and it was resolved that the right wing of the army should be led by General von Rüchel from Eisenach, the main army by the King and the Duke of Brunswick from Erfurt, and the left wing, with the Prussian and Electoral Saxon troops, by FL from Gera. On 8 October FL was again present in Jena.

FL lodged in the castle. Duke Charles Augustus had had quarters made ready for him there. Voigt, the head of the cabinet in Weimar, had pointed out to Charles Augustus by letter that Goethe was already lodged in the castle.[^83] Goethe had only just returned in August from Karlsbad and was now there in his capacity as Weimar Commissary of Provisions.
