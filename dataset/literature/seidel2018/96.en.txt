# Seidel (2018), p. 96
# translation by Claude

On the supply situation: "Our men suffered such a want of provisions that they plundered several villages." Count Schlippenbach

> *begged with wringing hands that the mischief be checked. Was it checked? Or rather, could it be checked? Hunger hurts! the men cried, when one wished to hold them back. And the officers felt only too keenly that hunger hurt.*

Fischer then writes of this situation:

> *The disorder spread ever further. On the night of the 21st to the 22nd [of October] a hussar had committed an assault upon his cavalry captain and wounded him with his sabre. Hard as it was for the Prince, he had to have him shot, and seized the occasion to speak out most emphatically at the parole against the spreading indiscipline. Yet the example no longer served as a deterrent; the sharpest words, as the officers' conduct immediately afterward showed, no longer bore any fruit.[^72]*
