added to this are territorial consolidations on the territory of the former Holy Roman Empire of the German Nation, such as the Confederation of the Rhine, and the Partitions of Poland with the rise and temporary fall of Prussia. These external events are accompanied by an intimated change in the attitude of the nobility, who no longer live purely in feudal fashion off the dues of peasants (and vintners) and off forest holdings, but instead seek out tasks for themselves in the military and in state administration, sometimes also engage themselves in (proto-)industrial activity, and send the next generation to university. — Personal dramas, as with Friedrich Ludwig and his wife Marianne, née Countess Hoym, unfortunately also occur along the way.

In engaging with this subject, it mattered a great deal to find a balance between the personal material on Friedrich Ludwig (hereafter mostly abbreviated as FL) and on Marianne and their immediate circle, on the one hand, and, on the other, the information on contemporary historical background that seemed necessary, which is interspersed and marked as "Excursus." Christian von Massenbach occupies a special role. For this reason a separate excursus, "Massenbach," is devoted to him as FL's companion and counterpart. — A balance was also needed between the personal material and the extensive economic information from Gerlinde Kraus's book (2001); the author undertook the labor of archival research in Upper Silesia for that work, with admirable success!

Archival work is also a distinctive feature of this book: the intensive use (in part also verbatim reproduction) of the extraordinarily rich sources in the form of letters and documents in the Hohenlohe Central Archive at Neuenstein (HZAN) and, via the Kalliope network of archives (a German union catalogue of archival finding aids), as well as elsewhere (see p. 251f.).
