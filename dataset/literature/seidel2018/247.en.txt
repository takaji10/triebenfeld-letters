In this manner the second requirement for an order of arrest is likewise satisfied here. The circumstance that v. Triebenfeld is the original debtor of the 50,000 and the said interest, and that the 80,000 florins here in question have been bequeathed to His Serene Highness the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, cannot be prejudicial in this matter. For the Prince, in respect of the 250,000 florins against whose pledging and eventual surety 50,000 florins were advanced by the General Invalids' Fund to v. Triebenfeld, is a debtor of v. Triebenfeld, and the General Invalids' Fund is, by virtue of its [...] surety, entitled to exercise all rights against the Prince which by [Gedenken] belong to v. Triebenfeld, for it has, by virtue of its surety, stepped into the place of the latter. The arrest upon these 80,000 florins is in every respect well founded, whether the Prince still be the owner thereof or should already have relinquished them to v. Triebenfeld for his satisfaction. Both the Prince and von Triebenfeld are bound to the General Invalids' Fund and neither now offers it any security. Von Triebenfeld does indeed possess estates in the lands here, but they are so encumbered with debt that they afford no security, and thus, pursuant to § 10 [...] 29 of the Court Ordinance, arrest lies against them, even though he is resident in royal lands.
