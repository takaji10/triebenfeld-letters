With Christian Wagner he had a special connection: in a true act of patronage, he financed for him, then twenty-nine years old, a two-year stay in Italy in 1782 (the classic tour) and a year in Dresden in 1787! Quite something! "I shall bring Schillinger along" was a remark made on one occasion when something needed to be settled in Ingelfingen (this Schillinger, to be sure, was not the Court Painter but a master builder — from the same family, originally of Ingelfingen). The painters Wagner and J. J. Schillinger knew each other well; both had attended the Carlsschule in Stuttgart, and Wagner had worked alongside J. J. Schillinger in Öhringen. In Ingelfingen, as already reported, FL appointed him head of the drawing school.

And otherwise? Beyond his involvement with the smithy of the Court Blacksmith mistakenly called the "Mint," nothing further is known of commissions to the sculptor Philipp Christoph Sommer, who had even acquired a house in the Mariannenvorstadt, nor are there any works by Leonhard Kern, which were on view in the aforementioned Hohenlohe exhibition — these came from the other courts. What music was played in Breslau, or rather Alt-Scheitnig, when FL invited guests there for a house concert and a dance? We do not know, and must leave it at this: that he — unlike his son August, who as his successor in Öhringen and later also in Slaventzitz kept a court orchestra — was no votary of the Muses. He evidently did know, however, what befitted a person of such elevated standing as himself — in Breslau, for instance, in the design of Scheitnig Park and the rebuilding of the residence in the city. To his contemporaries, as to us, he was probably noticed chiefly as a soldier and, likewise, as an urban developer.
