# Seidel (2018), p. 113
# translation by Claude

*By the urging of the good people of Jena, with whom I had for so many years stood on good terms, I overstepped the law I had set myself, not to meddle in public affairs; I took the pamphlet and found the author in the spacious old rooms of the Wilhelmian Pharmacy. After renewing our acquaintance, I came forward with my protest, and had, as was to be expected, to deal with a persistent author. But I remained just as persistent a citizen, and expressed the arguments, which indeed carried sufficient weight, with eloquent vehemence, so that he finally gave way. I still remember that a tall, sturdy Prussian, apparently an aide-de-camp, stood there motionless in expression and posture, and probably wondered inwardly at the boldness of a mere citizen. Suffice it to say, I parted from the Colonel on the best of terms, weaving into my thanks all the persuasive arguments that would actually have sufficed on their own, but which now brought about a gentle reconciliation. I also waited upon further excellent men; it was on Friday, 3 October. I found Prince Louis Ferdinand vigorous and friendly, after his manner; Lieutenant-General von Grawert, Colonel von Massow, Captain Blumenstein, the latter young, half-French, friendly and trusting. At noon, dined with all of them at the table of Prince Hohenlohe.*

FL had taken a seat opposite Goethe that day. The latter was flanked by the two aides-de-camp Marwitz and Blumenstein. Blumenstein later reported on the difficulty of finding suitable topics of conversation with the famous man. Finally he asked him about the play "The Bride of Messina" — by Schiller, of course!
