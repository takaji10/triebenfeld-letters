# Seidel (2018), p. 12
# translation by Claude

Casimir's three sons divided their father's inheritance among themselves by drawing lots. Count Albrecht Wolfgang received Langenburg, Friedrich Eberhard received Kirchberg, and Christian Kraft (1663–1743) received Ingelfingen, which already possessed a castle, as well as Schrozberg. He thus became the founder of the House of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen. The "bailiwick of Ingelfingen" then comprised Ingelfingen itself (with Lipfersberg, Kocherstein, and the Scheurachshof), Criesbach, Crispenhofen, and Hermuthausen. It was, and remained, a small holding.

In another respect, the Christian Kraft depicted below (p. 14) had drawn the lucky lot: as compensation for the lack of a residence at Schrozberg, and on account of the obligation to establish a dower seat for his mother at Ingelfingen, a sum of 10,000 florins was included in his inheritance. He then immediately began enlarging the castle and renewing the residence of Ingelfingen, and was able to move there from Langenburg in April 1702. The rooms in the castle did not quite suffice for the removal goods, so in 1704 he purchased rooms in the town hall from the town, which remained occupied for about 100 years (see p. 66f.). The New Castle, which today serves as the town hall, was finally completed around 1712.[^3] For his large family, among them many princesses, the building adjoining the castle to the east (the Lang House) was purchased in 1728, largely demolished, and rebuilt. For his son Heinrich August he purchased from a citizen of Ingelfingen the "Alte Amtskellerei" by the (former) Upper Town Gate, which has since been erroneously known as the Old Castle.

When Christian Kraft died in 1743, his four sons succeeded him with equal rights in the rather small territory of Ingelfingen. Philipp Heinrich (1702–1781) resided below in the New Castle, Heinrich August (1715–1796) above in the Old Castle, Christian Ludwig Moritz (1704–1758) in Schrozberg, and August Wilhelm (1720–1769), a general in the service of the Duke of Saxe-Gotha, far away in Ohrdruff in Thuringia (all four brothers had received an officer's training).
