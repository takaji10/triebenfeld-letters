# Seidel (2018), p. 178
# translation by Claude

> from G. Pfuhl, the W. envoy – he is very devoted to you – and speaks to me quite often of you, August! – He seems a worthy, good man, who knows how to judge without prejudice or one-sided regard. He has also said much good of Alexander to me – you can easily imagine how his conversation on subjects so dear to me made it very interesting for me – I shall leave Berlin the day after tomorrow to travel to Bellin, where I beg you to send your letters to me. – Now, dear August: I close with the most heartfelt thanks – which, though on the last page of my letter, surely stand in the right place in my heart – truly for your kind and loving attention and completion of the two matters here so important, the documents; if I never come to my end with it, then your mutual friendly cooperation, all of you, will increase its value for me, whether and when I attain to it – I must expect from time; the particulars of it you may learn from your father, who knows the whole course of the matter and has taken so noble and kind an interest in it. Give my regards to your beloved Louise and embrace your dear children for me – Angelique and Auguste commend themselves to your loving remembrance./Farewell, dear August/ever your tenderly loving mother/Marianne/At Adolph's request I open this letter again – he begs your forgiveness a thousand times over for not yet having answered you – but the drill hours and his duty overwhelm his time and strength – he sends you the enclosed with the wish that it might have your approval [sic].*

A true mother's letter, in which Marianne struggles hard for the love of her eldest son. Had she really never seen his wife and children until now? August has been married since 1811.
