Lombardini had been FL's Master of the Household in Upper Silesia since 1803.[^93] He worked in Breslau, or rather Scheitnig, and after 1809 was in Slaventzitz. In his function he was responsible "for everything" — for the staff, for guests, for the bookkeeping. In his reports appear the names of General von Grawert, von Triebenfeld, the financier/merchant/banker Goldstücker, and other persons. Among those who came to him with matters from outside were the Justice Commissioner Kippen and the works inspector König, and the forestry master Kaufmann. August had direct contact with Lombardini whenever he visited Slaventzitz.

His bookkeeping also formed the basis, in 1817, for the long list of employees with an indication of the salaries still outstanding (see appendix p. 249), naming 18 persons, headed by Bally, Hahn, and himself, who had the highest wage arrears. August would later pay them out.

Among these was also Court Councillor Hahn, who remained until at least 1815.[^94] Hahn traveled a great deal; on several occasions he reported by letter from Breslau. Among his tasks was the procurement of funds; he negotiated with the banker and jeweler Goldstücker (who, immediately after FL's death, would turn up in Slaventzitz).

The name Triebenfeld is likewise mentioned; no wonder, since the misery of the debts in South Prussia continued unabated (see the lists in the appendix, pp. 239/240).

"Bally" — the equerry and coachman — was present in Slaventzitz. He was mentioned in FL's letters of September 1806 in Berlin and then during the wait in Prussian captivity, and also on one occasion when he drove Triebenfeld far into South Prussia and back to the latter's estate at Kontopp.
