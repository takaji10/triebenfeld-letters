# Seidel (2018), p. 23
# translation by Claude

Marianne's mother was Princess Christiane Charlotte Sophie von der Osten-Sacken (1733–1811), née Baroness of Dieskau, widow of Count Hoym. Her first husband, Julius Gebhard, Count of Hoym (1721–1769, Privy Councillor of Electoral Saxony), was the father of her only child. The castle at Slaventzitz in Upper Silesia, which FL inhabited in the last years of his life, had come into the possession of the later Princess through the Counts of Hoym, and was eventually made over entirely to FL by the daughter. After the Count's death, the mother Christiane married the Chief Chamberlain of Frederick II, Carl von der Osten-Sacken, Prince from 1786, later Privy Minister of State and War, who died in 1794 in Berlin. From 1777 the couple lived in a palace on the Wilhelmstraße in Berlin. The daughter often visited them there, and after her separation from FL, from her residence at Bellin Castle. The Princess died in Berlin — she had long been cared for by the Councillor of Medicine and Justice Cosmar, whom she also named executor in her 100-page will.[^11] The daughter Marianne, too, had dealings with him in his capacity as guardian of her children. As administrator of the estate, the Princess had appointed the Minister of State and Justice von Kircheisen.

As mentioned at the outset, Marianne, after whom the Mariannenvorstadt (Marianne's Suburb) of Ingelfingen is named, separated from her husband and children in 1799, after eighteen years of marriage and the birth of eight children together. This will be discussed again on several occasions. She left the family residence at Breslau. The children left behind were then fifteen, twelve, eleven, and six years old, and the two youngest (Adolf and Alexander) were only two and one year old! The father, FL, had spent the first months of the year in Hohenlohe. He asked his sister Sophie Christiane for help. She was already somewhat acquainted with the older children from their occasional stays in Ingelfingen. Very probably she traveled to Breslau together with FL at once.
