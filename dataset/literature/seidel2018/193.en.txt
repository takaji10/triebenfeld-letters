# Seidel (2018), p. 193
# translation by Claude

> *My good wife, who is devoted to you with all her heart and grateful for so many proofs of your kind disposition, joins her heartfelt wishes for your well-being to mine, and is truly grieved that she cannot convey them to you herself in writing, but unfortunately her constant sufferings render her unable to hold the pen for you. When I returned from the wedding of Count Carl von Erbach at Fürstenau, I found poor Louise once again bedridden with a bilious-mucous fever, which so wore her down that, although she has lately recovered tolerably, she still cannot take a single step across the room without being supported by two persons. This great weakness naturally arouses my lively concern for the moment of her confinement, which I consider to be nearer than Louise herself does, [...], but her strength leads me to believe that it might come sooner. The children are, thank God, quite well, and Fritz is visibly benefiting under his new [...]. We all heard with great interest that you, dear Father, are now better satisfied with your health. May its firmness fully correspond to our wishes. Mine is likewise not particularly good, and the complete lack of hunting this winter — since throughout all of Hohenlohe we must forgo all [...] hunts owing to the quite small number of [...] — also robs me of the foremost occasion for exercise, which is always beneficial. Be so good as to thank dear Aunt on my behalf for her kind, pleasant letter of [...]. From it I learned that Gen. v. Kirch had written that Alexander has been appointed to the Garde du Corps, which pleased me; I therefore believe him to be no longer at Schlaventzitz.
