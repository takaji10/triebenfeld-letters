# Seidel (2018), p. 72
# translation by Claude

FL was, after all, frequently traveling, and so it happened — somewhat surprisingly — that the baptism of Alexander, born on 3 July 1798, was entered in the baptismal register of the parish of Ingelfingen during the period 14–21 July. Did the baptismal act actually take place there? Hardly. The newborn and the woman in confinement, together with attendants, would then have had to travel from Breslau with great speed! FL himself, however, was there at that time, for a letter survives, signed by him in Ingelfingen and dated 17 July, in which he informed the addressee of the child's birth and asked him to stand godfather.[^50]

In the entry, the Duke of Brunswick is named as an absent godfather (he was the addressee of the letter) — alongside other high-ranking gentlemen, e.g. Duke Peter of Courland,[^51] who had lent Friedrich Ludwig 300,000 thalers in 1790. Registered as present godparents were: the Princess Sacken (that is, the grandmother), Princess Sophie Christiane von Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen (the aunt), and Prince Heinrich of Anhalt. — There is no evidence of a visit by the Princess Sacken to Ingelfingen. It is possible that Pastor Knapp, who had held this office since 1768 (that is, even before FL's father's accession to government), allowed himself to be pressed by his princely employer! (assuming he was indeed the one who made the entry). A personal familiarity may be assumed.

Whether the family lived from the outset in the Schlegelberg house in Breslau, which his idol Frederick II had also used for a time (which naturally pleased him particularly), is doubtful; he purchased this town palace on the Albrechtstraße in the year 1794. He had not forgotten Ingelfingen and retained the name Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, incidentally also after he succeeded his childless uncle at Öhringen in 1805. In Prussia this played no role; there he was "Prince Hohenlohe", a regiment bore his name, and he was so styled in official documents. The palace, rebuilt in 1801, later received the designation "Haus Fürst Hohenlohe."
