# Seidel (2018), p. 86
# translation by Claude

*youngest children suffers through the provisions of the maternal testament; should I die sooner, my husband will scarcely be able to raise them according to their rank./My son August knows his sisters – he received them with love and that brotherly benevolence – which only further marks his good, noble, and sensitive heart; and I am likewise convinced of my son Adolph – they are both too good, too noble, for a stepbrotherly trait to be feared from them toward their stepsisters, who are in any case harshly disadvantaged in every respect, and surely you cannot wish for the misfortune of your poor innocent sisters. I demand no generosity – and I shall not beg for my children – not even from their siblings – I therefore merely commend to my beloved sons my two youngest daughters, and repeat, to the satisfaction of all parties concerned, those fair proposals for settlement regarding the purchase of Bellin, which I set out to date under 22 September in my letter to Mr. O.M.R. Cosmar and detailed there more closely, to which I refer in all points./Should this proposal be approved, then I declare – this time not according to prescribed formulas, but – wholly with a free and joyful heart, that I shall then consider myself and my children entirely satisfied, and shall preserve the love of my sons for their sisters and for me in tender maternal feeling – and shall acknowledge the shared responsibility of the gentlemen curators with warm gratitude./Berlin, 12 October 1811/Marianne, Countess von der Osten-Sacken/née Countess Hoym*

Does this text fill the gap that the previous absence of an explanation for the separation has undoubtedly been? Marianne tried thereby to leave behind something that, at the chosen moment, at least the eldest could take note of. She feels the need to present her motives to a small circle of confidants and to the family.
