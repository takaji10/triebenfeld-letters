The princely administration in Öhringen also felt itself responsible and at least kept books on the matter.[^121] The bundle of files for the period from 1804 to 1812 contains the relevant correspondence, with reports from the court, chamber, and domain administrations in Ingelfingen, Öhringen, and Silesia, rulings from government authorities, payment demands from creditors, other letters from private persons, promissory notes, cost statements, receipts, and more.

Almost everyone had debts, to banks and to sellers of estates; mortgage interest could not be paid, so the Berlin General Invalids' Fund would take the matter to court (the document that concludes this case is found in the appendix on p. 243f.). On 25 September 1806, that is, in the tense period before Jena and Auerstädt, FL evidently had occasion to set out his financial situation.

The following letter may have been addressed to the helper Zastrow; this is suggested by the salutation "Your Well-born" in the running text, as well as by the signature, though an original signature is missing:
