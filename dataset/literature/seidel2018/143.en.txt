### 13.2 Triebenfeld, FL, and Massenbach as His Beneficiaries

First a quotation from Friedrich Buchholz's article on FL. The author is not only emotional here, indeed polemical, but nonetheless:

> *His need for money [that of FL is meant] rose, namely, from year to year, and a certain Herr von Triebenfeld, in whose hands his affairs lay, was by far better suited to exploit the Prince's embarrassments to his own advantage than to bring him relief. This Triebenfeld, who through his intrigues worked his way up from a servant to a nobleman, plays so important a role in the Prince's history that the future biographer of the latter (should such a one be found) can scarcely give him too much attention.[^112]*

Triebenfeld had also seen to himself in the founding consistory mentioned above: he had, as already noted, received a donation of eight estates at an appraised value of 51,000 thalers and, some time afterward, sold them for a total of 750,000.

It is he — whom the author Friedrich von Cölln has named — on whom the discussion here must turn, for he became an important helper in FL's acquisition of estates in South Prussia and later, as his General Plenipotentiary, oversaw his disastrous finances right up to the Congress of Vienna. FL had knowledge of the opportunities on offer in Berlin.
