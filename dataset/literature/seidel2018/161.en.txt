# Seidel (2018), p. 161
# translation by Claude

Twenty-six letters can be examined in the Neuenstein archive, five of which fall within the years 1807/08, and thus were sent to Öhringen. Kahlert writes them as an independent free man, with great respect for the recipient. FL also replies and poses questions, as can be gathered from many of the openings of Kahlert's letters. He regularly gave thanks with formulations typical of the age: "[...] as I venture to assure Your Highness that my correspondence is not unwelcome to you, I take the liberty of troubling you anew with my writing." He also had a feel for what might be worth knowing for the "Most Serene Prince and most gracious Lord." From two letters, of 10 December 1807 and 16 April 1808, both of which also refer to preceding letters of the Prince's, the following is quoted by way of example and at somewhat greater length. They yield a picture of the times — one which FL, in Öhringen and then also in Slaventzitz, could otherwise not have obtained.

### 16.2 Kahlert as "Correspondent from the Capital"

Letter of 10 December 1807, from Breslau to Öhringen. The topics are: an (alleged) advance in the property and financial matters that Triebenfeld was pursuing for FL. – The news of a decree of the King abolishing serfdom in the countryside, so that now any citizen could purchase noble estates, which had caused a great sensation,

> *[since] the peasants will now begin to withdraw from all their obligations toward the manorial lords [...] the nobility [is] in a bad position. The King has [with regard to the French authorities, that is, the occupying power] agreed that 270,000 reichsthalers would be paid monthly. [...] Danger to the pension funds for the generals.*
