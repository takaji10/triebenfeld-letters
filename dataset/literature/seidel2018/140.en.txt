## 13 The "Squandering of Estates" after the Third Partition of Poland

### 13.1 Excursus IV: "South Prussia". The "Founders' Consortium" and its Allocation Practices

The second (1793) and third Polish partition (1795, among Russia, the Danubian Monarchy, and Prussia) led to a considerable expansion of the territory under Prussian rule (with an almost exclusively Polish population). The designation for the relevant part of Poland here was "South Prussia". Central places were Posen, Kalisch, and Warsaw; the "Chamber Departments" were also named after these towns, and these in turn were divided into roughly ten districts each. Politically, South Prussia was subordinate to a General Directorate in Berlin.

After 1806 (the Peace of Posen) and 1807 (the Peace of Tilsit), and then again in 1815 (the Congress of Vienna), substantial changes occurred. Thus, for example, in 1807 large parts of South Prussia became the Duchy of Warsaw, in union with Saxony. In 1815 a large part of it became Prussian once again: the Province of the Grand Duchy of Posen. This was of considerable relevance for the owners of the estates granted by the Prussian King in South Prussia. – The Elector of Saxony had become King in 1806 and, from 1815, ruled in personal union over the remaining parts of West Poland, where his ancestors had themselves once been kings.

Prussia's King Frederick William II had already, before 1795, promoted immigration from many German lands into South Prussia. From September 1798 dates an "Energetic Order for the Continuation of Colonization in the Newly Acquired Provinces of South Prussia and New East Prussia" issued by his successor Frederick William III.
