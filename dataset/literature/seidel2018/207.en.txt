After the establishment of the Kingdom of Württemberg, August was a member of the Württemberg Estates Assembly (Ständeversammlung) from 1815 to 1819, serving as its President from 1815 to 1816. After a new constitution was finally accepted in 1819, he was a member of the Chamber of the Lords of the Estates (Kammer der Standesherren) from 1820 to 1849 (the year of his resignation as Prince of Hohenlohe-Öhringen), again serving as President from 1820 to 1835. From 1838 to 1842 he had himself represented there by his son Friedrich, and thereafter by his son (Friedrich Wilhelm Karl) Hugo. In the early years of this political activity he must also have had contact with Christian von Massenbach, who likewise belonged to the Estates Assembly, though only briefly.

With regard to finances, August had not taken on an easy inheritance in Öhringen. When his father took over Öhringen, other Hohenlohe princes had for their part also lodged their own claims to possessions. FL already had a great deal to deal with on this account as early as 1805. His mountain of debt was immense, and there had been malicious tongues because of it. Somehow, however, August managed to cope with it. His inheritance from the fortune of his grandmother Sacken was, after all, considerable. Added to this was possibly also the circumstance that he had married "well." Nevertheless, after Hohenlohe was taken over by the Kingdom of Württemberg, Öhringen came "under the administration" of the Württemberg Kameralamt ("Ministry of Finance").

As noted, a substantial contribution to August's finances had been made, through the Transact of 1799 (see p. 77f.) and also through her testament, by his far-sighted grandmother, Princess Sacken, who died in 1811.
