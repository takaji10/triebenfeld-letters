In October he received the commission: he was assigned to the Prince as Cavalry Captain (Rittmeister) and aide-de-camp. The Prince was to arrive in Dresden on 3 November (1805).

> *Prince Hohenlohe commanded in Dresden the army, which consisted of 38 Prussian and 19 Saxon battalions and 50 Prussian and 20 Saxon squadrons, with 92 guns, the battalion numbering 700 men, the squadron 120 men, together at least 48,000 men. [...] In Dresden everything was ready for war and had the highest confidence in Prince Hohenlohe, who was known from the campaigns on the Rhine as the most enterprising of commanders. When I arrived in Dresden, our army was already fully underway on its march to Erfurt, to Hohenlohe's great vexation, since he had wished to march straight into Bohemia [...]. The Emperor [Tsar Alexander] remained in Berlin until the 10th [of November] and arrived in Dresden on the 11th. On the 12th he had a lengthy conference with Prince Hohenlohe, at the close of which we aides-de-camp were presented to him. The Emperor said: "The best compliment one could pay them is that they stand so close to Your Highness."[^78] Altogether, I have never seen a reigning sovereign who knew so well how to win over everyone as Emperor Alexander did. On the 13th he left Dresden in order to go straight through Bohemia to Olmütz [...].*

Von Marwitz did not mention that on this occasion in Dresden Tsar Alexander presented FL with the Order of St. Andrew.

> *In my post as aide-de-camp I had absolutely no duties whatsoever. There were two older aides-de-camp who had long been with the Prince, Major v. Pirch and Major v. Röder.*
