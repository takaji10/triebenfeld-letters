# seidel2018  55  en
# Seidel (2018), p. 55
# translation by Claude

Not a great deal emerges from this small piece: FL has written to her and encouraged her to spend money. She evidently holds FL's sister, Sophie Christiane, in great affection. In Berlin, including at court, she is treated well. She is invited out, goes dancing, and has many acquaintances.

All this seems to have little to do with high politics. But perhaps further reports were of significance to FL nonetheless — perhaps he had even asked her to pay particular attention to certain matters. For Marianne reported not only society gossip but also, in part, on persons and circumstances of particular interest to him, especially now that he had taken part in the meeting at Pillnitz as a member of the King's council of advisers. There the subject had been revolutionary France and how to deal with it — out of which came the Wars of the Coalition.

The royal house was always of interest. Marianne repeatedly mentioned the Duke of B. (sic, meaning the Duke of Brunswick). He would, according to Marianne, meet with the King in Potsdam and afterward travel to Vienna (there he was to discuss with allied Austria the campaign against France). At the same time, however, it seemed that in Berlin the power of another man in military matters was increasing. The Duke was far too entangled in all manner of business! Numerous other names were mentioned, among them that of Prince Louis Ferdinand, with whom she got on well.

Shortly afterward, on 3 March 1792, she wrote another letter. The opening of the letter is exceedingly formal, as, in my view, is still very apparent even in Monika Radermacher's translation (Marianne has now been staying in the Osten-Sacken household in Berlin for four weeks):

> *My dear Friend!*  
> *Your two letters have given me great joy, and the proofs of your trust, your friendship, and the kindness with which you wish to tell me everything that concerns you flatter me and touch me*
