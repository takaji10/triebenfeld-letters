FL received in all twelve letters from Glenck that year, and the correspondence continued at a somewhat lower frequency until 1817, first from the address Breslau, thereafter Slaventzitz.

In 1810 there was finally some good news for once: in the Friedrich-Ludwig shaft a new salt spring was tapped. Glenck reported this to the Privy Councillor von Braun at Öhringen. He replied:

> *However pleasing this event was to me, Your Right Honourable Excellency will readily believe that, as a grateful and loving son of my late father, I hoped with such longing that I might yet be able to save his honour after all. This has now, thank God, come to pass, and the joy of my heart is especially great on this account. But also in the main matter, with respect to the gracious Lordship, this event is most gratifying, particularly at the present moment. For the saline works will now certainly become, within a short time, a profitable enterprise, and will also be able to contribute its share toward the discharge of the general burden.*

Father and son Braun had already been councillors in the princely administration at Ingelfingen. The positive assessments of the news itself unfortunately had no consequences: von Braun found no support in the Öhringen administration for his enthusiastic estimate. There was no wish to invest any further.
