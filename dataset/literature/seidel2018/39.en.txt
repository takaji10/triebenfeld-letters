# Seidel (2018), p. 39
# translation by Claude

This second building phase began in 1786 and lasted until 1804 or 1806. In between, admittedly, the building fund — managed by the bailiff (Amtmann) Taurinus — ran dry. Then in 1792 came a good wine year, and the winegrowers' dues replenished the fund somewhat. Taurinus lived in the "Old Bailiff's House" (Altes Amtshaus, on today's Bühlhofstrasse).

Johann Georg Taurinus served the lord of Ingelfingen here. J.G.T. had owned the rear building, purchased the front building to add to it, and brought the whole into good repair. "1743" and a monogram "J.G.T." can be read on the side of the house wall. – In the cemetery there still stand two monumental, though somewhat weathered, grave memorials commemorating him and a predecessor of the same name.

In 1789 there must have been occasion for a new decree:

> *In order not to draw only strangers into the trade, We admonish the parents of such young people who have the desire and aptitude to devote themselves to those crafts which are either not to be found here at all or not in sufficient number [...] We shall, where necessary, have capable young people supported out of the charitable foundations available. Should, however, one of the scholarship recipients settle abroad, We find it equitable that he repay what has been expended upon him [Everything, in some form, has already happened before!]. But in order to do away with the bunglers among the craftsmen, henceforth no one shall be accepted as a subject who cannot produce a certificate of craft or a trial piece.[^21]*
