### 22.6 Arno Schmidt's "Historical Revue"

It was above all Massenbach's clear avowal, in his speeches and writings, of the ideals of the French Revolution and of a parliamentary system, that moved the writer Arno Schmidt (1914–1979) in 1949 to occupy himself with him.[^165] He saw in him a kindred spirit, an independent thinker, an early European. He wrote a "Historical Revue" entitled "Massenbach," in which the sequence of years since 1792 is presented as a stage drama, essentially as dialogues among the decisive figures of Prussia during Massenbach's lifetime. Almost all of them appear: the King, the Duke of Brunswick, Marwitz, Zastrow, and also our Prince Friedrich Ludwig; there is also a good deal of gossip; Fouqué, Arno Schmidt's favorite, could not be missing. Schmidt's assessment ran as follows:

> *He was a man who even then strove for the unity of Europe and wished to create safeguards against Russia. One of his prophecies was that in a hundred years the Russians would stand before Strasbourg, and that only America, alongside them, would be a serious factor of power. [...] this, in such consistency the first European, became to me so strangely and fraternally familiar that I confess — I wish to try to renew his memory among others as well.*

The whole piece idealizes Christian von Massenbach, whom we have also come to know as a very difficult, or at least controversial, character, but it is well worth reading nonetheless.
