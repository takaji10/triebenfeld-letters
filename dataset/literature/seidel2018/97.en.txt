### 9.3 Prisoner in Öhringen

FL was brought to Öhringen and was held there under house arrest as a French prisoner of war, a condition he observed. This does not mean that in the following two years he remained cut off from all communication. There was an exchange of letters and also communications carried by disguised messengers. He conducted a large part of his correspondence with Glenck from here (see pp. 129f.).

His son Prince August II had taken over his former duties in Öhringen in the summer of 1806; FL remained a prisoner of the French occupation (under house arrest in Öhringen Castle) until the end of 1808.
