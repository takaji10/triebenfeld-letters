In addition he purchased an estate at Alt-Scheitnig, in order to develop it as a residence and summer house outside the town, probably as early as 1783. The grounds comprised the properties on the eastern bank of the Old Oder. The connection to the town was made via the newly laid-out Fürstenstraße and the Fürstenbrücke. There he established himself, one might say, in feudal style, and added to the building complex the princely dwelling house, stables, coach houses, a billiard house, an orangery, and a riding ground.[^52] To this place he regularly withdrew. He had the Öhringen Court Painter J. G. Schillinger, who had also worked in Ingelfingen, paint rooms there as late as 1805. The design for the so-called Fürstengarten (Prince's Garden) was probably developed by FL himself with the help of the already renowned Breslau architect Carl Gottfried Langhans, the builder of the Brandenburg Gate in Berlin. The grounds contained a French garden and a landscape garden (the so-called "wild garden"), which merged into the adjoining municipal forest. He laid out paths and had an avenue of trees planted. In the "wild garden" the princely green spaces were not fenced in; thus they were accessible to the broad public as the town's first public park. On the occasion of one of the regular royal visits, FL had a statue of Frederick II erected, as well as antique figures. Here invitations, dancing, and musical performances alternated with one another. There is documented evidence of the performance of a play, in which he had to play the heroically conceived leading role (and thereby presumably, indirectly, himself). The Prussian King Frederick William II was also his guest. In August 1790, following a great dinner at Breslau castle, FL was visited by him with his entire entourage, which also included Goethe and Charles Augustus of Weimar[^53] — they were soon to meet again during the Coalition Wars against France.

Frederick William III was also there on several occasions, when he was in Silesia, usually in August, on his inspection tours ("reviews"); FL surprised him there in 1788, 1798, 1800, and 1804 with a particular display of splendor, lantern illumination, even with statues of the royal couple.
