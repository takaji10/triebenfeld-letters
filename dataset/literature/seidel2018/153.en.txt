Even the Princess complained about the billeting of French troops with her at the town palace and their costs. But independent of this, her own new acquisitions, such as Bellin Castle, where Marianne lives with her new family, and further estates in Mecklenburg, are also considerably encumbered. In 1809 this amounted to around 90,000 thalers, so that Marianne considered using the 4,000 thalers due to her annually from her inheritance for debt repayment.

With his creditors, FL still had one more chance, in the view of the author Fischer,[^126] because with new loans he first paid off the old debts. But things grew ever tighter, and the Princess became aware of this too. She wrote him at least one, already quoted here, very plainly worded letter — that he ought to have turned to her much earlier with his transactions — and asked her grandson August not to assume any further guarantees for his father. She was a knowledgeable and shrewd woman. She did not live to see her son-in-law's further financial decline; she died in 1811. Shortly before that, she had apparently still been willing to help him with 20,000 thalers.

Triebenfeld remained in constant contact with FL. The correspondence is extremely extensive and proves that he personally attended to FL's possessions in South Prussia as well.[^127] Thus he reported that it was necessary to hire a chief forester in Zagorow ("he must be content with 300 florins annually"). In this unfortunately undated letter, he also asks FL for his opinion regarding an exchange of estates (which never took place).
