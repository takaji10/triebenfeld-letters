# Seidel (2018), p. 171
# translation by Claude

> *me you have sufficiently seen, and now not, so far as possible, to forestall the consequences thereof through any account of the truth [sic]. The love of my son and his good opinion is my everything — for he deserves mine unreservedly, and I pay it to him with the most maternal feelings, which are the greatest blessing to my heart. — I wished to write to him myself at once — on second thought it seemed better to me to [...] [give] you this explanation — which I implore you — to communicate to him at once — with all frankness, then — never did I mention to my son anything of our circumstances [see above], with regard to the testaments — of which, moreover, I still know too little word for word — and besides, also in our meetings, I so gladly seek to indulge my heart through the pure outpouring of feeling, both mutually and also as a remembrance of our beloved mother and of my children — thus my son never mentioned his father in this connection, still less was there any talk of what I am supposed to have said — and on the other side, I could never have mentioned anything of it to my husband — since that was not at all the case, and I would likewise never have done so — because all carrying tales back and forth is thoroughly repugnant to my character — still less would I permit myself such an untruth, since truth is the divinity I worship and my highest law on earth. — I never mentioned my son to my husband except in a vain attempt to do justice to how full my heart was of him, of his conduct, of his loving manner toward me and my children. — And besides, it was my outpouring of maternal feeling, and of the most heartfelt gratitude — The one occasion that alone could have given rise to this misunderstanding — was on an occasion and prompting — the mention of which here is indeed necessary for my justification, but is painful to my heart — You know, dear Cosmar, how earnestly Sacken wished to draw closer to my son, for no other
