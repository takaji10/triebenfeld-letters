# Seidel (2018), p. 75
# translation by Claude

From Breslau, FL continued his predominantly preventive military-diplomatic duties. He came to Ingelfingen and Öhringen — rarely enough — only to attend to matters of government. The construction of the Mariannenvorstadt (Marianne's Suburb) became a success even without his presence. He had much to do with Öhringen, as the intensive administrative correspondence shows.[^58] The seniority of the Hohenlohe princes, the so-called Lehenhof (feudal court), had passed to the Kirchberg prince, while the office itself resided in Öhringen. The matters at hand were coordination among the various Hohenlohe princely houses, costs for road construction, and similar affairs.

Ingelfingen went through a grievous period between 1799 and 1801, with quite considerable burdens from the contribution demands of the army of the Franconian Circle. This encompassed the whole of northern southern Germany, under the military leadership of the Habsburg Archduke Charles, in the joint struggle against Napoleon. FL was indeed "principally" a general in Prussian service, but within his own territory of rule, that is, in Ingelfingen and its associated communities, he had to help enforce these demands.

FL had, as described earlier, worked hard for the neutrality of the Hohenlohe principalities and had even achieved it, under Prussian protection. This neutrality applied only to armed conflict in the form of combat operations, not to marches through or billeting of troops. When in 1800 levies — this time owed to France — fell due and Hohenlohe was to be drawn into the payments, the Hohenlohe princes invoked their neutrality. Indeed, with success: the French general of the Army of the Rhine, Moreau, had heard of the matter and communicated "that the land of Hohenlohe has been declared neutral by the French government [...] and is under no circumstances obliged to furnish any war contributions for the French army".[^59]
