Massenbach had, with his conduct and his publications concerning the Prussian state, overstepped its limits of tolerance. The consequence was that Prussia instituted proceedings against him, which however dragged on for years. FL too was asked for a statement; he was still in contact with him in 1815. In his response he evaded the matter somewhat, naming von Bülow (not Blücher) and Grawert as eyewitnesses. He had, after all, immediately "after Prenzlau" taken the entire responsibility upon himself, and had had only an indirect connection with the writings.

Massenbach was expelled from Württemberg in 1817 on account of his political activities.[^163] He first stayed in Heidelberg, where one of his sons was to study and where, with the publisher Mohr und Winter, a large part of his most recent speeches appeared (see above). The permission to reside there, granted by the city police, was withdrawn after a few days on higher orders. Since his inquiry as to the reasons for this went unanswered, he turned to Frankfurt, where he lodged his complaint concerning the proceedings with the Federal Assembly. In the summer of 1817 the Prussian authorities set every lever in motion to have him arrested for high treason. When the Frankfurt Senate finally consented, he was arrested under dramatic circumstances during the night of 18 to 19 August 1817 and taken to the fortress of Küstrin.

It may be assumed that FL took note of this development in Massenbach's affairs. He is likely to have reacted with considerable incomprehension. It is regrettable that it remains unknown whether he spoke of it at least with his son August — who must have witnessed Massenbach in the Estates Assembly — during one of the latter's visits to Slaventzitz.
