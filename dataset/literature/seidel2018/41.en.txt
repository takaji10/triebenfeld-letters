# Seidel (2018), p. 41
# translation by Claude

provided for a short text on the back, no longer legible today; it is composed in Latin and reads, translated:

> *This suburb was founded, with a generous hand, by Friedrich Ludwig, Hereditary Prince of Hohenlohe, pious and fortunate and great in war and peace, for the good of his native town and its citizens; and Marianne, his noble consort, ornament and adornment of our century, of her line and of her sex, gave it her name in 1782.*

On the front side, four-part coats of arms are carved into the two elliptical arches; unfortunately only the left one is clearly recognizable. With its quartering, the two "leopard" lions, the double eagle, and the three lilies at upper right, it corresponds to a Hohenlohe coat of arms. It would fittingly be the case, without doubt, if the coat of arms on the right represented that of Countess Marianne von Hoym zu Droyßig. And indeed it could be identified as such! Three of the four fields — an armored arm with a golden mace, a palm tree, and a crown — are still recognizable; at upper left (presumably) a bishop's mitre or a bishop's headband. A Hoym coat of arms with nine fields contains all these elements. That only four appear here is possibly an adaptation to match the Hohenlohe coat of arms.

Concerning the individual houses of Mariannenstraße, the architect Eduard Krüger wrote the following in 1923:

> *The rear of the houses stands open and adjoins meadows and orchard grounds, so that modest farming can be carried on. [...] The houses are arranged so that, besides the living rooms on the ground floor, there remains room for a workshop. [...] The attic spaces are fitted out for residential use.*
>
