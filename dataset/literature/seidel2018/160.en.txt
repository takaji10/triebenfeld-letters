## 16 The Correspondence with the Former Private Tutor Kahlert (1807–1818)

### 16.1 Introduction to the Person

The former private tutor Johann Gottlieb Kahlert (1756-1831) had rendered great service in the education of the children of Marianne and FL and had taught them in Breslau, presumably in Ingelfingen, Wiesbaden, and Berlin, that is, he had accompanied them on their travels and shared in their household moves. In all, for fourteen years, from 1789 to 1803, he stood in the Prince's service as private tutor and educator. In numerous letters to his children, FL had him expressly greeted. After the conclusion of this task, Kahlert kept his own household in Breslau, where he was a private scholar or perhaps also a professor — it is unclear which. From the children's grandmother, the Princess Sacken, he received a pension of 500 thalers (Prussian Courant); she had furthermore stipulated that FL must add a further 300 thalers to it.

He became a correspondent of FL.[^131] This appears to be of particular significance for 1807 and 1808, when the latter was living under house arrest in Öhringen as a French prisoner, and in occupied Prussia — and naturally chiefly in Berlin — the preoccupation with the defeat at Jena and Auerstedt and the capitulation of Prenzlau was the topic of the day. Kahlert reported on this (providing, as it were, a press digest) and communicated much about the quite varied "literature"; by this he meant political books and articles in periodicals. The most frequently named author was Massenbach; some works had appeared anonymously. The scene appears quite lively. Kahlert acts like a "capital correspondent," the cities being Berlin and Breslau (the capital of Silesia and his place of residence). Öhringen and then Slaventzitz are the provinces.
