Already by 1783, according to Himmelheber in his "Description of the Künzelsau District Office"

> *there live in the suburb, alongside the customary local craftsmen, a ropemaker, a wigmaker, and a potter. In 1784 the sculptor Philipp Christoph Sommer is granted credit so that he can build in the suburb. In the same year the houses of the tapestry-weaver and the hatmaker are completed. In 1786 a house each is erected for a stocking-weaver, a goldsmith, and a nail-smith. The goldsmith Nicolaus Berger of Mannheim acquires a house in 1787.[^23]*

Farther out from the old town core stand, or once stood, simpler single-story cottages. Mariannenstr. 42 (demolished long ago) was, for example, the family home of the Court Carpenter J.G. Schillinger, whose descendants will still prove important. The trades of the craftsmen living here and further within the town are named above (p. 36).

### 3.2 Further Establishments

Even before the planning of the suburb, the Princely House had brought about the settlement of trades. At the founding of a gold-goods factory, the Prince gave 10,000 florins, and a further 10,000 were added through the issuing of shares (!). A director, Lartigue, was brought in from Strasbourg; his successor was the deputy director Köhler. In Adolf Fischer, the former Öhringen pastor and author of the "History of the House of Hohenlohe" of 1871, this is recorded thus, along with the claim that the enterprise never became profitable.[^24] In Bundschuh's "Lexicon of Franconia" of 1800, by contrast, it states:
