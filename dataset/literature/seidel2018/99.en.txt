From this point on he would forever remain a co-loser of Jena and Auerstädt, and the one who, through the capitulation of Prenzlau, contributed to France's victory over Prussia. His capitulation had had a contagious effect on other units and their commanders.

It is the coming-together of dramatic events—the separation of 1799, the years from 1806 to 1808 with the withdrawal from the princely office in Öhringen and the military defeat, the quasi-banishment to Silesia—that lends this whole the character of a watershed. How, in the face of all this, could the flourishing of the House endure!
