### 10.5 Goethe and Friedrich Ludwig

The two men lived at the same time. Goethe was born in 1749, FL in 1746, and was thus three years older than the Poet Prince. Both had become prominent, though to different degrees and in different spheres of life. Yet this latter statement does not quite hold true: both stood in close connection with the respective state leadership.

Goethe had left his native city of Frankfurt and become, in the small state of Saxe-Weimar-Eisenach, a state official close to the ruling house, even a member of the Privy Council. Duke Charles Augustus's mother had brought him there. He was closely bound to the future territorial lord. FL had already, as Hereditary Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, joined the Prussian military, had risen almost to its very top, and had a lively exchange with the governmental apparatus, including with the kings, from Frederick II through Frederick William II to Frederick William III. In their rank and their access to the politically powerful, Goethe and FL were at least similar. A Hessian and a Hohenloher had each made a career, respectively, in Saxe-Weimar-Eisenach and in Prussia.

And they did in fact meet: in the War of the First Coalition against revolutionary France (1792–1794), planned as a punitive action by the powers of Central Europe, both were present. Goethe had been asked by Duke Charles Augustus to accompany him. The Duke, though not a Prussian, served as a general of cavalry in the Prussian army, and Prussia led the coalition. Goethe thus went along with him, but as a civilian remained largely uninvolved in the fighting. In the Ardennes he was chiefly interested in questions of geology and wished to make progress on the development of his theory of colors. Even on the day of the Cannonade of Valmy (20 September 1792) he was occupied with this. He kept a diary but, out of caution, destroyed it upon returning to the Empire.
