# Seidel (2018), p. 175
# translation by Claude

> however much he otherwise ruled as a sovereign, he will now conduct this business only if [...] you and all your siblings, as testamentary substitutes of Adolph in the Majorat, declare yourselves by deed: that you not merely approve, but indeed desire, the transfer of the 43,000 out of Adolph's estate onto Bellin, and the acquisition in exchange of the Hardenberg claim, all the more so since Adolph has claims on the Bellin estates that mostly rank behind these 43,000 Rth. I believe that your [brothers-in-law] must co-sign the deed as honest curators of their wives. This would relieve me of a great burden, until I can [...] arrive at the fulfilment of my wish concerning the estate [...] — to which I likewise hope, through your kind and loving assistance, in the sense in which I have already asked you before, to arrive in time. Forgive me, good August! for having to burden you so much with my tiresome business. — But so long as they oppress and torment — and if I must now bore you and others with it too, then one feels, in my view, a small [...] of purgatory. I shall reserve for another letter soon the pleasure of entertaining you with better subjects. Angelika and Auguste send you their best regards — and I ask you to commend me to your beloved Louise and to embrace your children in my name. — I also ask you to greet Alexander briefly from me; it has grieved me greatly not to have seen him, I wish it so dearly. — It was one of his comrades in Berlin, a certain von Maltitz — I would gladly have spoken with him, but I could not manage it. Do put in a good word for me with him, dear August, that he might send me his portrait — I know that he has recently had himself painted, so I ask only for a copy of it, in miniature. Farewell, good August/Your tenderly loving mother Marianne*
