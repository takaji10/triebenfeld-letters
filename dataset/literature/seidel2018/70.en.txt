### 6.3 Excursus II: Ingelfingen Castle[^49]

What, then, became of the Ingelfingen residence? It continued to be used until the middle of the century as an outpost of the princely administration at Öhringen. Only a few servants still lived here, such as the housekeeper Louise Breitschwert and the castle gatekeeper, the widow Wirth — the castle was thus rather empty. In 1843 a Cavalry Captain (Rittmeister) von Schlumbach died, "departed from the residency of the castle." It may be that he was the last representative of the administration.

For a few years beginning in 1843, at the wish of his father Prince August II, the latter's firstborn son Friedrich Ludwig Eugen lived in the castle with his wife Mathilde von Brauneck. There the couple's two sons were born, Kraft August (1844) and Alexander (1846). They were the last two "genuine" Ingelfingers among the members of the various Hohenlohe lines. The family soon moved back to Stuttgart. (The King had long since established a duty of residence there for the mediatized nobility.) The castle (and the castle garden, which was used as a nursery) were let out from Öhringen until the middle of the twentieth century. It was then taken over by the town of Ingelfingen, renovated, and converted into the town hall of Ingelfingen.

### 6.4 Breslau as Center of Life until 1806

In Breslau the first son, August, was born in 1784. This makes the place the center of his life, for five further children, including the two youngest, Adolph (1797) and Alexander (1798), were born there as well (Wolfgang Ludwig, the second son, died at not quite two months of age).
