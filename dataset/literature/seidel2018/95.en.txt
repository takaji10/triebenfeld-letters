It is altogether remarkable that he turns first to the concerns of the grooms, then reports on the aides-de-camp, and only last speaks of himself.

FL had at first received the order to betake himself with his aides-de-camp to Silesia, his destination being Slaventzitz. On the way, however, he was ordered to go to Spandau. The aides-de-camp, except for the one, traveled on. Major Röder and Captain Blumenstein then found lodging in the Breslau residence, the former seat of the Governor. – Incidentally: Prince Jerome, Napoleon's brother, took up quarters in the summer house at Alt-Scheitnig, which had suffered only slight damage from the fighting.

### 9.2 The Situation after the Defeat at Jena and Auerstädt. – In Magdeburg and Afterward, as Described by Massenbach

The remaining army continued to march under the Prince's command toward the Oder. Its first objective was Magdeburg, where the intention was to regroup, to properly provision men and animals at last, and to establish a new order. The Duke of Württemberg laid down his command there and departed, because he could not serve under the orders of Prince Hohenlohe. This is reported by Massenbach in his "Historical Memorabilia"[^71], who adds: "Duke Eugene is said to have made this remark. I cite it as a tradition." This already illustrates something of the confusion, which is described in detail. The Prussian General Rüchel likewise simply departed in the direction of Stettin.

In the days that followed, too, there were, as before, false reports and misunderstandings of orders. The reporting system between the units had broken down. "Complete disorganization had already set in." This included the fact that Blücher had orders to hasten forward with his soldiers. He did not do so, since he could no longer subject them to further night marches.
