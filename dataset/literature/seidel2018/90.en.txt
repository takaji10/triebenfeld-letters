August had no reluctance to report about Mama. In his reply, FL expressed sympathy for the rheumatic complaints reported to him. But he did not express any regret about the overall situation (the separation). When FL himself later mentioned Marianne, he spoke of "Madame Hoym" (or "Comtesse Hoym," since French was primarily the language used). In 1804 he also mentioned the fact that she had had a child — he meant the first of her two little daughters, who were born in 1802 and 1804.

FL's sister Sophie Christiane, who had come to Breslau immediately after 1799, looked after the two small boys, Adolph and Alexander, there. A "nursemaid" of Alexander's named Eleonore Born is documented for Breslau. FL regularly referred to Sophie as "votre tante" (your aunt).

In May 1803, as emerges from a letter of Privy Councillor Hermann from Öhringen to FL,[^66] she was now in Ingelfingen with the children. In 1804 FL traveled to Öhringen to see his uncle, whose health was already failing (he would die in 1805). All together ("notre famille": FL, his sister, his uncle, and the two children — the staff being additional) they made a carriage excursion to the hunting lodge of Friedrichsruhe, which in summer was also used, from Öhringen, as a residence.

FL was delighted by the children's behavior; they were six and five years old, respectively. It is unlikely, however, that this whole party also accompanied him on his visits to relatives in Langenburg and Kirchberg. His route also took him to Kupferzell and Ingelfingen, where he was, after all, still "the Prince."
