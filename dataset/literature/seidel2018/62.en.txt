Indeed, a few days before the date of the letter, on Maundy Thursday, 21 March 1799, the vastly superior Austrians under the command of the Archduke of Austria had defeated the French army under Jourdan at Ostrach near Sigmaringen, and then again four days later at Stockach. – In the long run this could do nothing to harm France's, or rather Napoleon's, ambitions.

Inserted here now is a letter in FL's own hand, written only a few years later, in September of the year 1806. It is a mixture of private matters and high politics (which will not become a topic here until after several further chapters). The son is already his successor and resides in Öhringen:

> *Dresden, 18 Sept. 1806/Dearest Son!/I thank you for your letter [written] soon after this arrival home from Bayreuth. This is the only one I have received from [her] up to now. Adele finds here my thanks for her dear letter from Kirchberg of the 8th, which I received the day before yesterday. From my sister I have likewise received a letter, for which I am most grateful. So too the first letters from Count v. Stetten of the 8th and 10th of this [month], which were agreeable and instructive to me. Do not leave me without frequent news in my love and cares for you all, make yourselves worthy of this effort. You will by now have been in Stuttgart, and I hope to be informed about what has unfortunately now come to pass regarding the Württemberg annexation. From the King of Württemberg I received the enclosure; I sent it to the King in Berlin and asked him for orders and advice as to how I should reply. I sent this by courier, this very night. I am sending you the letter itself in the original; both are to be kept among the files. My reply to the King of Württemberg you will receive in copy; seal it and see that it is sent off safely.
