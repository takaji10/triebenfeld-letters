# Seidel (2018), p. 227
# translation by Claude

FL was on hand. But the King's letters in reply also touched on personal matters:

In what was probably the first letter (of 24 February 1798, from Potsdam), the King addressed FL as "uncle" — a mark of respect given the difference in age. There followed an offer from the King to send the Surgeon-General Goerke from Berlin to Breslau, to assist there with a medical case.

The King told FL several times when he had had dealings with members of his family or relations. Thus he wrote of the — dutiful — request of FL's brother, Prince George (a Prussian officer), for permission to marry a young noblewoman named Klüchzner. The King had at first had reservations on account of the financial situation of both parties. But when he received a second letter from the Prince, in which the latter emphasized his earnestness, he communicated his favorable decision to FL as well. The child of this marriage was the aforementioned nephew Eduard.

In 1801 began the regular exchange of New Year's greetings with the King, now using the address "cousin." From January 1806 came the pause already mentioned, lasting until 1810 (probably on account of the capitulation of Prenzlau). Thereafter, however, he would "at all times take great interest in your petitions" — and such petitions there were! He congratulated him on the marriage of FL's daughter Emilie, and in 1816 thanked him for the announcement of Auguste's engagement. In 1813 he mentions a "favorable change in the political circumstances in Hohenlohe." Did he mean by this a loosening of the considerable restrictions the King had imposed on the nobility in the territories that had been newly added? FL had complained to him, for instance, about impending restrictions on the right of residence and inheritance of his two younger sons outside Württemberg.[^166]

In summary, one may certainly conclude that a personal relationship existed between FL and the Prussian kings of the time. It appears not to have been one-sided — although the King sometimes merely reacted to a piece of news, and will have done the same with many other figures
