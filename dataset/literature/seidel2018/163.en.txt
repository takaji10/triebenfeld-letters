# Seidel (2018), p. 163
# translation by Claude

> *to fight yet another battle and let the half-starved little band under his command perish. The consequence for the state would have been none other than that Prenzlau would have been burned and some further thousands would have bled in battle. The Prince did not do this, but instead sacrificed himself along with them; he deserves therefore no censure, but rather posterity will erect a column of honor to him for it.*

Letter of 19 April 1808 from Breslau to Öhringen. From it an extensive review of the press is reproduced:

> *[...] I continue, in accordance with your wishes, to give you some news of the latest publications in the matter of literature. [...] Of all other writings, two books at present attract the thinking reading public on account of their interesting content, both recently published in Berlin: The first is called: Materials Toward the History of the Years 1805/1806/1807, Dedicated to His Countrymen by a Prussian. It is composed in the French and German languages and is said to be by the Cabinet Councillor Lombard. The author's unmistakable tendency is, without dispute, to show Minister Haugwitz, and above all the King, in an advantageous light. Of the Duke of Brunswick it is said that, instead of all his former qualities, only two now revealed themselves: indecisiveness and weakness. Age had worn down a man who had counted so many fine moments in his life. He could resolve on nothing before the last man had arrived. We had, nonetheless, a vanguard [...] under the command of General von Tauentzien, who lacked neither talent, enterprise, nor competence, and behind him the corps of the Prince of Hohenlohe, whose reputation as a warrior is well established, p. 208, on him our fate depended, he led the left wing. He would have had to withstand the first blow and the crossings*
