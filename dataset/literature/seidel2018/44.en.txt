In the field of education, plans could be realized.[^26] A Latin school had existed since as early as 1710, evidence of the aspiration attaching to the residence, which FL carried forward. It was attended by the children of clergymen, including from the surrounding area, as well as of administrative officials and employees of the court administration. The principal aims were linguistic knowledge, with an emphasis on Latin, ultimately in preparation for the so-called Land Examination. With interruptions, the school continued to exist into the twentieth century.

FL himself brought into being, in 1792, an "Industrial School." It was intended to familiarize the children with agricultural and craft knowledge and to prepare them for practical life. For girls, this concerned learning sewing, embroidery, and lace-making, as well as cooking and washing; for boys, viticulture, gardening and field cultivation, tree cultivation, wood carving, and basket-weaving. Instruction took place as a supplement to the main school on two weekdays.

FL also founded a "Drawing School" (presumably in the sense of technical drawing), with the Court Painter Wagner as master. Instruction and materials were free of charge for the pupils. This institution served predominantly, it seems, adult education. Instruction accordingly took place on weekends. Its purpose was to promote "the industriousness, skill, and taste of the artists and tradesmen" in the Mariannenvorstadt.[^27]
