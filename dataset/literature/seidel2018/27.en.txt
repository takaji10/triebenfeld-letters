### 2.4 Excursus I: Further Descendants

The following records,[^15] which extend to the year 1818, came to light only recently in the Neuenstein Hohenlohe Archive. The author Fischer either did not know of them in 1871 or deliberately did not include them in his account. They are presented here as thoroughly characteristic of the period:

*First*: A letter concerning the existence of an illegitimate son of FL, born in December 1782 in Berlin, following a tryst in Leipzig at the beginning of the year (Marianne and FL married in April 1782!). The child's mother had married a Herr Heinrichs, and he, a painter by profession, wrote the Prince a lengthy letter in 1798 from his residence in Potsdam. Whether FL or his wife had known of the son beforehand is unknown. The Heinrichs couple had in any case received no support (maintenance payments); the mother's husband now requested it, since expenses (for example for clothing) were now increasing. Unfortunately it is not documented whether or how the Prince responded. Nor is it known whether Marianne had any knowledge of the letter. In the first half of 1799 she was in Breslau with the children, and then, indeed, quickly gone.

*Second*: FL already had one illegitimate child; the girl Caroline was presumably born in 1775, that is, seven years before the marriage to Marianne. The mother was a "schoolmistress" in Hohebach. The girl grew up with her grandfather, the Ingelfingen "Tafeldecker" Siller (a "Tafeldecker" is a court servant responsible for the table silver, or for everything connected with the dining table apart from the food itself). He had apparently received assurance that the Prince would provide for this child. The pastor in Kupferzell knew of the child's existence and origins.
