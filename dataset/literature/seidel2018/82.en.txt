# Seidel (2018), p. 82
# translation by Claude

> unless a settlement (Transact) has already been made concerning this, which I hereby not only repeatedly declare to be legally valid and binding, but shall also acknowledge once more before the court when answering the suit. In particular I bind myself once more hereby that, after the judicial dissolution of the marriage has taken effect, I shall leave to my husband, without any interference on my part, the right of upbringing of the princes and princesses, solely and exclusively. I likewise promise, in accordance with my husband's wish, in general not to reside in the same place as he, especially not in Berlin when he is present there, nor in Breslau and the Hohenlohe lands, save with his express permission.*

In another hand, presumably Marianne's own, but on the same document: "Done at Rheinsberg, 6 August 1804 / I approve the contents / Amalie Marianne Louise Charlotte Christiane / of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, born Countess Hoym." Here, then, is mention of guilt. This, however, refers exclusively to "the willful departure," not to its grounds. There must, after all, have been some cause for the departure.
