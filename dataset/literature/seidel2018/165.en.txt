Before a description of the effects of the occupation on the population, particularly the soldiers' families, Kahlert inserted a pleasant story:

> In Glatz a mint has been established which strikes [Bohemian] thalers. Unfortunately, however, a striking error had to occur right at the first die, in that through an oversight [...] the words *Moneta argentea* [...] had been abbreviated thus: Mon. argent., and by chance the one stroke in "Mon." was not stamped on, so that it now read Non. argent., which gave rise to much mockery [...]

Kahlert obtained these publications, which had just recently appeared, from the Frölich bookshop.

In the subsequent letters from FL's time in Öhringen, politics continued to occupy first place: remarks on the occupation and the burden of billeting, the hope for a withdrawal of French troops (expressed as "the departure of the guests"). — An edict on censorship forbade booksellers to stock any writings that contained offensive attacks on the government and the military.

> The booksellers thereupon declared that it was quite simply impossible for them to comply with this order, since they would then have to give up their entire trade, as at present all literary products contain such attacks, and moreover [...] would surely also be permitted to read, since Berlin might well supply [publico] very much, and the whole edict thereupon remained without effect. — With regard to the inequality of billeting, one now hears daily the ugliest complaints.
