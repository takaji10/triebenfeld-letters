# Seidel (2018), p. 85
# translation by Claude

> *no power on earth could have compelled me to sign all these documents, which were meant to be the legal detriment of my temporal well-being; I know that it would have been an easy matter for me to obtain far more favorable terms — but I signed all these papers in silent obedience. Good, transfigured mother! They did not flow from your heart, these cold demands of those documents — and so they shall not rest upon your coffin; I alone will bear all their consequences for myself! I signed all those papers, partly to reconcile my kind mother through my unconditional submission, partly because I recognized, with gratitude, that the welfare of my beloved children was thereby secured. Against this great pledge, my own welfare was cheap to me./Whatever ought to have been sacrificed **to me** — to the Princess Hohenlohe, to the heiress of the Hoym line — my mother gave away with a loving heart, for she gave it, after all, to what was dearest to her in the world, her children. The feast of reconciliation that I celebrated with my kind mother in a quiet corner soon led me back again to her warm mother's heart, and I then went, reconciled with myself, into a foreign land, in order there — as convenience demanded — separated from my loved ones, to live out my life, if possible, in peace./My mother departed into that realm which opens not to etiquette and calculation but to love and esteem — heaven gave me two children — the care of their upbringing, the untiring diligence devoted to their education, was the sole compensation — the greatest joy to a mother's heart, scorched by separation from her children — and I doubled this devotion to their education, since it is the only treasure I could gather for these poor creatures. Yet my duty as a mother commands me to uphold the peace of these my younger children with the very same warmth with which I sacrificed myself for the welfare of the children of my first marriage.
