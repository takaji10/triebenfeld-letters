# Seidel (2018), p. 144
# translation by Claude

Worthy of particular mention are his acquisitions in the Warthe valley. He went so far as to introduce German place names there as well! Some can be made out on the map (Emilienheim, Friedrichsfeld, Adelshof, Sophiental, Ludwigslust), others exist only under their Polish place names.

He corresponded successfully with Triebenfeld from 1796 onward. In that year he received

> *the ecclesiastical estates of the lordship of Zagorow and, from the crown domain [Starost], the lordship of Trombczyn, which consisted of nine granges and two villages, extensive forest and meadow lands in the Warthe valley. The management of these may have yielded little profit, since as early as 18 May 1805 Triebenfeld concluded a hereditary lease contract for their dismemberment [distribution] with eighteen German colonists — with subsequent authorization from the Royal South Prussian War and Domains Chamber at Kalisch dated 28 January 1806. Thus arose in the Warthebruch […] the following German villages: Sophiental [Olchowo], Ludwigslust [Maly Las], Friedrichsfelde [Tarzewo], Adelshof [Chrosniki], the grange of Adolfsberg, Ingelfingen [Anielewo], Emilienheim [Michalinow], Augustinhof [Augustinow], Schwentz [Swiecie], Kolonweiler [Wrombczyn], Klein-Öring [Grozdzikowo].[^113]*

Anyone to whom the German names sound familiar is right. They are first names of FL's children and Hohenlohe place names! A contemporary and somewhat expanded list by Triebenfeld is to be found in the appendix on p. 238.
