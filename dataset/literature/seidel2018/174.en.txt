The next of Marianne's letters to be presented bears the date 24 October 1816 and was written in Bellin. We find family matters, and once again the treatment of a financial problem:

> *My beloved August!/I have just returned from Leipzig and Halle, where I spent very happy days in the company of my good Auguste, her respectable and amiable husband, and Brother Adolph — on the return journey I spent 2 days in Berlin dans les horrens des Affairs: which have now reached, for each of you, the height of confusion. Cosmar has been very harshly accused by a second sentence, yet no other has been appointed in his place: he has quite lost his head and is more incapable than ever, and has even less good will to conduct our affairs. He cannot now become worse — for this reason, out of all the revolutions, a [...] boundary must arise for me — and since the old state of things was as bad as could possibly be, it can only get better — only the present is unbearable. I too am caught in the most fatal complications, since Count Hardenberg in Vienna holds a capital of 43,000 Rth. in the Bellin estates, and indeed as first mortgage. The good man is a moneylender, he torments and presses me without ceasing; Adolph has therefore expressed to Cosmar the wish that, instead of his moneys being placed and remaining on foreign mortgages, Hardenberg should be directed to other mortgages ahead of his own, and that in exchange the 43,000 should be acquired as first mortgage in Bellin for Adolph; all the more so since this is entirely secure — as these moneys belong to the first mortgage.
