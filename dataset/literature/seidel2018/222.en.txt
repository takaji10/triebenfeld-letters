He died in 1827 on his estate of Bialokosch, which had meanwhile belonged to the newly created Duchy of Warsaw, subject to the King of Saxony, and had come back to Prussia in 1815. The estate remained with his descendants (it is, or was until recently, used — after renovation and extension — as a children's home).

### 22.5 Massenbach in the Judgment of Historians

Massenbach remained a subject for historians and also writers (Fontane, Arno Schmidt) into the 20th century. In his afterword to Massenbach's book "Historical Memorabilia" (*Historische Denkwürdigkeiten*), Hans-Werner Engels gives an overview of some ten publications by the guild of historians (from Treitschke through Häusser to Poten, Knesebeck, Tomuschat, Mehring, and Klessmann) and shows how strongly their attitude or judgment was influenced by the intellectual currents of their time, for example by the by-then long-standing "hatred of the French".[^164] Treitschke: "Prince Hohenlohe, ill-advised by the vain fine talker M." Massenbach belonged to the "swarm of insolent slanderers". Schreckenbach: "a crank of the first order". Mehring: "[...] endowed with a happy talent for bluster". Fiedler: "[...] there was a veritable clique of the half-educated who, like Massenbach [...] dispensed teachings that were highly confused and permeated by a bombastic manner of speaking, writing, and thinking, and could only repel people of plain understanding".
