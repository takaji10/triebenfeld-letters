# seidel2018  61  en
# Seidel (2018), p. 61
# translation by Claude

> *a visit from the French has threatened us more closely than ever before. But now, thank God, the danger seems for the moment to have passed again, since I received yesterday the definite news that the French army under General Jourdan has been totally defeated and will probably be forced to retreat across the Rhine. — Farewell, dear son, and continue by your conduct and diligence to give your parents joy; proud am I then with a faithful heart, your [signature of Friedrich Ludwig] faithful father./P.S. send the enclosure at once to War Councillor v. Triebenfeld*

Again, health stood at the beginning. The list of persons mentioned was long: Mama and Aunt (could this possibly mean the Countess M. as Mama's companion?). Does Fräulein Stetten have anything to do with the aide-de-camp? Kahlert has been clarified, Bally is the equerry, responsible for horses and carriages and "always" (and for many more years to come) with FL; Glenck is new. The spelling with "ck" suggests it must be the son Glenck (see p. 129f.), who is said elsewhere to have still been studying in Freiberg in 1799. He later became FL's personal secretary and could therefore already have been in Breslau. That the letter went there thus seems clear. They are all there together, and Mama is also (still) there at the end of March. – Triebenfeld is to be handed an "enclosure." He is FL's finance and property manager (see p. 143f.).

Since "Mama" Marianne is still there, and the power of attorney for drafting the Transact for Princess Sacken mentioned above bears the date of 19 April, it may be assumed that she made her decision to leave, at least, in April as well. When she actually left Breslau we do not know. It must have been at a time when the Prince was absent.
