the Württemberg Estates Assembly. As representative of his elder brother Richard, he was a member there from 1816 to 1817. He did not hold back on this occasion, delivering speeches against the proposed draft constitution, demanding a unified Germany, a parliamentary form of government, the accountability of ministers to parliament, the abolition of the two-chamber system (Estates Assembly and Diet), and the immunity of deputies (incidentally: Prince August II of Hohenlohe-Öhringen, FL's son, was even President of this body; the two must have encountered one another often). From one of Massenbach's fiery speeches:

> *Nothing great, nothing splendid, nothing enduring through the centuries can be achieved without the realization of German national representation! Without it the whole of European mankind will not rise to lawful freedom. Like children we Germans are regarded, like schoolboys we shall henceforth be treated. Without national representation we Germans will wage wars, as we have always waged them, to enlarge and bring happiness to other nations; we shall remain small and unhappy, ever smaller and more unhappy. – Do we want that? Do we still not feel called upon to give ourselves a great, solid constitution, cast in a single mould? Not called upon to awaken again in ourselves the feelings that glowed in all Germans after the battles at Büren, Liegnitz, Dennewitz, at Leipzig? We must rekindle the glow of that warming substance; or we shall perish in the cold! I wish with my whole soul that the princes be most intimately bound to the peoples, and the peoples to the princes. I am a friend of the people only insofar as I am a faithful admirer of good princes. People and prince are one, one is the state.*[^161]
