# Seidel (2018), p. 78
# translation by Claude

> *full, unconditional, and now expressly judicial adherence, in every formality entirely lawful, and according to the laws of the place of execution, to this entire Transact in the best legal form, and is convinced that the clear judgment, together now with the good dispositions of Her beloved daughter, will fully coincide with this draft prepared through maternal representation. Should, however, contrary to expectation, the Princess of Hohenlohe be unwilling to make the present Transact her own, and should she instead withhold the consent which Her Serene Highness the mother has thus undertaken to obtain, then, for this unforeseen case, Her Serene Highness the mother, Princess of Sacken, hereby establishes the necessary consequences. [...]*

Two paragraphs follow. It is not necessary to reproduce these; the document is possibly a copy, or perhaps a draft, which bears the following closing line: "In witness whereof. Berlin, 9 Nov. 1799." No signature. It is unlikely that there was a new version or revision; this is suggested by the later, frequent references — for example in the divorce documents — to the Transact under the double date of 9/19 November. The 19th of November may be the date on which the copy signed by the three persons concerned, that is, including FL, was executed. It was with him, as mentioned in the preface, that the Princess of Sacken "spoke." On this point, the excerpt reads:

> *Thus, between the Serene Lady, the Lady Christiane Charlotte Sophie, Princess of Sacken, acting for herself and on behalf of Her beloved daughter, on the one part, and the Serene Lord, Lord Friedrich Ludwig, reigning Prince of Hohenlohe, Count of Gleichen etc., on the other part, the following Transact was discussed and concluded.*
