When the French and German army commanders once met in Öhringen in 1805 — at that time the unofficial capital of Hohenlohe — toasts were also proposed at the banquet. FL, in his own toast, emphasized Napoleon's achievements; in substance he said, in the French language, that he hoped to make his personal acquaintance (well and good, but then followed) — in order to defeat him in battle! This was, admittedly, hardly diplomatic — and things did indeed very soon turn out quite differently.

Regarding Breslau, it may further be noted that from 1781 Joseph Christian Franz von Hohenlohe-Bartenstein (1740–1817) was active there at the episcopal cathedral church, at first as Coadjutor, that is, as representative of the bishop. The Bartenstein (also Waldenburg) line of the overall House of Hohenlohe was Catholic. This distant "cousin" of FL became Prince-Bishop of Breslau in 1775. No personal contacts are documented.
