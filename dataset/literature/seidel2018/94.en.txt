# Seidel (2018), p. 94
# translation by Claude

> *grooms [G.] and [K.] wish to be discharged, I do not wish to withhold from you [...] that I must reduce the number of my horses. I have therefore given each of these grooms 5 ducats as a gift and had their monthly pay calculated through November, so that they leave quite content. Their request is that you might use your influence so that these men remain exempt from Württemberg military conscription. This is my great wish, and I hope that citing it will protect them. For the time being I am taking up residence at Liegnitz. N.S. Loncey, Pirch, Röder, Blumenstein, [Kriechenbach] send you and all of you their greetings. They share their fate with mine. Marwitz has gone to Mecklenburg to his mother-in-law. I still have with me the Cavalry Captain von [D.] as aide-de-camp. He is a fine man. Then also the engineer von Möldendorff, son of the Government President at Bayreuth. This is my entire suite. I have lost several riding horses, yet I am still well mounted. From the graze wound I received on the right arm on 14 Oct. I feel no inconvenience, apart from [...] still some swelling remaining from it. – Murat has remembered you favorably. Since I am not permitted to cross the Oder, else I would have betaken myself to Slaventzitz, where I could still have occupied myself usefully. I do not wish to leave the Prussian cavalry now, else I would have gone too soon, or I would have betaken myself to Ohrdruff. That I am a prisoner you already know from Berlin, from where Count Kapper was to inform you. I hope to receive news yet today from my mother-in-law, to whom I had sent [Ströbel]. Farewell all of you, and God keep you from all harm. To my sister, to you, to yours, to your brothers, cousins, all our lords and ladies, let there be here the assurance of my love. Ever with the greatest devotion. Your! Faithful Father [...] Hohenlohe.[^70]*
