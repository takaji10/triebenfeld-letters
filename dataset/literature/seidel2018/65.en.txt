# Seidel (2018), p. 65
# translation by Claude

In his new official function as "head of state," FL also occupied himself — at least a little — with the ideas of the French Revolution:

> *The various new theories are mostly ideals of a constitution as it ought to be in its highest perfection, and as a norm for legislation for man in the social condition, wholly unusable!*[^42]

His sovereign laws were meant to be "fulfilled and affirmed in their lawfulness and their intended benefit." This purpose was served, among other things, by a printed official gazette distributed free of charge. In this programmatic text he also addresses his frequent absence as a Prussian general and calls upon his administration, the "Councillors," to cooperate with the Prince of Hohenlohe-Öhringen, his uncle. It continues:

> *To Our government We commend justice, equity, loving treatment of the subjects, and honest zeal for the common good. We are pleased to be convinced that Our loyal servants, in the exercise of these duties, have regard for the rights of man, while at the same time watching strictly over the laws.*

This declaration of government concludes with three main points:

> *In all Our ordinances We shall always chiefly have regard to this: that 1. agriculture be promoted, 2. Industry be increased, 3. Commerce be enlarged and multiplied, because We recognize these as the most productive sources.*
