Alexander was the object of FL's worries over the restrictions imposed by the King of Württemberg. Two letters written by him in close succession are reproduced below:[^140]

Dated 13 January 1818, he wrote:

> *Dearest Father! I cannot describe how glad I am to learn from Aunt of your recovery, yet now, best of fathers, I hope that you do not doubt my ever-faithful and childlike reverence and childlike love [...]. I learned of your illness here very late, from Louis von Kirchberg, who was here [...], but fortunately I learned through a letter from Adele from Fürstenau that you, best of fathers, were once more on the road to recovery. That God may grant you a truly firm health for a good long time yet is my highest wish and my daily prayer. August [is] here at this moment, in order [I believe] to arrange lodgings in Cannstadt for his wife, who wishes to take the waters there [if this is correct, the letter was presumably written in Stuttgart]. That I have not yet myself hastened into your arms and expressed my childlike feelings to you in person is the fault of the new military organization, which is expected daily. After a brief drill regulation [...] to which I am assigned, the present drilling season is being put to very heavy use. The King is not infrequently present at our exercises and seems to have much patience with us. Once this is settled, my first business will be to request leave to come to you, my father, and I hope that this so agreeable moment is no longer far off. I am consumed with impatience to see you again, best of fathers, after so long a time,
