The next letter bore the date 27 January. It begins with congratulations on FL's forthcoming birthday (on 31 January) and touches once more on nearly all the usual themes: high politics, fears regarding the outbreak of a war, the exhaustion of the lands, family matters concerning the son Alexander, the delivery of a book by Kotzebue, and "an enclosed box [...] of Redlich's confectionery product in the form of a sugar cake, since these spices are ascribed a very strengthening power." He closes with the formula "to the most serene Princess Sophie I tender my respects and remain with the utmost submission/Most Serene Prince/gracious lord/your most humble servant/Kahlert." This was at the same time the last letter; FL was to die on 15 February.

And one further note: books — indeed, an entire library — did exist at Slaventzitz.[^132] It still existed after the First World War. A few folio volumes from it ended up in the Neuenstein archive. They are predominantly textbooks on the art of war. It seems FL and his guests had no interest in having any more of these sent to them by Kahlert.
