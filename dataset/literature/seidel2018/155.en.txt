# Seidel (2018), p. 155
# translation by Claude

> *neglected that it has become [...] to me. I cannot now, for honor's sake, leave here until I have found a remedy regarding the 5,000 thalers [...], for I cannot allow the honest Minister v. Zastrow to incur any unpleasantness. Meanwhile I know of no other remedy than that Your Serene Highness should as soon as possible send back to me the blank draft for 6,000 net which you graciously credited to Eisenhardt, so that I may take back the Con[...] mortgage of 10,000 presented for it from the bank, and thereby be able to help. But may Your Serene Highness be pleased to endorse this draft to me, or note upon it yourself, that you have [received] no money against it, and return it accordingly. I shall then immediately press for the return of my mortgage, and [...] I shall instead receive the money here from a certain Generalin von Bülow, in order to satisfy the banker [...]. The noble Minister v. Zastrow petitions with me before the French authorities that Your Serene Highness's Trombezyn estates be restored, and had this disgraceful incident not befallen me, I should already have been in St. Petersburg since that time, and could there have accomplished much through the Grand Duke. In the confident hope of a very speedy grant of my most urgent request, I remain, Your Serene Highness's*  
> *most humble servant*  
> *v. Triebenfeld*

One cannot fully understand (or decipher) all of this at first glance (for instance: were the French authorities to decide that the Trabezyn estates be returned to FL, had they been taken from him — or had he sold all or half of them? What role would the Grand Duke in St. Petersburg have played?). But it is clear that Triebenfeld, too, despite his detailed knowledge of the "real estate business" and of financing possibilities, and despite his resourcefulness, is running into difficulties here.
