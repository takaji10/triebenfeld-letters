For the following years, 1805 and 1806, no letters to or from Glenck are extant, or none existed; the Prince was, precisely in these two years, very much occupied with Prussian deliberations and preparations for the confrontation with France. At the end of 1806 the Battle of Jena and Auerstedt was lost, followed by the capitulation of Prenzlau and his capture. For two years, as has been described, he had to endure as a French prisoner in the castle at Öhringen. Nevertheless, despite guards, he remained reachable via "disguised messengers" and wrote and received letters, among them also to and from Glenck. Such is this letter from Öhringen dated 27 July 1807 (Öhringen is not stated, but the date indicates it — he was still a prisoner there at this time):[^104]

> *I very much regret, Your Well-Born [he always writes "Wohlgeboren" with the additional "h", and it is moreover the form of address for persons of lower rank of bourgeois status, as Glenck indeed is], having become burdensome to you with my request. You ! [the "!" always follows this additional form of address] would oblige me if you would come here on Thursday and could stay this day and the following one, since I have pressing correspondence to attend to for Berlin and Breslau, or perhaps for Silesia; if possible, the needs of Weißbach should also be seen to. It cannot, however, be arranged as quickly as you would like. I am glad to have found a means whereby the Hofräthin Hahn shall at once receive at least 1,500 thalers. This you may tell her from me. My women's [intercession] contributed nothing to it; it was my own eager good will alone that made it possible. From Hahn I have a report. It will also work out concerning Bitbus, and for that too I need your pen. My stay here will likely not be much longer
