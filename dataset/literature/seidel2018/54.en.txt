# Seidel (2018), p. 54
# translation by Claude

Breslau. Marianne writes from Berlin, where she is staying — presumably with the children and the tutor and private teacher Kahlert — in her parents' palace. The first letter, of 8 February, is reproduced here in translation with its first page:

> *My dear Friend. The letter I received yesterday, which testifies to your remembrance, your friendship, and your well-being, has overwhelmed me with joy, and I offer you my heartfelt thanks. I longed for news of you — my health remains good — and it would hardly be fitting to be ill in this constant whirl in which I must live here — I am treated very well in the city, as at court — so that I have nothing left to wish for. Nevertheless, I assure you that at your first sign I shall leave this region without regret, and shall be glad to follow you wherever it seems fitting to you. As for the rest, it is not on account of the French quintessence [la Quinte essence francoise] that you will have with you that I regret having left your country. And however courteous and honorable your proposal is, to make small purchases here and there in passing [en passant], I confess to you that I hold too low an opinion of my talent for buying to boast of it. And on the other hand, I possess too much patriotism to venture beyond the German border. — So there, my dear Friend. Do with your French colony as seems good to you — but be assured that my heart feels the loss of being far from you, your parents, and your excellent sister, the latter in despair over her renewed affliction of the arm and grieved that the suffering has increased, so that I reproach myself — I fear that the purchases she made for me, in order to accompany me, may have contributed to it. I beg you to convey my thousand greetings [...].*
