# Seidel (2018), p. 120
# translation by Claude

> *to bring myself to mind, were I not thereby exposed to the reproach of having, through my failure to report, failed to make my good will known. My duty therefore requires me to inform Your Royal Majesty that my health and strength will not hinder my activity, and that I have continually neglected nothing in order to keep myself fit for service. My sole and most eager wish is: to sacrifice my last strength in Your Majesty's service, and to prove my loyalty and usefulness with my blood. These are not empty words, but the expression of my true feeling. My intention is pure, I still feel myself capable, and my experience and example will surely be of use. It is neither pride nor greed that induces me to make this offer. — I lay claim to nothing, neither to rank nor to high salary. Employ me, Your Royal Majesty, wherever and however you wish. At any time Your Highness's commands will find me ready to obey, and I set my glory upon being able to increase, through obedience and resignation, the proofs of loyalty which I have always striven most eagerly to demonstrate. Do not, most gracious King, entirely reject my petition, and grant me the happiness of Your gracious attention, and take to heart my urgent situation, which without Your Highness's grace and support will utterly destroy me.*

A few days later he received the King's reply: "[...] the state of affairs does not permit me to give you an appointment in the army."[^90] In total it was a mere 8 lines.

"Berlin," then, again showed no inclination — and perhaps FL's final remark about his "urgent situation" had not been altogether fortunate either. That he had, at the urging of others, involved himself once more with military matters at all, turned out to be a mistake.
