The children were cared for, not only during these Berlin weeks, by Herr Kahlert, who would later work as a private scholar (or professor) with his own family in Breslau. When Marianne once made a journey from Silesia via Prague to Ingelfingen in 1791, the children were already there with him at their grandmother Osten-Sacken's. In this connection Marianne expressed herself on the rigid dress regulations and condemned the wearing of a corset by the girls. When the Coalition Wars against France then broke out in 1792, FL sent Marianne with the children from Breslau to Ingelfingen, to his father Prince Heinrich August, who was still reigning there, and gave him 1200 florins as a contribution toward their board.

It was thought that they would soon be victorious in Paris — "Paris would be splendid," Marianne wrote. Yet in the spring of 1793 FL was still in a field camp near Kaiserslautern. Marianne was able to visit him. In a letter to the tutor Kahlert she wrote:

> *I found the military circle heroically healthy. Nowhere does one hear less talk of war than in the army. The military sojourn here is truly most delightful. The camp on the height makes a splendid effect.*[^37]

Nowadays one is astonished at such remarks. The campaign was still continuing in the year 1794. FL suffered in Wiesbaden, or rather in the camp at Pfeddersheim, from a protracted throat inflammation; Marianne traveled there and stayed with him until his recovery. The children are in Wiesbaden with the tutor Kahlert. Walluf, the birthplace of the daughter Auguste, born on 16 November 1793, is near Wiesbaden. This place otherwise never plays a role again.
