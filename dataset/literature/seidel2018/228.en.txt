### 23.2 A Patron of the Fine Arts, of Music, Painting, and Poetry?

Nowadays, at any rate, there is the Hohenlohe Cultural Summer, which draws on the surviving princely houses and their buildings. In 2015 the Stuttgart State Museum was able to mount a major exhibition, "Art Treasures from Hohenlohe" — but, alas, Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, despite its flowering in the eighteenth century, did not appear in it at all.[^167] The other Hohenlohe princely families had themselves portrayed splendidly and with great symbolic weight; in the case of the Ingelfingen line, one must be content that portraits were made at all.

The practice of music likewise plays no role. The children presumably received some musical instruction; at any rate, in a letter to Berlin, where the children were staying at the time, FL gives his eldest son advice on how and where he should have the damaged pianoforte repaired. This son, his successor August II, had a genuine interest in the arts. FL, by contrast, understood the art of leading an army — in that, probably more than his likewise princely relatives.

Let us nevertheless attempt to assemble the little that has come down to us on musical and artistic matters. When the Ingelfingen palace was rebuilt at the beginning of the eighteenth century, FL had not yet been born; Italian artists executed the stuccowork. He was involved in the new furnishing at the end of the century. Landscape paintings adorn the walls, along with depictions with motifs drawn from antiquity. As artists he engaged the Court Painter Johann Jakob Schillinger of Öhringen and the somewhat younger Christian Wagner, who was married to a woman from Ingelfingen.
