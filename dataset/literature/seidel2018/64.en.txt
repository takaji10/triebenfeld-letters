## 6 From Ingelfingen to Öhringen and Breslau

### 6.1 Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen

When FL had become reigning Prince in 1796, following his father's death, he introduced himself with the following declaration of government:

> *We, Friedrich Ludwig, reigning Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, Count of Gleichen, Lord of Langenburg and Kranichfeld etc., General of Cavalry of His Imperial Majesty and of the Holy Roman Empire; His Royal Majesty of Prussia's most graciously appointed Lieutenant-General of His Armies; Inspector-General of the Lower Silesian Infantry, likewise Inspector-General of the royal troops in the Franconian Principalities, Governor of the royal capital and residence city and fortress of Breslau, Colonel-Proprietor of an infantry regiment and Knight of the Great Royal Prussian Order of the Black and Red Eagle and of other Orders, do hereby extend to Our dear and loyal subjects Our greeting.*[^41]

He had no greater or more heartfelt wish than "to further the welfare of Our dear and loyal subjects and to bring it to the highest degree of perfection possible given their circumstances and Our powers." By "their circumstances" he presumably means the rather poor winegrowers, so dependent on the annual course of the weather, and by "Our powers" his own finances, which — beyond his salary as a general — likewise depended in large part on the winegrowers' dues, that is, on the grape harvest.
