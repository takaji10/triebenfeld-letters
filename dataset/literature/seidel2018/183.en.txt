## 18 Sophie Christiane Louise, Princess of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen

She, FL's sister sixteen years his junior, has already been mentioned several times, above all because, living unmarried in Ingelfingen, she had taken charge of the children after 1799. Marianne, their mother, knew the children were in good hands with her. She had come to value Sophie Christiane during visits to Ingelfingen, and perhaps had even grown fond of her sister-in-law, who was nearly the same age as herself.

In 1814 Sophie Christiane had visited FL in Slaventzitz; at any rate, he reported in a letter on her return journey. Toward the end of his life she stood intensively by his side and must have been especially close to him. She came to her brother in Upper Silesia in October 1816, despite the long journey from Ingelfingen, where she still lived in the Princesses' Wing. She took on incidental costs — for example, replacing horses — and gave his daughter Auguste a new carriage, among other things. After his death, Sophie Christiane remained on the spot for several more weeks. To August she wrote after FL's death:

> *You have done infinitely much for the departed, but could not possibly fulfil all his wishes, since your situation did not allow it. Even in the last period you granted what lay within your power, by paying the outstanding salaries of his servants, by approving the certain 4,000 thalers for the acquisition of Bytkow, all of which greatly calmed and pleased him. He often said: August has after all come to love me dearly; how good that he came here and saw everything for himself. If only it had happened sooner! Louise is a clever woman; she contributed much to it. Well, my blessing remains with you both; I wish for nothing but your well-being.*[^136]
