# Seidel (2018), p. 151
# translation by Claude

> *be discharged within a few years. I most respectfully request that Your Well-born undertake this business and, given your extensive acquaintance, negotiate a loan of this kind. The power of attorney necessary for this I shall send by the first post, in whatever form you please. I have likewise instructed my General Plenipotentiary to hand over to the same immediately all parameters necessary for conducting this business, and to pay out, without delay, a [gratuity] of 5 % to the men of business assisting in this matter, as soon as the affair is concluded.*  
> *Freiberg, 25 September 1806[^122]*

According to present-day accounts from the locality of Zagorów,[^123] FL received the estates as a gift from the King. Unfortunately there is no record of this in the archive at Neuenstein. The five estates — Trombczyn, Nowa-Wieś, Oświery, Łazy, and Szlewek — are said to have had a value of about 80,000 thalers. After a few years he is said to have sold half to another German "investor," the large landowner Karl Gottlieb Weigel of Hennersdorf in Silesia and lord of the manor of Kopoyno (in South Prussia, hence a neighbor). The remainder of the property was intended for settlement purposes. The name Weigel reappears in the financial and simultaneously debt statement drawn up at the end of FL's life.

It is furthermore of importance that, after the military defeat, the whole of Prussia was in great distress. The near-contemporary author Klöden (1840) wrote the following on this subject:

> *The provinces [still] occupied by the French up to the Vistula were bled most dreadfully by Napoleon's accomplices [...]. Billetings, direct taxes, deliveries, war contributions, etc., knew no end, and were extracted with merciless severity.
