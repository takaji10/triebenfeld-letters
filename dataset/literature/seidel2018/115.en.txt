## 11 The Resigned Prince in Upper Silesia

### 11.1 Conditions in Saxony and Silesia

Property (manor estates) and money were abundantly present in Saxony as also later in Upper Silesia (so-called seats of lordship; Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen too had property there, even if no money). Marianne's mother, Princess Christiane zu Osten-Sacken, née von Dieskau, widow of Hoym zu Droyßig, administered a large fortune there, and was, in the assessment of the historian Gerlinde Kraus, an "early-capitalist entrepreneur of the 18th/19th century".[^86] The property and the income derived from it were precisely fixed between mother and daughter as her only child; after the latter's marriage, the share of the princely husband was likewise fixed. The extensive "Transact" ultimately secured further possessions in Silesia for the House of Hohenlohe. We do not know to what extent the wealthy ladies' funds had previously also played a role in the building activities at Ingelfingen – probably not.

The mother was the dominant personality, and had indeed taken note that her daughter was married to a Hereditary Prince who was enterprising, or also reckless, in economic matters – and how he handled money. When she heard of the separation in 1799 and the subsequent intention to divorce, she was "not amused". Nevertheless, Marianne went first to her, to the parental estate or to Berlin. Mother and grandmother kept in contact with the children and grandchildren respectively. Marianne then lived on the manor estate of Bellin, and after her second marriage bore the two daughters Angelica (1802) and Auguste (1804). From Bellin or also from Berlin, both women were in lively correspondence with the children and grandchildren, above all with the grandson August in Öhringen. Marianne died in 1840 in Mecklenburg, at the neighboring Marienhof near Güstrow.
