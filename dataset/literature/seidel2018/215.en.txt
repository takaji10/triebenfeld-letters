The fourth volume of the "Memoiren zur Geschichte des Preußischen Staates," which dealt critically with the events of 1806 and was directed against the conduct of the Prussian army, was confiscated and destroyed in 1810. — The publications listed here are only the most important books. The catalogue reproduced in Engels's afterword lists 35 titles, an extract of which is presented here and further below.[^156]

In several of these publications Massenbach himself gave his own account of the events concerning which the King had requested reports from him (the Battle of Jena and Auerstädt and the capitulation of Prenzlau). He even sent manuscripts in several installments from Bialokosch to FL in Öhringen and asked him by letter for his permission to have them printed; he had a need to justify himself.

At least for today's reader of Massenbach's many letters to FL, it is astonishing with what respect they were written. The salutation always indicates the difference in rank ("Most Serene Prince, most gracious Prince and Lord"), and the letter almost always closes "in deep devotion to Your Most Serene Highness," "most submissive servant" — after so much shared experience! Is all this merely the period's customary formulary language of a still-persisting society of estates? There must have been a linguistic and address code within the Prussian state that was maintained even in retirement.
