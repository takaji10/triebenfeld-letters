This record was submitted to FL, who responded, under the date of December 1791, with his own handwritten instruction, which his administrative officer then put into proper bureaucratic form: "We Friedrich Ludwig, by the Grace of God Hereditary Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, do hereby certify and acknowledge [...]." Under "A" there follows a "deed of gift" for a manorial dwelling in the (Ingelfingen) suburb worth 3,000 florins, which is given to the grandparents as "property" with a lifelong "rent-free right of residence." As "B" an "obligation" is listed: 1,000 florins are earmarked by the Prince for the Weißbach saline, which are to yield 4% interest. This sum is to flow to the girl. FL thus appears to have acted. The matter of the housing presumably also worked out. But the financial support through an annuity that FL had devised failed, as did so much else connected with the Weißbach saline.

The story continues some years later, in 1806: Caroline is now 31 years old. In that year, the year of the takeover of Hohenlohe, she turns to the King of Württemberg with a petition to the King, evidently formulated in official German and certainly composed by a professional scribe. It then lands in Öhringen. In a kind of letterhead, the sender names "Siller, née 'Hohenlohe'" as her surname.

The text contains a new fact: the model of the annuity as a cash payment from a deposit in Weißbach has since 1795 been changed to an annual 200 florins from the treasury of the Chamber in Ingelfingen. Responsible for this arrangement, acting in the name of his brother-in-law Prince Friedrich Carl, is Princess Marianne. He himself actually has nothing to do with the matter, but was brought by Marianne to the point of ceding this sum to the princely Chamber and forgoing 200 florins of his apanage.
