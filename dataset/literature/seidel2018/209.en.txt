The youngest brother, Prince Alexander, the only one who never married, died on 23 March 1829 at the age of 30 in the "princely palace" in Stuttgart, as a Knight of the Order of St. John. He had completed a military training in Stuttgart but was able to leave the royal Württemberg service and had come to Berlin, where, as he informed FL, Frederick William III "had arranged an appointment" for him. Alexander would now, from there, also come to know his mother.

A Hohenlohe legend has grown up around him.[^151] It is said that, having returned from Prussian service to Öhringen, he shot at subjects without cause (they would, strictly speaking, have been "subjects" of his brother August, or of the King of Württemberg). Alexander died in Stuttgart. After his elaborate burial in the woods by Friedrichsruhe Castle, he is said to have wandered about as a ghost. His grave there was desecrated in 1830 by two grave robbers searching for gold jewelry. The robbers were caught. On 24 August 1830 the solemn transfer of the body to Ingelfingen took place; there the coffin was carried into the well-attended church at the midnight hour; the final interment then took place there in the princely crypt.
