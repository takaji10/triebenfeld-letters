Prussia hoped to be able to reduce its troops in Frankfurt (for reasons of cost) and replaced FL with another general. When the situation, however, again threatened to become critical, FL was immediately ordered back there by the King.

FL's task was to monitor the demarcation line. In addition, he was to reduce, as far as possible, the suspicion of the Austrians, who had been prepared to agree only to a supplementary accord, and not to a peace treaty, with France. He also had to maintain contact with the French under their commander-in-chief Jourdan.[^34] In retrospect FL described this task as a Herculean task. A separate peace such as that between Prussia and France did not come about between the Empire, represented by the imperial Austrian troops together with the allied imperial estates, and post-revolutionary France. The French side, moreover, called the neutralization line fundamentally into question. It even threatened to occupy Frankfurt and there levy "requisitions" (as it had once already done years before). FL was called upon as a diplomat. In confidential talks he was able to assure Clairfait that he would never permit the French to march through Frankfurt. And they then indeed held still. For this the city of Frankfurt rewarded him with the first-ever conferral of the dignity of honorary citizen (the corresponding list is headed by his name!). He had declined a gift of money.

Jourdan was prepared, in the event of a renegotiation of the demarcation line between Prussia and France, to accommodate the Hohenlohe principalities in the preservation of their neutrality. For this, however, he had to await the order of the Revolutionary Directory in Paris. FL immediately sent a courier there as well.
