# seidel2018  47  en
# Seidel (2018), p. 47
# translation by Claude

> *his appointment presented no difficulty. [...] his income amounted [not counting a major's salary] to no more and no less than 600 imperial florins. To satisfy his needs with this [...] presented all the more difficulty since the prince's needs were very costly. Above all things he wished to be regarded as an excellent rider; and since such ambition can only be satisfied through excellent horses, it was chiefly this item that plunged the prince into debt and ruined all his affairs in advance.*[^28]

The quotation comes from the often somewhat tendentious author Friedrich Buchholz (see p. 102f.). Frederick II also expressed himself about the young officer: "Croyez moi, le prince vaut par les qualites militaires tout le destin de l'empire, et je l'aime veritablement par ses excellentes qualites de l'esprit et du coer. Il fera une carriere brillante" — "Believe me, the prince is worth, in his military qualities, the whole destiny of the empire, and I truly love him for his excellent qualities of mind and heart. He will have a brilliant career."[^29]

The sequence of his military ranks is impressive; see the chronological table in the appendix, p. 235f. Frederick William II, King of Prussia after the death of Frederick II in 1786, retained him. He took him along as his military expert in August 1791 to the Convention of Pillnitz, where the foundations for the War of the First Coalition against France were laid. After the King's death in 1797, FL wrote the following:

> *It is a misfortune, especially for me, that this good lord [Frederick II] died too soon for me. But I cannot complain of the young lord [Frederick William III]. He treats me with great respect, passes over me in nothing that falls within my sphere of activity, but given our great distance, much occurs in other matters where I either fail to grasp the necessary connection [sic — clause defective in the original] or learn of it too late.*[^30]
