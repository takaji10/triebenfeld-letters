In all three cases – are they perhaps merely examples? – it long remained open how the respective solution actually turned out in concrete terms. This indeterminacy is surely, in the main, also a matter of method, and to that extent these are also relevant contemporary testimonies (and it is only for this reason that they are presented here immediately one after another). The problem is smallest when no inheritance claims result, when boys, for instance, can be "handed over" to a cadet school.

FL's own conduct, and that of the Court Chamber, by no means corresponds to our own understanding, but it was in the end tolerated in that era. Punishments for unwilling fathers and considerable disadvantages for the "bastards" did, however, exist. Precisely in the *Ingelfingen Heimatbuch*[^16] an example from the court is included, from the year 1783 (thus before FL's reign): a high and noble overnight guest, His Serene Ducal Highness of Saxe-Meiningen, while himself still lying in bed in the morning, according to the testimony of the daughter of the court cook Gerster, had "pulled her with both hands and [...] fully accomplished the carnal commingling." She bore a child and – was punished by the Church, by the Ingelfingen parson! Although the place, time, and perpetrator were known, no – documented – judicial prosecution or punishment of the perpetrator followed.

For Marianne, all this was presumably of no real relevance. What perhaps did affect her was the case of Heinrich, since she, then just 18 years old, was at that time celebrating her wedding with FL, when he… – But let us leave that aside! The reasons for her decision to separate presumably remain her own secret. Or did she after all leave something behind concerning it?
