### 20.1 The End of the Life of the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen

Friedrich Ludwig, Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, died on 15 February 1818 in Slaventzitz and was buried there as well. General von Röder made his death known to the public. All the officers of the garrison of Kosel, situated 12 km to the southwest, paid him their last respects.

There was much to be settled, and the creditors came forward. Thus also came Goldstecker, mentioned by Friedrich von Cölln (see p. 101), with whom Hahn in Breslau had also had dealings, and he immediately affixed his seals to the estate. What von Cölln had meant by the "dumping of South Prussian estates" has already been described in detail. It was known, up to the King himself, that FL's mountain of debt was so dramatically high that intervention had become necessary. The inventory and debt lists repeatedly compiled by Triebenfeld (see appendix, pp. 239/240) still played a role as late as the Congress of Vienna in 1815!
