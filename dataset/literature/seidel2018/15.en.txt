Prince Heinrich August was already being assisted in the business of government some time before his death by his son, Hereditary Prince Friedrich Ludwig. An example of this may be found in a letter from the year 1788, which the old Prince addressed to his son. It concerned the sale of a mill: he placed the decision in his son's hands, "since in such matters, especially economic ones, which will in due course fall to you, I would not wish to act against your will, seeing that at my already advanced age no reckoning can be made on my life."[^5] The trust placed in the son, and the monument erected on the occasion of the golden wedding anniversary, point to a good relationship between father and son. In 1796 FL succeeded his father.

### 1.3 The Last Ingelfingen Princely Couple

Prince Friedrich Ludwig (1746–1818), through the building activity he had already promoted as Hereditary Prince, became the renewer and expander of the town. Since 1782 the Prince's wife had been Marianne, née Countess of Hoym zu Droyßig, daughter of Princess Christiane von Osten-Sacken. The suburb was named Mariannenvorstadt (Marianne's Suburb) after her. The Countess was eighteen years old at her marriage, bore eight children over the seventeen years of the marriage, separated from her family in 1799, and obtained a divorce (in order to marry once more, in Mecklenburg). A complete overview of the family may be found on p. 21.
