With him, then, Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen did after all still have a son of the Muses — even if as Hohenlohe-Öhringen!

Upon his father's death, August quickly contributed 4,000 thalers to settle a remaining debt on the estate of Bytkow. Even in old age he remained interested in Ingelfingen and ordered one son to reside there in the castle. That son and his wife did so, and had two children there, though only for a few years, after which they returned to live in Stuttgart, as the King of Württemberg expected of "his" nobility for at least three months per year.

After his resignation and definitive move to Slaventzitz in Upper Silesia, August lived there until his death in 1853 and was also buried there. His successor in Öhringen was his son Friedrich Wilhelm Eugen (Hugo), who was also appointed Prince of Ujest (Upper Silesia). It was only August's grandson, Prince Christian Kraft of Hohenlohe-Öhringen, who consolidated the estate of Bytkow together with the neighboring localities and there operated coal and ore mining (particularly for zinc) with the very greatest success, under the place and company name "Hohenlohehütte" (see p. 127).

### 21.2 The Sons Adolph and Alexander

Prince Adolph, August's brother thirteen years his junior, retained his name Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, made a career in the Prussian military, and was briefly Prussian Minister-President in 1862 (his removal came at the hands of Bismarck). He had helped his mother when she once found herself in difficulties with a mortgage (see p. 157). Prince Adolph resided at the Koschentin estate acquired by FL in 1804, which remained in the possession of his descendants for several more generations (the expropriation took place after the Second World War).
