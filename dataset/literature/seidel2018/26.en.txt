# translation by Claude

— scarcely conceivable. — It is surely advisable to return to serious historical sources. I again cite Adolf Fischer:

> *Amalie Louise Marianne Charlotte, Countess von Hoym, was regarded as one of the most spirited and charming young ladies of her time. She knew how to move through society with an elegant yet natural ease, and not only that — as the future and sole heiress of the extensive estates in Saxony and Silesia, she was one of the wealthiest women in the Duchy of Saxe-Weimar-Eisenach and in Prussian Silesia. Her financial circumstances did not, however, allow her free choice of her future husband. [...] Unlike her future husband, to whom the young woman had even been recommended by Frederick the Great, she, as a well-bred daughter of good family, at nineteen years of age could hardly have had the opportunity to make inquiries about the Hereditary Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, who was eighteen years her senior.[^14]*

The first children of this marriage were born in Breslau; only one son, who died at the age of one month, was born in Ingelfingen in 1794. The Prince once grandiloquently called Ingelfingen the "principal seat of his family"; but this can hardly be said to have been true. Neither he nor Marianne with the children were regularly or for any length of time there. Whenever she was there, she indulged her "free-thinking" (her own expression), enjoyed painting miniatures, arranged to have to attend church only once on Sundays, went hunting with enthusiasm, meeting for it with the new relatives in Schrozberg, and paid visits to Öhringen, Kirchberg, Langenburg, and Bartenstein.
