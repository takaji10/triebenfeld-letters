The two former spouses had no further contact with one another; there must have been an agreement to that effect. When, in letters, mother or father happened to speak of the other, they used, cautiously, neutral terms — I have pointed this out. There was no war of the roses.

FL had, to the great fortune of the children and also of himself, in the first period of the separation, the support of his sister Sophie Christiane. He was able to go on attending to his professional duties without worry. She was a complete substitute mother for the little ones; Adolph and Alexander would have no memory of Marianne from their childhood.

The other person of the utmost importance in this situation was the tutor Kahlert. FL could accept without difficulty that the fourteen-year-old August, and very probably also the three girls who followed him, were initially with their mother and grandmother in Berlin together with Kahlert. Kahlert had his full trust. FL maintained a correspondence with August that continued even after August went to study in Erlangen. The three girls must, however, then have come to Öhringen fairly soon, since from 1806 FL was a prisoner there, and August had by then advanced to Prince there. What could FL do there for the girls, what were the interests of the nineteen-year-old Adelheid (Adele), the eighteen-year-old Emilie, the thirteen-year-old Auguste? There is no information on this. In two letters from FL it emerges that he intended to hold a ball for Adele. They did have a certain social circle (chiefly the Hohenlohe relations) and, certainly very important at the time, their elder brother. FL himself could do little for them. When in 1808/09 he had to go to Upper Silesia, they remained behind under their brother's "protection." And they were to marry.
