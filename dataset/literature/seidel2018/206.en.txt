# Seidel (2018), p. 206
# translation by Claude

In 1811 August married Louise of Württemberg, the younger sister of his Erlangen fellow student Eugen and niece of Duke Frederick, who had by now become King of Württemberg. The couple had four children: Friedrich (born 1812), Friederike Mathilde (1814), Friedrich Wilhelm Eugen Hugo (1816), and Felix (1818). Three of the children were born in Öhringen, one in Stuttgart.

FL kept close contact with him as the eldest. And later August, now the Prince, often visited his father, who had grown old and was probably very lonely, in far-distant Slaventzitz. It was probably on one such visit that the idea arose of erecting a monument in the court garden at Öhringen, for whose creation the citizens had given up their gardens — possibly in remembrance of the (far more elaborate) monument that FL had had erected in Ingelfingen for his parents' golden wedding anniversary (see p. 15). It became a classicist obelisk. Amid laurel wreaths, the years 1807 (FL's captivity in the castle) and 1815 are to be recognized. A plaque reads:

> *Obelisk 1815. The princely House of Hohenlohe acquired the garden of the Brückenmühle on 19 July 1807. Citizens of Öhringen redesigned it as a part of the park, "Citizens' Joy," for Prince Friedrich Ludwig zu Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, then a prisoner of war in his own castle at Öhringen. In gratitude, in 1815 the Prince's son, Prince August II zu Hohenlohe-Öhringen, had the obelisk erected "To the good citizens of the town of Öhringen." — On the occasion of the "750 Years of the Town of Öhringen" jubilee, the town restored the monument in 2003 with the kind support of the firm Bürkert, based in Ingelfingen and Öhringen.*
