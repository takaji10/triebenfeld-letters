One may assume that both of them heard music at the house of Margravine Caroline, who resided in Erlangen — Erlangen belonged to Ansbach-Bayreuth. August reported this himself; he valued music his whole life long, having learned to play the piano (and later maintaining court orchestras of his own).

After the conclusion of his studies, an educational tour through European capitals was planned, as was customary for the nobility; once again his grandmother financed it, this time with the father contributing as well, together 20,000 thalers. The journey was to go to France, Belgium, and Holland, then on to Vienna. After visiting Munich, Stuttgart, Karlsruhe, Strasbourg, Paris, Mainz, Frankfurt, and Kassel, however, he had to break it off for reasons of state policy.

He met his father in Erfurt, who had his headquarters there, in order to be present, in agreement with him, in Öhringen. By 1805 almost the entire southwest of the Empire was occupied by the French, and upheavals were to be feared. The father had a great deal to do with the political-military unrest in Prussia and Saxony. As the situation grew ever more difficult and opaque in 1805/06, and the future of Hohenlohe was also uncertain, with mediatization impending, FL had quickly decided to have his son declared of age and to make him his successor as Prince of Hohenlohe-Öhringen. August himself had also seen that he would have a more important task in Württemberg than in Prussia. He had himself released from the Prussian military in Berlin by Frederick William III and entered the service of the King of Württemberg. This took place still in the summer of 1806. Frederick William III had, when August returned from "his journey" (as the King put it in a letter to FL — and he certainly meant the abbreviated grand tour), issued him a "patent" as an officer dated 1 October 1802.[^150] August had (or would have) accordingly completed his entire course of study as a member of the Prussian army.
