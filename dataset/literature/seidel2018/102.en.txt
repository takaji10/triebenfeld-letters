# Seidel (2018), p. 102
# translation by Claude

Regarding Triebenfeld, Forestry Councillor Friedrich August von Triebenfeld, we learn still more from von Cölln; he is introduced as a Major and "War Councillor" and is himself also entangled in all manner of "dealings." This relates to his activity as a supervisory official in the granting of estates in the part of Poland that had already come to Prussia after the second Polish partition. In this capacity, he is said to have received, between 1795 and 1798, eight estates with an assessed value of 51,000 thalers as a gift, which he then sold in 1802 for 750,000 thalers — he had thus provided most successfully for himself. Triebenfeld was FL's General Plenipotentiary in his difficult financial affairs — more precisely, the burden of FL's debts, which had arisen to a scarcely conceivable extent, in part because of the estates in former Poland (South Prussia).

For FL had also concerned himself with the purchase and leasing, and perhaps also speculative sale, of such estates — though without the success enjoyed by Triebenfeld. Near Zagorow in the Warta valley, he acquired an extensive manor as early as 1796 and renamed the surrounding hamlets, among others as Ingelfingen (Anielwo), and also after the first names of his children (see p. 144).

Triebenfeld was closely connected with FL for more than 20 years and concluded hereditary lease contracts there with German colonists on his behalf. He was above all FL's manager of debts. It would be a wonder if he or FL had not also had contacts with banks and bankers in these dealings; the banking house of Oppenheimer is mentioned. With the aforementioned Jew Goldstecker, apparently an art dealer or jeweler, one of FL's administrative officials had contact in Breslau around 1810. He will resurface right after FL's death in Slaventzitz. — The entire complex of the "squandering of estates" is devoted its own section (chap. 13).
