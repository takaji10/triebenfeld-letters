## 20 The Final Phase of Friedrich Ludwig's Life

FL did not fare well in his old age; besides his severe short-sightedness, he had been plagued for years by attacks of gout. The sequence of courses at table had long remained that of a princely court. Toward petitioners he behaved generously. Polis, his master of the horse and successor to Bally, died before FL did. His son August occasionally made arrangements directly with the "Master of the Household" Lombardini.

Blumenstein, his former aide-de-camp, had risen in the military and visited him on the occasion of a troop inspection. Pirch presumably came still later. From him comes a horrified report on FL's outward appearance and the meager hospitality he offered. FL was soon unable to pay his servants; the wages still owed at his death were assumed by his son August (see p. 203f.). The Governor's Palace in Dresden, which had suffered only minor damage in the fighting (and had been repaired), and the summer house in Alt-Scheitnig were rented out. Blücher lodged there on several occasions. Works of art acquired before 1806 had to be sold off, as did carriages and sledges. This too was presumably the task of Court Councillor Hahn. The entire estate at Alt-Scheitnig, together with the park FL had laid out there, was sold privately and taken over by the city of Breslau in 1815.

With Hohenlohe, with Ingelfingen or Öhringen as his former residences, he had had very little to do since his "banishment" by the King of Württemberg. Nevertheless, Öhringen administrative officials occasionally turned to him. He maintained regular correspondence with the Director of Salines Glenck and with Herr Kahlert, as well as with his son August.
