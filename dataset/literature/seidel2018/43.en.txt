# Seidel (2018), p. 43
# translation by Claude

> *here, besides the Lantiquian and Köhlerian jewelry factories, which on account of their tasteful work always have enough to do, good custom too in Frankfurt, where they attend the fairs.[^25]*

Which is correct? (Whether the gold and silver goods factory, established as a branch from Schwäbisch Gmünd after the takeover of the Adler inn in 1872 in its building, has anything to do with this seems unclear.)

In 1788, at the suggestion of Wilhelmine Leonore, Princess of Hohenlohe-Neuenstein-Öhringen and wife of Prince Heinrich August, a bathing establishment was planned on or in the Kocher river. The undertaking, a pontoon construction by the Court Carpenter Schillinger, proved, however, too difficult and was not pursued further.

Nor were there any lack of further plans: a grain market was to be established, a supplementary grain storehouse (probably not the one in the attic of the Inner Press House), a leather manufactory, a wax-candle factory, a wax bleachery, a large oil mill on the stream above the town. For the workers in the factory and the trade establishments, a consumers' cooperative was to be brought into being on the model of the Moravian Brethren. Which of these succeeded and lasted for any length of time remains an open question.

Equally unsuccessful was the effort to improve the roads and the postal and communications system. Thurn und Taxis was unwilling to establish a newly accessible post and travel station, so that FL, even as a senior Prussian officer, continued to have to make do with couriers. How he and his family managed the great distances between Ingelfingen and Breslau, and also Berlin, one may well wonder. By coach, of course.
