# Chodyła (2015), p. 126
# translation by Claude

in the Przespolewskie Ol. they were set at 12 *reichsstalers*, and in the Zbierskie Ol. at *10 thalers per huba*[^294]. The inhabitants of the Orlińskie D. and M. Ol., the Mycielińskie – Kazala St. and Nw. Ol., and the Stropieszyńskie – Gostynie and Radzany Ol. paid them in an unknown amount[^295]. In the Przespolewskie Ol. in 1779 the first three Olęders settled on Poroże paid *2 red złoty* *(ducats) of rent each and worked a day and a half a year*. Of others, settled later, it is said that they give no rent *since they still enjoy the freedom under their contracts for a time, except the innkeeper (...)*[^296]. The inventory of (a part of) the Przespolewskie Ol. of 1785, which names 22 Olęders, states that seven of them (one of them for part of a holding) had 3-6 free years left in favour of the manor, and so bore no burdens yet; and the inventory of the same of 1786 states that for the *tłoka* (harvest labour) *a barrel of beer must be given* to the Olęders. The table of hearths of this settlement of 1789, in turn, names 25 hearths in it, with the additional explanation: "*...the Hollendry, the New Colony by the name of Poroże Wielkie"; among them "cottages having their ground cleared and paying rent from it" 13, "Huts not yet out of their free years and paying no rent, besides one still without buildings 12 (of these, 7 huts settled in 1789, which began clearing in 1789 and are to pay rent of 36 złp. each in 1796, 2 huts settled, which began clearing in 1787, and rent of 36 złp. each in 1793) and in 1 hearth*"[^297].

Dues in kind were rendered in many, but not all, of the settlements, in oats, poultry and eggs, with the possibility of commuting them into cash at a set rate. Their amounts varied. Thus *2 capons* (fattened, castrated cockerels) were given only in Wrąbczynek and the Wrąbczynkowskie Ol., and *4 wiertele of oats of the Warsaw measure, 4 capons or 2 geese instead, 2 mendle (fifteens) of eggs in the Gadowskie St. and Nw. Ol.*[^298]. On the other hand, *4 wiertele of oats or 8 zł, 2 geese or 2 zł, 2 capons or 1 zł 6 gr, a kopa (sixty) of eggs or 24 gr, in all 12 złp.* (1 zł = 30 gr at the time) were given in the Białobłockie Ol., the Borowieckie St. and Nw. Ol., the Grądy St. and Nw. Ol. and the Konarskie Ol.[^299]. In Wielołęka, in turn: first 2 wiertele of oats of the Rychwał measure, 2 capons or 1 goose, and then 4 wiertele of oats or 12 zł, 2 geese or 3 zł, 2 capons or 2 zł, a kopa of eggs or 2 zł, in all 18 złp.[^300]. Further: the farmers were bound to give 4 wiertele of oats, of the new royal measure, and 4 capons or 2 geese once a year in the Lubieńskie Ol., 4 capons or 2 ganders in the Dębniałki and Piskory Ol., and 2 wiertele of oats, 2 hens and 1 goose, and 2 mendle of eggs in the Dzierzbińskie-Zamęty and Zdanowiec Ol.[^301]

The charter for the Stropieszyńskie Ol. of 1791 stressed the punctual payment of the rents and "*other dues*"[^302]. In Wierzchy, or the Nowe Tomickie Ol., the rents and other burdens to the manor were borne in 1789 by the entry-fine Olęders
