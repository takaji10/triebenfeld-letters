# 4. The legal status and the administrative and judicial organisation of the Olęder settlements in the Pyzdry Forest

In the Olęder settlements founded in the Pyzdry Forest, the legal relation of the first settlers and of the later inhabitants of the Olęder settlements to the feudal lord (the founder of the settlement and his successors), the rules of the Olęders' life together in the settlements, the organisation and functions of their self-government, and finally their obligations to institutions and third persons (among them the state and the Church) were set by the foundation charters of the settlements, also called laws (*prawa*), consents (*konsensy*) and letters (*listy*). Formally they were unilateral acts, issued and signed by the lords, the founders of the settlements; in fact they were agreements made between them and the locators acting on behalf of whole communes as their representatives, or, mostly, with the settlers as a body. Sometimes they could also be oral, or written but preliminary, or individual, and only in time were they replaced by final written documents common to the whole commune[^245]. So it was in Wielołęka, Konary, the Lubieńskie Ol., the Przespolewskie Ol., Wrąbczynek and the Wrąbczynkowskie Ol., the Gadowskie St. and Nw., and the Dzierzbińskie-Zdanowiec and Zamęty Ol., where the settlers, settling gradually, first obtained individual contracts fixing their free years, or, as in the Mycielińskie St. and Nw. Ol., preliminary charters[^246], which were replaced only later by collective charters (agreements) of a final character. The Olęder commune had to pay for the issue of a collective charter; the foundation charter for the Cekowskie St. Ol. of 1768 states that the settlers "*declare they will give for the contract a minted thaler each, and have already given one red złoty towards it*"[^247].

The Olęders, unlike the peasants owing labour service, were always personally free. This was expressly confirmed in the collective foundation charter for the Gadowskie St. and Nw. Ol. (*I have resolved... to give emphyteutic law to the Gadowskie Holendry, to free people subject to no subjection, brought in while my husband was still alive... and coming in during my possession... according to its contracts*)[^248].
