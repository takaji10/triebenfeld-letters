# translation by Claude

starost of Niszczewice, owner and possessor of the estates of Trąbczyn and others, there were 12 Olęders with a shepherd (10 with German and 2 with Polish names) in the settlement founded *not very long ago* in the forest or wood called Luśnie[^36].

The Trąbczyńskie Nowe Ol. probably arose after 1754 and before 1767. This seems to follow, indirectly, from two inspections, of the fields and of the boundaries of the Trąbczyn estate, both of 1767. According to the inspection of the fields, water flowing from the Stawisko had flooded the farms *of the Olęders settled in the Trąbczyn woods with ploughland measured out for tillage, and meadows*..., through the fault of people from the village of Łukom, acting on their owner's orders; and according to the inspection of the boundaries, the meadows of 4 Trąbczyn Olęders had been flooded, so far that they could not be mown, because the flowing water had been dammed in the pond by the throwing up of a dike[^37]. In 1791 Catholics and Lutherans lived in the Trąbczyńskie St. and Nw. Ol., and they then had 31 "self-tilling" (*samorolne*) hearths (farms)[^38], that is, farming ones.

Before 1750 and in 1772 the Przespolewskie Ol. arose in the district of Kalisz, in the parish of Przespolew Kościelny, on the noble estate of Przespolew Pański; they took in a part of Przespolew Pański and Poroże Wielkie. The approximate date of the founding of their oldest part can be fixed from the mention of the rents of the "olendrzy" in the act of tradition of 1750 to Leon Bielecki, *posesor*, that is the holder, of a part of the Przespolew estate[^39]. The founder, or rather the co-founder, of the second part of the Przespolewskie Ol., the settlement of Poroże Wielkie Ol., founded in 1772, was Marcin Stanisław Zaremba, Adjutant General of His Royal Majesty, owner of the property of Przespolew. On the strength of the foundation charter he issued that year to the "industrious Olęders" Marcin Schmarschel and Fryderyk Jakobcek, Marcin Lehmann and others, at least several German settlers, Lutherans, settled in the place. They were settled on wooded land, that is most probably in the forest, since they received 7 free years for setting up their farms and buildings[^40]. In the decree of the commission court of 1779 on the tradition of a part of the Przespolew estate in payment of a debt of its owner, Tadeusz Zaremba, to the creditor Tomasz Moraczewski, 3 Olęders living at Poroże are named (two of them probably the same as those named above in the charter of 1772), who paid, among other things, rent, and of the others it is said that they give no rent *since they still enjoy the freedom under their contracts for a time, except the innkeeper, handed over* [officially transferred] *to His Honour Pan Wolski* [probably yet another creditor]. *All these Olęders, in Przespolew as on the said Poroże, are handed over with their cottages, buildings and ploughland, rents and hubas*[^41].
