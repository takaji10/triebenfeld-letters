According to this charter the settlement, because the place was so small, had a little school and a cemetery in common with the neighbouring Kosmowskie Ol.[^77].

In 1776 the Lubieńskie (Lubień) or Biała Olędry were set up in the district of Konin, in the parish of Dzierzbin, on the estate of Biała (Panieńska) belonging to the convent of the Order of St Clare (the Franciscan nuns) of Kalisz. Their foundress was Anna Chlebowska, prioress of that convent, who together with other members of its governing body issued two emphyteutic-perpetual foundation charters on 10 November 1776. According to them the village was founded at the urgent *requests of the Holędrzy*. The settlers, personally free, Catholics and Poles, settled *by the right of free purchase* on the *deserta* [empty land] Lubień, and received 7 free years (counted from 10 XI 1776), but their years of freedom were to run out according to their separate contracts, that is agreements[^78] (Ill. 13). This last piece of information seems to show that they settled in woodland that needed *rudunek* (clearing), at first under individual agreements.

Probably as early as about 1770, and in 1781, two settlements were founded in the district of Pyzdry, in the parish of Zagórów, in the starosty of Pietrzyków: Wrąbczynek and the Wrąbczynkowskie Ol. Their earlier origin is confirmed by two *konotacje* (memoranda) issued to the Olęders by I. Gałęski, the plenipotentiary of Szczepan Turno, starost of Pietrzyków. In the first, of 23 July 1776, the manorial dues from 12 hubas (włóki) of farms were set *for the industrious people of Rąmpczyn*, that is for the inhabitants of Wrąbczynek, who still owed labour service (after the old *konotacja* had been lost). In the second, of 30 September 1777, a confirmation was issued of the measuring of the lands by the sworn surveyor Bogusław, which had found only 11 hubas, and accordingly the inhabitants of Wrąbczynek were let off the rent of 1 huba. It also contained the statements that on that day the community was to choose a sołtys and 2 assessors with the manor's consent, and that a royal law would be issued for them, that is a confirmation of the foundation charter by the monarch. This *konotacja* also contained the information: *Since the Rąbczynek Holendry, their free years for the rumunek being out, have today received the law under which they are to remain in their dues, so that these too* [that is the Olęders in Wrąbczynek], *settled together with them, are not raised to higher dues than the Holędrzy, they are ascribed an equalisation with those, so that they* [belong] *to the dues set out here*[^79]. It follows that the Wrąbczynkowskie Ol. were founded on overgrown, probably wooded, land, and that the two settlements were founded at the same time.
