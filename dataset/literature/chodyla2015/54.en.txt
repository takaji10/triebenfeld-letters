In 1778 the Pyzdrskie Olędry were founded. They were founded by the council of the town of Pyzdry (the president Karol Ogrodowicz and 5 councillors) in order to protect the forests belonging to the town. The settlers were Poles and Catholics, partly burghers of Pyzdry and partly peasants of the surrounding area. They settled in the Pyzdry forests and received 7 free years for clearing their holdings[^91] (Ill. 14). The issuing of their foundation charter on ordinary paper and not on stamped paper, as the law required, became the cause of long lawsuits between the Olęders and the town authorities[^92]. The Pyzdrskie Olędry were the only Olęder settlement in Greater Poland that arose on the estates of a royal town. Today, as was said above, 9 settlements or parts of them correspond to it[^93] (cf. Tab. 1).

In the same year 1778 the Jarantowskie (Jaruntowskie) or Jarantów Olędry were founded in the district of Kalisz, in the parish of Lipe, on a noble estate[^94]. From the remains of a Protestant cemetery it can be said that only, or also, Protestants lived there.

In 1781 Wierzchy, or the Tomickie Nowe Olędry, were created in the district of Kalisz, in the parish of Szymanowice, on the noble estate of Tomice. Their founders, by an emphyteutic-perpetual foundation charter issued at Tomice on 23 September of that year, were Jan Samek Glis[z]czyński, chamberlain (*podkomorzy*) of His Royal Majesty, owner of the estates of Tomice and Wierzchy, and his wife K.
