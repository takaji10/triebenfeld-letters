# translation by Claude

This document, which was in fact an agreement between the parties, also states that it concerns the Olęders settled on the Bilawy by the late Wawrzyniec Czarnecki[^103]. This suggests that at least one of these two settlements was founded before 1783, or else that further settlers were brought into this settlement by the same co-founder after 1783. According to the Table of the tax of the offering of 10 and 20 gr: *The Olędry called Piskory, from separate Olęders, whose years of freedom end from 1791 and who will begin to pay in that year;* (...) *in the year 1792 again 11 szczegulni* [individual, separate] *Olęders will end their freedom and begin to pay ...; one Olęder lived in Lipe*[^104].

In 1783 and before 1788 the Oborskie Olędry arose in the district of Kalisz, in the parish of Żegocin, on the noble estate of Obory, in the Obory wilds: the Stare Oborskie Ol., that is Wronów, and the Nowe Oborskie Olędry. Their founders were the owners of the village and demesne farm of Obory, the married couple Jan and Marianna née Rożnowska Szarzyński, treasurer of Latyczów and his wife[^105]. The settlers, whose number and confession the charter does not give, were, as the remains of a separate Protestant cemetery show, only or also Lutherans.

Before and in 1784 the settlements of the Orlińskie (Orlina) Duże and Małe Olędry were created in the district of Konin, on the estate and in the parish of Królików(o). Their founder, and the issuer of the preliminary charter, issued at Królików on 5 May 1784, was the owner of this estate, Count Franciszek Stadnicki, starost of Ostrzeszów, and directly his plenipotentiary Józef Koźmiński, and the locators were Bogusław Krol, sołtys, and Michał Arent. This charter contains the phrase ... *in future all the present and the earlier ones* [settlers] *will have one law*, which justifies the supposition that one of the two settlements, that is the Orlińskie Duże Ol., arose earlier, and the second, the Orlińskie Małe Ol., was founded later, in 1784. The founder also stated in it: *I allow this sołtys free clearing and rudowanie with his co-samsiads* [!] [neighbours], *whose first names and surnames will be written on this contract*. In 1784, 20 settlers settled (most of them with Polish names and some with German), Catholics and Lutherans[^106].

After 1783-1784 and before 1789 the Lisieckie [Ol.], or Lisiec, were founded on the river Powa, in the district of Konin, in the parish of Lisiec Wielki, in the Lisiec key (group of estates) of the starosty of Konin. In 1791 they had only 2 self-tilling hearths, inhabited by dissenters (perhaps Reformed Protestants, attached to the church at Żychlin, as another name of this settlement, the Żychlińskie Ol., would seem to show), and in 1796, 4 houses in all[^107]. Their founder was probably Józef Mycielski, starost of Konin, lieutenant general of the Lithuanian army, voivode of Inowrocław, owner of the estate of Szamotuły.
