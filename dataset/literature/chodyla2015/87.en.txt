# Chodyła (2015), p. 87
# translation by Claude

In the Stropieszyńskie, or Radzany and Gostynie, Olędry, the foundation charter of 6 XII 1787 set the entry money per morgen (equal to 300 square rods of 7½ ells) of ready land or meadow at 18 złp., and of overgrown land at 3 złp., payable in three instalments, by 1789; it assigned 4 morgen to the *szkolny* free of land payment and rent[^177]. According to the foundation charter of 1791, which at the same time confirmed and supplemented the first charter, the Olęders received 3 morgen for a school and a cemetery, and timber from the manor[^178]. In 1791 they had 6 *self-tilling* hearths[^179], that is farms.

In Wierzchy, or the Tomickie Nowe Olędry, founded in 1781, of the 12 settlers all but the first were in principle to hold ½ huba each, for which in 1786 they paid the founder together 24 ducats of grungeld, that is entry money[^180]. In time the settlement was extended and its area enlarged, since in 1789 it had 18 hearths[^181].

In the Zbierskie Olędry 14 settlers obtained, in 1761 and later, holdings of one huba in perpetuity; the huba equalled 30 morgen "*of the rydlandska* [Rhenish] *measure*", and the morgen had 30 rods in length and 10 in breadth, measured with the rod of 8½ ells; the whole huba had 150 x 60 rods. Besides, ½ huba was assigned for the teacher and ½ huba for the inn, free of rent, and for the "kirchow" (burial ground) a plot of 15x15 rods[^182]. In 1791 the Zbierskie Ol. had 17 *self-tilling* hearths[^183], that is farms.

So of the total of 58 Olęder settlements founded in the Pyzdry Forest, the area can be established exactly or approximately for 37. For the remaining 21 Olęder settlements the area of their land is unknown. If, however, one assumed that the individual holdings there too were of 1 and of ½ włóka (Chełmno-Crown?), then, with the known numbers of *self-tilling*, that is farming, hearths in 1791, their areas could be estimated approximately within two ranges, a minimum and a maximum. The matter is complicated, however, by the variety of the measures of land then in use, and by our not knowing them in the case of these settlements.

The data quoted above for the greater part of the Olęder settlements that arose in the Pyzdry Forest show that their land was given in włóki laid out mostly in the Crown measure, that is measured with the rod of 8½ ells, and in the *rydlandska* (Rhenish) measure, in which the morgen were laid out with the rod of 7½ ells. In some villages the number of farms changed considerably over the several, dozen or several dozen years after the founding, compared with the initial state, mainly because of further settling, but probably also because of the division of farms by inheritance. This is confirmed by the information given above on the number and size of the holdings from the foundation charters, inspections and inventories, together with the information of the tables of hearths of 1789 and of *self-tilling* hearths of 1791.
