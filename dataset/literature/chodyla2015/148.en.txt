At that time too, in carrying out those provisions that concerned the abolition of servitudes, the town of Pyzdry lost 785 ha of woodland to the owners of plots that had been burdened with the duty of protecting the town forests. The newly created plots were laid out as enclaves within forest areas with valuable stands of trees, mostly oak. This operation, carried out chaotically, contributed to the disorganisation of what had once been a rational forest economy, and to the appearance of a considerable number of new farms, also within older villages, without any planning principles. At the same time the process of the fragmentation of farms, begun already in the nineteenth century, went on, lasting here, with some breaks, until the law on the protection of farm and forest land was passed in 1971.

In the years 1945-2015, and also later, as a result of this process up to that time, and also of the creation of recreational plots, 10 new small settlements appeared. Today in the area of the Pyzdry Forest, which lies within the one voivodeship of Greater Poland and the 6 districts and 15 communes mentioned above, there are 328 settlement names, among them 6 little towns, 323 villages and other settlements.

Today the Old Polish, and also the later, Olęder settlements are inhabited only in a smaller part by the descendants of the first settlers – Catholics – and in a minimal part also by the descendants of the Protestants – Germans. This is because almost all the German Protestant inhabitants of these settlements were expelled from the Pyzdry Forest and forced to leave for Germany after 1945. The Olęder settlements they had inhabited until 1945 were settled to a greater or lesser degree by Polish newcomers, and some were afforested.

There have survived to this day in the Olęder settlements of the Pyzdry Forest – from the enfranchisement of their inhabitants on, really post-Olęder ones – such elements of their cultural landscape as the layouts of the villages and their fields, ditches and dikes, the course of some roads, plantings of trees, the former Lutheran churches, traditional farmsteads, schools and cemeteries. Unfortunately, the original names of these villages have survived only to a very small degree, since most of them were changed in the inter-war and post-war periods, among other things with the intention of Polonising them and shortening their linguistic form. Only six settlements contain the characteristic distinguishing element *(H)olędry* or *(H)olendry*: Ciążeńskie Holendry, Wrąbczynkowskie Holendry, Złotkowskie Holendry, Gadowskie Holendry, Holendry Jaroszewickie and Olendry Smaszewskie.

These monuments and this toponymy, being a common historical – human, European – cultural value, raise the tourist attraction of the micro-region of the Pyzdry Forest. They should be protected and cared for permanently by the local communities, local government institutions, religious bodies, schools, and social, cultural, sightseeing and tourist organisations. The Cultural Society "Echo Pyzdr" has done much to protect these monuments, document them and spread knowledge of them in society. Its work helps to deepen the historical awareness of the inhabitants of the individual places and of the whole region, and to spread knowledge of the important elements of the historical space and the cultural heritage not only at the local but also at the wider regional and national level.
