# Chodyła (2015), p. 52
# translation by Claude

of 19 June 1781, issued by Krystyna née Szembek Turnowa, wife of the *stolnik* of Poznań, the mother, and Stefan/Szczepan Turno, starost of Pietrzyków, the son, to Ignacy Dobrski, the description of the village of Wrąbczynek contains the information that there are handed over to him *the Olędry both on the Budy and the Nowe Olędry, whose freedom runs out this very year, with all their dues and rents...*[^80]. The inspection of 1776 of the place (land) *Benefickie* beyond the river Warta, belonging to the estates of the town of Pyzdry, tells of its being taken over, together with a part of the old road from Pyzdry to Konin, by an Olęder recently settled by Stefan (Szczepan) Turno, and that *in the wood Spławie the Olęders of the said Pan Turno are clearing, cutting the wood down to the stump and destroying the building timber*[^81]. According to the joint, collective and final emphyteutic-perpetual foundation charter for both settlements, issued at Pietrzyków on 20 October 1781 by the said Szczepan Turno, they were founded to bring the ruined royal estates of the starosty of Pietrzyków, and precisely *the villages of Rąpczyno*, into good condition. The founder therefore granted to Jakub Krysa [Kriese], sołtys, and to Wojciech Kurtz and Krzysztof Szultz, assessors, *...the demesne farm with all its lands and the village thickets* in the existing village of Wrąbczynek, with a total area of 11 hubas measured by the surveyor, *besides the gardens and village plots, and the pastures; and to the Rąpczyn Hollendry* [that is the inhabitants of the Wrąbczynkowskie Olędry], 9 ½ hubas cleared by them from the *deserta* [empty land] in the forest, having earlier set up in them a sołtys and 2 assessors, named with their first names and surnames. They received their free years, that is the time for building and setting up their farms, according to the individual contracts they had obtained earlier. They received, besides, 16 morgen of meadows they had cleared. The provisions of the charter applied to both settlements, as the phrase in it confirmed: *By this present law* [the collective one], *long promised them, I secure both the village commune and the Dutch commune...*". Settlers with German and Polish names settled in both settlements. Most of them were probably Lutherans, as is shown by the grant to both settlements of land for a teacher and for a burial ground[^82], although the visitation of the deanery of Konin of 1792 shows only Catholics in Wrąbczynek and only Protestants in the Wrąbczynkowskie Ol.[^83]. In an *asekuracja* (written assurance) of 20 XI 1781, issued by S. Dobrski, plenipotentiary of Turno, starost of Pietrzyków, besides confirming that the labour services had been done, the Olęders were promised that they would obtain the royal law (that is, the king's confirmation of the foundation charter issued by the starost-tenant) once they had paid a contribution for the purpose, and it is stated *that the Germans, who have been measured out on the lord's demesne farm, will be bound to pay the remaining half of the grungielt*"[^84]. The confirmation of this charter by King Stanisław August Poniatowski, however, came only on 2 February 1787[^85].
