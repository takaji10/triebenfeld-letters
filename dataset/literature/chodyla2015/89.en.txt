Only those hearths were to be counted as freed from the grain levy, and so as non-farming, whose holders had no land at all or had "*vegetable... gardens*", that is, were occupied solely in growing vegetables[^185]. So by non-farming hearths were meant hearths without land, only with a garden, that is craftsmen's and strictly smallholders' hearths, and perhaps lodgers' ones too, if they were separate cottages inhabited by lodgers (*komornicy* and *komornice*), single or with families (as, in the last case, the information in the tables of hearths and population of 1789 seems to show).

Third, the table of the hearth tax of 1789, since it contains the amounts of this tax from only one instalment, the Midsummer (June) one, makes it possible to establish the numbers of hearths in the settlements indirectly, by multiplying these amounts by two (to obtain the sum of the tax payable in two instalments for the whole year) and dividing the product by the annual rates of this tax, which for rural settlements were 2 Polish złoty per hearth under the law of the Diet of 1789[^186].
