The Olęders' subjection as to the land showed itself in their rights in rem to the land, through the definition of the forms of holding it and the conditions of the freedom to alienate and sell farms, in their rights of use on the lord's land (the so-called servitudes), and in their dependence on the manor's monopolies (chiefly that of propination). The most important of them, the right to the land, took in Greater Poland in the eighteenth century (apart from a few exceptions of emphyteusis in the sense of a short-term lease of land, for several decades) in principle the form of emphyteusis in the sense of a lease unlimited in time, perpetual, tied to the requirement that the holding of land first be bought by entry money, which once the entry money was paid turned in practice into a perpetual rent tenure (German *Erbzinsrecht*). This was guaranteed by the emphyteutic-perpetual charters, tied to the prior payment to the manor of entry money (*grundgeld*) for the land received. The sources show that the following settlements enjoyed perpetual rent tenure: the Pyzdrskie Ol. (in which, according to the census of 1789, lived *entry-fine holders, rent-payers* [settled] *on the forests of the town of His* [Royal] *Majesty, Pyzdry*), Wrąbczynek and the Wrąbczyńskie/Wrąbczynkowskie Ol., the Białobłockie Ol. (and in them at least the brothers Józef and Jakub Hersa), the Borowieckie St. and Nw. Ol. (which, however, had not paid the *grundgeld* by 1789), the Lubieńskie or Biała Ol. (where the Olęders settled *by the right of free purchase*), the Gadowskie St. and Nw. Ol. (who had still to pay *the grungielt customary among the Holendry*), the Grądy St. and Nw. Ol., Konary or the Konarskie Ol., Wielołęka (where in 1791 the settlers received a second emphyteutic-perpetual foundation charter), the (Ciemierowskie) Stare Ol. (described as *a rent-paying, entry-fine village*), the Cekowskie St. and Nw. Ol., the Dębniałki and Piskory Ol., the Dzierzbińskie – Zamęty and Zdanowiec Ol. (whose inhabitants had earlier obtained individual charters), and at least partly the Jarantowskie Ol., the Kazimierskie St. and Nw. Ol., the Kosmowskie Ol., the Kuszyńskie or Jaszczurowo Ol., the Mycielińskie St. and Nw. Ol., the Oborskie St. and Nw. Ol., the Plewińskie (St.) and Nw. Ol., the Przespolewskie Ol., the Stropieszyńskie or Radzany and Gostynie Ol., Wierzchy or the Tomickie Nw. Ol. and the Zbierskie Ol. (where too, in these settlements, the census of 1789 names *Holendry and entry-fine holders, Olendry and entry-fine holders* or *loose entry-fine holders*)[^250]. The foundation charters for some settlements give the amount of the entry money. In the Cekowskie St. Olędry and the Mycielińskie St. and Nw. Ol. the settlers paid 8 Polish złoty of gruntgielt, that is entry money, per huba; in the Stropieszyńskie or Radzany and Gostynie Ol. (where, according to the charter of 1787, they gave on this account 18 złp. per morgen of ready land or meadow and 3 złp. per morgen of overgrown land, in three instalments, by 1789); in the Wierzchy Ol. (*where they were given a receipt for paying 24 ducats of grundgeld in 1786*)[^251].
