Of these soils, the black earths, on which only 4 settlements arose, are counted as fertile; the marsh, muck and alluvial soils, on which 24 Olęder villages were founded wholly and 4 in part, as moderately fertile; and podzols and dune sands, on which 28 settlements were founded wholly and 4 in part, as infertile[^128]. So half of the Olęder settlements in the Pyzdry Forest were founded on fertile and moderately fertile soils, half on infertile soils, and 4 on mixed soils, though with infertile soils predominating.

5) The Olęder settlements in the Pyzdry Forest were founded by the founders, the owners or holders of the landed estates, in agreement with the locators, the representatives, and perhaps also the recruiters, of the settlers, acting on behalf of the whole future commune, or with whole communes; they often issued to them first individual or collective but preliminary charters (which were in fact agreements between the two parties), and only later (for instance once the necessary number of settlers had gathered) collective and final ones, which settled the Olęder character of the village. On the royal estates they were founded by their tenants (lessees) of the time, on the church estates by the bishop and by the superior and other members of the governing body of the convent of the Franciscan nuns in Kalisz, on the town estates by the magistrate of Pyzdry, and on the noble estates chiefly by the rich and middling nobility. The co-founders or founders of the largest number on a single noble property, that of Grodziec-Królików, no fewer than 8, were its successive owners, Józef Wężyk and Count Franciszek Stadnicki. On the few other properties at most 3-2 were founded, or only 1. Most of them were founded on so-called raw root, that is on places not settled before, in forests or woods, and in a few cases also on the sites of old villages dating back to the Middle Ages but deserted, that is overgrown again with forest, which is also shown by the long free years, 7, meant for clearing, setting up and building the settlements. These settlements were settled by people often described in the foundation charters as "honest" (*uczciwi*), that is personally free (at least once they had crossed the Polish border).
