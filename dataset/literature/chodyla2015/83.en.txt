# Chodyła (2015), p. 83
# translation by Claude

who kept the tap (that is, sold the lord's drink) received ½ huba of land.[^140]. In 1791 there were probably at least as many farms in the Gadowskie St. and Nw. Ol. as farming hearths, that is 22[^141], but it is not known how many of them were of ½ and how many of 1 huba.

In the Grądy St. and Nw. Ol., according to the charter of 1791, the Olęders probably had holdings of 1 huba. This seems to be suggested by the 7 free years meant for clearing the forest. Each huba, that is włóka, was to have 30 morgen measured with the Crown rod. Half a huba was received by *the Grądy community* (that is, both settlements) *for a little school gratys* [free of charge, without payment] *and as much for the innkeeper without payment. They have already paid the entry money for them*[^142]. According to the tariff of the grain levy of 1791 they had 18 self-tilling hearths, that is farms[^143].

In the Grójeckie/Grodzieckie Ol., or the Ol. in Grodziec (later called Janowo?), according to the inspection of the estates of Grodziec and Królików with their appurtenances of 1771, the settlers were settled on 10 hubas[^144].

In the Jaroszewickie/Jaroszewskie Olędry, according to a decree of the landowners' court of 1792, there were 9 farms of an area not more closely stated[^145].

In Konary, or the Konarskie Olędry, the number of włóki was not stated in the second charter of re-founding and enlargement of 1785. It is known only that the settlers received holdings of one huba in the charter, 1 huba to have 30 morgen measured with the Crown rod. The settlement also received 1/2 włóka for a school/teacher together with the Borowieckie Ol.[^146]. In 1791 Konary had 19 "self-tilling" hearths[^147], that is farms.

In the church (monastic) Lubieńskie Ol., founded in 1776 by two collective charters of 10 November 1777, the holdings assigned earlier under individual contracts were probably of one huba and paid for by entry money. The huba was to measure in length *16 rods, that is surveyor's laski of entering feet* [?], *in breadth 45 such rods*. The village also received ½ huba for an inn[^148].

In the Luśnie (Lusina) or Łukomskie Olędry, understood at first as a part of the Trąbczyńskie St. Ol., there were 12 Olęders with a shepherd in 1771; eleven of them held holdings of a size not more closely stated[^149]. There were as many of them according to the decree of the boundary commission court of 1776 on fixing the boundaries between the estates of Trąbczyn and Łukom[^150]. In 1789 the Luśnie Ol. had 10 hearths, and so probably as many farms in all[^151].
