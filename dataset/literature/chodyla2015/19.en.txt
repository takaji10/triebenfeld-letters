For the questions of the distribution of the Olęder settlements, and partly of their layout, cartographic sources proved useful. Among the Polish ones: the "szczegulna" [detailed] map of the voivodeship of Kalisz by Karol de Perthées, court cartographer of King Stanisław August Poniatowski, of 1791 and about 1801, at a scale of 1:225,000[^29], the *Karta topograficzna Królestwa Polskiego* [Topographical map of the Kingdom of Poland] of 1822-1843 at a scale of 1:126,000, prepared by the Quartermaster General's staff of the Polish Army and published in 1834 [*recte* 1843], and the *Karta dawnej Polski...* [Map of old Poland] of 1859 at a scale of 1:300,000 by General W. Chrzanowski[^30]. Among the Prussian ones: two maps of South Prussia, made in the years 1796-1805 and 1802-1803 under the direction of Brodowski, Gilly and Langner, one at a scale of 1:57,600, called the "large" Gilly, and the other reduced to 1:150,000, called the "small" or "printed" Gilly[^31].
