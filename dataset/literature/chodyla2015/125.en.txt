# Chodyła (2015), p. 125
# translation by Claude

Gross income was reduced by the unknown costs of the household's own consumption and of investment, and above all by the obligations to the manor, the Church and the state, which decisively determined the value of the net income.

On account of the perpetual, hereditary use of the land, the Olęders settled in the Pyzdry Forest on private estates were bound to render to the lords – the founders of the settlements and their heirs – money rents for the land, dues in kind and labour (the repair of roads, and *tłoki*, seasonal labour service at harvest and haymaking, and other services, set on a yearly basis after the free years had run out), and to buy the manor's drink under the compulsion of propination. They also bore some administrative fees, for instance on the sale of land, mentioned above.

The charters and other records show that the most important burden was the money rent to the manor, paid on the whole once a year, on a set date (mostly 11 XI), and in an amount according to the size of the holding (farm). It was collected from the individual farmers and handed over jointly by the sołtys of the village, against a receipt, to the feudal lord or his representative. Such receipts for rents received, of the years 1769-1783, are known for the Ciemierowskie/Szymanowickie or Stare Ol.[^288] and in the Pyzdrskie Ol. (in their commune or sołtys book[^289]).

For the Cekowskie Nw. Ol. there is an exact list of the payments and the amounts of rent for the individual farmers, named by their surnames: *Krzysztof Stefan, in whose place now Grymberk, from 1 huba 80 zł; Gotfryd Reszka from 1 huba 9 morgen 105 złp 10 gr; Wojciech Pruchno from ½ h. and 5 morgen 46 złp. 20 gr, from 6 I 1782; Krystian Kurcz, in whose place Kups, from 6 I 1785; the rest of the Olęders settled on 1 and ½ h., from 6 I 1787; Hertel from 1 h. 80 złp; Puntzler from 1½ h. 120 złp; Heinc from ¾ h. 60 złp; Szwander from 1¼ h. 100 złp.; Kups from 1 h. 80 złp.; Krzysztof Kowal from ¾ h. 60 złp.; Gajzler from ¾ h. 60 złp.; Jan Paszkowski from 1 h. 80 zł; Grynberg from 2 h. taken up in 1782, 160 zł.*[^290]. A register of rents paid and labour done, and of fines imposed for various offences, together with transactions of purchase and sale of farms, of the years 1792-1813, is known from the village (sołtys) book of the Dzierzbińskie [-Zamęty] Ol.[^291].

The annual rates of rent per huba varied, but were mostly close to one another. Thus it is known that they came to: in the Dębniałki and Piskory Ol. 72 złp; in the Gadowskie St. and Nw. Ol. 74 złp.; in the Ciążeńskie Ol., the Pyzdrskie Ol., the Białobłockie Ol., the Borowieckie St. and Nw. O., the Konarskie Ol., Wielołęka, the Cekowskie St. Ol. and the Oborskie Ol. 76 złp.; in the Lubieńskie Ol. 78 złp.; in the Kazimierskie Nw. Ol. 45 złp., but per half-huba; in Wrąbczynek and the Wrąbczynkowskie Ol. and the Cekowskie Nw. Ol. 80 złp.; in the Grądy St. and Nw. Ol. 90 złp.[^292] In the Dzierzbińskie – Zamęty and Zdanowiec Ol., the Kosmowskie Ol., the Kuszyńskie Ol. and the Plewińskie Ol., on the other hand, they were set at *60 tynfs per huba*, and in the Wierzchy Ol. at *30 tynfs per half-huba*[^293].
