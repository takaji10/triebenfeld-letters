In the Greater Poland region they were inhabited by people who had come in: Dutch of the Mennonite confession and their descendants (in about 0.8%); in the great majority (about 54%) by Protestants who had come in, chiefly Lutheran, with relatively few Reformed, chiefly ethnic Germans, though in part also Poles); and in a large percentage (about 38%) by Catholic, Polish people, and in part also by people mixed in confession and ethnicity, that is Protestant-Catholic and German-Polish (in about 7.2%)[^5].

An area of Greater Poland in which Olęder settlement also developed fairly vigorously in the eighteenth century was the Pyzdry (or Pyzderska) Forest, from the early Middle Ages to the present day one of the still fairly large forest complexes, which has kept the features of a natural landscape to a considerable degree. It lay and lies, broadly, on the west, east and north between stretches of the river Warta and its tributaries, the Prosna and the Powa, and on the south it reaches a line running roughly through the places Brudzewek, Brudzew, Lipe, Jarantów, Stawiszyn, Ceków Kolonia, Zbiersk, Mycielin, Stropieszyn, Kosmów, Ceków Kolonia, Przespolew Kościelny, Poroże, Smaszew, Gadów, Nowy Świat, Lisiec Nowy and Kowalewek, which partly follows the course of small streams flowing into the Prosna, the Czarna Struga and the Powa, tributaries of the Warta.

This forest area, a part of which has now been set apart as the Nadwarciański Landscape Park (which takes in a part of the Pyzdry Forest in its southern part, but also land north of the Warta), measured, as it stood in the middle of the eighteenth century, about 44,000 ha, that is 440 km². Of this, more than 7,000 ha belonged to the town of Pyzdry. In the Pyzdry Forest the watersheds, the sandy soils and the dunes were the most heavily wooded. Pine predominated among the trees, with some birch, and on wet ground oak and alder. Along the western edge of the forest, parallel to the Prosna, ran the road from Kalisz through Pyzdry to Poznań and Gniezno, and its eastern edge was served by the route joining Kalisz and Kruszwica through Konin. The middle of the forest was crossed by the Stawiszyn-Lądek road along the Czarna Struga, from which above all the economic penetration was made, by settlement going deep into the Pyzdry Forest and gradually reducing its area. This settlement went on in several phases: in the Middle Ages (twelfth/thirteenth to fifteenth century), in early modern times (1501-1793), in modern times (1794/1796-1918), in recent times (to 1945) and in the present, up to today, and within them in several periods. Its growth or its halting were tied to the booms and slumps of social and economic life, deepened by political events, by wars (above all the Swedish "Deluge" (1655-1659/60), the Great Northern War (1700-1721), and the First and Second World Wars) and by natural disasters, among them above all epidemics.
