For baptisms and weddings, on the other hand, the Olęders living in the Pyzdry Forest went to the pastors of the Protestant churches at Grodziec, Nowa Kaźmierka, Konin, Stare Prażuchy, Stawiszyn, Zagórów and Żychlin[^359].

The schools, confirmed in the foundation charters for 16 settlements, which stood in wooden cottages on half-peasant or smallholder holdings with land and garden, mostly also housed the houses of prayer; in the nineteenth century these were replaced in some settlements by newer wooden buildings, exceptionally still standing today, in Piskory and Stary Borowiec, and above all by brick ones, which survive today in, among other places, Białobłoty, Łazińsk Pierwszy, Nowe Grądy, Michalinów Trąbczyński, Stare Prażuchy, Stawiszyn, Wrąbczynkowskie Hol., Zamęty and Zagórów[^360].

An important element of the spatial organisation and the cultural landscape of the Olęder villages were the village burial grounds. For some of the settlements they are mentioned in the foundation charters cited above. Their existence is also shown by their positions on old maps (especially on the Map of the Quartermaster General's staff of the Polish Army of 1839), and above all by their remains (chiefly with gravestones of the second half of the nineteenth and the first half of the twentieth century) surviving to this day in more than 50 settlements, among them: the Ciemierowskie Stare Ol. (today Ciemierów Kolonia), the Wrąbczynkowskie Ol.[^361], the Cekowskie Nw. Ol. (Prażuchy Nw.), Plewnia Kolonia, Kuźnica, Poroże (Stare), Szadykierz[^362], the Piskory Ol., and the Orlińskie Duże Ol.[^363]. They varied in area according to the size of the village's population. In their centre a tall wooden cross was put up. They were surrounded by a low fence, exceptionally by a wall, also of bog iron ore, as can be seen in Orlina Duża. The existence of burial grounds is confirmed, for instance, by the foundation charters for 10 of the 26 settlements in the district of Kalisz: the Dębniałki and Piskory Ol. (they were to receive land for a common "burial ground"), the Dzierzbińskie – Zdanowiec and Zamęty Ol. (they were to receive for a common cemetery for the dissenters a plot of 14 by 14 rods), the Kuszyńskie or Jaszczurowo Ol., together with the Kosmowskie Ol. ("*...for the smallness of the place on the said Holendry called Jaszczurowo..., so these Jaszczurowo Holendry are to be buried on the Kosmowskie Holendry...*"), the Przespolewskie Ol. (they received a plot for a cemetery, which they were to enclose with a fence), the Stropieszyńskie St. and Nw. Ol. (they received 3 morgen for a school and a cemetery, and timber from the manor), and the Zbierskie Ol. (*for the "kirchow", that is the cemetery, they received a plot of 15x15 rods*)[^364].
