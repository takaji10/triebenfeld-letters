# Chodyła (2015), p. 121
# translation by Claude

Among the members of the self-government the main burden of administrative duties lay on the sołtys. He was above all bound to see to order and good husbandry in the commune, and that the whole commune met its obligations to the manor – rents, labour services and payments to the manor and the Church, and taxes to the state treasury – punctually and in full. It was for him and the assessors to collect these dues (recorded for the Stropieszyńskie or Gostynie and Radzany Ol.). Since the commune was usually jointly responsible for the manor's dues, the commune self-government was bound to remove an Olęder who farmed badly and to put another on the farm (Cekowskie Nw. Ol., Plewińskie St. and Nw. Ol.). The sołtys probably also, with the lord's support, carried out the judgments of the commune court[^279].

Most of the 58 eighteenth-century Olęder settlements in the Pyzdry Forest, of various sizes in their area of land (especially arable), their number of hearths and their population, were independent spatial-economic and administrative-judicial units (communes). They formed separate one-village communes with their own self-government, a sołtys and assessors. Some of them, however, 14 (the Borowieckie St. and Nw., the Grądy St. and Nw. Ol., the Orlińskie D. and M. Ol., the Trąbczyńskie St. and Nw. Ol., the Mycielińskie St. and Nw. Ol., the Plewińskie St. and Nw. Ol., the Stropieszyńskie – Radzany and Gostynie Ol.), formed two-village communes, and in one case the Gadowskie St. and Nw. Ol. and probably the Smuszewskie Ol. formed a three-village commune, with a common sołtys and assessors.

Perhaps some of the Olęder villages or communes founded in the Pyzdry Forest had their own seal as a mark of their self-government, with which its organs – the office and the court (or rather the office-court) – sealed their letters and documents, which, as originals or copies, were entered in their own commune books – sołtys books, sometimes town books – and were also sent to other communes[^280]. Commune and sołtys books (containing, among other things, copies of foundation charters, registers of rents and labour services, transactions of sale and purchase of farms, inventories of movables and the Olęders' wills), kept by the commune clerk, who was probably most often the teacher, survive for only three Olęder communes, the Pyzdrskie Ol. and the Dzierzbińskie – Zamęty and Zdanowiec Ol., one common book for the last two[^281]. (Ill. 25)
