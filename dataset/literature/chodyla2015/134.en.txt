Of other equipment, the querns for grinding grain and the looms for making linen deserve notice[^336].

In the farm buildings were kept all kinds of tools: carts, scythes, *radła* (ards), ploughs, harrows, rakes, pitchforks and others. Among them the wooden *radło rylcowe* (sole ard) used in western Greater Poland, Bohemia and Silesia is noteworthy: it consisted of a straight beam, forked and running into handles, and of a share-beam set slantwise in the beam, with an iron share with an eye fitted on it. Such a share was found in 1958 in the Jarantowkie [sic] Ol. (Jarantów Kolonia), and its occurrence here (and not only here) is explained in the literature by the inhabitants of this village descending from Olęder villages inhabited by Czech people from the area of Zbąszyń[^337].

People dressed according to the customs brought from their places of origin, differently – as is obvious – on weekdays and on feast days, in clothes probably mostly of their own making, of linen and sheep's wool, but partly also sewn from cloth bought in the towns and little towns.

The food of the people of the Olęder settlements that arose in the Pyzdry Forest probably did not differ fundamentally from that of others, in proportion to their prosperity. They ate rye bread and other grain or flour dishes, groats, vegetables, butter, cheese, from time to time meat, in Lent probably herrings and other fish, and honey from their own apiaries, and fruit, and they drank beer, then general, and also milk[^338].

At family celebrations (christenings, weddings) and on other occasions they were obliged (under the manor's monopoly and the compulsion of propination) to drink vodka and beer in the lord's inn or from the lord's distilleries and malthouse. This is stated expressly in the foundation charters for the Cekowskie Nw. Ol., the Przespolewskie Ol., the Zbierskie Ol., the Grądy St. and Nw. Ol. and the Wrąbczynkowskie Ol.[^339]. A ban on bringing in "*beer and spirits and any drink*" from elsewhere was in force in the Cekowskie Nw. Ol., the Dzierzbińskie-Zamęty and Zdanowiec Ol., the Plewińskie St. and Nw. Ol., and also in the Wrąbczynkowskie Ol.[^340].
