# chodyla2015  66  en
# Chodyła (2015), p. 66
# translation by Claude

Perhaps as early as about 1777, and certainly in the years 1785-1789, the settlement of Stara Huta-Grójeckie Olędry (today Stara Huta) arose in the same district, in the parish and on the noble estate of Grodziec ("Grojec"). Its founder was probably Count Franciszek Stadnicki, judicial starost of Ostrzeszów, then owner of the estates of Grodziec, Królików and Biskupice. The settlement was founded on empty land overgrown with forest. The inhabitants of this village were Catholics and Poles[^108].

About 1786, and certainly before 1790, the Siąszyckie or Złotkowskie Olędry were founded in the district of Konin, in the parish of Grochowy, on the noble estate of Siąszyce. Their founder was most probably the then owner of the Siąszyce estate, Kazimierz Biernacki, mentioned above, by then styled *stolnik* of Piotrków. According to the *manifestacja* (protest) of his plenipotentiary of 1790, Onufry Gałczyński, a *towarzysz* of the National Cavalry, with a few of his men and 2 privates, attacked in December 1789 the Olęders settled *on the manor land of the village of Siąszyce*, took the cattle of two of them, Łukasz and Bernard, and carried off one Olęder, Łukasz Łyszkowski, to Konin, forcing him to enter military service in the squadron in which he served, although he was a farmer and an immigrant and the new law of the Diet forbade it[^109] (Ill. 20). In 1791 this settlement had 9 "self-tilling" hearths[^110].

In 1787 the Mycielińskie Stare and Nowe Olędry (today Kazala St. and Nw.) were founded in the district of Kalisz, in the parish of Kościelec, on the noble estate of Mycielin, by two charters, in fact settlement agreements, made at Mycielin on the same day, 30 March 1787. Their founder was Maciej Łuczycki, owner of the estate of Mycielin. He made one preliminary agreement with 2 "honest", that is personally free, peasants with German names, who, being unable to write, put crosses under it. The settlers were settled on land partly *wyrudowany* [cleared], partly overgrown with various trees, called Kazalnia, towards the boundary of the village of Korzeniew, and received 6 free years for setting up their farms[^111] (Ill. 21). The second agreement, also a preliminary one, was made between Maciej Łuczycki and 6 Olęders, also "honest" (3 of them with Polish names, 1 with a German one, 1 named only by his first name and 1 whose surname is not given). *They were settled on a part of the ground, that is of the thickets dense with various trees, called Krzewina*, on land called Rusiny, by the boundary of the lands of the village of Słuszków, which needed *rudunek*. They were guaranteed 7 free years. Should there not be enough land for the sixth of these settlers, he was to be settled on another part of the ground called Kazale [Kazalnia] and to clear his holding there. The agreement was signed by the founder and the Olęders, in the case of the latter, who could not write, with crosses[^112].
