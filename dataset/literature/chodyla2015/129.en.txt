The amounts paid in one instalment of the hearth tax, also called the chimney tax, according to the number of hearths in the settlements, are given in the table of this tax for three districts of the voivodeship of Kalisz of 1789[^319].

The annual rate of the tax of the offering was set for most of the 58 settlements on the basis of the average income of their owners over the last three (exceptionally two) years. According to the data surviving in the table of the tax of the offering of 10 or 20 groszy of 1789, this tax was paid on the annual rent, on the propination of beer and vodka, and on the dues in rye (counting the korzec at 6 zł)[^320].

The grain levy from the self-tilling hearths was fixed in 1791 and served the needs of the army[^321].

Besides, all the inhabitants of many Olęder settlements, Catholics but also, to a large extent, Protestants, were bound to render to the Roman Catholic Church dues in the form of fees *jura stolae* (that is for baptisms, weddings and funerals), *meszne* (mass dues) and *kolęda* (Christmas offering). The Protestants paid them until 1768, as they often did after the toleration law of that year too, especially when they settled on land formerly settled and later deserted, in Olęder villages that arose on the site of medieval settlements assigned in this respect to Catholic parishes, which was sometimes contained in their foundation deeds (*akty erekcyjne*)[^322]. The foundation charters set out these obligations in general terms, saying that they would be fixed in separate agreements made between the settlers and the parish priests through the manor (that is the feudal founder of the settlement), or else they gave conditions already agreed earlier.
