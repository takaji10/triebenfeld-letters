# chodyla2015  124  en
# Chodyła (2015), p. 124
# translation by Claude

The second important branch of the economy of the inhabitants of the Olęder settlements in the Pyzdry Forest was the raising of animals: cattle, sheep and pigs, and also horses and poultry. This is shown by direct information about permission to keep sheep and cattle, and by indirect information about meadows and pastures, given in the charters for settlements already named and others, namely for: Wrąbczynek and the Wrąbczynkowskie Ol. (where the settlers received 16 morgen of land cleared for meadows, and Jakub Wróbel one and a half morgen for keeping sheep and cattle), the Pyzdrskie Ol. (free pasture for livestock in the forests), the Gadowskie St. and Nw. Ol. (permission to raise cattle and other livestock, but without damage to the manor's pastures), Wielołęka (permission to fatten hogs, free pasture for cattle in places set aside), the Cekowskie St. Ol., the Dzierzbińskie – Zamęty and Zdanowiec Ol. (they may keep as many horned cattle as they wish), the Przespolewskie Ol. and the Zbierskie Ol.[^283] The charters for the Cekowskie St. and Cekowskie Nw. Ol. set limits on the number of stock kept. The first allowed *the raising of 25 sheep, and the second the keeping on a huba of 7 cows, 4 horses, 4 oxen, 10 pigs and 30 sheep*[^284]. In the Ciążeńskie Ol. there were fees for pasture: *for a cow 1 zł, for a head of young stock, not counting calves, 18 gr*[^285]. In the Dębniałki and Piskory Ol. the settlers received free pasture in the forests and pastures in return for each of the 16 farmers ploughing within the year 10, and in all 150, *laski* (rods) of the manor's ploughland on the Bilawy, *for fallow, spring crop and strip*. The raising of poultry also played a significant part. This is confirmed indirectly by the information quoted below about dues in kind: in geese, capons and eggs.

The people of the Olęder settlements founded in the Pyzdry Forest drew additional income from non-farming occupations: from bee-keeping, which was allowed by the settlement charters for the Pyzdrskie Ol., Wrąbczynek and the Wrąbczynkowskie Ol., and Wielołęka[^286].

The people of the Olęder settlements founded in the Pyzdry Forest in the years 1746-1792 also most probably took an active part in trade, mostly local and retail, selling chiefly the surplus of their crops (grain) and their stock (cattle, horses, pigs, poultry and dairy produce) and of what they made or obtained in their non-farming occupations (for instance crafts and bee-keeping), and buying in return grain and other foodstuffs, timber, or tools at the markets and fairs in the towns and little towns. The foundation charter for the Cekowskie St. Ol. (allowed trade in salt and bread), and that for the Cekowskie Nw. Ol. of 1774, guaranteed the settlers freedom to trade in hogs, horses, cattle and sheep, and forbade trade in salt and iron[^287]. None of the foundation charters for the other Olęder villages states this expressly, but there is no doubt that the Olęders were guaranteed freedom of trade, since it was chiefly in this way that they could obtain the money to meet their financial obligations to the manor, the state and the Church. Jews were often the intermediaries in the trade in farm produce.
