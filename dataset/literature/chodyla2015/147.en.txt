At the same time and later, the individual parts of the Pyzdrskie Olędry turned, as the area of the larger farms was divided and new settlers were brought in, into villages of many farmsteads spatially and separate administratively. As a result, what was originally one settlement in administrative and judicial terms, with colony-type, scattered (dispersed) buildings in the form of so-called *pustkowia*, turned into 9, or even, counting in turn round the edge of the forest, 12 separate settlements: Kruszyny, Zamość, Grądy Dolne and Górne, Baraniec, Zimochowiec, Tłoczyzna, Glinianki, Trzcianki, Zapowiednia, Żdżary and Królewiny. At this time, too, 2 or even 3 separate settlements in time split off from some other Old Polish Olęder settlements; for instance the Białobłockie Ol. divided into Białobłoty, Garncarski Kąt and Krzyżówka.

After the enfranchisement of the peasants in the Kingdom of Poland (1864), a new wave of settlement appeared in the years 1870-1880 on private landed estates, among them those bordering on the town woods of Pyzdry from the south and east, on the parcelled lands of demesne farms. Then and later, up to 1914, 55 rural settlements, on the whole smaller ones, and at least 9 industrial settlements were founded on them. Among the settlers of the Old Polish and later Olęder settlements and other rent-paying settlements in the Pyzdry Forest, at least 82 in all, the German element predominated. The population of the new colonies, partly forming fairly compact groups, partly scattered, differed from the native population in language, religion, its own social institutions, such as the school and the house of prayer, dress, customs and manners. It came chiefly from Silesia and to a lesser extent from the Neumark (Ciążeńskie Holendry, Wrąbczyn, Wrąbczynkowskie Holendry, Olchowo, Mały Las, Tarszewo, Babia, Józefów, Zarzew, Augustynów, Święcia). In the nineteenth century there were significant changes in the buildings, the population and the confessional, and probably also the ethnic, composition, and in the literacy of the inhabitants of the Olęder settlements that arose in the Pyzdry Forest in the eighteenth century[^367].

In the nineteenth century there were also changes in the church organisation of the Olęder settlements. The settlements with an exclusively or mostly Protestant population, almost exclusively Lutheran[^368], were joined to the Lutheran parishes of the Evangelical-Augsburg diocese of Kalisz. It used the Protestant parish churches at Stawiszyn (1780), Grodziec (from 1796), Prażuchy (from 1808), Konin (from 1826) and Zagórów (from 1858) and the filial churches at Józefów near Kaźmierka Stara (from 1843) and Lisewo (1858) and at Żychlin[^369].

In the inter-war period about 12 new settlements arose, often in the character of hamlets or outlying hamlets with their own names (though etymologically mostly tied to the names of old villages).
