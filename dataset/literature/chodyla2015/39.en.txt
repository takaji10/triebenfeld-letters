This village was in a way re-founded and enlarged by his son-in-law Count Franciszek Stadnicki, starost of Ostrzeszów, owner of the estates of Grójec (Grodziec), Królików and Biskupice, by his emphyteutic-perpetual charter, issued at Biskupice on 3 February 1791. He did so... *to increase the population and multiply the intrats* [income] *from useless woods, moved by the requests* [!] *of free incomers, partly already settled and partly still to settle..., who have already paid the gruntgielt* [entry money]. The free years were probably 7, or at least 5. In this charter the founder also assigned the village half a huba free of charge for a little school, and laid on the settlers, who were of mixed confession, Catholics and dissenters (Protestants), the obligation to pay fees for baptisms, weddings and funerals to the parish priest of the parish church at Grodziec; the dissenters too were to hold their baptisms and weddings in that church[^57].

In 1768 the Cekowskie Stare Ol. arose in the district of Kalisz, on the royal estates, in the lease (*dzierżawa*) of Ceków, in the parish of Kosmów. Their founder was Jan Bezan, captain of the Front Guard of the Crown of His Royal Majesty and the Commonwealth, and the foundation charter he issued at Ceków on 18 April 1768 was confirmed on 26 January ("tycznia") 1774 by their next lessee, Franciszek Byszewski. 9 *honest men of the Olęder estate* (2 with Polish and 7 with German names), Lutherans and Catholics, were settled in the settlement. They settled "in the thickets of the Ceków estate", that is in woodland that needed *rudunek* (clearing), for which, and for setting up their farms, they received 7 free years. They were also bound to pay tithe to the parish priest of the parish church at Kosmów. The settlers declared that they were ready to pay for the "contract", that is the collective charter, a minted thaler each, having already paid for it *one red złoty*, that is a ducat[^58]. According to the inventory of the Ceków estate of 1774 there were then 5 Olęder farmers here, newly brought in, settled under a contract, that is under the foundation charter described above. *"They have put up huts for themselves, by which they want to bind themselves further according to the measuring out of the hubas* [łany, włóki], *only that His Honour Pan* [Antoni] *Kawecki, owner* [of neighbouring] *Kuszyn, forbids them, maintaining that it is his own ground; where the lands were allotted them by the late His Honour Pan Jan Bezan, holder of the estate of the village of Ceków, and for that reason they do not want to bind themselves by these"*[^59].

In the years 1770-1772 six more Olęder settlements were founded in the district of Konin, in the parishes of Królików and partly Grodziec and Królików, on the noble estates of Grodziec and Królików.
