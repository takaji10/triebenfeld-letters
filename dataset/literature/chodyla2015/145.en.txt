The second important branch of the economy of the inhabitants of the Olęder settlements in the Pyzdry Forest was the raising of animals – cattle, sheep and pigs, and also horses and poultry. The Olęders took an active part in trade, selling the surplus of their crops (grain) and their stock (cattle, horses, pigs, poultry and dairy produce), sometimes also trading in goods made or obtained in non-farming occupations (for instance crafts and bee-keeping), and buying in return grain and other foodstuffs, timber, or tools at the markets and fairs in the towns. On account of the perpetual, hereditary use of the land, the inhabitants of the Olęder villages founded in the Pyzdry Forest were bound to render to the lords – the founders of the settlements and their heirs – money rents for the land, dues in kind and labour (*tłoki*, seasonal labour service at harvest and haymaking, and other services, and carting journeys), paid on a yearly basis after the free years had run out, and to buy the manor's drink under the compulsion of propination. They also bore some administrative fees, for instance on the sale of land. The most important burden was the money rent, paid on the whole once a year, on a set date, and in an amount according to the size of the holding (farm). It was collected from the individual farmers and handed over jointly by the sołtys of the village against a receipt from the feudal lord or his representative. The inhabitants of all the Olęder settlements founded in the eighteenth century in the Pyzdry Forest were also bound, like the inhabitants of other villages, to render to the state taxes in money, paid every year in two instalments (March and September) – the poll tax, attested in separate tariffs for the years 1775 and 1789-1790, the *hiberna* in the settlements on royal estates in the tariff of 1775, the offering of 10 gr (on noble estates) and 20 gr on church estates of 1789 – and a tax in kind, the so-called levy (*zsypka*) from the "self-tilling" [farming] hearths, in the form of a set number of korzec of rye and oats from such a hearth. Besides, all the inhabitants of many Olęder settlements, Catholics but also, to a large extent, Protestants, were bound to render to the Roman Catholic Church dues in the form of fees *jura stolae* (that is for baptisms, weddings and funerals), *meszne* and *kolęda*. The Protestants were bound to pay *jura stolae* both until 1768 and often after the toleration law of that year, especially when they settled on land formerly settled and later deserted, in Olęder villages that arose on the site of medieval settlements assigned in this respect to a parish, sometimes also in the foundation deeds of these parish districts.

6) The material and spiritual culture and the daily life of the people of the Olęder settlements in the Pyzdry Forest in the eighteenth century are not easy to describe, because the information in the written sources is very scanty. Supplementing it with data from museum documentation, the ethnographic literature and modern research, it can be said that, in the case of dwelling and farm buildings, the architecture of the buildings has common features characteristic of the area of the Pyzdry Forest.
