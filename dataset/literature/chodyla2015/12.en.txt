The decline in the number of settlements was overcome gradually, on a wider scale, only from the 1720s, by the founding of rent-paying settlements, which from the second half of the 1740s were founded above all under Olęder law. As a result the number of places grew, in absolute and in relative terms, through the founding of entirely new villages and hamlets on what was called raw root (*surowy korzeń*, untouched land) and through the rebuilding of old ones, but on other legal terms and mostly with new inhabitants.

In following the development of the earliest settlement, Olęder settlement included, in the Pyzdry Forest on the basis of Old Polish sources, one has to remember that, in the old administrative divisions, its area lay within a single voivodeship, that of Kalisz, but within three of its districts, namely those of Pyzdry, Konin and Kalisz. Today the area of the Pyzdry Forest lies within a single voivodeship, that of Greater Poland, six districts (Kalisz, Konin, Pleszew, Słupca, Turek and Września) and fifteen communes: Blizanów, Ceków-Kolonia, Chocz, Gizałki, Grodziec, Lądek, Malanów, Mycielin, Pyzdry, Rychwał, Rzgów, Stare Miasto, Stawiszyn, Tuliszków and Zagórów[^6].

Among the rural settlements that arose in this forest complex in early modern times, and especially in the second half of the eighteenth century, the Olęder settlements, 58 in number, decidedly predominated, and this study is devoted to their earliest history. These settlements arose in the years 1746-1793, and today 67 villages or parts of villages correspond to them, mostly under changed names.
