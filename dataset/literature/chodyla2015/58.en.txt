They received 7 free years for building and setting up their farms, which ran out for all the Olęders in 1788, except the first, whose freedom ran out in 1787[^95]; so they settled on overgrown, most probably wooded, land.

In the years 1782 the Kazimierskie or Niniewskie Stare Ol. were founded, and in 1790 the Kazimierskie Nowe Olędry, situated in the district of Kalisz, in the parish and on the estate of Chocz. For the first of them the foundation charter of 1782 is unknown[^96]. The second received a charter issued on 9 November 1790 by the Rev. Kazimierz Lipski, mitred provost of Chocz, abbot of Lubin. It shows that the locator of this settlement was Georg [Jerzy] Müller. The settlers, who were Germans and Lutherans, received 7 free years (counted from 9 XI 1790), running out in 1797[^97]. This last piece of information shows that the village arose on wooded land, which needed so long a time for clearing and setting up the farms. It seems that the Kazimierskie St. Ol. were founded earlier on land of a similar character, and that their founder too was the said Kazimierz Lipski (Ill. 15), from whose first name both settlements took their names.
