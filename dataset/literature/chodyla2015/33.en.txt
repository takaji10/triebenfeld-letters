# Chodyła (2015), p. 33
# translation by Claude

north-west of Lisewo, the new settlement of the Lisewskie Ol., also called Olsz or Olszowiec. The information of two surveys of the starosty of Pyzdry, that of 1765 on the entry-fine village of Lisewo paying rents, and that of 1789 on the farmers and the rent-paying cottagers in Lisewo and on four *contract settlers on isolated holdings* (*pustkowianie kontraktowi*) by the village of Lisewo[^46], does not undermine the view that this second settlement was Olęder in its legal character, even though its inhabitants are not expressly called Olęders. Some doubt on this point can be raised only by the fact that its inhabitants were on individual contracts (agreements). This, however, seems to have been temporary, and they seem to have obtained a collective (though to us unknown) foundation charter, since in the later sources too the addition *Olędry* was consistently used in the name of this village. In the registers of the parish of Szymanowice of the years 1775-1780 there are entries on inhabitants of the "Lisewskie Olędry *alias* [otherwise] Olszowiec", probably mixed in nation and confession[^47]. This settlement also appears as *Holendry Lisewskie* on the map of K. Perthées. The village now called Kolonia Lisewo, on the other hand, was not an Olęder settlement (at least not an Old Polish one). It was founded with German and Lutheran settlers in the so-called Frederician colonisation in the period of so-called South Prussia, in its Poznań department, on the state domain of Pyzdry, as a colony by the demesne farm of Lisewo, which in 1804 numbered 20 families, 99 persons, with an area of 23 łany and 13 morgen[^48].

Before 1755-1756 the Ciemierowa/Tomickie/Szymanowickie Stare Olędry were founded, situated in the district of Kalisz and the parish of Szymanowice, and recorded on Gilly's map. Their founder was probably Ignacy Samek Glis[z]czyński, starost of Pasierby, so called from the village of Pasierby, the seat of a non-castle *starosty*, in the district of Gostynin, in the voivodeship of Rawa, owner of the estate of Tomice. From the form of the names of the Olęders written in the baptismal registers of the parish of Szymanowice in those years[^49] it can be inferred that Catholics, Germans and Poles, lived in this settlement, though it is not out of the question that some of them were Protestant Lutherans, who were also often forced to receive this sacrament at the hands of Catholic priests.

In 1761 the Zbierskie Olędry were founded in the district of Kalisz, in the parish of Zbiersk, on the noble estate of that name. Their founder was Stanisław Dzik Kożuchowski, land judge of Wieluń. According to the emphyteutic-perpetual foundation charter he issued at Zbiersk on 16 December 1761, the colonists were settled in the forest, and received 8 free years for clearing and setting up their farms
