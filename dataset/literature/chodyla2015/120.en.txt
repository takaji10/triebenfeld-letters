Such a *wilkierz*, a very extensive one (of 41 points), written in German, undated, but most probably from before 1793, survives for the Wielołęka Ol. Because of the limited size of this study its content cannot be discussed in detail. It is worth saying, however, that it regulates such matters as the role of the commune self-government in ensuring harmonious life among neighbours and order and peace in the village, among other things by controlling the movement of persons coming into it from outside; the organisation and working of the commune court; the keeping and watching over of honesty (also by imposing penalties); it set the rules of trade in cattle, horses and other goods, of neighbourly help in rebuilding after fires, the conditions for returning neighbours' livestock seized for damage, help for widows and persons over 70, and the inhabitants' share in founding and maintaining a church and a school after the free years had run out[^273]. A *wilkierz* is also mentioned in the foundation charters for the Zbierskie Ol. and the Dzierzbińskie – Zamęty and Zdanowiec Ol. The first states of it: *They are to be judged by the sołtys according to their law for good conduct, which they will give, alias the wilkier, and from him the wronged party is to have appeal to the manor*[^274]. The second contains this information about it: *They are given a wilkir, which will belong to the charter, and the charter to the wilkir; this, however, is the most principal* [the first] *point in the wilkir: that when there is need they should be ready with their whole heart, with whatever proper weapons they can have, for a sudden need, that is should an enemy break in, for defence, and also for defence [against] fire, should the forest catch fire by some chance*[^275].

From the judgments of the commune court the Olęders had the right of appeal to the manor, that is the lord's court (in the Pyzdrskie Ol. to the court of the magistrate, in the Cekowskie Nw. Ol. and the Zbierskie Ol. to the *dominium* court, that is the lord's), for which the right to judge in criminal cases of robbery, arson, homicide and serious bodily harm, and of course all offences against the lord, was also reserved[^276]. Court fines went half to the manor and half to the court (Cekowskie Nowe Ol.), or to the manor alone[^277].

The lords also guaranteed the Olęders their protection, and promised them and their descendants, in their own name and that of their heirs, to keep the terms of the foundation charters. They sometimes also bound the Olęders not to seek anyone else's protection against them, stating that *should anyone make use of it, he cannot claim payment for his plot*[^278].
