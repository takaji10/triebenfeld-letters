Within each of these ways of founding Olęder villages, mostly according to their form on the ground, shaped chiefly by factors of the geographical environment, there were three types of spatial form: 1) the lowland settlement adapted to the relief and the water conditions of the land, the row village (*rzędówka*), a village with compact, more concentrated (a regular street village) or looser (an irregular street village) buildings; 2) the loose, scattered settlement (in the form of larger or smaller irregular clusters of farmsteads, sometimes with a green or a pond); and 3) the mixed settlement, combining some elements of the first two types. The more or less compact settlement, that is the row village (also called the marsh row village), and the loose, scattered settlement were, in principle, two new types of village layout brought by Olęder settlement into Greater Poland, and the most widespread in it. Row villages – street villages with strips of land holding various kinds of farmland (fields, meadows, pastures and others) – formed in the conditions of Greater Poland one, two, but sometimes three straight, bent or broken rows, lines of farmsteads (farms), placed in various ways in relation to each other (parallel and perpendicular), more or less concentrated and of various lengths (mostly 1–1.5 km), much less, however, than in Royal Prussia[^189].

The layout of most Olęder settlements in the period closest to their founding can be established only from the later Map of the Quartermaster General's staff of the Army of the Kingdom of Poland of 1839, and from the modern Map of Poland of the Surveyor General of the Country at a scale of 1:100,000 and the tourist map of the Pyzdry Forest at 1:55,000. From an analysis of these maps, taking into account the fact that as a result of the enfranchisement reform of 1864 the layout of these rent-paying villages without a demesne farm did not change fundamentally, it can be said that of the 58 Old Polish Olęder settlements in the Pyzdry Forest, 9 settlements were marsh row villages, founded where marshy and dry land met, on mixed ground, with rows of buildings in various arrangements, sometimes partly scattered (Białobłockie Ol., Cekowskie St. Ol., Gadowskie Ol., Kazimierskie St. and Nw. Ol., Lisewskie Ol., Luśnie Ol., Myszakowskie Ol., Piskory Ol.); 2 settlements were regular and irregular street villages founded on mixed ground (Dębniałki Ol., Grodziec Ol.); as many as 40 settlements were loose, scattered settlements; and 7 were mixed (Białobłockie Ol., Gadowskie St. and Nw. Ol., Orlińskie St. and Nw. Ol., Wierzchy Ol. and Wrąbczynek).

The layout of the Olęder settlements (made at the founding on raw root, or taken over from older settlements), the network of roads, the arrangement and system of the land, and sometimes the placing of the buildings, shaped their geometric forms. Accordingly, from the point of view of their physiognomy, the Olęder villages represented various types and subtypes of form.
