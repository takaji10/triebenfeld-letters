# Chodyła (2015), p. 108
# translation by Claude

In the Cekowskie Nowe Ol., or Prażuchy, their neighbours, which arose on the same royal lease of Ceków in 1774 and 1780, at least 8 Lutheran colonists, with German and Polish names, settled according to the settlement contract, namely: Wojciech Pruchno, Krzysztof Stefan (sołtys), Gotfryd Reszka, Krystian Kurc, Krystian Heinc, Frydrych Heinc, Gotfryd Szwander, Jan Paszkowski. Later, in the years 1782, 1785 and 1787, there were settled: Kups, Hertel, Puntzler, Krzysztof Kowal, Gajzler and Grynberg[^208].

In the 6 Olęder settlements founded before 1770 and in 1771-1772 (and re-founded in 1787 and 1789) in the district of Konin, on the noble estates of Grodziec and Królików, the population was mixed in nation and confession. In the Białobłockie Ol. at least several colonists settled, Lutherans with German names. The number, names, nationality and confession of those brought in to settle in 1787 are unknown. In 1790, 157 Catholics and 48 dissenters lived here. In 1792 the Olęders Józef and Jakub Hersa, brothers, and Andrzej Langa/Lange lived in this village[^209].

In the Borowieckie St. and Nw. Ol., the settlers, settled in an unknown number, bore German and Polish names and were Lutherans and Catholics by confession. According to the inventory of the properties of Grodziec and Królików of 1779, 27 farmers lived in the Borowieckie Ol., named with their first names and surnames (4 of them of Polish sound). In 1790 there were 52 Catholics and 42 dissenters in this settlement[^210].

In the Grójeckie or Grodzieckie Olędry there settled, in an unknown number, probably 10 farmers, Germans – Lutherans[^211].

In the Grądy Stare and Nowe lived settlers with German and Polish names, in 1790 exclusively Lutherans, 100 in number[^212].

In Konary Ol., in turn, whose locator, that is establisher, was Jan Kimel, mentioned above, sołtys of the Wielołęka Ol., settlers with German and Polish names were settled, in 1790 exclusively Lutherans, 81 in number[^213]).

In two settlements founded in the district of Konin about 1771 and in 1776, but on the church estates of the convent of the Order of St Clare (the Franciscan nuns) of Kalisz, namely in the Gliny Olędry and the Lubieńskie Ol., lived Poles and Catholics[^214]. In the foundation charter for the latter settlement the settlers are described as honest Olęders *...of the honest faith and rites of the Roman Catholic church...*, being Poles now and *hereafter*.[^215].

In Poroże Małe, in the district of Kalisz, which arose in or before 1775, settled settlers whose names and confession are unknown.
