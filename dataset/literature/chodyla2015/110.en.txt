# Chodyła (2015), p. 110
# translation by Claude

Baszcz, Bończyk, Baczkiewicz, Baranowski, Broniarczyk, Depczyński, Drzewiecki, Frankowski, Galeski, Konieczny, Kotecki, Kruszyński, Łyszkiewicz, Modrzejewski, Nowiński, Rodowski, Sobierajski, Stencel[^221].

In the Jarantowskie Olędry, founded in 1778 in the district of Kalisz, in the parish of Lipe, on a noble estate, probably Lutherans and Catholics, Germans and Poles, lived[^222].

In Wierzchy, or the Tomickie Nw. Ol., which arose in 1781 in the district of Kalisz, 12 settlers settled with Polish (8) and German (4) names (Jan Bukowiecki, Jędrzej Markwat, Kazimierz Ignaczak, Stanisław Opielak, Wojciech Szulc, Wojciech Korbal, Krystian and Wojciech Janke, Józef Szumański, Maciej Jedwab, Karol Białeszyński and Karol Szewc), most probably Catholics[^223].

In the Kazimierskie or Niniewskie St. and Nw. Ol., founded in the same district in the years 1782 and 1790, lived Germans, Lutherans, who left behind a church and a cemetery. The locator of the latter was Georg [Jerzy] Müller[^224].

Settlers of the same nation and confession settled in the Dzierzbińskie – Zamęty and Zdanowiec Olędry, also founded in the district of Kalisz, in 1783, by one foundation charter, but forming separate communes. Two settlers, with names of German sound, are known from the charter: Marcin Szynwald and Jan Fitz, Lutherans. Their confession is also shown by the grant in the charter of land for the *bakałarze* (teachers/schools) and for a common cemetery "*for the dissenters*"[^225].

In the Plewińskie (Stare) and Nowe, or Kuźnickie, Olędry (founded before 1783 and in 1793), in the district of Kalisz, 19 settlers settled (14 with German and 5 with Polish names), Lutherans. They included Bogusłw Werner and Paweł Pus, who made the foundation agreement with the founder, Antoni Kawiecki, on behalf of the whole commune, and Krystian Hek, Rejmand Gaysler, Marcin Nyps, Jędrzej Fire, Bogumił Konczak, Krystian Vizur (?), Marcin Tulik (or Talik), Jan Reska, Bogusław Lang, Bogusław Litman, Samuel Bigiel, Jan Gierat, Bogusław Stary Werner, Maciej Piwecki, Jan Ściernisko, Bogusław Glapa and Krzysztof Figa. Their Protestant confession is confirmed by the village's receiving land for a *szkolny* (teacher)[^226].

In the settlements set up in 1783, Dębniałki or the Bolimowskie/Brudzewskie Ol. and the Piskory Olędry, in the district of Kalisz, the first joint settlement charter of 1784 names only these settlers: 2 with German names (Mikołaj Hayschler, sołtys, and Michał Helman, assessor) and 1 with a Polish name (Franciszek Borowy, assessor), Catholics and Lutherans. There were, however, more settlers (6 Olęders received free years to 1791 and 2 to 1792); the dissenters were also to receive plots for a burial ground and for a teacher (school), common to both settlements[^227]. It is also known that 11 settlers with German and Polish names, Catholics and Protestant Lutherans, were settled in the Piskory or Lipeckie Olędry[^228].
