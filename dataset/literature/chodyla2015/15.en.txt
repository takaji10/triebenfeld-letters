As Tab. 1 shows, of the total of 58 Olęder settlements that arose in 3 Old Polish districts of the voivodeship of Kalisz, in 21 parishes (4 of which lay on the border of two districts), 5 were founded in the district of Pyzdry in 4 parishes, 28 in the district of Konin in 8 parishes, and 25 in the district of Kalisz in 10 parishes. Today these villages, as 67 rural settlements or parts of them, lie in the voivodeship of Greater Poland, in 6 districts and 15 communes, namely in the districts of Kalisz (Blizanów – 3, Ceków Kolonia – 9, Mycielin – 5, Stawiszyn – 1), Konin (Grodziec – 8, Rychwał – 4, Rzgów – 1, Stare Miasto – 1), Pleszew (Chocz – 4, Gizałki – 7), Słupca (Lądek – 1, Zagórów – 8), Turek (Malanów – 2, Tuliszków – 2) and Września (Pyzdry – 13)[^8].

Below we present in outline only the beginnings of the history of these Olęder villages, from the founding of the oldest in 1746 and of the later ones up to 1793, when, as a result of the Second Partition of Poland, they came, like the whole of Greater Poland, under Prussian rule, in the province called South Prussia, in which Olęder settlement went on, in the Pyzdry Forest too, but in other political and partly also other economic conditions[^9].
