Under the *oblata* (official copy) of the text of the foundation charter the Olęders are named, in the number given above, settled in the years 1761-1778, partly the same as the first ones of 1761 but partly others, probably among other things because farms were sold and bought. This charter laid down *that from the beginning and the choosing of his huba every inhabitant is to take a card to remember his free years and to go to his lord, so that their first names and surnames be written down*[^50] (Ill. 6). From these two premises it can be inferred that the founding of the whole village took as long as 17 years. The inventory of the Zbiersk estate, which was then held by Leon Gąsiorowski, royal chamberlain (*szambelan*), drawn up in 1771 by the lessees, the married couple Jakub and Franciszka Przespolewski, names 6 Olęders in this settlement (the sołtys among them) and their dues, and 3 Olęders not yet paying them[^51]. The inventory of the Zbiersk estate drawn up in 1775 found, among other things, that there were still 14 farmers in the Zbierskie Ol.[^52].

Before or in 1767 Wielołęka was founded as an Olęder settlement in the district of Konin, in the parish of Grodziec, on the noble estate of Królików. In a protest lodged in 1767 with the Crown Tribunal at Bydgoszcz by Piotr Kurcewski, owner of the estate of Kuchary Borowe, against Józef Wężyk, sword-bearer and judge of the land of Wieluń, owner of the estates of Grójec (Grodziec) and Królikowo, there is already mention of the *deserta* [empty land] *Łęka, now called Wielołęka with Olędry*[^53]. This village was founded first by a collective foundation charter issued by Józef Wężyk, castellan of Konary and of Sieradz, given at the castle of Grodziec on 6 September 1770. A copy of this charter in German shows that the settlers had earlier received separate, individual foundation charters, from the issuing of which their seven free years, meant for clearing and setting up their farms on the deserted village overgrown with forest, were to be counted[^54].
