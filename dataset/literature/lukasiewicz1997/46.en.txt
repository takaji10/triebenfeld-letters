On learning of this Rüchel brought an action that he had been cheated and withdraws from the contract already concluded, of course without effect. Finally Triebenfeld in 1813 informed Hardenberg in person that estates were sold commonly at a price exceeding 3-, 4-fold the price of purchase. We have gathered 18 transactions of the sale of donated estates, and these are only the cases where it was possible to catch the prices of the donations[^78].

The only person among the donataries who lost on the sale of a donation was Colonel Hägen of the garrison of Głogów (in 1801 in the Wrocław garrison). Hägen bought Bogumiłów in the district of Sieradz for 15 000 thalers and sold it for 12 000 thalers. Here too however the affair is not clear, since the buyer was the minister Hoym himself, who ordered the carrying out of a valuation of the estate, and then bought it in the name of Baron von Stössel[^79].

The assessment of the value of the estates given away

Ultimately, with reference to the estates given away the most cautious assessment would say that their value was from 4 to 7 million thalers, which lies between the value that would follow from the tax estimates and the maximum value — given by Held. Nearer the truth however is the sum of 4 million. For while we are aware of the deflation of the value of income for tax purposes, this value is nevertheless comparable with the assessments of the noble estates and with other data[^80]. That the noble estates however did not in their mass fall so far to speculation was pointed out for South Prussia already by Holsche[^81], which Danuta Rzepniewska confirmed. If Holsche warned against the new valuations of estates, which give 2-, 3-fold their real value, this did not relate to that degree to the mortgage valuations of a few years before[^82]. The above assessments are also confirmed by the "Black Register" and the structure of indebtedness from Prussian credits. Admittedly the indebtedness of the noble estates reached over 33% of their value, but at the same time credit covered only 14% of the owners. Then of 600 estates covered by credit the owner changed in about 70, and that often twice. Finally, if 10% of the debtors were Germans, the height of the loans granted them was 20% of the average indebtedness of an estate by way of Prussian credits[^83].
