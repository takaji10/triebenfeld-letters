The opening of the mortgage, credits etc. indeed hastened its development, however, as Leopold Krug had already pointed out, in spite of the clear tendency of the rise in prices, they were in different areas of the Prussian state extraordinarily differentiated, and in a series of areas the price of a Magdeburg morgen did not exceed 15-20 thalers. At the same time, however, he wrote that the dominant tendency is growth, which caused the price of a morgen to exceed 100 thalers already and it goes on rising. In his assessment the real price of a morgen, that is, one following from the income it yields, should stand at about 50 thalers, thus half as low[^84].

The data of Held and Zerboni were cited in their day by serious Prussian statisticians, although incidentally and without comment. Leopold Krug wrote that Frederick William II gave away in South Prussia a considerable number of state estates, „denen Wert von manchen Schriftstellern zu 20 Millionen Thaler angegeben worden ist". Quite similarly caution was kept by Karl Holsche: „Einige wollen den Werth dieser Schenkungen auf 20 Millionen Thaler angeben, welches ich jedoch nicht verbürgen mag"[^85].

Both the work of Krug and that of Holsche met with letters of praise from Frederick William III, and this was not the only reaction of the young king to Hoym's machinations[^86], who in 1798 himself asked to be relieved of the post of minister of South Prussia. He was replaced by von Voss, removed after the insurrection of 1794[^87]. Holsche[^88] however doubted whether the dimensions of the donations could be so great, citing the fact that the „Canon", that is the height of the taxation of these estates, was for the department of Poznań only 15 816 thalers. Admittedly the archival list of the „Canon"[^89] exceeds 20 000 thalers, but even that does not change the state of things. The revenues from the domainal estates put together by Wilhelm Klewitz show that their whole value could not exceed 15 million thalers[^90]. All these coordinates strengthen us in the conviction that the value of the estates given away was nearer 4 than 7 million thalers.
