The officials opposed corruption with full self-sacrifice and did not count the consequences. Joseph Zerboni di Sposetti paid for it with imprisonment in a fortress, Hans von Held with removal from his post in Poznań and then a year and a half in the fortress at Kołobrzeg, Friedrich von Cölln with removal from his post in Poznań, the president of the Poznań Chamber von Garpe[^67] with removal to retirement in April 1797. The war and domains councillor Otto von Reinebeck, second director of the Kalisz Chamber, saved himself thanks to the intercession of his brother-in-law, the secret superior finance councillor Burghoff, wherefore in spite of the efforts of the Silesian minister, Frederick William II recommended only that "the young man be kept briefly". A ricochet struck Johann Kausch, who was imprisoned only on account of keeping up a correspondence with Zerboni, "appointed" by Hoym head of an anti-state conspiracy. It was to be a callow association of several officials from Głogów, of whom Hoym wrote[^68]: „Se. Majestät werden sogleich finden, daß eine schon seit mehreren Jahren existirende geheime Ordensverbindung in einen förmlich Revolutions-Club hat verwandelt werden soll..."[^69] In reality it was a fashionable pastime of the youth of the period in masonry, and it is obvious that for Hoym it was merely a pretext for tripping up the critics of the way the giveaway action was conducted. In consequence its stopping came only with the death of Frederick William II.

Speculation in estates

The technical side of the giveaway of estates was in the hands of councillor Peter August Triebenfeld[^70], Hoym's confidant and right hand, who prepared the deeds of donation. Of course, the first on the list of buyers was himself. In 1797 for 50 000 thalers he bought an estate, which he then sold in 1801 for 450 000 thalers to the Prince of Hessen-Darmstadt, as it turned out at a low price. The next owner sold the acquired estates still in the same year for 750 000 thalers to the Saxon master of the hunt Schürding. Triebenfeld himself described in a memorial published later by Wilhelm Dorow what course such operations took[^71]. Thus the buyer, after the purchase of the estates, sought an official judicial valuation of the estate.
