There took place however a quite spontaneous process of the estates given away passing either into Polish hands — without regard to political reasons — or into Prussian hands, often unconnected with the circle of the donataries[^91]. As the lists of borrowers from the years 1795-1806 show, there appeared also a group of officials who acquired estates in Poland for the economic reasons indicated earlier. Joseph Zerboni di Sposetti in the period of the rule of Frederick William III became, without a donation, a considerable landowner in the department of Kalisz, first taking a credit of 30 000 thalers on the estate of Pługawice in the Berlin Widows' Fund, and then in 1805 acquiring, for the enormous sum of 460 000 thalers, likewise coming from credit, Opatów and Wieruszów, where he really did farm and invest[^92].

Among the later ministers of South Prussia the sympathies of the Polish nobility were enjoyed rather by Karl Georg Hoym than by Voss. The latter held that for the economic elevation of the province it was necessary to carry out quick reforms, which the nobility feared. Hoym on the other hand kept up good contacts with Feliks Łubieński, Bishop Ignacy Krasicki, Józef Wybicki. To Łubieński he proposed in the framework of the giveaway the estate at Koski, which the latter, as he boasted in his memoirs, did not accept, but arranged its purchase for 40 000 by the hitherto leaseholder Szymanowski. This was to serve the keeping of the estate in Polish hands. Szymanowski however straightway sold it for 90 000[^93]. Łubieński mentions only in his memoirs that his son Piotr was married to the daughter of Michał Szymanowski[^94].

Łubieński mentions further that the donatary — General Favrat — was a figurehead, in whose name the estate was received by one Count Lubiński appearing in the German sources, director in Kalisz of the College of Landrats and landrat of the district of Kalisz. That enigmatic Count Lubiński is the Count Feliks Łubieński distorted in German spelling, civil-military commissioner with the Kościuszko army in 1794, brought before the court by the Prussians and sentenced to a fine of 400 thalers[^95], after 1806 minister of justice of the Duchy of Warsaw and one of the most eminent of its organisers, the educator of the sons of those merited in the work of building the Congress Kingdom. As Tadeusz Mencel gives in his biography, thanks to the intercession of Giorolamo Lucchesini he also received the starosty of Nakło. Another figurehead was General Dolffs, behind whom stood in reality the son of Minister Goldbeck. Earlier too we mentioned the acquisitions of Minister Hoym, obtained in a similar way[^96].
