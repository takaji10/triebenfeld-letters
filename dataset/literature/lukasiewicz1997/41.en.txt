The failure of the giveaway of estates

Such a state of things was as far as possible from the assumptions of the whole operation. It had several aims: on the one hand the procuring of money for the stripped bare Prussian treasury[^49], on the other the economic elevation of the domains, which were in a bad state, by means of the private money of the Prussian nobility[^50].

No less essential was the introduction into Great Poland of the German national element. It made — as was often stressed — an outpost and support for the Prussian state among a Polish population hostile to Prussia. If the Prussian authorities complained of the low economic level of the seized lands, it must be remembered that they themselves laid hands to it in the mass trials of the insurgents after 1794. Confiscations of estates were admittedly a rarity, but the basic form of punishment — the money fine — was onerous. As many as 2000 persons were brought before the court. Janina Muszyńska gathered information on 567. As follows from the list she presents, the punishments were extraordinarily high — on average 200-1000 thalers[^51]. Already on 16 May 1794, in the edict on the ius incolatus[^52], a loophole was left which gave the king, in accord with the law, the possibility of granting landed estates to nobles from outside the province. As it says in the edict — "the concession of particular estates honoured by Us (that is, by the king — p. m.)"[^53]. In fact however Frederick William II waged to an equal degree a struggle against the unlawful buying-up of estates by Prussian speculators, encouraged by the favourable prices of estates in Poland — for in Prussia complaints were already being made in the eighties of speculation and the rise in the prices of estates. In connection with this there appeared on 12 VIII 1795 a "Publicandum" threatening confiscations against speculators who come to South Prussia and, contrary to the law on the ius incolatus, buy up landed estates, thereby winding up a speculative conjuncture, artificially driving up prices on the local market and so diminishing the purchasing power of the towns[^54].
