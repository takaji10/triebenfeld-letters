## a) The filial of Lissewo-Pyzdry.

The town of Pyzdry (Peisern), which arose in 1257, is a foundation of the Cistercian monastery of Ląd on the Warthe. In the 14th century (about 1370) it belonged to the six towns whose elected councils "formed the highest municipal court for the province of Great Poland, without appeal to Halle and Magdeburg"1). According to the "inspection" of the year 1564 there were here alone 182 tax-paying craftsmen and "33 butchers". In the years 1790/94 Peisern counted over 3000 inhabitants.

Evangelical life in Peisern and its environs is demonstrable only from the Prussian period. For the German population numerously represented here it was intended to organize a congregation, for which on June 6, 1806, 100 Reichsthaler were appointed. Since 1798 there existed at Peisern an evangelical burgher and garrison school in a rented house. The teacher received 60 thalers salary for the instruction of the garrison children from the Royal war treasury and 10 thalers school money from the burgher children. The school was visited by 34 pupils2). The Prussian authorities also concerned themselves with the erection of schools in the country. Thus on September 3, 1799 the intendant Kolbe "of the intendancy of Peysern at Ciążyń" made proposals concerning the erection of new rural schools at Ostrowite (Ostrowska Kolonia), Wola Łaszczowa, Młodojewo, Lissewo, Kąty, Ciążyń, Gozdowo and Rataj. "The timber for it — he wrote — can be granted free. The inhabitants would . . . find themselves very ready, not only to accomplish the hauling of the timber," but also "to procure from their own means the materials, such as stones, lime and straw, for the roofing."
