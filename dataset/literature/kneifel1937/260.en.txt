In the year 1809 the evangelical population of Peisern exerted itself for the taking into possession of the Roman Catholic hospital church (church of the Holy Ghost) there, in part burned down in 1807. For decades it held to this plan without, however, attaining its goal. On February 16, 1844 even the Roman Catholic town dwellers Josef Laskowski, Wincenty Basiński, Kazper Borysiewicz, Martin Powalski, Anton Jumrich, Karl Jumrich, Josef Jankowski, Jakob Nowiński, Anton Kruszyński, Wojciech Gieryn, Josef Maytler, Johann Lesinski (and some twenty others) turned to the governmental commission with the request to hand over to the Evangelicals of Peisern the church named for their use. The commission refused this, with the hint that the Evangelicals could acquire the church standing empty only by purchase. To this, however, the evangelical population here was not in a position. Therefore it also let fall the thought of taking possession of the Catholic church without payment, the more so as the Kalisch voivodeship commission required that "the evangelical congregation must first show the presence of sufficient funds for the maintenance of the pastor and of the church servants". For many years no evangelical services took place at Peisern, because there was no suitable room. The Evangelicals here went to Wreschen for divine services or "to a Hauländer prayer house 1.5 miles away" (Lissewo). At the request of the "elders of Peysern," Andrae and Farner, Pastor Samuel Gottlieb Schröck of Wreschen came again and again to Peisern, where he held divine services in a rented hall. A school also was established here. In 1814 the school room burned down, and the school thereupon came to an end. Only "the service we continue in the suburb and meet the costs from our own midst"3).

After the Congress of Vienna the region of Peisern fell to Russia, whereby the parish of Wreschen lost a number of evangelical colonies4). Nevertheless Pastor Schröck cared for the Evangelicals in and about Peisern until his death († November 15, 1818). After him Pastor Stegmann of Grodziec cared for the fellow believers here.
