The parsonage, one of the most beautiful in our territorial church, is a modern, spacious building, in which on the ground floor the church chancellery is housed, further on the first floor the pastor's apartment, and on the second the apartments for the cantor and the sexton.

Through the building of the home for the aged and of the parsonage Pastor Wende acquired enduring merit in the development of the Kalisch congregation. In the building of the parsonage there stood at his side in especial helpfulness the members of the church council: A. Fibiger, E. Fulde, R. Fibiger, A. Stenzel, E. Rathe and A. Knoch.

In the World War 1914/18 Kalisch, as then a border town, suffered severely. Already in the first days of August 1914 the German troops (the 155th regiment, Major Preusker) bombarded the town, which was in part sorely devastated. As the reason there was given the shooting of German soldiers by civilians. The partial destruction of the town also involved many evangelical congregation members in suffering. After the close of the war Kalisch slowly recovered, rising like a phoenix from the ruins to new bloom. Through the shifting of the border, however, it lost its former interstate economic significance.

In the year 1922 the church was renovated; in 1930 three bells were procured in place of those confiscated in the war). On April 12, 1922 the singing choir received a new statute, confirmed by the Lodz voivodeship office, on the basis of which it now bears the name "Kalischer Evangelischer Gesangverein". [^p62-13]: 13) In 1817 Benjamin Repphan founded a large cloth factory at Kalisch, which was dissolved in 1910. Its last owner Emil Repphan emigrated to Germany (Berlin). Emil Repphan endowed the city of Berlin by deed of gift of December 27/29, 1913 with 5 million marks for the establishment and maintenance of a forest school, the "Repphan'sche Waldschulstiftung der Stadt Berlin" [Repphan forest-school foundation of the city of Berlin]. The donation, burdened with a life annuity, was accepted by resolution of the municipal corporations of January 15, 1914. By supreme decree of April 27, 1914 the acceptance of the donation was approved. The founder Emil Repphan died on March 30, 1931, his widow Marie Repphan née Jouin on March 12, 1932. (Letter of the lord mayor of Berlin of September 4, 1936.) — To his home town of Kalisch Repphan likewise endowed larger sums of money, among other things also for school purposes. — In 1824 Adolf Fiedler erected a cloth factory at Opatówek, which was later bought from him by Nitsche.
