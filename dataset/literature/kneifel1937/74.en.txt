Since, however, no candidates presented themselves, it again entrusted the administration of Grodziec (until September 1899) to Pastor Rosenberg of Stawiszyn. For the vacant pastorate Pastor Arnold Hoffmann of Rożyszcze applied, whose candidacy the consistory, however, declined to confirm, because he did not belong to the body of pastors of the Warsaw consistorial district. Meanwhile Pastor Rosenberg removed to Konstantynow, and administrator of Grodziec became, from September 10, 1899, Pastor Karl Henkel of Konin. On October 29, 1900 the congregation again received a shepherd of souls of its own, Pastor Rudolf Buse.

Proceeding from the recognition that the work of the cantors is exceedingly important for the congregation, Pastor Buse instituted cantors' conferences, which enjoyed great popularity. From July 8, 1910 he labored also as superintendent of the Kalisch diocese. In the World War, arrested by the Cossacks in 1914, he was banished to Russia8). After severe suffering he departed this life on January 3, 1917 at Saratov, where on January 5 of that year he was buried by his predecessor in the Grodziec pastorate, Pastor August Loth.

The care of Grodziec was assumed by Pastor Sachs of Turek. In June 1915 Pastor Otto Krenz came here, who officiated until 1919. After Pastor Sachs had once more administered the congregation, the pastorate was assumed on May 1, 1922 by Pastor Adolf Gustav Ulbrich.

As the farm buildings had burned down, he built new ones in 1923. In the years 1924/25 he surrounded the church square with a beautiful brick fence, for which among other things 481.50 dollars flowed in from America from former congregation members. On the front side of the parsonage Pastor Ulbrich erected likewise, from voluntary gifts, a fence. In the year 1924 the house of prayer at Orlina-Wielka burned down. It was then rebuilt, enlarged and provided with a gallery. Scarcely was it erected when the school inspectorate of the gmina Rzgów sought to appropriate it. The congregation set itself against this. There came a protracted lawsuit, which was decided first in the supreme administrative court in favor of the evangelical parish.

[^p74-8]: 8) The author remembers still vividly that autumn day of the year 1914 on which Pastor Buse, with 3 or 4 other evangelical fellow-sufferers, sitting on a wagon, surrounded by about 15 Cossacks, drove through the author's home town of Władysławów. The wagon halted before the commune office at Russosice (in the immediate vicinity of Władysławów). Many of the curious hastened thither. "We have taken spies — said the Cossacks — and among them is also the pastor of Grodziec". The author, who was present there, approached the wagon to see the pastor and the fellow prisoners. Sad and downcast they sat there. —
