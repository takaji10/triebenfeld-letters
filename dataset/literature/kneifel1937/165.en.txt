Therefore on October 20, 1815 Pastor Röhl petitioned the Grand-ducal Posen governmental commission for a frontier passport to travel, in order that "I may visit my spiritual children in the present Poland safely and unhindered as before." His request was granted. In spite of the frontier the Evangelicals in and about Slesin were regarded as belonging to Witkowo. On March 26, 1816 the church elder Gottfried Kunkel reported to Pastor Röhl that at Slesin the evangelical congregational life had died out, because Walichnowski had taken from the Evangelicals the house in which they had held worship. This circumstance, he said, compelled them to transfer the place of assembly for the evangelical population of Slesin to Szyszyner Hol.5) In 1817, however, the school and prayer house at Szyszyner Hol. burned down, so that Walichnowski saw himself constrained to the declaration that he would assist the Evangelicals in the building of a house of God at Slesin. But this never came to pass. Until 1817 Pastor Röhl visited regularly every 6 weeks the colonies Butter-Hol., Szyszyner-Hol., Grabina and Józefowo, "until at last the frontier separated them from the parish"5). The "Slesin parish," which embraced a part of the present congregation of Konin and of the filial Maślaki, was accordingly only an attempt at a permanent founding of a congregation. The attempt miscarried.

In the course of the years, especially in the Prussian period, German-evangelical officials and craftsmen immigrated into Konin6). In 1820 there were settled here: Friedrich Öhlers, Samuel Fibich, Michael Lempke, Karl Liebe, Nikolaus Wunderlich, Johann Schönweiß, Christoph Höft, August Reder, Michael Friedrich, Christian Thimm, Gottlieb Engel, Johann Gesse, Martin Petschke, F. Orive, Friedrich Schicke. In the open country about Konin German settlement activity had begun already after 1750. In 1767 there arose on the lands of the estate of Sławsk, whose owner had been Leszczyc-Zielonawski, the settlement Węglewskie Holendry (Wenglewer Hol.). At the same time the Holländereien Sławsk, Kolno and Rumin were founded. About 1775 the colony Brzezińskie Holendry (Briesener Hol.) was laid out. Many others followed these village foundations7).
