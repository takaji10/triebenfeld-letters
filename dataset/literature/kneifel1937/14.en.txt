Likewise he suggested the idea of convening synods, of introducing a serviceable hymnal for all congregations, among other things. Great weight was laid by Sup. Modl also upon the conscientious preparation of sermons by the pastors of his diocese. In praise he highlights: ". . . Pastor Rüdiger (Prażuchy) has shown me the drafts of the sermons in a complete collection." On the other hand, however, he censures: "Yet must one not sigh when men like . . ., who has already been 41 years in office, are unable to point to any draft of any worked-out sermon from their whole conduct of office?" Characteristic of Sup. Modl's fidelity to office and industry was, among other things, the fact that of his journeys and visitations he kept "a travel journal . . . dutifully and with the utmost conscientiousness."

After Sup. von Modl's death, on May 24/June 6, 1861 Pastor Franz Johann Wladislaus Stockmann in Kalisch was elected superintendent of the Kalisch diocese and in 1865 appointed consistorial councilor. When he died on January 15, 1869, the pastorate chose from its midst, under the chairmanship of Gen.-Sup. Ludwig, on May 16, 1870, Pastor Karl Ludwig Teichmann of Turek as his successor. On the model of the other dioceses Superintendent Teichmann held pastoral synods, of which the diocesan synod that took place on July 16 and 17, 1879 at Bełchatów, in which 16 pastors took part, was at the same time joined with the 25-year jubilee of office of the pastors Haberkant-Kalisch and Fiedler-Bełchatow. The subject of the deliberations was: lectures by P. Biedermann-Tomaschow on occasional addresses and by P. Zander-Alexandrow on visits to the sick, further on the melody collection to be introduced, cemetery boards, the reordering of congregational boundaries, among other things[^p14-15] Sup. Teichmann (†1890) was likewise concerned to influence congregational life in a positively ecclesiastical sense. After him Pastor Ludwig Julius Daniel Müller-Petrikau became superintendent of the Kalisch diocese. "His administrative experience permitted him to express the right and clear opinion in many a dark and difficult matter. The late Gen.-Sup. Everth therefore distinguished him with great appreciation and often asked his counsel in confidence"[^p14-16] Sup.
