For himself and his descendants he reserved the right of patronage, especially in the filling of the pastorate, yet permitted the holding of trial sermons before the congregation, which "might represent to him its opinion of the qualifications of the candidate." As first pastor of the congregation of Grodziec the church patron and the representatives of the localities elected unanimously the candidate of theology David Bergemann of Fraustadt. At the same time they requested the Royal Consistory at Posen to perform his ordination in the shortest time and to entrust him with the administration of Grodziec3). In conclusion Count von Stadnicki and the representatives of the villages declared "they were minded to keep this contract forever and under no circumstances to depart from it." This act of foundation was confirmed on January 9, 1797 by the South Prussian government at Posen.

Hard were the beginnings. The building of the church, begun in 1797, was completed only in 1803. The house of God was not large, only 14 ells high, built in the manner of a "preußische Mauer" (Prussian wall), that is, of timber with bricks laid in between. The parsonage, a simple wooden building (erected 1800), consisted of three small living rooms and was thatched with straw. The cantor's house and all the farm buildings (of timber with straw roofs) Pastor Bergemann erected likewise in the year 1800. His activity in the congregation lasted 10 years. On August 11, 1807 he died unexpectedly "of bodily pains" at the age of 41 years. His earthly remains were buried on August 13 of that year before the altar in the old church by Pastor Friedrich Heinrich Wilhelm Ernst Jonathan Grimm of Stawiszyn4).

The congregation, now shepherdless, called as its shepherd of souls Pastor Johann Christian Gottlieb Rissmann, a native of Glogau, who exercised his office here until 1815. He then went to Ostrowo5). Into the period of his activity fall the grave events of the year 1812, which did not remain without influence upon the religious life of the little congregations.
