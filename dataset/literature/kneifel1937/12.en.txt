Through the decree of February 22, 1817, in place of the departmental consistories existing before, a "provisional evangelical consistory U. A. C. for the Kingdom of Poland" was created at Warsaw. Members of the consistory later included, among others: Pastor Karl Gottlieb Bartsch of Władysławów and Pastor Ernst Johann August von Modi of Kalisch.

In the year 1828 the Reformed Pastor Diehl was permitted to carry out his old favorite plan: by imperial ukase of February 14/26 of that year the Warsaw general consistory for both confessions was founded. It comprised 3 dioceses: the Warsaw, the Płock and the Kalisch. To the last were assigned from the Kalisch voivodeship the congregations: Kalisch, Konin, Żychlin (Reformed), Władysławów, Grodziec, Stawiszyn, Prażuchy, Sobiesęki, Pabianice, Petrikau, Zelów (Reformed), Wieluń and the projected parishes in Zduńska Wola and Kleszczów; from the Masovian voivodeship: Konstantynów, Alexandrow, Lodz, Zgierz, Dąbie, Ozorkow and the planned congregation at Tomaschow. In all there were thus 21 parishes. As superintendent of the Kalisch diocese Pastor Ernst Johann August von Modi of Kalisch was appointed, who held this office until his death (†1860).

The church law of February 8/20, 1849 dissolved the union between the Lutherans and the Reformed, in that it subordinated the Lutheran congregations in the Kingdom of Poland to the Warsaw evangelical-Augsburg consistory. To the three earlier dioceses there was added as the fourth that of Augustowo, whose parishes until then had been incorporated in the Płock diocese. Territorially the Kalisch diocese embraced the government of Kalisch, with the exception of the county of Łęczyca, and the government of Petrikau, without the counties of Lodz, Brzeziny, Rawa and Będzin.
