# Kneifel (1937), p. 248
# translation by Claude

## 17. Zagórów.

In the year 1145 the German Cistercian monks founded the monastery Ląd on the Warthe (Lond, Lande). The first 25 abbots were of German origin1). Only with the year 1553 did the German monastery pass into Polish direction. The first Polish abbot was Jan Wysocki. After his entry upon office the German monks fled with the records of the monastery of Ląd to the monastery Heinrichau in Silesia.

To the colonizing activity of the monastery there go back, among others, the foundations of the towns: Lądek (Landeck) 1269, Pyzdry (Peisern) 1257, Zagórów 1445. In order to connect the last-named town with the nearby monastery, a dam 4 km long was thrown up.

The immigration of German-evangelical colonists into the Zagórów region can be documented from the year 1746. At that time Paul von Kolno-Prusiński, owner of the estate of Trąbczyn, concluded with German peasants a hereditary lease contract, by virtue of which he left to them for clearing woodland on which they founded the village of Łazińsk. It is the oldest settlement in the commune and at present also the largest cantorate, with over 150 evangelical farmers. In Łazińsk itself there are 115 of them, besides 15 farmers of the Huta Trąbczyńska, 10 from Trąbczyn B, 5 from Trąbczyn C and about 12 farmers from Trąbczyn D. The present school at Łazińsk was built in 1838/39, the bricks being replaced by the so-called "Sumpferz" or "Wiesenerz" (bog ore or meadow ore), a building material of importance in this region.
