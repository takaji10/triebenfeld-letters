The new congregation embraced, besides Kalisch, Opatówek and Dęby as well, then Koźminek and for a time also Sieradz. In Opatówek Gottlieb Lucae filled the office of teacher in 1805.

After the creation of the province of "South Prussia" Kalisch became in 1798 the seat of the "South Prussian evangelical consistory," of which Consistorial Councilor Karl Friedrich Herzberg became a member and at the same time preacher at the Kalisch evangelical church. He died on November 10, 1812 "of consumption and lung disease at the age of 43 years . . ."[^p58-9] Pastor Sienell served the parish until his death, which took place on October 12, 1820[^p58-10] He reached an age of 45 years (died "of lung disease").

In the course of time there made itself felt within the congregation the desire for a fixed church order. At the wish of the Kalisch church council Deybel von Hammerau sent the "Warschauer Kirchliche Ökonomie-Ordnung" (Warsaw ecclesiastical economy ordinance) and announced on June 12, 1820 that the "ordinance," as experience had taught, was not without defects, wherefore in its use "only the best and most suitable was to be chosen." The congregational meeting that took place on September 20 of that year called, for the purpose of working out a church order, the following 12 committee members: Nieszkowski, Prof. Borodicz, Assessor Welke, merchant Elbel, treasury controller Rebsch, manufacturer Meyer, Weygert, Wölfel, Eiselin, Hoffmann, Rothert and Scheppe. From 1820 to 1824 the preliminary work lasted, which the church elder Assessor Gerhardt furthered with his "Proposals for the organization and direction of the accounting and treasury system," in which the principal labor was performed by Pastor Ernst Johann August Modl, appointed in 1821 as Sienell's successor. In 1824 the "Economy Ordinance" was drafted.
