# Kneifel (1937), p. 10
# translation by Claude

15 new schools were established in Kujawien. As for the schooling of the evangelical town children, matters were in part better, insofar as in several South Prussian towns garrisoned by troops — like Peisern, Kalisch, Petrikau and others — military and burgher schools were founded. With time the number of schools grew constantly, so that about 1865 in the Kalisch land it had risen to 42, in the Warthe lowland to 22 and in Kujawien even to 77 evangelical village schools and cantorates. Through the "provisional school ordinance" of February 7, 1797 for the evangelical schools of South Prussia, then through the school constitution of February 24, 1834, further through the decree of the Warsaw school curator of August 16/18, 1847 and the Supreme Edict of August 30/September 11, 1864, all these schools were given legal security. About 1850 (with individual schools already earlier!) there made itself felt on the part of the school authorities the endeavor to transform the cantorate schools in towns and larger villages into elementary schools. Thus in 1857 the Kalisch land (including the Warthe lowland and Kujawien) had approximately 34 evangelical elementary schools with 37 evangelical teachers out of a total of 39 evangelical elementary schools in the Kalisch school district.

The German settlements were at first typical islands of language and religion in the midst of an environment of another kind. As such they sought, in order to maintain their faith and hand it on to coming generations, to draw close to one another, and thereby marked themselves off religiously from their surroundings. This endeavor, supported by Roman Catholic lords of the manor out of economic considerations, found clear expression in the founding of congregations. In the period 1776–1799 the parishes came into being: Władysławów, Stawiszyn, Kalisch, Grodziec and Babiak; in the 19th century: the parish congregations Kowal-Chodecz, Dąbie, Prażuchy, Łask, Sobiesęki, Wieluń, Przedecz, Konin, Zduńska Wola, Turek, Sompolno, Zagórów, as well as the filials Maślaki, Józefów and Lissewo-Pyzdry; in the 20th century: the congregations Koło, Izbica, the filial Ludwikowo, Kozy and Sieradz.

All evangelical-Augsburg congregations that took shape after 1775 on the territory of the present Kalisch diocese were administratively attached to the church district of Bojanowo.
