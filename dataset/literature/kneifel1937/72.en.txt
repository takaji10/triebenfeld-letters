Your old superintendent, under whose eyes a part of you has grown up and who himself has reached the age of gray hairs, will do everything that your church may be completed and adorned with a tower." These comforting, fatherly words instilled into many new courage and new cheerfulness. In spite of all — the building stagnated. The roof of the church was covered only by half in makeshift fashion, the tower not built, the interior of the house of God not finished. Nevertheless the zeal of the church council did not flag. On May 22, 1852 it concluded a contract with the organ builder and cabinetmaker Karl Köhler of Kalisch, according to which the latter pledged himself to rebuild the organ and set it up in the new church; besides, he was to deliver pulpit, altar, baptismal font and altar rail. At the same time the church board ordered of him also benches, hymn boards and the like. All the work was executed in 1853.

Years passed, but the completion of the house of God was delayed ever more. The ministry of the interior decreed indeed on May 12, 1859 that Count Bieliński was obligated to complete the church building according to the original plan and cost estimate. Soon afterward Count Bieliński died († 1860), which was quite unfavorable for the further building. The widowed Countess Virginia von Bielińska, to whom the church council of Grodziec now turned again and again in the building matter, held out in 1865 the prospect of an assistance of 5000 zlotys, without, however, redeeming her promise. The Kalisch superintendency therefore saw no other way out than to call upon the help of the government. The latter, by its measures, moved the countess to make good the obligations assumed by the lordship. On September 22, 1866 the building of the church, which had lasted 20 years, was finished7).

On August 2, 1860, together with the old house of God (St. Peter's and Paul's church), the dilapidated cantor's building also was torn down. The plan and cost estimate for the erection of a parsonage and cantor's house the government confirmed on March 9, 1863. In the spring of the following year the building of the parsonage was begun by the architect Reich of Pleschen and completed in 1868. It cost 2971 rubles 33 kopecks, the consistory granting 750 rubles.
