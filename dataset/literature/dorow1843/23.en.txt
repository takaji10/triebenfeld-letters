The prince believed he had already several 100,000 thalers in his sack, when it was said that the court agent Heymann Ephraim had seized to himself all the moneys lying at the Widows' fund and paid 5 per cent for them; one advised him to turn only to this man. The prince did this without delay, causing the Heymann Ephraim to be asked to come to him. This Jew, however, had the noble effrontery to refuse the prince (who was in truth akin to the royal house) the money, on the ground that the money cost him much. The prince's heart was set on obtaining money. He therefore stooped to beg the Jew. The Jew, moved by the prince's humanity, at last granted the money for 6 years (it was 200,000 thalers) on the condition that His Highness must for once and all suffer 30,000 thalers

proxeneticum [brokerage] to be deducted from the 200,000 thalers at the very payment of the sum, for he must give a share both to Herr Minister Count von der Schulenburg and to all other gentlemen. If His Highness consented to this, the capital should stand at his service for 6 years at 5 per cent yearly — the prince, who found the interest low, closed the bargain. Thereupon Minister Schulenburg scolded the Jew for a stupid devil and forbade the money to be paid to the prince, because one must not let oneself be made a fool of by a man so nearly akin to the royal house.
