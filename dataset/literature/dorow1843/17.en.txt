These very books speak of those who, instead of estates, had ready money and traded with it; thus, for example, a certain Government, now Kammergericht Councillor Sch —, who had scraped together some 36,000 thalers, lent them to a certain B—i at Sulislawice, whence he will probably never receive anything back. For the honour-loving patriot and honest man it was more than outrageous to see, even at the so-called Johannis transactions, councillors themselves driving a trade in money on the market in their parade uniforms. On one side there hung, vampire-like, upon such a nobleman of Poland, marked out by these men and already half plundered, an official, and on the other side a Jew, to hold the prey fast, and these vultures never missed their aim.

Meanwhile — what is also to be remarked here — after scarcely 1½ years of Prussian government, when the Poles had discovered and felt the weakness of the Prussian officials and their greed, the insurrection broke out. Hastily a corps was armed on the part of Prussia and Russia against these insurgents, and they were reduced to silence; against which, however, all Poland was partitioned.
