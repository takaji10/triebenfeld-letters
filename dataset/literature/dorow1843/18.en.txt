The money-fine was indeed little fitted to turn the Poles to more loyal sentiments, and Hoym was anxious in what manner he should raise money, organise the province, and yet keep the Poles in good humour.

By Herr Schulz's classification and all the self-interested classification commissioners not the half was brought out that the Poles had formerly given in the fourth groschen.

An honest man (War Councillor v. Triebenfeld) helped Hoym out of this need. He called upon the Poles, a great part of whom still sat in Breslau, to forestall a new classification and to declare quickly how much they would add to the 10 per cent. The patriot represented to them that the Silesians gave 28 per cent and were happy with it. The Poles therefore declared voluntarily 14 per cent more, and so 24 per cent, and to maintain besides iron magazines, whereby all the troops could be fed, and without demanding for it a single groschen.

Count Hoym had wished only 5 per cent more, and was very much surprised when the honest man brought him the Poles' resolution; nay, he was so moved that for the present he would have nothing said of the tax in kind.
