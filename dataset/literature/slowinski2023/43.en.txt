A long letter of Bruno's to his brother Gustav, dated 2 August, has survived. It is not a reply to the letters written in July by father, brother and sisters. They evidently crossed in the post. If they reached him at all… For in what way would they have found their way back to Łazińsk? Returned – because there was no longer any unit at the old field post address – or did Bruno himself bring them back in his baggage? But what he writes is to some extent an answer to the family's worries and anxieties connected with the soldierly service of brother and son. He writes, namely, that on the 22nd of that month they are to set out for the front. He asks them to hold off sending parcels until they give the front number of the field post. He does not complain about the supplies. "The food is also good. Every day there is meat or something of the kind of meat. There is also more bread (750 g a day). On Sunday there is even 1/3 of a bar of chocolate and 1/5 l. of schnapps. At the front itself the food will not be worse but even better." The soldier young in service ("until the third year of the war I could have remained in the schoolroom") believes that he joined the Wehrmacht at the end – when the culmination of the fighting is already coming. He fears – of course apart from the fighting itself and its possible consequences – the coming winter. "The old front-line soldiers tell that in the north the harshest was the past winter. Then they had to freeze, because there was no time for preparations. For this winter, however, the preparations are already being made in summer – if those men are still alive, then I hope they will not freeze. So it will probably be with us too, although by that time they may yet be transferred a number of times." Presumably the memories of the front-line soldiers who survived the fighting in the mighty frosts remained firmly fixed in Bruno Mielke's memory. "My hope and my petition is to survive this one winter, and if God grants the grace of victory again, then my hope is not empty. In any case it is beautiful that one of us takes part in this, and for me it is an obvious duty, even if I, with my 31 years, do not quite understand it! Would you believe that I already feel it?"
