Enlivened, I pictured your house at Łazińsk then." On the margin he adds that it is a good thing to know Russian; interpreters with the rank of officer have a much easier life…







He also reassures the family that they are well supplied. Recently they have received a temporary, borrowed postal code. "You may […] therefore also write to me. Since the code is borrowed, please do not forget to draw a circle (the size of 1 Pfennig) under the sender, without this the letter will not reach me. Address: Soldier Bruno Mielke, postal code: 39 801. Sender: Gustav Mielke. Łazińsk, post office Dromin (Trabczyn) via Konin Wartheland (Germany), and then the circle. Parcels can be sent too, unfortunately only up to 100 g. That is not much. Only what keeps a long time and is light counts"[^11]. Perhaps it is also worth quoting a rather peculiar request: "bake and dry small biscuits (little cakes) from flour, which you can dry on a pan or on a baking tray, perhaps with fat. They are sometimes also added to clear soup. I would like to dip them in tea or coffee as well, and that will taste wonderful." He writes that they should try to bake exactly such little cakes as he is sending as a pattern. "We received an allocated portion of these biscuits, of which I took 40 g to eat. Since this is forbidden, I afterwards got into unpleasantness, and I would like you to send me some of them."

After the hard experiences of the Winter War of 1939/1940 with the Red Army and the loss of ten per cent of its territory – in October 1940 Finland concludes with the Third Reich an agreement on military and economic cooperation. From that time on the Germans transfer their units to the territories of Lapland. In May 1941 Finland officially becomes an ally of the Third Reich. On 25 June it declares war on the USSR. 


[^11]: The postal number denoted Feld-Kommandantur 578 or Ortskommandantur 578, that is, a territorial command not assigned to a specific unit.
