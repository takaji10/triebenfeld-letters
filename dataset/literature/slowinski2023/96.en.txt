# Słowiński (2023), p. 96
# translation by the editor's files

 To the left of the cantoral school, on a gently rising hillock, there lay a richly wooded cemetery, on which our forefathers founded the first grave in the year 1727. The most ancient place of rest, whose weathered and rotted gravestones of wood and iron existed still until the year 1945. The colonists built their own school quite early, and at the same time a house of prayer, and before long Łazińsk, numbering 150 inhabitants, became the largest cantorate in the later commune of Zagórów (Hinterberg). The cantor in Łazińsk not only taught, but also held the Sunday services. Until 1933, instruction and services took place in the old building. Under the direction of the then teacher and cantor [Gustav] Mielke, a new building was erected, which served as both school and house of prayer. The Polish authorities allowed him to carry on his activity for four years, until he was transferred to Galicia because of his overzealousness in cultivating Germanness.

As a result of the German attack in September 1939 and the German Wehrmacht's victory over the Polish army, the Polish state ceased to exist for the second time after twenty years of existence. Among the consequences of this were, among other things, displacements, which, however, did not directly affect Łazińsk. The courageous attitude of the Polish and German inhabitants prevented the plundering of German farms by Poles as well as the expulsion of Polish farmers[^29] from their holdings. At that time more pupils attended the school at Łazińsk than ever before. Teacher Mielke, too, taught again at this school from 1940. Besides him, his sister taught there, as well as two young teachers from Thuringia. The school was now attended by 153 children.
