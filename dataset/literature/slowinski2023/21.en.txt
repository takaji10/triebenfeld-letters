# Słowiński (2023), p. 21
# translation by the editor's files

 registration plates began to appear at Łazińsk and on the nearby Kopczyk, and their passengers tried to document the former homesteads. Not all the locals liked these visits. Some even called the police, so that it would identify the newcomers and question them as to what purpose their walks around the surrounding farmsteads served.

Bogdan Karaszewski decided with his wife that the flat allocated to them required a considerable renovation. Possessing numerous skills that come in handy in such work, in 1995 he set about the job. The scope of these works must have been serious, since even the interior doors qualified for replacement. Removing a door together with its door frame – in such a way that the ceiling does not fall on one's head – requires a certain experience. The operation succeeded, and the extracted, rotted-through door frame revealed an unexpected sight. It turned out that this partition between rooms, which everyone had considered a single, monolithic wall, had been built of two walls, between which there is a considerable space. When the dust had settled, there appeared before Bogdan Karaszewski a sizable pile of papers sealed up tightly. Someone had secured them in an extraordinarily careful manner and hidden them cleverly. The first package extracted contained many letters bearing the postmarks of the Wehrmacht field post as well as a Book of Baptisms (Księga chrztów). Further down lay wrapped-up coverings of church altars and carpentry tools. When the find had been taken out, from the narrow crevice, into the empty space, books began to slide down from above… This extraordinary collection of books "descending" downwards was opened by a biography of Joseph Goebbels. In all, thirty-one volumes came into Karaszewski's hands. Apart from three gnawed at and damaged, probably by mice, the majority of the books survived in good condition, with the mark of time visible on the covers or on some of the pages.
