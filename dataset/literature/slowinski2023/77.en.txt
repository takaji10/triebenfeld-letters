Many German writers worked on producing entire series of books intended for Wehrmacht soldiers. In the book found at Łazińsk, *Deutsche Haltung vor Fremden* (ed. 1943), on the last page there is a note – a kind of recommendation: "*Zuerst erschienen als Tornisterschrift des Oberkammandos der Wehrmacht*" [First published (approved) by the High Command of the Wehrmacht as a publication for soldiers' knapsacks]. These were the so-called Feldpost-Bücher. "Anthologies were published containing a fascist-nationalist mixture intended to provide entertainment, some form of spiritual sustenance, and above all ideological, National Socialist support"[^18]. On orders from above, no doubt, the "sponsors" of such editions for their compatriots were regions or cities. An example may be the one mentioned by Hubert Orłowski in *Literatura w III Rzeszy* (Literature in the Third Reich) – *Der Posener Türmer. Ein Gruß an die Kameraden im Felde* (The Watchman of the Poznań Tower. Greetings to Comrades in the Field), which on the order of the Mayor of Poznań the publisher NS-Gauverlag Wartheland brought out for Christmas and New Year 1943/1944. What could one read in these works? Well, the individual is nothing! He is worth as much as he submits himself to the "voice of the blood", to the "holy fatherland", to a sense of duty or to the order of the Leader. "Since the Leader sacrifices himself for all of us and for Germany, it is likewise necessary to sacrifice oneself for Germany and blindly devote oneself to the Leader".
