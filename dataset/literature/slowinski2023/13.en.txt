When we browse the pages of the internet records, we in fact have insight only into the times of the Second World War. The scholarly literature is exceptionally modest. I therefore thought that we would never come closer to sketching a fuller picture of the history of the town and of this land if we did not try to learn something more about those who lived here over two hundred years ago and whose traces time is erasing. Despite the whole tragic period of the years 1939–1945, one must remember that this is only a part of the history of this land and of many of its inhabitants, which one should attempt to tell. More persons shared this thinking of mine. In September 2020 an editorial committee came into being and work began on the preparation of a publication about the Evangelicals of the Zagórów land.

In the course of gathering materials for the book there came into my hands an extraordinary collection of letters and books discovered in the wall of the house of the cantor Gustav Mielke at Łazińsk. With the support of a numerous group of translators of German, with a leading share by Jacek Kin, it proved possible to decipher these hand-written documents. This is chiefly the correspondence of Bruno Mielke, a Wehrmacht soldier fighting in the northern sector of the Eastern Front (*Eismeerfront*), addressed to his brother Gustav, to his father and to his sisters. After the reading of the letters a study came into being which will be placed in the book being prepared for print[^4]. I decided nevertheless that the preserved collection of letters and the contents it holds should interest historians and become the subject of a somewhat broader and deeper analysis.
