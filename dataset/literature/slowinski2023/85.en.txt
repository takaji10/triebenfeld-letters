Almost always, when the younger son mentions his father or asks that greetings be passed on to him, he does not fail to mention the errors his father committed and commits. There is a slightly patronizing note in these remarks. Now, when Gustav has returned to the Church, Bruno has one wish – that this renewed partaking of the Lord's Supper "may let your soul blossom anew and strengthen your spiritual hunger even more." Today this depressing worry, which tormented him because of Gustav's conduct, begins to accompany him on account of his sister Anni, who, it is said, has not been seen in church for a long time. An important question is posed, one that says a great deal about Bruno's spiritual formation. "Is this not a sign of her dubious inner state? Is this not the first sad fruit of 'Germania' with the spirit and 'religion' taken over from Hans?" A few words about what so troubles the writer.

In the 1930s, Nazism became a kind of political religion. Most of German society, unfortunately, believed in the dogmas proclaimed with extraordinary effectiveness in Goebbels's propaganda. It was a sinister mixture of myth and antihumanism. After the First World War, the crisis expressed itself not only in the economic and social sphere, as evidenced by the erupting revolutions and uprisings. The crisis and a marked weakening of influence also affected Christianity. In its place, the idea of nationalism was entering with growing force, taking in Germany the form of völkisch ideology, from the word *Volk* – nation. The primacy of "race," "society," "nation" over the rights of the individual held sway. The strongest race – the Aryan race – had the right of precedence, even one wrested by force. In this mixture of Nazi, chauvinist moods and various forms of occultism, there also ran a strong criticism of Christianity.
