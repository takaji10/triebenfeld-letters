 on Sunday. "On 27 September we thought in Łazińsk of a prayer of supplication before God, and I recited to the congregation the Lord's verse, as a greeting from the distant front. Everyone was moved by how a man remains brave in the danger of death, because he has entrusted his life to God. Many thought of their own family members, who are also serving their heavy duty somewhere along that long and distant front. We pray every Sunday for our members of the armed forces, even if we do not name each and every one of them. Our dear brothers can be certain that the community of faith and love for the good Lord bind us to one another, even if thousands of kilometres divide us."

The letters dated in December carry the added line: Eastern Front. "Who of us soldiers from the wastelands of Russia would not want to be with his nearest and dearest at Christmas!" "You must surely think – he writes on 6 December – that I will be spending the holidays in sadness and despondency. It is God's will, and my superiors sent me to the front. Resisting this in any form would be resisting the will of God and the authorities." Bruno regards himself as a messenger "for my family in the east, and for her and for my people I want to offer up this small sacrifice and spend Christmas at the front". He honestly admits that he has landed in a good spot – it is a fairly quiet sector. The Russians have so far attacked very rarely – hardly at all. His service consists of standing 6–8 hours of sentry duty (in pairs), bringing food and firewood, and sometimes other things. Apart from that he sleeps, and if there is good light, he writes letters and reads the delayed newspapers. That is why he asks his brother not to submit an application for his release. "First of all, it would do no good; secondly, at the front it is better in winter. In the rear there is training, drills and so on. There is none of that here. It is dangerous, true – but it is calm, and I firmly believe that nothing will happen to me."
