Worrying whether he would manage with the demanding studies, he describes his preparation as follows: "I have an education as a teacher (also in music), but unfortunately I do not know any foreign language (apart from Polish, which I have mastered well orally and in writing). But it seems one cannot do without Greek and Latin […] As for ability, I am admittedly not a genius, however – as the former director wrote on my certificate – I am diligent, because I, despite the lack of prior preparation (the director's assessment), with God's help did not have to repeat any subject. I am already 29 years old, I am still a bachelor, but I have a youthful appearance and disposition (this is how I am often assessed)".

But the day of June 22, 1941, came. Hitler opens a new front. He attacks his recent "ally" – the USSR. A letter dated June 26, 1941, to Bruno's sister Flora from the Lehmann family of Dresden has survived. They thank her for the package with sausage and other food items. They report at the same time that the nearest meeting of the Women's League "wants to thank our dear führer for the fact that he attacked at the right time, because it is enough to think about what would have happened if it had been otherwise. Right at the beginning the Russian hordes would have flooded over you, and in the past week we thought of Łazińsk very often". That the Russians might appear in Dresden was ruled out from the start.

No one yet knows what terrible consequences Hitler's decision will bring. Bruno Mielke begins to realize that this war may freeze his plans and dreams for a long time. In a letter dated July 8, 1941, he informs his sisters Kadzia and Flora that he is cramming Latin "for Vienna." He holds to his intention, even if in August or later he goes into the Wehrmacht.
