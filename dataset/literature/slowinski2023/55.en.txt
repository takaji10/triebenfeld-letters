# Słowiński (2023), p. 55
# translation by the editor's files

> ***Make my steps firm on your paths***  
> (Ps 17:5)

The first decade of January 1943 brings letters from sister Anna to her brother at the front (7 January 1943) and a letter from Bruno (10 January 1943) to Anna, which, however, is not a reply. They must have crossed in the post. Anna thanks him for the birthday chocolate sent from the front, which will be the main ornament of the table. Together with her sisters she has prepared a parcel, into which they put socks knitted between Christmas and the New Year; she made a head protector, Kadzia wrist protectors, and Fela knee pads. Anna's fiancé Hans added flash bulbs for the camera. And everyone is delighted that he will be able to take photographs, and cannot wait to see them.

The brother's letter has a completely different character and weight. Everyone is living in anticipation of Anna's planned wedding. Bruno hopes that it will not stop at this one wedding. Perhaps Gustav, perhaps Kadzia too will follow in her footsteps this year. He himself also greatly longs for the moment when he will be able to introduce his sister-in-law to them. He sends a complete set of photographs with the request that Anna paste them into his album. Yet beyond the ritual assurances of remembering her in prayer and the birthday wishes, Bruno believes that in the first days of this year, so very important for his sister, he must share with her the gnawing anxiety he feels about… his sister's spiritual safety. He reports that her fiancé Hans, in his first letter to him, writes clearly and openly that "he rejects the teaching from Scripture and wants to protect you from such things".
