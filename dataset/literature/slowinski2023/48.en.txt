# Słowiński (2023), p. 48
# translation by the editor's files

 of thanks for the fulfilment of his earlier request and ("I hope I am not giving away a secret") for the sending of an ointment against lice, which everyone here has. "On Saturday I washed myself, rubbed the ointment all over, and put on fresh underwear. Now no louse bites me, so the ointment works." The question is, for how long. On the order of the Wehrmacht, a preparation called T-14 was produced – a repellent powder against lice, fleas and mites, etc. Each soldier was issued two such packs[^12].


This letter also contains a description of the prescribed daily order, or rather the day-and-night order. "We now have 6 hours of sentry duty within 24 hours – that is still bearable. Yes, here, dear life functions quite differently, and so do mealtimes. We eat dinner at 6 p.m., supper at 3 a.m., and breakfast at 1 p.m. Why so? Because at night we must be more on the alert and more watchful than during the day. But when day breaks, there is nothing to fear, and then our sleeping time begins." And the most important, joyful news. After barely eight months of service, as of 1 December 1942 he was promoted to Senior Private. Captain Lebech, together with Lieutenant Hensler, made the personal effort to travel to his outpost, and in view of the enemy read out the order of promotion for exemplary service and a deep sense of duty.



[^12]: Preparation T-14 Ungeziefermittel gegen Läuse, Haarlinge, Flähe, Rausemilben usw. A powder produced for the army [with DDT?] in Munich, a repellent "against lice, fleas and mites etc.". They were probably attacked mainly by pubic lice. If Bruno Mielke mentions an ointment, he must have received from home an additional product available on the "civilian" market. I thank Mr Tadeusz Karpiński – the organizer of the DDD Museum – for making the illustration available and for his valuable explanations. As an aside, it is worth adding that DDT was also used on a mass scale by the Allied armies as a protection for soldiers against the *Salmonella typhi* bacteria carried by lice, which cause infection and the deadly dangerous typhus (www.muzeumddd.pl).
