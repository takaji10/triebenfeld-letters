(In Pieczenga, the former Finnish Petsamo, where Private Bruno Mielke was stationed and fought, there is a cemetery where "Unseren 12 000 Toten Kameraden" lie)[^20].

It is not difficult to find in the soldiers' letters quoted here those Nazi canons, stated outright and repeated many times. At times, however, one can get the impression that this is done mechanically, without any reflection, even a hidden one. Mielke volunteers for a medic's course. He does so, as he writes, for two reasons: such skills may prove useful for a lifetime, and secondly, he hopes that in this field promotion will come more easily to him. Latin will come in handy here, which he continually studies in his free moments with a view to studies in Vienna. He is also fully aware that "the medical service is not easy and no less dangerous in time of battle, since it must be at the front and dress the wounds – but it is all the more beautiful for that. […] If the war were to last until next summer, I shall be able to prove myself in action, but I am not afraid. I have a very strong faith". For now – in comparison with last winter – this winter, should nothing extraordinary happen, will be lighter. Of course, if the Russians push too far forward, then all those stationed in the rear will be called up to help.



[^19]: Ibid., p. 196.

[^20]: Nowadays, in the territory of the Russian Pieczenga [formerly Finnish Petsamo], where the XIX Mountain Corps, which included Bruno Mielke's unit, waged its battles, there are many monuments. Above all, there is a new cemetery (Friedhof, Kładiszcze) built in the years 2000/2001 (in the place of dozens of rows of wooden crosses) for the 12,000 mountain riflemen, infantry soldiers, airmen and sailors who fell in the fighting. **UNSEREN 12 000 TOTEN KAMERADEN**. On 23 stone slabs are engraved the known names of all who perished there. **The bilingual inscription on one of the plaques states that the cemetery-monument came into being thanks to the efforts of the veterans from the Alpine countries, who thank and are grateful to the Russian veterans for their help. A separate plaque commemorates EISMEERFRONT 1941-1944** [the fighting on the Arctic Ocean front]. On a nearby hill the Russians erected a dominant monument dedicated to their soldiers who fought in defence of the Fatherland on the subpolar, Arctic lands.
