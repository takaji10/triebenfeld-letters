The collection of books that an effort was made to preserve can be helpful in describing the family that lived in the cantor's house. The very titles say a great deal. *Biograpfie Joseph Goebbels* by Wilfrid Bade, Lübeck 1938, *Die Nationalversammlung von Potsdam. Deutschlands große Tage. 21 bis 23 März 1933* by Hans Wendt, Berlin 1933 [The National Assembly in Potsdam. Germany's Great Days], *Deutsche Geschichte im 19. Jahrhundert* by Heinrich von Treitschke with an introduction by Alfred Rosenberg, Safari – Berlin 1937 [History of Germany in the 19th Century], *Der deutsche Aubruch 9.11.1918 bis 18.3.1938* by Walther Gehl [The German Awakening], *Deutsche Haltung vor Fremden* by Bruno Behm, Graz 1943 [The German Attitude Toward Foreigners].

A few words about these titles and their authors. *Deutschlands große Tage* is a little book describing the assembly convened after the elections, which took place on March 21, 1933, in the garrison church in Potsdam, during which Adolf Hitler was entrusted with forming a government. Earlier, on March 15, the victorious leader of the NSDAP announced the founding of the Third Reich and stripped his political opponents of their parliamentary mandates. Walther Gehl (1897–1942), one of the authors of books militant in pen and weapon, simultaneously works "on the spiritual development of the Wehrmacht." He is the author, among others, of *Der nazionalsozialistische Staat* (1933) and of many history textbooks for secondary school students. He died in June 1942 on the eastern front. Bruno Behm (1892–1974), in turn, is an Austrian writer of very rich biography who came from the far right. He made a name for himself with the statement that "If Jews complain about their fate to the whole world, they must be told that they brought this fate upon themselves." Yet in genre terms, the heaviest volume is the book *Deutsche Geschichte im 19. Jahrhundert* by Heinrich von Treitschke (1834–1896), the author of the well-known 1879 essay that provoked a broad anti-Semitic response.
