# Słowiński (2023), p. 49
# translation by the editor's files

To the soldiers present at the post, the captain said that they should be proud to have such a comrade. "He would be pleased if we had only such soldiers." Curiously, before reading out the order, the commander asked Mielke to which class of nationality he belonged. The answer came that he belonged to the Volksliste. After a moment's hesitation and reflection, the captain read out the promotion order. In that very same letter the Senior Private was already drifting into daydreams about his chances of further promotions… "This I want to include in my prayer: that God may grant victory to our dear fatherland, and thereby peace to Europe and bread; that God may grant my dear siblings a happy, blessed marriage; that God may give me the strength to fulfil my soldier's duty with blessing and with honour."

In the hiding place there were two letters written in December to Bruno. They probably reached him after the New Year – in those days a letter took a minimum of fifteen days to reach the Eastern Front. On 12 December Anna Steinbart, who had replaced Mielke in his work at the school, writes from Konary. "Dear comrade in work in the grey uniform. You cannot imagine how happy the children were that their teacher had written to them. At once they took fire with the desire to reply to you." In the following sentences we read a personal confession that working with small children gives her much pleasure, but that the profession of teacher and educator is difficult and responsible. She feels that what she is doing is not going well, and without inner satisfaction she cannot go on doing it. She also lacks contact with the local people. "So I probably shall not be your successor for very much longer." In reply to the request for a photograph, she writes that she does not like being photographed and can only send one picture, and a somewhat blurred one at that. On the card there is an annotation by Bruno that he replied on 31 January 1943.
