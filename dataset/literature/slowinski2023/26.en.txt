If someone who is preparing for flight cares about his little library, it must have meant something to this family! Why did this hiding place come into being at all?! Was it out of fear that the collection of front letters of the son and brother would fall into the hands of soldiers, thereby giving a pretext for a harsh reckoning with the family? Was the Goebbels biography put into the hiding place for the same reason, along with other books that the household members considered important, because they constituted their national-socialist ideological identity? Were the remaining volumes hidden out of fear that the Russians or Poles would burn them – just as the works condemned by that same Goebbels were burned? In one of the memoirs we read that the farmers being resettled in April were told that this was a temporary operation. The question is, who was saying this: representatives of the new administration or local Nazi leaders, who believed in the führer's wonder weapon? If documents important to the family are left in a home being forcibly abandoned, then presumably somewhere at the bottom of the soul a little flame of hope burns that perhaps this is only a painful moment, that soon we will return to the family nest and to the land that sustained us for decades. But – and this question also arises – does protecting the writings of Rosenberg, Goebbels, or other authors like them not show that the terrible defeat which the resettlers and all of Germany are experiencing has taught them nothing? Are they unable, or do they not want, to abandon the idea of "their" great Third Reich?
