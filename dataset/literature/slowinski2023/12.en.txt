In the letters written in the language of persons well-read not only in the books of the Bible one can find positions of the authors that are well and clearly articulated. Fluent use of the pen was for these inhabitants of Łazińsk an everyday occupation, done with zeal or sometimes even with passion.

Among the numerous threads and information touched upon in the correspondence of the war years, one topic does not exist. In Zagórów, from September to 15 November 1941, the liquidation of the ghetto was carried out. The deported Jews were brutally murdered in the forests near Kazimierz Biskupi. One may get the impression as if the thousand Jews, neighbours for two hundred years, had never been here at all.

Successive generations of Poles, Jews and Germans created that community which, regardless of periodic tensions and collapses, slowly opened up to "strangers" and increasingly often was able to communicate, to build together – the best proof of which was the survival and the wise management of the Zagórów community in the times of the severe crisis of the 1930s. The cataclysm of the Second World War brutally destroyed that world. The war, its fatal consequences and the inexorably flowing time caused this old, yet close-to-us history to be slowly obscured by a "cloud of oblivion".

In the years past there have already appeared many documents, including books[^3], on the history of the inhabitants of the Zagórów land – Poles and Jews. Apart from a few small studies, very little is known about the community of the German settlers, the Evangelicals, and about their history.
