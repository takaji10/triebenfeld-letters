After they had been disposed of, it is visible that they made up for the personnel shortfalls in the companies caused by the losses suffered. Bruno Mielke is assigned as an operator to serve the heavy machine gun and gets as his helper private Atze. This is a weapon of the direct field of combat.

There has been preserved the only description of a battle in the whole collection of letters. "From 12 o'clock at night (from Friday to Saturday) our heavy weapons were made ready (artillery, grenade launchers, anti-aircraft guns). It is unbelievable how everything booms, rolls, seethes and bubbles, and when the shells whistle across to the other side, carrying death and destruction. Again – as always – there were many fallen and wounded on both sides (among the Russians many more). Were all of them ready for death? I think about it in spirit and this is always the most important question for me. One more thing is astonishing, which I heard today: Russian prisoners testified that the Russians in those same days were planning an attack on our positions, only we forestalled them. And yet one can feel such wonderful care and love of God."

One thing is certain – Mielke was in that hell. On Remembrance Day, the first day of spring, 21 March, he writes that the lack of leave is wearing him down, as is the uncertainty whether the information passed on that he would get it will be confirmed. He wants to be with his family for the Holidays and hopes that his brother will hold the wedding which everyone is awaiting. The day is wonderful, sunny and relatively calm. The Russians have not yet answered their last attack of 15 March. But probably they no longer can. Probably it is as Hermann Göring foretold, that the enemy is fighting with his last reserves. And we, "through the totalisation of the war, can field one more army. That is why we are all firmly convinced that the Lord will send us victory this summer and will make the poor people on both sides attain peace."
