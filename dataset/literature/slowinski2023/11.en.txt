The problem of national identity, the attempts to define oneself in relation to the new state, were not easy topics. Among the documents discovered in the wall of the cantor's house at Łazińsk there is a letter from Gustav Tonn, a teacher from Płatkownica, to Bruno Mielke, which also reveals the deeper reflection that accompanied some of the German settlers over their fate in the Polish state. These few sentences form a synthetic and vivid illustration of the problems with which they struggled. From the letter it can be inferred that Bruno was rejected by the education authorities, or else was refused the right to teach. Thus Tonn writes to him: "And yet, dear Bruno! Do not be too candid; get to know the man before you undertake anything. Also do not make too much of a show of your Germanness. In order to obtain a piece of bread and keep yourself, one must keep much to oneself. You need not disown anything, but neither should you profess anything. And if Poland is to be our homeland – and it is – then we must also be patriotically disposed towards Poland. In the times of Russian rule I worked for Russia, and now I do it even more willingly for Poland. [Be therefore prudent as serpents, and harmless as doves]. But only do not give up; talk with the inspector and perhaps it will be better. The Lord will assist and help to turn this injustice away from you. […] I, when I came from Russia, was also slandered as a great friend of the Germans, but God sent me a faithful soul who supported me, and everything turned out for the best.
