> ***You give me the shield of your salvation***  
> (Ps 18:36)

The short letter that Bruno writes on Sunday 20 December 1942 (it reaches Łazińsk on 5 January) announces the sending of a longer description of how he spent Christmas, and contains only one account: "During the hours of the service, from 9 to 11, I stood on duty at my post, which is the more dangerous one (today we had to make a change). The main post consists of an oblong stone bunker. That is where I really ought to be. But 'by mistake' I went to the side bunker. While I was just there, giving myself to prayer, a shell from the Russian artillery struck the main post, broke a hole in the wall, and threw many stones inside. What would have happened to me if I had been there just then?" And he concludes: "Let God be praised and glorified."

Mielke sits down to the account of the first day of the holidays just before midnight, right after coming off the post where, between 9 and 11 p.m., he had stood with his good comrade Schwartz. He honestly admits that he had feared these holidays and an onslaught of longing. But it was not so bad. In the forenoon the captain and the divisional chaplain came on a visit. They even walked up to his post, and the chaplain was so kind that he asked after his family and gave him a bar of chocolate, wishing him a Happy New Year. It was not so bad, either, because "the joyful strength is given me above all by my faith in the living God, who is with me […]".
