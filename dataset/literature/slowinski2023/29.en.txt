In a letter to the dean of the Evangelical-Theological Faculty of the University of Vienna he writes: "Dear Mr. Dean! The time of the autumn semester will soon come. I would gladly begin my studies this autumn (for my intention of coming to Vienna has become a certainty). But unfortunately the Wehrmacht and the war will most likely call me away. For the time being I have not yet been called up, but I recently received a summons to be on standby, so that I expect the order to report any day now. As soon as I am released from military service after the end of the war, I will want – if God preserves me in life and health – to lay down my office and come to Vienna. If, according to God's will, I were to fall in the war, then my brother will inform you of it". This letter, dated October 10, 1941, was written by Bruno Mielke in Konary, in the Grodziec commune, where he worked as a teacher. Earlier, from the year 1940, a document has survived signed by him as a teacher and lower school commissioner in Maślaki, in the Wilczyn commune. It is a letter to the Konin starosta documenting the need to build new school buildings in Maślaki and Lipnica. The inhabitants – I quote: "led by the village headman, consider it obvious to build a new school worthy of the New Germany and are ready to help financially".

It may be worth mentioning here that both Maślaki and Konary were, from the end of the 18th century, localities that came into being as a result of agreements made by the local Polish manor owners with German settlers of the Protestant faith. Over more than a hundred years these localities became important regional centers of the Evangelical community – of mainly German origin, but also of Polish followers of Luther (the German element predominated).
