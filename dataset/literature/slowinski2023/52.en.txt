Did he sing the old thirteenth-century carol *Adeste fideles* (Let all the faithful sing, praising their God), or perhaps "Children, children, hurry all together.//To the manger in the stable at this festive time"? It is an extraordinary image when we try to picture a German soldier, standing on Russian ground stained with blood, at an forward outpost, humming *Silent Night*. He saw in his mind's eye the family going to church, his father at prayer; he thanks his sisters for the presents sent in the parcels, the tasty cake, the warm leather gloves. Now he will no longer freeze – even if it were 50 degrees below, as happens on the central sector of the front. "The temperature here is such that there was even a thaw the day before, which the warm Gulf Stream ocean current brought from the north." Although the command looks after them, and even the food at Christmas is better, still – they are at the front!

They had to be especially watchful, because the Russians had attacked the previous Christmas. They had expected an attack yesterday as well, but they did not come. "But today, on the first day of the holidays, from 9 to 11 they carried out an attack with artillery and mortars – quite devilishly. Thank God there were no losses or damage. In the afternoon, in reply, our men fired on the Russian bunkers – that is how it is at the front. The day before yesterday, shortly before midnight, blood flowed again – one of the comrades was hit in the face. Among our soldiers behind our walls and bunkers, only head shots occur, and they are fatal." This is the cool account of a soldier under fire, who writes that here danger exists at all times.
