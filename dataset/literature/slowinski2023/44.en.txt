# Słowiński (2023), p. 44
# translation by the editor's files

It is worth pausing for a moment over this last sentence. How is it to be understood? As the pride of a soldier who is told that he is fighting for a homeland free of Jews and Russian hordes? Or perhaps in this "feeling" there flows from somewhere in the subconscious the question of what, in truth, he is taking part in? He does not write about the war operations – for it is obvious that the censorship would never let such a letter through. But he reveals another enemy – nature, which in his account is more dangerous than the opponent. Or perhaps he indicates that this opponent has an extraordinarily dangerous ally? The question will always arise when reading letters from the front. To what extent should they be understood literally, and to what extent should one read what is not there or what has been "said between the lines"? In any case it is evident that Private Mielke has not yet come to know the true war machine that grinds human flesh, and with growing apprehension he awaits that encounter.

This letter is – to use the language of war – loaded with emotions. Above all he writes about his great dream, of which he thinks unceasingly. "…if I return happily and in good health, I will prepare myself for the most beautiful of professions, in order to exercise it with the greatest blessing […] My heart in any case is full of longing for a life dedicated to God – and as a pastor I know how to do that best." His thoughts also run to his future wife, who must hold views such as his. That is the first condition, about which he will ask her first of all. Does she belong to the Saviour and is she pious? Married life, as he imagines it, must be "the holiness of the Lord and blessed by the nearness of God and the Spirit".

He gives the same advice to his brother, who in a letter asks his opinion about Truthilde. "Observe her and converse with her thoroughly, how far she is sanctified by Christ." Brother Gustav is 43 years old (he had been cantor, teacher and headmaster at Łazińsk since 1925) and, as one can gather from other letters, the search for a candidate for wife has been going on with varying success for quite some time.
