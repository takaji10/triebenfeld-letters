# Słowiński (2023), p. 30
# translation by the editor's files

 Alexander Gross, the parish priest of the Evangelical-Augsburg parish in Zagórów, and worked as a teacher and cantor in nearby Anielewo. The last months of 1941 were a time of growing expectation of being called up into the army and departing for the front. In one of the cards we find this entry: "I would like to get all my visits done as much as possible right now, because from August I must be on my guard: at that time I may be called up to the Wehrmacht at any moment, for only until 31.7.41 am I recognized as 'indispensable'. In fact I am glad that in this great time I will take part in the fight. I had already wanted to volunteer".

Bruno tries to put some of his affairs in order and writes an application for leave so that he can travel to Konin and visit Łazińsk. In the letter of October 10 he shares with his brother Gustav the information he obtained during some course at an NSDAP school in Będlewo near Stęszew. Namely, an official subordinate to the Reich Governor laid out at length before the course participants visions of allocating land to rural schools, in this way encouraging teachers to settle in the countryside. Bruno Mielke considers that "it is obvious that in the Warthegau proper schools must be built and that the land must not be spared after the war with Russia". And here follows a longer disquisition on the advantages of such a solution in opposition to taking over complete farms, which will be subject to considerably stricter regulations and production obligations (the future school land will, in this respect, have more freedoms). Foreign (read: Polish) farmhands cause the farmers particularly many troubles. Hence he urgently urges his brother to take a prompt interest in the possibility of obtaining such a grant – preferably close to Zagórów or Konin. He writes that Polish owners were there, after all. "I hope that not everything has yet been divided up". In the whole of it – one of the longest letters – there is not a crumb of reflection that he is urging his brother to take part in the plunder of land and houses from those who for years had been their neighbors.
