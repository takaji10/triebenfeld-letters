# Słowiński (2023), p. 72
# translation by the editor's files

 Fascist Grand Council carries out a surprising coup, depriving Benito Mussolini of power. A few days later – on 3 September Italy (the government of Marshal Badoglio) capitulates! That same day Allied troops land in southern Italy. The arrested Duce is freed by German commandos on Hitler's orders. And this event awakens the admiration of Bruno Mielke. "I am particularly pleased by our Führer's loyalty. This matter in Italy will at least become clear, and further steps will certainly be taken soon". Under the Germans' umbrella, Mussolini creates in northern Italy the puppet Republic of Salò. This is another letter in which Mielke allows himself commentary on broader events concerning the war and politics.

But that is not all. From the advice he gives his brother, we can infer that Gustav, sitting in Łazińsk, notices a change in the behavior of the Poles, who in various ways give the occupiers signals that they know more than what Goebbels's organs write and proclaim. "One can get angry that the Poles are becoming brazen; nevertheless, I would like to ask you to confine yourself to quiet observing and reporting, provocative intrusions breed hatred, and hatred is capable of many things. So that the saboteurs disappear, let the gendarmerie take care of it; it is called to this. The Poles hope in vain for political freedom. The Germans are in form!". As a birthday present he sends his brother a drinking glass made from the casing of a round fired on the eastern front in Russia. Let it be a small memento from the front and may it always accompany you on your travels.

Perhaps this "raising of heads" of the Poles resulted not only from news from the front, but also from the attitude of the older of the brothers toward their former neighbors.
