From the newspapers he learns that larger combat operations on the Murmansk front can be expected only in the middle of June. He assumes that they will not go directly to the front line, but will certainly take the place of those who go to the first line. He thinks that the time of an important decision "on the matter of Russia" is approaching. The next passage, worth recording in full, is a kind of testament which he sends to his family.






Tactical insignia of the 2. Gebirgs-Division.


"Dear Brother, there is one thing I would like to suggest to you, as man to man: let us not deceive ourselves and let us look the truth in the eye. It is clear (for me and for you) that a stage now lies ahead of me that is more or less threatening to life. (That the English have attacked German ships and will continue to do so is probably certain). Our transports are of course well protected. So if anything should happen to me, according to God's will, I ask you to deal with my property in freedom as follows: 1. That morga of meadow near Zagórów should belong to Kadzia; deal with it in such a way that it is as good as possible for Kadzia. 2. The money gathered in the savings bank in my savings account is to be divided as follows: 30% for Kadzia; 20% for Anni; 25% for the church, the home and foreign missions; 25% for you, at your free disposal. You will have the ways and means. Whatever of the clothes and things you can use, take for yourself; the rest Kadzia may alter for Father. My 'large furniture' should still serve Father. Dearest Brother, I am not writing the above in gloomy forebodings nor out of deep, dark despair and resignation; on the contrary, I feel freer when I know that everything is in order, and therefore I can be at peace."

He writes the letter on the way to the front "on the march" on 10 June 1942. "By God's grace we have arrived happily in Finland. The journey by ship went without problems.
