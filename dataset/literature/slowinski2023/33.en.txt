# Słowiński (2023), p. 33
# translation by the editor's files

Gustav wrote a long letter to his training brother on May 1. "During all my work one thought now wearies me – it appears since you are far away. Such a will to work as I had when you were still here I no longer have. During lessons you appear every so often in my thoughts. And when the break comes and when I look through the window – then too I see you. You were always nearby, and now you are suddenly so far away – to this is added the worry of where you will be in a few weeks. […] Apart from you I have no one. I have no friends at all; our girls need support themselves, and father… you know after all – he sleeps and eats, and reads all the time. But he is doing well. Without you I am rather lonely and would best like to leave here as well – to where you are and where you expose yourself to dangers". If only he got leave, he would set off to Klagenfurt immediately.

At present he is feverishly occupied with putting affairs in order. A few days earlier they (the cohorts from 1899 to 1895) received a summons to register for the duty of defense. "So we were at the Municipal Office on April 28 and registered. I, Mundt, Grunwald Heinrich, Schmechel, Stolp, and many others. This means that we too may be called up to the army in the coming days". In the closing sentences he writes that he does not understand those people who are "happy" when they try to take away our faith. "I am a Christian and in recent days I have been made to feel it again and again". He could not bear it if some young one tried to arouse disgust or harass them – and misfortune would happen. "I know – you are able to digest something like that. But one cannot tolerate it for too long". (This fragment is precisely the openly signaled contemptuous, even aggressive attitude of adherents of the Nazi ideology toward Evangelicals, Christians faithful to the Church in general).
