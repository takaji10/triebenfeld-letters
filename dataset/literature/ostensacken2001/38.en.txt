# ostensacken2001  38  en
# Kraus (2001), p. 38
# translation by Claude

It is natural that Prince Friedrich Ludwig zu Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen as a loving and solicitous father worried about the future of his children. The debts heaped up additionally in the course of the years therefore weighed on him urgently. A possibility of securing his family's livelihood in the long term he believed he could accomplish with the purchase of the Rittergut Bytkow in the Upper Silesian district of Beuthen, which he acquired on 24 April 1801 for a consideration of 43,000 Reichsthaler. To this Rittergut belonged a zinc smeltery in the wood of Bytkow and two hard-coal pits at Ignazhütte. After the purchase the complex was renamed "Hohenlohe-Hütte".[^p38-22]

With the engagement of the engineer John Baildon, who came from Carron near Edinburgh, the prince had drawn to himself a first-class adviser. The director of the Silesian mining office, Friedrich Wilhelm Graf von Reden, had been made aware of Baildon on an instruction journey in 1793 and had first taken him into the Prussian state service. With his technical knowledge Graf Reden succeeded in laying the foundations for an enormous industrial upswing in Upper Silesia. Still in 1790 the annual cut of wood had amounted to 200,000 Festmeter. The region embraced, by estimation, nearly 28 million cubic metres, but by the usual charring the wood was badly used, and experts complained of the increasing ravaging of the woods.[^p38-23] In the nearer surroundings of Beuthen mighty seams of hard coal had been discovered, and the task had fallen to Baildon to erect at Gleiwitz the first coke blast furnace on the mainland.[^p38-24]
