# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 168
# translation by the editor's files

we may conjecture, and what Bruno Mielke for a long time did not say openly. Despite the many times repeated declarations of fidelity to the Führer and of subordination to the powers above, there stands over them a supreme authority to which this soldier gave himself entirely. It is God.

The description of everyday life at the front is written on 27 January. The end of the month brings hope of a winter growing milder. Surely the worst has already passed, and in any case, in comparison with other sectors of the front, things were milder with them – for temperatures reached at most minus 20-25 degrees. The worst in all this were the incredibly strong snowstorms, unknown in the Wartheland. He rejoices at the coming time of the withdrawal of their company a few kilometres to the rear. Surely there too there will be no "bread roll with butter", but at least they will be able to sleep more quietly, wash themselves well and get rid of the lice. After six weeks a return to the front line. "Perhaps then I shall have the luck that my leave begins, and when I return from leave, that is after your wedding, then it will already be warm and bright; then the great offensives will begin and the war – so may the grace of God ordain – will end. And how is your wedding? Have you already decided to go to Erika?".

It is hard to establish when the soldier Bruno received the letter written by his brother Gustav on 28 January 1943 (probably around the middle of February). The most important news is the information about the state of Bruno's savings recorded in his savings book (*Sparkassenbuch*). The deposits were regular and the interest was added. At the end of 1942 he has at his disposal the sum of 4871 marks. Gustav also reports that he was summoned to the Education Office in Konin. "There I was handed an act confirming the status of an official. I also had at once to take an oath. So I am an official for life – a teacher of the Führer". From Poznań the sister Anni brought the developed photographs of Bruno, which he had sent from Lapland to the family. Everyone admires how well he looks in uniform. And in the closing lines of the letter Gustav Adolf, sitting in the kantorate surrounded by forest, reports coolly and as if in passing: "Time and again news comes that some men from Łazińsk or from the neighbourhood have fallen on the Eastern Front. The fighting is after all not easy. Not a few of them perish".
