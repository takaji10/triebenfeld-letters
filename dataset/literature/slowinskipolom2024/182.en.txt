 learns in his spare moments with his thoughts on studies in Vienna. He is also fully aware that "medical service is not easy and no less dangerous during the fighting, for it must be in the forefront and dress the wounded – but it is all the more beautiful. […] If the war were to last until next summer, I shall be able to prove myself in action, but I am not afraid. I have a very strong faith". For the present – in comparison with the past winter – this winter, should nothing extraordinary happen, will be lighter.
Naturally, if the Russians do not push too hard forward, then all those stationed in the rear will be called up to help.

So for the time being he is doing well. Sleeping, clothing and food are in order. He is positively astonished "how they transport all this so far away into this wild region". And one more astonishing request. Someone stole from his medical bag 25 safety pins. He asks – if they can – to send them to him. He will put them back in, and in this way will not have to report the theft.

It is worth noting the dense correspondence which Bruno Mielke conducts. And it is not only his "privilege". The OKW – the High Command of the Wehrmacht – attached great weight to the smooth functioning of the field post. It was held that frequent contact of the soldiers with home and with their loved ones raises morale in the army, and also positively influences the mood in the families, that is, in the rear of the front. No matter where he was stationed on the far Eastern Front, in Finland or in Norway, the letters circulated smoothly and as a rule reached the addressee. From their reading one may infer that there were no special restrictions as to the number of cards or letters sent. Weight and formats, however, were strictly observed. Post reaching the front arrived at the battalion, where it was distributed to the companies, for those units were best informed about the addressee's whereabouts.
