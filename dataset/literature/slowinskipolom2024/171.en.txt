What happened at Stalingrad was a great shock for the Germans, a source of sharp tensions and discussions in the political and military leadership of the Third Reich. For not everyone agreed with the Führer, who called this spectacular defeat (of which the whole world was already speaking) a "heroic spectacle of German history". Generals Karl Strecker and Helmuth Groscurth did not end their report on the capitulation with the sacramental *Heil Hitler*, but with the traditional cry: "Long live Germany"... The news from the east also brought a spark of hope to the subjugated nations, an echo of which we shall soon see in the letters of the following months. But at the beginning of February it was not yet known how great was the significance of the lost battles on the Volga and the Don. The southern front, too, had not achieved its intended objectives (the seizure of the oil sources) and Army Group A had to withdraw from the Caucasus. Yet all the signs in heaven and on earth said that something important had happened.

In this sense Bruno's matrimonial exertions, his wedding plans, may be regarded as a kind of defensive reaction, a flight from the black everyday, a peculiar repression of what was reaching him. For Bruno Mielke was no fool. Two weeks later he sends a letter which is already considerably more open. "For a week now we have been here in reserve and are leading the barracks life of soldiers. Today is the first Sunday – to my and everyone's joy without duty. One can truly meditate spiritually and religiously. Spiritual is my thinking and my writing of letters to you. In the religious sense I am, as before, still lonely and in the desert. When the hours of holding the service come, I am always sorely tempted.
