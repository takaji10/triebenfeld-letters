Those who depart soonest are its witnesses: politicians, creators, participants in events great and in those of every day, victors and vanquished – living people. What they accomplished, built or wrote remains far longer, but even that implacable time consumes and buries. There remains the memory of the succeeding generations and the sorrow that those who have departed we can ask about nothing any more.

In the course of the last years much has been achieved, so that this vanishing world should not be obscured by the "cloud of oblivion". There already exists a considerable collection of documents concerning the past history of the inhabitants of the Zagórów land. In the year 2007 there appeared the book which I entitled *Zagórów. Album miasta 1407-2007*, accompanying the celebrations of the 600th anniversary of the granting of town rights. It is a panoramic picture of the town and its citizens across the centuries. Five years later there appeared the book *Dwa życia jak dwa psalmy. Historia mówiona Leona i Toshy Jedwab*. Through the fate of a Jewish married couple we come to know the life of the local Jews and the drama of the Holocaust. It is worth mentioning the valuable output of the Zagórów historian Bogdan Grzonkowski, the collections of Andrzej Bernat, the publications of Jarosław Buziak. Katarzyna Połom's website: "Zagórów – miejsce z historią" (zagorowzhistoria.com) is an important address on the net, where successive texts appear about the documents that have been found, about people and events in former and recent times. It is also difficult to list all the exceedingly valuable initiatives popularizing "close" history, which are undertaken by the Municipal Centre of Culture, directed by Łukasz Parus, and by the Society of Friends of the Zagórów Land, with Grzegorz Cieślewicz at its head. The picture of the history of the town and of the Zagórów land is, however, still incomplete. It is extraordinarily difficult to reach the accounts and documents from the life of the Evangelicals of Zagórów and its surroundings, mostly Germans. The hard laws of the end of the war caused that after 1945 there remained here a handful of the formerly numerous, active and industrious community.
