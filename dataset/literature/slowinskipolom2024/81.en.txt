On 19 April 1922, the Ministry of Religious Denominations and Public Education officially established the Faculty of Evangelical Theology at the University of Warsaw. The highest number of students was recorded in the academic year 1929/1930 – there were as many as 102 students, among whom women also studied. Bishop Bursche faced criticism concerning the established faculty both from the German side and from the priest-professors of the Faculty of Catholic Theology of the University of Warsaw. The Evangelical Military Chaplaincy had already functioned before the emergence of independent Poland. The first chaplain nominated by the Polish authorities was the Rev. Grycz from Cieszyn Silesia. On 4 November 1918, the Rev. Ryszard Paszko – before the war the parish priest in Koło – addressed the Ministry of Military Affairs with a memorandum on the appointment of chaplains in the Polish Army. First the Section of Foreign Denominations was created, later the Department of Non-Catholic Denominations, and finally, in 1927, the Bureau of Non-Catholic Denominations of the Ministry of Military Affairs, covering five denominations. On 25 September 1919, the Rev. Ryszard Paszko became chief chaplain of the Evangelical Military Chaplaincy with the rank of lieutenant colonel. The structures of the chaplaincy were systematically expanded, though there were difficulties on the part of the Church, which did not want to release clergy from parish service for the military chaplaincy, and on the part of the state, which sought savings in filling the chaplaincy posts. Another difficulty was the distrust and fears of the military authorities toward the soldiers coming from German families – these were most often assigned to the units stationed in the eastern districts, most often in the Border Protection Corps. A process of conversion to evangelicalism was also noticeable among the soldiers and officers of the Polish Army, perhaps inspired by the earlier conversion to Lutheranism of Marshal Józef Piłsudski.
