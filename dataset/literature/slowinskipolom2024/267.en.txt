### Mrs Dolkowa


Damian Kruczkowski

The figure I would like to tell about appeared often in the recollections of my grandmother, Zdzisława Powała née Duszyńska, born in 1933 in Dolany. From childhood I liked listening, and Grandma did not need to be asked twice before she would begin to spin tales from the times of her childhood. Mrs Dolkowa, who was in fact Genowefa Dalke, née Zeigner, was my grandmother's teacher. She taught, among other things, German at the school in Lądek during the Second World War. Grandma remembered only the surname, moreover given in dialect form, a few situations, the general positive impression Mrs Dalke made on her, but she was unable to recall her first name. When the idea arose of gathering material for a book about the Protestants, I thought that Grandma's Mrs Dolkowa could be commemorated in it. But one had to find out a little more about her. And so one afternoon I wanted to share with Grandma the dark beer I happened to be drinking. I offered to pour her a little of it into a glass.

– Oh no, no, – Grandma replied, – but when we were kids and walked to the bar in Lądek for beer, the German, besides pouring into our little pails exactly as much as we had bought, would sometimes, when it was hot, add a small glass of dark beer for us. It was good. We always drank it, and on the way out we would say, each as best we could, *auf wiedersehen*. The German would laugh, because we didn't necessarily say it correctly.

– Mum, you should have said *danke* – my mother, Grandma's daughter, would prompt.
