Happily, the letter of 4 May 1944 has survived. It came by air. (The field post number 48 341 relates to the 67th Medical Company of the 2nd Mountain Division). Here we learn that Bruno Mielke was on leave in Łazińsk. He could therefore have left behind part of the letters which had reached him at the front.

He writes to his brother Gustav. A great joy for him is the information that at Easter his brother partook of the Lord's Supper[^66], that he prevailed upon himself to a strong fidelity to his God. That he did so after a long time, overcame the inhibition and opened himself to "the forgiving grace". He had long awaited such a letter and thanks for the trust and sincerity shown him. "It makes me happy also because I can well empathise with your situation and understand you and your needs". There was a time when his brother's attitude – the leaving of the Church – caused him great, oppressive worry. During the leave they talked about it with the father, who spoke of the elder son with concern and in "inexpressible sadness". "Papa, despite his many errors and weaknesses, is a dear soul! I am so grateful to my Lord that I still have him". Almost always, when the younger son mentions the father or asks that greetings be passed on to him, he does not fail to mention the errors which the father committed and commits. There is in these remarks a somewhat condescending note.
Now, when Gustav has returned to the Church, Bruno has one wish – that this renewed partaking of the Lord's Supper "may let your soul blossom anew and strengthen your spiritual hunger all the more". Today that oppressive worry, which tormented him because of Gustav's conduct, begins to accompany him 


[^66]: The Lord's Supper. In the tradition of the Evangelical Churches, the Eucharist – the Lord's Supper is, next to Holy Baptism, a Sacrament. In accordance with the words of its institution by Jesus Christ (Mt 26,26-28; Łk 22,19-20; 1Kor 11,23-26) and with the exposition of the Books of Concord, the Lord's Supper is the true body and true blood of Jesus Christ, truly present, and is administered to believers under the forms of bread and wine. That is why the faithful receive the Eucharist under both kinds: bread and wine.
