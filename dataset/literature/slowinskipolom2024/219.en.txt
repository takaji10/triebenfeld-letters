#### School

To get to school I went through the forest to Huta. I had about two kilometres to walk one way… I took the shortcut to school across that hill of sand…

There was once no division of Łazińsk into Łazińsk Pierwszy and Łazińsk Drugi. Łazińsk was a very large Hauländer village. I remember that hill of white sand. It was not overgrown as it is today. It could be seen from the windows of our house. We used to come here to play. I loved coming here… I took the shortcut to school across that hill of sand, to the school on Łazińsk, today called Łazińsk Pierwszy. On the way I passed the village of Huta. I went there for at most two years. I started when the war broke out, for in 1939 I had just turned seven. Every day I crossed the high hill on which a windmill stood. At that time the school was also the house of prayer.

Next to the school there was a cemetery. The second cemetery for the inhabitants of Łazińsk was closer to Huta, on the way to school. The school building had two classrooms, with two separate entrances to them. One entered from both sides. There was one more building there, a smaller one, the so-called old school, but I no longer went there. I remember that as a child I very much wanted to learn to play the violin; I kept dreaming about it, talking about it, but life did not allow it. I finished only five grades. When the war ended I was eleven, and very quickly I had to become an adult. In post-war Poland I could no longer study. As a Hauländer of German origin I had to work in order to eat and live… We, the Hauländers, were blamed collectively for the Second World War.

#### They resettled us to Łazy…

When the Germans resettled us to Łazy at the beginning of 1940, I started going to school in Michalinów Trąbczyński. I remember that the shortcut to school went across the yard of a farmer whose name was Schulz. I was always afraid of his dogs, but the shortcut was tempting.
