# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 44
# translation by the editor's files

After the fall of independence, the drama began. The descendants of the settlers were forced to sign the Volksliste[^18]. Some did so of their own will, others with open reluctance. The arguments that convinced people to sign the nationality list were the events described above and others like them, the ban on the renovation of buildings, and worse living conditions for those unwilling to sign the list. After signing the German nationality list, the men were conscripted into the German army – and yet some of them had only just taken off the Polish uniform…

The Polish population, mostly Catholic, was in a wretched position: forced labour, deportations. The Catholic church was closed, and people could not lead a normal religious life. In an even worse situation were the Jews, who were first placed in a ghetto and later transported to the extermination camps. The parish itself and the Protestants were not, as one might have supposed, in the best of positions. Hitler, like Stalin, permitted the existence of the Church as a necessary evil and a temporary instrument for ruling over people. Already during the hostilities, many cantorates went unstaffed, for the cantors had been called up to the army. The Hitlerite authorities restricted the possibilities of the Church's activity and kept the clergy under surveillance. Such acts as baptism or marriage performed in church were not recognised by the Hitlerite authorities. Records were drawn up no longer at the parsonage but at the state office. [^18]: *Deutsche Volksliste*, VDL – the Volksliste (German Nationality List). The so-called signing of the Volksliste involved filling out a detailed questionnaire containing questions about religious affiliation, since when the family had lived in the given area, what schools had been completed, about relatives in Germany, etc. The questionnaire was sent to the home. Once it had been collected, by decision of an official, the persons surveyed were assigned to one of the four groups of the Volksliste.
