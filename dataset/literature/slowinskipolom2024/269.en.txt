Some time ago, much earlier, I talked with Mrs Kuske from Lądek, who comes from Piotrowo. I deeply regret not having asked her about more details. At the time Mrs Kuske recalled that the teacher would sometimes give her some little notes, which she carried “to the boys in the forest”. Possibly some partisans or fugitives were stationed for a time in the vicinity of Lądek with whom Mrs Dalke was in contact. That would make sense in the light of the account of her son Jan Dalke, who recalled that after the war the militia, although it often settled accounts with people for wartime transgressions, never touched Mrs Dalke's family, indeed it protected it.

So I began searching for that very son whom Bronisława Kowalska had mentioned. In old telephone directories I even found a telephone number and an approximate address, but the months were passing and I had neither the occasion nor the courage to contact Mr Jan. Facebook came to the rescue. In various circumstances I made contact with Mr Jan's daughter-in-law, his daughter, his niece and his brother's daughter. Suddenly and unexpectedly Mrs Dolkowa's family grew. Already then I knew that the story of Genowefa Dalke would be told. I met with Mr Jan Dalke on 7 September 2022 in his flat in Słupca. Mr Jan readily spoke about his mother and her story.

Genowefa Zeigner came into the world on 11 October 1911 in Linden in Wolfenbüttel as the daughter of Julius and Berta née Petrich. Her parents had gone to Linden for work, which in those times was a common phenomenon. The marriage was contracted in 1906 in Konin. When Genowefa was half a year old, the Zeigners returned to the Konin land, to Nowy Czarków in the municipality of Kramsk. Besides Genowefa, the couple had two more daughters and a son: Berta, Helena and Otto.
