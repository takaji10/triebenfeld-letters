# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 256
# translation by the editor's files

Zagórów was not far away, a proverbial stone's throw. And in these regions too I found many forgotten cemeteries, houses built of bog iron, churches and chapels. And here, too, the descendants of the old settlers told of their complicated fates. Zagórów – a small, quiet town – charmed me with its beauty and history. There are many monuments here connected not only with the settlers. Exquisite is the Warta valley with its oxbow lakes, dunes, meadows and all manner of bird species. I watched how its image changed in the different seasons. It is a place one can fall in love with. Over the years of wandering I accumulated a great many tales and stories. I wanted to fix them in the form of a book that would at the same time be a lesson in history. I wanted to preserve the tangled, complicated fates of the settlers, especially in the wartime and post-war periods. But I am not a historian. I don't know whether I would be capable of writing a popular-science book. That is why I needed such “warm characters”, who would introduce the reader to the world of the German settlers, the Jews and the Poles. Characters who would be guides through that world. Hence there appear Edward, Zosia and Grandma Eryka – though invented, they have certain traits drawn from real life stories. Thanks to the real Edward and a few other people I made contact with the inhabitants of the nearby areas. Long, interesting conversations with them “helped build” my protagonists. In my novel I wanted to include various threads connected with the Germans, the Jews and the Poles living in these areas. I needed facts, authentic stories.

Very important to me was the meeting with Bogdan Grzonkowski, a historian from Zagórów, a man of passion, who for years researched the history of his little homeland. He let me make use of his rich archive. It was he who introduced me to the history of Leon Jedwab, the fate of the Zagórów Jews and the annihilation of that community in the Kazimierzowskie Forests. He also told local anecdotes. I made use of that in the pages of my book. We should remember that the history of the Hauländer settlers, in their great majority Germans, is a part of our history, an important chapter in the history of Poland.
