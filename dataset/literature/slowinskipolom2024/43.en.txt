#### IV. The Period of the Second World War (1939-1945)

After Hitler's coming to power, fascism and German nationalism sought their supporters and henchmen not only in Germany but also in Poland. As a result of such activities, the Evangelical Church became an arena of propaganda warfare, especially after 1936. The propaganda conducted from the centre in Łódź also reached the vicinity of Zagórów. Its task was to stir up anti-Polish moods – and this with regard both to Protestants and to Catholics. With sorrow one must state that there were those who fell for the bait of this propaganda. The hitherto good relations between the Polish population, which for centuries had lived alongside the settlers who had come from Germany and Silesia and alongside their descendants, began to sour. The international situation and the propaganda activities stirred up dislike, and even hatred. The slogan: “A Protestant is a German”, and the growing psychosis, meant that many Poles – Catholics – saw in the Protestants Germans, representatives of a hostile nation.

It was precisely this view – apart from methods of intimidation and coercion – that greatly facilitated the task of the occupation authorities in persuading the descendants of the settlers that, despite their Polish citizenship, they belonged to the German nation. Even before the outbreak of the war, this led to many lynchings and tragedies. Among others, in the neighbouring Pyzdry, the cantor Adam was unjustly suspected of espionage and anti-Polish activity; he was bestially beaten and drowned in the Warta. All millers of the Protestant faith were suspected of sending spy reports; they were visited by the police and the army, and some of them were also arrested[^16]. In Szymanowice, on the first Sunday of September, directly after mass, three Protestants were murdered – farmers who had supplied wagons to the Polish Army[^17]. These tragedies, arising from the psychosis fanned by the hysterical proclamations of the populists, called forth further ones – a tit for a tat. Meanwhile, many Protestant parishioners took part in the September Campaign, serving in the Polish Army.



[^16]: According to the oral account of Helena Winter.

[^17]: Wojciechowska Bogna, *Pastor z Wierzchów* in „Gazeta Poznańska" no. 209 of 7.09.1990, p. 9.
