### First Visit to My Parents' Homeland in 1981


Günter Hennings

For many years after the war, the Polish border was closed to Germans who wanted to see their homeland again. The breakthrough came with the change in the eastern policy of the Chancellor of the Federal Republic of Germany, Willy Brandt, and the signing of the Treaty of Warsaw[^79].

Now it was easier to travel to Poland as a German tourist and visit places that had until then been off limits. Over time my interest in visiting and getting to know the birthplace of my ancestors grew, especially as my parents often talked about the old days and thus stirred my curiosity. At Pentecost in 1981 my wife Charlotte and I – we were then teachers in Uelzen – travelled to Poland with my parents [Ella and Willi Schmechel – added by the translator]. Two decades have passed since then. Full of apprehension, we set out in our VW Polo early in the morning on the Saturday before Pentecost from Seelze towards Poznań. In those days the journey still went with difficulties. After an hour on the motorway heading east came the first stop. The onward journey led through the former GDR with all the attendant inconveniences which, at the time, though in a weaker form, also applied to us, Germans from the West.
