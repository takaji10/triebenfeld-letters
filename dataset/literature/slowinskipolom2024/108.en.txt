"The school in Wrąbczynkowskie Holendry until the war in 1939 was Polish-German – recalled Gustaw Kuske from Lądek in a conversation with Wiesława Kowalska, the author of the publication *Puszcza Pyzdrska. Podróż sentymentalna* – Up to the 4th grade the Polish and German children were taught separately. From the 4th to the 7th grade instruction was only in Polish, jointly for the Polish and German children." Every child of the settlers spoke both German and Polish fluently. The children were taught in two shifts (the older classes from 8 o'clock, and the younger ones from 11), and there were about 20 pupils in a class. The children attended religion classes separately[^47].

Looking through Frieda Banik's school report card for the school year 1933/1934, I learn that the children in the fourth division of the third cohort still attended classes: arithmetic with geometry, nature study, geography, history, drawing, handicrafts, singing and physical exercises. Frieda's report of 15 June 1934 was signed by the head of the division, Juliusz Bauer.

In 1935 the ailing Bauer was replaced by Olga Richterówna, a German teacher who had come from the Koło county. In beautiful Polish she keeps the entries in the school chronicle and prepares her pupils to take part in patriotic ceremonies: the successive anniversaries of the recovery of Poland's independence, on the occasion of the name day of the President, of Marshal Rydz-Śmigły, or of another anniversary of the death of Marshal Józef Piłsudski… As of 15 November 1937 the one-class German public school is renamed a public
