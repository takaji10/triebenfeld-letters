# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 290
# translation by the editor's files

The colonists built their own school, at the same time a house of prayer, quite early on, and before long Łazińsk, numbering 150 inhabitants, became the largest kantorate in the later gmina of Zagórów (Hinterberg).

The cantor in Łazińsk not only taught, but also celebrated the Sunday services. Until 1933 instruction and services took place in the old building. Under the direction of the teacher and cantor of the time, [Gustav] Mielke, a new building was erected which served as school and house of prayer. The Polish authorities allowed him to carry on his work for four years, until he was transferred to Galicia on account of his over-zealousness in cultivating Germandom.

As a result of the German attack in September 1939 and the victory of the German Wehrmacht over the Polish army, the Polish state ceased to exist for the second time after twenty years of existence. Among its consequences were, among other things, expulsions of population, which, however, did not touch Łazińsk directly. The courageous attitude of the Polish and German inhabitants prevented the plundering of the German farms by the Poles as well as the expulsion of the Polish farmers from their holdings. In that time more pupils attended the school in Łazińsk than ever before. Teacher Mielke, too, taught again at this school from the year 1940. Apart from him, his sister and two young teachers from Thuringia taught there. The school was now attended by 153 children. More Germans lived in the vicinity than before. They were Germans who had arrived from the Reich [Reichsdeutsche], as well as resettlers from Volhynia and from other areas.

At the end of the war, in the year 1945, the inhabitants of Łazińsk met the same fate as all Germans: the loss of their land, flight, expulsion, deportation, dispersal. It rarely happens that the former neighbors meet anywhere at all and share their memories.

In contrast to the former inhabitants of Łazińsk, the inhabitants of the neighboring Orlina maintain contact to this very day. Here it is Mrs. Marga Todt, a former teacher at the school in Orlina, who looks after keeping the bond alive. Every year at the beginning of May she organizes the "Orlina Meetings" in her home in Mühlheim an der Ruhr.
