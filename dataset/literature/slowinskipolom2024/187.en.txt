"Is this not a sign of her doubtful inner state? Is this not the first sad fruit of 'Germania' with the spirit and 'religion' taken over from Hans?" A few words about what so disquiets the writer.

In the thirties Nazism became a kind of political religion. The majority of German society, unfortunately, believed in these dogmas proclaimed with extraordinary effectiveness in the Goebbels propaganda. It was an ominous mixture of myth and anti-humanism. After the First World War the crisis expressed itself not only in the economic and social sphere, of which the erupting revolutions and revolts were the proof. The crisis and a clear weakening of influence also struck Christianity. Into its place there strode with growing force the idea of nationalism, which in Germany took the form of völkism, from the word *Volk* – nation. The primacy of "race", "society", "nation" over the rights of the individual held sway. The strongest race – the Aryan race – had the right to precedence, even one won by force. In this mixture of Nazi and chauvinist moods, of various forms of occultism, there also ran a strong criticism of Christianity. It was resolved that this religion stands in opposition to the national-socialist ideology, since "it was not a religion of the sword"[^67]. There is no place in it for heroes, for the "Übermensch", the overman. Christians are people guided by the principles of mercy, of love of neighbour, penitents... It is the religion of weak people, whose canon of laws cannot concern the true Germanic man. As one could read outright in the letter quoted above of one of the girls to Bruno Mielke, the Church acts against the Reich and the Führer. The realities of everyday life in the Nazi world forced Christians into behaviours that allowed survival. But is that all?
