#### “I gave them all the gold…”

##### Wanda Jurkiewicz, née Andrzejewska (1923-2022) remembers

When the war was on, I was fifteen. One day my father took my brother to the dentist, for his teeth hurt. The dentist was a Jew; he asked Father to wait or to come later, because Mayor Diesterheft and a gendarme were with him just then. When those two had gone, the dentist said to my father: “All the gold I had, I gave them, and they promised me they wouldn't deport me.” Then he had a little peace, but in the very end they deported him all the same. A vile man was that Diesterheft.

When the war ended, the mayor fled with his son on a motorcycle. In the forest the Russians caught up with them; Diesterheft said they should take him to his flat, to Zagórów, and he would reward them for letting him go. He gave them all that gold he had got from the Jew, but they took him to the forest and did to him what he had done to the others: they killed him, took the gold and drove off[^88].

#### Revenge also reached the mayor's wife and daughter

The inhabitants of Zagórów organized a “defilade” straight after the war, during which they made Mrs Diesterheft walk along ringing a little bell and telling everyone what she had been. It was a great humiliation. However, she and her daughter later managed to escape from Poland.

#### There were also noble Germans during the war

Some Germans, as soon as they knew something was afoot, would give warning so that the Poles could flee in time. And one of the Beutler brothers knew everything about Diesterheft, about what was going on there. When he learned that Poles were to be deported to a camp, and that Cieluba would be deported too, he rushed off at once to warn him to flee and hide.
