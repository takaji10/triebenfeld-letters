 1931 and 1932. In it the doctor opened a clinic, and in one of the previous houses, after prior reconstruction, he organised a small hospital.

In the doctor's surgery, splendidly equipped for those times, there was even the most modern X-ray apparatus! Breede was therefore praised as a specialist, people gladly turned to him for advice, and long after the war he was remembered as a kind and devoted physician who often helped the poorest free of charge. Good renown was paired with social prestige, which the Doctor's wife prized so highly. In the interwar period, like her husband, Janina Breede sat on the town council, among the most important personages of the Zagórów of that day. She took part willingly in social organisations, including those of a patriotic character. She was active in the ranks of the Polska Organizacja Wojskowa (the Polish Military Organisation), and was even among the members of the delegation taking part in Marshal Piłsudski's funeral. Her husband Hugo was chairman of the Union of Reservists of the Polish-Bolshevik War and a man engaged in the social life of Zagórów.

– Even in her maiden days Mrs Breede ran a Sokolic group[^73] – recalls Mrs Barbara Nowakowska, whose mother belonged to this organisation.

The Doctor's wife would travel away with the girls to camps in the vicinity of Warsaw and in the capital itself. Sports competitions, games and movement exercises, learning national songs – the cultivation of a love of sport and of the Homeland – all this was for the members of the group not only a patriotic duty but also an undoubted adventure. They were also impressed by their leader: an elegant, intelligent young lady from a well-to-do, educated family of patriotic views was no small authority for the girls. Janina continued her activity even after marriage.
