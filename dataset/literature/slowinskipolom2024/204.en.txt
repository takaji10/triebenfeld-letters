# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 204
# translation by the editor's files

 and what news there was. She was not, however, intrusive. Perhaps it was mere curiosity? I do remember, though, that once, at a session of the gromada council in Oleśnica, Mr Janicki came and said that Mrs Breede was a German spy! That she comes to the forest at twelve o'clock at night, that she has a radio station and is in contact with her son, the one in the army. I do not believe it – the Doctor's wife almost a Mata Hari – the grotesque association flits through my mind. Somewhat amused, I enquire whether that was long after the war and where the certainty came from in the first place that some radio station existed at all? I learn that this story broke in the 1960s, that the Russians located and confiscated a radio station at one of the farms in Imielno. At the same time it turned out that in Ląd one of the priests (who was not a priest at all, but only passed himself off as one – Mrs Halina explains to me) was also a German informant in the monastery. Both he and Breede were, according to my interlocutor, caught out. Interesting. Did the doctor's widow really "broadcast" for the needs of the German army, or did she simply, as a mother, want to make contact with her son? Why, after the radio station was confiscated, did she not bear the consequences of the alleged spying? Today it will be hard to verify.


– I remember Mrs Breede… Though by the time I saw her more often, there was no longer any real contact with her. After what she had been through, she was closed in on herself and distrustful. She lived in the attic of the house on Konińska Street. Though ill, she always tried to dress tastefully and neatly. Her faded and unfashionable clothes must once have been elegant and attested to the class of their owner. I remember she wore a little hat without a brim – old-fashioned, but very chic. On her hands she wore summer gloves… – Małgorzata Kasprzak tells me. Barbara Karczmarek, née Kasprzak, an inhabitant of Zagórów from birth, echoes her:

– Yes, I remember too. Poor, thin and stooped, once certainly a blonde. She would sort of slink along the walls as if she did not want to be noticed. My mama was always enchanted by her way of speaking; one could see at once that she was a highly educated woman. But when mama wanted to help her out, giving her new clothes – Breede would politely but firmly refuse.
