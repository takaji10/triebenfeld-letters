 his new domain, Poland was by that time already partitioned among three powers, but on these lands, around the settler villages, there still existed old indigenous Polish villages with which the settlers came into contact through neighbourly relations and commercial exchange, quickly learning — for practical reasons — the language spoken by the majority of the local population, while at the same time preserving their own, German, at least in the first periods of settlement. With time it looked very different: some confined themselves to reading the Bible in the language of their old homeland and to inscriptions on gravestones, while at home or with neighbours they spoke Polish. There were families in which German was not used at all, and others in which an effort was made to remember the ancestors' homeland and the children had to learn it as a matter of course.

As I have already mentioned, the laborious work — and in the initial period surely also many problems of an organizational nature, as well as those arising from life on waterlogged terrain — was far from an idyll. The nineteenth century reaped an enormous harvest among the young, and especially among children, both newborns and those raised, as it used to be said. Andreas Beutler died on 23.11.1826 at the age of merely 48 — one might say almost in the prime of life. Five years earlier, at the age of 38, his wife Eva Rosine had died. A few years after the death of them both, in 1831, an epidemic of cholera swept through the territories where their descendants now lived. That one, and the one of 1852 — far more severe — the Beutler family survived. The second wave was so strong that, as sources from that period report, the local Catholic population made its way weeping and lamenting to the sanctuary in Licheń to beseech the Mother of God for grace and preservation from the pestilential air. It is certain that of all the children Andreas and Eva had, four reached adulthood: Johann (Jan), Martin (Marcin), Louise (Ludwika) and Andreas (Andrzej) junior. Andreas, the son of Andreas, was a key figure for this story, for it was with his descendant that I sat in the magic garden by the beautiful house and listened to tales of the family and of ancestors from long ago.
