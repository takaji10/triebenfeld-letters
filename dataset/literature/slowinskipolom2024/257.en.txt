# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 257
# translation by the editor's files

 of Wielkopolska. They were given the worst land to cultivate, that is, areas lying in the flood zone of the Warta or sandy wastelands. They managed to bring them under cultivation. They built houses, kantorats, churches, marked out cemeteries. They blended in and grew into Polish society. They kept the faith of their forefathers, though in everyday life they often used the Polish language. The war and the post-war years changed everything. Many descendants of the settlers left for the West, often against their own will. For a large part of them, as for Johann – one of the protagonists of my book – Poland remained their homeland. So, at the end, let me allow myself a quotation: “The last traces of the history of these lands are crumbling churches, old cemeteries, collapsing cottages and the memory of old people. Before long time will erase it all. Then tell everyone about it. That there, deep in the forest, on the small hills overgrown with lilies of the valley, there are cemeteries in which lie the ashes of people who once came to these lands from far away. They spoke a different language. Here they lived for hundreds of years, worked hard. They came to love this land.”
