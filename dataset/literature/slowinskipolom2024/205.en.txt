# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 205
# translation by the editor's files

Even so, one can see that she was unable to bear the weight of human pity. Though with each year things must have grown harder for her. She lived in extreme poverty, suffered cold and hunger, and was buffeted at almost every step.

– I think Breede's life ended with the end of the war, when the sons fled Poland, the doctor was already dead, and she stayed here and had to come face to face with the inhabitants of Zagórów. That was the end of her life… – said one of my interlocutors. – After that, it seems, she was doing penance on earth for her sins as if in purgatory…

Barbara Nowakowska tells me how, soon after the war – despite her own difficult situation – the widow of Dr Breede helped my interlocutor's mother in caring for the children.

– She used to visit our house very often and was treated almost like a member of the family, like a friend. When mama sometimes had to go away, Mrs Breede would look after us, help with homework. It was not constant, but it happened fairly often. That is precisely how I remember her. I have the most delightful memories of her from my childhood. She was always perfectly proper towards me and my mama. I remember how beautifully she dressed me in a little dress for the nativity play, when I took part in the performance…

Barbara's mother remembered Breede from the time when the latter ran the Sokolic group and held her in respect, so there was no question of treating her badly in her home, even though she knew what was said in Zagórów about the Doctor's wife.

– She was no monster, although many may have been put off by her grand manner from pre-war times and her habit of expecting obedience from others – says Mrs Nowakowska. – But even if some of what she was later accused of was true, she paid a very high price for it all.

Despised by society, she lived in the attic, slept in a heap of rags and cardboard, together with the mice – so I was told. All the same she tried to keep up appearances to the last moment, and for kindness shown her, she repaid in kind – with a small favour, help with studies, a kind word.
