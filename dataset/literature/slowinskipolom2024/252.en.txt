# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 252
# translation by the editor's files

 it is worth recounting the history of the family and telling how and when the Brudlers came to the vicinity of Zagórów. Now, the Brudler family was connected with Zagórów and its surroundings from the mid-nineteenth century, when Johann Christian Brudler, a blacksmith, came to these parts. Johann Christian was born in 1795 in the village of Strzyżewo (German: Strehse) in the municipality of Zbąszyń (German: Bentschen).

At the end of the nineteenth century Strzyżewo belonged to Międzyrzecz county and numbered 100 households and 781 inhabitants. Protestants (605) predominated over Catholics (165) and Jews (11). Johann's parents were Christian Brudler and Dorothea Elisabeth née Mikke. The boy learned the blacksmith's trade from his father, who practised it precisely in Strzyżewo. Originally the boy was to take over his father's smithy, but because of his father's early death those plans were never realized. The changing economic conditions presumably caused the young man to long to seek his fortune in another place, and he set off eastward. The route of his wanderings can be traced from the birth records of his children and the death records of his wives and of himself. He came to the municipality of Krzymów near Konin, where in Roztoka he met his first wife, Anna Rosine Rostock. The couple had eleven children, which was nothing extraordinary in those times. Of all those children, for the purposes of this story the most important will be Christian Ferdinand, born in 1826. In the municipality of Krzymów Johann Christian was engaged in tilling the land. At a certain stage of the family's history they moved to Władysławów, where Anna Rosine died in 1851. Frequent moving about was within the standards of the life of farmers or small craftsmen of those days, dictated by changing fortunes and the need to find a better place to live and work, one that would guarantee a living for the family. Johann Christian contracted his next three marriages already in the Protestant church in Zagórów. At that time he lived, as one can read in the marriage record (1855) with his second wife, Kristine Schultz, in Huta Trąbczyńska, where he was a shoemaker. Kristine died after four years of marriage, and still in that same year, 1859, Johann Christian married once more. His chosen one was 35-year-old Wilhelmine née Krakowski.
