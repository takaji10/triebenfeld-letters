In the towns – multicultural clusters – a progressive Polonization was noticeable, up to decisions to introduce services in the Polish language, occurring from the end of the nineteenth century. Additionally, despite the efforts of the partitioning power and the designation of a university for Lutheran pastors in Dorpat, thus far removed from Polish influences, assimilation among the future clergy made headway, especially through the Konwent Polonia operating at the university.

Before the outbreak of the First World War, in the area of the Russian partition there was in essence no question of a sharpening of relations between the German- and Polish-speaking population – unlike in the Prussian partition. Prussian policy toward the eastern provinces and its anti-Polish character, however, intensified the processes of self-defense of Polish society, part of which were debates on the possible role of the German-speaking colonists as German outposts and instruments of Germanization. Whereas national origin or a sense of national identity did not carry much weight until the outbreak of the war in 1914, and the center of political conflicts focused on the Russification measures of the partitioning power, already in the period of the struggles over the future of the lands of the individual partitions, over their political and territorial shape and ethnic composition – a polarization of positions occurred; the problems of national identity and of national demarcation between the German- and Polish-speaking population revived. This theme also revived within the evangelical Churches. The occupation of the lands of the Russian partition by the Germans initiated a process of political transformations culminating in the rebuilding of the Polish state and the regaining of independence after 123 years. The Russian administration carried out – especially after the battle of Tarnów and Gorlice – the forced resettlement of tens of thousands of politically unreliable German-speaking inhabitants from the north-eastern territories of Congress Poland. The presence of the German troops, however, met with a reserved attitude among the German-speaking evangelical inhabitants of the former Russian partition.
