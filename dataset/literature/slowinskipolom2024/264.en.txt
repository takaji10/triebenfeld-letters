As she grew older, Ida began to decline in health. The community interview from the time when she was applying for a place in the care home showed that she lived in a little room in the attic, which lacked basic household equipment, and that she herself suffered from the ailments of old age and lapses of memory. Ida was certainly no longer capable of work on the farm. It was determined at the time that she required the care of a second person.

In the course of writing this story I showed a draft of the text to an acquaintance. And here again we are dealing with a small miracle, or perhaps precisely with Ida's overriding desire to have her story told, for it turned out that my acquaintance, as a teenager, had probably come across Ida several times at the care home in Ślesin. My acquaintance often visited the home to see her grandfather, and it was from that very time – the second half of the 1980s – that she remembered the woman, of whom she speaks as follows: “The Ida described in the text I connected with a lady I often saw at the care home. She caught my attention because she made a rather unpleasant impression (at least on me as a teenager), particularly her black clothes, her red face and her greasy hair. Somehow I have a strange association that they spoke of her as ‘that German woman’ and did not address her by her first name. I remember that my grandfather treated her with dislike, the other residents too. I felt sorry for her.” Could this have been Ida? Highly probable, since in the municipality of Zagórów the same features of appearance were remembered, which later caught the eye of the teenager visiting the care home and were remembered for years. Ida Disterheft remained unmarried all her life. Perhaps it was her conscious choice, or perhaps factors such as those I wrote about above combined to bring about that state. Her origins, her own and others' wartime experiences, the life experiences of the post-war years, the presumed disability, the loss of contact with her sisters in Germany, perhaps also the shared surname with the mayor meant that even if she might have appealed to someone, unfortunately it would have been socially unacceptable to ask for her hand.
