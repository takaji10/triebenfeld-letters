# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 278
# translation by the editor's files

But the Beutlers were always decent. One of them, a very upright man, worked with my uncle at the mill in Zagórów. It was a mill, a sawmill and a repair shop.

#### I cannot forgive

I still feel resentment towards the Germans living in Zagórów. Because of Diesterheft, in revenge for taking part in the “Barka” action, my father, Feliks Molski, was murdered together with his friends from the Polish Military Organisation.

#### “The Diesterhefts kept the stolen monstrance under the floor”

##### Janina Woźniak (b. 13.01.1929) remembers

I remember Diesterheft (the mayor of Zagórów during the occupation) – a terrible man. Mrs Diesterheft was also an awful woman. She hurled abuse at Poles. Goods were bought in their shop on ration cards. Under the floor of their flat they had hidden the monstrance plundered from our parish church. Dr Breede's sons were “Hitlerites”. They struck my grandfather so hard on the head that his cap fell off. He was 62 years old.

◼

##### Helena Nadolna (10.01.1932 – 2.01.2013) remembers

Diesterheft was said to be a decent enough man before the war, but under the occupation he turned into a devil. He extorted gold from the Jews and gave away the POW men. After the war he was said to have fled from Zagórów by bicycle, and then he left for America. Diesterheft's wife went to Germany, and their sons probably died in the war. The younger was active in the Hitlerjugend, the elder fought at the front.
