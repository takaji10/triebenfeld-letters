Congregations were active in Golina, Myśliborz and Cienin – that is, directly in the vicinity of Zagórów – and also further afield: in Lisew, Nieświastów, Wyszyn, Wola Łaszczowa, Żychlin and so on. In keeping with the premise that the Reformation was a renewal of the Church, often entirely new congregations were not founded at all; rather, in existing churches services began to be held and the sacraments administered according to Reformation principles. In Konin, services were held in the castle. Even the parish priest Lutomierski favoured the religious novelties from Wittenberg. In turn, the inspection carried out by canon Wysocki in the year 1550 found that the monastery estates of Ląd were leased to dissenters, and even turned up correspondence with Wittenberg. In the second half of the sixteenth century, Lutherans also served as castellans of Ląd, among them Rafał Przyjemski.

Indeed, Protestantism must have been very widespread in Zagórów and its surroundings, since:

– the monastery in Ląd, seized by the spirit of the Reformation, possessed three towns (Lądek, Kłodawa and Zagórów, with the parish churches located in them) as well as 63 villages;  
– the coat of arms of Zagórów is based on the so-called Luther Rose[^12];  
– under the pretext of national matters (the Cistercians hailed from Germany), in the autumn of 1553 the Cistercians were driven out to Silesia, to Henryków (Heinrichau).

After the election of Jan Wysocki as abbot in Ląd there began the period of the Counter-Reformation, which destroyed the Polish current of the Reformation in this area. The old teaching returned to the surrounding churches, yet in hearts and minds there remained the influence of the new way of thinking, which is visible to this day.
