# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 241
# translation by the editor's files

By car we went to the vicinity of several neighbouring farmsteads – my parents recalled names I had once heard. Some of the farmsteads no longer existed. Presumably no one wanted to live in them, or they were inhabited only for a short period, then fell into ruin and were dismantled. My aunt Eugenie Plitt, who kept up a written correspondence with the Langwiński family, informed them of our visit to Łazińsk. We drove back in the direction of Grabina.

On the way there, we met Mr Langwiński, who farmed the farmstead of my grandfather's brother Emil Schmechel. Looking for us, he had driven out to meet us. He had probably learned of our visit to Łazińsk from two of his guests, whom we met on the way to my grandparents' former farm, because that day their youngest daughter was receiving her First Communion and a Communion celebration was taking place at the Langwińskis' home. There we were warmly received and entertained. One of the older guests still remembered the families of Gustav and Emil Schmechel exactly. He was interested in what had become of them. He probably knew what had happened to the men in the last months of the war. At the end of the visit we arranged to meet their daughter Wanda in Poznań, we took photographs, and we exchanged small gifts as well as addresses. After the visit to the Langwiński family we drove on to the next destination of our journey, to the place where my mother spent her childhood and youth[^81].

The loud barking of a dog announced our arrival. Before we entered the farmyard, the residents present, of the surname Parusz, came up to the entrance gate. The conversation between my parents and Mrs Parusz proceeded rather matter-of-factly and with reserve. Her son and daughter-in-law, like Charlotte and me, merely watched the people talking. How good it would be to know at least a little of the language. Then we paid a short visit to the neighbour Barcok[^82].



[^81]: Włodzimirów – supplemented by the translator.

[^82]: Perhaps the surname in question here is Bartczak – translator's note.
