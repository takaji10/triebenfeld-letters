# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 227
# translation by the editor's files

My wife Edith also enrolled, but after a few months she gave up. It was too difficult a language for her. It was much easier for me. I took my son and my grandson to Łazińsk, to show them where I was born. True, there is no house any more, but we easily found the place by the little lake and the old willow that remained. A ditch with water led to the lake. I remember an anecdote: one day Mama dressed our sister Irka in a pretty dress. We brothers were seven. We assured Mama that yeees! We would look after Irka! But, being boys, we egged her on to jump over the ditch. She jumped, but right into the middle of the water. Oh, did we catch it from Mama! I have not forgotten that, those happy pre-war years… Now I show everyone that famous ditch.

#### The film in Berlin

The film *People from the Iron Houses* made an impression. The cinema was full. After the screening many people wanted to know where that place was, where those people from the iron houses had lived. Many people were moved by the film. It sparked a discussion about what the war unleashed by Hitler had done. Words of support were voiced for a dialogue that would lead to the mutual forgiveness of wrongs, to the release of this wartime and post-war trauma. The film *People from the Iron Houses* helped me greatly. If I had doubts about whether I was doing the right thing, returning to my home region and opening up the memory of times past, this film gave me wings. My reason and my heart told me not to think too much, but to go on and act for the sake of the future! I commanded myself: "Go there and talk to people. That is the most important thing!" This film says beautifully that we must talk, that we must reach one another, so that what the war did may never happen again… So that war may never happen again. When German friends watched this film, they stressed how apt were the words at its end: "Where there is a border, there is also a road"… That is what we must hold on to and act on! This film is therapy for many people…
