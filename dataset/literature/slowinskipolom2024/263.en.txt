So much from Mrs Wanda's recollections. The residence registers further inform us that Ida Disterheft de-registered and moved away to Koło. Nothing is known at the present moment about her stay in Koło and the surrounding area. This scrap of memory is all that could be obtained. The family of the last farmer for whom Ida Disterheft worked evidently, after returning to Szetlewek from the vicinity of Koło, decided against a conversation, claiming that they remember nothing about her and know nothing.

Ida's fate is the frequent fate of women and men of German roots who for various reasons did not leave for Germany after the Second World War. One can only surmise why Ida did not do so, for what the real reason was we will probably never learn. The life of Polish citizens of German origin in the times of the People's Republic was not easy, of which Ida herself is an example. Though born and raised in a municipality where Poles made up the overwhelming majority, though recognized as a Polish citizen when Poland regained its independence in 1918, though she might have felt a Pole, in 1939 the occupation authorities classified her as a German, and a German she remained for many, in the eyes of many, long after the war. To this was added the shared surname with a war criminal, and the recipe for a lousy life was ready. As I mentioned above, in the 1950s she was in Szetlewek, then she spent some time in Koło or the surrounding area. In 1971 she came to Szetlewek, to a farmer who gave her work and a roof over her head for over a dozen years, for it was with him that she remained until she received a place in the Social Welfare Home in Ślesin. She knew farm work; after all, she was born in the countryside, and in Łazy her parents had their own farm. She helped them as all village children do.
