# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 221
# translation by the editor's files

 German origin to take German citizenship and to join the German army, the Wehrmacht. Refusal was threatened with a camp or death. My two older brothers were killed, and we do not even know exactly where, or where their burial place is. Bruno was killed somewhere in the Carpathians during the fighting with the Russians in 1944, and Gustav came home at the end of the war. You could say he was a straggler, because he had left his unit on his own. The Russians were already coming. When Gustav arrived, you could hear the Russian tanks coming down the road. He thought he would be able to stay at home, that the war was over for him. He thought everything would be as it was before… He was glad he no longer had to serve in the Wehrmacht. Yet the day after his return the Russians took him away, and he never came back. He vanished without a trace…

#### Resettlement without return

In Łazy the Germans assigned us a Polish farmstead which was larger than ours. Such were the customs they introduced then. The owners of the farmstead in Łazy, an older couple with a son, were moved into our farmstead on Łazińsk. Their son commuted to work somewhere nearby. The whole family survived the war there in peace. After the war they returned to Łazy, to their farmstead. But the new Polish authorities did not allow us to return to ours.

#### Abram

I remember an episode like this. During the war, things left behind by Jews were brought into the village. My mother got very upset. She said she did not want any other people's things. Probably the adults guessed what was going on. Officially it was said that the Jews had been moved out and all their things were there for the taking. There was one Jewish family in the village, called Kowalski. They ran a shop on Łazińsk. I remember that even at the beginning of the war that shop was still operating, so this family must have been taken away sometime in the middle of the war… I remember that at one point Mr Kowalski had a star on his clothing, front and back, but the shop was still open…
