#### Łazińsk — the village's history spans over 280 years[^93]

Łazińsk is a village formerly in Konin county, in which a predominantly German population lived. Heading southwest from the county town of Konin on the Warta, one arrived after a while at the gmina of Drommin (Trąbczyn), to which — besides the village of Michalinów — belonged the settlement of Łazińsk, inhabited almost exclusively by Germans. Until 1945 some 170 German families still lived there.

It was the year 1718 when 14 German families arrived from Silesia, led by their village headman, and from the owner of the Trąbczyn landed estate they received a large area of forest in order to bring it under cultivation. From hard pioneering work there arose the village of Łazińsk "from the green root" [*aus grüner Wurzel*].

The families living here were named Kuhn, Merk, Schönfisch, Haupt. These surnames were still encountered in this area until 1945, and can be found many a time on the ancestor table of the Weber/Rhytthammer family.

In the year 1968 the German settlement could have celebrated the 250th anniversary of its existence, had history taken a different course.

To the left beside the kantorate school, on a gently rising hill, lay a richly wooded cemetery in which our ancestors laid the first grave in the year 1727. A most ancient resting place, whose weathered and rotted gravestones of wood and iron existed still until the year 1945.



[^93]: Source: Note in: Der Heimatbote, 8/81-3 Kartoteka Wartheland (the Warta Land), Poland. Translated by Jacek Kin.
