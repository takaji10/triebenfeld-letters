 Ala, after the war helped me a great deal, thanks to which I was able to educate my sons.

Mr Rydel, too, was a noble man. After the war he looked after the graves in the Protestant part of the cemetery. I held him in great esteem, because he helped us a lot during the war. When he came to the house to inspect during the occupation, he would close his eyes and pretend not to see certain things. My mother always said that under the Germans she could hide something, but under the commies – nothing could be done. I was at his funeral, and whenever I came to Zagórów I would go to the cemetery to light a candle on his grave.

#### Pastor Gross apologized.

##### Fr. Mieczysław Rydecki (b. 1932) recalled

I am the son of Edmund Rydecki, a member of the POW from the interwar period. In 1940 my father was arrested and deported. He was imprisoned in the jail in Sieradz. Help came to us from my mother's sister-in-law, Auntie Klara Wilantowa, who was of German origin. She gathered whatever gold jewellery there was in the family and set off with it for Sieradz. I don't know the details, but after a year and eight months had passed Father returned home. A few months later, sometime towards the end of 1943, he was summoned to the gendarmerie. The summons must have been brought by the pastor Aleksander Gross, who was well disposed towards the Poles; he apologized from the doorway for having had to come. Father was sent to work in the basket-making workshop in Pyzdry.
