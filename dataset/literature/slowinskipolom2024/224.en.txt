# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 224
# translation by the editor's files

#### A moment of stability

In the end we ended up at a state agricultural farm (PGR) in Działyn near Poznań. Our aunt Emilia, father's sister, was already there. We reached Jarocin on the little railway, and then by an ordinary train to Poznań and Działyn. After a few months in Działyn we were moved to a state farm somewhere near Gniezno. A bus from Gniezno ran there. My aunt was arranging papers in Gniezno for departure to Germany. Her husband had gone there earlier, and she was able to apply on the grounds of family reunification. Then we, as close relatives, had a chance to travel to join them. Neither Papa nor Mama was with us. Leaving was not easy. Sometimes the applications had to be submitted many times over. A family we knew, the family of Armin Muench, applied to leave twenty times. Several times they failed to leave because their permission came unexpectedly and too late for them to reach Konin. They could leave Poland on condition that within six hours the whole family would be on a train in Konin. Their family numbered twenty people. Everyone worked somewhere. By the time they received the information and got together, they no longer had any chance of reaching Konin – besides, they had no means to do it. And there were no telephones then. We wanted to travel to our uncle in West Germany, but the pressure was to keep us in the GDR, in communist East Germany. GDR officials even tried to pull us out of the train and not let us past the communist cordon. After the experience of the war and of the passing of the Russian front we were afraid to stay in their zone of influence. In the end we managed it.

#### Departure

We managed to leave Poland in the spring of 1950 thanks to the help of Aunt Emilia. We were orphans. We did not know what future awaited us there, what might become of us… It was very hard for us… After reaching Germany it was not easy either. After all, we were some strange newcomers – neither Germans nor Poles. There, too, nobody rejoiced at our arrival. We were on foreign soil. I had no education, I knew no trade.
