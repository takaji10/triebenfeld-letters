The limousine of the distinguished guest therefore had to drive not only over the flowers scattered by the children.

"Mr Matuszak" also had a nice son, Kazimierz, who in his mature years served for many years as the local gravedigger. People valued this man's services more than those of the two feuding local doctors – Breede and Lidmanowski. For Mr Kazimierz had the habit of looking in on a house where someone was ill; he liked to have a chat with the sick person, comfort him, tell him a joke, and by way of farewell would, just in case, take the measurements for his grave. After such a visit the patient would usually leap out of bed and quickly recover, without any need of calling the doctor and taking medicines[^77].
