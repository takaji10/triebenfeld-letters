# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 18
# translation by the editor's files

Nationalist moods, however, pass not only through the heads of many Poles. They have long been present in the milieu of the Jewish community. Representatives of the Zionist party, organizing collections of money for the purchase of land in Palestine, operate legally, at times unofficially. In the council of the Jewish community the Zionists win ever more seats in the successive elections from 1924 onwards. There may have been no aggression in this against others, but the Zionists treated the country in which they had lived for centuries as a temporary place of residence. A foreign side… The community of Evangelicals in the last town board before the outbreak of the war was represented, as alderman, by Teodor Milicz. The state police also notice the growing activity of some German settlers intoxicated by Nazi ideology. In the house of the cantor in Łazińsk, Gustav Adolf Mielke – at the same time a teacher and the head of the German Evangelical school – there had been assembled a small library of "works" propagating the vision of a great Germany and of a happy nation of masters. Apparently, in this whole hot cauldron, which in the political sense Zagórów was in those times, Mielke's activity must already have overstepped the bounds of tolerance. He was forced to leave Łazińsk and, as a punishment, transferred somewhere to Galicia. Of course, he returned at once after the entry of the Wehrmacht. In Zagórów itself the well-known merchant Julius Disterheft organizes an underground cell of the NSDAP. Under the cover of commercial business he rides around the district, at home he organizes men's gatherings, creating a disciplined and hostile group – a fifth column. The shocked citizens of Zagórów would learn of this immediately after the occupation of the town by a detachment of the Wehrmacht. These nationalisms, manifesting themselves so variously in the small Zagórów milieus, are like rays focused in a small lens, showing the picture of a world falling apart, marching ever faster towards the edge, beyond which lies the black abyss and the death of the millions of victims of another war. That march towards perdition could not be stopped, for infinite space was needed for the building of the Thousand-Year Reich.
