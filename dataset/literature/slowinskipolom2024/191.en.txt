They made slaves of their neighbours, they killed the patriots, they plundered their houses and land, and they still believe that "in this time moving towards destruction", on the ruins of the old ideals, they will preserve their sunny land.

This is a very important and dramatic picture of a man fitted, through a cruel and ruthless system, into ultimate situations, a man cornered by great history. For the members of the family to whom he wrote, such repeated declarations of a man who fought for many months in the front line and claimed that everything that befell him happened "by the grace of God" must have had a weight and force of arguments not to be shaken. For this is perceptible – even in this comparatively modest group of letters – that regardless of whether he is marching to the East to build the promised paradise, or retreating under the growing pressure of the adversary, he never gives a sign that he is going through a crisis of faith. Still his chief dream is to become a pastor, to celebrate the services and other rites and to proclaim the word of God. "It is not excluded that God will have to lead us all through very hard roads". He did not abandon his God for the new "filthy" faith. But – and this remark is worth raising – the image of the Christian:
the tiny, humble "servant of God" which the Nazi propaganda derided, does not entirely accord with what we perceive in the attitude of this soldier. He fights in the front line, kills enemies and lice, is tough and does not want to be worse, nor weaker, than his comrades. He differs, perhaps, from some of them in that he sees, or only has a presentiment of, the coming days of defeat. In this sense one may risk the remark that he did not walk those evil paths in darkness. Another matter is whether the light that shone for him was clear to the end.

In reading Bruno Mielke's letters, one gains the impression that they were written in two languages. Personal experiences, care for the family, confessional avowals – this is vocabulary straight out of the Bible, religious writings and sentimental ballads about love. Here even the descriptions of the side against which he is fighting do not "reek" of hatred. These are Russians, adversaries, in the sharpest version the enemy.
