 *bis 18.3.1938* by Walther Gehl [The German Awakening], *Deutsche Haltung vor Fremden* by Bruno Behm, Graz 1943 [The German Attitude towards Foreigners].


A few words about these items and their authors. *Deutschlands große Tage* is a little book describing the assembly convoked after the elections, which took place on 21 March 1933 in the garrison church in Potsdam, at which Adolf Hitler was entrusted with the formation of a government. Earlier, on 15 March, the victorious leader of the NSDAP had proclaimed the founding of the Third Reich and deprived his political opponents of their parliamentary mandates. Walther Gehl (1897-1942), one of the authors of books who fought with pen and weapon alike, was at the same time working "on the spiritual development of the Wehrmacht". Among other things he was the author of *Der nationalsozialistische Staat* (1933) and of numerous history textbooks for secondary school pupils. He fell in June 1942 on the Eastern Front. Bruno Behm (1892-1974), in turn, was an Austrian writer of extreme right-wing origins with a very eventful biography. He made his name with the statement that "If the Jews complain to the whole world of their lot, one must tell them that they brought that lot upon themselves". But the weightiest volume in genre terms is the book *Deutsche Geschichte im 19. Jahrhundert* by Heinrich von Treitschke (1834-1896), the author of a famous essay of 1879 which provoked a broad anti-Semitic response. It is in that text that the sentence appears: "*Die Juden sind unser Unglück*" (The Jews are our misfortune). The Nazis editing "Der Stürmer" took this sentence as the motto of the paper and… of their actions. The late author, however, had no influence on whom the publisher would invite to write the introduction to the next edition. It was Alfred Rosenberg, the creator of a criminal ideology, whose *Der Mythus des 20 Jahrhunderts* (The Myth of the Twentieth Century), alongside Adolf Hitler's *Mein Kampf*, played a decisive role in formatting German minds in the spirit of the racial superiority of the master race, for whom an appropriate living space was to be conquered. (By 1944 a million copies had been sold).
