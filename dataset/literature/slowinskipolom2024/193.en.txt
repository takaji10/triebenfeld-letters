# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 193
# translation by the editor's files

 I still had with me a roll of sugar sweets (*Zuckerchen*). I gave them to the smaller of them, who, like her sister, had on her neck a little casket with a Christ's cross. A quiet 'thank you very much' and the bright gaze of the blue children's eyes was for me the thank-offering. I should not like to see that child again! (Quietly I prayed about it). This, together with the previous community of prayer, brings peace into the heart which the world does not know and cannot give".

A broad search in the Polish and German sources available on the internet, and enquiries into the further fate of Bruno Mielke, brought no results. The letter of 14 May 1944 is the last material trace he left behind.

*You walk in darkness, and your ignorance is like the cloak of night*[^71].

In the last year of the war the 2. Gebirgs Division is transferred from Norway to Denmark. Later it took part in heavy fighting against the Western Allies at Trier and Philippsburg – Heilbronn. Part of the division ends the war in Württemberg; the remaining units capitulated in the Tyrol[^72].
