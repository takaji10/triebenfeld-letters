The dream which runs through many of his surviving letters – including those from the front – was for Bruno to take up studies at the Evangelical-Theological Faculty of the University of Vienna. Worried whether he would manage on the demanding course of studies, he describes his preparation thus: "I have an education as a teacher (also in music), but unfortunately I know no foreign language (apart from Polish, which I have mastered well orally and in writing). But I suppose Greek and Latin cannot be done without […] As for abilities, I am indeed no genius, yet – as the former headmaster wrote on my certificate – I am diligent, because I, despite the lack of previous preparation (the headmaster's assessment), with God's help did not have to repeat a single subject. I am already 29 years old, still a bachelor, but I have a youthful appearance and disposition (so I am often judged)".

But 22 June 1941 came. Hitler opens a new front. He attacks his recent "ally" – the USSR. A letter of 26 June 1941 to Bruno's sister – Flora – from the Lehmann family of Dresden has survived. They thank her for the parcel with sausage and other foodstuffs. They mention in passing that the nearest meeting of the Women's League "wants to thank our dear Führer for the fact that he attacked at the right time, for one need only think what would have happened had it been otherwise. At the very beginning Russian hordes would have flooded over you, and last week we thought of Łazińsk very often". That the Russians might appear in Dresden was excluded in advance.

No one yet knows what terrible consequences Hitler's decision will bring. Bruno Mielke begins to realise that this war may freeze his plans and dreams for a long time. In a letter dated 8 July 1941 he informs his sisters Kadzia and Flora that he is cramming Latin "for Vienna". He holds to his intention, even if in August or later he goes into the Wehrmacht.
