Among the books there are, of course, items such as the Holy Scripture of the Old and New Testament (*Die Bibel oder die ganze Heilige Schrift des Alten und Neuen Testaments*, Vienna 1886). The Bible – in accordance with old tradition – was a wedding present for Samuel Julius Mielke and Natalie Tonn in the year 1892. The volume contains, before the title page, pages for keeping a family chronicle with handwritten entries of the recipients, which I write about further on. Among the books are two collections of sermons, *Rozważania na każdy dzień roku* [Devotions for Every Day of the Year], a New Testament in a pocket edition and other mystical, religious works. There are guides to married and sexual life (*Liebe und Ehe. Ein Buch Für Eheleute* and *Das Liebes und Geschlechtsleben des Menschen*) and hymnals for festive occasions. There is also *Schlag nach über England* – a guide to England and the British Empire. Presumably published with an eye to compatriots preparing to visit the island after a victorious invasion…[^53] It is difficult to enumerate all these items in detail.

It is worth returning once more to the political part of the library collection and devoting a few words to Goebbels's biographer. Wilfrid Alfred Karl Bade (born 1906) belonged to the very active figures in Nazi propaganda. He was one of the leading lecturers and "creative interpreters" of the thoughts and ideas of the Führer and of his chief Goebbels. Besides the biography of Goebbels first published in 1933, he authored among other works such titles as *Die SA erobert Berlin* (The SA Conquers Berlin), *Geschichte des Dritten Reich 4 Bünde* (A History of the Third Reich in 4 Volumes), *Das Hohelied vom Dritten Reich* (The Song of Songs of the Third Reich) and *Tod Und Leben. Verse des Krieges* (Death and Life. Verses of the War). Bade died in December 1945 in Russian captivity in Kaunas. Yet more important than the author of the biography is its subject – Paul Joseph Goebbels himself. The doctorate in German philology which he received at the university in Heidelberg in 1922 was an academic confirmation of the intellectual prowess of the future minister of propaganda. His fanatical service to the Führer, his undoubted oratorical talent and his path to the summits of power must have made a very strong impression on these "well-read" inhabitants of the kantorate in Łazińsk.

If someone preparing for flight takes care of his little library, then it must have meant something to that family! Why was this cache created at all?! Out of fear lest the collection of letters from the front of a son and brother should fall into the hands of soldiers, thus giving a pretext for a harsh reckoning with the family? Was the Goebbels biography and the other books that the household regarded as important, because they constituted their national-socialist ideological identity, not put into the cache for the same reason? Were the remaining volumes hidden out of fear that the Russians or the Poles would burn them – just as the works cursed by that same Goebbels were burned? In one of the memoirs we read that the farmers being expelled in April were told that this was a temporary operation. The question is who said this: representatives of the new administration or local Nazi leaders who believed in the Führer's wonder weapon?
