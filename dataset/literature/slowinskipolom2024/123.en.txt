# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 123
# translation by the editor's files

 and the first in the family born in Dąbrowica, in 1900. When she reached the so-called marriageable years — that is, when she could already marry — she wed Tadeusz Molicki, with whom she emigrated to Argentina, precisely to Buenos Aires. There, still before the war, they owned a grocery and general-goods shop. They sailed to Poland on a visit in 1939, and the outbreak of the Second World War overtook them there, which effectively made it impossible for them to return to Argentina, and so they decided to remain in Poland. During the war they settled in Silesia — they lived in Bytom, where Tadeusz Molicki worked as an accountant in a leather-glove factory in Świdnica (*Handschuhfabrik-Alfred Scholz – Schweidnitz*). The niece of his wife, and daughter of Mrs Gertruda, Lila, has preserved to this day a wall thermometer bearing the factory's name, and it still works. After Tadeusz's death Natalia lived with her sister — precisely Gertruda — in Skulsk. Through her niece she is remembered as an exceptionally kind and likeable aunt who, even in old age, disliked older people, but felt very much at ease in the company of those younger than herself.

Natalia Molicka née Beutler died in 1991 and was buried at the Evangelical cemetery in Konin. Emma Beutler, born in 1902, was the second daughter of Adolf and his first wife Amalie. The family associates Emma with a photograph, already as a middle-aged woman, living in Canada, to which she had gone with her husband Erwin Byczyński. In the photograph taken on the occasion of her son's wedding around 1967 one can see an elegant woman with decided features that betray a propensity for frequent smiles. Erwin Byczyński, her husband, came from the neighbouring municipality of Lądek, from beyond the river, and despite his Polish-sounding name also traces his line back to German settlers of the eighteenth century, who, perhaps on account of the cooper's trade they practised, were initially called Tonne[^49], a name that gradually underwent polonization and sounded Beczka, Beczyński, Bitschinski, up to the form Byczyński. We shall yet meet the Byczyński family in this story.



[^49]: *Tonne* (German) – barrel.
