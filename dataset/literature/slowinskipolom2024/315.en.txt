 we begin work at the next cemetery, we try to involve local communities in these activities. First of all, we do this because the local authorities should always be asked for permission, but also because if the local community joins in such work, then besides learning something about the place where they live, they will respect their own work more and will not allow further devastation of the cemetery grounds. In many cases this is exactly what happens. It is the local residents nearby who then keep watch so that the cemetery does not overgrow anew with grass and bushes, so that no one destroys the gravestones.

It is true that there are also places where neither the local authorities nor the residents nearby ever supported our activities, where no one ever appeared to help. In the history of the Stowarzyszenie FRYDHOF, but above all already in the history of the saving of the heritage of the Evangelicals of the Zagórów municipality, there is inscribed the municipal councillor Jan Kloc. A man of tireless energy, incredible passion and human goodness. It is chiefly thanks to him that the Evangelical-Augsburg cemetery in Michalinów Trąbczyński looks today like a cemetery. We began the work together: FRYDHOF, the Towarzystwo Przyjaciół Ziemi Zagórowskiej and Jan Kloc. But it was Mr Jan who completed the work of the renovation of this cemetery. One must not forget one more figure for whom heritage – in this case the heritage of ancestors – is not a matter of indifference. That figure is Mr Erich Kuhn, born on the Łazińsk in the Zagórów municipality, living there until the 1950s. When, in the difficult post-war times, the Evangelicals had to leave the People's Poland, Mr Erich's family and he himself left as well. Yet he never forgot his little homeland, and for more than twenty years now, every summer Erich Kuhn gathers a group of former inhabitants of the Zagórów municipality of the Evangelical-Augsburg confession, who live in the territory of the Evangelical-Augsburg parish in Detmold in Germany, and they come to Zagórów, where a Polish-German church service is then held.
