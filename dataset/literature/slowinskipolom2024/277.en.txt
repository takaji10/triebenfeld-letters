# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 277
# translation by the editor's files

#### Evenings with Legion songs

I remember the evenings when the former POW men from his group gathered at our place, but I remember only one surname – Roman Brzeg. At those gatherings Legion songs were sung. I remember almost all of them to this day. Father recited beautifully; I remember a very long poem beginning with the words: “In the hospital, on the ward, an unknown soldier is dying, his breast heaves in his last agony…” – I don't remember the rest. I was thirteen when Father left for Łódź to work for the railways (PKP). The evenings with songs came to an end…

I explain my father's acceptance of the Volksliste imposed on him by the fact that he had to hide his membership of the POW and, at all costs, protect the four children aged between 11 and 16.

He died at the age of 66 and was buried in Zagórów in the grave of his parents.

#### My brother deserted from the German army

As a result of the hostilities my brother ended up in the West; there he probably deserted from the labour service units[^87] or from the German army. He was in the Polish army under his mother's maiden name – as Leon Majewski. He never returned to Poland; he settled in England. He worked as a car-body designer for General Motors. He died at the age of 65.

#### The Beutlers were always decent

##### Tadeusz Molski (b. 1920) remembers

Unfortunately, I have the impression that in the 1930s most of the Protestants were supporters of Hitler. And the worst was Mayor Diesterheft.



[^87]: Reichsarbeitsdienst (RAD; Reich Labour Service) – a mass organization for the civil and military preparation of youth in the Third Reich, which during the Second World War performed auxiliary functions on behalf of the Wehrmacht.
