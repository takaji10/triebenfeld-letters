Was it the passage of the Gospel according to St Luke on the birth of Jesus? "And Joseph also went up from Galilee, out of the city of Nazareth, into Judaea, unto the city of David, which is called Bethlehem; (because he was of the house and lineage of David:) To be taxed with Mary his espoused wife, being great with child" (Lk 2,4-5)[^58].

Did he sing the old thirteenth-century carol *Adeste fideles* (Let all the faithful sing, praising their God), or perhaps "Little children, little children, hasten now together // to the manger in the stable at this festive time"? It is an extraordinary image when we try to imagine a German soldier, standing on blood-stained Russian ground, at an advanced post, humming Silent Night. He saw in his mind's eye the family going to church, the praying father; he thanks his sisters for the presents sent in the parcels, the tasty cake, the warm leather gloves. Now he will no longer freeze – even if it were 50 degrees – as happens on the central sector of the front. "Here the temperature is such that there was even a thaw the day before, which the warm Gulf Stream current Golfstrom brought from the north". Although the command looks after them and even the food at the Feast is better, still they are at the front!

They had to be especially vigilant, for the Russians had attacked on the previous Christmas. They expected an attack yesterday too, but they did not come. "But today, on the first day of the Feast, from 9 to 11 they carried out an attack with the use of artillery and mortars – quite devilishly. Thank God there were no losses and no damage. In the afternoon, in answer, ours shelled the Russian bunkers – that is how it is at the front. The day before yesterday, just before midnight, blood flowed again – one of the comrades was hit in the face. Among our soldiers behind our walls and bunkers there happen only shots in the head with a fatal outcome". This is the cold account of a shelled soldier, who writes that danger here exists always.
