##### Marta Janas (b. 1955) remembers

My grandparents, Hulda and Emil Banik of Wrąbczynkowskie Holendry, before the war had quite a lot of land, a whole eight hectares, and then they kept buying more land. At home Grandma Hulda kept the documents certifying their holdings; they had been drawn up still in the Russian language and bear the tsarist official seal. Grandma guarded them like a treasure – she and Grandpa had the hope that they would return one day… They did not return. To this day my brother and I keep those documents as a keepsake.

#### It was a Protestant teacher who taught my mother the Polish patriotic songs

My mother Elza Banik attended the third grade still at the Protestant school in Wrąbczynkowska Kolonia (Wrąbczynkowskie Holendry), where Mr Munch and Mr Kuske from Lądek taught… It was located in that house to the left of the chapel, right by the road. It was her Protestant teacher who taught her the most beautiful Polish patriotic songs and religious songs. It was from Mum that I learned them.

And in connection with their origin they too went through their ordeal after the war. My mother, as a little girl, was separated from her mother; she was made to tear up weeds – thistles – with her bare hands. In dybki (wooden clogs)[^91] she and Grandma walked to Pyzdry, because Grandma was in service with the Skorupkas in Zapowiednia…



[^91]: Dybki – wooden clogs.
