# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 324
# translation by the editor's files

There are only a few scholarly publications. I have come to the conviction that we will never create a more complete picture of the history of the town and the region unless we try to find out a little more about those who lived here for more than two hundred years and whose traces time has erased.

Despite the whole tragic period of the years 1939-1945, it should be borne in mind that it is only one part of the history of this land and its inhabitants that one should attempt to tell. It is a broken link in the chain of mutual relations which formed a peculiar code of life and an image of those generations, of this community that lived simultaneously together and apart.

It was in the year 1718 that fourteen German families arrived from Silesia, to whom the owner of the Trąbczyn estate leased a large area of woodland and wasteland for perpetual use, on which in time the village called Łazińsk came into being. The owners of the Trąbczyn estate, Paweł and Katarzyna Prusiński, allocated land for the first two settlements. These were the villages of Trąbczyńskie Luźne Stare Olędry and Trąbczyńskie Luźnie Olędry (today Łazińsk Pierwszy and Drugi).

The settlers were German farmers of the Evangelical confession. Evidently the experience with the new settlers was good, for the estate owner Prusiński had a further settlement founded. The newcomers took over the leased land under Dutch law. They were free people, with their own self-administration, with a cult of work and strict morals. In the years 1746-1789 the owners of the Łukom estate had further settlements founded. These are the present-day villages of Bukowe, Imielno, Huta Łukomska and Myszakówek.
