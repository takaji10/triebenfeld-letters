### The Evangelical-Augsburg Church in the interwar period (1918-1939)


Pastor Waldemar Wunsz

The influence of sixteenth-century Reformation thought on the territories of the then Republic of Poland is beyond doubt. This is clearly visible at the courts of the Wielkopolska and Lithuanian nobility and among the townsfolk, who embraced the new confession en masse. Clergy educated at the University of Wittenberg preached the reformed teaching, contributing to the growth of evangelical influence. The Counter-Reformation halted it, and later contributed to a marked shrinking of the Reformation's reach. The renewed influx of people of the evangelical confessions is connected with the settlement and colonization processes on Polish lands. These were connected with the deliberate bringing in of newcomers to settle the land, mainly from German-speaking territories, and later with the planned colonization campaigns of the partitioning powers. The principal areas of settlement for the evangelical population groups were the regions lying north and south of the Warta river, in the vicinity of Kalisz and Konin, the villages in the Vistula valley near Warsaw, and the heavily industrializing Łódź. Parish and diocesan structures of the evangelical Churches took shape, which was connected with the earlier attainment of religious tolerance by the dissidents. The Evangelical-Augsburg Church united in its parishes a small group of Polish-speaking evangelicals, descended from the sixteenth-century Reformation, decimated by the Counter-Reformation and the partitions, and a more numerous group descended from economic emigrants who had arrived since the close of the eighteenth century.
