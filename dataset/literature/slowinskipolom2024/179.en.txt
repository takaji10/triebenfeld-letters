As a birthday present he sends his brother a goblet made from the casing of a bullet fired at the Eastern Front in Russia. Let it be a small keepsake from the front and always accompany you on your journeys.

Perhaps this "lifting of heads" of the Poles resulted not only from the news from the front, but also from the attitude of the elder of the brothers to the former neighbours. In a considerably later letter of Gustav Adolf to the sister managing the household (12 December 1944, field post number 33 977), when he is already serving as an artilleryman in the naval infantry in Hamburg, he encloses advice on how to behave with the slave woman allocated to the farm. "Like with a Polish girl. To begin with you must take her firmly by the scruff, so that she knows where her place is. You must lock everything up and portion everything out to her. After all, it is not known whether she does not steal and is not in league with the farmhand". Further on he inserts an extensive instruction on how to run the pig breeding. And he adds a warning not to delay the pig slaughter, for they (the girl and the farmhand) might steal the pig from the shed.

There has survived in the cache in Łazińsk an interesting example of the various forms of a peculiar undermining of the morale of the German rear. It is a planted forgery of a document appointing the addressee a "reproducer", who is to "service 9 women and 18 girls" for the raising of the birth statistics. (It was known in the neighbourhood that Gustav had long been looking around for a candidate for wife). The "document" states that this honourable task may not be protested against by wives and will not be recognised as grounds for divorce. The "official paper", **a copy of which we reproduce in Testimonies pp. 307-308**, is an example of the fictitious, misleading information and ordinances produced underground and circulated among the Germans.
