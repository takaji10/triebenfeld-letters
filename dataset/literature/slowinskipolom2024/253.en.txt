Johann Christian Brudler died in 1881 in Michalinów Oleśnicki (German: Emilienheim), and his fourth wife in 1902, also in Emilienheim. There, too, three of their children were born: August (1869), Heinrich (1873) and Pauline (1876). The already-mentioned Christian Ferdinand Brudler took Anna Julianna Franzke as his wife in 1844 in Żychlin; she bore him, among others, a son, Adam. It was most probably Adam Brudler with his wife Elisabeth Theofila née Pisching who set out on the road, and it was their son, whose likeness can be seen in the photograph described at the beginning, Ferdinand Brudler, who was born in 1886 in Bobrów (German: Boberfeld). In terms of fecundity the Brudler family did not stand out against the background of other families of that period. Ferdinand had six brothers: Wilhelm, Karl Adam, Friedrich, Theodor, Ludwig and Adolf, and three sisters: Olga, Maria Magdalena and Amanda. The family supported itself from farming in Bobrów.

The year 1913 arrived. The year in which the photograph sent to the book's editors was taken. It was then that in Zagórów Ferdinand Brudler married Pauline née Haupt, the one standing beside him in the photograph, the daughter of August and Ernestine. The young couple settled on the Łazińsk, where still that same year their firstborn son Johann came into the world. Unfortunately, the boy lived only twelve weeks. After the death of their firstborn the spouses moved to Brudzew (German: Brückenau) near Września (German: Wrenschen). There, too, three daughters came into the world:
