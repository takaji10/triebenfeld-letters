### Welcoming the Reverend Shepherd


Professor Lech Słowiński about his home town of Zagórów

The little town's cowherd, "Mr Matuszak", was an intriguing figure and a joker of a kind you rarely meet. He grazed the town's cattle for many years and all the farmers were happy with him. He knew perfectly well where, on the vast meadows along the Warta, the grass had already been grazed down, where it was flooded with water, scorched by the sun, and where it had already grown back. Besides, "Mr Matuszak" was a good diplomat and never came into conflict with the Zagórów gooseherds, always finding some kind of compromise in the dispute between cattle and geese. And there were far more geese on the Zagórów meadows than cows. Their numbers, at times, ran into the thousands. Hence the constant quarrels between the gooseherds and the owners of cows, whose enormous flocks of geese ate and fouled the grass with dung. It was thanks to "Mr Matuszak's" wise manoeuvring that open war between the gooseherds and the farmers never broke out. He proceeded on the assumption that there was grass enough for both the cows and the geese; one only had to move the herds of these creatures about in a reasonably sensible way, so that the one did not get in the way of the other. I have already mentioned above that "Mr Matuszak" had an incredible sense of humour, and it was this that won everyone over to him.

I remember that in the early 1930s the bishop of Włocławek, Father Karol Maria Radoński, announced a pastoral visit to Zagórów. The town did its utmost to receive the distinguished guest worthily. All the streets were swept, every blade of grass was plucked from the cobbles, the pavements were washed, and the window panes, gleaming with cleanliness, were adorned with holy pictures. Even the Jews and the Protestants joined in these general preparations. The Father Bishop was to be welcomed at the entrance to the town coming from the direction of Słupca and Ląd.
