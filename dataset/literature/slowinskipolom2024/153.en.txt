The Germans tried yet again to resume operations, but on 22 September the Führer's order came to break off combat operations. (The bridge at Parkkino was destroyed in a daring raid by a Russian aviator on 28 September, thereby cutting off from indispensable supplies the two divisions that remained fighting in the north-east). The front line on this section stabilised until September 1944.

The family followed and deeply experienced the events on the distant, northern wing of the Eastern Front. The scattering of July letters attests to this. Father, sister and brother independently of one another send post full of heartening words. The brother Gustav writes (22 July 1942): "Today I was again in Zagórów with Kadzia (sister Leokadia). Even if in Zagórów one sees and hears more, yet nothing could distract my thoughts and I thought of you all the time. […] How many hundreds of times the question arises how you are faring and why it was precisely you who were transferred so far away". […] The sister Anna with her fiancé Hans are at home in Łazińsk. Hans has two weeks' leave. In general many Łazińsk folk come home often on leave, chiefly those who are stationed in Germany or in France". It pains him that his brother is so far away that there can be no question of leave. On the southern front things go from victory to victory, so perhaps Bruno, crowned by this victory, will soon return home. He also has a question and at the same time a request there and then. "Have you not yet had the opportunity to have yourself photographed in your soldier's uniform? In a helmet and with the other things. We – and I most of all – would be very glad".

Two letters of the father – Samuel Julius Mielke – have survived. The one of 20 April was written on the Führer's birthday and is devoted above all to him. "May the Lord God preserve him in good health for a long time, until he overcomes all evil and we can sing with him the great Hallelujah of peace". He is also troubled by the fact that many of the locals avoid the church. In the second letter (28 July 1942) we read: "Dear Bruno, beloved son of my heart!
