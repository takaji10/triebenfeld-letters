#### III. The Founding and Growth of the Parish

##### 1. The Beginnings of Pastoral Care. The Erection of the Parish and the Building of the Church

Until 1817 pastoral service to the Protestant settlers was at first provided by pastors from Września, and later from Grodziec, Konin and Żychlin. Pastoral care demanded a great sacrifice of time and health on the part of the pastors, who had to travel by horse-drawn carriage for tens of kilometres to reach those chapels. After all, they had their own duties in their mother parishes. The growing number of Protestants made constant pastoral care a necessity, which involved the calling into being of a new parish and the building of a church.

In 1829 efforts were set in motion to build an Evangelical church at Kopojno, on the estate of Gottlieb Weigl. In accordance with the protocol of 7 December 1829, signed on 15 November 1833, Weigel established (for the future parish) an annual deputat consisting of 40 quarters of rye, 10 quarters of barley, 25 quarters of oats and 6 barrels of beer. In his letter of 22 March 1836, addressed to the superintendent[^13] of Kalisz, Karol Weigel wrote that he wished to support the Protestants (“it is my greatest wish”). Unfortunately, in that same year Karol died, and his son and heir Aleksander withdrew from his father's dispositions.

In 1836 the Provisional Church College (composed of Matthäus Luther, Ludwig Sperling, Johann Kelsch, Johann Gottlieb Kittel, Karl Heit) turned to the above-mentioned superintendent Modl[^14] with a request for help in building the church, and this was promised to them.
