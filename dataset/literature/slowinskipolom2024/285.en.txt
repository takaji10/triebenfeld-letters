#### A meeting in flooded Olchowo

Grzegorz Szumański, one of the last goose breeders in Zagórów, recalls how in the times of his youth, in the mid-1960s, the waters of the Warta once again flooded the whole valley. His elder brother Janek had some business with one of the farmers at Olchowo. The only way that allowed reaching the flooded village, cut off completely from the world, was to get there by boat. At that time, in most of the Zagórów houses located on the southern slope of the valley, whose gardens ran down to the meadows, they kept simply built boats, popularly called troughs. That is what the Szumańskis did too. They took an oar and a punting pole and set off across the floodwaters. What they found on the spot they never forgot. They could sail into the farmyard; only the well and the barn stood above the water line. They sailed the boat right into the house, straight into the entrance hall and the kitchen. The household had set all the furniture and beds up on hollow blocks, on which they had laid boards, and in this way they moved about the house. Nobody was thinking of fleeing or of a temporary evacuation. On a rise some 50 m away stood a little herd of cows, and a bit farther on, higher up, the cemetery, which – as the locals remembered – the water had never flooded. Out of the whole populous village of former times (before 1939 more than 60 families were settled here) three farmsteads remained…
