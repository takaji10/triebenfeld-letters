The man could view the family home only from the street; its then inhabitants did not agree to let him inside. Georg Breede followed in his father's footsteps and himself became a doctor, and his brother – an architect. Both missed their mother and took the news of her death hard. Yet it took fully twenty years before Georg decided to travel to his mother's home town. Hugo, tormented by memories from the war period, never managed to cope with his pangs of conscience and could not bring himself to come with his brother. He was ashamed because of the errors of his youth.

Wanda Jurkiewiczowa says:

– You see, madam, Dr Breede, father and son, were in my opinion honest people. Georg did not know how to reward all those who looked after his mother after the war. It pained him greatly that she was left alone in Poland. We were together at the grave of his parents and grandmother.

Standing at the grave of the Breede spouses, resting together in the Zagórów cemetery, I cannot resist pondering their story, above all the fate of the tough Doctor's wife. The words of one of Coma's songs appear in my head of their own accord and crawl onto my lips in a whisper: "Only now do I know how low falls he who did not fulfil his time in humility…"[^76].

I carried out most of the conversations that enabled me to attempt a portrait of so ambiguous a figure as Janina Breede over the span of two years (2020–2022). I met my interlocutors in person or conducted telephone conversations. A great help was also the article by Bogdan Grzonkowski entitled "In Memory of Dr Hugo Breede", published in Gazeta Słupecka no. 22(145) of 3. 06. 1993.



[^76]: Coma. *Zaprzepaszczone siły wielkiej armii świętych znaków*, from the album of the same title, 2006.
