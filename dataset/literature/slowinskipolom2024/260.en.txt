This is precisely why I wrote above that this story begins from a definitive end. In 1988 Ida Disterheft was buried, her grave was paid for for the next twenty years, only to be forgotten in the end. When no one came forward any more who would have wanted to let the woman rest in peace, paying the administrative fee for another two decades, her remains were removed from the grave and placed in the ossuary. Perhaps it was meant to be just so, so that Ida could appear in the pages of this book. In this way she has been commemorated, for times and people will pass, and the book will tell of a forgotten woman, wandering even after death, from the Zagórów area, a descendant of a family that had chosen the municipality of Zagórów as its home more than a hundred years before Ida was born. Ida Disterheft died on 7 May 1988 in the Social Welfare Home in Ślesin, where she had been staying since 1983. The home in Ślesin was at that time a branch of the Konin facility. As follows from the death certificate I managed to obtain, the death was reported by Irena Wisznarewska, an employee of the home in Ślesin. When I received Ida's death certificate, I did not yet know that the last address of residence listed in the document was the address of the aforementioned facility, nor that Mrs Wisznarewska was employed there.

The record confirmed my surmises – Ida Disterheft was born in the municipality of Zagórów, precisely in the village of Łazy, on 27 March 1913. Her parents were Gottfryd and Natalia née Helwing. Today I already know that there was no direct kinship between Ida and the occupation mayor of Zagórów, and if there even was, it was very distant.
