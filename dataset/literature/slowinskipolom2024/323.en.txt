#### Protestants in the parish of Zagórów

Summary

The small town of Zagórów, situated in eastern Wielkopolska on the southern rise of the Warta valley, and the surrounding settlements and villages were inhabited for centuries by Poles, Jews and Germans. A symbolic expression of this multicultural region were the three Zagórów houses of worship: the Church of Saints Peter and Paul on the Small Market Square, the synagogue on Średnia Street and the Evangelical-Augsburg Church on Konińska Street. The horrors of the Second World War brutally destroyed this world.

In recent years, numerous works and publications have appeared on the history and the life of the Poles and Jews living in this region. But the history of the town and the parish of Zagórów still remains incomplete. It is extremely difficult to gain access to narratives and documents from the lives of the Protestants who lived in Zagórów and the surrounding villages – for the most part Germans – since few records exist and eyewitnesses can hardly be interviewed any more.

The harsh laws of the Polish state at the end of the war in 1945 meant that little remained of the once numerous, active and economically engaged community.

Hence the picture of the contribution of the German-Evangelical population, which lasted at least two centuries, is largely incomplete. If one looks at the records on the internet, we have only an insight into the period of the Second World War.
