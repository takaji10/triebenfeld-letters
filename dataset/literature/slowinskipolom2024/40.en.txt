# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 40
# translation by the editor's files

From Christmas of 1856, Zagórów became an independent parish, receiving as its first permanent pastor-parish priest Wilhelm Winkler. In the following year the parish purchased two houses at 51 and 52 Konińska Street, in which, after renovation, the parsonage was set up. In connection with the elevation of Zagórów to the rank of a parish, the young heir Weigel declared on 10 January 1858 that he would give 12 morgs of land and brick for the building of a church, if only the parish were transferred to Michalinów Kopoiński. The Consistory rejected this proposal, and on 28 February 1862 it finally approved the building of the church in Zagórów. In the following year, representatives of the Consistory went to Count Taczanowski, the owner of Zagórów, to obtain assistance for the building of the church. The request was granted. Help also came from the Ministry of the Interior, which in 1877 resolved to appropriate 3000 roubles for the building of the church. The successive pastors: Winkler, Eugeniusz Biedermann (in the years 1860-1865), Karol Edward Erdmann held office in Zagórów very briefly. The reasons for their departures were prosaic: an unhealthy and damp parsonage, low pay and unsettled parish affairs – the location of the church and the extent of the parish. Despite the short time of their service in Zagórów, they cannot be accused of idleness – quite the contrary. On 8 May 1878 pastor Karol Edward Erdmann consecrated the foundation stone for the building of the church. On that occasion a church building committee was elected, composed of Aleksander Arndt, Wilhelm Schmechel, Wilhelm Münh, Wilhelm Kuch, Morstin Ziegelchorf, Emmanuel Munk and the members of the Church College. The technical works fell to Przanowski of Słupca, and the master builder was Gustaw Jursch of Orlina Duża. The town of Zagórów contributed from its forest 110 pieces of timber for the building of the church. The construction of the church lasted four years and cost 13 131 roubles 78 kopecks. The solemn consecration took place on the first Sunday of Advent in 1882.
