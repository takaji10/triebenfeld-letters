# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 237
# translation by the editor's files

 before Christmas 1945, the town of Demmin (Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania). The exact route between Szczecin and Demmin is unknown to me. Closed transports in railway goods wagons at that time went via Szczecin or via Kohlfurt south-east of Görlitz to the territories lying west of the Oder and Neisse. The next day we were quartered on an estate in Poggendorf. Here I went to school until the end of January 1946. A few days later, probably on 4 February, we travelled by train through Dresden (Dresden, because of the hostilities, lay entirely in ruins) to Nordhausen in the southern Harz. After a night spent in a camp situated outside the town we made a day-long march across the border which then existed, from the Russian zone into the British zone. There the English troops were waiting for us. We went by lorry to the Friedland camp, and then by train, via Hanover and Hamburg, we reached Bad Segeberg. After a day's stay in the Bad Segeberg camp and registration we arrived in Rendsburg. From then on Rendsburg was to become, for us and for the Runge family, our home for the years to come.
