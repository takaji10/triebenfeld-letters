# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 240
# translation by the editor's files

 a small centre for the inhabitants of the nearer surroundings. A weekly market was held here, bigger shopping was done, confirmation classes were attended, and news was exchanged. We went to the market square to park there. We walked to the church in which my parents were confirmed in 1940 and 1942. The church was open, so we were able to stay there for a longer while. Then we went to the building in which the Rau/Janke family once lived. We lingered there for a moment, and passing the church once more, we returned to the market square. On the site of the former warriors' monument (Kriegerdenkmal) there was a square with flowers.

After a short coffee break we drove to Łazińsk, a few kilometres away to the south. As far as Grabina the road was still in good condition, but beyond it only sandy tracks led on. We drove through a countryside whose landscape had changed greatly since my parents' departure in 1947. Small trees had grown up; where large ones had been cut down, the next generation had sprung up. The whole landscape seemed altered and in different dimensions. But appearances were deceptive. Nothing here had become smaller or larger. Driving along the sandy track that ran through a field overgrown with rye, we caught sight of the farmstead on which my grandmother and grandfather farmed. We turned off the sandy track and, along a grass-grown way, reached my grandparents' former property. We parked the car in the entrance to the farmyard.

At last the moment had come of which I had constantly thought and of which I had hoped that one day it would come. My wish to see the homeland of my ancestors was fulfilled. Across the grass-grown farmyard we approached the entrance and knocked at the door. But there was no one there. The door was not locked. We wondered whether to go in, but in the end we did not. The owners were absent. We only walked around the house and looked inside through the windows. At the kitchen window stood the coffee grinder with which my grandmother ground coffee. It had survived all these years and surely still worked. After photographing the rear of the house facing the well-kept orchard, we walked back onto the farmyard in order to view the barn and the pigsties and to capture the impressions in photographs.
