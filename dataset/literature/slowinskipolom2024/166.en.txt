Two letters to the brother Gustav survive from January. On the twentieth he writes, deeply weighed down by illness, how he spent New Year's Eve. "At 22 I was able to vanish behind one of the snow-covered hills and, praying, receive the year 1943 from the hand of God". Bruno is troubled by the state of his brother's spirit, one who carries some great burden and who stands between him and God. Since that burden did not succeed in being drowned with the old year, let him heed a brother's advice and go to the pastor. "He will summon the wonderful, divine power (which Christ transmits to him together with his office) and will name the sin by its name. And then it will turn out that that sin, were it ever so great and filthy, will have to yield". An honest conversation is necessary, and the beginning is always heavy.

Just as in the earlier letter to his sister, so here too we see the great zeal of the candidate for a future pastor in the religious, spiritual formatting of his siblings. It is difficult to determine what to call Gustav's sin (if – as Bruno writes – it is a sin), but perhaps these travails of the brother may be an echo of the ban on administering baptisms, funerals or direct work at the services. "But there is an answer to it, which you will find in the Letter to the Romans 8,35". He does not, however, quote it. Let us recall: "Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? Shall tribulation, or distress, or persecution"… (Rom 8,38) and the further verses which say that no powers "nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord" (Rom 8,39)[^59].
