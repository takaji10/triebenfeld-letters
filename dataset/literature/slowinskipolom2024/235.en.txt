# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 235
# translation by the editor's files

 Russian soldiers or other strangers appeared, we hid from them as a precaution, because it was not known whether these strangers had come with bad or good intentions. We were not spared looting.

On 20 February 1945 our father was taken away. He shared the fate of the other German men. It was said that these men had been taken to road works in the Konin area. Shortly afterwards they were transported to the then Soviet Union. There they were held in captivity near Moscow and worked at peat extraction. The conditions in the camp were catastrophic. A dysentery epidemic broke out there, and many of them died, among them my father. Willi Wall, an acquaintance from Łazińsk, survived the forced labour in Russia and returned to Germany at the end of 1949. He recounted that my father died in the night of 10 April 1945. In April 1945 we were expropriated. At first it was said that this was a temporary measure. Our farmstead was taken over by the Męch family, which had five children. Their eldest son Marian later inherited the farmstead. From then on my mother, my sister, Aunt Lidia and I lived in the part in which grandfather had his life tenancy (grandfather died on 30.1.1945). And so, from one day to the next, on our own land we were merely tolerated and dependent on the whims of the new owner. We fed ourselves, among other things, on products we had buried in the ground. Milk was given to us by the new owner.

In the village (Grabina) there was now a Polish municipality. We were living in Poland again. The official decrees now bore the coat of arms and the name of the new Polish state and were addressed to us, the German population living in the area. There, in harsh terms, they enumerated what we were not allowed to do. From June 1945 until the expulsion I lived with the Pietrala family. (Maria and Józef Pietrala in the village of Osiny – translator's note J.K.). There I made friends with their daughter (Teresa Krystyna – note by J.K.), who was younger than me. Later, too, I kept in touch with her by letter. She married Mr Langwiński (Wacław – note by J.K.), bore five daughters, and ran with her husband a farmstead which had earlier belonged to my uncle Emil (on Łazińsk; before the Langwińskis the Fagasiński family lived there – note by J.K.).
