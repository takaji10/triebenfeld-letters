# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 242
# translation by the editor's files

After a short while we drove to Trąbczyn, to the place where, after losing their family homes, my parents worked and lived with a Polish family until their departure for Germany[^83]. Towards evening we set off in the direction of Poznań, crossing the lovely Warta valley over the then still existing wooden bridge next to the monastery at Ląd. At the petrol station before the town there was a long queue of cars. We had no choice but to wait, since we held petrol vouchers which we had to redeem. At that time in Poland there was no other way of obtaining petrol or diesel. The next day we toured the centre of Poznań and enjoyed the wonderful, almost summery weather, in order to set out towards Hanover the following day. The drive to the border passed without problems. At about two o'clock we arrived at the border with the former GDR at Świecko near Frankfurt an der Oder. After the Polish border guards had stamped our passports, nothing happened for a long time. On the opposite side several Polish cars were waiting, returning to Poland from the Federal Republic of Germany packed full with bags. One of the cars was towing a trailer loaded with tyres. It was calm at the border. The car standing before us was quickly cleared. Just as I thought that, since the check was proceeding so smoothly, we would soon be home, a Polish customs officer approached us. After returning our passports, we were subjected to a thorough inspection; every piece of our luggage was checked very carefully. After all these "formalities" we could finally continue our journey home with relief.

*Translated by Jacek Kin*



[^83]: In the case of the Modelski family – I know this from the accounts of Günter's father, whom I met in person in Germany in the year 1989 – translator's note.
