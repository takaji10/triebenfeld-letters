| 1746 | Paul von Kolno-Prusinski, owner of the Trabczyn estate, concludes an hereditary lease contract with the German-Evangelical settlers (also called Hauländers or Holländers) and grants them the wooded land lying to the south for cultivation. Until 1838 church records are archived in: Trabczyn, Grodziec, Konin, Wreschen, Zychlin.<br>Paul von Kolno-Prusinski, owner of the Trąbczyn estate, concludes with the German Evangelical settlers (also called Hauländers or Holländers [Holendrzy]) a hereditary lease agreement and grants them the wooded land lying to the south in order to bring it under cultivation. Until the year 1838 church records are archived in the localities of: Trąbczyn, Grodziec, Konin, Września, Żychlin. |
| 1793-1807 | After the 2nd Partition of Poland, the Kalisch Land – in which Zagorow lies – comes to Prussia and belongs until 1807 to the Chamber-Department of South Prussia. The 5 estates in the Zagorow area (Trombczyn, Nowa-Wies, Oswiery, Lazy and Szlewek) are confiscated by Prussia and given to the Prince zu Hohenlohe-Oehringen (Ingelfingen). He sells half to Karl Gottlieb Weigel, a great landowner from Hennersdorf in Silesia and lord of Kopoyno; the rest was designated for settlement purposes. |
