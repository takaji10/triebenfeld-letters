# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 279
# translation by the editor's files

#### Janke went to war but never reached the front

Not all the local Germans behaved badly towards Poles during the war. Janke was a blacksmith and a driver; he lived near the shrine on Konińska Street. Before the war he was good; he had a son, Hubert. Together with the German Henke he took part in the deportation of the Jews. Then he went to war, but he never reached the front. He was killed when partisans in the Lublin region blew up the railway tracks and German soldiers died.

#### Rydel and Krygier always helped us

Rydel, on the other hand, a Zagórów man of German origin, saw what he wanted to see – he truly favoured the Poles. He worked at the gendarmerie post, and when someone sent a parcel to a person under arrest, all it took was for Rydel to be there. The sexton Albert Krygier was also a good man. With some German families during the occupation the Poles had good relations. Once I went to the Rydels, the butchers, and played with their children, Irenka and Hans. I could always fill myself up at their place.

#### Dr Breede never refused help to the poor

Once we got fume-poisoned, and we went to that doctor for help. He was good; he never asked about money. His wife, née Stawicka, was a teacher by profession. The Breede sons belonged to the Hitlerjugend. That organization had its seat in what is now the building of the Social Welfare Home. Before the war it was Dr Lidmanowski's hospital. The juvenile fascists once entered the crypt beneath the Catholic church, searched for treasures, opened coffins and desecrated the place.
