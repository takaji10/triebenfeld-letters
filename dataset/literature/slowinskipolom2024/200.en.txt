# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 200
# translation by the editor's files

– He had a premonition that if he ended up in the East, the Russians would kill him there. And if they came here, to Zagórów, he would lose his life that way too. So if he can still choose, he prefers to die in his own home – Pluciński related to Andrzej Bernat.

Hugo Breede, however, probably did not suspect that death would outpace the Soviets and pay him a final visit in the very same year in which the conscious infection took place. The Doctor's wife was widowed in the winter of 1941. From then on she managed the house on Berdychów Street alone. Once again I give the floor to Halina Cieślewiczowa:

– The school they attended stood on Kościelna Street, where the primary school was. And my neighbour, Mrs Matelska, recounted that once, as she was walking past that school, one of the German pupils ran up and knocked the beret off her head.

I can guess what the aforementioned Matelska may have felt. Humiliation, anger at the cub's prank, and at the same time fear, effectively stifling the reflex of protest in defence of her own dignity. And suddenly something happens that she does not expect:

– One of the Breede brothers, who saw this, seized his schoolmate firmly by the nape of the neck and ordered: "Pick it up and apologise to her! She is my neighbour and you must show her respect. It does no harm that she is a Pole!".

A brave gesture, I think to myself. The boys, indoctrinated in the Hitlerjugend, had nevertheless retained some human reflexes, contrary to the principles then drummed into them.

– All in all, they were not bad boys… – Mrs Halina agrees with me. – It was only the times that were bad for everyone…

Unfortunately, I did not hear more testimonies of good deeds towards Poles on the part of the young men. I only know that both sons of the doctor couple struggled to the end of their lives with the memories and pangs of conscience caused by their membership of the aforementioned youth organisation.
