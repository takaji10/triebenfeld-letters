Unfortunately, it did not pass without further difficulties. On 9 February 1837 the Protestants of Lisew declared that they had their own house of prayer and pastors from Grodziec, and that consequently they did not wish to belong to the Zagórów parish. The faithful of Chruściki, Holendry Tomickie, Pyzdry and Ciemierów did likewise. Despite the schism, the Protestants of Zagórów itself and the nearer surroundings resolved to bring about the founding of a branch in Zagórów and the election of a church college. The final and legal establishment of the branch congregation did not come until 1843. In the meantime, services were held by pastor Scholz of Żychlin in rented premises. In 1841 the mayor of Zagórów declared that Zagórów possessed 3924 Evangelical souls and ought to be a parish with its own church. For the time being, it was decided to move the branch from Zagórów to Łazińsk, which had a newly built house of prayer (1838-1839) able to hold 300 persons.

The heiress of Trąbczyn, Michalina Mączyńska, declared to superintendent von Modl in 1846 that she undertook to render assistance in the form of grain and fuel, provided that a church arose at Łazińsk and a pastor exercised his ministry there. On 6 July 1846 von Modl came to Łazińsk and established that indeed the local Protestants wished to have their religious centre here. This time the faithful of Koszuty, Młodojewo and Kosewo decidedly opposed the location of the church at Łazińsk, and consequently the Consistory[^15] did not give its consent to the earlier proposal supported by von Modl. In 1850 a building was purchased at 54 Konińska Street, one part of which was destined for a house of prayer, and the other for a school and the cantor's dwelling. Until Christmas of 1856, Zagórów was administered by pastor Karl Friedrich Orive, parish priest of Grodziec.



[^15]: Consistory – the council standing at the head of a diocese or a Church.
