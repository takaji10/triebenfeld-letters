After the grievous experience of the winter war of 1939/1940 with the Red Army and the loss of 10 per cent of its territory – in October 1940 Finland concludes an agreement on military and economic cooperation with the Third Reich. From that time the Germans transfer their units to the territories of Lapland. In May 1941 Finland officially becomes an ally of the Third Reich. On 25 June it declares war on the USSR. The German-Finnish offensive in the north begins – just as the newspapers had earlier announced! – in June, precisely on 29 June. General Eduard Dietl, commanding the 20th Mountain Army (Armee Lappland), gives the order to attack. The campaign was given the codename *Silberfuchs* (Silver Fox). As the Finns themselves put it, for them it was a war of continuation – they wanted to regain the territories lost to the Russians.

The Germans wanted something more. The main objective of the operation was the port of Murmansk and, in the first place, the severing of the route between Murmansk and Petrozavodsk and Leningrad. For the Red Army the railway line by which military equipment arriving from the USA and England was transported to the port of Murmansk had key strategic importance. Finnish Petsamo, Russian Pieczenga, where large forces were concentrated, is a settlement lying on the river of the same name, which flows into a fjord whose beginning lies 5 km from the Barents Sea.

From later letters we learn that Mielke was stationed a few kilometres from Petsamo – in Parkkino. In this small locality, situated on the river Petsamojoki, sappers built a bridge through which the entire supply of the attacking troops passed. The task of the company in which Bruno Mielke served may have included, among other things, the guarding of this bridge. The attack of the combined Finnish and German formations did not succeed.
