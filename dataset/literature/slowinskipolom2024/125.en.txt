# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 125
# translation by the editor's files

 many of them, after all, fought in September 1939 in the Polish Army, because they were citizens of Poland, the country in which they had their lands, homes, families, their heritage, a country that was their homeland. In life there is not only black and white; there are, rather, many — even very many — shades of grey. Recognized as Germans, they had to submit to the realities prevailing in occupied Poland. During the Second World War Erwin worked in the German police in Łódź. There he met his wife Wanda. In the winter of 1945, like many Germans or those recognized as Germans or recognizing themselves as such, Erwin Beutler's family — consisting of himself, his wife, twin boys wrapped in blankets and a little daughter — set off westward, fleeing the Red Army, of whose cruelties legends circulated. During the flight one of the twins died of cold and hunger, which led the Beutlers to decide to return home. Only after the war did Erwin Beutler and his family leave for Germany, to Burgdorf in Niedersachsen, where Erwin died in March 1990.

Alfred Beutler, born in Dąbrowica in 1913 — son of Adolf, another victim of the elemental force of war. Alfred served in the Polish Army and as a Polish soldier was demobilized in 1939. One of the cards he sent to the family contains his photograph in a Polish uniform, and it was sent on the occasion of a name day, which shows the extent to which the minority of German origin had taken up Polish customs. In Germany name days are not observed — birthdays are. The irony of fate and the mad ideologies decreed that, in order to confirm his loyalty to the new, no longer Polish authority, Alfred was conscripted into the German army. The hell that broke loose at the end of the war claimed young Alfred's life, alas. Only in 1952 did the family learn what had become of him. A letter sent to Alfred's brother Arnulf informs that Alfred Beutler died on 17.04.1945 in Austria — the car in which he was travelling drove onto a mine, and Alfred was killed on the spot. His cousin, the grandson of Andreas the teacher from Święcia, also Alfred Beutler, likewise served in the Polish Army; he managed to leave the occupied territories, distinguished himself in valour at Monte Cassino, and for this was decorated with the order of Virtuti Militari.
