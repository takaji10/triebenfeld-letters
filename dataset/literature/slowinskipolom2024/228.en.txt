# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 228
# translation by the editor's files

#### Who am I?

It is not easy for me to answer, although people often ask me this question… To answer so simply, where is my Homeland?… I do not know… I cannot answer unambiguously… I have spent most of my life there, in Germany. My children and grandchildren were born there. I probably feel more at home there… But my roots are in Poland… my childhood. Everything connected with those places and those memories is very important to me. It is my identity. I gladly come here.

#### My Poland in need

What I went through in post-war Poland made me think that perhaps I would not be able to face the past. But when I finally did, when I came to Poland for the first time after the war, I was very glad that it had happened. That I came to this land where I was born. Solidarność gave me the reason, and then martial law. I began to collect gifts, to prepare transports of them, in order to help those in need in my Zagórów region. I did everything that was within the reach of my modest means… I gained new friends and the joy of life. For in giving there is much joy… Years later I received for this activity a federal medal in Germany, as well as a beautiful letter of thanks from the Zagórów authorities, of which I shall quote a fragment: "We remember and appreciate your commitment to delivering gifts for poor families from our area, your help in seeking work beyond the western border in a time so difficult for Poland as the fall of the communist system and the beginnings of capitalism. Particularly valuable and significant for us remains the fact that the aid which flowed in thanks to your efforts was not intended only for Protestants, but extended simply to people in need" (Zagórów–Detmold 2017).
