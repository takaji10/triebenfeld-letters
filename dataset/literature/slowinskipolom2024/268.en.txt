 managed to catch a bit of it. To this day I remember how Dolkowa taught us: *Bitte, Frau, kann ich raus gehen*? That means “please, Miss, may I go out?”, when we wanted to go to the toilet, – Grandma recounted. – But Klaudia (Grandma's later sister-in-law) caught nothing, couldn't learn. Dolkowa would always say to her: “you're the biggest one, and you can't do anything”. Maybe that's why she dug her heels in, because it was her mother who was taken to dig trenches, her father was deported, and Pałucka (Klaudia's aunt) had been assigned to look after them. Dalke. Mrs Dalke, but when our parents said “Dolkowa”, then I said it too.

There were many such conversations. We returned to the subject of Mrs Dalke several, if not a dozen or so, times. I very much wanted to learn her first name. By pure chance – although the cemetery in Lądek is small, I had nevertheless never ventured into that part of it – I came upon the grave of Genowefa Dalke. Thanks to this I had several new pieces of information, but confirmation was still needed that this was indeed the person Grandma recalls. Here again Grandma came to the rescue during a further conversation. Grandma said about her teacher as much as she knew, but she often added new facts, such as the story of how, as children, they went to the park adjoining the palace in Lądek to gather chestnuts, which Mrs Dolkowa would then pour into sacks. The sacks were carted off somewhere so that someone else could grind them into meal, and from it produce glue. The last thing Grandma remembers is the location of the house in which Mrs Dalke lived during the war, a house that still stands.

– You must ask Bronia when she comes, because they lived on the Wielodwór, right next door, after all.

One day I came into Grandma's and found Mrs Bronia sitting at the table drinking coffee. Ten years younger than Grandma, Bronisława Kowalska, a friend of Grandma's born during the war. It was in her grandparents' house, the Sieczkarkis', that the Dalke family was billeted after the war.

– Mrs Bronia, does the surname Dalke mean anything to you? Genowefa Dalke. – Does it mean anything? Of course, – an animated Mrs Bronia replies. – She lived later with the children in that old house that belonged to my grandparents
