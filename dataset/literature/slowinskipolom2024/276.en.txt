#### “For my mother's funeral I invited the female pastor”

For the funeral of my mother, Irena née Weber, I did not invite a Catholic priest, but the female pastor Halina Radacz, closest in outlook to my mother. The grave of Anna Maria Grymin, one of our ancestresses (Grandma was a Grymin by birth), lay right by the wall of the Protestant cemetery. We used to place little lights there for as long as that grave of green-tinged sandstone stood. Two years ago we could no longer find it…

#### The Beutlers always felt and were Poles

##### From a letter by Alicja Beutler of 2009

My father Alfred Beutler was the second youngest of the seven children of Emilia and Emil Beutler. Four of the older ones emigrated in 1911-1912 to the United States. Only Regina, my father and the youngest, Adolf, remained in Poland. Both brothers took an active part in the Volunteer Fire Brigade in Zagórów. They all felt and were Poles. My father was in the POW, fought in the Legions; from his stories I remember that he was probably in the II Brigade. He was discharged from the army only after the Polish-Bolshevik war, in 1922. My father was in the signals, had the rank of corporal; in his platoon he had above all volunteer students from Warsaw. I know that he received the “Medal for Lida”; I saw the award certificate. (…)

#### My father had to undertake to raise the children in the Catholic faith

My parents were married during one of his leaves from the army, on 18 November 1919. At the wedding in Lądek Father made a “declaration of faith” – he was a Protestant, but he undertook to raise the children in the Catholic faith.
