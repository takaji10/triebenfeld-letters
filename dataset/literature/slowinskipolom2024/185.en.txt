For a whole week now we have been papering the walls and working on their beautification. The cold and the darkness no longer weary one so. A little heating suffices and at 23 o'clock it is still grey. Soon there will be no night. Outdoors there is a thaw, and in some places last year's moss is showing". In the next paragraph he shares with his brother a joyful piece of news. Namely, on 20 April, the Führer's birthday, he was promoted to the rank of corporal (Obergefreiter). True, after two years of service that is nothing great, but dear to the heart all the same. He regards it also as a testimony of the grace of God, which has blessed his service and above all preserved him from all obstacles. As a corporal he also held his first service of worship in the melting snow. A clump of moss served him as an altar. "This hour in solitude allows one to see one's weaknesses". The sight of comrades drinking and carousing all night also fills him with sadness. He is almost ashamed that he sits here in the north listening to the rustle of the pines, while his *Kameraden* (comrades) must wage heavy fighting on the southern front. "In comparison with them – we are here on furlough", and at once he adds – "may it not get worse". He also has luck with his superiors. The commander of his company is Oberleutnant Hengl. He is the son of the Generalleutnant Ritter von Hengl commanding the 2nd Mountain Division[^65].

Bruno has luck with his commanders, but has no luck with girls. He writes that he "foundered" with Anna Steinbart and now links his hopes with Elizabeth Kaschuba, who is trying to creep into his heart. "Not ugly, educated, an excellent housekeeper". [^65]: General of mountain troops Georg Ritter von Hengl was a distinguished professional officer. During the First World War he fought, among other places, at Verdun and in Russia. Later, as an aviator, he shot down 10 English aircraft. For his war merits in his native kingdom of Bavaria he was granted a title of nobility. On the Arctic front in Russia, in the year 1943, he commanded the 2nd Mountain Division, and in the year 1944 – the period from which Bruno Mielke's letters survive – he commanded the XIX Mountain Corps. They were stationed in the Petsamo/Pechenga area. He was taken prisoner in May 1945 in Norway. Wikipedia. Accessed 23.02.2023, 23:40.
