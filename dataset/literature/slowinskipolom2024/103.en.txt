##### The cantors' school on Łazińsk

Łazińsk was the oldest Evangelical settlement and the largest kantorat in the parish of Zagórów, hence the impressive number of pupils of the cantors' school – around 160 children. It was the largest of the Evangelical schools in the territory of the parish, and its owner was the collective body of the cantorate, the inhabitants of the village of Łazińsk, German colonists. Like all the others, it was initially a denominational school with German as the language of instruction. The first school building was erected in the years 1838–1839 using bricks and a building material very characteristic of the Hauländer settlements – bog iron ore.

Wishing to attract and encourage the teachers to more intensive work, the inhabitants of the village placed at the teachers' disposal 20 morgi and 218 rods of land and built the farm buildings: a barn, a stable and a woodshed. The teacher's dwelling, consisting of two rooms and a kitchen, was likewise located in the building of the Evangelical chapel. There are no exact data on the development of the school, on the names of the teachers and their periods of service. The last teacher, Gustav Adolf Mielke, held the post of teacher and headmaster from 1932 – we learn this from the Chronicle of the Public School on Łazińsk, kept by Wincenty Magryta in 1946. Mielke also served in the settlement as cantor.

The erection of the next building, in which classes were held on the eve of the outbreak of the Second World War and during the occupation, the inhabitants of the village owe to the efforts of Gustav Mielke. The new building consisted of three classrooms and the headmaster's office. "The Evangelical religious brotherhoods contributed their funds to the building of the school […]" – writes Wincenty Magryta in 1946, who was then acting as the school's headmaster. In 1932 Gustav Mielke was stripped of the post of headmaster on account of "overzealousness in cultivating Germandom" and transferred to Galicia[^43]. He returned to his post after 1939 and taught until he was called up to the army 


[^43]: According to the memoirs of Eugenia Plitt.
