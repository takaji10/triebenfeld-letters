# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 226
# translation by the editor's files

In the spring of 1950 we were sent to the Friedland transit camp in Lower Saxony, in West Germany. From there we children were distributed among relatives who were already in Germany. My sister Frieda and I ended up in Ostharingen in the Harz mountains, with our aunt Emilia. I began work there as a farmhand. I worked with a horse and a plough. The experience of working with horses at the state farm (PGR) in Poland came in useful. I liked this work, and the farmer was happy that he had "got" me. But in time I decided to leave. I went to Detmold in North Rhine-Westphalia to see my brother Helmut. He helped me with the formalities, and I was given a guardian, since I was not yet twenty-one. I started working. With the money I earned I paid for courses, so as to further my education. I learned cabinetmaking. My training and apprenticeship lasted three years. I then added a specialist course in furniture making. I could earn more. And I was good at it. I had more and more design ideas of my own and more and more clients.

#### Edith

I was lucky to have found precisely that guardian, because later I married his daughter Edith, and to this day I am growing old happily with her. (Edith Kuhn, b. 10.12.1934, passed to the Lord on 21.08.2022, after the work on the article had been completed).

#### Returns

When I left Poland I knew Polish well, but in time I forgot almost everything. Years later, when I returned to my home region, I could manage only a few simple sentences. They helped me in enquiring about the places of my childhood. Home, the real one, the most enduring one, remained in Poland, on Łazińsk, among my trees, dunes, tastes and smells… Already as an adult I enrolled in Detmold in a Polish language course. I attended the course for two years, twice a month for two hours.
