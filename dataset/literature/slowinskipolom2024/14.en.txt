 Hakatists and the Jewish Bundists[^5] are decidedly hostile towards the Polish administration. Irrespective of nationality or religion, all become citizens of the Commonwealth. Rudolf Poschitzki of Olchowo has, in a document, his nationality recorded as German, and his language as Polish. His grandparents and father use the Polonized version of the surname, Poszycki. He served in the years 1931–1933 in the Polish Army; mobilized in August 1939, he writes a fine page as a soldier of the Poznań Army in the battles on the Bzura[^6]. Among the documents discovered in the house of the cantor in Łazińsk there is a letter of Gustav Tonna from Płatkownica to Bruno Mielke, which also shows a deeper reflection on the presence and life of the Germans, of the Evangelicals, on this land. From the letter one can infer that Bruno was rejected by the education authorities, or that the right to teach was refused him. [^5]: Bundists – members of the General Jewish Workers' Bund in Poland, Russia and Lithuania (the Bund – the party operated in the years 1897-1948). They regarded the Jews as a separate nation, striving to create secular schooling and to develop culture in the Yiddish language, which was to strengthen their autonomy. They took part in various formations of the international movement of socialists and social democrats. They criticized the Bolshevik government of the USSR and at the same time urged cooperation with it. They were determined opponents of the Zionists and of the religious movement Agudas Israel. Representatives of both of these currents were present in Zagórów.

    Hakata – the League of the Eastern Marches (1894-1934), operating under Bismarck's patronage, regarded the Germanization of the Polish lands under Prussian partition as its principal aim. They had the means and a programme for driving the Poles out of their land by every possible method. After Hitler's seizure of power, most of the members joined the organization created by the NSDAP, the Bund Deutscher Osten (League of Germans in the East). HaKaTa – an abbreviation of the surnames of the organization's founders: Hansemann, Kenemann, Tiedemann.

[^6]: Michalski Bogdan, *Imiona zapomnianych mogił*. In: Zagórów – miejsce z historią, accessed 27.09.2023, 21:35.
