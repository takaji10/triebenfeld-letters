I recognized her thanks to the film *People from the Iron Houses*, which was made in 2014 by Wika and Przemek Kowalski of Pyzdry. Her name was Margeritte Todt. At the end of the war she managed to flee from the Russians, and after the war, in Germany, she organized regular meetings with her former pupils, who came from these villages. Young German women who had completed their pedagogical studies were given work assignments during the war. They were sent precisely to villages like ours.

#### Treibball…

And the match in Orlina was won by our Trąbczyn school! It was not football, it was Treibball. A kind of team game with a smaller ball, like a volleyball. You had to be able to catch it well, throw it far and run fast in order to win. I was short, so nobody really counted on me. I was sent in last, but it was I who threw the ball the farthest behind the opponents' line and ran very well. We won! I remember we walked to Orlina and back on foot, there was no transport of any kind. It was quite a long way, some 5–6 kilometres. In Orlina Duża there was a narrow-gauge railway station; we did not have far to go to it, but the school only used it when the destination of the school trip was somewhere farther away, e.g. in Poznań. We would take the little railway to Jarocin and from there an ordinary train.

#### War

When the war broke out I was still a child. I did not really know what was happening, but fear appeared… The Germans pressed on, the Poles defended themselves. There were killings too. I once heard that somewhere nearby a German had been killed, and then a Pole. I was afraid… sometimes I slept away from home, somewhere out in the field… Everyone was afraid.
