# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 261
# translation by the editor's files

 and all of them were of the Protestant faith. Protestant settlement in the municipality of Zagórów developed particularly in the first decade of the nineteenth century thanks to Prince Friedrich Wilhelm von Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, who on the Zagórów lands granted to him by the King of Prussia founded new villages and settled colonists from Germany in them. It was most probably at that time that Ida's ancestors also came to these parts, as the entries in the registers of vital records confirm. I had the death certificate, the place of birth and the name of the reporting person. The search then stalled for a good few years. Still, Ida Disterheft kept returning to my thoughts – now on the occasion of visits to the cemetery and further visits to the ossuary, now during walks through the Protestant cemetery in Zagórów – or she would appear quite without any apparent cause, as if she were determined to remind me of herself and to motivate me to further searching. Ida's efforts proved effective. I also learned that the name of the street of Miss Disterheft's last place of residence had been changed, the numbering had changed too, and that was probably what had discouraged me earlier from continuing the search.

Very often, however, it is the case that the most obvious and simplest solutions are within arm's reach. So it was this time as well, for one day, as I was idly browsing Facebook pages, the name of a group I regularly look through struck me: Historia Gminy Ślesin (The History of the Ślesin Municipality). Of course! It dawned on me that after all I could ask the group's members about the address and about Irena Wisznarewska, and knowing already the potential of the Internet and Facebook, I could be sure the action would end in success. As I thought, so I did, and at express speed I had a lead on Magdalena Wisznarewska, who turned out to be Irena's daughter. Here enormous thanks are due precisely to Magdalena Wisznarewska, who took an interest in the story of Ida Disterheft, all the more so as her mother could not remember such a person. It is hard to blame Mrs Irena, if over the years of her work at the care home dozens of people came under her care. Almost everything we know today about Ida is the merit of Magdalena Wisznarewska.
