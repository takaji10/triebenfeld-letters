There were about a hundred wedding guests in Dąbrowica, and very fine cakes for the occasion were baked by the neighbour and friend of the Schwemmer family, Rudolf Tews. After the wedding the newlyweds settled in Zarzewek, where Reinhard took over the mill and the folwark from his father Edmund Schwemmer, who had died in 1939. Mrs Gertruda worked as a bookkeeper in the office at the mill. During the war, and already after its end, the children of Gertruda of the Beutlers and Reinhard of the Schwemmers were born: Richard, Edmund, Roman, Lila, Edwin and Renata. To this day Gertruda and her husband are remembered in Zarzewek and the vicinity very warmly. They harmed no one, and no neighbour harmed them — after all, they were their own; they were at home.

The Waleriańczyk family from Zastruże recalls that during the war, as children, they often received from Mrs Schwemmer flour and semolina beyond the ration-card allotment, by which the miller's wife exposed herself to consequences from the German authorities. When in 1945 Gertruda too was arrested and spent six months in prison in Konin, after which her trial as a "German woman" was to take place, Mrs Waleriańczyk testified in favour of Gertruda Schwemmer, deposing: Mrs Schwemmer saved my children from death by starvation. This testimony made it possible to release Gertruda from prison. Fate, at the beginning of the married life of the Schwemmer couple, painfully tested the couple, for their firstborn son Richard died at barely three months of age. The doctor brought from Zagórów kept watch over the dying child all night, but the boy could not be saved. Moments of terror were experienced by Gertruda Schwemmer in 1945, when Soviet soldiers burst into their house in Zarzewek, and in the flat there were only she and a neighbour. The fate of thousands of women, especially of German origin, who during the war were brutally raped and often murdered, would have awaited them.
