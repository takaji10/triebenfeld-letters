### History enchanted in photography – the Brudler family


Damian Kruczkowski

Two young people gaze calmly into the lens with the seriousness that was customary in those days. The couple is dressed modestly but elegantly. She is wearing a long dark gown that conceals almost her entire body. Delicate jewellery betokening a certain prosperity, above all a long chain, as well as her hair done up in the fashion of the early twentieth century, lend the woman in the photograph a certain dignity. The man has his arm around the woman, and on his handsome face a carefully trimmed moustache is visible. The young man is dressed in a long dark frock coat, a white shirt and a light-coloured cravat. Either of them could pass for passengers aboard the greatest and supposedly unsinkable transatlantic liner of its day, the RMS Titanic. But the photograph dates from 1913, when the floating colossus Titanic had already been resting at the bottom of the ocean for nearly a year. The photograph, meanwhile, shows inhabitants of the Łazińsk in the municipality of Zagórów, Ferdinand and Pauline née Haupt Brudler.

When work on this book began, materials began arriving from many quarters and from various people: documents, photographs, notes, finished texts. One of those people was Daniel Brudler, who sent the photograph described above depicting his relatives. When Katarzyna Połom and I saw this couple, we thought at once that it had to find its way into the book, as yet another testimony about those who once settled the Zagórów land, on which they were born, lived, worked and died. We asked Daniel Brudler for permission to publish the photograph and for more information about the Brudler family. He gladly agreed and shared what he had managed to gather about his family.
