### Where there is a border, there is also a road. A conversation with Erich Kuhn


Wiesława Kowalska

Erich Kuhn, elder of the Evangelical-Augsburg congregation in Detmold in Germany, a former resident of Łazińsk in the Zagórów municipality. For his services to cooperation between Poles and Germans, in 2017 he received from the German state authorities the "Medal of Merit for Work towards International Understanding". Erich Kuhn is a descendant of the Hauländers who founded the village of Łazińsk in 1746 on the lands of the Trąbczyn estate, belonging to the spouses Paweł and Katarzyna Prusińska née Rozdrażewska. Erich is not sure where his ancestors came from. It could have been Pomerania, Brandenburg or Silesia. They were certainly Protestants and used German in their everyday life. They were free peasants with great skill in farming difficult terrain: dry or waterlogged, which is why the landowners were happy to bring in a group of Hauländers, granting them the hereditary lease of unused land. Erich Kuhn's family lived in Łazińsk without interruption for about 200 years. When the Russians entered in 1945, the men from the Hauländer families were deported to Russian labour camps, and by decision of the new communist authorities the Hauländer families, after the confiscation of all their possessions, became homeless, without means of subsistence and without any rights. A similar situation befell all the Hauländers of German origin of the Pyzdry Forest who remained in their homeland after 1945. Today Erich tells his story.
