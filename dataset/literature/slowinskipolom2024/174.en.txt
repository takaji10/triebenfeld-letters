# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 174
# translation by the editor's files

On 4 March the "enjoying of quiet" in the rear comes to an end. An urgent order arrives rousing the Volksdeutsche (Germans from Poland, that is, the Wartheland) to report to the 2nd battalion. Straight after taking up their positions he sends the family a short message. At first they complained that, laden down, they had had to move in a blizzard. But the commander sent sledges for them, fed them well and at once distributed them to the advanced combat posts. After they had been disposed of in this way, one can see that they constituted a filling of the personnel shortfalls in the companies caused by the losses suffered. Bruno Mielke is assigned as an operator to man the heavy machine gun and is given as helper the private Atze. This is a weapon of the immediate field of combat.

There has survived the only description of a battle in the entire collection of letters. "From 12 at night (from Friday to Saturday) our heavy weapons were made ready (artillery, mortars, anti-aircraft guns). It is unbelievable how everything roars, rolls, seethes and bubbles, and when the shells whiz over to the other side, carrying death and destruction. Again – as always – there were many fallen and wounded on both sides (among the Russians far more). Were all ready for death? I think of it in my spirit and this is always for me the most important question. One more thing is astonishing, which I heard today: the Russian prisoners testified that the Russians in those same days were planning an attack on our positions, only we forestalled them. Yet one feels so wonderfully the care and love of God".

One thing is certain – Mielke was in that hell. On the Day of Remembrance, the first day of spring, 21 March, he writes that the lack of leave wearies him, as does the uncertainty whether the information passed on that he would get one will be confirmed. He wants to be with his family for the Feast and hopes that his brother will celebrate the wedding for which everyone is waiting. The day is splendid, sunny and comparatively quiet. The Russians have not yet answered their last attack of 15 March. But presumably they can no longer. Presumably it is as Hermann Göring announced, that the adversary is fighting with its last reserves. And we "through the totalisation of the war can field one more army.
