It is said that the inhabitants of Zagórów active in nationalist groups could not shake off the impression that Breede wanted to know too much about "certain matters" – as my interlocutors put it enigmatically. In September 1939 the situation was already clear: she and her husband signed the Volksliste, proclaiming their belonging to the German nation. I am not surprised that Hugo Breede did so – he considered himself a German. But she – a Pole from a worthy family? Today it is truly hard to grasp. Why did she let herself be seduced by German propaganda? What enthralled her in the words and gestures of the Führer? How did it come about that she believed the tales of a master race told by an ungrown bawler with a tiny moustache? Was she driven by fear of losing her hitherto social status? Did she fear poverty, humiliation? Did she see in the Hitlerite expansion a chance to improve her hitherto existence? Or perhaps – quite simply – she was faithful to the marriage vow she made to Hugo on the day of the wedding: "I vow to you love, fidelity and conjugal honesty, and that I will not leave you until death…"? We shall not learn this today; we cannot ask her about it – the dead will not defend their own case today. They say she was already mad then. Then, when she eagerly sent her sons to the Hitlerjugend, where the boys quickly learned to treat Poles with contempt. Then, when in black officer's coats and a fitted costume she drilled the firefighters in front of the seat of the Zagórów volunteer fire brigade…

– I remember from childhood how Breede, in black officer's coats, shouted in German at our lads from the fire brigade – Tadeusz Molski relates in a telephone conversation. – It was something nasty. Why in German? After all, she knew Polish perfectly… She consistently and in unison with her husband assured the families of the Peowiaks arrested in 1939 that the men were alive, albeit sent to forced labour. She told everyone far and wide that on the radio Hitler had promised that there would be no revenge for the previous war on the Poles, so there was nothing to worry about.
