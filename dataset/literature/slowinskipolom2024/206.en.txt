– She taught my youngsters too. Before they went to school, she would come to them – Cieślewiczowa recalls. – By way of thanks I always fed her. If she came on a Sunday, I gave her dinner; she warmed herself at our place, because at home she was cold.

I ask my interlocutor how she recalls her acquaintance with the Doctor's wife.

– I knew her from this side, as she came along so hungry, so frozen… I did not look at her through the prism of her past; I came to know her as a very cultured, warm lady. What weighed on her conscience from the time of the occupation, I do not judge. Perhaps she had no choice but to behave as she did. It is hard for us to judge now… The young do not understand what times those were and what situations. – Mrs Halina smiles at the memories. – My husband always used to tease me, saying: "Oh, look, your friend is coming!" And I would say to him: "Don't mock! You never know what life will bring to anyone! You may be someone great, and later you are nothing…".

Most of the inhabitants of Zagórów of the post-war generation remembered her as an eccentric, tragicomic figure, pinning on her for good the label of a pauper – a madwoman. In the 1970s she trotted along the little town's streets, startled-looking, gaunt, with two goats on a lead. In no way did she resemble the pre-war elegant lady with the proudly raised brow. She pastured her animals either by the narrow-gauge railway or by the windmill on Lidmanowska Street. The owner of the windmill, Mr Chmielewski, often chased off the boys playing football with the words: "Do not destroy my grass here, it has to be nice for the goats!". Andrzej Bernat, who came to Zagórów in 1972, had not known this figure, exotic to him, before. Once, during a game of football by the aforementioned windmill, he asked one of his pals who that was.

– I heard in reply, literally, that she was the wife of the old German doctor who had made a Holocaust of the local Jews. As a teenager I could not quite fit it into my head; I thought to myself: what a woman that must have been!
