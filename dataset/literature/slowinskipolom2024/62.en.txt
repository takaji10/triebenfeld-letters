# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 62
# translation by the editor's files

 the spouses Prusiński was Trąbczyńskie Luźne Nowe Olędry, later called Łukomskie Olędry (today part of Łazińsk Pierwszy). The village was founded in the years 1754-1767, and was settled by settlers of the Lutheran and Catholic faith, that is, of German and Polish origin. Between 1746 and 1789 further villages were founded in the noble estate of Łukom: Bukowa, or the Łomowskie Olędry (today Bukowe), Imielno Olędry (today Imielno), Zagórowskie Olędry (today Huta Łukomska), Myszakowskie Olędry (today Myszakówek). The population of these villages was mixed in faith and ethnicity, of Polish and German origin. In a similar period, in the noble estate of Trąbczyn, the village of Łazy Olędry (today Łazy) was founded. The foundress of the village was the widow Katarzyna née Rozdrażewska Prusińska. The settlers were Polish peasants freed from corvée labour and transferred to Hauländer law.

The last village located under Hauländer law in the years 1794-1800 was Koszelewska Łąka, settled by German, Evangelical settlers from Western Pomerania. Later, until the enfranchisement of the peasants in 1864, no other Hauländer villages were founded, but the process of enlarging the already existing villages continued.

In the years 1789-1791, in the eight Hauländer settlements listed above: Bukowe/the Łomowskie Olędry, Imielno, Łazy, Myszakówek, Stara Huta Zagórowska and the Trąbczyńskie Stare and Nowe Olędry, and probably the Luśnie, counted together with them, there lived in total 503 people, of whom 256 were Catholics and 247 Evangelicals. A population that was exclusively Evangelical and German lived in Myszakówek, and an exclusively Catholic and probably exclusively Polish one in Bukowe, Łazy and Stara Huta Zagórowska. In the remaining four settlements lived inhabitants mixed in faith and ethnicity, with Protestants constituting a decided majority of them in Imielno and in the Trąbczyńskie Stare and Nowe Olędry and the Luśnie Olędry.
