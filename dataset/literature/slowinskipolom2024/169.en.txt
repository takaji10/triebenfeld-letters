# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 169
# translation by the editor's files

On 30 January 1943, from Berlin radio, in the time of the daily communiqués, there suddenly begin to flow the sounds of the dark adagio from Anton Bruckner's *Seventh Symphony*. It precedes the first official communiqué of this war about the lost battle of Stalingrad, the capitulation of the 6th Army and the capture of the thousands of exhausted and starving soldiers encircled in the cauldron. There is no possibility that this communiqué did not reach the front. They presumably also had some information channels of their own. Only speaking of this catastrophe threatened an accusation of spreading defeatism and of repeating the news from leaflets scattered on the Russian lines. With the consequences one can imagine.

What a strange letter, then, reaches the brother Gustav. About the planned marriage, about the fact that he will take his wedding vows only when he is released from service. For as a soldier he will get only a few days' leave. The betrothal and the wedding are to be the concluding festival of thanksgiving, a joyful beginning of private life. Bruno describes at length the girl from Trzemeszno, Elfride Fiedler: that she is housewifely, lively and talkative despite her low education. But that is good, for he does not want a wife with higher education who would lecture him unceasingly. There is, however, a certain catch – the girl, her parents, her siblings belong to the community of sabbatarians. But if she decides to marry him, she will have to convert to Lutheranism, and that before the betrothal. "I have not the slightest desire to go to church alone". If she does not do it, he will break with her at once. It is a long letter describing the multitude of thoughts that constantly accompany him when he dreams of the longed-for marriage. He asks how his brother is getting on with Erika, whether the sisters Anna and Kadzia will be taking their vows this year.
They are not to wait with that until his arrival on leave. That is all. About his service at the front he writes one sentence. "Things go variously with me". By the standards of wartime he receives quite a few letters from potential candidates. But the post brings in this matter more disappointments than hopes. The twenty-three-year-old maiden from Trzemeszno thanks for Bruno's photograph, on which he looks younger than in reality.
