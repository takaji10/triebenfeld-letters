A long letter of Bruno to his brother Gustav dated 2 August has survived. It is not a reply to the letters of the father, brother and sisters written in July. They probably crossed in the post. If they reached him at all… For how did they find their way back to Łazińsk? Returned – because there was no longer a unit at the old field post address – or did Bruno bring them back himself in his baggage? But what he writes is to some extent an answer to the family's cares and anxieties connected with the soldierly service of brother and son. He writes that on the 22nd of that month they are to set out for the front. He asks them to hold off sending parcels until they give the front field post number. He does not complain about the supply. "The food too is good. Every day there is meat, or something in the nature of meat. There is also more bread (750 g a day). On Sunday there is even 1/3 of a bar of chocolate and 1/5 l of schnapps. At the front itself the food will not be worse and even better". The soldier young in service ("until the 3rd year of the war I could have remained in the schoolroom") considers that he joined the Wehrmacht at the end – when the fighting is already reaching its culmination. He fears – of course apart from the fighting itself and its possible consequences – the coming winter. "The old front soldiers tell that in the north the hardest was the past winter. They had to freeze then, because there was no time for preparations. For this winter, however, the preparations are already being made in summer – if those men are still alive, then I hope they will not freeze. So it will probably be with us too, although by that time they may be transferred a few more times". Surely the memories of the front soldiers who survived the fighting in mighty frosts remained strongly imprinted in Bruno Mielke's memory. "My hope and my petition is to survive this one winter, and if God grants again the grace of victory, then my hope is not empty. In any case it is beautiful that one of us takes part in it, and for me it is an obvious duty, even if I, with my 31 years, do not entirely understand it! Would you believe that I already feel this?".
