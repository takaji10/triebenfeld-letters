In the 16th–18th centuries, and to a lesser extent later, until about 1864, this settlement developed in various regions of Poland: in Żuławy, in the lower Vistula valley, in Mazovia, and from 1597 in Greater Poland, at first in its western part (the former voivodeship), and later (especially from the third, fourth decade of the 18th century) in the eastern part of this region (the Kalisz voivodeship and, from 1768, the Gniezno voivodeship created out of part of the latter).

In 1746 Hauländer settlement also began in the territory of the Puszcza Pyzdrska. The settlers arriving here were mostly free peasants of the Lutheran faith of German nationality, coming mainly from rural settlements in Silesia, Pomerania and Brandenburg, lying in what was then Prussia, but also from western Greater Poland, and to a lesser extent peasants of the Catholic faith, also of Polish nationality, coming most probably for the most part from Greater Poland. They usually received on lease one włóka (łan) of land to develop by drainage and/or clearing. The size of the włóka, amounting to 30 morgi, depended on the length of the rod, that is, the measure with which it was measured, and ranged from 17.95 ha to 24.20 ha.

Around Zagórów Hauländer settlement was carried on only in the noble, private estates. Its pioneers in the present-day Zagórów commune, and at the same time in the Puszcza Pyzdrska, were the owners of the Trąbczyn estate, the spouses Paweł Prusiński and Katarzyna née Rozdrażewska. In 1746 they located the first two villages under Hauländer law. These were the Trąbczyńskie Luźne Stare Olędry (today Łazińsk Pierwszy and Łazińsk Drugi) and the Trąbczyńskie Luśnie Olędry (today part of Łazińsk Pierwszy).
