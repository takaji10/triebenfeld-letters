#### Hauländers vs Prussian colonists

A characteristic feature of the Prussian colonization, in contrast to Hauländer settlement, was the creation in the colonies of farms of very varying size, on the assumption that the smallholders (who were the most numerous) would support themselves mainly from wage labour on the large farms. The colonists were peasants who had come from Germany. Most of them came from Württemberg; smaller groups arrived from other provinces. All the colonists were granted tax relief and exemption from compulsory military service. They were people who were personally free, and they received the land in perpetual lease. Something new was the material assistance granted by the Prussian state to the colonists in non-cash form (farm implements, livestock, building materials) or in cash form: a grant for setting up the farm, reimbursement of travel costs, and payment for clearing. The amount of the assistance depended on the financial situation of the colonists, who were for the most part poor people, and had come to Poland encouraged by the possibility of improving their condition. In South Prussia an effort was made to create fairly large farms, from 20 morgi to 6 łans.

The colonization of the years 1793-1806 was not, however, in the end a successful undertaking. The assignment to the newcomers of wastelands and land requiring clearing or drainage deterred many of them, who had expected to receive in this province land already prepared for cultivation and of a larger area. For this reason a large part of the colonists either returned to the Reich, or departed for Russia, where the conditions of settlement were more favourable. Comparing Hauländer settlement and Prussian colonization, it is nevertheless clearly visible that Hauländer settlement was conducted more rationally. The Hauländers came from nearby areas and knew the local conditions better than the colonists, who often came from distant regions of Germany. They were generally in a better financial situation.
