On the card there is Bruno's annotation that he replied on 31 January 1943.

The brother Gustav sends copies of documents confirming Bruno's professional position as a teacher and reassures him that everything is in order. He is also planning to go to Konin to check at the Sparkasse whether his teacher's salary, together with interest, has arrived in his account. He also tells of the two courses he attended: air-raid defence and the conducting of ceremonies. With bitterness he writes about the issued ban on baptisms, funerals and other church rites being conducted by lay preachers and cantors. The pastor Gross quoted earlier wrote about this. "Our neighbouring parishes have been experiencing tensions since the summer, because no cantor or, as it is called in the ordinance of the Reichsstatthalter, lay helper may any longer hold services; only pastors may hold them. Thereby depriving the kantorate councils of the possibility of acting and of experiencing the readings during the services every Sunday". The cantors, who were most often also administratively teachers, are as teachers separated from the Church. This decision can be interpreted as an effect of Nazi policy whose aim was to push the Church away from the teaching and upbringing of the young. This ban pained Gustav deeply. He greatly liked taking an active part in the service and always did so gladly. Christmas, New Year's Eve, the New Year therefore sadden him. Apart from that he has nothing special to say. "Yesterday [he writes on 19 December 1942] we had the pig slaughter".
