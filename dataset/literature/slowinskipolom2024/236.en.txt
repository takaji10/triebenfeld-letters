# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 236
# translation by the editor's files

My sister Traute lived from May 1945 until the deportation in the neighbouring Grunwald farmstead. At weekends we met at my grandfather's life-tenancy quarters. In December 1945 the date of the expulsion was announced. Until then the farewell to our land had been taking place in instalments, through the weekend visits. Now it was to be the last time.

I remember that last time very precisely. As I left, I kept looking back every so often, so as to remember everything for the indefinite time of absence. There was a sad and depressing mood. We knew that we would not return just yet, and perhaps never again. It was a conscious farewell, above all a very painful one for Mama, for the years spent on Łazińsk were her whole life. But more strongly than the sorrow for what we had lost and left behind, I felt a full readiness to set out on an unknown, new path of life. I turned round once more; snow was falling; it was 11 December 1945. Only after tens of years was I granted to be here again. On that December day all the inhabitants of Łazińsk had to report to the Municipality Office in Grabina. Most of the belongings we had with us were taken from us. It took a long time, so that not everyone was processed on the same day. About half of the families left on the first day on the narrow-gauge railway, the rest on the following day. It happened that relatives who wanted to remain together were separated. We went by the narrow-gauge railway – my mother, my sister, my cousins Anna Hohm and Hilde Haupt with their little children and their mother (my aunt Rytthammer, née Schmechel), Aunt Lidia, the Runge family and I – on the first day from Grabina to Witaszyce. After a stay there of many hours we travelled in closed goods wagons through Poznań to Szczecin. Szczecin was one great heap of rubble. We arrived at the station of one of Szczecin's suburbs, probably Kreckow (Krzekowo). Then came a march along a gravel road to the sugar refinery in Szczecin Gumieńce (Stettin-Scheune). We spent the night at the sugar refinery. In the morning we went on to another goods station. Then we made a week-long journey in a closed goods wagon. The journey was often interrupted.
