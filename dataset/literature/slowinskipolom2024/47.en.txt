# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 47
# translation by the editor's files

 the panes were smashed and the crystal chandeliers were looted. The houses of prayer were converted into seats of the gmina authorities or into schools. The drama begun in 1939 went on: the Protestants, deprived of their property, of pastoral care, of the possibility of baptisms, funerals and church weddings, departed by degrees for Germany. For long years there remained in their hearts grief, sorrow, and here and there – hatred. The situation began to return to normal only after the lapse of many years. As a result of the state's legal regulations with regard to the Evangelical-Augsburg Church, the parish's real estate began to be returned. This real estate had already been settled upon, and often tenancy agreements had to be concluded. The tenants, in turn, did not always wish to pay, treating church property as former German property. It was this problem that had to be faced by the delegate of the Consistory for the territory of the parish – Bernard Otello – and later by the superior of the diocese, the Rev. senior Ryszard Trenkler of Toruń. The real estate at Wierchy was administered by the Church Property Fund (Fundusz Mienia Kościelnego) until 1997. As a result of the actions of bolder Protestants, among them Edwin Rydl, who gathered signatures under a petition for the opening of the church, the church building too was handed back. The parish, once numerous, became a diaspora parish and a very poor one, for such were its members. The first post-war administrator of the parish was the Rev. Karol Świtalski, who on 18 July 1954 celebrated the first service after the Second World War. The pastor also took over the management of the parish property. This caused many difficulties, yet it helped to cover the financial needs of the congregation only to a minimal degree. What mattered most, however, was the recovery of the church and the resumption of pastoral work – to which, after all, the Church of Christ on earth was called. Before the Rev. Świtalski and the members of the parish there stood the difficult task of rebuilding religious and church life, of overcoming hatred and dislike. It was also necessary to maintain the church, which after 1945 had been stripped of its chandeliers, bells, organ and liturgical furnishings. The archives, too, vanished somewhere, and among them – the registers of vital records. They have not been found to this day.
