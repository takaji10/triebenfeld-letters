# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 98
# translation by the editor's files

The situation was changed by a tsarist decree of 11 September 1864 and by the regulation of the authorities of the Congress Kingdom of the same year, as a result of which the kantorate schools were subjected to partial state control, being divided into three categories:

- a house of prayer,
- a house of prayer with Sunday school instruction,
- an elementary school under state supervision.

As I mentioned earlier, requirements concerning the education of persons wishing to serve as teachers in the cantors' schools soon appeared. This guaranteed the pupils of the schools the care of a qualified pedagogue, able to provide access to a broader and verified knowledge of the world around them. In 1938 there appeared the Regulation of the Council of Ministers of 17 December 1936 on the Recognition of the Fundamental Internal Law of the Evangelical-Augsburg Church in the Republic of Poland, in which it was laid down that:

> "To provide for the religious needs of their parishioners, the parishes (filial parishes) erect and maintain churches, schools, charitable institutions and parish houses – and also establish parish cemeteries. In the larger and more extensive parishes, preaching stations for the occasional holding of services in private premises, as well as houses of prayer, that is, chapels and congregation halls, that is, kantorate schools, may be established in individual localities, in which the cantor, that is, the lay assistant of the parish priest, in his stead baptizes children, buries the dead, holds read services, teaches the parishioners religious songs, and also teaches the children religion and prepares them for confirmation"[^42].



[^42]: Paragraph 16 point 1, https://sip.lex.pl/akty-prawne/dzu-dziennik-ustaw/uznanie-zasadniczego-prawa-wewnetrznego-kosciola-ewangelicko-16835480 (accessed 11.11.2021, 20:28).
