# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 119
# translation by the editor's files

The fates of the sons of Ludwik and Emilia illustrate the family's next leap up the social ladder of the world of that day. From village to town, from a small farm to an almost considerable latifundium. Work, work and work again — that is the value that was unquestionably instilled in successive generations of Beutlers, and that they took deeply to heart. Wherever on the earthly globe they appeared, even if they had nothing in the way of material possessions with them, they made indisputable and salutary use of that treasure, faithful to the Protestant ethos of work and to the family tradition. The land in Dąbrowica near Rzgów, on which Adolf Beutler, born on 4 January 1874 in Bilczew, farmed into his late years — in practice, until the end of the Second World War — most probably belonged to the dowry of his first wife Amalie née Kinast, or was bought shortly after their wedding. The first daughter of that couple, Natalia, was born already in Dąbrowica in 1900, and in Dąbrowica, two years later, another daughter, Emma, came into the world. Adolf Beutler steadily expanded the farm and developed horse breeding. The farm lay a proverbial stone's throw from the Warta, though on a slight elevation of the terrain, which was very important in view of the flooding.

At the beginning of the twentieth century there were as yet no flood embankments along the Warta, and so the river, overflowing year after year, flooded the meadows, fields and farms lying closer to its banks. Mrs Gertruda still remembered the roar of terrified animals that the farmers of the flooded farms evacuated by boat to safer ground. Life in the countryside, as has been said earlier, is no idyll. The lack of proper health care, the lack of specialist medicines and equipment, continued to reap a sad harvest among the rural population. Emma and Natalia were still little girls when their mother, Adolf's first wife, Amalie Beutler, died. Life abhors a vacuum, especially in the countryside, with the work of a large farm being steadily developed, with small children to raise, and so Adolf entered into a second marriage.
