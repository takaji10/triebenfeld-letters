In the following years the number of pupils in the schools increased considerably, and the German and Polish schools functioned in agreement side by side or shared buildings and staff for years – until September 1939. After the outbreak of the war, the occupier introduced the Regulation on Schooling in the General Government of 31 October 1939, which presupposed national segregation: children of German nationality could attend only German schools, Polish children only Polish schools, which from then on remained under the strict supervision of the occupying authorities. In March 1940 Polish children were deprived entirely of the possibility of education, whereas the children of German citizens attended the *Deutsche Volksschule*. After the Second World War nothing returned to the old order; the majority of the German settlers abandoned their homes, and the school buildings passed entirely into the hands of the new Polish authorities.

##### The school in Zagórów

From 1850 there functioned a schoolroom together with the cantor's dwelling, carved out of half of a building purchased for the sake of the then-forming parish community at 54 Konińska Street (in the other part of the building, in accordance with custom, the prayer hall was organized). Religion in the Evangelical school in Zagórów was taught by the pastor, and the remaining subjects – a qualified teacher (usually the cantor). In the interwar period there were already several primary schools operating in Zagórów, including private ones, and the seven-year public school on Kościelna Street was also attended by children of the national minorities. E. Kneifel confirms that before the outbreak of the Second World War nearly 20 children of the Evangelical confession were receiving instruction there. In 1835 the teacher was August Gabler. From 1940, when the Poles were deprived of access to education, the school was renamed Deutsche Volksschule im Hinterberg. The post of teacher was then held by Mr. Gulke.
