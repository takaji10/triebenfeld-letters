# Słowiński and Połom (2024), p. 134
# translation by the editor's files

 years later, through the efforts of the whole community of Łazińsk, whose works were directed from 1925 by the cantor Gustav Adolf Mielke, a new building was erected, housing classrooms, an office and the dwelling of the headmaster and cantor in one person. Yet Gustav Mielke's engagement did not concern only educational matters and religious duties. He was a highly active propagandist of German nationalists and of Nazi propaganda. This activity – even for such a multicultural region – must have aroused serious reservations on the part of the authorities, for Mielke was forced to leave Łazińsk in 1936 and settle somewhere in Galicia. He returned together with the Wehrmacht in 1939. Throughout the war the school, a greatly curtailed kantorate and the Mielke family were housed in this building, sizeable by village standards.

One must also record further sentences from the memoirs quoted above. "The aggressiveness of the local Germans towards the Poles and towards the third nation that had settled in large numbers in Zagórów since old times – the Jews – would reveal itself only with unprecedented cruelty in the period of the Hitlerite occupation"[^51]. The entry of the Russians on 18 January 1945 and the rebuilding of the Polish administration put an end to the long history of this kantorate. All the farmers were expropriated in April of that year. Eugenia Plitt, an inhabitant of Łazińsk, remembered that at first it was said that this was a temporary measure… Years passed and the former German school and kantorate were adapted into a residential building for teachers. Four flats were partitioned off. In 1993 the couple Dorota and Bogdan Karaszewski received the allocation of one of the premises. It was also the time when cars with German registration plates began to appear in Łazińsk and on the nearby Kopczyk, their passengers trying to document the former homesteads. Not all the locals liked these visits. Some would even call the police to check the identity of the visitors and ask what purpose their walks around the local farmsteads served.



[^51]: Słowiński Lech, ibid.
