### We knew we would not return here just yet…


Told by Eugenie Plitt, written down by Günter Hennings



Florentine Schmechel (1918).


Photo from the collection of Günter Hennings

In the Konin voivodeship, about eighty kilometres south-east of Poznań, there is a small scattered settlement called Łazińsk. It was here that I grew up together with my sister and two brothers. Our ancestors came to Poland from eastern Brandenburg more than two hundred and twenty years ago. There is no record of whether they were recruited to settle on favourable terms by Polish recruiters, or on the prompting of the Prussian state. And so our ancestors, on foreign soil, with great zeal and with the help of their families, began the cultivation of the fields and meadows assigned to them and the building of the house and the farm buildings. On one of the beams in the room the date 1778, the year the construction was completed, was carved. This house, like many others in this region, was built of bog iron ore (Raseneisenerz, Raseneisengestein, Rasenerz, Sumpferz, Wiesenerz), while the roof was covered with straw. During the Second World War the exploitation of the ore began. It was transported on a narrow-gauge railway which was built for that purpose.

According to my grandfather's accounts, the Schmechel family was not the first to settle in Łazińsk. My grandfather's grandfather was the one who settled here with his family. This must have been shortly before the year 1778. The immediate neighbourhood from that time until the beginning of 1945 was predominantly German. We felt at ease in this region, and surely hardly any German from Poland who before the year 1939 visited Germany as a German from abroad would have said that he was travelling to his old homeland. We felt we were Germans, but our true homeland lay in Poland, in the region in which we could live and feel at ease as Germans.
