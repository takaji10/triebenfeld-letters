 concerning everyday life in the care home – on the whole quite calm, but not lacking either comical or sad occurrences. Rev. Kowalska remembers well that the senior home was created to provide elderly people with care and access to participation in services, but at the same time – a certain independence in everyday existence. In connection with this, there was no round-the-clock care in the EDS. My interlocutor explains:

– Mrs Erika Fandrich came for four hours during the day; in that time she cleaned the corridors and did the residents' shopping according to a list they had prepared, and she brought dinners from the Social Welfare Home. The house had seven rooms; in six of them our seniors lived, and I occupied the seventh. Each of us had her own kitchenette (consisting of a fridge, an electric cooker, a sink and a cupboard), thanks to which each resident prepared her own breakfast and supper.

If need be, the carer could assist them in this. The shopping was done by Mrs Erika Fandrich out of the residents' own funds and settled according to receipts. The residents had complete freedom as to when they got up, when they ate their meals, what shopping they commissioned from Mrs Erika. Wednesday, in turn, was the day of Rev. Andrzej Mendrok's official duty; at that time he occupied an office in the attic, where he kept the institution's documentation.

– The one year I spent in the EDS was a turning point, for the residents were already beginning to age to such a degree that they were gradually ceasing to be self-reliant – says Rev. Kasia Kowalska. – I remember the time when dementia began to enter the senior home ever more boldly, and I had six baby monitors set on my windowsill, listening at night for whether something troubling was happening in any of the rooms.

Sometimes the carer's intervention was necessary, as for instance when one of the ladies, accustomed to the layout of her former flat and no longer able to assimilate the new arrangement, would go out at night to the bathroom and on the way back habitually end up in another resident's room and grow angry that someone was occupying her bed.
