# breyer1935mutterland  2  en
# Breyer (1935b), p. 2
# translation by Claude

The Polish researcher H. Wiercieński, in a treatise on German immigration devoted to the Germans of Congress Poland, says the following:

> *"The German settlements on Polish soil are as old as Poland itself. Numerous names of places and castles bear witness to this; the history of the country, too, and that of many Polish families speaks of it. The German settlers were bound to the native economic circles by common economic interests. Marriage strengthened the mutual relationship. From this the adopted homeland gained many deserving citizens, who often stood out from the sameness of the mass of the people and who knew how to win names of historical importance for themselves and their descendants. To this day we find many bearers of German names among the outstanding minds of the country*[^1]*."*

Over the centuries German people streamed continually, with greater or lesser interruptions, into the lands beyond the Oder. Dr. Lück, in his epoch-making work "Deutsche Aufbaukräfte in der Entwicklung Polens" (German constructive forces in the development of Poland), has shed light on this German question of fate in the East and has often given us quite bitter insights. His research covers the whole territory of the Polish state and all the centuries of the Polish nation's existence as a state. My task is not drawn so wide in space and time. I want to show that the greater part of the Germans who immigrated into central Poland in the 17th, 18th and 19th centuries came from the neighbouring eastern provinces of Germany, the old ones and above all West Prussia and Posen, acquired later through the partitions. From there German farmers, craftsmen, teachers, pastors, scholars and artists streamed to the culturally undeveloped lands of the East, to find new places to work here and to do good German work.

[^1]: "Ekonomista", year 1908, vol. II
