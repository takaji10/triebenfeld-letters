Characteristic of the settlement history of the Dobrzyń country is the additional name "Räumungen" (clearings) for most German villages. It is also found in Straßburg county. All these facts speak for it that *the Germans of the Dobrzyń country come from neighbouring West Prussia* and the western counties of East Prussia.

Research into the origin of the Germans has gone furthest on the *Kuyavian lake plateau* (Kujawische Seenplatte). This was treated at length in Heft 2 of the "Deutsche Monatshefte in Polen". Therefore only a short summary is to be given here. The dialect, the family names, the Alt-Stettin hymn books and prayer books, the way the houses and barns are built, and the folklore point beyond doubt to eastern Pomerania as the country of origin. The place names from the church books of the Lutheran congregations of Sompolno, Babiak, Koło, Chodecz, Przedecz and Maślaki carry special weight as proof. The 529 places of origin found for the period 1800 to 1860 are divided among the counties of the former province of Posen as follows: Mogilno 129 (27 in a hundred), Strelno 89 (19 in a hundred), Obornik 40 (8.4 in a hundred), Kolmar 24 (5 in a hundred), Witkowo 21 (4.4 in a hundred), Schubin 20 (4.1 in a hundred), Hohensalza 17 (3.6 in a hundred), Posen 15 (3.1 in a hundred), Schroda 13 (2.7 in a hundred), Bromberg 12 (2.5 in a hundred), Znin 10, Wreschen 8, Wirsitz 7, Jarocin, Kempen, Filehne 4 each, Schmiegel, Samter and Adelnau 3 each, Wollstein, Lissa, Krotoschin, Grätz, Pleschen, Neu-Tomischel, Rawitsch, Ostrowo, Fraustadt 2 each, Kosten, Gostin, Czarnikau, Meseritz and Samotschin 1 place name each[^4]. By stock, *90 in a hundred fall to the Pomeranians*, 10 in a hundred to the Silesians. This proportion shifts still further in favour of the Pomeranians if we add the rural, and in part urban, settlers who came directly from eastern Pomerania. From Jastrow came 3, and 1 settler each from: Berlinchen, Pasewalk, Alt-Stettin, Flatau, Damlug in Pomerania, Stolp in Pomerania, Bublitz, Saba in Pomerania, Alt-Glebsow, Neugardt, Sandow, Gollnow, Hammerstein; further, one each from Tiefenau in Old Prussia, Perleberg in the Prignitz, Schwerin on the Warthe, Bachwinkel, Altstadt, Stieglitz near Grünfier and from Schneidemühl.
