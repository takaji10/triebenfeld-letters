# Rusiński (1947), p. 120
# translation by Claude

a blessing for their neighbourhood[^120-1]. In accordance with the principles of their confessions (the Catholic and the evangelical) the Olęders devoted much care to the cult of the dead. The Catholics buried their dead in the parochial cemeteries, like the rest of the Catholic population. The evangelicals usually had a separate cemetery at each settlement, established on ground received gratuitously from the lord for this purpose. The cemetery ground the Olęders might fence as they pleased[^120-2]. The burial rites were performed, as has already been mentioned, by the teacher.

[^120-1]: Geschichte, p. 401.
[^120-2]: AW. Góździn, A. I: There is permitted to them also a piece of free ground for a churchyard, on which they will be free to bury the bodies of the dead, each according to his custom, and this ground they may enclose with a fence. AW. Kąkolewo, A. 1: „in gleichen vergónne ihre Todten nach hollinderischen gebrauch ehrlich zur Erde bestadten, wo zu ich ihnen cinen freyen Kirchhoff so im ihren Griintzen gebe, welcher auch von Niemanden so]l gekriincket, oder von cinem einigen Gesstlichen turbieret werden”. ["in like manner I grant that their dead may be honourably committed to earth according to the Holländer custom, for which I give them a free churchyard within their boundaries, which shall also be encroached upon by no one, nor molested by any ecclesiastic”] AW. Dziewczastruga, B. 1028: a place for burial is likewise promised to be designated for them.
