Brauneck (the owner was Goetzendort Grabowska). On the whole, however, cases of a real increase of the Olęders' obligations occurred rarely[^102-3]. At the moment of the enfranchisement[^102-2], as appears from the mortgage records, in the great majority of cases the obligations fixed in the location charters were binding. This is expressly emphasised in the reces for the Stodólsko Ol. 4: “Die Wirthe,[^102-4] welche aus der Stelle der Stiftungs-Urkunde „Jeżeliby zaś od kogo mieli mieć jaką oppressyą etc” die Verbindlichkeit der Gutsherrschaft zum Schutz und zur Vertretung ihres Eigenthums herleiten wollten, erkennen nunmehr an, dass sie im vollständigen Besitze der Ihnen vermöge der Stiftungs-Urkunde verliehenen Grundstücke und Rechte sind, und dass der ihnen verheissene Schutz und die Vertretung daher erfüllt, und nicht weiter zu beanspruchen sind”. ["Those farmers who wished to derive from the wording of the foundation charter 'should they however suffer any oppression from anyone, etc.' an obligation of the manor to protection and to the representation of their property, now recognise that they are in full possession of the lands and rights granted to them by virtue of the foundation charter, and that the protection promised them and the representation are thereby fulfilled and are not to be claimed further."]

Exceptionally it even happened that the lords diminished the Olęders' obligations in later charters, as was the case in the Chełmskie Ol.[^102-5], and probably also in the Lubaskie Ol. 8. It must finally be noted that the relations presented above cannot be an expression of the “Polish system”, as Schmidt would have it, if only for this reason that the real economic oppression of the Hollanders was initiated a whole 200 years earlier by Albrecht Hohenzollern in East Prussia[^102-6].

The causes of some of the lawsuits must also be sought in the craftiness and ill will of the Olęders. As has already been stated above, even German literature admits that the cause of many lawsuits was the unauthorised extension by the Olęders of their original servitude rights and the ploughing in of land[^102-7], which in the Olęder settlements occurred notoriously.
