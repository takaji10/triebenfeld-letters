To the teacher's duties belonged the teaching of religion and the performance of certain religious ministrations, and the exercise of the children in reading, writing[^115-1] and counting. At the same time the teacher performed the function of community secretary; he kept the community book, transacted the correspondence of the community, and probably also the private affairs of the Olęders.

The relations of the teacher with the community shaped themselves markedly unfavourably, which in the result greatly weakened the value and level of the school instruction. The cause here was the circumstance that the teacher, as one engaged[^115-2] and largely supported by the community, was entirely dependent on it, and thereby powerless in the face of the caprices and arbitrariness of the settlers. The Prussian officials, who carried out directly after the second partition a revision of the evangelical schools in the occupied territories, reckoned among the most important ailments of the school system, among others: the complete lack of respect and understanding for the persons of the teachers, sometimes indeed through their own fault, and the widespread custom that every settler presumed to be entitled to give commands to the teacher, and, when his child was reprimanded at school, came himself to the teacher and abused him in the most brutal manner. Still more forcefully the attitude of the Olęders to the teachers is characterised by Stenger. The surviving lawsuit records concerning matters between the settlements and the teachers show also that the latter were often charged with utterly unserious accusations and expelled for trivial reasons[^115-3]. Probably a cause of this development of relations was also the insufficient supervision of the school system (partly exercised by the pastors).

It remains in turn to discuss the extent, level and significance of schooling in the Olęder settlements. Recent German literature has expressed the view that the schooling in the settlements inhabited by German settlers (among others in some Olęder settlements), despite all its primitiveness, was at least by two centuries ahead of the conditions prevailing contemporaneously in the Polish villages[^115-4].
