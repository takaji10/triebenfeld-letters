# rusinski1947  52  en
# Rusiński (1947), p. 52
# translation by Claude

The competences of the communal organs in their own sphere comprised two kinds of matters: communal administration and communal judicature. The administrative provisions binding the members of the community were gathered together in the so-called wilkierze (Wilküre). Although issued at different times and by[^52-1][^52-2] different lords, in their content they are almost identical, which[^52-3][^52-4] indicates that they were written according to a common pattern, or rather that the later wilkierze were modelled on the wilkierze written earlier. Comparative research into the origin of the norms contained in the wilkierze would exceed the framework of the present work; in any case it seems a certain thing that these norms are based on foreign models. We quote one of the wilkierze in full in the appendix. From the wilkierze there shines forth a strong sense of communal solidarity. A considerable number of the provisions contained in them are designed to remove the sources of discord between the individual members of the community, or bind them to help one another in need. The latter, moreover, lay in the Olęders' own interest, for the community answered for the manorial duties usually solidarily; the property situation and solvency of a neighbour could therefore be a matter of indifference to none of its members. Control over the farming of the individual members of the community belonged to the important functions of the communal administration. The communal organs were even bound to remove by force an Olęder who farmed badly and to fill the farm with another. That this duty was in fact performed is proved by the note on p. 38. In some settlements the individual farmers were bound to report to the communal authorities the more important legal acts accomplished by themselves (contracts of sale and purchase, wills, bequests etc.). These acts were entered in a special book kept by the self-governing organs for the time being. Several such books have survived;3 they contain much interesting material on the life of the settlements. At times the legal acts entered in the community books had to be presented for additional lordly approval. On that occasion the lord collected certain fees.4
