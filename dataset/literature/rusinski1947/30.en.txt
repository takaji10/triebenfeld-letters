# Rusiński (1947), p. 30
# translation by Claude

2. As follows from the surviving charters for Ol. Ujskie[^30-1] and Ol. Nowe Dwory,[^30-2] the Holendrzy, as in Prussia, receive their lands in perpetual lease. / 3. Above, on p. 12, there has already been demonstrated the connection through the person of the owner that exists between the Wieleń settlements and the Holendry settlements in the Świecie starostwo.

4. In the two charters mentioned above are given the Christian names and surnames of the settlers.[^30-3] The majority of the names and surnames have a Flemish-Dutch sound. 5. Both charters mentioned call the settlers "Olęders" in the sense of their nationality, and the later charters of 1677 for the Wieleń Olęders use the designation: "honourable men of the Olęder nation". The earliest surviving inventories of the Wieleń estate also call the settlers Olęders,[^30-4] setting them in opposition to the German settlers.[^30-5]

6. On the first page of the community book of Folsztyn we find the following note: "Im Jahr 1637 ist die Kirch im Folsteyndorff gebawet. Es haben die deutschen burger auh helfen, Holtz zufuhren, auh den Zimer leuth gelohnet, Das sie mit den holendern gleich wart dor zu haben. Den Form auff der Kirch haben die holander allein bawet. Die Klock haben die holander allein gebaufft vor hundert und zwanzieg Reichstaller. Das Neu biet werck vumb die Kirch haben die holander allein bauven lassen. Dem Zimer man haben wier gegeben 18 Rechstaler drey tohn bier" [In the year sixteen hundred and thirty-seven the church in Folsteyndorff was built. The German burghers also helped, bringing timber and paying the carpenters, so that they should be on an equal footing with the Holendrzy in that matter. The frame of the church the Holendrzy built alone. The bell the Holendrzy alone paid for, for one hundred and twenty Reichstaler. The new pew work around the church they had the Holendrzy build alone. To the carpenter we gave eighteen Reichstaler and three tons of beer]. True, further on the year 1681 is given as the date of the founding of the book; nevertheless the above note must have been entered shortly after 1637, or copied from an older entry, for otherwise the details given there would no longer have been known. This mention testifies in an incontestable way to the nationality of the settlers.

The kinship of language and the community of religion must have led to the complete germanisation of the Holendrzy. There also contributed to this the settlers' lack of any stronger attachment to the land; they melted away
