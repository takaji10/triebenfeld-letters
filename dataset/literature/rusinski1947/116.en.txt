From the official revision of schools carried out in 1793 in the territory of the second partition we learn that, for instance, 20 pupils attended the school in the Siereckie Ol. in the time of its greatest attendance. The settlement numbered 43 hearths in 1789, which, applying the coefficient of 6.05 persons per hearth, gives in the result 260 persons. Thus one person in every thirteen attended[^116-1] the school, assuming that children from other settlements were not[^116-2] sent to it and that attendance was only during the winter months. Almost until Christmas the school stood empty 1. Relations of this kind were not at all exceptional. The Prussian authorities carrying out in 1793 the revision of the evangelical schools in the Trzciel district give, as a universal defect of the school system, the slowness of the parents in sending their children to school. The parents, in the opinion of the authorities, perform this duty unwillingly and irregularly, sometimes only when the child is to approach the sacraments[^116-3]. There follows the characteristic remark: „Diese Klage muss man besonders iber viele den Einwohvon der Haulander fuhren, die ihre Kinder żusserst nern sogenannten. schlecht zur Schule anhalten, sie nur immer zum Hitten ihres Viches brauchen, und sie durch solche Vernachlissigung beynahe selbst bis zum Vich herabwirdigen[^116-4]. ["This complaint must be made above all of many of the inhabitants of the Hauländer, who keep their children most badly to school, use them only ever for the tending of their cattle, and through such neglect degrade them almost to the level of cattle themselves"] The scope of the school instruction did not go beyond[^116-5] the teaching of religion and the beginnings of writing, reading and counting. The level of the teaching and its effectiveness were the resultant both of the education and pedagogical abilities of the teacher, and of the attitude of the parents and children to the school.
