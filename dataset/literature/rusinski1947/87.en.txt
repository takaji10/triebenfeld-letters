# Rusiński (1947), p. 87
# translation by Claude

money was very various; in Popowo it even amounted to 23[^87-1] zł a year, elsewhere less was paid (8 zł, 6[^87-2] zł).

b. Rents in kind. Rents of this kind we meet with in the second half of the seventeenth century; in the earliest settlements rents in kind do not occur. The most common rent was oats, delivered on the average in the quantity of[^87-3] [—] wierteles or ćwiertnie of local measure. (In the Borujskie Olęders, for example, there was in use the wiertel of Poznań measure, containing[^87-4] [—] garnce.) In exceptional cases the rent reached[^87-5] [—] wierteles of oats from the huba.[^87-6] Far more rarely was the rent reckoned in another kind of grain, for example in rye or in barley.[^87-7] The object of the rent might also be poultry (from the huba on the average 1–4 capons or hens, or 1–4 geese, exceptionally also ducks),[7] or eggs (in the quantity of from 1[^87-8] mendel to 1[^87-9] kopa), in rare cases butter[^87-10] (1 garniec) and cut straw (sieczka)[^87-11] (2 korcy). It should be noted that when the rent was reckoned in oats or in other naturalia, no special fees were usually taken for the forest servitudes. The rent defined as a payment for the servitudes amounted likewise on the average[^87-12] to [—] wierteles of oats, more rarely embracing other naturalia.[^87-13] Sometimes, again, naturalia took the place of a separate payment for the pasture servitudes.[11] In an isolated case wood ash likewise appears[^87-14] as a due. And sometimes the lord left the Olęders the choice of paying the rents in kind or their equivalent in money.[1]
