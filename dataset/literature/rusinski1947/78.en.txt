# Rusiński (1947), p. 78
# translation by Claude

36 garnce to the barrel,[^78-1] amounted therefore to about 8 garnce of beer and ½ of a garniec of vodka a year. The capacity of the garniec of Kościan measure is not exactly established; it was presumably close to the capacity of the Berlin garniec, which held 4.58 litres.[^78-2] The actual consumption must have been at least[^78-3] twice as great, for the Olęders, thanks to their complete freedom to trade in agricultural produce and in all other goods, often went to the towns and neighbouring settlements,[^78-4] and this, as happens today likewise, was undoubtedly an occasion for the consumption of liquors. On the other hand, of course, in the Olęder taverns a certain percentage of the liquors might be consumed by the inhabitants of other settlements or by merchants passing through. The quantitative ratio of the consumption of vodka to the consumption of beer stood at 1 : 16. On the average, beer and vodka were consumed in approximately the same ratio in Bronice and Kwiejce Nowe according to the data of 1773.[^78-5].[^78-6] b. Milling. The total number of millers has been given by estimate in Tab. II and III. Almost all the mills existing in the Olęder settlements were windmills. The building of a mill required, in all probability, a special permission from the lord.[5] The Olęder millers paid for the mill only a fixed money rent.[6] The millers' incomes were diminished by the fact that the obligation to grind at the lord's mill did not as a rule bind the Olęders, who might therefore cart their grain to any mill whatever, whereas the neighbouring corvée peasants were bound to grind their grain only in the manorial mills. c. Crafts. As Tab. II and III show, crafts were but weakly developed in the Olęder settlements. This is presumably to be explained by the fact that the Olęders had a closer contact with the neighbouring towns than the rest of the population (cf. above). The lord did not forbid the community to keep craftsmen, requiring only that they be “honest”.[^78-7] In Podgradowice and Rakoniewice the Olęders were permitted “to make linen for their own household needs, but for no one else for money — not permitted, under penalty”.[^78-9].[^78-8]
