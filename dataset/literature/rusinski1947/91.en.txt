Closely related to the labour dues were the cartages and the “journeys”. In the first case the Olęders were most often obliged to cart firewood from the lord's forest to the manor, usually[^91-2] twice a year;

at Christmas, Easter and Whitsuntide, in the amount of 1–2 loads[^91-3] of[^91-4] (the so-called festival wood, drzewo świętne)[^91-6]. The cartage could be combined with the performance of certain[^91-5] works, as for instance when the Olęders had the obligation to cart wood already felled 4. More rarely we meet the obligation to cart timber in logs to the sawmill, or else the cartage of a certain number of kopas of the lord's grain from the field to the manorial barn 8. In the second case it was the duty of the Olęders to cart to the towns, usually the larger ones, the products of the lord's husbandry (timber, grain, liquors). The Olęders were obliged to carry out the transport with their own teams[^91-7]. The length of the route was either specified in miles 8, or the town to which the future journeys were to be made was named. The towns most often in question were Czersk, Leszno, Poznań and Wschowa, more rarely Komorniki, Toruń and Żydaczów. Sometimes these journeys could be very distant and consequently burdensome. Thus, for instance, the inhabitants of the Karpickie Ol., situated in the neighbourhood of Wolsztyn, were obliged to make the journey to Toruń once a year, or to pay in its stead 5 czerw. zł.

Among the labour dues an important place was occupied by the so-called tłoki [compulsory ploughing-works] (in the sources almost always in the spelling tłuki; German Bit(t)age, Bit(t)dienste. As the very name indicates, these were labour dues performed in case of sudden need. The tłoki differed from the other labour dues in that they were rendered for a remuneration on the part of the lord, usually in beer, so that these labour dues had the character of hired labour.
