# rusinski1947  75  en
# Rusiński (1947), p. 75
# translation by Claude

The Olęders' total income from fishing is made up of two items most closely linked with one another: the incomes from their own fish-farming and the incomes from the fishing servitudes. The separation of these two items is here extremely difficult to carry out. For it is not known whether, and in what cases, the possession of Olęder land carried with it the right of fishing in the bordering waters (rivers, lakes, marshes, ponds). The same doubts are raised by the ponds, which often stood upon Olęder land, and which either did or did not enter into the area of the individual farms. While it seems a certain thing that the adjoining waters, which separated the lord's lands from the Olęders', and likewise ponds lying indeed upon Olęder land but not entering into the composition of those lands, remained the property of the lord, and the right of using them must be regarded as a fishing servitude, it is so much the harder to decide whether, in cases where, for example, the Olęders received lands upon a river or lake bordering upon another estate, they entered into the existing manorial rights to those waters, or whether these belonged to them likewise only in the character of servitudes. The sources rather support the second possibility, for they distinguish fairly clearly the waters lying upon Olęder land and entering into the area of those lands (ponds[^75-1] dug by the Olęders, the seasonal floods upon the riparian lands),[1] and the remaining waters. On this assumption the lion's share[^75-2] of the Olęders' fishing income will fall to the fishing servitudes. The rule was that the lord permitted fishing in all the bordering waters, the manorial ponds upon Olęder land excepted. The Olęders were not, however, permitted to set up wears upon flowing waters.[^75-3] Sometimes the lord forbade the taking of fish in the spawning season.[^75-4] In exceptional cases, when the lord reserved to himself the exclusive right of fishing, he gave the community priority in letting it out on lease.[5][^75-5] The sources make no mention of professional fishermen among the Olęders.

[^75-1]: AW. Dłuskie Stare Ol., A. 1: “If water should fall to anyone's share in his measured-out land, he shall have the free use of it”. AW. Białężyńskie Ol., B. 59–60: “for the preservation of fish they shall be free to make and dam up fish-ponds for themselves”. AW. Folsztyn, B. 2: “It shall also be permitted them to take fish in the ditches, on the floods of the river, with their zabrodnie, without any hindrance from my subjects”. AW. Meryń Stary, C. 7, fol. 101: “Fischerei ist in ihren Gränzen, Lacken und Seen ganz frei, weil dieselben beflossen als unbeilossen mussen verzinst werden” [Fishing is entirely free within their bounds, lakes and waters, since these, whether flowing or non-flowing, must be rated].
[^75-2]: Rel. Posn. nr 638, 1791, fol. 602, the Ol. Róża: “on the Warta they are permitted to take fish with rods, sacks and baskets, as far as their boundaries reach, on this condition, that they hinder me in nothing in my ponds”.
[^75-3]: There were usually enumerated here the implements with which the fishing might be carried on: rods, hand-nets, baskets and sacks, larger nets and zabrodnie.
[^75-4]: AW. Drzewce, B. 2.
[^75-5]: Such was the case in the settlements of the Sieraków estate.
