In this way he arrived at the figure of 832 localities, that is, as he writes, he succeeded in doubling the number of settlements given hitherto.[^14-1] The author indeed qualifies this, observing that some of the localities he mentions may belong to settlements of other types, and that he may have counted some others twice, “but all that again does not amount to very much”. In reality the number of settlements in the territory of Wielkopolska is about 550, so that the author's errors amount to one third of the figure derived by him. The errors are in fact even more considerable, for the author, without having carried out any source research, has, despite the best of intentions, overlooked a whole series of Olęder settlements. [^14-1]: The figure given is difficult to check, for the author compiled his enumeration in a very unclear manner. In one place he states that a given settlement corresponds to another one, elsewhere he writes again: “see” the other settlement, or even: “is it identical” with the other settlement? It is likewise unknown whether, in the case when a village, for example, consisted of three parts also bearing their own separate names, the author counted it as 3 or as 4 villages (e.g. the Szamocin Ol.; here, by the way, the author made an error, since the colonies of Antoniny and Atanazyn were never part of the Szamocin Ol., having been founded only about 1820 as a result of the Germanising action in the annexed territories). The classing among the Olęder settlements of colonies that arose in the Prussian period is of course a mistake, since these colonies lacked the characteristics of that settlement (a government or some other entrepreneur recruited the individual colonists, and the whole settlement was the planned creation of that entrepreneur).

(as Birkwerder and as Bzower Hld; the author, moreover, admits the mistake himself, though outside the text), Lubaskie Ol. (as Lubascher Hld. and as Miłkówko Hild.), Łoskońskie Stare Ol. (as Alt Laskun and as Gozdowiec), Obra (either Nowe or Stare Oborskie Ol.), Olszewniak (as Olszewniak and as Chmielowo), Pietronki (as Miren Hld. and as Pietronke), Wymyślanka (as Wymyslanke 1 as Maschlanke). Some settlements the author counted even three times, e.g. Mechnacz (with the author separately Rozbitek, Mechnacz, Urbanówko; the Ol. Mechnacz were sometimes called Urbanówek because they were located (in 1784) also on part of the abandoned holdings of Urbanówek), or the Szarki Ol. The author cites Głuchowo wrongly, following Schmidt. The document A. 1, kept in the village records, was assigned by mistake to the folder of Głuchowo in Szamotuły county, whereas it relates to Głuchowo in the parish of Goraj. Nor is it at all a location document, but a copy of a judgment of the patrimonial court of 1741. At that time the parish contained the Ol. Róża (founded 1024), and in all probability the document mentioned concerns precisely that settlement. The Fitzen Hid. and Frey Hld. given by the author are parts of the Pącheńskie Ol.; they did not form independent communes; neither name figures in the sources of the Polish period. The Ol. Glinki included two houses marked on MGd as Sątop Ol. (with the author Hollendry); there are no data showing that these farms already existed in the Polish period. The name Jabłonka Nowa (Neu Jablonke) appears for the first time only in the list of 1845 (1843). This is probably a part of the Nowosileńskie Ol., or else a completely new settlement. The old village of Wierzchabno has nothing in common with the Ol. Strychowskie. It cannot have been part of them, if only on account of its position in relation to the Olęders. The name Goraj Ol. refers to one of the settlements belonging to the Goraj estate, or else all the settlements of the Goraj estate are understood by it.
