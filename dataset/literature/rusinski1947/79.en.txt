d. Forest industry, embracing the burning of charcoal, the making of tar[^79-1] and of potash, likewise had no great significance in the Olęder settlements. It is indeed possible that the sources are silent concerning such an industry because it rested only upon the utilizing of the stock of timber standing upon the Olęder hubas and bought together with the land. The working-up of the timber had then, however, only a transitional character and ended with the clearing of the land. A permanent production resting upon the exploitation of the lord's forests was impossible without an agreement with the lord, and of such agreements the sources have preserved very little record.[^79-2] The classification records mention a tar-burner in Herbardowo only once;[^79-3] besides this they also mention the production of tar in the Ol. Króteckie. Concerning this last settlement the records supply a series of very valuable data concerning the production of tar. The Olęders distilled tar in common in the Krótek forests. The burning took place[^79-4] [—] times a year, and from each burning were obtained[^79-5] [—] barrels of tar. The value of each barrel was 10–11 zł. For each burning the Olęders paid the lord[^79-6] [—] zł, or else delivered a barrel of tar. The annual production of the furnace, after deduction of the manorial rent, thus represented a value of 350 zł.

[^79-1]: Trade. As a rule the Olęders enjoyed unrestricted freedom of trade. “All trade, however, in salt as in herrings and other goods, as also in grain, shall be free to them to carry on”.[3] Trade as a profession was carried on by the innkeeper, selling above all salt and herrings, then iron and other articles of everyday use.
