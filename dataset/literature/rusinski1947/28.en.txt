# Rusiński (1947), p. 28
# translation by Claude

Similar mentions we meet also in relation to some settlements of the Poznań county.[^28-1] There were undoubtedly more settlements of this kind than follows from the chance, scanty records of the sources; there must also have been numerous intermediate forms, i.e. settlements with a population mixed in respect of religion. The Evangelical confession dominated above all in the northern part of the Poznań county. The Prussian classification files give, among the Olęder settlements, [them] as purely Evangelical (Lutheran). On the other hand, the further the settlement reached to the south and to the east, the stronger in it was the share of the Catholic population. Important conclusions concerning the religious relations of the Olęder settlements are suggested by the social stratification of the population of the settlements. As follows from Table IV, about 36% of the population consisted of landless people, who for settling in the settlement needed neither an individually expressed consent of the lord nor material means for redeeming themselves and establishing a household. On the other hand, on[^28-2] the basis of settler registers sometimes inserted in charters, and of inspections of one and the same settlement carried out at different times, it can be established that the settlers did not bring with them the needed contingent of hired labour forces, but hired them as the farm grew and the demand for labour increased. It is most probable that the Olęders sought such labour forces in the nearest neighbourhood. This met with no obstacles of a legal character, owing to the fact that in the villages of Wielkopolska there existed a number of labourers who were not subject (the free, contract workers), whom the Tol. even groups[^28-3] into the categories: "the free, occupied with work" and "beggars". Undoubtedly their ranks were also powerfully swelled by runaway subjects. Hence it must be accepted that, especially in settlements located among Polish villages, the poorer, landless population was Polish and Catholic. This conclusion is confirmed by contemporary sources1 and later Prussian returns of population. The fact that, for example, in the census of 1843 a considerable percentage of the Catholic population is shown in almost all the Olęder settlements.8 cannot at all be explained by assertions about an alleged polonisation of the German population, and in no case can there be any question here of a change of religion.
