# Rusiński (1947), p. 85
# translation by Claude

8D a. Money rents were reckoned in different monetary units, whose rate was subject to continual fluctuations, and in consequence of which their mutual relation was continually changing. In the further considerations all the rents have been recalculated into Polish złotys of thirty groszy.[^85-1] Often, indeed, they were fixed alternatively in two distinct monetary units, and in connexion with this the value of the one coin was given in relation to the other.

]

Among the money rents the most important, and sometimes the only, item was formed by the rent from the land (the gruntczynsz). In the earliest settlements it was very low: in the Ujskie Olęders (in 1597) it came to 20 zł from a huba of 30 morgs,[^85-2] in the Ol. Nowe Dwory (in 1601) 25 zł from a huba which, as follows from the later surveys, was of 60 morgs.[^85-3] In the Ol. Róża (in 1624) the rent was nominally somewhat higher, amounting to 36 zł from the huba;[^85-4] in reality, however, owing to the fall in the value of the złoty, it was no higher than the rents given previously. The rent remained at this level throughout the seventeenth century.[^85-5] At the end of that century dues began to be reckoned in tymfs. Thus in the Dłuskie Stare Olęders (in 1688)[^85-8] the rent from the ślad was fixed at 64 tymfs. The real value of the tymf, however, did not amount to even half the value of the złoty current hitherto, so that in reality we have to do here with a lowering of the rent.[^85-6] In the first half of the eighteenth century the dues are reckoned either in “good money”,[^85-7] that is in the złotys current before the introduction of the tymf, or in thalers, reckoning at first 36 gr, and as the value of the złoty fell 3.6 and even 8 zł to the thaler, in which case the value of the rent stood, as before, at 30–36 zł (of silver), or else 8–12 thalers[^85-9] — or else in debased
