2. In some cases the alienation of a farm required the lord's consent.[^46-1] Here we have already to do with a serious restriction of the Olęders in the free disposal of their land. A case, however, in which the lord on the basis of this provision made it impossible for an Olęder to alienate his land we have not found in the sources.

3. Exceptionally the lord excluded definite categories of persons from the circle of possible purchasers. Thus, for example, all the Olęders in the Sieraków estate were not permitted to alienate their farms to the church. Similarly it was in Ol. Sikorka 3. In the Kiączyńskie and Włościejewskie Olęders the purchasers could not be Dissidents.[^46-2] In the Uścikowskie Olęders the purchaser had to be a free man.[^46-3] The Olęders in Daleszyno were not permitted to sell land "across the border".[^46-4] This last condition was presumably designed to exclude the sale of land to an inhabitant[^46-5] of a neighbouring village, over whom judicial lordship was already exercised by someone else. Such a sale would lead to a division of judicial lordship over the purchaser between different lords. Whether any such division of the lord's lordship over the Olęders occurred at all seems doubtful. In any case the sources do not mention it.[^46-6] 4. We also meet restrictions of a proprietary character. In the Cisiogórskie Olęders it was not permitted to sell land "by morgs, in pieces, only half a huba together".7 This provision was intended to prevent[^46-7] the excessive fragmentation of the lands. In the Świącyńskie Olęders, on the other hand, the lord reserved to himself "that one man should not hold two plots, but that each should content himself with one".[^46-8] In the Trojanowskie Olęders a settler alienating his farm could take for the land only as much as he himself had paid.
