This figure is very small, which is to be explained at least in part by the fact that in 1789–1800 the peasant population was increasing at a faster rate than the remaining population. Presumably, therefore, the most fitting course will be to assume that the szlachta and the clergy in 1789 amounted to 3% of the total rural population, that is, in round figures, 3,000 persons. If we add this sum to the figure of 97.5557 souls of the remaining rural population, we obtain 100,557 souls. If we then deduct from this sum the population and hearths of the Olęder settlements calculated previously, there will fall to the non-Olęder rural population 85,570 persons and 13,353 hearths. Hence, as the average per one rural hearth (without taking the Olęder hearths into account), we obtain the figure of 6.41 souls. As is known, however, Tod. 89, on the basis of which we carried out the above calculations, is territorially incomplete. The actual number of rural hearths for Kościan county, after deducting the Olęder hearths, is 13,915 [sic] [sic] hearths.[^24-1] Tod. 89 therefore lacks 552 hearths. Multiplying them by the coefficient of 6.41 we obtain 3,538 souls. The whole rural population of Kościan county will thus amount to 104,095 persons. This is, however, a minimal figure, for the omissions in Tol. which could be established with regard to the Olęder settlements occurred undoubtedly 1 also with regard to the remaining settlements. This number must therefore be somewhat increased. In this way the total of the rural population of Kościan county will be expressed by the round figure of 105,000 persons.

For the former Poznań county we have far more modest source materials. They concern above all the part of the county that remained with Poland after the first partition. Here only the already-mentioned tariffs of hearths and of the hearth tax for the years 1789–1790 (Tp. 89, 90) come into consideration.
