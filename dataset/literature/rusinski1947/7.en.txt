5. the Dutchmen's right to the land rested as a rule on a lease for years, amounting to some fifteen to several tens of years.[^7-1] Later, however, we meet also with perpetual lease.[^7-2]

From the serf population of the time the Dutchmen differed in law also in that, like the settlers under German law, they were personally free.[^7-3] All these differences, and further the already-mentioned type of agricultural culture, consisting in the improvement of the land by drainage and in the tilling of the soil in one piece, and finally the linguistic distinctness, constituted a complex of characteristics distinguishing the Dutchmen in the new settlement environment. C. The characteristics of the “Olęder” settlements in the area of the former voivodeship of Poznań

“Olęder” settlement in the former voivodeship of Poznań constitutes the counterpart, and at the same time a further stage, of Dutch colonisation in the area of Royal and Ducal Prussia.[^7-4] To denote the settlers we find in the Polish sources most often the term “olędrzy” (occasionally in the spelling “oleęndrzy”), more rarely “olendrzy” and “holendrzy”, while in the German texts: Holldnder and Hauldnder. The duality of the German terminology provoked among German authors disputes as to whether the terms Holldnder and Hauldnder had distinct meanings[^7-5] or were two equivalent designations.[^7-6] The disputes arose from the fact that the “Olęders” also cleared forests, founding their settlements far from bodies of water, on dry, wooded ground.[^7-7] Opinions therefore appeared that the term Hauldnder had no connection with Dutch colonisation proper, but denoted a settler farming land cleared by himself (Hauland, from the word hauen — to cut, to clear 7,
