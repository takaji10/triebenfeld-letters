Barcinek the land was granted to the Olęders "by emphyteutic law... namely until the fourth generation of male blood",1 while in the Połajewskie Olęders the settlers received the contract originally for three years only.2 In connexion with the transition from temporary lease to perpetual grants — the wie-[^44-1]czyste holding — there appears the so-called okupne (German Grundgeld). This was the payment for the land assigned to the Olęders. Thus while the first Olęders, who had not redeemed themselves, could dispose only of the buildings and the movable property, and the land[^44-2] remained the exclusive property of the lord, the later Olęders, after payment of the okupne, became owners of the whole farm, of the land as well as of the buildings and movables. Likewise the Wieleń Olęders, before perpetual right was granted them in place of[^44-3][^44-4] the lease, had to redeem the land. Unredeemed settlements[^44-5] were among the Olęders an immense rarity. Besides the already mentioned Ol. Ujskie, Olęders sat partly on unredeemed farms in Daleszyno 4, in Chorzępowa, and probably 1 also[^44-6] in the Stare Łoskońskie Olęders 5.

According to the tenor of the charters the Olęders received their farms in heritable ownership (in charters issued[^44-7] in German: erb- und eigentümlich). It was, however, not full ownership but only usufructuary; the lord remained still the owner of the land, but so long as the Olęder complied with the provisions of the charter he had no right to remove him from the land. By virtue of the tenure there belonged to the lord, therefore, only the right to the services specified in the charter. Stein R.8$,
