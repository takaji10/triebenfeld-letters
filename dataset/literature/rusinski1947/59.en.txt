Of course such inhabitants did not belong to the Olęder community, and consequently do not form the object of our considerations.

The population of the Olęder settlements was differentiated by occupation only to a small degree. Nearly all the members of the community were employed in agriculture, whether as their principal occupation or at least incidentally. Exceptionally this did not apply to the communal herdsmen, whose basis of subsistence was the rearing of domestic animals; it must nevertheless be emphasized here that stock-raising in the settlements did not pass beyond the bounds of a subsidiary occupation alongside the proper agricultural economy; among the Olęders we nowhere meet with farms devoted specially to stock-raising. Likewise only exceptionally do we encounter craftsmen who at the same time were not engaged in agriculture.

The most important source for the occupational composition of the population of the Olęder settlements of Kościan county is the Tol. It was drawn up according to the scheme laid down by the Crown Treasury Commission, which provided for the division of the rural population into the following groups: 1. farmer peasants (gospodarze rolnicy), 2. cottagers, 3. lodgers, 4. manufacturers, 5. craftsmen,[^59-1] 6. tapsters and innkeepers, 7. journeymen and servants, 8. the unattached “busied with work”, 9. beggars. In practice there was usually indicated as well the economic and legal relation of the inhabitants to the lord, there being added as need arose: “okupnicy” (holders by purchase), “czynszownicy” (rent-payers), “kontraktowi” (contract-holders). In the Olęder villages there was usually given: “Olęder farmers”, “okupni”, “okupnicy”, “czynszowi (czynszownicy)”. This whole scheme leaves much to be desired, for it mixes occupational with social categories, and is thus incomplete. Further, the figures contained in the table inspire far less confidence than, for example, the figures of an inventory or of a lustration, for they are nowhere explained in detail. The categories of persons ad 1, 2, 3, 7 and 8 in the Olęder settlements were without doubt occupied with agriculture. At point 4 we have a term that is rather obscure; the occupational allegiance of the persons classed here must be decided in each individual case. Point 9, as one may easily perceive on glancing into the table, the lustrators interpreted very broadly. Besides undoubted beggars there were assigned to this group all the old persons incapable of work who remained at the charge of the community (widows, orphans), and presumably also other persons who could not be brought under any of the preceding categories. These persons we too have counted among the agricultural population, as occupationally passive. Besides, this column supplements the column “the unattached at work”; the lustrators, as may be gathered from the table, did not in the majority of cases themselves very well know which of the two columns to give precedence.[1] On approximately the same principles the hearth tables were at the same time drawn up.
