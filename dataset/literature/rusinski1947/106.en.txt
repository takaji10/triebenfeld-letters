As a rule, if a settlement arose on the site of another, previously existing settlement, the obligations paid formerly were binding upon it[^106-2]. The same of course applies to settlements transferred to Olęder law without a change of the previous inhabitants[^106-3]. The manor sometimes took over entirely[^106-4], or in part[^106-5], the obligation of paying the church obligations due from the settlement, or else, by way of partial repayment of the dues, bound itself, after grain had been delivered to it, to brew gratuitously for the Olęders a certain quantity of beer[^106-6]. In other cases again the Olęders received the assurance that “they would always have the protection of the lord, so that they should give no more than what equity demands and what the church authority has ordained”. Sometimes also[^106-7] the Olęders had the obligation to pay the church obligations through the intermediacy of the manor[^106-8]. The amount of the dues shaped itself variously. The fixed dues, measured per unit of land, amounted per huba to 2–8 zł[^106-9], or a korzec of rye and a korzec of oats[^106-10]. The lump-sum due, on the other hand, did not usually exceed 50 zł[^106-11], or the corresponding quantity of grain[^106-12]; exceptionally it exceeded even 100 zł[^106-13]. One must suppose that in fixing it the decisive role was played by the area of the village and the number of settlers.
