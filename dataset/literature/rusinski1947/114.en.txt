The school land usually amounted to ½ wł., more rarely 1/4 wł., exceptionally still less[^114-2]. In the Ujskie Ol. one włóka was assigned to the school, from which, however, the Olęders were obliged to pay half the rent. If the lord settled the Olęders on a site where buildings of another village, or of a folwark, still existed, he offered one of the buildings gratuitously for the school[^114-3]. Apart from the income of the school land the teacher drew incomes also from the contributions of the community. In the latter the most common, but at the same time the most precarious item was formed by the dues from each child making use of the school instruction; the rest consisted of certain offerings fixed in agreement with the teacher, in money and in kind. By way of example we state that the income of the teacher in the Siereckie Ol. in 1793[^114-4] consisted of: 1) the profits from the use of 1½ włóka of school land, 2) a tribute at the rate of 16½ thalers, 3) 4 contributions (Opfer), not further specified, 4) from every farmer one loaf, 5) one pound of butter and 4 cheeses from each huba, 6) from every farmer a cart-load of wood, 7) dues according to a separate tariff for singing at burials, and 8) dues from the pupils at the rate of 6 zł from the older children and 3 zł from the younger children.

The community could not always afford to maintain a professional teacher. The lord also sometimes stipulated in the charter that a craftsman[^114-5] or a farmer[^114-6] should be appointed as teacher. In one of the cases known to us the functions of teacher were performed by a weaver[^114-7], in another an innkeeper 8, and, as Stenger relates, they were often entrusted to cobblers or[^114-8] tailors[^114-9]. Candidates for teachers came either from the neighbouring little towns, from which lack of work or other considerations had induced them to depart, or else, as regards candidates without a trade, they were recruited from the rural labourers who in one way or another had acquired the ability to read and write.
