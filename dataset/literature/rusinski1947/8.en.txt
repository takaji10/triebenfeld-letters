# Rusiński (1947), p. 8
# translation by Claude

This view, however, has been recognised — and quite rightly so — as unjustified, for the term Hauldinder is met with for the first time only in the official Prussian records after the first partition. Before that, to denote[^8-1] all the settlers, the term Holldnder alone was used. That both terms were used interchangeably, irrespective of the manner in which the settlements arose, is demonstrated irrefutably by the following example: for the settlement of Ujskie Olędry, founded on a drained riverside lowland, not on forest ground, the German name Usch Hzuland became established, whereas the settlement of Jędrzejowo, which arose by the clearing of forest, bore the German name Hollanderdorf. At present the view prevails that the word Hauldinder is a later, synonymous variant of the word Holldnder[^8-2] (just as the Polish “holender” passed into “olęder”), and that this variant arose through the confusion of the name of the settlers with the name of forest clearings.[^8-3]

“Olęder” settlement was not the only form of settlement in Wielkopolska in the early modern period. Hładyłowicz[^8-4] divides all the settlements that arose in those times into: holendry, budy, huty, hamry 1 kuźnice, sołtys villages and colonies, forest settlements and others. Hence, as the first and most important task of the study, there presents itself the need to define what is to be understood by “Olęder” settlements in the area of the former voivodeship of Poznań, and in what these settlements differed from the remaining ones. Such a distinction is all the more necessary in that, besides the considerable number of settlements about which all the sources agree in speaking as “Olęders”, there existed a certain number of settlements whose character appears unclearly in the source tradition; and finally such as are classified wrongly as “Olęder” by certain sources, lists of localities or maps, although with the settlements of the proper “Olęder” type they
