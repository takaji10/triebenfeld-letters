Against such ploughing-in of land the manor sought to protect itself in various ways: it threatened the guilty with severe punishments,[^70-2] it reserved to itself the power of carrying out at any moment a measurement of the lands,[^70-3] it bound the communal authority to carry out at intervals an inspection of the boundaries and of the boundary marks,[^70-4] or else to carry out measurements of the whole settlement.[^70-5] The same aim was served by the provisions concerning the obligatory dimensions of the boundary ditch or of the balk,[^70-7] or concerning the width of the public roads running[^70-6] through the lands of the settlement.

Attached to the farms there were as a rule gardens. Their size ranged from ¼ to 2 morgs. The ground upon which the buildings stood did not, as a rule, enter into the measurement of the lands,[^70-8] and neither, of course, did the boundaries and balks.[^70-9] Occasionally the lord added to every włóka a piece of meadow.[^70-10]
