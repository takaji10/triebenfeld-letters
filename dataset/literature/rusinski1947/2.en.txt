The greater part of the source materials utilised is held in the State Archive in Poznań. Of these, the most valuable are the so-called village records, and next the grod books and land books for the four grods of Kościan, Poznań, Wałcz and Wschowa.[^2-1] The village records form a collection arranged alphabetically according to the individual villages, in three divisions: in division A the original diplomas (charters, grants, etc.) are kept; division B comprises copies of the diplomas; finally, in division C the remaining documents are grouped together. Among the other records use has been made of the land and mortgage records, those of the former Landratsämter, the urban records, and finally the Prussian fiscal records (of the domains, the classification and the cadastral survey), as well as old maps. The Prussian classification records were discussed by M. Bar in his work Westreussen unier Friedrich dem Grossen, Leipzig 1909, p. 210, and by M. Kniat on p. 13 ff. of his work on the economic significance of the burdens of the peasant population in Poland in the XVIII century. For the demographic part of the study particular importance attached to the following items kept in the same Archive: the table of the hearths of Kościan county, and the table of the hearths and population of the Wschowa land of 1789.[^2-2] From the holdings of the Archdiocesan Archive in Poznań the protocols of the parish visitations of 1777–87 proved particularly useful. Valuable statistical material was supplied by the manuscripts of the Fiscal Archive in Warsaw, containing tables of hearths from 1775, 1789? and 1790 (sygn. LI. 111 and LI. 508. 113), and further the table of the population of Kościan county of 1789, kept in the Main Archive in Warsaw (sygn. 163 no. 5 A). This table, like those in the other counties, was compiled as a result of a resolution of the Sejm of 22 June 1789 ordering the carrying out of the first census in the history of Poland — an incomplete one, it is true.[^2-3] Unfortunately, the scrupulous searches for an analogous table for Poznań county, carried out by the Historical Atlas Commission, remained without result. Among other sources, mention should be made in the first place of the extensive Opisy parafij of 1. 1783–4, drawn up by the parish priests of the time at the request of Primate Poniatowski, who wished in this way to obtain material

[^2-1]: The citation of these records presents certain difficulties. They were handed over by the individual courts to the archival depository only in part, which is why the numbering of the individual volumes could not for the time being be carried out. They are therefore cited according to the courts' own numbering, and where a series of identical sigla occurs, or where there are none at all, the number of pages or folios is given with each volume by way of distinction.
