c. Plant production. More exact data concerning plant production we possess only for the settlements seized by Prussia in the First Partition (of 1773) and for the settlements of the Sieraków estate. The data relating to the settlements in the area of the First Prussian Partition, contained in the Prussian classification records, are schematic and for that reason inspire less confidence; the data relating to the Sieraków estate, contained in the inventory of 1759 already several times mentioned, are on the whole set out conscientiously. As follows from the above sources, the plant production of the Olęders, owing to the poor soil, presented a most modest appearance.[^71-2] Wheat was not cultivated at all.[^71-3] Of the other cereals there were cultivated in the Sieraków estate[^71-4] rye, oats and barley, and among the other produce peas. In the settlements classified in 1773 production embraced sometimes also buckwheat and flax. The sowing per unit of land presented itself variously, according to how long the individual pieces of land had to lie fallow. In the Ol. Króteckie, for example, the land had to remain untilled for 6–8 years, and in the Kwiejce Nowe for 6–9 years.[^71-5] The average sowing in the Ol. Bielawy, Jasionna, Obelżanki and Rzecień, where there is given also a specification of the lands of the individual settlers, amounted, per włóka of 30 morgs, in korcy of the Berlin measure (⅛ of the korzec of Warsaw), to: 10 korcy of rye, 4 korcy of barley, 3[^71-6] korcy of oats, ⅛ of a korzec of peas, 1¼ of a korzec of buckwheat and 3 garnce of flax. On the whole, however, it would seem, more oats was cultivated than barley. The yield was 3 grains, sometimes still less, exceptionally 3½ grains.

In the gardens hops were occasionally grown, sometimes potatoes and carrots, but as a rule only for household use. Exceptionally, in the Ol. Kwiejce Nowe and Bronice,[8] where the cultivation of cereals was almost impossible owing to the very barren[^71-7] soil, the Olęders lived from the cultivation of potatoes and carrots, which they carted for sale to Drezdenko, distant 2 miles.
