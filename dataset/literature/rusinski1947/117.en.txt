Be that as it may, even if one takes into account that the Olęder schools were founded mostly for reasons of a religious nature, and that the results of the school instruction were very modest, it must be stated that their very existence constitutes in the history of the settlements an indisputably positive item. Advantageously, too, for the interests of the settlement must have reflected the teacher's performance of the function of community scribe. In this way, through the transaction of the community's affairs in writing, the necessary order and tidiness could be maintained in them.

C. Religious worship

As a rule the charters guaranteed to the Olęders freedom of confession[^117-1] and of religious practice. This had of course significance only in relation[^117-2] to the evangelical confessions, for the Olęders of the Catholic confession could always perform their religious practices without hindrance. The restrictions in the exercise of religious practices which occasionally occurred concerned exceptionally only the religious life of the individual members[^117-3]; more often they restricted or excluded the collective practice of religious worship.
