3. Other servitudes. Owing to the difficulty of separating the Olęders' own fish economy from the fishing servitudes, both these branches have been discussed together on p. 75. Here we merely add that for the use of these servitudes the lord exacted no fees.

Among the servitudes must likewise be counted the right, belonging to the Olęders in some cases, of digging clay and gathering stones free of charge upon the lord's lands,[^83-5] and the right of cutting reeds upon the lord's marshes.[^83-6] Of other kinds of servitudes the sources have preserved no information.

E. The lord's non-recurrent economic benefits

To this category belong the lord's non-recurrent material benefits of the nature of gifts, or else immaterial benefits which nevertheless had an influence upon the development and profitability of the Olęder farm. Both the one and the other kind of benefit was promised to the Olęders in the charter, which made it possible to demand them of the lord should certain conditions, defined in the charter, arise. Thus, for example, for building the Olęders received bricks from the manorial brickyard at a reduced price.[^83-7] In another case he promised every settler who completed the building of a dwelling house the gift of a barrel of beer.[^83-8] In the event of destruction through the elemental calamities (fires, wars, floods) the lord, besides the timber for buildings already mentioned above as given free of charge, promised the Olęders “to extend a merciful hand”, among other things, presumably, by taking their Olęder
