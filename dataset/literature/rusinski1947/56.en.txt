This fact finds its explanation in the historical development of settlement within the area here discussed. The fertile lands had long since been settled by the local population, which, growing in numbers, occupied later, in part, also the less fertile lands. The colonists of the Middle Ages likewise chose lands as fertile as possible. The stock of land left at the Olęders' disposal therefore constituted the least valuable remnant remaining after the selection of land carried out by the settlement of many centuries.

2. Forms of settlement. In correspondence with the topographical conditions there developed two basic forms of Olęder settlement: the type of lowland settlement presenting itself as a street village, and the type of settlement[^56-2]. In both kinds of settlement the arrangement and disposition of the farm buildings is bound up closely with the character and shape of the piece, or pieces, of land belonging to the individual farms. The lands of the farms comprised in a lowland settlement consisted of a series of elongated strips running at right angles to the edge of the waters or of the morass. Such a strip of land began at the lowest point, by the body of water, and, running up the slope, ended upon dry ground lying higher. Here, at the top, parallel to the edge of the waters or of the bog, ran the road linking together the whole series of strips of land lying next to one another and belonging to the individual farms. Along this road, at the highest-lying end of the land, the Olęders raised their farm buildings. As a rule these stood on the same side of the road on which the land lay, but they could also stand on the opposite side, in which case the road separated them from the lands of the farm. Typical settlements of this kind are the oldest Olęders of the Wieleń estate. In the later period street villages occur also upon dry ground, and the lands are there laid out less regularly.
