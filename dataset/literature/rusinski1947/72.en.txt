# Rusiński (1947), p. 72
# translation by Claude

dens. In the sources there is no information whether[^72-1], and to what measure, the Olęders reared fruit trees.

The plant production embraced also hay. It was, however, harvested not in all the settlements[^72-2] in very unequal quantities (in some of the Olęders of the Sieraków estate up to 15 loads upon a one-włóka farm), so that it is difficult to give here any average figures.

d. The rearing of domestic animals, in contrast to plant production, developed in the Olęder settlements on the whole successfully. Draft animals, it is true, were kept, in connexion with the small agricultural production, in numbers not very great (on a one-włóka farm on the average 3 or 4 oxen, or else 2 oxen and one or 2 horses), but cows and hornless stock in a quantity approaching the norm prevailing in the corvée villages, and sheep sometimes even in a larger number. As a characteristic feature it should be noted that the Olęders more often possessed horses than the tenantry, who for draft purposes used chiefly oxen. The draft stock was always the property of the Olęders. Since meadows entered only occasionally into the composition of the Olęder lands, and even when the Olęders possessed them, on account of their meagre extent they played no serious part in the stock-raising economy, the breeding of cattle and sheep in the Olęder settlements in the summer season rested almost exclusively upon the pasture servitudes, that is upon the right of pasturing herds, under certain conditions and within a defined range, upon the lord's lands. Since the lord, in his own interest, could not permit the pasturing of his lands by cattle in unlimited numbers, restrictions upon stock-raising came into being. The restrictions concerned as a rule sheep, more rarely goats, whereas hornless stock, cows and draft animals the Olęders were free to keep in any number.[^72-3] We meet with two basic kinds of restriction concerning the keeping of sheep. Relative restrictions, in the form of payments for the pasturing of sheep upon the lord's lands, or in general for the keeping of them, hindered it, but did not by any means make it impossible; absolute restrictions, on the other hand, consisting in the fixing of a certain maximum norm which the number of sheep upon a farm of a given size ought not to exceed,

[^72-1]: Kl. XII 6 II, fol. 111, the Ol. Stróżewskie.
[^72-2]: Schmidt (Geschichte, p. 398) imagines that the Olęders reared apple trees, pear trees, plum trees and cherry trees.
[^72-3]: Exceptionally in the Ol. Budziszewskie: “since concerning the hubas the Olęders lodged a complaint with the manor, suffering wrong in the pasture from the half-huba holders, who keep as many or more cattle and give from the pasture half as much less oats according to law... every Olęder keeping five head of horned cattle of whatever kind apart from horses owes the manor... on St Martin's day two wierteles of oats of the great skocki measure; and every Olęder keeping more than five head of horned cattle is bound each year to come to an agreement with the lord”.
