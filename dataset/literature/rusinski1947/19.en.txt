Either, therefore, the name “holendrzy” was used in the location charter in a different sense — which is the most probable, especially as the charter speaks of “holendrs of German origin” [“holendrów niemieckiego pochodzenia”] — or else the settlement transformed itself gradually from its initial Olęder one into a settlement of a different character. In any case, at the time of the partitions Jędrzejewo cannot be classed among the Olęder settlements.

Finally the question arises whether there existed Olęder settlements that did not enter the enumeration of localities of 1789 — either because by that time they had ceased to exist, or else because they had been founded after 1789. To the first category belongs the settlement of Esterpole in the parish of Brodnica. It was located by Kasper Cerekwicki by a charter[^19-2] of April 1781 1. Three years later his estate was bought by Józef Wybicki, who did not confirm the charter issued by the previous owner, did not permit the Olęders to settle permanently, and when they left the village he turned the lands abandoned by them into a folwark, which he named Esterpole after the Christian name of his wife Estera née Kowalska (the village had previously borne the name Przylepki). Even before the first partition the Murzynowskie Ol., located by a charter of 1 November 1613 in the Międzyrzecz starostwo,[^19-3] had been detached from the voivodeship of Poznań. The king of Prussia unlawfully seized the village of Murzynowo with the said Olęders in 1770 and annexed it to the Marchia; and when several years of efforts to recover the seized area proved fruitless, and on account of internal weakness armed repossession could not be thought of, the Sejm of 1782 confirmed the cession of the seized villages to Prussia (Rel. Posn. no. 599, 1782, fol. 450). To the second category belong the settlements: 1. Dzięczyna in the parish of Poniec[^19-4]; 2. Miłostowo in the parish of Kwilcz.

[^19-2]: AW. A. 1–4.
[^19-3]: The source information presents itself here as follows: MP — Holendry Dzięczyńskie; MG — Dzienczyn Holl.; MGd. — Dzienczyn KI (ein); TV — Dzięczyna, Colonisten, d. 4; AGH court of Bojanowo — Dzięczyna, Colonie; Verz/1845 — Dzięczyn, Kolonie, d. 6. On account of the absence of all the sources of the Polish period except MP, one must assume that this settlement arose after 1789 and before 1791 (the year in which MP was compiled). Whether it had at all the character of an Olęder settlement seems doubtful, the more so as in the German period it is called a “colony”.
[^19-4]: Before 1791 Miłostowo consisted of a contract village and a folwark. In that year Krzysztof Unrug sold to the Miłostowo commune both the lands it had hitherto occupied and the folwark fields, on the terms of perpetual lease, reckoning 100 Dutch ducats per huba, and granted to it, by a charter of 10 June 1791 (Rel. Posn. no. 641, 1792, fol. 450), the rights and the organisation of the Olęder settlements. We have here thus one of the cases in which a hitherto corvée village was converted into an Olęder one without any change in its personal composition.
