# Rusiński (1947), p. 58
# translation by Claude

5 OO The buildings were, on the whole, in good condition. To the farm there belonged also a yard fenced on all sides.[^58-1] In many instances the lords made, in the founding charter, stipulations as to the manner in which the future buildings were to be erected. In the Ol. Charłub every dwelling house “ought to be built properly, of two chambers and with a façade”. In the Ol. Dymaczewskie “those homesteads which they[^58-2] are to build ought to be spacious, built fundamentally of squared logs, and set out according to a regular plan”.[^58-3] Elsewhere it was stipulated that the Olęders should “erect proper buildings upon foundation sills”.[^58-4] It may be concluded with great probability that timber buildings were more frequent in the settlements which arose upon clearings, where building material was easier to come by.

B. Occupational and social composition

The occupational composition of the Olęders. Among the population of the Olęder settlements we must distinguish: 1. the Olęder farmers, who sat on lands received from the lord or bought up, and who stood with the lord in a direct economic and legal relation regulated in the founding charter; 2. the remaining population, economically dependent upon the Olęders[^58-5], such as, for example, certain craftsmen, the journeymen etc.; 3. it is also possible that in one and the same settlement there dwelt persons who did not belong to the communal society of the Olęders. Such was the case in the instances of mixed settlements already discussed. It could also happen that within an Olęder settlement there lived someone belonging to the manorial household service.[^58-6]

[^58-1]: Kawczyno, the file as above.
[^58-2]: Rel. Cost. nr 223, 1790, fol. 182.
[^58-3]: Rel. Posn. nr 489, 1751, fol. 69.
[^58-4]: The Ol. Wymyślanka, Rel. Posn. nr 641, 1792, fol. 260; the Ol. Węgielna, ibid., fol. 824. Similarly in the Ol. Komorowskie, Rel. Posn. nr 645, 1793, fol. 102.
[^58-5]: As a rule the Olęders were unrestricted in the keeping of the labour forces they needed (journeymen, lodgers). Only now and then did the lord stipulate that the Olęders were not to keep about them “bad folk (vagabonds, thieves), through whom harm might come upon the community” (charter for the Lubaskie Olęders of 1773, Kl. XII 6 V, fol. 116). In exceptional instances the lord himself drew direct profit from the fact of lodgers being kept in the settlement. In the Jędrzejewskie Olęders each of them had to work off for the lord, during the harvest, one day in the week (charter of 1763, Kl. XII 13, fol. 239); in the Radoskie Olęders every lodger was bound to pay to the manor an annual rent in the amount of 1 złoty 15 groszy (charter of 1751, AM. Sieraków, GC. 53, fol. 79). A similar provision we meet with in the charter of 1788 for the Trojanowskie Olęders: “No one shall keep lodgers with their livestock without an agreement with the manor concerning rent and labour dues” (AW. B. 1037).
[^58-6]: Cases of this kind are known to us in only two instances: in Przyborowo (AGH sąd Szamotuły, Tax Acten fol. 46) and in the Kwiejce Nowe (Kl. XII 6 II, fol. 43). In both these settlements there lived, outside the community, manorial forest guards.
