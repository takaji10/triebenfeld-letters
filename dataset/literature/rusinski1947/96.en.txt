Reflecting on their causes, he comes to the conviction that in some cases the lords did in fact violate the rights guaranteed to the Olęders in the charters; on the other hand he does not spare bitter words for the settlers, ascribing to them malice and litigiousness. He also justly draws attention to the unclear and dispute-provoking drafting of many charters. Similarly, a few years later, the Prussian official Holsche treats the matter[^96-1]. He states, however, that the lords, probably in order to entice the settlers, assigned them a very low rent, the real value of which moreover continually diminished[^96-2].

The author of the brochure Uber Ursprung u. Verbreitung des Deutschtums im Lande Posen (1849) accuses the lords of arbitrariness and of failure to keep the provisions of the charters, but admits that in some settlements the Olęders, by way of compensation, ploughed in for themselves the neighbouring lands, or extended the scope of the servitudes, which in the result gave rise to numerous lawsuits[^96-3].

Schmitt treats the matter in an extreme way[^96-4]. He calls the charters “Germanic immunities”, which did not correspond to the “Polish system”. According to him the settlers were oppressed in every way, even a regular serfdom being imposed upon them. The charters, even the royal ones, were as a rule not respected. As an example the author cites the case in which the settlers of the Ujście starostwo were, at the starosta's order, flogged because they gathered hay from the starostwo meadows. The author does not know, however, that in the neighbouring state domain of Podstolice already in Prussian times the domain officials flogged the Olęders of Brzekiniec, and that the latter in petitions to the Prussian king begged earnestly that the rights which they had enjoyed under Polish rule be restored to them[^96-5].
