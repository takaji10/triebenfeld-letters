To count them in the period of the partitions among the German population is the same error as to count among the Holendrzy the inhabitants of those settlements that had originally been settled by Holendrzy. It must further be stressed that not only the non-Olęder population,1 but the Polish Olęders too[^34-1] possessed more numerous families than the German Olęders. A characteristic example in this respect is afforded by the Olęders of the Sieraków estate. In the purely Polish Ol. Chorzępowa there fell in 1759 to an Olęder family (we take account only of those in possession of land) 6.5 persons, while in the remaining Olęder settlements at the same time 5.1 souls fell to a family.[^34-2] A Polish surname in a settlement thus represented a greater number of persons than a German surname.

Pursuing the line of the considerations conducted on the number of the Catholic Olęders, and taking into account that the number of Catholic Germans among the Olęders could be somewhat greater than the number of Evangelical Poles, the number of Polish Olęders on the territory of the former Poznań voivodeship must be estimated at 35–38% of the total Olęder population. For the whole of Wielkopolska this ratio will turn out still more favourable to the Poles, for as can be inferred already from a cursory review of the archival material, the Polish element was most strongly represented in the settlements situated on the territory of the former Kalisz voivodeship.

D. The origin of the settlers and the causes of the colonization

The origin of the settlers stands in connexion with their nationality. 3,[^34-3] It is understandable that the fatherland of the first settlers, the Holendrzy, was the Netherlands. As for the Polish Olęders, the Catholic part was recruited from the local population, whereas the Evangelicals, who appeared most numerously in the settlements of the southern part of the Poznań voivodeship, in all probability came from neighbouring Silesia. Such names as the Polsko-Czeskie and Niemiecko-Czeskie Olęders likewise testify that a certain number of settlers arrived from the south (in this case from Bohemian Silesia or Moravia). The coming of the German Olęders likewise remained connected with religion. The Catholics came in all probability from the interior of the Empire, from Bavaria or Swabia.
