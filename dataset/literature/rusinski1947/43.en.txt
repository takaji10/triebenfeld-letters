2. The law of things. There come into consideration here the whole body of norms which regulated the relation of the Olęders to the land and real property in their possession, defined the manner, conditions and scope of disposing of real property, and concerned the movable property of the Olęders. In the earliest-founded settlements the Olęders, as in Royal Prussia, were settled on the conditions of a temporary lease. In Ol. Ujskie the lease ran for 44 years,[^43-3] in Ol. Nowe Dwory 60 years.[^43-4] On similar conditions there were probably settled the remaining settlements of the Wieleń estate, namely Folsztyn, Herbardowo and Marianowo 5.[^43-5] The charter reserved to the Olęders priority in the leasing of land for further years, provided only that they offered a rent not lower than the rent of the other applicants. It was permitted to the Olęders to dispose of their farms (strictly, the buildings and the movable stock) even before the expiry of the lease, but without prejudice to the duties owed to the lord. In Ol. Ujskie the temporary lease, renewed several times, lasted right up to the partitions,[^43-6] whereas the Wieleń Olęders, after the expiry of the first lease period, were granted by counterpart charters of 1677 the land in perpetual lease. Erroneous is the opinion of Gebhardt[^43-7] and Schulz[^43-8] that the grant in heritable ownership appears for the first time only in the charters of 1677 for the Olęders of the Wieleń estate.
