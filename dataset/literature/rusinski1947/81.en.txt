The Olęders paid for the pasture a certain rent in money or in kind, or rendered certain labour dues in a fixed amount, irrespective of the number of sheep pastured upon the lord's lands.[^81-1] b. The payments were assessed upon every sheep (or upon a certain number, e.g. upon every 4, upon every 25 sheep),[^81-2] and that in such a way that either all the sheep were subject to payment,[^81-3] or else the Olęders might pasture a certain number of sheep without any payment, certain dues applying only to the remaining surplus.[^81-4] c. Payments assessed proportionally to the area of the pasture set aside for the Olęders belonged to the rarities.[^81-5] Swine and pigs might for the most part not be pastured in the lord's forests without a special agreement with the lord.[^81-6] In some settlements of the Wieleń estate the Olęders might pasture their swine upon the lord's acorns, but they might not gather acorns for a store;[^81-7] in another case the lord permitted at the most 2 pigs from every huba to be pastured upon the manorial acorns.[^81-8] It was strictly forbidden to the Olęders to take in for pasturing cattle, sheep or hornless stock belonging to others.[^81-9] Only in few cases were they permitted to pasture their herds upon all the manorial lands. The lord then demanded that they should do no damage with their cattle to the manorial grain and meadows.[^81-10] For the most part the pasture servitudes referred to the lord's forests and pastures. Plantations and cultivated grounds were excluded.[^81-11] Sometimes pasturage could take place only upon a set-apart part of the pasture or of the forest.[^81-12] The charters contained —
