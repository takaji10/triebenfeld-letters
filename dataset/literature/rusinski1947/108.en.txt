There occurred, however, cases in which the lord exempted the Olęders for ever from all taxes, thereby taking upon himself the obligation of paying them out of his own purse[^108-2]. Of course the taxes could here be shifted onto the Olęders in the form of an increased rent. We meet the obligation of paying taxes already in the earliest period of the colonisation. Thus, for instance, the Nowe Dwory Ol., founded in 1601, were obliged to pay the “crown levy resolved at the sejm”[^108-3]. The same provision we find in the charters of 1677 for Folsztyn, Herbardowo and Marianowo. In the charters of the first half of the eighteenth century there appear mentions of the hearth tax[^108-4]. The Olęders in the royal estates pay the hyberna[^108-5]. The most important tax, however, from the end of the seventeenth century was the poll tax. The charters either mentioned generally the obligation of paying this tax, or recommended paying it in the amount fixed by the authorities, and then the Olęders were usually obliged to pay into the proper cash-box the amount falling to them, like the other taxes[^108-6]; or else they bound the Olęders to pay on this account a certain fixed lump sum into the lord's hands. The latter pocketed the surplus above the tariff, or else made good the difference between the tariff and the sum paid in. The fixed amount of the tax was determined per unit of land (huba), or else fixed collectively for the whole community, which apportioned it among the individual members. In the first case its average amount lay within 3–6 zł per huba, in the second it amounted to about 50 zł. Only exceptionally, in the Sieraków estate, did the poll tax exceed 100 zł[^108-7].
