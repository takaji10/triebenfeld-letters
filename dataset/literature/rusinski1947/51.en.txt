The constitution of the communal organs did not present itself uniformly throughout the whole period of the colonization. On the part of the lords there sporadically revealed itself a tendency to restrict the collegiate prerogatives of the communal authorities in favour of the sołtys and to make the sołtys more dependent on the manor. At the location of several later settlements even a hereditary sołtys was instituted, whereby these settlements became, constitutionally, to a certain degree like settlements located contemporaneously under German law. In Appendix I these settlements have been included in the group of transitional settlements. The same significance attached to the command that the electors of the community be presented to the manor for confirmation. Considerably rarer were attempts to transform the communal constitution of settlements already in existence. Above, on p. 40, there has already been presented the evolution which the settlement of Jędrzejewo underwent. At the close of the eighteenth century an attempt was made to impose a hereditary sołtys by lordly nomination on the Stare Jaromierskie Olęders, and on the Olęders of Stare and Nowe Ujście.[^51-3] Conversely, however, the settlement of Rzecień, located on the principles of German law, became with the passage of time, constitutionally, like the Olęder settlements. In general it must be stated that the Olęder communal constitution once granted to a settlement in the location charter was respected by the manor also later.

b. The functions of self-government may be divided into delegated[^51-2] and own functions. In the first case the organs of self-government performed duties falling within the scope of the manorial administration, entrusted to them by the lord. Thus the sołtys and the ławniks were bound to see to it that the whole community punctually and completely performed the manorial duties. It was also the duty of the sołtys to collect the rents and fees due to the lord and hand them over to the manor. Moreover, as regards the fees from the sale of farms, from contracts, he had to see to it that no embezzlements were committed and that[^51-4] no false sums were declared. After the expiry of the period of freedom the communal authorities were bound to report to the manor the names and surnames of all the settlers.[^51-6] To them also belonged the duty of watching over the boundaries between[^51-5] the lands of the settlement and the neighbouring lands.
