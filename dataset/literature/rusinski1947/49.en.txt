3. The law of succession. Of the norms of succession in force in the Olęder settlements the sources contain only very scanty information. If an Olęder died without leaving heirs behind him, his property always passed into the ownership of the manor. As can[^49-1] be inferred from the divisions of property registered in the community books, the children inherited from their parents in equal shares, one of them as a rule taking over the farm and paying off the remaining siblings. Wills registered in the community books seem[^49-2] to indicate that the complete disinheritance of one of the children was not practised, perhaps not even permissible. As a rule the interests of orphans were protected by special provisions,[^49-3] or, in the event of the remarriage of one of the parents, the interests of the children of the first marriage.[^49-4] Wills had sometimes to be drawn

[^49-1]: The charter for Ol. Karpicko (AW. A. 1) provides: "but should an Olęder die without an heir, all his goods shall fall to the lord". Similar provisions are found in the charters for the Olęders of the Sieraków estate, for Ol. Kobus, Krosna etc. Somewhat differently does the charter for Czapury treat this matter (AW. B. 1 a): "but should any of them die without issue and without a natural successor, then the goods of that one go ad fiscum Conventi [to the treasury of the Convent], and the Convent shall be able to sell them at their proper valuation to whom it will; the nearest, however, to buy shall be the nearest neighbour, and should he not wish it, any one whatsoever of our Czapury Holendrzy...". Erroneous is the supposition of Schmidt (p. 348) that in the earlier settlements, after the death of an Olęder without issue, his property passed into the ownership of the community. Cases of inheritance by the community of part of the property of an Olęder who died without issue, though beyond the borders of the former Poznań voivodeship, are recorded by Baranowski (Wsie holenderskie, p. 73).
[^49-2]: The principle of inheritance of the land by the eldest son, to which Schmidt refers, did not exist. On the contrary, as can be inferred from the entries in the community books, the inheritance of the land even by daughters, with payment to the sons, was a matter of daily occurrence.
[^49-3]: Cf., for example, the wilkierz for the Nowodąbrowskie Olęders (AW. B. 1).
[^49-4]: Very detailed provisions in this respect are found in the charters for Ol. Huta, Krystynka and the Milczewskie Olęders: "Since considerable wrong is done to the children after each deceased father through the disposition of their stepmothers, I hereby establish for them, by good order, complete security: as soon as the father dies, the sołtys with the ławniks and the natural guardians shall at once set down the whole remaining substance of the deceased, as it stands in inventories and furnishings, and likewise value the goods conscientiously, and set down that sum together with the other movables and enter the register into this protocol where their other transactions are entered, as he is bound to do; and should the surviving widow be about to marry, then the sołtys and the ławniks together with the guardians shall assign to the children, in proportion, the shares due to them out of the paternal1 substance under the marriage contract, so that the children may enjoy the substance won by their father's labour of blood. But should there be no children from the second marriage and the mother should die before the second husband, then one half of that substance which she bequeathed to her second husband by the marriage contract is to fall to the children of the first marriage as natural successors, while the other half is to remain with the second husband. But if she has children from the second marriage, then from that portion of her own, also after her death, the children of the first marriage are to share equally, as in the maternal substance, since only by the proportion of the expressed marriage contract out of the paternal substance will those children of the first marriage be satisfied. And should she survive her second husband and have no children at all, then that part which was bequeathed to him by the marriage contract belongs to his successors and not to the children of the first marriage",
