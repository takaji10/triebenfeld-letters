# Rusiński (1947), p. 39
# translation by Claude

at the moment of the suit was sitting on the lands of Bniński (the Biezdrowo estate in the Poznań county), and in consequence the presence of the latter at the hearings had the character of a statutory substitution [of a party],1 or else[^39-1] he appeared as a patron, requested by the Olęder, or appointed to him[^39-2] by the court.[^39-3] We have also the interesting case of the lord enrolling in the grod books[^39-4] a complaint against his own Olęders, charging them that they act disobediently[^39-5] and do not render to the complainant their dues and the debts they have contracted. Elsewhere, again, the lord stipulates: "Should any excess or criminal case arise, they must not go to any court without my knowledge, or that of my possessor".5 Presumably this stipulation would have been superfluous had the judicial lordship of the lord over the Olęders been undoubted and complete. On the other hand we have also to record such an action, in which the plaintiff, the Olęder community, appears "in competenti domini sui assistentia" [before the competent assistance of its lord]. In any case the question whether[^39-6] the Olęders had the possibility of using the public courts cannot be resolved with complete certainty. Moreover, even if that possibility existed, it would not have been of greater significance for the Olęders in view of the judicial procedure of the time, too slow and not protecting against the reprisals of the defendant. For Olęders enjoying the freedom to leave the village it was far simpler to sell the farm and settle under another lord. It seems that this possibility was availed of fairly often.

[^39-2]: Kutrzeba Stanisław, Dawne polskie prawo sądowe w zarysie, Lwów 1921, p. 65; Balzer, Przewód sądowy, p. 36.
[^39-3]: This last possibility seems less probable owing to the fact that Bniński, as a state official (land judge of Poznań), had no statutory claims to a substitute. (Cf. Rafacz, Zastępcy stron, p. 37; the same, Dawny proces polski, Warszawa 1925, p. 97.)
[^39-4]: Rel. Cost. no. 223, 1790, fol. 519.
[^39-5]: Rel. Cost. no. 164, 1729, fol. 177 c.
[^39-6]: Rel. Cost. no. 224, 1791, fol. 676.
