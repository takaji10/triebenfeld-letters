# Rusiński (1947), p. 40
# translation by Claude

B. Manor law in the Olęder settlements. 1. The legal relation to the lord. a. The charter.

While the public authorities issued no legal norms concerning Olęder settlement at all, the system of norms concerning this settlement issued by the lord was broadly developed.[^40-1] They regulated a range of matters falling either within the domain of public law (the lord's judicature, communal and church self-government) or within the domain of private law (personal status, the law of things, succession). They concerned, therefore, the relation of the Olęders to the lord, the mutual relations of the Olęders within the settlement, and finally the relations of the Olęders to institutions and third persons (among others the church). The basis of the legal relation to the lord was the common charter (contract, deed — Latin privilegium, ius, German Gerechtigkeit), issued to the Olęder community by the lord, most often after the surveying of its lands, more rarely at a later period.[^40-2] Exceptionally the proper charter was replaced by its surrogates, for example a provisional preliminary agreement,[^40-3] or else the parties accepted as binding the charter of another community. Although the charter formally was not a contract but a unilateral act, issued and signed[^40-4] by the lord, in fact its content was settled by way of agreement between the lord and the settlers.[^40-5] As a rule the charter was written on parchment, in a more or less ornate form, with the lord's seal pressed in wax directly on the document, or hanging on a ribbon or cord threaded through a cut in the lower part of the parchment. More rarely the charter had the humbler form of a sheet of paper with the lord's signature and seal. The language of the charters was mostly Polish; of the 142 known charters 108 were issued in Polish, 32 in German,[^40-6] and 2 in Polish and German.
