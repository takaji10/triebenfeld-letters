# Rusiński (1947), p. 11
# translation by Claude

1 based on the equality of all the members of the commune in their relation to the lord, or through the disappearance of the equal standing of the Olęders within the commune in favour of the sołtys. The first case occurred especially when individual Olęders settled their accounts with the lord separately for the earlier bringing of new lands, not covered by the charter, under the plough, or obtained individually the consent for the future use of parts of the manorial lands.[^11-1] The enlargement of a settlement, however, through the creation and annexation of new farms caused no differentiation within the commune. For usually in such cases the charter contained a formula permitting new settlers to join the commune, of course with the lord's consent,[^11-2] or else the lord issued a new common charter for all the members of the commune.[^11-3] The question of Olęder settlement formally ceases to exist with the introduction by Prussia of a uniform communal self-government and of the regulation of rural conditions carried out by virtue of the statute of 1823. The years of the regulation thus form the lower chronological limit of the present study.

[^11-1]: Such cases were especially frequent in the settlements belonging to the Wieleń estate.
[^11-2]: Rel. Cost. no. 155, 1719–20, fol. 144, Tarnowa: “For future times, should any Hollender besides the present ones wish to settle on these lands, then this is to take place with the authority and by the will of the lord.” Rel. Posn. no. 643, 1792, fol. 94, Sękowo Ol.: “Should a new inhabitant wish to have lands made out to himself, then the names written on the margin of this charter are the ones he must bear.”
[^11-3]: cf. the charters for the Nowe (Wielkokrobielewskie) and Gębickie Ol. in Appendix I.
