This matter has already been discussed above[^112-2]. As a favourable trait of the Olęders the author emphasises their strong feeling of communal solidarity (social sense), and states also that sobriety generally prevailed in the Olęder settlements. These last two remarks must be recognised as correct. It is true that taverns, or the tapping of the lord's liquors, were to be found in almost all the settlements, and for the labour dues called “tłoki” the settlers received their remuneration in beer, possibly[^112-1] in spirits; this proves nothing at all, however, that the use of liquors was practised among the Olęders habitually. In a certain contradiction with the feeling of communal solidarity stood the sometimes strikingly weak attachment of the Olęders to their land 1 and their inclination to move from one settlement to another[^112-4]. It seems to follow from this[^112-3] that the Olęders, often newcomers from abroad, were accompanied even in later generations by a feeling of a certain strangeness. The lord also cared for good morals in the community, threatening sometimes even with the expulsion from the settlement of individuals without moral principles and character.
