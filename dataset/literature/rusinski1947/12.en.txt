CHAPTER I The rise of the settlements, their geographical distribution, and their numbers in the period of the Four-Year[^12-1] Sejm

The oldest surviving location charter from the territory of the former voivodeship of Poznań concerns the Ujskie Olędry. They were founded in 1597. The second surviving location document, in order, is the charter of 1601 for the Olęders of Nowe Dwory in the Wieleń estate. In this second case, as Schmidt 1 rightly observes, one can point to an undoubted connection between Dutch settlement in Royal Prussia and in the voivodeship of Poznań. Namely, Jan Czarnkowski, heir of the Wieleń estate, had as his wife the widow of Jan Kostka, starosta of Świecie, née Herburt. She, while still the starościna of Świecie, settled Dutchmen by the village of Stwolno (in 1593). That she took part also after her marriage to Czarnkowski in colonising the Wieleń estate with Olęders is proved by the names of the settlements: Herbardowo (Herburtowo) and Folsztyn (from the surname Herburt z Fulsztyna). These settlements, and also Maryanowo O.[^12-2], arose likewise shortly after 1600 3. The next settlement in chronological order is that of the Murzynowskie Olędry, located in 1613 in the Międzyrzecz starostwo, and then the Olędry Róża, founded[^12-3][^12-4] in 1624 4 in the Krobielew estate. All these settlements arose on low-lying, waterlogged ground; the Ujskie Olędry and the Olęders of the Wieleń estate on the Noteć, the Murzynowskie Olędry and the Olędry Róża on the Warta.

[^12-1]: Geschichte, p. 323.
[^12-2]: According to the mention in the lawsuit records of the Wieleń castle, cited by W. $chulz (Die zweite deutsche Ostsiedlung, p. 140), this settlement was located by a charter of 3. 3. 1614.
[^12-3]: The exact date cannot be established, for the relevant location charters have not survived.
