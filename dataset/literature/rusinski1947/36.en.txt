# Rusiński (1947), p. 36
# translation by Claude

in itself settles the question to what extent the inflow of the settler Olęders was the result of recruiting action on the part of the Polish lords.[^36-1] The source material also supplies the explanation. Of the more than 140 location charters quoted in Appendix I, only in 28 cases do we meet mentions that in the bringing in of the Olęders a locator co-operated, and here the lord could conceivably indirectly contribute to the bringing in of settlers by promising the locator advantages for the colonizing of the settlement. In no charter is there the slightest trace of a mention of the arrival of settlers as a result of a previous agreement with the lord or of any propaganda action on his part; instead there often recurs the phrase, which we shall quote here from the charter for Ol. Chromiec: "Since certain Holendrzy have undertaken to clear and to break ground for themselves in the Chromiec ground and forests, and have petitioned for this, I, inclining to their petition..." etc.[^36-2] The decisive reason, therefore, for the wanderings of the German Olęders to Poland was the hard conditions of life in their own country.[^36-3] There can be no doubt that for a subject peasant of the March the prospect of existence abroad in the character of a free man must have presented a strong incentive to emigration. Confessional differences did not hinder immigration, for a considerable part of the szlachta conducting the colonization action was Protestant.[^36-4] It seems, however, rather doubtful whether the settlers were induced to leave their country, among other things, by motives of a psychological, emotional character, expressing themselves in a weaker attachment to the native soil, or in an innate inclination to wanderings.[^36-5]

[^36-1]: German literature tries to demonstrate that the German settlers were drawn to Poland solely by the Polish lords. Special weight is attached to the advertising prospectuses printed by certain owners interested in settlement (Reklameblätter). These works serve mostly propagandist, not scholarly purposes. Let us mention here, for instance, the work: Wiesec Hans R., Uns rief Polen! Deutsches Schicksal an Weichsel und Warthe, Leipzig 1937.
[^36-2]: At times such a company of settlers was no longer a blessing but a plague for the neighbourhood, if, having destroyed a stretch of forest, it did not reach an agreement with the lord, as a result of which it left the forest in a state of devastation and began clearing in another estate. Cf. Holsche, Geographie u. Statistik, vol. II, p. 493.
[^36-3]: Cf. Rutkowski, Poddaństwo włościan, p. 57 ff., and Schmidt, Geschichte, p. 325 ff. Most instructive in this respect is the work: Horst-Gotthard Ost, Die zweite deutsche Ostsiedlung im Drage- und Küddow-Gebiet, Leipzig 1939. The author demonstrates in an entirely convincing manner that the flight to Poland from the Neumark was caused by economic oppression1 and the expropriation of the peasants (Bauernlegen).
[^36-4]: Cf. Beheim-Schwarzbach, Hohenzollernsche Kolonisationen, p. 447 ff.; Schmitt F. W. F., Geschichte des Deutsch-Krone Kreises, p. 140; Maas, Die Entstehung, p. 86.
[^36-5]: Such, it seems, is the opinion of Lepucki H. in relation to the German colonists in Galicia (Działalność kolonizacyjna Marii Teresy i Józefa II w Galicji, 1772–1790, Lwów 1938, p. 152).
