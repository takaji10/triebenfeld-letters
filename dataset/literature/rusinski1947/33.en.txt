Yet even in the Prussian classification files for the settlements taken in the first partition Polish surnames occur, though usually given in a form so germanised that deciphering them presents very great difficulties.[^33-1] All these fragmentary source data require completion in two directions. They take insufficient account, or take no account at all, of the lower social strata of the settlements (the journeymen, the lodgers). The reasoning about the origin of these strata from the local population, carried out in discussing the religious relations of the settlements, applies in full to the present considerations. The percentage of Poles among the landless Olęder population was undoubtedly larger than the analogous percentage of Polish farmers.[^33-2] Further, the surname of an Olęder cannot always be regarded as an exponent of his nationality. It must be remembered that a certain percentage of the population of Wielkopolska could bear German surnames as a result of the assimilation processes accomplished in the Middle Ages, and also as a result of the later entering of mixed marriages.
