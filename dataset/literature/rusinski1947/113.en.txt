B. Schooling

According to the Prussian classification records for the part of the Poznań county taken in the first partition, in every third Olęder settlement there existed an evangelical school[^113-1]. Probably the number of schools in the settlements with an evangelical population was still greater in the remaining part of the former voivodeship, where there was a greater number of populous settlements able to maintain a teacher. On the other hand it must be taken into account that in the Catholic Olęder settlements there were no schools at all[^113-3].[^113-2] In any case, however, the estimated figures given in Table II must be regarded rather as minimal. The Olęder schools were typical denominational institutions (Kantorat-, Lektoratschulen). They were conducted by church singers or other persons who at the same time performed religious ministrations in the community. They stood in for the evangelical clergymen for the Olęders. Hence the inhabitants of a new settlement usually tried to secure for themselves the religious ministrations of a cantor or lector, striving with the lord for permission to found a school and for its endowment. It is obvious that in schools of this kind the first place was held by religious preparation. With regard to some schools the source notices even raise the doubt whether anything other than the teaching of religion 3% was taught there. Similar reflections are suggested also by the circumstance that there were no schools either in the Olęder settlements with a Catholic population, which had easy contact with their clergymen and the possibility of using the parochial schools, or in those evangelical settlements where the pastor was on the spot, or in the nearest neighbourhood (e.g. in the settlements of the Wieleń estate).

The school was usually housed in a separate building, erected with the lord's consent. This building was at the same time the local house of prayer. Only in the Sieraków estate were the Olęders, under the provision of the then owner Henryk Bruhl, not permitted to have separate school buildings, and instruction could take place only in the cottage of one
