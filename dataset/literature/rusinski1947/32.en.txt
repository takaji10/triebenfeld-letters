# Rusiński (1947), p. 32
# translation by Claude

How did the matter present itself in reality? For the most part Polish was the population of the settlements founded in church estates, and of the noble settlements mentioned in the preceding section, settled by Catholics.[^32-1] In many other settlements alongside Evangelical Germans sat Catholic Poles.[^32-2]. Nor were there lacking settlements in which part of the population was recruited from Poles, Evangelicals.[^32-3] The German

[^32-1]: Catholic Germans lived in the clerical villages of Czapury and Wiorek. In relation to some other settlements we have the following data: In Janikowo, according to the court records of 1834 (AW. Kliny, C. 3, fol. 39 ff.), there lived the farmers: Cholewa, Nowak, Hauska Walenty, Mańka Bartłomiej, Włodarczak Antoni, Świątek Walenty, Węclewski Franciszek, Krzypiński, Karpiński; Barcinek (AW. CG. I, 1795): "Emphyteutische frey Bauern: Christoph Hintz, Lorenz Mantey, Andr. Sobiejewski, Hirth Woitek Pies, Schifer Knecht Joseph" [emphyteutic free farmers: Christoph Hintz, Lorenz Mantey, Andr. Sobiejewski, farmer Woitek Pies, shepherd servant Joseph]; Krzyżewniki (Kl. M.): 11 half-farmers, I innkeeper, | fisherman, 1 lodger, 5 smallholders, | herdsman, all the surnames Polish except one; Naramowskie Ol. (AGH court of Poznań, the Naramowice estate, vol. I, 1809, fol. 56): Joanna Dorota Knopka sells an Olęder farm to Jan Kamiński. The document is signed by Marcin Guziński, sołtys of the Naramowskie Olęders (fol. 59), Michał Dobroć and Marcin Fryć, ławniks; Czerwonak Ol.: in 1803 (AGH court of Poznań, the Czerwonak estate, vol. no. 12, fol. 11) Paweł Jastrzębkowski of Ol. Czerwonak sells a farm to Simon Laufeld. In 1810 (AGH vol. no. 11, fol. 2) Tomasz Przybylski sells a farm to Andrzej Ryske. The village of Chorzępowa, which in 1753 was transferred to Olęder law, was inhabited in 1759 by Poles alone (AM. Sieraków, C. 53, fol. 102). Polish inhabitants, if not in their entirety, at least in part, must also1 have been possessed by the other villages transferred to Olęder law without a change of their previous inhabitants. Thus, for example, the charter for Ostrów of 1789 is submitted for enrolment: "Honestus Christanus Rozna cultetus et Albertus Casimirus Biały scabinus de villa Ostrowo" [the honourable Christian Rozna, cultivator, and Albert Casimir Biały, ławnik, of the village of Ostrowo] (Rel. Cost. no. 220, 1787, fol. 371). Poles sat undoubtedly in the Polsko-Czeskie Olęders. Lück casts doubt on this (Deutsche Aufbaukrifte, p. 331, map), appealing to figures from the pre-war1 and the present period. It is not known to him, however, that in 1843 the population of the settlement consisted of 167 Catholics and 32 Evangelicals. Do the data he adduces, then, not rather testify to the germanisation of the settlement in the Prussian period?
[^32-2]: Such a mixed population was possessed, for example, by the Sroczewskie Olęders. In 1834 (AGH court of Śrem, the Książ estate, vol. I, fol. XVI) there lived here: Zimowski Mateusz, Kowalski Wincenty, Szatkowski Wojciech, Degner Eleonora, Hełmikowski Franciszek, Pihl Samuel, Pihl Gottlieb, Appelt Gottfried, Pihl Daniel, Adamski Szczepan, Hoeter Samuel, Binnert Ignacy, Barsch Ludwika, Starzebecker Christian, Floeter Renata, Rozdzielski Józef, Rozdzielska Marianna, Zimowski Krzysztof, Brzeziński Jakub, Kulmaj Daniel, Rosin Jan, Jankowska Katarzyna, Tomaszewski Franciszek, Floeter Beata. The classification records of Ostrówek of 1773 mention 4 Catholic farmers. Correspondingly we find there 4 Polish surnames: Jan Mateusz, Walenty Kępa (or Gęba; under the call number AW. Ostrówek A. I there is a charter of 1750 in the name of Jan Gąba), Michał Rodzoński, Jan Wojan.
[^32-3]: Of the Chrząstowskie Olęders VO 78 (19 August 1777) relates that they are inhabited by Dissidents, who had come to an agreement with the previous parish priest on the payment of 36 zł, and since the statute of 1768 they have paid nothing. (Cf. Vol. Leg. vol. VII, 570 1 ff.) Meanwhile in 1787 there was filed to the Kościan grod books in the name of the community the manifest "Antoni Taterka, sołtys of the Chrząstowskie Olęders", undoubtedly a Pole (Rel. Cost. no. 220, 1787, fol. 524 and 530). There was in the village, then, at least a majority of inhabitants who were Poles, if they were able to choose a sołtys from among themselves. Evangelical Poles are found also in Daleszyno. According to VO 78 (27 August 1777) the Olęders who settled there were Lutherans, and yet the inspection of Daleszyno of 1778 (AGH court of Gostyń, the Daleszyno estate, vol. no. 55, fol. 8) gives, among others, the surnames: Fryderyk Wróbel, Samuel Wróbel, Fryderyk Adam Kaźmierczak. These settlers even bear forenames then popular among the Evangelical Olęders. It was similar in Zaborowo Ol. According to a note in Rel. Cost. no. 216, 1783, fol. 32: "On these Holendry there are six farmers, that is Kazimierz Drelich the sołtys, Jan Sztybania, Michał Cierpiński, Szymon Cierpiński, Woyciech Kwaśniewski Ręczkoś, Jakub Holendrzy". The census of population of 1843 (Verz./1845, no. 1417) showed in the village of Zaborowo Evangelicals alone. Several dozen Evangelical Poles are still in Zaborowo today (cf. the census of 1921, p. 80).
