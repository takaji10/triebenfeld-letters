C. The individual branches of the Olęder economy

The full sweep of the economic activity of the Olęders will show itself most completely against the background of their share in the social income of a given estate.[^65-1] The division of the Olęders' income according to the different branches of production will lead to a picture of the individual branches of their economy; the division of income between the Olęders and the lord will be connected, on the one hand, with the distinguishing of the income from their own land and of the profits from the lord's land (the servitudes), and, on the other, with a discussion of the manorial dues. The share of the state and of the church in the Olęders' incomes will present itself to us as the public and ecclesiastical dues. Finally, the division of income between the individual settlers will require, among other things, an examination of their endowment with land and of the social structure of the individual occupations. In discussing the economic activity of the Olęders it will be indispensable to distinguish its twofold character: technical and socio-economic.

I. Agriculture. a. The agricultural technique of the Olęders, like the forms of settlement, developed along two paths. In the lowland settlements it consisted, in the first period, in the improvement of the land through drainage, in the second in the keeping, in a state fit for cultivation, of land once drained. In the settlements of the second type it consisted in the clearing of forests and thickets, and subsequently in raising the fertility of the land thus stripped of wood and in defending it against the renewed invasion of weeds and scrub. The considerable number of Olęder settlements justifies the supposition that the Olęders were able to rise to their task. They were enabled to do so by the multi-year exemptions from all dues granted them by the lord (the wolnizny),[^65-2] while the prospect of a free, independent existence upon land bought by themselves doubtless spurred the Olęders on to greater efforts. Not always, however, did the settlers receive as their lot the improvement of the lands allotted to them by the lord. In many instances they took over arable lands already manured before them by the Polish peasant, sometimes even already with buildings and crops. In column 6 of Annex I there is described the economic character of the land in the individual settlements at the moment of the founding of the Olęders. Only certain cases, clearly discussed in the founding charters or in other sources, have been recorded. The terms “village”, “folwark”, “ready-made arable land” signify that the Olęders here took over land already prepared for cultivation, whether after a village or folwark existing there before, or else were settled upon part of the lands of a settlement still continuing in existence. The improvement of the land naturally had no connexion with the rise of the settlement in such cases, likewise when an already existing settlement was transferred to Olęder
