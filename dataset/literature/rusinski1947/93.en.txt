# Rusiński (1947), p. 93
# translation by Claude

Among other works mention must be made of the obligation to cart timber to the lord's buildings[^93-1] and to shear sheep[^93-2], and finally of assistance at the catching of fish[^93-3] and at the brewing of beer[^93-4]. The Olęders were also obliged to care for the integrity and good state of the boundaries and the boundary marks of the settlement[^93-5], and to guard the lord's forest (to watch it against thieves[^93-6] and to put out fires[^93-7]). It must be noted that all these labour dues, undefined as to quantity, are on the whole met with rarely, and then applied in a moderate way; the defined labour dues prevail.

4. Other burdens in favour of the manor. a. The propination compulsion. In almost all the Olęder settlements the propination compulsion was in force. It was either complete, that is, the Olęders were forbidden on pain of penalty to make any liquors, or to obtain them from anyone but the manor[^93-8], or partial, that is, the Olęders might brew a poorer beer, the so-called cienkusz or konwent (Nachbier)[^93-9], while other liquors they were obliged to take only from the lord's brewery or tavern[^93-10].
