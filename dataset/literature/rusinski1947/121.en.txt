Concluding remarks

In the foregoing considerations the political position of the Olęders has not been dealt with, because the question of a separate attitude of the state and of private individuals towards peasants of Polish 1 and German nationality in the old Commonwealth did not exist. National affiliation was, moreover, at that time, in the period of the continual mingling of nationalities and of two-way processes of assimilation, a notion that could not be established even in rough approximation. The pursuit, therefore, of a national policy, even if tendencies in that direction had existed, was a perfectly impossible thing. All attempts, therefore, at writing about the discrimination of German settlers in relation to Poles must be regarded as an undertaking doomed in advance. On the contrary, the gap[^121-1] between the favourable economic situation of the free Olęders and the incomparably worse position of the subject kmiecie continually deepened. The sources contain likewise no mention whatsoever of any steps on the part of the landowners towards the polonisation of the German Olęders subject to them. Probably processes of polonisation of the settlers did exist, but they took place spontaneously, without any purposeful action on the Polish side.

Since they were not szlachta, the Olęders took no part in the political life of their time. Probably to the constitution of the Olęder settlements, and to the resulting absorption solely in the affairs of the community, together with their estrangement from the neighbouring settlements, must be ascribed the fact that the stratum of Olęders, in contrast to the burgher stratum, produced no personality of any outstanding eminence for those times.
