It is clear that this had to implant among the Olęders, who were in great part Germans, especially after the catastrophe of the partitions, the conviction that special, far-reaching consideration was due to them from the Prussian authorities. Without entering into whether and to what extent they really profited by these considerations, the lawsuits brought by the Olęders from motives of this kind must be accepted as a natural and practised thing. The litigiousness and ill will of some of the Olęder communities is moreover emphasised by an eye-witness, a perfectly well-informed witness, judge Stenger, who at the same time draws attention to the political back-cloths of the lawsuits, writing that the Olęders were driven into them by “unauthorised advisers of the secular and ecclesiastical estate”.[^103-2] It remains to examine how the economic relation of the Olęders to the manor shaped itself after the partitions. Here we must consider separately the settlements in private estates and in state estates. The number of the latter increased markedly, in consequence of the taking over by the state of church estates and of certain szlachta estates, in which Olęder settlements were particularly numerous (the Połajewo, Sieraków and Międzychód estates). In the szlachta estates the position of the Olęders improved markedly in consequence of the abolition of patrimonial jurisdiction. Undoubtedly advantageous to the Olęders was also the hostile attitude of the Prussian government towards the Polish szlachta. On the other hand, as regards the state domains, the economic situation of the Olęders deteriorated in a perfectly visible way. In all the domains they began to take from the Olęders the lands they had ploughed in, to restrict the servitude rights and to increase the labour dues. We have already adduced examples relating to the Ujście starostwo and the domain of Podstolice. Likewise the inhabitants of the Trzydomy Ol., lying on the ground of the Rogoźno and Budzyń starostwo, write in 1794 a petition to the Prussian king with the request to be kept to the obligations which they had rendered in Polish times[^103-3].
