# rusinski1947  101  en
# Rusiński (1947), p. 101
# translation by Claude

The unclear, often ambiguous content of the charters created in many cases an occasion for disputes and litigiousness. In the charter for the Cisiogórskie Ol. we find, for instance, the following, unclearly drafted provision: “At the harvest they are to help in the work according to need as the other Olęders shall be; in law, however, so many days shall be expressed as is the custom of the Olęders … Wood for the lord's need, as also for the kitchen, they are to cart like the other Olęders 1”. It is an obvious thing that “the custom of the Olęders” can be interpreted very widely. Elsewhere the Olęders are to present themselves for work at call, and at the harvest they are to labour as long as until everything has been cleared away[^101-2]. The scope of the servitudes was likewise unclearly defined; e.g. the provision: “sheep[^101-1] and other cattle they shall keep in moderate number, so that the lord's livestock may have their ease” 3, directly encourages litigiousness.

Disputes arising from the desire to raise the Olęders' hitherto existing obligations and to restrict their rights form a serious percentage of all the conflicts of the lord with the Olęders. The pretensions of the manor, although for the most part, as has been shown above, economically justified, often stood in contradiction to the promises given to the Olęders in the charter[^101-3]; hence, from the legal point of view, they must be qualified negatively. This matter, as appears from the utterances of the contemporary Prussian judge Stenger, caused the courts great trouble, which, although it stood on the ground of the complete respect of the provisions of the charters, could not here fail to notice the discord between law and equity[^101-4]. There occurred also, though rather rarely, demands of the manor bearing already the stamp of exploitation. To such, for instance, belonged the additional fees mentioned by Schmidt (p. 382), imposed by the Szpiech family on the Olęders of the Wieleń estate. Similarly in 1791 Ignacy Bniński tried to impose new charters on the Stare Jaromierskie Ol. and the Stare 1 Nowe Ujskie Ol., in which[^101-5] he clearly raised their hitherto existing obligations and at the same time established hereditary sołtys[^101-6]. In the Wielkie Budziszewskie Ol. the Tomicki family twice, in 1759 and 1770, raised the services and added labour dues. The Prussian councillor v. Zacha, contrary to the charter, considerably raised[^101-7]
