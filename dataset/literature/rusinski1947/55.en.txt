CHAPTER IV

The economic conditions of the Olęder settlements
A. Topography of the settlements. Forms of settlement. Building

I. Topography of the settlements. Olęder colonization was in its entirety agricultural colonization. The configuration of the ground on which the settlements were founded, and the character and productivity of the soil, exercised a direct influence on their economic life, and at the same time exercised a formative influence upon certain manifestations of the material culture of the Olęders, such as the manner of settling (the forms of settlement), building and the standard of agriculture. In view of the foregoing factors, all the Olęder settlements can be divided into two categories: lowland settlements, founded in river valleys and on the banks of standing waters, and settlements situated on dry ground, brought into being by clearing. Intermediate forms, of course, also occurred. The morphology of the lands occupied by the settlements presents, on the whole, a uniform appearance. Almost all the settlements seated themselves upon the outwash plains and dunes of the frontal moraine, frayed by the waters of the ice age. Only around Nowy Tomyśl did the reach[^55-1][^55-2] of settlement embrace also a part of the flat ground moraine. The soil of settlements of either type was of no great economic value. In the lowland settlements it was, on the side of the water basin, as a rule marshy, more rarely alluvial (the so-called mady), while on the slopes of the valleys it passed into a system of sandy or stony dunes and elevations of the glacial moraine. The soil of the settlements of the second category, sandy and barren over almost its whole extent, only exceptionally rose to medium quality.
