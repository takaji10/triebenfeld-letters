One may doubt whether the remuneration in beer always constituted a sufficient equivalent for the works performed by the Olęders. Initially, in all probability, the tłoki were rendered in small quantity and only in case of absolutely urgent need. Since the Olęders received remuneration for their work, the tłoki were not regarded as an obligation burdening the Olęders, and sometimes no mention of them was made in the charter. Thus, according to the inventory of the Wieleń estate of 1653, the Olęders who were located in that estate at the beginning of the seventeenth century were obliged to work one day at harvest, and yet in the later charters of 1677 we find no mention of these labour dues. On the whole, however, the obligation of rendering the tłoki is clearly formulated in the charters[^92-1], and the tłoki are even clearly distinguished from the labour dues “for the breaking-in of new land”. The charter for the Jerackie Ol. provides that: “other labour dues (apart from those for the breaking-in of new land and the customary tłoki) the manor ought not to demand of the aforesaid Olęders”[^92-2]. The fixing of the number of tłoki in the charter must be regarded as advantageous for the Olęders, for it protected them against possible abuses on the part of the lord. Most often hand labour was rendered (hence the tłoki were sometimes called corvée [szarwark])[^92-3], but we meet also tłoki rendered with draught teams[^92-4].

Owing to the number of working days being sometimes left undetermined more closely[^92-5], the tłoki formed a transitional form between labour dues defined in quantity and those undefined. Among the latter, performed “when the need arises”, the most important group was formed by the so-called corvée works. These include works at the repair of bridges and dykes (carting sand, laying faggots), at the digging and cleaning of ditches, at the repair of the roads passing through the territory of the community, and at the repair of mills 1 manorial buildings[^92-6].
