4. Communal self-government. a) Organisation. The broadly developed communal self-government constituted, as we have mentioned, an essential feature of Olęder settlement. In principle every settlement formed a separate unit of self-government. In exceptional cases two or even three settlements could have self-governing organs in common.[^50-2] The constitution of communal self-government in the Olęder settlements presented itself differently from that in the remaining villages. This concerned above all the organs of self-government. These organs consisted of an elected sołtys[^50-3] and ławniks. Their term of office lasted one year. Quite exceptionally in the Radoszkowskie Olęders the sołtys, and in the Uścikowskie Olęders the whole village bench, was elected for life, while the common sołtys of the Czerwonackie, Janikowskie[^50-4] and Kliny Olęders was elected for a three-year period. The choice both of the sołtys and of the ławniks was made by the assembly of all the farmers of the community. Only in the Karpickie Olęders did the choice of the ławniks belong to the sołtys. The ławniks were most often 2 in number,[^50-5] more rarely 3,[^50-6] exceptionally 4. In the same composition the substitutes were appointed. As a rule the choice of the organs of self-government fell at Whitsuntide, at times, however, the term of office ended at St Martin's (11 November) 5, or St John the Baptist (24 June) 8. We meet no provisions restricting passive or active electoral rights. Undoubtedly, however, there existed in this respect certain customary rules. It is certain only that women had neither active nor passive electoral rights. The choice fell as a rule on the older farmers of the settlement. In the few cases
