The author's charges concerning the so-called tłoki rest on an obvious misunderstanding. As has already been emphasised, these labour dues never had a voluntary character, and their German name Bit(t)age derives[^99-2] from the word Bede — tax, tribute. The author did not here avail himself of the results of Fr. Guradze's work, which exhaustively explained the matter several years before[^99-3]. In this light the arguments both of Schmidt and of later authors[^99-4] about the oppression of the Olęders through the conversion of the tłoki from a voluntary into a compulsory labour due must be regarded as pointless. Of cases in which the promised payment in beer was refused for the tłoki we have found none in the sources.

A proper view of the development of the manorial obligations of the Olęders is possible only when a broader source basis is taken into account. As regards the rents, their amount, as has already been shown above, rose from 20 zł at the close of the sixteenth century to 100 tymfs in the pre-partition period, that is, 5-fold. Meanwhile the silver content, which in the złoty according to the mint ordinance of 1580 amounted to 20 g., diminished in the tymf to 1.97 g., that is, 10-fold8. In reality[^99-5] the differences were still greater, for in those settlements in which the rents rose at all, the rise was always belated in relation to the fall in the value of money. Thus, for instance, in the Wieleń settlements cited above the rent remained the same from the beginning of the seventeenth century to 1677, although in that period the value of money had fallen by more than half. Moreover the price of land rose as its supply dwindled, and in the same way the value of the pastures 1 forests, and hence of the servitudes, increased.
