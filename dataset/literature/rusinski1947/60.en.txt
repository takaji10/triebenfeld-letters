The hearths are there divided into the following categories: I. “manor houses and manorial buildings, that is to say the palace, the outbuildings, the folwarks, the stables, the sheepfolds, reckoned as one hearth”; 2. “the rectory and other parsonage buildings”; 3. “monasteries, monks, nuns”; 4. “breweries”; 5. “taverns”; 6. “mills”; 7. “Jewish hearths”; 8. “hearths in village cottages, futors and forest cottages possessing land”: a. “a cottage with two farmers”, b. “a cottage with one farmer”; 9. “hearths in cottages and garden cottages”: a. “a garden cottage”, b. “a cottage without land and without garden”; 10. “hearths in the houses where craftsmen dwell”: a. “manufacturing hearths”, b. “craftsmen's hearths”. Table II presents the occupational composition of the population of the Olęder settlements of Kościan county, computed on the basis of the population table, supplemented as need arose by the hearth table.

The corresponding source for Poznań county is lacking. The occupational and social composition of the inhabitants of the settlements seized in 1772 is furnished by the Prussian classification records. Another, still earlier source, namely the inventory of the Sieraków estate of 1759, contains a detailed specification of the population of the eight Olęder settlements belonging to that estate. Both sources, as far as their lists of population are concerned, are considerably more complete than the Tol.; they do not, however, agree with it in point of time. It may nevertheless be assumed with great probability that in 1759–89 no deep, fundamental changes occurred in the occupational and social composition of the Olęder population of the settlements enumerated above; on this assumption we have brought together the data of the two sources in Table III. A comparison of the percentage results of the two tables will reveal certain differences, which however become minimal if we take into account that in Tab. II the shepherds, given separately in Tab. III, have been assigned to the agricultural population, as have also part of the innkeepers, the millers and the teachers or readers. In both tables the dominant significance of agriculture in the Olęder villages is striking. The figure of those engaged in agriculture was in reality still larger, for account must be taken of the fact that a considerable part of the members of the families classed as occupationally passive in other occupations was likewise employed in agriculture, if not constantly, then at least in the seasons of more pressing work. It may therefore be safely assumed that the number of persons employed in agriculture (active and passive) reached 98% of the total Olęder population.
