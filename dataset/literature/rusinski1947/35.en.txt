The Olęder colonization was caused by the same factor that marked out the roads for many other mass movements of population, namely the difference in the conditions of life in the earlier fatherland and in the place marked out for emigration. This difference aroused the striving to improve the hitherto existing conditions of existence by way of emigration. Conditions of life, in the understanding of the time, meant above all the economic and religious situation. The better that situation presented itself in the country regarded as the goal of emigration, the stronger the intensity assumed by the movement of emigration. Naturally the vision of prosperity and wellbeing in an unknown country could also be created by skilful, appropriate advertising emanating from that country. That too could set emigration in motion. If, however, reality proved considerably humbler, emigration in the long run was unthinkable. The Olęder colonization, based in large measure on emigration from Germany, continued with unabated force right up to the partitions; this would not have been the case had the settlers not found in their new fatherland distinct advantages and an improvement in their condition.[^35-7] The very fact alone

The settlers belonged to the group of Bamberg emigrants who settled in the villages of the town of Poznań. The remaining "bambrzy", however, although they likewise received location charters (Bir, pp. 348–367), were settled on conditions essentially different from those of the Olęders. Here the community did not appear as a solidary whole; at times charters were even issued individually for particular settlers; there was no self-government developed as among the Olęders, and the sołtys was mostly appointed by the municipal organs and had the character of a municipal official.
