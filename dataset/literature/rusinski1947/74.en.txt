2. Bee-keeping. Bees in hives the Olęders might keep in any number, without any dues.[^74-4] Sometimes, however, they were forbidden to keep bees in tree-hollow hives (barcie).[^74-5] The products of this keeping (honey, wax) they might dispose of at will. Exceptionally, in the Ol. Złotkowo they were bound to sell the wax exclusively at the manor.[6][^74-6] The income from bee-keeping did not, however, constitute a serious item in the Olęders' economy. A case such as that of the sołtys in the Ol. Kliny, who possessed 11 bee-hives, the sources quote as a kind of curiosity.[^74-7] Professional bee-keepers there were none among the Olęders.

3. Hunting and fishing. The right of hunting game upon their own land did not belong to the Olęders. They were permitted only to hunt birds. They might indeed also organise hunts of wild boar, bears and wolves, but the animals killed they were bound, on pain of severe penalties, to deliver to the manor. The lord on his part promised them in return a certain reward, not more closely defined.[^74-8] Only in the Ol. Ujskie was the right of hunting unrestricted.[^74-9] The Olęders' income from hunting, within limits thus curtailed, presumably had no great significance. It is obvious that the relations described above were not favourable to the formation, among the Olęders, of a group of professional hunters.
