# Rusiński (1947), p. 76
# translation by Claude

4. Industry. a. Tavern-keeping. Taverns stood in almost all the Olęder settlements. They were, however, always, as it were, branch establishments of the manorial breweries (mielcuchy), for the propination monopoly was in force in the Olęder settlements and the innkeepers received ready-made manorial liquors, which they were bound to retail in the settlement at a price fixed in advance.

Exceptionally in Ostrówek the settlers, to whom Kerol Maciej Grudziński in the founding document of 1750 made over the brewery with its requisite equipment and a granary for the storing of malt, themselves engaged in the brewing of beer. Production was here organized in the following manner. The individual settlers were bound to deliver to the brewery, from every huba,[^76-1] [—] wierteles of barley and the quantity of hops needed for the brewing of [—] barrels of beer.[^76-2] They were obliged to haul the wood in turn in the amount of 2–3 sążni; for this they received the aforementioned 10 barrels of beer. The rest was presumably retailed by the innkeeper, whom the lord freed from rent, while his remaining dues the sołtys, the ławniks and the community were bound to take upon themselves. The incomes from the tapping of liquors (the production of vodka was similarly organized) the sołtys and the ławniks were bound to divide evenly among all the members of the community. We have here, then, to do with a typical co-operative organization.[^76-3] The organization of the sale of the manorial liquors presented itself in the Olęder settlements as follows. At the founding of the settlement the lord set aside ½ of a włóka (huba), free of rent, for the innkeeper. One of the members of the community paid the okupne, settled on the land,[^76-5] and even before the tavern was built began the tapping of the manorial liquors[^76-4]. His remuneration, besides the aforementioned ½ of a włóka free of all rents,[3] often embraced also the so-called świętojanka, that is the 21st barrel or, respectively, the 21st garniec of the beer or vodka tapped.[4] In rare cases other advantages were granted to the innkeeper.
