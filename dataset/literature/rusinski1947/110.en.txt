Not badly either, as may be inferred from the old inventory, did the Olęders fare in the Sieraków estate. Worst of all was the state of affairs in the settlements of the northern and western part of the Poznań county. The majority of the settlers, because of the wretched soil, was condemned to mere vegetation. Certain incomes were afforded only by the keeping of cattle and sheep on the basis of the pasture servitudes, and, in a few cases, the growing of garden plants (vegetables, root crops). The standard of living of the Olęders did not at all differ from the scale of life of the rest of the rural population. In the one case as in the other the basis of nourishment was grain and its products. According to the classification records in the Stróżewskie Ol., where the quality of the soil was still tolerable, the annual consumption per person amounted to[^110-1] ? korcy of rye, one korzec of barley and 1½ korca of peas; in the Bronice Ol. 4 korcy of rye and 1 korzec of barley. Of the consumption of meat by the settlers it is difficult to say anything definite; in any case it must be stated that the keeping of swine was not here at all greater than in the corvée villages. On the whole, however, the Olęder farm developed in better conditions than the farm of the corvée peasants. The economic position of the Olęders was privileged, if we take into account at least their personal independence, the ownership of the land, buildings and livestock, the possibility of freely disposing of the fruit of their work (freedom of trade), the low rents, the minimal labour dues. The Olęder therefore, although he sat on unfruitful soil, had in comparison with the corvée kmieć greater chances of attaining prosperity.
