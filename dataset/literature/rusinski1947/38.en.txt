The complainants received here, even before the delivery of the proper judgment, the royal glejt, binding them until the resolution of the matter to only those services which they had been paying before the dispute began, and moreover protecting them against possible acts of repression on the part of the starosta. How these relations presented themselves in the noble estates it is difficult to establish. The old Polish judicial process made no provision in its rules for the peasant population that was not subject. Presumably there existed in this respect some judicial practice, but cases of this kind have not yet been sufficiently investigated. Probably in practice there prevailed the tendency to treat free peasants on a par with subjects. As regards the Olęders, it seems a certain thing that they were granted the right of independently filing complaints and manifests before the grod and land offices,[^38-1] even against their own lord. Such a right, as follows from the practice applied at entries into the land books and grod books, did not belong to subject peasants; these could give testimony before the grod or land offices only in the presence of their lords. Very unclear is the question of the appearance of the Olęders before the public courts. In one case, in which we succeeded in finding the relevant court records, an Olęder who had himself filed a manifest against his former lord and the Olęder community for the unlawful removal from the farm he occupied appears at the court hearings "in assistentia Illustrissimi Lucae Bniński Judicis Terrestris Posnaniensis" [in the assistance of the most illustrious Łukasz Bniński, judge of the Poznań land court].[^38-3] There arises here the possibility that either that Olęder[^38-2] . / 628,
