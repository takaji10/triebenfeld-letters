# Rusiński (1947), p. 107
# translation by Claude

The Constitution of 1768 abolished the obligation of paying church dues by the dissenters. The Olęders took advantage of it[^107-1]. According to Kniat's calculations[^107-2], in 1773 per włóka of land in the rent-paying villages of the notecki district there fell: in the szlachta estates 26–66 gr, in the state estates 19 gr, sometimes indeed nothing at all was paid, in the church estates 7 thalers 42 gr Prussian as tithe. In this last case, however, the payers were almost always Catholics; besides, it is difficult here to distinguish the dues paid iure parochiali [by parochial right] from the manorial obligations. If we take into account that 1 in the rent-paying szlachta and state settlements part of the inhabitants was Catholic, there will fall to the evangelical population a quite minimal sum.

In the northern part of the Poznań county, in places where the parochial administration had been organised by the evangelical church, it also laid claim to services on the part of the Olęders. These services could have a compulsory character, just like the services in favour of the Catholic parish church[^107-3]. It even happened that the Olęders were bound to services in favour of both churches at the same time[^107-4]. The services in favour of the evangelical church sometimes reached a considerable amount[^107-5].

H. Public obligations

The Olęders, like the other peasants, were obliged to pay certain public dues. The charter for Góździn of 1735 states this as follows: And inasmuch as the Commonwealth through the sejm, or the voivodeships through the sejmiks, shall have ordained by their resolution any taxation for the new colonies, then the aforesaid Olęders shall have to comply with that resolution, having no prejudice to themselves thereby, for, dwelling in one fatherland with the others, we must bear

G. 5, cf. also Schmidt, Geschichte, p. 403) the Olęders were bound to give the pastor: 1 zł per huba (part in good coin, part in szeląg coin), for every 2 hubs 1 cart of hay, a New Year offering, a tribute in eggs at Easter. Moreover, at each of the 3 feasts: Christmas, Easter and Whitsuntide, every farmer was bound to give as an offering 1½ gr. Apart from this, for the individual religious services, rather high dues were in force.
