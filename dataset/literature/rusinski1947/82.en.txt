Forest servitudes. By these we shall understand the Olęders' entitlements to the use of the lord's forest within defined limits — apart from the pasture servitudes. The range of the Olęders' forest servitudes presented itself one way in the rent-free years, another in the later years. In the first period the Olęders always had the right of taking timber of every kind from the lord's forest, provided there was not enough of it in sufficient quantity and quality upon the lands allotted to them.[5] After the term of the wolnizna had expired[^82-6][^82-7], there belonged to the Olęders as a rule the right of gathering in the lord's forest timber for fuel, while timber for building and rods for fences they might not cut without the consent of the manor.[8] Permission to make use, to any extent, of timber of every kind was a rarity. Often the Olęders received timber free of charge for the repair of buildings,[^82-8] or against the event of a fire, in this last case by a separate agreement with the lord. In general it was a principle that the felling of timber had to be preceded[^82-9] by the obtaining of the appropriate “receipt” from the lord.[19] The sale of timber from the lord's forests was strictly forbidden.[^82-10] In some charters we meet also with permission to gather in the lord's forest pine-needles (igliwie) for manure.[^82-11] The question of the gathering of mushrooms and berries, however, was not regulated in the charters.[^82-12] Cases in which the Olęders were at all de-
