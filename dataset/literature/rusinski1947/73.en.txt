Returning to the first kind of restriction, it must be said that these always had the character of a payment for the pasture servitudes; for whenever the Olęders were obliged to pay dues for the keeping of sheep in general, there belonged to them the right of pasturage. The pasture servitudes and the payments connected with them will be discussed elsewhere. Here we may remark that dues payable upon the total number of sheep were rare; as a rule a certain contingent was entirely exempt from dues, and only upon the surplus were additional rents paid. On the average the limit was the figure of 50[^73-1] sheep. It could of course happen that the relative restrictions, in the form of additional rents for stock-raising, had in fact an absolute character, when they were set so high that, their existence notwithstanding, stock-raising presented no interest whatever. As a rule the Olęders were allowed to keep[^73-2] [—] sheep upon one huba. Exceptionally permission was given for the keeping of 60 or even 100 sheep,[^73-3] while sometimes the norm was lower: 30, 25, 20 sheep, for example.[^73-4] In the Olęders of the Sieraków estate it was at first free to keep sheep without any dues; but in order to prevent abuses on the part of the smallholders, [the free contingent was fixed] in proportion to the area of the farms. Probably the number of sheep possessed by the larger farmers was decisive. Sheep-keeping there attained a great flowering; according to the inventory of 1759 there fell, upon the average, 22 sheep and 9 lambs[^73-5] to the Olęder włóka. In 1776 dues were introduced in the amount of 15 gr from every sheep above 20 head; it is not known, however, how this affected the extent of the keeping.

The pasturing of the Olęder sheep was as a rule in the hands of a common communal herdsman. Sometimes there was kept in addition a separate herdsman for the cows, for the pigs, even for the horses.[8] The income of the Olęders from stock-raising was thus diminished by the remuneration of the herdsmen. The sources, however, give no information as to the kind and amount of this remuneration. Nor is it easy to compute the herdsmen's remaining incomes.
