For their administrative and judicial acts the communal organs received a certain remuneration, usually from the community, exceptionally from the lord. This remuneration comprised certain fixed annual fees (from the farm 2[^54-2], or from the unit of land[^54-3]), further fees for the individual complaints (Klugegeld).[^54-4] The costs of the journeys of the sołtys or the ławniks on the community's business were borne by all the farmers proportionally. In the Stare Oberskie Olęders the lord exempted each sołtys for the time being from all labour dues 5.[^54-5] c. The communal treasury, insurance against fire. The revenues from the administrative fees and judicial fines formed the communal chest. The receipts were kept in a special box kept by the sołtys, of which the ławniks held the key,[^54-6] or, which happened rarely, the lord.[^54-7] Before the expiry of the term of office each sołtys with the ławniks made up the annual accounts, either before the whole community or at the manor. The revenues from the judicial fines usually fell in their entirety to the lord;[^54-8] at times, however, the lord reserved to himself only a part of the revenues,[^54-9] or else applied them to some definite purpose, e.g. the maintenance of the ditches within the settlement.[^54-10] It is possible that in the communal chest there were also kept, where they existed, funds for the maintenance of the communal herdsman, the night watchman, the teacher or the lector. The chest revenues could also be applied to replenishing the so-called fire chest. Such a chest was founded on the lord's order, among others, in Ol. Radoszkowskie.[^54-11]

[^54-4]: In Ol. Chromiec each party paid 12 szelągs per complaint; of this the sołtys took 4 szelągs and the rest the panel of judges. In Ol. Nowodąbrowskie (Jastrzębnickie) the fee was 0 grosz on each occasion. Of course the losing party was ordered to reimburse the costs to the opponent.
