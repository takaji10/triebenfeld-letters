# Rusiński (1947), p. 89
# translation by Claude

Sometimes the lord exempted the Olęders from the payment of market dues[^89-1] in the towns lying within his estate. One must suppose that when the Olęders were not exempt from the dues, the rates applied generally were binding on them. The sources, however, say nothing about this.

Among the administrative dues the greatest significance belonged to the fee on the sale of farms. Its amount and kinds have already been given above, on p. 66. Apart from this, in a few settlements we encounter the dues already mentioned for the confirmation of more important legal acts (records, wills, divisions of property, marriage contracts). In the Milczewskie and Lubaskie Ol. the lord collected 11 tymfs (14 zł) for each act[^89-2]. In the Stare Borujskie Ol. we encounter a formally administrative fee, though by its character very close to a rent: the Olęders paid it every 50 years at the rate of 36 zł per huba in return for the manor's recognition of their right of ownership to the land they held[^89-3]. Maas's remark that the Olęders paid dues when marrying their daughters off outside the community rests on a misunderstanding[^89-4] 3. Labour dues. In the charters for the two earliest Olęder settlements on the territory of the former voivodeship of Poznań manorial obligations in the form of labour dues do not appear. In the Ol. Róża, located in 1624 in the krobielewskie estate, we already find the obligation to repair the bridge over the river Warta[^89-5], while the inventory of the Wieleń estate of 1653 enumerates among the Olęders' obligations, among others, one day of labour and the cartage of 3 loads of wood. Labour dues on a wider scale we find only in the charters for settlements located in the eighteenth century. Attention must, however, be drawn to the fact that their character is different from that of the serf labour of the enserfed peasants; the labour dues always burdened the Olęders to a small extent, were for the most part strictly fixed in the charter and arose by way of mutual agreement of the parties, and were not conditioned by a personal dependence on the lord, as were the labour dues of the enserfed population. In accordance with the provisions of the charters we can distinguish labour dues fixed in a definite quantity and labour dues binding in an unspecified quantity.
