In the later settlements dues in kind and labour dues also appear. As Kniat has shown, who on the basis of the classification records of 1773 investigated and presented statistically the manorial obligations[^100-1] of the rent-paying settlements of the so-called notecki district, which were mostly Olęder settlements, the dues in kind and the labour dues of these settlements, converted into cash, amounted to 5–30% of the sum of the rent. If we assume that certain fees, e.g. for the servitudes and others, may have been included in the rent, the total value of the dues in kind, labour dues[^100-2] and fees will still not exceed 10% of the rent sum. These results find confirmation in other sources[^100-3]. Of course there are[^100-4] localities in which the dues in kind and the labour dues have a considerably greater value in relation to the rent, but there the rent is lower than the average given by us[^100-5]. In the final conclusion it must be stated that the increase of the manorial obligations of the Olęders was as a rule only nominal, while the real value of the obligations on the whole diminished. This fact will stand out still more sharply if we compare it with the process of the continual increase of the burdens of the enserfed population 5. The legal-moral aspect of the question presents itself somewhat differently. Between the lord and the Olęders disputes sometimes arose, which after the occupation of the country by Prussia expressed themselves in a whole series of lawsuits. The complaining party was most often the Olęders, although it was sometimes the reverse[^100-6]. Three basic causes of the lawsuits may be distinguished: a) the unclear formulation of the obligations 1 rights of the Olęders in the charters, b) the desire for profit or ill will on the part of the lord, c) the same on the part of the Olęders.
