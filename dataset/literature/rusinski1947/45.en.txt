# Rusiński (1947), p. 45
# translation by Claude

following the definition of the Prussian territorial law (Landrecht) of 1721.1,[^45-1] describes this kind of relation to the land as "perpetual rent law"[^45-2] (Erbzinsrecht), by virtue of which the holder could dispose of the land at will, provided only that he paid to the lord the services due. The rent-holder was bound to bestow on the land such care "as if it were his own property". Guradze Fr. presents the matter similarly;[^45-3] in Schmidt, on the other hand, we have the concepts of perpetual rent law mixed together with the concept of perpetual lease, which had a different significance.[^45-4] The matter presented itself differently in those few cases in which an Olęder sat on unredeemed land. Here the relation of the Olęder to the land was rather like a perpetual lease.

It is characteristic that in the charters, apart from a few exceptions 5, no sanctions were provided for the event of the Olęders[^45-5] failing to pay the services provided in the charter. This is explained by the fact that the Olęders answered for the manorial duties for the most part solidarily, so that the obligation of bearing the services for a dilatory Olęder, or one not in a position to pay, fell upon the remaining members of the community.

The charters enumerated in detail the rights belonging to the Olęders by virtue of their possession of the land: "of which land both they themselves and their possessors may make use at their pleasure; namely it shall be free for them to sell it, to give, to bestow, to barter away and to turn to their own best advantage". The right of disposing of the land was, however, not un-[^45-6]limited. Beside substantive restrictions on the alienation of the land we have a series of formal conditions which the seller, and at times also the purchaser, had to fulfil before the deed of sale was recognized by the lord as valid.
