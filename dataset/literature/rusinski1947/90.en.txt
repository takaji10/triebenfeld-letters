The number of working days, as indeed the quantity of the other labour dues, fluctuated within very wide limits. Beside cases in which there was no labour due at all, we find a case in which it was rendered at the rate of 50 days[^90-1] yearly per huba[^90-2]. On average the number of days of labour due amounted to 6–12 days per huba; lower labour dues or their absence we encounter in cases where other labour dues were also rendered, not specified in days (e.g. cartages). Usually the kind of work and the season of the year in which the work was to be performed were specified (ploughing, carting manure, sowings, the reaping of grain, haymaking etc.). More rarely we meet the obligation to perform certain agricultural works on a defined area of land, that is, the so-called jutrzyny [dawn labours] 3. In exceptional cases[^90-3] an intermediate combination was applied, the so-called apportioned labour due (robocizna wydziałowa), consisting in this, that a certain number of working days was fixed, but at the same time a minimum of work to be performed in the course of the day was specified. Thus, for instance, in one of the settlements the Olęders were obliged to “plough for 3 days, 12 ridges at a time”[^90-4], in another case it was their duty, from St Adalbert's day to St Martin's day, to plough a morg of land every week[^90-5]. The jutrzyny usually covered ploughing only (30[^90-6] to 60[^90-7] ridges), sometimes mowing only[^90-8], more rarely ploughing and mowing[^90-9], and exceptionally all the works except sowing[^90-10]. Jutrzyna labour dues could also consist in the mowing and drying of hay on a defined piece of meadow[^90-11]. In the Cisiogórskie Ol. the Olęders were further obliged to keep the meadows allotted to them “under watch … lest they become overgrown”[^90-12]. To this category of obligations also belonged
