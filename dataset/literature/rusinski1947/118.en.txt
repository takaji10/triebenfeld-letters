The placement in the charters of the few, above-mentioned restrictions in connection with the obligations towards the church suggests the thought that they were introduced into the charters in consequence of the interventions of the proper parish priests. Sometimes the lords took up no position of their own at all in the religious matters of the Olęders, recommending that in these matters they should proceed as “the Commonwealth permits” 4. The sources contain not a single mention of any control of the religious life of the Olęders undertaken on the lord's initiative with a view to its conformity with the provisions of the charter. Nor is there to be found in the sources a single case of a later restriction of the freedom of confession guaranteed to the Olęders in the location charter. As for the above-mentioned restrictions contained in the location charters, these could not be imposed on the Olęders, as free men able to choose their future lord at will, without their consent.

Where there were particularly numerous clusters of Olęder settlements with an evangelical population, there the Olęders, with the manor's consent, built themselves churches and maintained at their own cost clergymen of their confession 5.
