# Rusiński (1947), p. 47
# translation by Claude

7 to every Olęder "to pay off the field without a report to the manor and to sell it".1[^47-1] The same condition often bound the alienator also in relation to the community. The deed of sale had to be made in the communal office, and if the community possessed a community book, the appropriate entry had to be made. The purchaser was bound to pay the price of the land into the communal office, which covered from it all the fees arising from the sale, paid the debts contracted by the alienator, and only then paid out the remainder to him.[^47-2]

2. The alienator or the purchaser were bound to pay certain fees to the lord and the community. The fee most often collected by the lord from the alienator was the 10th grosz, more rarely the 12th grosz,[^47-3] exceptionally 5% of the sum of the sale.[^47-4] At times the lord collected a fixed fee, e.g. 72 zł in Ostrów and in Borgowo (1785),[^47-5] 2 thalers in the Chełmskie[^47-6] and Stare Łoskońskie Olęders,[^47-7] 1 thaler in the Budziszewskie Olęders,[^47-8] 1 red złoty

in Rudka Olęders.[^47-9] In the Budziszewskie and Stare Łoskońskie Olęders the obligation to pay the fee burdened the alienator and the purchaser solidarily. In the Chełmskie Olęders and in Złotów the seller and the buyer covered the fee in equal shares. In the charter for Czapury we have the provision that the settler "on sale or gift shall be bound to content the Convent".[^47-10] The purchaser was usually bound to buy for the community one, and exceptionally 2 barrels of the so-called introductory beer (German Nachbarbier) from the lord's brew.[^47-11]

3. Among the formal conditions must also be reckoned the right of pre-emption, naturally provided that it operated only upon the offer of a price equal to that of the highest bidder.
