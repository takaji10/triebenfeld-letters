Other occupations. The community might sometimes keep its own watchman. In Popowo the lord assigned to such a watchman, who was at the same time to have an eye upon the manorial buildings, “a piece of land”.[^80-4] A certain percentage of the population of the settlements was formed by teachers or readers, performing the functions of schoolmasters and of religious ministers. Their economic situation will be discussed on later pages. Here we note only that they drew their principal incomes from the land assigned to them by the lord, free of all rents. D. The Olęders' rights of user upon the lord's possessions (servitudes)

Besides the incomes from their own economy the Olęders drew profits from the lord's possessions. These flowed from the servitudes, chiefly the pasture, the forest and the fishing servitudes. I. The pasture servitudes were the foundation of the stock-raising economy of the Olęders. Cases in which the Olęders had no right at all of pasturing their herds upon the lord's lands occurred extremely rarely.[^80-5] As a rule the Olęders had the right of pasturing cattle in any number, and that often without payment;[^80-6] the right of pasturing sheep was in rare cases wholly forbidden;[^80-7] most often it was
