A somewhat higher rent we note in the settlements founded in the 1780s; it amounts there sometimes to 75–88 zł,[^86-3] that is 60–70 tymfs, reckoning the tymf at 38 gr. An exceptionally low rent in the Ol. Radoszkowskie amounts, from the huba (włóka), to 16 zł (in 1781); in the Włościejewskie Olęders (in 1783), on the other hand, we have dues quite high, amounting to 100 tymfs from the huba. About this sum the rent fluctuates in the settlements founded in the last quarter of the eighteenth century.[^86-4]

Payment in money was made for the most part also for the pasture servitudes. Separate payments for pasture appear at the end of the seventeenth century. They persist until the partitions in the amount of 2 thalers or 10 tymfs at the fixed rate per huba, or else 6 to 10 gr for every head of cattle. In the Ol. Stodólsko the fixed payment amounted to only 1 thaler, but the use of the pastures was limited in time.[5] In the Ol. Wymyślanka[^86-5] the Olęders were bound to pay both kinds of dues simultaneously.[^86-6] A manifestly punitive character had the sum fixed in the Ol. Głęboczek. There it was permitted to keep 50 sheep free of payment, while for every sheep beyond that a fee of as much as 1 tymf was payable.[^86-7] In a few cases the payment for pasture was already included in the general rent.[^86-8] Sometimes, again, a single payment was made for the pasture and forest servitudes together.[^86-9]

In several settlements the Olęders paid to the lord the so-called stróżowe (watch-money). This was assessed either upon every huba of land (thus, for example, in Krzemieniewo 1 zł was paid from every huba[^86-10]), or the whole settlement was bound to pay a certain fixed lump sum.
