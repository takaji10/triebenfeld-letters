In the light of these figures there fade the assertions of German scholarship concerning the pioneering role of the colonists of German nationality in the tillage of the soils of western Wielkopolska. At the same time these figures undermine the assertions of Maas concerning the great afforestation of the Wielkopolskan landscape in the period before modern colonization[^66-1], and justify the critical position of Hładyłowicz.[^66-2]

The drainage of the land was carried out in such a way that the water was either conducted by ditches to a lower-lying basin (a river, a lake), or else collected in deep ditches and pits. In the charters we occasionally find assurances that these ditches or pits would not be counted in to the Olęders in the measurement of their lands.[^66-3] Exceptionally the Olęders were permitted to dig ditches even through the land of others.[^66-4] The clearing of wooded land was accomplished probably for the most part by the felling and grubbing-up of trees and bushes.
