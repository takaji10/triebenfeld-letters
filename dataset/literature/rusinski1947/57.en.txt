For economic reasons he endeavoured to prepare for cultivation a piece of land lying in one block, and that in a form approximating a square or else a polygon. For the same reasons he set his buildings at the most convenient point, most often in the middle of the cleared land, without regard to the position of the neighbouring farms. He linked his farm by a separate way to the main track running through the settlement or at the least distance from it. Cartographic examples of both forms of settlement are given at the end of the book.

Beyond the two described categories of settlement there occurred Olęder settlements of other types. These arose in a twofold manner. In the first case, when an existing village was transferred to Olęder law, or the Olęders bought up a village left empty together with its buildings, or else, taking over a deserted village already without buildings, restored its original shape, there arose an Olęder settlement in the form existing previously. In the second case, when the Olęders had to conform to the special requirements of the lord, there arose a planned settlement, of shapes traced out in an artificial manner.[^57-1] 3. Building. In the sources we find scanty information on the manner of erecting the farm buildings in the Olęder settlements, on their shape and dimensions, and on the material used in their construction. In principle the complex of buildings belonging to one farm consisted of a dwelling cottage, a barn and a byre. Cart-sheds are met with more rarely. The byre was often built on to the cottage; exceptionally a second byre, or a separate granary, entered into the complex of buildings. The cottage was built either of logs or of sawn boards, or as a clay-walled cottage, and so likewise the byre and the barn. There also occurred buildings simply put together out of brushwood.[^57-2] Part of a building might be of timber, while the remainder was plastered with clay. For the most part the buildings had a covering of straw (sheaves), more rarely of reed, and exceptionally the more considerable buildings were roofed with shingles.[^57-3]

[^57-1]: According to the charter for the Krosien (AW. Krosna, A. 2) the Olęders “are to build in a line, in the order appointed for them by me (i.e. the lord)”. In the Kiączyńskie Olęders (AW. A. 1) “the buildings are to be set up along the ditch by which the water has from of old been accustomed to flow to the great pond called Kiełpin... in good array and order, so that they form a village”. In Góździn (AW. A. I, fol. 71) “I do not permit them to build otherwise than in a cluster, in the likeness of other villages”. In the Borkowskie Olęders (AW. A. 1) the Olęders “ought to build themselves up as is proper, and their homesteads are not to be built far one from another, but in two rows, so that on one row stand the dwelling houses, on the other the barns”. The same in the Dymaczewskie Olęders (Rel. Posn. nr 489, 1751, fol. 69).
[^57-2]: In Kawczyn (Rel. Cost. nr 196, 1761, fol. 477).
