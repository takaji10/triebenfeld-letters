# Rusiński (1947), p. 94
# translation by Claude

they were always obliged to drink the lord's liquors[^94-1]. Where the Olęders had the freedom of making liquors, it was usually restricted to production for their own needs, possibly for sale within the settlement[^94-2]. In isolated cases the Olęders enjoyed an unrestricted right of making and selling liquors[^94-3], usually, however, for a separate annual fee, and that usually in money[^94-4], exceptionally in malt[^94-5]. To the earliest-located Wieleń Olęders, who at first could brew beer for their own needs without any fees, there was imposed in the eighteenth century the obligation to buy 5 barrels of beer from the manorial brewery[^94-6]. The propination compulsion also covered spirits[^94-7].

From the propination compulsion, which restricted consumption to the lord's liquors, while the purchase of liquors nevertheless depended on the will of the settlers, there must be distinguished the compulsion to buy a certain definite quantity of liquors, that is, indirectly, a compulsion of consumption[^94-8]. Such a compulsion we meet rarely in the Olęder settlements, and in a mitigated form. Thus, for instance, in the Ol. Sikorca an Olęder arranging a christening or a wedding was obliged to take a barrel of beer at the normal rate[^94-9]. Here one would also have to include the obligation, mentioned above, of buying the lord's beer in return for exemption from the propination compulsion. Similarly there would have to be classified the cases in which the purchaser of an Olęder farm was obliged to take a barrel of the so-called introductory beer[^94-10].

If there was not a sufficient quantity of liquors in the lord's tavern or malt-kiln, the Olęders could obtain them elsewhere, usually with the knowledge[^94-11] or even with the consent[^94-12] of the manor.
