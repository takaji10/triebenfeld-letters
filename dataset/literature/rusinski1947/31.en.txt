By the Holendrzy, at least in part, there were probably also settled the later lowland settlements on the Warta. On the other hand it seems a certain thing that the settlements

located in forests, on clearances, were colonised in the seventeenth century with the help of German settlers. In relation to these settlements, just as to the later ones originating from the beginning of the eighteenth century, the sources give almost no surnames of Polish sound. Already by the middle of the eighteenth century, however, we meet not only new settlements in which the presence of the Polish element can be established beyond doubt, but Poles also appear[^31-1] in those settlements in which they had not been before. In the period of the partitions it is already difficult to find a settlement in which not one of the inhabitants bore a Polish surname. The national relations of the Olęder settlements in this period have repeatedly been discussed in the hitherto existing literature. German scholarship mostly identified the Olęders with the Germans,[^31-2] and only a few authors noted that there also existed Polish Olęders.[^31-3] W. Maas, who has lately occupied himself with this question, expresses the supposition that they made up up to 25% of the total Olęder population.[^31-4]

[^31-1]: In the list of the population of Ol. Nowe Dwory of 1653 there figures only a single surname that was named in the charter of 1601.
[^31-2]: Thus presented the matter Holsche (Der Neizdistrikt, p. 223), and Schmidt (Geschichte, p. 348 ff.). Particularly interesting in this respect is the work of Schmidt. The author, although in the introduction he assures us of his objectivity, subsequently passed over in silence in the further course of the work the mentions of Polish Olęders found in the previously published works of Beheim-Schwarzbach and Fr. Guradze, identifying all the Olęders with the Germans. Moreover he even adduced source information that did not exist. According to him, for example, the colonies of Gross and Klein Lubs, lying in the neighbourhood of Wieleń, already figure in the inventory of the Wieleń estate of 1653 as German settlements, whereas in reality these settlements were founded a good 130 years later, already after the country had been taken over by Prussia. Nor did Kótschke make use of the results of earlier research; in the Geschichte der Ostdeutschen Kolonisation published jointly with Lbert, p. 151, he lays down briefly: "holender: deutscher Kolonist" [the Holender: a German colonist]. On the same position stood also Warschauer (Geschichte, p. 103), Liick (Deutsche Aufbaukrifte), and Schulz (Die zweite deutsche Ostsiedlung, p. 74).
