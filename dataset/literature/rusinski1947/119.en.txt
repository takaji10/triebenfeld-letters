The sources contain no closer data concerning the pastors; it is to be supposed, however, that they were persons as a rule enlightened and prepared for the exercise of clerical functions. Of course exceptions occurred here too[^119-1]. The relations of the pastors with the community were not always satisfactory. Mention has already been made above of the lawsuits of the Herbardowo community with the local clergyman. A wholly unfavourable testimonial was given to his parishioners by the pastor of the evangelical community of Piotrowo, to which the Króteckie and Miłkowskie Ol. also belonged[^119-2].

The ordinary religious needs of the Olęders were fully satisfied by the local teacher, often called the “ferlezer” (German Vorleser, Lektor, Kantor). He held the Sunday service in the community (read the gospel, delivered the sermon, sang religious songs), conducted the burial rites (escorted the dead to the cemetery, sang songs and delivered funeral orations), and taught the children the principles of religion. The defects in the choice of teachers have already been mentioned above. In consequence they often lacked the sufficient moral qualities for guiding the spiritual life of the community. Hence also Stenger speaks of them with irony[^119-3].
