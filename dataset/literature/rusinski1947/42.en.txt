Exceptionally, even at the location of new settlements, the lords took into account the possibility of settling subjects in them, as is proved by the following stipulation in the charter of 1729 for Ol. Kotusz: "...it is added to this that these Olęders are to belong to no other duties than the dues expressed above, and likewise their possessors or successors are to remain free people, except for those who had already been subjects before".[^42-1] Whether subjects were in fact settled there seems very doubtful. In any case the sources do not cast doubt on the principle itself of the personal freedom of the Olęders. This principle naturally extended also to the families of the Olęders. It is harder to establish whether the remaining, landless, socially lower stratum of the Olęder population was also personally free. Direct data in this respect we possess only in relation to the Sieraków estate. The inventory of that estate of 1759 contains a division of the whole population of the settlements into free and subject; the whole landless population of the Olęder settlements, with the exception of 1 family,[^42-2] is personally free. Only in the already mentioned Ol. Chorzępowa do we find 2 subject families.[^42-3] This confirms the supposition that 1 the landless inhabitants of the settlements were regarded as personally free with very few exceptions. Personal freedom distinguished the Olęders positively among the subject population. In passing, however, we shall note here that, as the not yet worked-up inventories[^42-4] and lists[^42-5] show, the number of non-subjects among the remaining peasant population was greater than it has hitherto been estimated.[^42-6] In the present state of research, conducting comparisons loaded with tendentiousness between the Olęders and the rest of the peasant population, as some German authors did, seems at the least premature.
