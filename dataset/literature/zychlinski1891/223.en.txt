More than once his family itself and his friends urged him, on account of his poor health, to give up the troubles of country husbandry; he always answered that the most sacred duty of a Polish citizen was to guard the piece of native soil entrusted to him, and to keep with the Polish peasant that relation of mutual trust and attachment which alone can save our younger brethren from the treacherous whisperings of hostile elements. The late Włodzimierz Miączyński held to this principle steadfastly until his death, and for that alone he deserves honour.

"No less warmly was he attached to his family. When the death of the late Count Aleksander Miączyński orphaned his two children, Count Włodzimierz loved the two orphans with his whole soul, and from then on the one aim of his life was to prepare the most brilliant future for them both. The untimely death, in the flower of youth and grace, of his niece, the late Wanda Potworowska née Miączyńska, wife of Gustaw, dealt a heavy wound to his heart. With all the greater care he surrounded his remaining nephew, whom he had early named his heir. As guardian he administered until the coming of age of Count Władysław Miączyński his property, Pawłowo, and handed over to him this estate in model order.

"Feeling death approach, he prepared for it with Christian calm, pouring out his heart into the bosom of his companion and friend of more than ten years, the worthy canon Father Karkowski, parish priest of Trąbczyn. Almost the whole family gathered at Trąbczyn and surrounded the failing old man with the most tender care; to the last moment he kept his full presence of mind and, like an old soldier, would not die in bed but as if at his post, busy all the while with the affairs of his estates and with the future of those nearest him in view. Death overtook him sitting on a sofa and giving orders. There was barely time to hand him the cross and the blessed candle. Pressing the image of the Saviour to his stiffening lips, he died as he had lived: "with God and with honour", as was rightly and beautifully written on the wreath that adorned the coffin.

"That the gentry of the neighbourhood and the clergy would gather in numbers to pay the last service to so worthy a man, an officer of 1831, could not be doubted [printed "watpić"]; but what warmed every heart was the sight, beside the dead body of their lord and benefactor, of several hundred peasants in sincere tears of grief.

"The body was carried out of the larch-wood manor house to the little wooden church [printed "drewniane", for drewnianego], which by a generous gift of the deceased will soon stand in brick, late in the evening, by the light of torches and of burning tar barrels set out along the gravel road. The next day, after a solemn service, the body was laid in the chapel on the cemetery a verst from the church, where the family vault of this branch of the Counts Miączyński stands. Both from the manor to the church and from the church to the cemetery the coffin was carried in turn by the family, the gentry, the officials of the Trąbczyn estates and the peasants. Three priests spoke during the funeral ceremonies, and the words of the preacher who took leave of the body at the cemetery brought tears to all present."

Count **Mieczysław** (12th generation), born 13 February 1818, heir of the estates of Wieniec and Izbica in the Kingdom of Poland, married Marya **Słubicka** of the Prus I arms, born 1828, died 18 October 1865, buried at Trąbczyn, daughter of August, general of the former Polish army [printed "wojsk wojsk", the word twice], and of Łucya née Countess Zboińska, daughter of a voivode. Of this marriage there was only one daughter, Countess Zdzisława, born 10 June 1851, who died in the flower of her age on 23 August 1876, wife of the late Zbigniew Dzierzykraj **Morawski** of the Nałęcz arms, son of Józef, member of the Prussian House of Lords, former director-general of the Old Landschaft of Posen, and of Eugenia née Dzierzykraj Morawska, daughter of Józef, referendary of state, and of Paula née Countess Łubieńska.
