Likewise in the Warthebruch the towns of Konin, Zagö-röw and Kolo. In Kujawien, in Dombie, German clothmakers settled. Already in 1798 a German clothmakers' guild formed itself here. In the little town of Wiskitki clothmakers, cloth-shearers, dyers, linen weavers and leather tanners made themselves at home. About the same time there are found, in the Gostynin land, in Gombin, craftsmen and agricultural burghers. The same holds for Pabianice, Lentschütz, Petrikau and Brzeziny, where also individual cloth weavers found a livelihood. In Kowal and Chodetsch numerous craftsmen immigrated, among them a number of weavers. In the year 1797 the Prussian king presented to the Count of Lüttichau the Grodztwo of Kowal at the town boundary there. During the Prussian rule many evangelical civil and military officials, craftsmen, merchants and other seekers of a livelihood moved to Warschau7)

By this the soul-count of the Lutheran congregation of Warschau rose considerably. The father of the writer Bogumil Goltz, born in Warschau, was here director of the city court8)

E. Th. A. Hoffmann (1776—1822), the poet of German Romanticism, musician, writer on music and painter, stood in 1802 in the service of the Prussian administration in Plozk, then from 1804 to 1806 in Warschau, where he composed several operas and directed the musical society of the Prussian officials as conductor[^p92-9]

Besides Warschau the towns Kalisch, Peisern, Plozk, Bialystok, Tykocin, Kaiwar ja, Wilkowyszki, Wyszogrod, Rypin, Lipno and others showed many new citizens. Bialystok belonged to the Hetman Xaver Branicki, whose heirs sold the town in the year 1802 to the Prussian king for 270 970 thaler. It was the administrative center of the province of New East Prussia. This naturally had as a consequence a reinforced influx of administrative officials, craftsmen and other persons. After the Congress of Vienna the Prussian king sold Bialystok, ceded at Napoleon's instigation in the Tilsit er peace of 1807, to the Russian Emperor Alexander I. for 200 000 rubles. In New East Prussia there were settled about 1292 families with approximately 5560 souls, at an expenditure of 200 000 thaler.

The Prussian colonization in Central Poland, which embraced a period of practically only seven years (1800—1806), founded new German settlements and strengthened and enlarged those already existing through the inflow of additional forces. Everywhere there were points of departure to which new villages could attach themselves, further school and cantorate congregations as well as parishes could be organized. Thus step by step the shaping of the school, church and other life in the German-Lutheran colonies took place.
