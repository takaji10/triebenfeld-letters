The evangelical villagers received also in the „Willkür" (Village Willkür), in which the village laws found their record, the right of free exercise of religion, and that with the express assurance that they „should not be forced into another form of worship". Around 1610 there took place at the little town of Bobrowniki the founding of the settlement Bogpomöz (Gotthelf) on the Weichsel, the oldest German village in the Dobrzyner Land[^p40-2]

With this colony there stood on a small island of the Weichsel the ruins of the old knights' castle Beberen (Bobrowniki). In the year 1630 there arose, through the initiative of the Starost of Bobrowniki A. Tulibowski, the lowland village Neu-Bögpomöz. In 1625 the colony Lentzen was founded, then around 1650 Woluszewo. Up the Weichsel as far as Warschau the German peasants pressed forward. Thus in the year 1629 several families settled on the so-called Sächsische Kämpe on 136 Morgen. The everyday language of the Weichsel peasants was Low German. Beside agriculture they pursued fruit-growing (plums) and in part fishing. For centuries they had to struggle with high water and inundations. As to their origin, the German colonists who immigrated into Mittelpolen in the first half of the 17th century came from the colonies already „established" in the previous century around Thorn, Graudenz, Schwetz, Kulm, Bromberg, Dirschau and from the Danziger Great and Small Werder. If the German rural settlement movement in the 17th century was restricted exclusively to the Weichsel lowland, it extended in the following century over all areas of Mittelpolen[^p40-8]

After 1725 the German colonization set in again in increased measure. The old villages in the Weichsel lowland created new daughter colonies right and left of the river. In 1731 there arose on the lands of the lord of the manor I. Milewski Osieker Lengden. The founder of Rajszewo in the year 1775 was Archbishop Prince Poniatowski. To him the villages Wiesendorf and Skierdy near the town of Neuhof (Nowydwör) also owe their origin in 1782. The Mennonite settlement Kazun the brothers Husen laid out in 1776. A decade later there followed near Kazun the colony Dembina-Holland. Adjoining the Weichsel lowland, one began with the colonization of the Dobriner Land. Around 1700 Michael Hieronymus Podoski, Castellan of the Dobriner Land, founded Michalki (Michalke) near Rypin. According to the church chronicle of Rypin there existed already in the year 1719 the villages Oborki and Tomaszewo, likewise in 1720 Kierz and Gaj. Around 1750 were founded: Bialowiezyn, Ryszewek, Kotowo and Witkowo. The inhabitants of the Dobriner Land came presumably predominantly from West- and Ostpreußen. This is confirmed partly by the places of origin in the marriage and death registers of the evangelical church congregations, partly also by the dialect of the colonists. In Kujawien the German settlement activity took its beginning only in the second half of the 18th century. As first German village there is mentioned here in 1753 Kamieniec with the surrounding localities Sinki and Koneck-Holland. In the course of the years Kamieniec developed into a closed German colony. Around 1770 there arose the Holländerei Bycz within the area of the estates of Orle, which belonged to the bishop of Kujawien. At the same time were laid out: Gawrony, Broniszewo, Pasieka, Kölsch-Kiejsze, Tymin-Timingen, Kaczyniec and Sinogac. Around 1795 there were in Kujawien about 110 German villages, which on the average counted fewer than ten colonists.
