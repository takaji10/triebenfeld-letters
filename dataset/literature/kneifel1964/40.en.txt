The evangelical villagers received also in the village statutes ("Willkür"), in which the village laws found their record, the right of free exercise of religion, and that with the express assurance that they "shall not be compelled to any other form of worship." Around 1610, near the small town of Bobrowniki, the settlement of Bogpomöz (Gotthelf) on the Weichsel was founded, the oldest German village in the Dobrzyner Land[^p40-2]

Near this colony, on a small island in the Weichsel, lay the ruins of the old knights' castle Beberen (Bobrowniki). In the year 1630, on the initiative of the starost of Bobrowniki, A. Tulibowski, there arose the lowland village Neu-Bögpomöz. In 1625 the colony Lentzen was founded, then around 1650 Woluszewo. Up the Weichsel as far as Warschau the German peasants pressed forward. Thus in the year 1629 several families settled on the so-called Sächsische Kämpe on 136 Morgen. The everyday language of the Weichsel peasants was Low German. Besides agriculture they practiced fruit growing (plums) and in part fishing. For centuries they had to contend with high water and floods. As to their origin, the German colonists who immigrated into central Poland in the first half of the 17th century came from the colonies "established" already in the previous century around Thorn, Graudenz, Schwetz, Kulm, Bromberg, Dirschau, and from the Danziger Grosses and Kleines Werder. If the German rural settlement movement in the 17th century was confined exclusively to the Weichsel lowland, in the following century it extended over all the territories of central Poland[^p40-8]

After 1725 the German colonization resumed on an increased scale. The old villages in the Weichsel lowland created new daughter colonies right and left of the river. In 1731, on the lands of the lord of the manor I. Milewski, Osieker Lengden arose. The founder of Rajszewo in the year 1775 was Archbishop Prince Poniatowski. To him the villages Wiesendorf and Skierdy near the town of Neuhof (Nowydwör) also owe their origin in 1782. The Mennonite settlement of Kazun was established by the brothers Husen in 1776. A decade later there followed near Kazun the colony Dembina-Holland. Adjacent to the Weichsel lowland, the colonization of the Dobrzyner Land began. Around 1700 Michael Hieronymus Podoski, castellan of the Dobrzyner Land, founded Michalki (Michalke) near Rypin. According to the church chronicle of Rypin, the villages Oborki and Tomaszewo already existed in the year 1719, likewise Kierz and Gaj in 1720. Around 1750 there were founded: Bialowiezyn, Ryszewek, Kotowo, and Witkowo. The inhabitants of the Dobrzyner Land came presumably predominantly from West and East Prussia. This is confirmed partly by the places of origin in the marriage and death registers of the evangelical parishes, partly also by the dialect of the colonists. In Kujawien German settlement activity began only in the second half of the 18th century. As the first German village here, Kamieniec is mentioned in 1753, with the surrounding localities Sinki and Koneck-Holland. In the course of the years Kamieniec developed into a compact German colony. Around 1770 the Holländerei Bycz arose within the ambit of the estates of Orle, which belonged to the bishop of Kujawien. At the same time there were laid out: Gawrony, Broniszewo, Pasieka, Kölsch-Kiejsze, Tymin-Timingen, Kaczyniec, and Sinogac. Around 1795 there were in Kujawien about 110 German villages, which on the average counted fewer than ten colonists.
