# Kneifel (1964), p. 42
# translation by Claude

The colonists in the 17th and down to the end of the 18th century were exclusively Lutherans. During my earlier years-long researches I have found no trace whatsoever of any possible immigration of German Catholic settlers into central Poland in that period. In judging the German rural colonization in the period named, the following is to be stated: 1.

Leading secular and clerical personages of the Polish people called German peasants to reclaim and settle their lands and allotted to them the soil that became their new home. 2. Through the offer of settlement they granted them not only trust but also the legal possibility of putting down roots in the new home and of becoming citizens of the Polish state. 3. The colonists repaid the trust placed in them with achievement, transforming large areas of Poland into fruitful arable land and thereby promoting, to the best of their powers, the general prosperity and the flourishing development of their new adopted homeland. 4. In none of the numerous founding charters of our settlements was the demand raised that the new inhabitants of the land surrender the Lutheran faith and the German mother tongue. The Polish lords of the manor took account of these two facts. They took them into the reality of Polish Catholic life, by which the land was after all shaped. 5. The German Lutheran peasants brought material benefit not only to the Polish Catholic lords of the manor but also to the state itself. In demeanor and achievement they were not a negative and destructive but rather a state-affirming and constructive element. Their German virtues — diligence, thrift, and endurance — joined with religious earnestness, devoutness, and vitality, and borne up besides by cultural will and striving, meant for Poland a remarkable increase in inner values and gifts of which the land stood in need for its building up and development.
