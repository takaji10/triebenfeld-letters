# Kneifel (1964), p. 42
# translation by Claude

The colonists in the 17th and until the end of the 18th century were exclusively Lutherans. During my earlier years-long researches I have found no traces whatsoever of a possible immigration of German-Catholic settlers into Mittelpolen in that period. In the appraisal of the German rural colonization in the named period let it be stated:1.

Leading secular and spiritual personalities of the Polish people called German peasants to the clearing and settlement of their lands and allotted to them the soil that became for them the new homeland. 2. By the offer of settlement they gave them not only trust but also the legal possibility of striking root in the new homeland and of becoming citizens of the Polish state. 3. The colonists repaid the trust placed in them with achievement, by transforming great areas of Poland into fruitful cultivated land and thereby promoting as best they could the general prosperity and the wholesome development of their new chosen homeland. 4. In none of the numerous founding charters of our settlements was the demand raised that the new inhabitants of the land give up the Lutheran faith and the German mother tongue. The Polish lords of the manor took account of these two facts. They took them up into the reality of Polish-Catholic life, by which the land was after all stamped. 5. The German-Lutheran peasants brought material benefit not only to the Polish-Catholic lords of the manor but also to the state itself. In bearing and achievement they were not a negative and destructive but rather a state-affirming and constructive element. Their German virtues — industry, thrift and endurance —, paired with religious earnestness, inwardness and liveliness, and carried moreover by a cultural will and striving, meant for Poland a notable increase in inner values and gifts that the land needed for its building up and expansion.
