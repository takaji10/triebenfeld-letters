In the Warthebruch, which embraced the tracts of land from the former Prussian border to Leśnik (south of Uniejów), colonization rose only after 1750. In the year 1767 the estate owner and district judge S. Leszczyc-Zielonawski founded, on the lands of Slawsk, the colony Wenglewer-Hol. and the Holländereien Rumin, Kolno, and Slawsk. On September 4, 1781, King Stanislaw August confirmed the privileges of the settlement of Majdany situated in the estate complex (Schlüssel) of Chelmno. Around 1785 the villages Police, Daniszew, and Leszcze already existed. The settlement of the Kalisch land began two decades earlier than in the Warthebruch. According to the school chronicle of Czachulec-Schlachtholz, the village of the same name is said to have been founded in 1726. In 1746 Paul von Kolno-Prusinski, lord of the manor of Trombezyn, assigned to German peasants woodlands and abandoned land for reclamation. On them they created the colony Lazinsk Hol. After 1770 there were established: the large settlement of Prazuchy on the possessions of the village of Cekow, and in 1775 Zbiersk Hol. In 1782 Martin Wagrowski, lord of the manor of Sobiesenki and Stok, called upon Silesian colonists to settle on his estates. Kazimierka Stara possessed a founding charter from the year 1782. The owner of Biskupice colonized in 1784 Adler Hol. (Orlina). In the immediate vicinity of Kalisch, in contrast to the medieval colonization in this region, compact German localities were lacking. The immigrants were Silesians, with the exception of the Pomeranian colony Wielopole, Józefów, and in part Bialobloty. The settlement of the Gostyniner Land came about only in the last two decades of the 18.

century. Around 1780 the owner of the Strzelce estates founded the village of Strzelce. Likewise, soon afterwards, the lords of the manor of Sieraków and Sójki founded the colonies Sieraköw Hol. and Wierzbie Hol. Taken as a whole, up to 1795 the number of evangelical villages in the Gostyniner Land was small. The immigrants came from the regions around Schubin, Labischin, Margonin, and Wirsitz. In the vicinity of Lodz the oldest colony documented by charter, in 1782, was Ruda-Bugaj. Its founder was Walenty Chobrzynski, lord of the manor of Bruzyca. In the years 1782 to 1789 "Holländisch-Rude" was built up. A further "Dorfétablissement" was in 1784 Pustkowa-Góra, whose foundress was the superior of the regular Premonstratensian sisters of the convent at Lentschütz, D. Sokolowska. In 1791 A. Reptowski, canon of the Posen cathedral church, conferred upon the settlement of Brzyczka its chartered rights ("Gerechtsame").

The old German settlers of the Lodz region were Pomeranians from Kujawien and Silesians from the Kalisch land. They were surplus forces that the original villages gave off to their daughter colonies. From the early history of our colonies it was reported that the first pioneers dwelt in huts of earth, suffered from want of food, and succumbed to physical exertions or carried away serious damage to their health. Among the pioneers there were only a few descendants of Holländer who had moved up the Weichsel from the Danziger Werder region[^p41-4]

Ahead of them went the reputation of exemplary settlement technicians, who developed distinctive economic forms while preserving liberal rights. The name "Holländer" was later transferred to every free German colonist to whom, at his establishment for the purpose of reclaiming woodlands and abandoned land, certain basic rights were assured. The name "Holländer" was thus not a designation of ethnic affiliation but rather the expression of a legal status and activity.

[^p41-4]: 4) There can be no question of a preponderance of the "holländisches Element" ("Dutch element") in the earliest settlements, as Gen.-Supt. D. Julius Bursche believed he could establish in the contribution on the Evangelical-Augsburg Church, vol. V (see bibliography), in the "Ekklesia."
