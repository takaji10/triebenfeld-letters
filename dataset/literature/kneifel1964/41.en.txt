In the Warthebruch, which embraced the districts from the former Prussian border to Leśnik (south of Uniejów), the colonization rose only after 1750. In the year 1767 the estate owner and district judge S. Leszczyc-Zielonawski founded on the lands of Slawsk the colony Wenglewer-Hol. and the Holländereien Rumin, Kolno and Slawsk. On 4. September 1781 King Stanislaw August confirmed the privileges of the settlement of Majdany situated in the Schlüssel Chelmno. Around 1785 there already existed the villages Police, Daniszew and Leszcze. The settlement of the Kalischer Land began two decades earlier than in the Warthebruch. According to the school chronicle of Czachulec-Schlachtholz, the village of the same name is supposed to have been founded in 1726. In 1746 Paul of Kolno-Prusinski, lord of the manor of Trombezyn, assigned to German peasants forests and deserted lands for clearing. On them they created the colony Lazinsk Hol. After 1770 were established: the large settlement Prazuehy on the possessions of the village of Cekow and in 1775 Zbiersk Hol. In 1782 Martin Wagrowski, lord of the manor of Sobiesenki and Stok, summoned Silesian colonists to settle on his estates. Kazimierka Stara possessed a founding charter from the year 1782. The owner of Biskupice colonized in 1784 Adler Hol. (Orlina). In the immediate vicinity of Kalisch there were lacking, in contrast to the medieval colonization in this area, closed German localities. The immigrants were Silesians, with the exception of the Pomeranian colony Wielopole, Józefów and partly Bialobloty. The settlement of the Gostyniner Land came about only in the last two decades of the18.

century. Around 1780 the owner of the estates of Strzelce founded the village of Strzelce. Likewise soon afterward the estate owners of Sieraków and Sójki the colonies Sieraköw Hol. and Wierzbie Hol. Taken as a whole, until 1795 the number of evangelical villages in the Gostyniner Land was small. The immigrants came from the districts around Schubin, Labischin, Margonin and Wir sitz. In the surroundings of Lodz the oldest colony, documented in 1782, was Ruda-Bugaj. Its founder was Walenty Chobrzynski, lord of the manor of Bruzyca. In the years 1782 to 1789 „Holländisch-Rude 66 was built up. A further „Dorfétablissement" was in 1784 Pustkowa-Góra, whose foundress was the superioress of the regular Premonstratensian canonesses of the convent at Lentschütz, D. Sokolowska. In 1791 A. Reptowski, canon at the Posen cathedral church, granted the settlement Brzyczka its „Gerechtsame66 (chartered rights).

The old German settlers of the Lodz area were Pomeranians from Kujawien and Silesians from the Kalischer Land. They were surplus forces that the original villages gave off to their daughter colonies. From the early history of our colonies it was reported that the first pioneers dwelt in earth huts, suffered want of food and succumbed to bodily exertions or carried away severe damage to their health. Among the pioneers there were only a few descendants of Hollanders, who had moved up the Weichsel from the Danziger Werder area[^p41-4]

Before them went the fame of model settlement technicians, who developed particular economic forms while preserving liberal rights. The name „Holländer 6 6 (Hollander) was later transferred to every free German colonist to whom, at his settlement for the purpose of clearing forests and deserted lands, certain basic rights were assured. The name „Holländer 6 6 was thus not a designation of ethnic stock but rather the form of expression of a legal status and activity.
