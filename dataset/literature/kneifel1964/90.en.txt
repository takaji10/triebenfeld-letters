With the settlement activity one aimed, as Baron von Schroetter, the leading head in New East Prussia, explained, not merely at „the increase of the mass of the people and the increase of the national wealth through the wealth of the Golonisten, but also and principally the promotion of the culture of the land directly through the colonists themselves and the raising of the general industry through their example". Whoever therefore imputes Germanizing tendencies to the Prussian authorities in their colonization endeavors of that day does not do justice to the historical events of those days.

The stream of German settlers poured itself forth more or less over all of Central Poland, so that state and private colonies became mixed together[^p90-2]

In Dember Holl, near Kalisch, evangelical farmers settled. On the ku ja wischen lake plateau there arose seven larger Swabian villages: Friedrichstal, Wilhelmstal, Rosental, Lilienfeld, Neu-Renneberg, Schwedelbach and Czarnocice8)

In the Chodecz region German great landowners acquired estates whose descendants assimilated almost without remainder to the Polish environment. In the Warthebruch there was laid out in 1796 the large Leśniker linguistic island. For the taking over of 10 hides of forest land the colonists paid the Polish estate owner Milewski 189 thaler of ground rent. In the Gostynin land, in the forest region of Gostynin, the great merchant von Treskow, owner of the Strzelce starost estates, created ten villages in which mainly Marchian colonists settled. The Prussian domain administration owes the Swabian villages of Leonberg, Neu-Düttlingen, Nagold and Luisental their origin. The two first colonies, founded on fruitful soil, developed well. Widely known was the Herrnhuter settlement of Leonberg (1801/02), a purely Swabian foundation, which in the last decades before 1945 grew strong economically as well. In the Lodz region there were founded in 1795 the Holländereien Radogoszcz (Radegast), Zabie-niec (1793), Rexul, Wozniki and Karasica4)
