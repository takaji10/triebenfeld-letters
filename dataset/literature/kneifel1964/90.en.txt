With the settlement activity the aim, as Freiherr von Schroetter, the leading mind in New East Prussia, explained, was not merely "an increase of the population and an increase of the national wealth through the wealth of the colonists, but also and chiefly the promotion of the culture of the land directly through the colonists themselves and the raising of general industry through their example." Whoever therefore imputes Germanizing tendencies to the Prussian authorities in their colonization efforts of that day does not do justice to the historical events of those times.

The stream of German settlers poured itself more or less over all of Central Poland, so that state and private colonies became mingled[^p90-2]

In Dember Holl, near Kalisch, evangelical farmers settled. On the Kujavian lake plateau seven larger Swabian villages arose: Friedrichstal, Wilhelmstal, Rosental, Lilienfeld, Neu-Renneberg, Schwedelbach, and Czarnocice8)

In the Chodetz region German great landowners acquired estates whose descendants assimilated themselves almost without remainder to the Polish environment. In the Warta marsh the great Leśniker language island was laid out in 1796. On taking over 10 Hufen of forest land the colonists paid the Polish landowner Milewski 189 taler in ground money. In the Gostynin land, in the forest district of Gostynin, the great merchant von Treskow, owner of the Strzelce starosty estates, created ten villages in which chiefly colonists from the Mark settled. The Swabian villages of Leonberg, Neu-Düttlingen, Nagold, and Luisental owe their origin to the Prussian domain administration. The first two colonies, founded on fertile soil, developed well. Widely known was the Herrnhuter settlement of Leonberg (1801/02), a purely Swabian foundation, which in the last decades before 1945 grew strong economically as well. In the Lodz region there were founded in 1795 the Dutch settlements of Radogoszcz (Radegast), Zabie-niec (1793), Rexul, Wozniki, and Karasica4)

In the same year, on the woodlands of the manorial estate of Skoszewy, the colonies of Gtogowiec, Boginia, and Glombie. In their neighborhood there followed after a short time the settlement of Plichtow and Laski. The owner of the estate of Groß-Kaly, I. Wyrzykowski, handed over in the year 1796 to 18 colonists the wooded tract of Grabieniec, comprising 17 Hufen, for clearing; east of Brzeziny the villages of Przylek and Kielbasa were laid out; south of them, in the region of Koluszki, Erazmow, Felicjanow, and Katarzynow. About the same time there were "established" north of Beichatow the settlements of Kalduny, Rassy, and Myszaki; further, in 1795 Danielow and the Parzniewice huts 5). In the domain office of Laznow the South Prussian War and Domain Chamber founded in the years 1799 to 1805 the following villages: Neusulzfeld (Nowosolna), Neu-Wionczyn, Wionczyn Görny, Wionczyn Dolny, Friedrichshagen (Augustow), Wilhelmswalde (Borowo), Groembach (Laznowska Wola), and Grünberg (Zielona Göra).
