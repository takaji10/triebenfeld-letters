## VI. The Church in Prussian Times

### (1793 — 1806)

### 1. The Prussian Colonization in Central Poland

From the Polish territories acquired through the partitions of 1793 and 1795 and administered until the Peace of Tilsit of 1807, Prussia formed the provinces of South Prussia and New East Prussia and New Silesia. In 1793 the South Prussian territories were divided into two administrative districts: into the Posen and Lentschütz ones, respectively in place of the latter the Petrikau or Kalisch one. In 1796 three chamber departments took their place: the Posen, Kalisch and Warschau ones. New East Prussia was divided in 1795 into two chamber departments: the Plozk and Bialystok ones1)

With the Prussian administration, which set the state Prussian colonization in motion, an epoch of development important for Protestantism began.

In New East Prussia, in the Plozk chamber district, the number of evangelical-Lutheran families, including those that had immigrated, amounted around 1798 already to 5879 and continued to rise. In the Bialystok chamber department likewise a few thousand Lutherans had moved in. In South Prussia, in the Kalisch and Warschau chamber department, there were, according to the „Generaltableau über den Fortgang des preußischen Kolonistenwesens vom 13. Februar 1806", 7885 souls. At the same time there settled in the towns of the province of New East Prussia 778 craftsman families with 2654 souls. In the Central Polish towns of South Prussia the number of immigrants was probably, if not higher, then certainly not lower. For the founding of colonies in South Prussia in the period from 1798 to 1806 there were expended from the extraordinary budget of the Prussian state 1 841 083 Reichstaler. The mere establishment of a settler holding cost here 1000 thaler.
