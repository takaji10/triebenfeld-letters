## VI. The Church in the Prussian Period

### (1793 – 1806)

### 1. Prussian Colonization in Central Poland

From the Polish territories acquired through the partitions of 1793 and 1795 and administered until the Peace of Tilsit of 1807, Prussia formed the provinces of South and New East Prussia and New Silesia. In 1793 the South Prussian territories were divided into two administrative districts: the Posen and Lentschütz ones, or in place of the latter the Petrikau or Kalisch one. In 1796 three chamber departments took their place: the Posen, Kalisch, and Warsaw ones. New East Prussia was divided in 1795 into two chamber departments: the Plozk and Bialystok ones1)

With the Prussian administration, which set the state Prussian colonization in motion, an epoch of development important for Protestantism began.

In New East Prussia, in the Plock chamber district, the number of Evangelical-Lutheran families, including those who had immigrated, already amounted to 5879 about 1798 and continued to rise. In the Bialystok chamber department likewise a few thousand Lutherans had moved in. In South Prussia, in the Kalisch and Warsaw chamber departments, there were, according to the "Generaltableau über den Fortgang des preußischen Kolonistenwesens vom 13. Februar 1806," 7885 souls. At the same time 778 families of craftsmen with 2654 souls settled in the towns of the province of New East Prussia. In the Central Polish towns of South Prussia the number of immigrants was probably, if not higher, then certainly not lower. For the founding of colonies in South Prussia in the period from 1798 to 1806 the sum of 1 841 083 Reichstaler was expended from the extraordinary budget of the Prussian state. The establishment of a single settler holding alone cost 1000 taler here.
