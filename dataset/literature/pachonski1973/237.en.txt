Jan Pachoński

THE POLES IN THE REPUBLIC OF VENICE

(1794-1797)

The beginnings of the Risorgimento coincide with a period of Polish struggles for independence and social progress. One little-known stage of Polish-Venetian relations, which preceded the formation of General J. H. Dąbrowski's Polish Legions in Italy, deserves in particular a careful examination[^1].

A few scattered details on the Poles of Venice (1794-1797) have been given by Władysław Smoleński, Alexandre Kraushar, Simon Askenazy, Marian Kukiel and Adam Skałkowski. The reader will find a somewhat fuller account of these events in the monograph of Jan Pachoński, Légions Polonaises, vérité et légende, 1794-1807, vol. 1, Warsaw, 1969 (in Polish).

[^2]: This insurrection was launched to free Poland from Tsarist occupation, but it was directed against Prussia as well; it hoped, however, for the benevolent neutrality of Austria. The insurgents looked to social changes in an independent Poland.
