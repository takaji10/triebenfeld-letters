Although the question of his appointment had not yet been settled by mid-July, General Łaźniński, as leader of the republicans, together with the secretary of the French Legation, Jacob[^27], did not hesitate to approach the commander-in-chief of the Army of Italy, Napoleon Bonaparte, and ask him to oppose this candidacy. When the Signoria nevertheless decided to give the command to General Komarzewski and summoned him from Padua (he stayed in Venice from 6 to 12 September, at the Petrillo inn), it ran first into the veto of the minister Lallement, who by then had drawn closer to the Polish republicans; and then, through the efforts of the Deputation in Paris (28 September 1796), the French ministry of foreign affairs opposed it as well. So the Signoria and General Komarzewski ended by giving way.

Sołtyk's role as delegate of the Centre at Venice

The Austro-French fighting in northern Italy in 1796 thinned the ranks of the Polish emigrés. At the head of the Poles of Venice stood S. Sołtyk, as delegate of the Lwów Centre, its plenipotentiary, with its funds at his disposal. From 3 August 1796 he had moved from the Petrillo inn to J. Durante's. He used the pseudonym Weygtynowski so as not to expose his family, who had stayed in Poland.

On 30 May he received a visit from Clément Leszczyński and Augustin Trzecieski, envoys of the Lwów Centre, with whom he held talks at the minister Lallement's and at Cacault's in Florence (on 6 June)[^28].
