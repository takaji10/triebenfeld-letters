In these circumstances Kościuszko considered it necessary to destroy Fersen's corps, and on 7 October he set out from the Mokotów camp. When, on that same day, a halt was made at Korytnica, Major Molski arrived in the evening, sent by Dąbrowski with a report on the capture of Bydgoszcz[^329]. The favourable news, proclaimed at once to the soldiers, raised their spirits. It was, however, the last ray of joy. Three days later, on 10 October, a catastrophe occurred unexpectedly. At Maciejowice, where an engagement with Fersen took place, Kościuszko's corps of 7,000[^330] was almost completely destroyed and 22 guns were lost. Kościuszko himself, gravely wounded, together with his adjutant Fiszer and his secretary Julian Ursyn Niemcewicz, and Generals Kamieński, Kniaziewicz and Sierakowski, fell into captivity[^331].

On 12 October the news of this rout reached Warsaw, causing great agitation and dejection. The new Commander chosen that same day at a session of the Supreme National Council was Tomasz Wawrzecki. He was a man personally worthy and a great patriot, a former deputy to the Great Sejm, lately distinguished by his part in the rising in Lithuania, but otherwise "in knowledge not exceeding the intellectual sphere of a Lithuanian landowner". Wawrzecki himself was also aware that he lacked the qualifications of a leader, and for four days he declined to accept the honourable office, but in the end he yielded to the entreaties and urgings of all[^332].
