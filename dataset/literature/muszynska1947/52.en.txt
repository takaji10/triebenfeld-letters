Since the commission's chief concern was to have a suitable armed force, at the end of August Michał Jerlicz, formerly a colonel in the Polish army, was ordered, under threat of punishment, to see to its formation from various fugitives, riflemen and cantonists. The craftsmen were ordered to work on the making of the things needed for the army, and the Jews of Działoszyn were to supply arms and ammunition. In the summonses that were sent out in the neighbourhood of Wieluń and Ostrzeszów, dated 15 August and signed by Stokowski, the nobility were called upon to present themselves under arms. Whoever could not come in person was obliged to send substitutes. From the noble estates one recruit was to be supplied for every 5 houses, and from the royal and ecclesiastical estates two in the same proportion. In the small town of Grabów in the Ostrzeszów district the armed movement had begun earlier, on 23 or 24 August. At that time a detachment of insurgents under the command of Kazimierz Madaliński and Jabłkowski came to the town; they received from the town council and the burghers an oath of fidelity to the fatherland and took the cash box. To complete the account of the events that took place in these parts, it must be added that Psarski summoned 12 citizens from Kępno [Kempen] to Ostrzeszów, swore them in, and chose 4 of them for the Commission of Order and as many for the criminal court. At Bobrów he urged the burghers to rise against the Prussians, set up insurgent authorities, and ordered the collection of a double hearth tax and of the "*ofiary*"[^137].
