To reinforce this inadequate garrisoning, Lieutenant-General Schwerin was to draw in, according to the instruction of 20 March, as many troops from East Prussia, West Prussia and Silesia as he should consider necessary to secure that province, and moreover, in order to achieve this security the better, he was to move troops into the Commonwealth and occupy its territory from the Vistula as far as the Narew. In this last case the Prussian king would be committing a new act of lawlessness towards the Poles, though this lawlessness had long been foreseen. Already at the time of the Second Partition, Russia, Austria and Prussia had jointly resolved to occupy the country in the event of disturbances in Poland; to Prussia fell the observation of the entire course of the Vistula and the greater part of Samogitia. At this opportune moment, the violation of the territory of the Commonwealth by Prussian troops was now announced by Buchholtz's diplomatic note of 24 March, sent to the Permanent Council. In it Buchholtz complained of the violation of Prussian territory by Madaliński and reported that the Prussian king, to secure his borders, would draw a cordon from Zakroczym to Ostrołęka. In reply the Permanent Council expressed the hope that the entry of Prussian troops into Poland did not signify a lasting occupation, and that the Prussians would make good the damage caused in connection with this entry[^71].
