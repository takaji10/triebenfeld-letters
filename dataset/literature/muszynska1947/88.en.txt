The remnants of Székely's corps, which Col. Hinrichs had gathered at Świecie, were too weak to undertake any action. In the Prussian camp, therefore, there was a lurking fear that Dąbrowski, whose forces were greatly overestimated, would strike at Gdańsk[^263].

The commandant of Gdańsk, General Raumer, also reckoned with this possibility, especially when he learned that Polish patrols were already reaching Chojnice [Konitz], and he applied appropriate measures. He placed guns on the ramparts, strengthened the guards, erected palisades, sent out patrols, and finally set up a camp on Bishop's Hill (Bischoffsberg)[^264]. The matter could not be taken lightly all the more because the people of Gdańsk favoured the Poles, and even the inhabitants of Starogard [Preußisch Stargard], Kościerzyna [Berent], Skarszewy [Schöneck] and Nowe Miasto were ready to supply them with help and with men for the ranks[^265]. In order, therefore, to halt Dąbrowski's possible march on Gdańsk, General Raumer used a ruse[^266]. He sent small detachments of troops into the vicinity of Kościerzyna and Starogard and ordered them to collect provisions and forage, to create the illusion that a larger corps was approaching in those parts. General Dąbrowski, who had previously learned of the concentration of General Beyern's corps[^267], and, not being informed of it in any detail, formed the supposition that it was this corps that was marching to West Prussia, abandoned the march on Gdańsk[^268].
