# Muszyńska-Zygmańska (1947), p. 80
# translation by Claude (machine translation, checked and corrected in a bilingual QA pass, 2026)

At the same time as the report Rymkiewicz dispatched a letter to Wybicki, entreating him, for the love of the fatherland, that in case Dąbrowski should delay in striking at Bydgoszcz, he should forget on this occasion that he was his friend, and make use of the power of the law entrusted to him by the nation as its plenipotentiary, and prevail upon him to take this much needed step[^229]."

Dąbrowski was at that time still at Łabiszyn, from where he had sent out numerous detachments to patrol as far as Walkowice [Wałkowice], Żnin, Rynarzewo, Strzelno [Strelno], Barcin, Pakość [Pakosch], Gniewkowo [Argenau], Nakło [Nakel], Podgórz near Toruń, and Inowrocław. Major Bielamowski he ordered to set out from the vicinity of Poznań toward Pyzdry, in order to observe Major-General Schwerin, who was supposed to be in the neighbourhood of Kalisz and Śrem. He did not move to Bydgoszcz with his whole corps, as he himself says, because he did not wish "to expose the soldiers and ammunition at such a distance, in driving the enemy from his entrenchments and storming the town[^230]."

Meanwhile Rymkiewicz's report settled the whole matter. When Wybicki, "full of satisfaction that Bydgoszcz would be ours," went to Dąbrowski "with the determination not to leave him until the order to march was given to the corps," he found him already "disposed" to this step. Dąbrowski only enjoined Rymkiewicz, before setting out, that, "since the whole fate of this expedition depended on the seizing of the heights," he should seek "by various means to hold on to them, and, having reconnoitred all the positions as exactly as possible," should man them with artillery, which would hasten the surrender of the town[^231].
