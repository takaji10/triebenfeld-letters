Here, on 24 September, took place the planned union with the risings of the Kalisz, Łęczyca and Kuyavian voivodeships and of the Poznań and Gniezno voivodeships. The latter two, two days earlier, in conjunction with the insurgent detachment of Krupecki, had clashed with the detachment of Székely's corps at Kcynia [^207]. The armed strength of the Greater Poland insurgents who had arrived at the assembly point did not exceed 4,000 and had first to be "regulated"[^208]. It turned out that the cavalry, though on good horses, was badly armed and for the most part ignorant of the simplest rules of military service. Yet young men and decrepit old men alike had presented themselves, and all were seized by the ardour of love of country. The main voivodeship forces retained a separate organisation and entered Dąbrowski's corps as regiments of light cavalry. Employed together with the regular army on various patrols and pickets, they gradually, under the skilled hand of their leader, acquired a sense of discipline. The infantry, which in Dąbrowski's division was in general inferior to the cavalry, swelled the ranks of the regular soldiers. The whole strength of the corps now amounted to over 6,000 men, besides the 600 horse of Madaliński's brigade[^209].

With such an army Dąbrowski set out in 3 columns for Gniezno and arrived there on 25 September[^210]. On the following day he sent to Kościuszko the citizen Miaskowski, "known to the public," with a request for the dispatch of a few 12-pounder guns and howitzers, paper money and more infantry. Miaskowski was also to renew the request that the corps stand at Gąbin, "not allowing the enemy to extend himself over the Warta and the Bzura"[^211].
