On the day after the capture of the city, 3 October, according to the account of a German eyewitness[^252], all the officials and some of the citizens were summoned to the building of the Chamber. Dąbrowski, Wybicki and a number of Polish generals and officers also arrived there. In the presence of all, the cash boxes were counted and carried off, and the portraits of the Prussian kings Frederick II and Frederick William II were removed from the conference hall. After this had been done, General Dąbrowski, who spoke correct German and was "a very modest man, wise and of great polish", stepped forward and ordered the local merchants to pay a contribution of 300,000 thalers, which in the end, owing to the intervention of 2 citizens, was reduced to 10,000 thalers. Next the officials were required to swear that they would hand over all the money and valuables in their keeping that Colonel de Székely had plundered, to which the Germans questioned, however, answered unanimously that they knew nothing. After a while General Dąbrowski spoke again, declaring that the Poles had come not as enemies but as friends. He referred to the cruelties perpetrated by Col. Székely, described his inhuman treatment of the clergy at Włocławek and of persons of both sexes whom he had robbed and wished to hang, and finally had brought beneath the gallows at Inowrocław. As proof that the Prussian king approved of Székely's "tyrannical" conduct and had recommended that he be even more savage, Dąbrowski showed those present a royal letter intercepted near Gąbin. Finally he announced that he was arresting all the Prussian officials, so as to hold hostages in exchange for the imprisoned Poles[^253].
