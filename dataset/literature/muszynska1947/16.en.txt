# Muszyńska-Zygmańska (1947), p. 16
# translation by Claude (machine translation, checked and corrected in a bilingual QA pass, 2026)

On 9 April the Prusso-Russian declaration announcing the Second Partition was handed to the Targowica Confederation by the new Russian ambassador in Warsaw, Sievers, and the Prussian envoy Buchholtz[^33]. Many days still passed, however, before the last Sejm of the Commonwealth was compelled to recognise the partition treaty.

The Prussians carried out the official taking of possession of the new province between 7 and 24 April. This operation was directed by two commissioners appointed by the king, General Möllendorff and the Silesian minister of justice Danckelmann. A whole staff of officials came to their assistance. Some surveyed the boundaries exactly and set up posts upon them bearing the inscription "Royal Prussian Territory"; others announced to the inhabitants the notification patent of 25 March and summoned them to swear homage to the king on 7 May. On official buildings and town gates the Prussian emblem[^34] now appeared.

After this official taking of possession of Greater Poland and the rest of the seized lands, the newcomers from Brandenburg proceeded to organise them. The country was named South Prussia, since this name pleased the King of Prussia most[^35]. Von Voss became chief minister of South Prussia, and von Buggenhagen Oberpräsident (chief president). Voss set about organising the administration at once, with the help of the Silesian minister Hoym[^36]. The whole territory was divided into two departments. The first, called the Poznań department after the seat of the authorities in Poznań, comprised the former Poznań, Gniezno and Kalisz voivodeships and was divided into 17 districts (powiaty; German Kreise). The second, the Łęczyca department, named the Piotrków department soon after the seat of the authorities was transferred from Łęczyca to Piotrków [Petrikau], comprised the former Sieradz voivodeship with Wieluń Land, part of the Kraków (Częstochowa)
