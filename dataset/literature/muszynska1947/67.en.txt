Dąbrowski was accompanied on the march by Józef Wybicki, appointed on 9 September plenipotentiary for the Łęczyca, Kuyavian and Sieradz voivodeships. He was to remain constantly with the division of "citizen General Dąbrowski", and received from Kościuszko instructions identical with those that Rogaliński, Zakrzewski and Gorzeński had received three days before; with them he was also obliged to confer and cooperate[^177].

Wybicki writes of himself in connection with this appointment in his memoir[^178]: "Whether from his own conviction, or from the inspiration of President Zakrzewski, or finally at the request of General Dąbrowski himself, I do not enter into it, the Commander named me plenipotentiary with the corps destined for Greater Poland. For some days I resisted my nomination, for I confess frankly that it was easy to reckon that, despite the Spartan zeal of my Greater Poles, that small handful of regular troops which was coming to their aid could not in the end overcome the enormous Prussian force... Yet I yielded at last, as befitted a good Pole, to the Commander, and accepted with ample powers the instrument of plenipotentiary, which was very flattering to me".

Meanwhile, in order to cover Dąbrowski's march toward the Bzura, Kościuszko dispatched several detachments. There set out from Warsaw: Madaliński, with a force of 1,000 men, toward Witkowice; Kołysko, with a detachment half as large again, by way of Nadarzyn toward Bolimów; Prince Józef Poniatowski, at the head of 2,000 soldiers, to Mszczonów.
