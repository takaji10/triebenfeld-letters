"They advised forming a military commission, composed of brigadier generals, which would henceforth govern". Dąbrowski, aggrieved that they wished in this way to deprive him of the command, appealed to Kościuszko's order, by virtue of which he had received command of the army. He declared "that he had so far done nothing without common deliberation", but "to a formal commission he cannot consent from his post", and in that case he resigned the command. "The obstinacy had already reached such a degree that Madaliński with his brigade and some of the risings wanted to separate from Gen. Dąbrowski", and only Generals Rymkiewicz, Mniewski and Skórzewski and the plenipotentiary Wybicki "used all their eloquence and reason to soothe minds so bitterly at war with one another". In time "though only on the surface, harmony, friendship, unity and love returned again"[^293].

Bielamowski learned of Ledivary's march to the relief of Toruń during his stay at Strzelno, from a letter of Col. Ledivary of 7 October to Major-General Schwerin, intercepted by an insurgent from Mniewski's detachment. Ledivary wrote that he had been ordered by the chief commander to scatter and defeat Dąbrowski's corps together with Major-General Schwerin, and that he was now marching to Toruń, to save that town, which Dąbrowski was threatening. Bielamowski sent the intercepted letter on to Dąbrowski, but the latter received it too late to avoid the pointless march on Toruń. For the letter reached Dąbrowski only in the night of 11 to 12 October, whereas on the 10th Dąbrowski had already crossed the Vistula on the march to Toruń[^294].
