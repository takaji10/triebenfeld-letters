The march on Toruń, undertaken in spite of everything, might be explained by a wish to calm the agitated minds of Dąbrowski's subordinates, who were dissatisfied with his tactics[^290].

For after the capture of Bydgoszcz, even before the departure for Toruń, reproaches had arisen against Dąbrowski "that he is not active enough and has no determination at all, with the result that not only do all the operations of the war proceed at a tortoise pace, but many are even omitted..., that from day to day the march on Toruń is put off, giving the garrison time to strengthen itself"[^291] Rymkiewicz reproached him for not having stood before Toruń on the day after the capture of Bydgoszcz, for then "this town would have surrendered or been taken easily by force of arms[^292]. Even Madaliński, "always honest, forgot in this moment what he owes to the Fatherland". Envy arose in him "that the name of Dąbrowski is famous, while his own is barely mentioned, and he already imagined for himself a right to the supreme command, which was due to him as a general of higher rank, as the first to the national rising". Realising, however, that he could not himself command so large a corps "in the sight of a more numerous enemy", he proposed to Rymkiewicz that the latter should "command under his name". He met with a refusal, however. Rymkiewicz, when he "could no longer bear the spirit of faction, armed with pure patriotism, rose up with republican energy, reminding Gen.
