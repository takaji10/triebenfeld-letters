Manstein now moved towards Gostyń [Gostyn] with the intention of cooperating with General Elsner. On the road to Leszno, at Poniec, Elsner received on 27 September word from Manstein of his march on Gostyń, together with a summons to go with him towards Jarocin [Jarotschin], in order to support Major-General Schwerin in a planned attack on the insurgents at Konin and to prevent the Polish troops from withdrawing towards Silesia. But this plan too came to nothing. For on that very same day, 27 September, alarming news reached Elsner and Manstein that Dąbrowski was marching on Poznań; they therefore turned back from their road and by forced march went to the aid of the threatened city. Elsner arrived on the spot on 1 October, Manstein the next day[^308]. This was during Bielamowski's approach to Poznań, already described by us, which gave rise among the Prussians to the belief that it was Dąbrowski's advance guard, he intending to take the city. All suppositions of this kind, moreover, had their source in the lack of precise information. The capital of Greater Poland, as a result of the loss of communications, as the "Südpreussische Zeitung" of the day writes[^309], had become like an "island in the sea" cut off from the rest of the world.

The arrival of new troops in Poznań by no means put an end to further fears for the safety of the city.
