# Muszyńska-Zygmańska (1947), p. 141
# translation by Claude (machine translation, checked and corrected in a bilingual QA pass, 2026)

In Greater Poland, alongside the restoration of the old order, trials of the insurgents now began. Their tracking down and punishment was undertaken by special commissions, which had been appointed while the insurrection was still in progress. For East Prussia [Translator's note: so printed; South Prussia is evidently meant: the commissions at Wrocław, Brzeg and Głogów, in Silesia, dealt with the insurgents of South Prussia (see p. 143).] they were installed in Wrocław, Brzeg and Głogów; for West Prussia, in Grudziądz and Toruń. The former were presided over by Minister Danckelmann, the latter by Minister v. Carmer. With the establishment of the West Prussian commissions, the commission set up in Toruń by Székely, under the chairmanship of the judicial councillor (Justizrat) Kühze, ceased to function. On 26 January 1795 an order was issued that there was to be no appeal from the verdicts of all five commissions, but that for a verdict to become valid royal confirmation was required[^451].

From 1 February 1795 Count Hoym[^452], who after the recall of Voss in September 1794 had become chief minister of South Prussia[^453], was appointed to supervise the commissions in Silesia. His intention was to bring the trial of the insurgents to an end as quickly as possible, and in accordance with the royal instruction[^454] fines and confiscation of property were to replace prison sentences. Hoym meant to devote the fund thus collected to the reforms in South Prussia that had been announced in part in the Wrocław patent of 24 September 1794, probably drafted by himself[^455]. For while on the one hand he had resolved to teach the Greater Poles obedience by punishing the rebels, so as to deter them in future from stirring up any disturbances, on the other hand he intended to introduce certain reforms, to remedy the lack of credit and cash, to put the mortgage registers in order and to lower the rate of interest, in order to inspire confidence in the new ruler among the inhabitants.
