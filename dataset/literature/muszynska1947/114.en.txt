On 18 October both corps drew near the Bzura, crossing the exceedingly dense forests of those parts, which made their march wholly unobserved. The next day Sochaczew and Kamionna were attacked, but the Prussians repulsed the attack. General Klinkowström, alarmed by the roar of the guns, marched out of the camp at Potoki, reached both bombarded places, restored communication between them, but on the second day returned to Potoki, fearing for the magazines at Łowicz. On 20 October Kołysko, reinforced with troops from Warsaw, renewed the attack on Sochaczew, received two days later the help of a detachment of Prince Józef's corps, and on the 23rd of that month attacked the town again "unluckily but with stubborn courage"[^349]. At the same time the Poles also shelled Kamionna, though from a respectable distance[^350].

The events that began to unfold on the Bzura moved Lieutenant-General Schwerin to take new steps. He understood that the decisive moment was approaching. He therefore sent off as quickly as possible 2 battalions to reinforce General Klinkowström's corps, and himself resolved, with the rest of his army, to leave at last the camp at Chrzonowice and take up position in the proper theatre of war. Before the march he issued final orders: he sent 3 battalions of Bonin, 5 squadrons of Bieberstein and 1 of Trenck in the direction of Rawa, and the battalion of Pfau and 2 squadrons of Bieberstein's regiment to reinforce the garrison of Piotrków, and, delaying no longer, set out on 20 October with 5 battalions and 11 squadrons for Skierniewice, and then for Łowicz and Kiernozia.
