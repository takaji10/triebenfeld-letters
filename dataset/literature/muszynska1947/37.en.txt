Kościuszko's camp at Połaniec, was marching towards the Prussian border in order, on Igelström's orders, to join the Prussians. Having taken up a position at Szczekociny, he called on Favrat to strike jointly at Kościuszko, who was following in the Russians' footsteps. He met, however, with a refusal. Favrat, although at bottom he acknowledged the justice of Denisov's intentions, remained passive, contenting himself with moving from the Pilica to Żarnowiec. For he knew that the king was coming to his camp, and wished to leave to him the laurels of victory.

Yet even the king, after arriving on 3 June at Favrat's camp at Wola near Żarnowiec, did not at once resolve to attack Kościuszko. He wished first to await the arrival of the horse battery and at the same time to induce his opponent to send part of his troops back towards Warsaw, so as to defeat him the more easily once weakened.

To this end, General Frankenberg with the crown prince's corps was to march towards Warsaw on 8 June, while General Schönfeldt was to begin preparations for crossing the Vistula, in order to create in Kościuszko the impression that the enemy intended to strike at the capital. The manoeuvre failed, since Kościuszko had already decided earlier on the movements of his army. On 5 June news suddenly reached the royal quarters at Wola that the insurgent troops had begun a march on Denisov's position and had forced his advance guard to retreat. The Prussian king could therefore no longer delay. Fearing that the Poles might defeat Denisov, he immediately issued orders for a march towards Szczekociny. The battle was fought the next day. Kościuszko took the field with his 14,000-strong corps, of which roughly ⅓ were peasants, facing an opponent superior to him by 12½ thousand[^91].
