# Muszyńska-Zygmańska (1947), p. 9
# translation by Claude (machine translation, checked and corrected in a bilingual QA pass, 2026)

, until finally, on 5 December, a motion of the Austrian vice-chancellor Cobenzl [Translator's note: the vice-chancellor was Philipp Cobenzl; the envoy at St Petersburg named later in this paragraph, whom the text prints as "Ludwik", is his relative Ludwig Cobenzl. The author distinguishes the two only by office.] moved the matter of compensation onto a new track. Cobenzl declared that the emperor consented to Prussia's pretensions, but that in this case the assent of Russia was necessary. Should the Prussian king, however, occupy Polish lands before Austria had effected the exchange of Belgium for Bavaria, the emperor would then be compelled to occupy a corresponding area in Poland himself until the Belgian-Bavarian exchange had come about. Cobenzl's words provoked violent opposition from Haugwitz. He knew that Catherine would never allow Austria to acquire new lands in Poland, and in that case all Austrian efforts in that direction would needlessly protract the whole affair. He therefore demanded that the Viennese court declare itself clearly regarding Prussia's intentions, without advancing pretensions of its own in respect of Poland. News arriving from Belgium of further successes of Dumouriez, and the fear that Prussia might refuse to support with its army the Austrians fighting the revolution, finally induced the emperor, on 19 December, to make concessions. Austria officially agreed to Prussia's demands, but its game was a double one, for, in secret from the Prussians, it intended to obstruct them at St Petersburg. The envoy Count Ludwik Cobenzl received instructions in accordance with which he was to demand from Catherine compensation for Prussia in Poland, in order to secure for Austria Prussia's help in the war against France, but on the other hand he was to oppose an excessive growth of Prussia. Cobenzl was also to announce that, in the event of a new partition of Poland taking place before the Bavarian exchange was accomplished, Austrian troops would then enter the southern voivodeships of Poland. This last instruction, however, the Austrian envoy did not venture to put forward[^5].
