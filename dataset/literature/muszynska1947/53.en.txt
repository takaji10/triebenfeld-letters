Later he gathered 25 horsemen and, on news that the town of Kalisz was fighting the Prussians, hastened there with them. The alarm proved false, but it aroused the vigilance of the Prussians, who placed two cannon on every road and reinforced the guards. The insurgents therefore withdrew, attacking on the way a detachment of Prussian hussars convoying wagons loaded with grain; 13 hussars and 700 korcy of oats fell into their hands. Sieroszewski then went with his detachment to Stawiszyn, and from there to the village of Kosmów, to rescue the citizen Kawiecki, whom the Prussians had seized for supplying arms to the insurgents. The detachment of Prussian soldiers conveying the prisoner, at the sight of the insurgents, retreated into the fields, abandoning Kawiecki. Sieroszewski dashed after the retreating men and took their whole detachment, consisting of 30-odd soldiers, prisoner. In this action the deserving citizens Maciej Konopnicki and Józef Cybulski fell. Sieroszewski then set out back to Stawiszyn, where General Skórzewski marched in with scythemen, whereby the insurgent detachment grew to 300 men. From there they pressed on to Koło, near which town a junction was made with the detachment of Andrzej Jachowski. The Prussian hussars were driven out of Koło[^139] and pursued as far as Kłodawa[^140]. 19 Prussians were killed on that occasion and 40 were taken prisoner. The Poles had 4 killed and 6 wounded. After this event there was again a return to Stawiszyn, whence, after a few days' rest, the insurgents went to Słupca, the assembly point of all the voivodeship forces[^141].
