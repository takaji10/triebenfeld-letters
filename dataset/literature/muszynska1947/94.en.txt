During the crossing Madaliński's brigade and 200 riflemen raised an alarm at Toruń, and as a result of this manoeuvre "the enemy corps stationed at Podgórz [Podgorz] went in large part across the bridge into Toruń[^285]". Colonel Ledivary, whose soldiers were exhausted by the ceaseless march, confined himself to a trifling harassment of the rear of the retreating Polish troops[^286]. Dąbrowski himself admits that he did not lose a single man, because the enemy "followed him sluggishly[^287].

The unsuccessful march on Toruń was not what Dąbrowski had intended. His aim was "to remain with the combined forces at Bydgoszcz and hold out through the winter between the Vistula, the Brda and the Noteć, to detach his numerous cavalry in all directions, as far as possible, to watch every step of the enemy; to this end to send from each rising, as far as circumstances allowed, considerable detachments to their own voivodeships, lands and districts to increase their strength, collect taxes and alarm the enemy everywhere; to help the rising in those places where it could not yet be carried out; to set up magazines at Bydgoszcz and Inowrocław, to bring the old regiments and brigades up to strength and form new ones, all of which could be done without oppressing the citizens, simply from the domains (Ämter) of the Prussian king, which are very numerous in these parts; to uniform and drill the army, so that in the coming campaign it might act with greater energy and benefit to the country[^288]". To storm a fortress with a fairly strong garrison, with the help of only a small number of regular troops, who would first have to be ferried over to the other bank of the Vistula, was in his view a bad move.
