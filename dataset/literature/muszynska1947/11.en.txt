By virtue of the convention concluded, Prussia received Gdańsk [Danzig] and Toruń [Thorn], the remainder of the Poznań, Gniezno and Inowrocław [Inowrazlaw] voivodeships, the Brześć Kujawski voivodeship with Dobrzyń [Dobrin] Land, the Łęczyca [Lentschütz], Kalisz [Kalisch] and Sieradz voivodeships with Wieluń [Welun] Land, and in addition the Płock voivodeship, part of the Rawa and Mazovia voivodeships on the right bank of the Vistula (Zakroczym Land), and a strip of the Kraków voivodeship (Częstochowa [Tschenstochau]). The boundary line ran from the north, from Działdowo [Soldau] on the Wkra, to the Vistula opposite Wyszogród, thence along the Pilica to Koniecpol, and finally below Częstochowa to the borders of Silesia[^14].

The parts of the Poznań, Gniezno and Inowrocław voivodeships lying on the Noteć had been obtained by Prussia already in the First Partition[^15]; as the so-called Netze District they formed, together with Royal Prussia, which likewise fell to Prussia at that time, the province of "West Prussia". Having now received the remainder of the Poznań, Gniezno and Inowrocław voivodeships, and the voivodeships of Brześć Kujawski with Dobrzyń Land, Łęczyca, Kalisz and Sieradz with Wieluń Land, Prussia possessed the whole of Greater Poland[^16].

Russia seized part of the Vilnius voivodeship, further the Minsk voivodeship, the eastern part of the Nowogródek, Brest-Litovsk and Volhynia voivodeships, and the Kyiv, Bratslav and Podolia voivodeships — an area up to the line running from Dünaburg through Pinsk to the Zbruch.

Prussia was enlarged by 1,060 square miles, Russia by over 4,500; there remained to Poland about 3,830 square miles[^17].
