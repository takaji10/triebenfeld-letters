When it had grown quite dark, he mounted his horse and rode toward the right wing of the Prussian camp, while he ordered his orderly to ride to the left. Both began to fire pistols and to give the command "squadrons forward". Schwerin, not suspecting the ruse, took such fright that he left behind everything he had robbed from Gorzeński and "fled through Witaszyce all night, so that he did not halt until Gostyń". How much truth there is in this story is not known, but it must be stated that similar alarms occurred often at that time[^314]. Of other places, Schwerin plundered the small town of Żerków [Zerkow] and the villages of Miniszew and Panienka[^315], and in the course of his further march, on 6 October, he stripped Eleonora Zabłocka, née Bogacka, at Śrem completely[^316]; her husband Jan was in the insurrection. He took her food, all her wardrobe, and all her livestock, and so laden with spoil he finally reached Poznań 3 days later. The number of Prussian soldiers in the city was now considerable, for it amounted to 6 battalions and 20 squadrons[^317].

Bielamowski followed Schwerin's movements. On 5 October Dąbrowski sent him from Bydgoszcz an order[^318] to march with his detachment at once to where the column of Major-General Schwerin was, with the instruction that he should start no engagement and should spread the word "that our army is leaving this region and is to attack General Schwerin". Bielamowski carried out the task entrusted to him conscientiously and with great skill. In a report of 12 October[^319] he gave Dąbrowski precise information on Schwerin's movements from the moment of his leaving Kalisz up to his entry into Poznań.
