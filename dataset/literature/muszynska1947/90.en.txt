# Muszyńska-Zygmańska (1947), p. 90
# translation by Claude (machine translation, checked and corrected in a bilingual QA pass, 2026)

Dąbrowski meanwhile was thinking of spreading the insurrection in the Netze District and in West Prussia. To that end, and in order to obtain fresh reinforcements as well as provisions and forage, he sent numerous cavalry detachments out from Bydgoszcz [^270].

Colonel Sieroszewski set out for Nakło and on the Noteć seized 13 vessels coming from Berlin with salt, iron, linen, cloth, etc.[^271]. The detachment of Lipski, major-general of the Gniezno voivodeship, sent in the direction of Chełmno [Kulm] and Grudziądz, dispersed an enemy patrol at Chełmża [Culmsee], taking 18 prisoners. Colonel Wyganowski, with 300 horse from the Poznań rising, went to the Wałcz district in order to raise an uprising there and choose recruits. Colonel Sokolnicki hastened to Fordon, to occupy that town and gain control of the banks of the Vistula, and moreover to round up vessels, wagons and men fit for transport, and to procure provisions of every kind. General Mniewski, with the whole detachment of his cavalry, and Colonel Mchowski, with 50 horse, together with infantry and artillery, were also directed to the Vistula, toward Solec Kujawski [Schulitz]. The former was to keep in touch with General Lipski, patrol toward Toruń, leave one detachment at Panadzewa and another at Podgórz to observe the Toruń bridge, which he was to burn in case of need; the latter was to station himself at Solec, send patrols toward Toruń and Gniewkowo and spies into the neighbourhood of Toruń, round up as many vessels and men useful for that purpose as possible, and maintain communication with General Mniewski. Dąbrowski ordered Lieutenant Moszczeński to remain with the cavalry at Łabiszyn, to send patrols from there toward Żnin, Wałkowice and Inowrocław, and to report as often as possible on the enemy's movements.
