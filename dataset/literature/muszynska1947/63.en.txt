On the same day the Kuyavian and Sieradz insurgents took Inowrocław, seized the treasuries and the Bydgoszcz post, but on the very next day, on the march against Bydgoszcz, they suffered defeat at Rynarzewo at Székely's hands[^165] and fell back to the neighbourhood of Szubin and Kcynia. As a result of the victory Bydgoszcz was saved from capture by the insurgents[^166].

In this short time Székely became a veritable terror to the insurgents and to the districts through which he passed. According to Rymkiewicz's words[^167], his entry into Greater Poland was "like a violent storm that overturns and lays waste everything it finds on its way". Already on the day after his departure from Warsaw, on 30 August, he issued at Sochaczew a proclamation[^168], which he ordered published as quickly as possible by all ecclesiastical and secular authorities. The four articles of this proclamation contained the broad plenipotentiary powers he had received from the king. Thus he threatened all those captured with arms in their hands with hanging. The same fate, or the penalty of life imprisonment in fortresses and in addition confiscation of property, was to befall those of the more prominent nobility who should be leaders or participants in the rising, and even their wives if they proved to be accomplices. Corporal punishment or death threatened also all those who hid insurgents on their estates and failed to report them to the nearest authority. Székely was likewise to seize suspected persons and send them to fortresses. All sentences were to be passed without prior investigation and without any legal formalities.
