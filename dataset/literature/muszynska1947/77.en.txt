For three days the Prussians reckoned with the possibility of an assault on the city by Polish troops[^220]. They took Bielamowski, who had reached the very gates of Poznań, for the advance guard of Dąbrowski's corps and hid themselves in the city and in the entrenchments[^221]. The alarm of the commandant Diether was all the greater at that moment because he did not know how large the enemy was, while the newspapers reported that Dąbrowski was leading a corps of 18,000[^222]. Diether himself, it is true, had done what was in his power to secure Poznań and had already thought of strengthening the city's fortifications almost from the outbreak of the Greater Poland insurrection, that is, from 24 August. He therefore reinforced, and raised new, entrenchments before the Wrocław Gate and on the hill of St Martin, which was designated as the main point of defence; he then threw up a redoubt before the "Brumska" gate, at the spot where the Muscovites had entrenched themselves 23 years earlier[^223]. He regarded these measures as insufficient, however, and so, in response to his alarm about the danger threatening the capital of Greater Poland, the generals Elsner with a thousand men from Leszno and Manstein with a detachment half again as large from Kościan, by way of Mosina, were hastening there at a rapid march to help[^224].

While Bielamowski was marching toward Poznań, General Madaliński at the head of 100 riflemen with two guns marched on Trzemeszno [Tremessen], into the region of Inowrocław, in order to draw the enemy's whole attention to himself, so that the enemy could not guess Dąbrowski's real intentions and hinder the corps itself in its crossing of the Noteć. The third column, dispatched from Gniezno and made up of Greater Poles, went under its general Lipski to Barcin and destroyed the Prussian post there. Dąbrowski
