# Muszyńska-Zygmańska (1947), p. 18
# translation by Claude (machine translation, checked and corrected in a bilingual QA pass, 2026)

In the work of introducing the new institutions in Greater Poland there were occasional interruptions on account of exceptional celebrations.

First of all, to seal the new state of affairs, in accordance with the provisions of the partition convention of 23 January 1793, the inhabitants had to pay homage to the King of Prussia. It was rendered in all the larger centres on 7 May 1793. On that occasion 1,500 representatives of the noble estate and 700 of the urban estate came to the capital of Greater Poland and, into the hands of the commissioners Möllendorff and Danckelmann, swore the oath of subjection to their new ruler. To lend splendour to the moment, a solemn service was held in the former Jesuit church, and in the evening a magnificent ball was given for the deputies in the Gurowski Palace, later called the Działyński Palace. On the occasion of so great a celebration, festivities were held throughout Greater Poland. Frederick William was pleased. Möllendorff —[^44] a man "shrewd and cunning, a master of political intrigue, able to cover everything with a semblance of good nature and to charm with his grey hair, sweet glance and courteous manner"[^45], soon popular in Poznań and among the nobility of Greater Poland — received, in recognition of his services, his nomination as field marshal[^46].

More than three months later, on 18 August, it was ordered that the surrender of Mainz by the French be celebrated, and on 25 September — at the very time when the Sejm assembled at Grodno, terrorised by Sievers and Buchholtz, was signing the partition treaty with Prussia — the king's birthday[^47].
