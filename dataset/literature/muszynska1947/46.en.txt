# Muszyńska-Zygmańska (1947), p. 46
# translation by Claude (machine translation, checked and corrected in a bilingual QA pass, 2026)

Wilczyński, Wit Pomorski, Antoni Żółtowski. From Rąbiń the insurgents moved under Niemojewski, chosen general for the Poznań voivodeship, to Śmigiel [Schmiegel], where the inhabitants declared for the uprising and handed over whatever arms each had. From there they proceeded in turn to Racot, whence their detachment[^116] then set out for Kościan and there, on 31 August[^117], fell by surprise upon the already mentioned General Manstein, who had arrived with 150 horse[^118] and, having garrisoned Leszno and Wschowa, was marching on Poznań. During the attack the insurgents captured the general's adjutant and took 6 horses. But Manstein soon recovered the adjutant, and the insurgents had to fall back before him with a loss of 16 men and made for the village of Mchy[^119]. Manstein, who had lost 5 men in the skirmish and had 20 wounded, did not continue his march but set out on the return road to Leszno and Wschowa. In the palace of the Sułkowski princes at Rydzyna [Reisen], near Leszno, all the arms were confiscated on his orders on 1 September, namely 6 large cannon, 1 small cannon and 31 old rifles, 9 sabres, 4 rapiers, pistols, etc.[^120].

Independently of the insurgents who had gathered under Niemojewski's leadership, other armed detachments were also active in the Poznań voivodeship. Thus, according to the memoir of Adam Turno, there assembled on 22 August[^121] in the village of Morków in the Leszno district "some 2,000 of the people, infantry for the most part armed with scythes, and cavalry of nearly 200 horse". Unfortunately, however, the commander of the insurgents, Bolesz, proved a most imprudent man. "Having taken up quarters in the manor house with all the citizens, and having set no picket or guard even on the chimney, he ate and drank in peace.
