When the pointless cannonade died down, Schwerin ordered a rest, but did not go in pursuit of the Polish army, even when towards evening Colonel Ledivary arrived at Witkowice[^360].

Meanwhile on the other side of the Bzura Dąbrowski, having first sent the prisoners and the booty off to Warsaw, began to entrench himself at Brochów, in order "to operate further from there both offensive and deffensive[^361]". On 24 October he received from Warsaw the order to storm Kamionna and Sochaczew anew together with Prince Józef and to clear the Bzura of Prussian troops, but he did not obey the summons, just as earlier he had not obeyed the instruction to support Poniatowski in his uncertain attack on Kamionna. Surveying the situation with a keen eye, he realised clearly that it was impossible to withstand the mighty army of Lieutenant-General Schwerin, once the latter should unite all his corps. He therefore handed over the command to General Rymkiewicz on 27 October and set out for Warsaw, summoned there to a council of war[^362].

By leading the Greater Poland division to a place of safety, Dąbrowski fulfilled his duty. Of all the commanders of the Polish forces, he alone rose to the height of the task entrusted to him. During the 40 days that the expedition lasted he always operated successfully, though he had arrayed against him a Prussian army seasoned in battle. He himself led an army that was far inferior to its opponent in numbers and in quality, yet he was able to work a miracle with it. He crossed the Bzura, the Warta, the Noteć and the Vistula "always in the face of a superior enemy". He took Bydgoszcz and aroused in the Prussians anxiety for such towns as Poznań, Toruń and Gdańsk.
