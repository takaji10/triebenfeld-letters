In Greater Poland the flame of rebellion smouldered throughout November and even into December. The ranks of the insurgents had, it is true, shrunk, since many of them had returned home, but the rest, mostly people of no means who had nothing to lose, went on roaming the forests in more or less numerous bands and carefully avoided contact with the Prussian army[^400].

The last armed efforts of the Greater Poles deserve all the more attention because they had help from nowhere, since already at the beginning of November the enemy had won the victory in the main theatre, and afterwards the new commander, Lieutenant-General Favrat[^401], moved against them with redoubled energy in order to establish peace as quickly as possible in South Prussia. Colonel Hinrichs, who on 6 November had taken up position at Włocławek and was crushing insurgents in the forests between Płock and Włocławek, he ordered to remain in the Vistula region and to watch over the security of communications on the Vistula.
