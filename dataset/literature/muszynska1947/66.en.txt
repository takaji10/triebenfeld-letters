an order from the Commander "to proceed with the army entrusted to him to Greater Poland by the road and by the means that his prudence and talents shall show him to be best and most convenient". The further words of this order ran: "He shall endeavour to unite as speedily as possible with the corps assembled in that province, shall assume the supreme command over them, and shall have the power to command and to issue his orders to all voivodeship generals and to all officers of the Greater Poland armed force, of whatever rank, to turn their detachments wherever he shall judge necessary; he shall confer with the civil government concerning the provisioning and supplying of the national army with all necessities. He shall use all his strength to harass the enemy and drive him from the invaded lands, to seize treasuries and magazines, in a word, to do the enemy the greatest possible harm and destruction; he shall endeavour to maintain the greatest possible communication with the main corps and its divisions, and shall give reports of his movements as often and as cautiously as possible[^175]".

Aware of the importance of the mission entrusted to him, Dąbrowski set out at once. He took with him the brigades bearing his own name and Rzewuski's, amounting to 900 cavalry and 1,000 infantry from the 4th and 13th regiments, together with 100 riflemen of Sokolnicki, recruited from Warsaw youth alone, and in addition 12 three- and six-pounder guns. The strength of the corps was soon increased by the joining of Madaliński's detachment, consisting of 600 horse and 400 recruits from the first regiment, together with 4 cannon.
