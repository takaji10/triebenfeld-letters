In the same newspaper there appeared at the same time a report of the appearance in the forest near Rypin in Dobrzyń Land of 100 armed peasants under the leadership of Zieliński, a native of the village of Skąpe. Lieutenant Branky, who had set out against them, was captured, whereupon the treasuries in Rypin fell into the hands of the insurgents[^114].

While these things were happening in Kuyavia, the inhabitants of the other voivodeships likewise set about action.

In the Poznań voivodeship, citizens such as Hipolit Rogaliński, Józef Niemojewski, Antoni Gliszczyński, Józef Miaskowski, Antoni Wyganowski, Hieronim Jaraczewski, Wojciech Sadowski, Sebastian Bieńkowski, Ireneusz and Bonawentura Koczorowski, Piotr Zaremba and Jan Zembrzuski gathered on 21 August at a general assembly in Kościan, resolving to act at once. First they seized the Prussian magazine in Śrem and set up an insurgent camp near that town. From there, 600 horse strong, they moved towards Rąbiń[^115], where they drew up and swore the insurrection act. At the same time they issued a proclamation calling on the peasants to join their ranks, promising each, as a reward, a reduction by half of the dues to the lord. At Rąbiń, too, members were appointed to the insurgent authorities for the Poznań voivodeship, that is, to the Commission of Order and the criminal court. The first of these consisted of: Celestyn Sokolnicki, Józef Jaraczewski, Hipolit Rogaliński, Antoni Wyganowski, Antoni Gliszczyński, Jan Zembrzuski, Józef Sczaniecki, Piotr Zaremba, Rev. Śmigielski, Ignacy Kurowski, Hieronim Jaraczewski and Stanisław Mycielski.
