# Muszyńska-Zygmańska (1947), p. 22
# translation by Claude (machine translation, checked and corrected in a bilingual QA pass, 2026)

The intention of saving the fatherland, threatened in its very existence, by means of armed struggle against the partitioning powers began to mature among the Poles in the spring of 1793. Numerous secret societies arose with the aim of preparing an insurrection. There were three principal centres of conspiracy: one in the army, which felt the humiliation of the country with particular keenness and which, moreover, as a result of the curtailment of the state's frontiers, was threatened in its own existence; a second among the "Jacobins" in Warsaw; a third among the émigrés in Saxony, extending in part to Paris. The moderates, such as Ignacy Działyński, colonel-proprietor (szef) of the 10th infantry regiment and the "soul of the conspiracy"[^59], such as Kapostas, a Warsaw banker, such as Jelski, starosta of Starodub and a deputy from the time of the Great Sejm, together with the emigration headed by Ignacy Potocki and Kołłątaj, intended to take up arms after suitable preparation and mutual understanding between the emigration and the country, and with support from abroad.
