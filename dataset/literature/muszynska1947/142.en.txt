He held that the Prussian soldier in Greater Poland should change his hostile attitude towards the population, cease abuses and strive to win its affection[^456].

According to a plan adopted in advance, the whole enormous trial of the insurgents, embracing more than 2,000 accused[^457], was to be completed by 1 June 1795. After that date the cases of the accused not yet judged were to be taken up by a special commission. All the others were to be dissolved. Minister Hoym made the establishment of this commission known in an announcement of 24 April 1795[^458]. Poznań was designated as its seat, and it was subordinated to the former Prussian envoy in Warsaw, Buchholtz, who since September 1794 had held the dignity of Oberpräsident of South Prussia. The members of the commission were: the president of the Płock Chamber, Privy Councillor v. Harlem; the president of the Piotrków Regierung, Count Reuss; and the director of the Piotrków Chamber, Privy Councillor v. Zitzewitz; the assistant was Lt. v. Klebowsky[^459].

Patents issued from time to time called upon the insurgents to appear voluntarily before the commissions, promising those who proved obedient to the summons a remission or reduction of punishment.
