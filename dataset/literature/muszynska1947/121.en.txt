In order to be able to cooperate with Suvorov "most effectively in forcing Warsaw to surrender and in bringing the war to an end[^372]", he devised a new plan, according to which a suitable disposition of the troops was to protect South Prussia from a fresh invasion on the one hand, and on the other to blockade Warsaw and force it by hunger to surrender. The plan was to come into force on 11 or 12 November. But when, in accordance with the new plan, the troops had already occupied the positions assigned to them, when General Pollitz had occupied the passage between Gąbin and Iłów, where a breakthrough of the enemy was to be expected; Major-General Schwerin had taken up positions inside Greater Poland on the Warta, resting his right wing on Szadek and keeping in communication with General Pollitz; General Beyern, after occupying Włocławek, had reached the Vistula with his left wing and extended his right towards the Warta, in order to cover Poznań, Gniezno and Toruń; and Schwerin himself had at last gone from Łowicz to Skierniewice in order to secure the area from Łowicz to Rawa, it was then, on 12 November, that Favrat arrived to take over the command in place of Schwerin, whom the king was removing, dissatisfied with his lack of activity[^373].

Meanwhile the heart of the Commonwealth, its capital, had already ceased to beat.

Amid the general confusion, as the front on the Narew was breaking up (it had, among other merits, that of holding back part of the Prussian forces from striking at Dąbrowski's division), as the Lithuanian army was disintegrating, and as enormous desertion took hold of the whole army and entire corps surrendered, Suvorov was approaching Warsaw by forced marches at the end of October[^374].
