How the conspiratorial activity developed we do not know precisely. In any case, the conspirators in the country were not idle. They intended to extend the plot to other provinces, as is proved by the sending out of emissaries, who soon reported the readiness of Greater Poland, Lesser Poland and Lithuania. They then dispatched a delegation to Leipzig, made up of Aloe and Walichnowski, to come to an understanding with Kołłątaj and Potocki, and with Kościuszko, generally marked out as the future Commander (*Naczelnik*) of the insurrection. According to a plan adopted, it seems, under the influence of the radical faction, it was intended to begin the uprising in the autumn, when the enemy troops would go into winter quarters. The outbreak was to take place simultaneously in many places, and Warsaw was to give the signal for battle. It was supposed that the five-thousand-strong garrison of Polish soldiery in the capital, aided by almost as many burghers, would easily overcome the eight-thousand-strong Russian garrison. Beyond this it was intended to rely on the regular soldiery stationed between the Pilica and the Vistula; of its total strength of thirteen thousand, six thousand would strike at the Prussian troops, and the remainder, together with part of the Warsaw garrison, at the Russians in Volhynia and in the Lublin region[^61].

Kościuszko, like Kołłątaj and Potocki, took a sceptical view of the conspirators' plan. He judged that an outbreak of the uprising in the autumn would be premature, but, in keeping with the promise given to the delegation, he appeared on the Galician border in order to become better acquainted with the preparations.
