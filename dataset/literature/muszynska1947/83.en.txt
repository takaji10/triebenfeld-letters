Meanwhile Colonel Hinrichs, who had taken over the command in Székely's place, seeing the Prussian defeat, withdrew with the remainder of the corps and with two guns in the direction of Świecie [Schwetz], losing "many captives" on the way. Madaliński dashed after the retreating force at the head of 2 brigades of cavalry and pursued it as far as Fordon. The Polish riflemen also went there, leaving their former position between Bartodzieje and Czersk. On that same day Dąbrowski extended his outposts toward Wtelno as far as Osielsko[^235] and Pałcz[^236] on the Vistula. In the battle, which was the bloodiest that fell to be fought in the course of the whole expedition, 100 Prussians fell, 50 were wounded, and 313 were taken prisoner[^237]. The Poles had 25 killed and 30 wounded[^238]. During the fighting, as Dąbrowski states in his reports, the Greater Poland scythemen distinguished themselves most particularly. Madaliński also stood bravely, setting a good example to his soldiers[^239].

When the victorious Polish troops entered the town, they found the houses shut up, and the inhabitants of both sexes "made an immense outcry and lamentation, begging for life and mercy." From some of the houses shots were even fired; Dąbrowski, however, ordered these to be opened at once, warning that "if any house is found closed, it will be subject to plunder." Assured of their safety, the people of Bydgoszcz came out of their houses and treated the soldiers, but lest "on account of the treating any offences should occur," the latter, at the first signal for departure given on the commander's order, "fell in in their usual order." Dąbrowski pitched his camp on the right bank of the Brda. 300 soldiers from the reserve formed the garrison of Bydgoszcz[^240].
