To prevent the German officials from reporting everything that had happened to their superiors in Poznań, all the Prussian officials were imprisoned on 24 August in the Franciscan monastery[^130]. On 3 September the commandant of Poznań, Colonel Diether, came up with one battalion, three squadrons and as many guns. The insurgents[^131], with whom the Kalisz and Poznań voivodeships under Niemojewski had by that time already joined, withdrew before the Prussians in the direction of Słupca. For there the union of all the voivodeship risings was to take place. In the haste with which the departure was made, 2 cannon and part of the magazine were left behind in Gniezno, but, most important of all, the order to withdraw the post at the Franciscan monastery was forgotten. Made up of 30 peasants, it at first would not give way, but in the end fled. Only two peasants from Żerniki[^132] held out to the last with scythes in their hands, until they fell to enemy bullets. In recognition of their sacrifice the Poles later laid their bodies in the cemetery of the Franciscan monastery near the tomb of the Blessed Yolanda. Col. Diether, having carried out a search for arms in the monastery and set the Prussian emblems up again in the town, marched back to Poznań. The released officials went with him[^133].
