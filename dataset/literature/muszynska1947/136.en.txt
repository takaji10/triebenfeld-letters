# Muszyńska-Zygmańska (1947), p. 136
# translation by Claude (machine translation, checked and corrected in a bilingual QA pass, 2026)

With the fall of the insurrection the fate of the Polish nation came to depend entirely on the will of the triumphant partitioning powers. Nothing was any longer able to hold back the final partition. The insurrection, that armed reflex of a nation being quartered, which was meant to tear it from the hands of its butchers, in the end brought it political death.

The superior force ranged against it, however, was great. Hopes of help from other states proved vain. France did not grant the 12-million loan on the mortgage of the starostwa, which Potocki and Kołłątaj had expected from that quarter[^430]. All the efforts made in this matter by the Polish diplomatic agent to the revolutionary government, Franciszek Barss, were futile. The Committee of Public Safety objected that he had no official standing and that the rising in Poland "had been made by the nobility"[^431], and in the end it announced that "France will not let go the smallest particle of gold, nor expose the life of a single man for the sake of consolidating the revolution in Poland, if it is aiming at an aristocratic or royal government, or at a change of the reigning dynasty, or at a change of the form of government to another no less bad[^432]".
