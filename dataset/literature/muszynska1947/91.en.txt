According to a contemporary account[^273] by Lainé, the town secretary of Świecie, the Polish troops arrived there at noon on 6 October[^274]. Wybicki took possession of the treasuries, abolished the Prussian offices, and established a commission for civil matters, composed of 5 representatives each from the nobility, the burghers and the clergy. The town secretary Synakiewicz was elected president of the town, but as he refused to take up the new office, the mayor in office hitherto, Gannowski, was left in that post. Citizen Kruszyński became general of the rising, and Moszczeński, the owner of the village of Niewieszczyn, colonel. On the following day a fair was held at Świecie, and taking advantage of this the insurgents seized cloth and leather goods against receipts. The inhabitants were disarmed and ordered to furnish a recruit. 60 young men from the town itself and 200 from the commune were incorporated into the insurgent ranks. Craftsmen had to work day and night preparing the necessary equipment. On the night of 11 to 12 October the Poles marched out of the town, but returned the next day, 13 October, and left for good on the same day, taking with them, besides horses, all the local tailors and musicians. To supplement this account it should be added that when the Yellow Hussar regiment, stationed on the other bank of the Vistula, saw the Polish troops entering Świecie, it did not dare to attack them. Wybicki, who writes of this in his memoir[^275], then experienced "what power belongs to opinion spreading alarm in a revolution".
