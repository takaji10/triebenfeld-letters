The rest of Dąbrowski's brigade, with 2 three-pounder guns, turned toward Wielki and Mały Wilczak, to observe the enemy and prevent him from crossing the Brda and the Bydgoszcz Canal. Dąbrowski himself, at the head of the Greater Poland insurgents and 4 battalions, moved out in 2 lines; on both wings of the infantry he placed the remainder of the cavalry; in reserve he left Colonel Sokolnicki with 1 battalion and several detachments of riflemen, while the guns he set up in front of the first line so that they could fire on the town and the Prussian artillery[^232].

In this battle order the Polish army advanced right up to Bydgoszcz and took up positions near the Jewish cemetery. Székely, with three companies of Hinrichs's rifle battalion, with artillery and cavalry, stood on the left bank of the Brda; in Bydgoszcz there remained only 4 garrison companies under the command of Colonel de Witten. Before long firing began on both sides, but it had little effect. Dąbrowski, not wishing to waste ammunition, therefore sent his adjutant, Major Zabłocki, to Colonel Székely with a proposal to surrender the town. Székely, acting "against all the customs of war," replied that "either Dąbrowski had himself lost his reason, or he supposes that he has lost his," in demanding such a thing of him; that Dąbrowski "ought to know that he is dealing with the famous de Székely, who has beaten the French everywhere." Finally he threatened Major Zabłocki that, if he did not leave the town at once, he would "have him shot in the head"[^233].
