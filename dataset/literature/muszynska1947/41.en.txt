At this time only the rising in Lithuania, Courland[^102] and Samogitia[^103] was achieving successes. Soon the most important event became the siege of the capital.

The victorious Prusso-Russian troops from Szczekociny were making their way there. Their march, however, by the road through Kielce, Końskie and Opoczno, with numerous rests, proceeded with such slowness that the Prussian king's original intentions, to forestall Kościuszko in the march toward Warsaw, came to nothing. For when Kościuszko had already on 28 June taken up positions at that city, the allies were to cross the Pilica only between 30 June and 2 July. Earlier still the Prussians had occupied the Bzura, so that armed insurrectionary detachments should not break through it into Greater Poland. The Prussian crown prince took up guard over this river until such time as the main army should advance closer to Warsaw, and on 27 June he established an entrenched camp at Potoki near Łowicz[^104].

In the first half of July the Prussians and Russians drew up before the earthworks of Warsaw. The siege began, lasting over two months, from 13 July to 6 September.

After the Prussians' capture of Wola on 27 July, a period of little activity followed among the besiegers, while the opposing side for its part tried to act offensively[^105]. The Poles undertook a number of sorties against the enemy positions, and even tried to break through the cordon of troops surrounding the capital. In this last case, however, the attempts missed their mark. On the one hand there was a lack of capable leaders, and on the other the undertaking itself proved extraordinarily difficult.
