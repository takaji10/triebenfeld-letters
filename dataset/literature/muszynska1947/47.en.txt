"The commandant with the other gentlemen" saved himself by flight through the windows. Only the village head from Koronowo, who stood with his men on a hill beyond the village, suffered no loss and withdrew safely; the rest of the insurgents were dispersed[^122].

Another insurgent episode took place in the district of Rawicz, at Jutrosin. The armed movement in that small town began through the doing of the mayor Walenty Zatorski, the parish priest Gogolewski, Skoroszewski, the leaseholder of the parish estate at Dubin, and a Mr Budziszewski. On 21 August, for 1 o'clock in the afternoon, Mayor Zatorski summoned the burghers to the town hall, whereupon he ordered them to assemble in the market square and there await the arrival of Skoroszewski's detachment of insurgents, with which they were to unite and march on without delay. Whoever had no rifle was to stand at the place of assembly armed with a scythe, a pike or a pitchfork, and each was obliged to provide himself with food for three days. On 24 August a certain Umiński arrived in the town at the head of a detachment of 24 men and 4 wagons, took down the Prussian eagles and took, against receipts, a small amount of money left in the town treasury. He forbade the Prussian officials, on pain of death, to communicate with the authorities. The insurgents, however, were not masters of the little town for long. From Rawicz there came up Colonel Dossow, who, having just come from Silesia at the head of the 2nd battalion of the Götze regiment and 2½ squadrons of Dolfs cuirassiers, was marching by way of Rawicz and Zduny to Kalisz to cover the magazines there[^123], and he began a search for the accused.
