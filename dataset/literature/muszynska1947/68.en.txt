Driven out of both places, the Prussians lost 100 men; several officers and 80 soldiers were taken prisoner, and in addition rich provision magazines fell into Polish hands. Kościuszko, informed of this event in a report, congratulated Dąbrowski on his success, promising to send "tokens of the nation's gratitude" for the officers who "distinguished themselves"[^180]. A few hours later a detachment sent out by Dąbrowski attacked the Prussian detachment at Pieczyska, where the magazines "were taken despite the enemy's strong defence, and burned through carelessness"[^181]. On the following day, 14 September, Madaliński joined Dąbrowski at Witkowice. Wybicki was present at the meeting of the two generals, and it is also his merit that Madaliński, although "the general senior in rank"[^182], submitted to Dąbrowski's authority. He was, after all, "only a soldier of fortune and did not have those talents which Dąbrowski could have acquired in the Saxon army"[^183], yet he continued to lead his own brigade[^184].

To continue the further march in safety, Dąbrowski asked Kościuszko to order Gen. Bronikowski, who was standing idle with a considerable detachment, to join him. Bronikowski did indeed receive such an order, but sent out his infantry too late. For this reason Dąbrowski was unable, as he himself recalls, to strike at the rear of the enemy army marching on Koło and Radziejów, nor to arrive in time at Gąbin and halt that army's pursuit of the Kuyavian insurgents[^185].
