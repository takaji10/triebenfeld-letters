# Muszyńska-Zygmańska (1947), p. 108
# translation by Claude (machine translation, checked and corrected in a bilingual QA pass, 2026)

While the armed movement in Greater Poland was developing favourably, to the glory of Polish arms, the situation in the other theatres of insurrectionary fighting was growing ever more grave. True, on the Narew the Poles were still holding their ground against the enemy, even gaining certain successes over the Prussians, but the Lithuanian troops, whose morale had deteriorated considerably after the fall of Vilnius, were no longer capable of resisting the pressure of the Russians. One after another the corps withdrew towards Grodno, abandoning even Samogitia, the holding of which Kościuszko had recommended. Meanwhile Suvorov was approaching Brest-Litovsk with a corps of 18,000 and 60 guns. To check this column Kościuszko sent Józef Sierakowski with a division numbering some 8,000 soldiers[^325], but fortune did not favour Sierakowski. On 19 September he suffered a great defeat near Brest; Suvorov took all 28 of his guns and scattered his division, all but annihilating it.
