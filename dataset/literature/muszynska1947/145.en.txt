How many Greater Poles underwent fortress imprisonment is hard to establish. It is known, however, that at the beginning of 1795 the Prussians were holding in Toruń 42 citizens from West Prussia and 60 from South Prussia; in Grudziądz, 48 from West Prussia and 25 from South Prussia[^471]. As to the names of these prisoners, we can give barely a few. Thus in the fortress of Grudziądz were confined, for a period of 2 years, Piotr Trzebuchowski, captain of cavalry in the Kuyavian uprising[^472], and the monks of the Benedictine monastery at Mogilno, Kajetan Smoliński and Izydor Domagalski, for 3 and 4 years, because they could not pay the fine imposed on them, in the amount of 6,000–8,000 thalers[^473]. In Toruń, Jan Żmichowski of Rawicz, private secretary of the starosta Mycielski, spent nearly half a year in detention[^474]. There are no closer data which would allow us to establish how many, and which, of the Greater Poles were held in the Silesian fortresses. We know only that in Głogów a citizen of Gniezno, Kłosowski, was kept in detention for 6 weeks[^475]. Those released had to swear the oath of allegiance anew[^476].

In South Prussia the Prussian government placed under sequestration the estates of the following Greater Poles: Wojciech Węgorzewski of Objezierze[^477], Antoni Chłapowski of Dąbrówka[^478], Celestyn Sokolnicki of Jarogniewice[^479], Mateusz Rosen of Kruchów[^480], Biskupski of Kuchary[^481], Jan Zembrzuski, owner of Great and Little Siekierki[^482], Antoni Grudziński, lord of Popowo, Kukulin, Rybkowo, Łagiewniki, Jagniewice, Sarbia and Podlesie[^483], Ksawery Łukomski, lord of Drzazgowo, Wysławice and Sokolniczki[^484], Florian Borzęcki, leaseholder of Porażyn[^485], the starosta Leszczyński of Grabów[^486], who died of wounds received in the uprising, the Landrat Józef
