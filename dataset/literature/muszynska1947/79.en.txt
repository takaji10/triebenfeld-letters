In the skirmish the infantry of Bielamowski's regiment held "most valiantly," sustaining, under the command of Lieutenant Colonel Mchowski, who "together with Captain Grzegorzewski distinguished himself," platoon fire that lasted nearly an hour. There was a moment when Dąbrowski very nearly fell into captivity, and only thanks to the help of the citizen Dziewanowski, from the Kuyavian uprising, and of Lieutenant Dąbrowski did he escape the hands of the enemy cavalry. The battle ended in defeat for the Prussians. Székely, leaving on the field 1 officer and 23 privates, together with many severely wounded soldiers and dead horses, withdrew under cover of night in the direction of Bydgoszcz, dragging behind him 12 wagons of the gravely wounded. The Poles had 7 killed and 9 wounded, and in addition 30 horses run through with bayonets. The most grievous loss was the death of Major Jarząbkowski of the 13th regiment, who "performing his duty with distinction," died the next day of the wounds he had received[^227].

In pursuit of the retreating Székely General Rymkiewicz dashed off, on Dąbrowski's orders, with 800 horse toward Bydgoszcz and reached it without hindrance. Half a mile from the town he scattered the hussars who raised the alarm against him, then took the heights above the town, posting vedettes on them, while he himself halted with the cavalry by the forest in such a way "that everyone had to suppose that infantry was with him." The town itself, surrounded by an old and not very high wall, lay before him as on the palm of a hand. Székely stood beyond the town in the forest, on the other side of the water[^228]. From Germans who had run out of the town Rymkiewicz learned that there were no more troops in the town than three battalions of garrison infantry. He therefore sent a report to General Dąbrowski on the night of 1 October with the assurance that, having placed guns on the heights, he "held the town in his hand." He wrote that half of the corps would have sufficed to take the town, for he had found it "unprepared."
