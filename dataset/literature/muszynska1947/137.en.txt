When in November the new Committee of Public Safety took an interest in foreign policy, the Polish cause again became topical. The old project of a Swedish-Danish-Polish-Turkish coalition was then brought forward; Parandier, appointed provisional agent to the provisional Polish government, was instructed to establish relations with the insurrectionary government based on trust and friendship and to declare that the French Republic would not refuse Poland help, so far as its own situation permitted, provided only that it had a guarantee that this help would serve the cause of liberty. Yet this help would have been belated and unnecessary. For it was not known that Kościuszko had already been a month in Russian captivity, and that the cause of the liberty of the Polish nation had been decided a few days earlier[^434].

The insurgents also counted on the service that Turkey was to render their cause. They hoped that, under French influence, it would declare war on Russia or Austria in order to make good the losses suffered two years before. These calculations too, however, were to fail completely. In Constantinople the former French envoy in Poland, Descorches, did indeed work in this direction, but his influence carried little weight. Forgotten by the terrorist government, he waited in vain for those 4 million francs so indispensable for setting the project of a Swedish-Danish-Polish-Turkish coalition on a real footing[^435].

Sweden, which was to join the coalition, although it regarded the insurgents with sympathy and had established diplomatic relations with the insurrectionary government, gave no help whatever[^436].
