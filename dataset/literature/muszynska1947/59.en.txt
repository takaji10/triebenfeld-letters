On the following day Kościuszko shared the joyful news "with the citizens, inhabitants of Warsaw", and, pointing at the same time to the effects that the Greater Poland rising might have on the further course of the siege of the capital, included in the proclamation the following words: "This happy event will upset certain designs of our enemies, but may also drive them to desperate steps and to hastening against Warsaw all the exertions of their fierce and vindictive pride[^153]". Kościuszko also held up the Greater Poles as a model to the citizens of the Volhynia voivodeship and others "torn from the body of the Commonwealth by the last violent Muscovite seizure", reproaching them in a proclamation sent out on 5 September that, while everything that bears the Polish name seizes upon every means of throwing off the "insulting yoke", they alone "remain in sluggish tranquillity", and with these words he encouraged them to rise: "You have no army in your country, but you have people, and these people will soon become an army. The Greater Poles had no army, yet having made a holy pact in one day in seven voivodeships, they crushed the invaders and raised an armed force of more than ten thousand. They gave all their brethren an example, one very necessary for them, that a rising can be carried out without the help of an army, provided only that the interest of the Fatherland unites the minds of all, and the love of liberty kindles them with a holy fire. The difference of faith and creed did not hold them back from this work. Both of these are no obstacle to loving the Fatherland and liberty. Let everyone honour God according to his own faith, and there is no faith that forbids a man to be free.
