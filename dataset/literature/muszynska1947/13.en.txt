In all, the number of soldiers in the Greater Poland Division amounted to about 6,200 men[^24].

With such a force, moreover without guns or ammunition, the considerably more powerful Prussians would have had to be faced, had Byszewski not received from Grodno an order to withdraw before them behind the Pilica and the Bzura[^25]. Even before that, however, there had been some lesser clashes.

On 26 January Major von Platen of Trenck's hussar regiment clashed in the small town of Sieraków [Zirke] with a detachment of Polish cavalry numbering 150 horse. After brief resistance the Poles surrendered. Two days later there was a clash at Kargowa [Unruhstadt], where Major Więckowski was stationed as garrison with 40 men of Brodowski's regiment. Major Milkau, entering the town with Frankenberg's infantry battalion, called upon the Polish soldiers to surrender. Więckowski, however, did not heed the summons but, shutting himself up in the town hall, defended himself with his detachment for some time until he fell, struck by an enemy bullet. Among his men several were wounded or killed; the rest laid down their arms[^26].

General Möllendorff reported these events to the King of Prussia on 1 February, the day after his own occupation of Poznań.
