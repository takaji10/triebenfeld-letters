To gain his bearings, Hundt sent Lieutenant Voss toward Fordon, still on 7 October, with 40 foot soldiers, as many hussars and 1 three-pounder gun. Marching all night, Voss reached the vicinity of Czarnowo[^278] early on 8 October, and having learned that 100 insurgents were encamped in that village, resolved to attack them. The attack succeeded for the Prussians. 2 officers, 15 soldiers and 50 wagons of forage fell into Prussian hands. On news of stronger Polish patrols approaching in the direction of Chełmno and Chełmża, and of an intended crossing at Solec by 2 companies from Dąbrowski's corps with two guns, Voss set out on the return journey[^279].

Meanwhile Dąbrowski did not storm Toruń. When he already had everything in readiness, he received certain news "that relief had reached Toruń". At first he supposed that "a corps had arrived at Toruń from Płock", but soon he was informed by reports that this relief was the corps of Colonel Ledivary[^280]. On hearing of the danger threatening Toruń, Ledivary had set out at once from Brześć Kujawski. On 11 October he stood already on the left bank of the Vistula, at Podgórz, and after leaving one battalion and cavalry outposts there, he crossed the Vistula in the night of 11 to 12 October, sent detachments out along that river, and marched to the village of Mokre[^281] in order to strike the enemy's left wing with his main forces[^282]. In such conditions Dąbrowski, who "at almost the same moment received a report from Włocławek that a corps was marching from Poznań and that the posts around Łabiszyn were retreating", was forced to abandon the attack on Toruń and "return again across the Vistula to Bydgoszcz"[^283].
