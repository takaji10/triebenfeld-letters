The soldiers of this regiment took horses, forage and rents from the district, imposed contributions, collected recruits, and forced some of the nobility by violence to join the rising. On the night of 21 to 22 November three insurgents of the Kuyavian regiment rode into Strzelno, two of them fully armed and the third with only a scythe; they announced that a larger detachment of insurgents would arrive in the town within the next few days, took 300 thalers from the cash box, and then departed[^406].

In view of such events the Prussian officials in Kuyavia did not feel secure. The most various and contradictory reports heightened their anxiety. They expected a new invasion. Indeed, leaflets distributed in the villages as far as Podgórz near Toruń announced plainly the early arrival of a corps already on the march under General Mokronowski, for which forage and provisions were to be collected. In Inowrocław the Germans addressed a request to the commandant of Toruń to reinforce the town's garrison as quickly as possible. Hundt then sent 106 infantry and 7 hussars, so that the garrison grew to 133 men. In Gniewkowo the tax collector sent money to Toruń every day so that it should not fall as booty to the insurgents[^407].

Nor was there yet peace in the neighbourhood of Szubin. In the forests there the indefatigable Krupecki roamed throughout November together with the leaseholder Śminowski. Their detachment numbered 60 armed men, and grain for its maintenance was supplied by a certain clerk from the surrounding estates. On 12 November 2 insurgents from Krupecki's band came to Szubin, where a fair was then being held, and gathered information about the stationing of the Prussian troops[^408].
