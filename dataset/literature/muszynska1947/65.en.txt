The dignity of plenipotentiary was conferred on: for the Poznań voivodeship, Hipolit Rogaliński; for the Kalisz voivodeship, Hiacynt Zakrzewski; for the Gniezno voivodeship, Nicefor Gorzeński; they were to send the Commander a report of their activities every week[^170]. On that same day, 6 September, there also appeared an appeal of the Supreme National Council to the Greater Poles, whose purpose was to strengthen the civic spirit among the citizens. It emphasised the service the Greater Poles had rendered to the national cause by their action in these words: "You have risen, fellow citizens, and God has not failed to bless your rising. Numerous hosts of hired champions trembled at the first sound of your endeavour, and those who had come with insolence to the capital of the country to threaten a free people and finally to put it in the chains prepared for it, fled shamefully by night, giving by their example a loud lesson to such invaders that the assailing of a free land cannot go unpunished[^171]".

Three days after the issuing of the appeal, on 9 September, a corps stood ready to march to Greater Poland[^172] at Młociny. In view of the anticipated clashes with Russia, which was drawing troops from Livonia, White Ruthenia and Ukraine, Kościuszko did not send many soldiers, wishing as he did "to make up for the deficiency in the person of the commanding general"[^173].
