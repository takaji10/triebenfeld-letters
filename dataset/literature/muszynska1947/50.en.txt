The surprised Prussians shut themselves up in the town hall, but after an hour's resistance they surrendered, losing to the victors a rich magazine and a cash box containing 700 thalers. The prisoners taken, after swearing that they would never take up arms against the Poles, were sent by the insurgents, together with the Prussian officials, to the Silesian border[^134]. On the same day Lipski and Zbijewski were elected land generals, the Rev. Prelate Ostrowski, who had arrived at the insurgent camp with a detachment of 20 men, was made a rotmistrz (cavalry captain), and a civil-military commission and a criminal court were called into being. An expedition to Łęczyca undertaken on 6 September to attack the garrison there did not succeed, and the reserve of 50 men left behind in Sieradz for that time had to retreat before the troops of Major Bonin, who was leading from Silesia a grenadier battalion from Pfuhl's regiment to cover the magazines at Sieradz and Kalisz [^135]. Bonin took cruel revenge on the people of Sieradz for their joining the rising.

For he plundered the town and the surrounding country, then set out toward Piotrków, and, having received a relief force of 150 infantry and 800 cavalry, turned through Sieradz toward Kalisz. On the way he seized the castellan of Wieluń, Karśnicki, the master of the horse Bartochowski, and the citizen Bąkowski, whom he put in chains and sent off to the Silesian fortress at Nysa. At that time even several women fell at the hands of the Prussians. Thus there were murdered the rotmistrz's wife Kożmińska, Miss Gątkiewiczówna, and the citizen Krąkowska, who was cut down with broadswords[^136].
