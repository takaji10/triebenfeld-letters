Eight days after the capture of Bydgoszcz, on 10 October, Dąbrowski set out in 2 columns for Toruń. The first, which he led himself, crossed the Vistula at Solec; the second, under Rymkiewicz, crossed at Fordon. At Zła Wieś (Bösendorf), on the Fordon-Toruń route, the two columns joined. The strength of this force was 1,000 infantry, 1,500 cavalry and 10 guns. Only 1 battalion of the Greater Poland rising remained in Bydgoszcz. On the following day, 11 October, Dąbrowski halted in the vicinity of Przysieka, where preparations were being made for the assault planned for the next day. According to the plan drawn up, the cavalry were to blockade the town on all sides and set it on fire in several places, in order in this way to create confusion in the town and enable the infantry to attack from a single point[^277]. Nor was Hundt, the commandant of the fortress of Toruń, idle. Fearing that the strength of the garrison might prove insufficient, and knowing how important Toruń was to the Prussians on account of its richly stocked magazines of munitions and provisions, he applied, immediately after the capture of Bydgoszcz, to General Favrat, who commanded the corps on the Narew, with a request for reinforcements. Soon 2 companies of Pfuhl's regiment did arrive in Toruń; a further 5 companies of the same regiment took up position at Płock and Bobrowniki, in order to come to the aid of the threatened post if need arose. On 7 October detachments of Polish cavalry appeared on both sides of the Vistula near Toruń, intending to quarter at Podgórz.
