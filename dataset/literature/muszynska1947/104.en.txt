Schwarz recalls that when he arrived in Poznań on 5 October he found the Prussian officials in the church of St Martin, where they had hidden in order to resist, at least for a time, the "superiority" of the insurgents from this highest point of the city and under cover of the Prussian troops. The sight that met Schwarz's eyes was as follows: the floor of the church was covered with straw used for sleeping, and those gathered there ate and drank, laughed and wept by turns, believing that the coming day would be the last of their lives. As time passed in this way and the insurgents somehow did not come, the Germans gradually recovered from their fright and on the following day left their hiding place[^310].

At that time the third of the Prussian generals operating in the territory of Greater Poland, Major-General Schwerin, was approaching Poznań. Until then he had been stationed at Kalisz, to which he had withdrawn before the advancing corps of Dąbrowski. On 23 September he set out by the road to Raszków against a band of insurgents near Dobrzyca, but after 2 days he returned to Kalisz, fearing for the magazine there. On 27 September he moved to Konin, where together with Generals Manstein and Elsner he planned an attack on the insurgents there; but on news that Dąbrowski was said to be at Słupca and a detachment of scythemen at Pyzdry, he went back again to Kalisz and for the moment contented himself with gathering information about the enemy. Schwerin set out toward Poznań only on 2 October, by the road through Pleszew [Pleschen], Jarocin, Śrem and Kórnik [^311]. At Pleszew he robbed the castellan Gorzeński, but he was reportedly not to get away with the loot any farther than Radlin[^312]. In that place, as Adam Turno describes in his memoir[^313], Captain of Cavalry (rotmistrz) Łojewski, sent by Kościuszko with an order to the Greater Poland insurrections, was at that time staying as a guest of the parish priest.
