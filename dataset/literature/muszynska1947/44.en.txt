For the help of regular troops promised by Kościuszko they were to wait for quite a long time yet, since as long as the enemy besieged Warsaw it was impossible to send it.

Nevertheless, small detachments of partisans were more than once able to harass the Prussians[^111].

The first in Greater Poland to raise the standard of the rising was the castellan of Kuyavia, Dionizy Mniewski. On 20 August, with a detachment of Kuyavian partisans, he attacked the Prussian posts at Brześć Kujawski [Brest-Kujawski], destroyed them and took possession of the town treasuries. There fell then "the valiant citizen Skarbek", and "Trzebuchowski, deputy for Kuyavia, made a colonel of the rising, and Wichrowski and Kosiński, citizens, contributed much to this rising"[^112]. From Brześć Mniewski hastened to Włocławek and halted there on 22 August. In driving out the town's garrison, Lieutenant Marwitz of Alt-Pfuhl's regiment and several soldiers fell. An hour later 11 boats with ammunition, intended for the Prussian besieging army, came up the Vistula from Grudziądz. Taking advantage of the opportunity, Mniewski with his detachment struck at the Prussians escorting the boats. The attack succeeded. Thirteen Prussians fell, and the whole transport fell into the insurgents' hands. Being unable to use all the ammunition, the Poles sank the greater part of it. For themselves they kept 100 barrels of powder, of which, however, 3 blew up. After accomplishing so bold a deed, Mniewski's insurgents[^113] made camp near Włocławek on the road leading to Brześć.
