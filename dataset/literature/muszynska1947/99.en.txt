Their whole band then set off in the direction of Wągrowiec, from where on 20 September an emissary of Krupecki appeared before the mayor in Rogoźno [Rogasen] with a demand that men be supplied to bear arms. Two days later this same detachment, together with the Poznań insurgents under Niemojewski and the Gniezno insurgents under Lipski, clashed, as we know, with a detachment of Székely's corps at Kcynia [^a99]. Colonel Hinrichs, who had come to the aid of the fighting Prussians, had to fall back to Szubin in the face of the steadily growing number of insurgents. The Poles had 22 wounded in this skirmish and as many taken prisoner; the Prussians only 5 wounded and 2 horses killed[^299].

According to the "Südpreussische Zeitung" of 24 September[^300], insurgents were also gathering in the vicinity of Zduny and near Śrem, from where a considerable detachment of theirs, though estimated on too high a scale, being reckoned at 2,000 peasants, was said to have set out in the night of 19 to 20 September in the direction of Kłecko. Only in Leszno and its surroundings, ever since General Manstein had been stationed there as garrison, did quiet prevail[^301].

From the time of the capture of Bydgoszcz, before Dąbrowski sent detachments out into the Netze District, dates an attack by insurgents on Chodzież [Kolmar]. On 4 October 40 armed men halted outside the town, whereupon 24 of them rode into it, took cloth from the local Jews against receipts and seized the whole stock of salt, at once selling it off at a low price to the citizens. With the 400 thalers received from the sale the insurgents set out on their way back. Even before they had left the town a small episode took place. A patrol of Trenck's hussars arrived from Piła, but, informed in good time of the presence of the insurgents, halted outside the town.
