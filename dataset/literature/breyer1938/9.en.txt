The floor of the valley was wholly unsettled in the Middle Ages, except for the island of Ochle, inhabited around 1300, and the raised strip of land near Krzymow, taken into possession some decades later.[^6] The swamp forest of that time had no attraction for people ready to settle; the spring floods deterred them.

### The Cistercian Monastery of Lond

The Polish duke Mieszko the Old, whose mother was the German count's daughter Salome of Berg, visited the city of Cologne, his mother's home, around 1135. There he came to know the monks of the Cistercian order at Altenberg. The strict rules of the order, above all the careful tending of the fields, from whose yield the monks lived, made a good impression on the prince. He resolved to invite the brothers of the order to settle in his principality. As the place for the order's work he chose the district of the castellany of Lond, which stretched along the right bank of the Warthe.[^7] In 1145 the deed of gift for the monastery of Lond to be founded was drawn up. It listed 13 villages given to the monastery, which lay mostly on the edge of the Warthe valley or a little way from it. They were Dolany, Szetlewo, Rzgów, Chorzeń, Głowiew, Rybaki, Grabieniec, Sławsko and Kwiatkowo, which still exist today, and some villages that have vanished in the course of time, such as Mokre, Stara Łąka, Świniarowo and Wyramowo, which in our opinion could be the still existing Wilamowo. The monastery's property grew very considerably in the 13th century by purchase, gift and exchange, mostly in the near neighbourhood of its villages. In this way were added Radolin, Sługocin, Kopojno, Skokom, Oleśnica, Osiecza, Zagórów, Bronniki, Wrąbczyn, Jaroszyn, Kowalewo, Policko, Tury and others. Instructive for the history of settlement are also the service villages, partly taken over and partly laid out by the monastery itself for particular purposes, whose names point directly to a particular service.
