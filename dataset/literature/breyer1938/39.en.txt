# Breyer (1938), p. 39
# translation by Claude

After the refusal there was probably nothing left for the sorely tried colonists but to take up the "unwanted wanderer's staff", as 41 families from around the town of Konin had in fact done before them in the spring of 1815, led by the Evangelical governor of the town of Kalisz: they emigrated to Bessarabia and founded the village of Alt-Arzis on steppe ground.[^18] Unfortunately we do not know the closer circumstances of the departure of this second group. That German marsh farmers from around Konin also took part in the colony of Paris, founded in Bessarabia in 1816, is quite likely; the family names point to it.[^19]

As late as October 1818, 40 relatively well-off Hohenlohe colonists from the village of Święcia turned to the government in Königsberg with a petition for leave to move back, after the government offices at Posen had previously given them a refusal.[^20] In Königsberg the Święcia farmers likewise had no success.

In the thirties single marsh dwellers found their way into the rising Łódź region, as appears from the church books of Pabianitz, Alexander and so on, although there were still enough chances of settlement in the Warthe marshes of the time.

Around 1830 the Kowalewken Holländerei near Konin vanishes, two decades later the Rumin Holl., which belonged to the crown estate of Rumin, and Klein-Öring near Zagórow. In the marsh east of Konin, where a lively German settlement had developed between 1820 and 1840, the emigration to Volhynia tore considerable gaps. It began shortly after 1872 and ended around 1890. Some figures may show us the scale of this ebbing away of German farmers. In 1882 the German share of the population of the commune (Gemeinde) of Krzymow, later Brzeźno, was 45 per cent, since of 4,596 inhabitants 2,158 were Germans. By the Polish census of 1921, 739 Evangelicals lived in the commune of Brzeźno, which is only 15 per cent. A picture not quite so dark is given by the neighbouring commune of Wysokie, formerly Kramsk. In 1882, among 3,716 inhabitants, 433 Germans lived there, which is barely 12 per cent.
