the dukes' time may come the service villages of Bronniki (shield-makers), Rybaki (fishermen), Łagiewniki (makers of small casks) and Kuchary Borowe (cooks). The villages of Świątniki (church servants) and Kuchary Kościelne were laid out for particular duties of the monastery. Should not Kochowo, north of the monastery on Lake Powidz, also be taken for a service village of the cooks? When the number of the monastery's inmates had grown over time, more servants were needed. In the privilege of 1261 we find these further service settlements: Szetlewo "cum cocis" (with the cooks), Rzgowo "cum sanctuariis et stabularis" (with the church servants and stable-hands), Grabieniec "cum camerariis" (with the chamberlains) and Sławsko "cum piscatoribus" (with the fishermen).[^8] The name of the monastery's property "Dziedzice" points to particular legal forms. It may have been a village of free peasants, in no bond of servitude to the monastery. The village owed the monastery only rent. The monastery's possessions also grew by the gifts of single church dignitaries. At the end of the 12th century the Archbishop of Gniezno lived as a hermit in the village of Dobrow on the Warthe. He was the last descendant of the powerful noble family of the Poraj. His father's inheritance, the villages of Dobrow, Rzuchów and Leszno, he left to the monastery of Lond for all time.[^9]

Among the finest cultural deeds of the German monks were without doubt the many new villages they founded, the more so as they also called German colonists into the country. In 1250, as the charters show, the monastery, with the consent of Duke Bolesław, founded under German law the village of Wola Londska, also called Freidorf, and settled it with Germans and free Poles. The abbots did the same in founding the villages of Biskupice or Bischofsdorf, Kotlin or Kesselberg, Wola Koszutska, Kuchary Kościelne and others. In 1296 we find in the village of Kossut a German village headman (Schulz), Christian, who two years later sold his headman's holding and moved to Prussia.[^10] It should be said here that of the German monks' services to the cultivation of the land we know only scattered fragments. Deep research is needed here.
