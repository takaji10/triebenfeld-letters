*Kaczka* and *Mechnówka* arose in 1792. The land given out was covered with swamp forest and brushwood. These were the first settlements of the Leśnik language island. In 1794 we come upon the village name *Myśliborzer Holland* for the first time in the church books of Rosterschütz.

To judge by Gilly's map, whose groundwork was surveyed around 1796, the following new settlements may have arisen in the marsh and on its edge after 1790, listing them up the Warthe from Peisern: Tarnower Holl., Komorzer Holl., the Neu-Peisern town Holländerei, Wromczyneker Holl., Zagórower Holl., the Camaldolese Holländerei of Bielawy, and the Holländereien Smolniki, Rysiny and Młynek on the edge, while in the middle of the marsh we find Lubin and Dzierzin. In the land between the rivers near Dombie arose Falborzyce and Dombrowice Holl., near Brudzew on the other hand Sokołek Holl., on the Ner Lyszyce Holl.

If we sum up the German villages founded in old Polish times by private and church colonisation, the result is this. In the marsh there were 20 Holländereien and on the banks 20, a considerable number all the same. Some settlements, such as Lubin, Dzierzin, Sokołek, Lyszyce, Romonek, Tarnowo and Krzymowo, have since vanished from the marsh. They were mostly small scattered settlements.

We would not leave unmentioned the relatively numerous water millers of the Warthe marshes of that time. In the church book already mentioned we find in 1778 a German miller at Szadow, in 1779 at Powiercie, in 1781 at Posoka beyond Koło, and in 1783 at the Glinka mill near Konin. At Powiercie in 1781 there were a farrier and a farmer.

### The South Prussian Period

In the first years of the Prussian taking of the Warthe marshes, German farmers of Pomeranian stock settled on the Borzewiska estates of the landowner Milewski. The settlement charter, drawn up in German in 1796, gives us an insight into the terms of settlement. On taking up the land the colonists had to pay 189 Taler of entry money. The manor kept the right to sell drink for itself. The settlers received six free years.
