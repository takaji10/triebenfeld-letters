Cartload after cartload of drift sand is carried off to the swampy spots of the meadows, so that new bread-land may arise. Tough and untiring, the German goes about this honourable work of cultivation. In proud awareness he treads here in the footsteps of his forefathers, who often, in far harder conditions, set foot in this swamp wilderness with courage and resolve to fight.

> *"They knew but toil and meagre bread,*
> *Mire and wasteland breathed fever's death,*
> *The foul waters welling at the spade*
> *Their will forced sudden into ditch and drain,*
> *And ever deeper into the wild drove*
> *The hoe; slowly the sown field rose..."*
> *(Maria Kahle)*

These proud memories the sight of the neat villages of the Warthe marshes calls up in the ethnic German (volksdeutsch) wanderer. And confident hopes that the strength of the people will last fill him as he comes to know better the splendid German stock of this still, dreamy Warthe landscape.
