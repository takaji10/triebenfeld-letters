At the same time the lord of Piorunow laid out in swampy ground the German village of *Genowefa Pior.*, in which mostly poor settlers established themselves, so-called "Büdner" (cottagers). Near the old village of Groß-Politz the new village of Klein-Politz arose around 1810, which like the old one was taken up mainly by well-to-do farmers.

From what has been set out it follows that most of the German settlements were laid out at the end of the old Polish period. The private interests of the Polish lords were the cause of the German colonisation. The German settlers took over either felled stretches of forest or mouldering meadows grown with alder bushes. The quality of the soil was in most cases poor, mainly sandy and swampy, a so-called "Schliefboden" or "alder ground", as the colonists called it. In single places there was also very fertile silt soil, the "Letten".
