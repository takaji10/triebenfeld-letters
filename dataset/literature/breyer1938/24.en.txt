At the same time the landowners, besides tilling their fields better, also thought of enlarging them. Deserted land, marsh ground and wastes came under the plough again. In some regions of Congress Poland new manor land was won by clearing out peasants (Bauernlegen). Not least, the landowners took up a new settling of their waste lands by German colonists. In 1818 the Holländerei of Budki grew in area by the founding of *Neu-Budki*. Around 1820, 18 German marsh farmers laid out the village of *Konstantynow* and *Wysoker Holl.* in the forests of the estate of Podgórz. The settlers received from the lord rights of wood and pasture, but no school land. At the same time *Rostoka-Holl.* arose close by the bank of the Warthe, which received the right of settlement from the estate of Krzymow. The lord of the manor of Wola Laszczowa, K. Potworowski, in March 1836 let out the "Great and Little Forest Ground" of 400 Morgen, bought from the government domain of Stare Miasto, Konin county, to German takers, who founded the village of *Neu-Czarkow*. As purchase money the new settlers bound themselves to pay a hundred złoty for each Morgen. For the pieces of land with timber on them a separate payment for the timber was asked, by the valuation of trusted men named by both sides. There were two or three free years, according to the quality of the forest land. Near the village of Alt-Budki the villages of *Neudorf* and *Rosenberg* were founded around 1835. The latter small settlement vanished after some decades. The German farmers gradually vanished from Neudorf too, as in general German landholding has much declined in this region.

In 1837 the German farmers of the village of Lesnik enlarged their field area by buying pieces of land from the neighbouring estate of Borzewisko. For the newly acquired land they received five free years.

In March 1841 a contract of sale was drawn up at the manor of Jabłkow, Konin county, between the lord, Karl Osterloff, and 27 German and 10 Polish farmers. On the estate's lands, partly swampy and partly overgrown with brush, together with the pieces of field farmed until then by the estate's peasants, arose the villages of *Bilczew* and *Strumyk*.
