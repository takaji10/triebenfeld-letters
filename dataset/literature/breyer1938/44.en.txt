If over 110 years the size of the holdings underwent no great swings, the same cannot be said of the holders. Of the farming families of about 1818, the direct descendants have stayed in the village in only five cases, which makes 12.5 in a hundred. Changes of ownership appear quite often in the German villages in the course of time, which likewise, on a small scale, helps to make the conditions of stock changeable. In the example of Schwenz we see a constant up and down of the villagers. Most marsh villages can count themselves lucky, since whenever ownership changed, apart from the mass emigration to Volhynia, German buyers from the nearer or wider neighbourhood always stepped in, and in this way the uniform character of the village community has been kept.
