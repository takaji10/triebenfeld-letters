--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Eingabe Triebenfelds an Staats Kanzler Hardenberg für den notleidenden Fürsten Hohenlohe. Medizinalrat Cosmar habe sich als Testamentsvollstrecker der Fürstin Sacken in die Majoratsgüter Sławięcice, Lasowice und Oppurg (79500 Rthl Ertrag) eingenistet, gestützt auf einen Transact von 1811, den die Sterbende nur mit drei Kreuzen zeichnete; er ziehe alle Einkünfte ein und lege keine Rechnung. Die Curischen Erben wollen für 426000 Rthl 112000 nehmen. Erbeten werden ein Vorschuss von 112000 Rthl, Aufhebung des Transacts, Cosmars Entfernung und ein Bericht des Oberlandesgerichts Brzeg. Triebenfeld bietet 60000 Rthl Kaution.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Petition by Triebenfeld to State Chancellor Hardenberg on behalf of the distressed Prince Hohenlohe. It says the Councillor of Medicine Cosmar, as executor of the will of Princess Sacken, has lodged himself in the entailed estates of Sławięcice, Lasowice and Oppurg (79,500 Rthl in yield), relying on a settlement of 1811 that the dying Princess signed only with three crosses; that he draws all the revenues and submits no account; and that the Curland heirs will take 112,000 for 426,000 Rthl. Requested are an advance of 112,000 Rthl, annulment of the settlement, Cosmar's removal and a report from the High Court of Appeal in Brzeg. Triebenfeld offers 60,000 Rthl as security.

(to the Prince State Chancellor von Hardenberg)

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord, I am most submissively [text lost] to lay before Your Most Serene Highness the enclosed letter of the Prince of Hohenlohe, and at the same time to beg that Most High Person for the most gracious permission to depict to Your Most Serene Highness the most pressing distress of the Prince, and therewith to lay at Your Serene Highness's feet a plan for his rescue and for the relief of his sufferings.

From of old the Prince of Ingelfingen was Your Most Serene Highness's most faithful adherent and admirer; this man, who has been brought to misfortune through no fault of his own but merely through his yielding to the influence of others upon his benevolent disposition, throws himself into Your Serene Highness's arms; he begs to be protected from the chicanery of the medical councillor Cosmar, which aims at nothing else than that the good Prince shall go hungry in his old age and perish in misery.

The Sławięcice, Lasowice and Oppurg estates amount in value to a fortune of more than two million Rthl, which, according to the instrument enclosed in copy under A, were confirmed as an entailed estate by His Majesty in 1799, and which today, in the most straitened of times, still yield 79,500 Rthl of income, according to the statement appended under B. Of these considerable estates the Prince, as lord of the entailed estate, is to enjoy the revenues. But by every artifice that can be conceived the medical councillor Cosmar has contrived to nest himself both in the entailed estates and in the estate left by the deceased Princess Sacken, so that he commands everything as though it were his own property, draws in all the revenues, and gives no one a farthing of them. He is called executor of the testament — but what could the Princess Sacken dispose of by way of death? She was of these estates, and that only in part, merely holder of a life interest. The Act A, sanctioned by His Majesty, determines everything: to the [uncertain: divorced] Princess, now married von Sacken, belonged all the estates. After her separation she ceded her whole inheritance of her own free will to her [text lost] wedded lord, the Prince of Hohenlohe, and the Prince founded out of it the trust entail which His Majesty sanctioned. How then could the Princess Sacken still appoint an executor of the testament? Over the ready money she left she might well make testament; but since she has for the greatest part bequeathed everything to her favourite, Prince Adolph, there vanishes every legal ground upon which she, and far less her bosom friend Cosmar, might meddle in the affairs of the entailed estates. But in order to force his way into these affairs notwithstanding the legal barriers standing against it, to [uncertain: dringen] dispose at his own will, and to command the incomes without accountability, as though over his own property, the medical councillor Cosmar has undertaken the most audacious venture of a determined pettifogger. He drew up the far-reaching settlement here enclosed under C at a time when the Princess von Sacken, if indeed she was still living, could not possibly have surveyed the content and the consequences, since she lay at her last gasp, and in the most favourable case was already so weakened in her vital powers that she signed the far-reaching document, instead of with her signature, only with 3 crosses. This settlement is the sum of all intrigues. There are set out in it so many debts and demands which the Princess Sacken would have to claim, that they exceed everything, and which were never thought of in the deed of foundation. The Prince has already made mention of this to His Excellency the Minister of Justice von Kircheisen, as the copy appended under D shows. In his answer under E, however, silence is kept upon it. This settlement further states that Cosmar may command all the incomes of the entailed estates, and needs render no account to anyone — Whether a second executor of the testament of this kind is anywhere to be found in history I doubt; but on that very account the Princess, at the making of the 3 crosses, could not possibly any longer have been in possession of a sound understanding, for the settlement goes to this, to defraud the monarch and all the Prince's creditors, and to deprive the Prince in particular of honour and of everything, and this the deceased Princess cannot have wished! She knew too well what the Prince had endured for her daughter's sake. There could therefore lie at the ground of this contrivance nothing but the most unbounded malice; for the completion of this notorious settlement, wholly contrary to the sanctioned Act of the year 1799, they even knew how to move the Prince to his approval by the most shameful misrepresentations — Now Cosmar had, in his opinion, a game already won; he at once installed his inexperienced under-age son as administrator of the estates. Drew with him all the revenues, paid neither interest nor capital, and, despite all admonitions of the Higher Provincial Court at Brzeg, which he merely put off with defiant sarcastic answers, rendered no account either — The Prince is to draw yearly, according to the 1st agreement, 24/m Rthl, and his son August, now Prince at Öhringen, 18,000 Rthl; also the latter was, immediately after the death of the Princess Sacken, to be paid 100/m Rthl as a "Praedikat" toward the settling of his debts. But 3 years have passed, and the old gentleman has received no more than 2,500 Rthl, and the son, Prince August, not a Groschen; where then do the revenues of yearly 79,500 Rthl remain? where is the possibility of obtaining compensation from Cosmar? But here Cosmar's plan comes to light — whatever is said to him by the Prince, he bears it all without [uncertain: blushing] — The Prince's letter to His Excellency the Minister of Justice [text lost] a proof of it. His Excellency's answer, however,

leaves Cosmar's excuse to be guessed — in fact this man bears every humiliation, only so as not to have to give up his plan and not to have to let go his booty. The foundation of Cosmar's plan rests upon the capital of 300/m Rthl which the Courland heirs have to claim against the Prince, and which with the interest included [uncertain: v] 1805 — amounts to 426,000 Rthl. His Royal Majesty has guaranteed the debt, and the former Princess Hohenlohe signed [text lost] the instrument as principal debtor herself, together with the Prince. In the oft-mentioned settlement of 1811, however, the Prince has bound himself to pay this capital together with interest out of his own means, and to free the former Princess, now married Princess von Sacken, from the payment — A double absurdity — firstly because so considerable a payment is expected of the Prince, who has not ten, let alone hundreds of thousands to pay — secondly, because they believed they could set aside the rights of third interested persons without their consent, — the deceased Princess surely thought too nobly that she should on the brink of the grave still have set herself to defraud her sovereign and more other persons of many hundred thousand Rthl. No one can here mistake the machinery of a self-seeking chicaner, to whom everything had hitherto succeeded — Cosmar knew that the Courland heirs already had a judgment by default in their favour by which the Prince was condemned to pay this considerable sum; he knew that with the Prince, whom he had already for 3 years let go hungry, no object of an execution was to be found — He concluded from this that the monarch would now be applied to by the Courland heirs for payment; and thus he supposed that His Royal Majesty, incensed after this metamorphosis, would disclaim the matter, and out of vexation wholly annul the trust entail founded in 1799, which was sanctioned only without prejudice to the most high interest and to that of any third party. Then, in his opinion, the former Princess, now Countess Sacken, could come forward again, reclaim the estates, have the poor Prince driven out of everything and left to starve — only then would Cosmar's plan have wholly succeeded, and his reward would certainly have been princely — Your Most Serene Highness alone can soon remedy these intrigues and rescue the good Prince out of Cosmar's claws — An agreement has already been reached with the mandataries of the Courland heirs, according to which they will take 112,000 for the whole demand of 426,000 Rthl, give receipt for everything, and hand out all the documents — Just so an agreement has already been reached with all the other creditors, that they will content themselves with a yearly payment on account of 12,000 Rthl, which the Prince has assigned out of the entailed-estate revenues due to him / but which Cosmar, despite all demands even from the Higher Provincial Court at Brzeg, will not pay / — It only depends upon this, that Your Most Serene Highness most graciously grant an advance of 112/m Rthl for the payment of the Courland heirs, even if only for a short time; but especially that you deservedly annul the settlement of 1811 so maliciously forged by Cosmar, which in the manner of its drawing up is more than suspect, is contrary to the Act of 1799 sanctioned by His Royal Majesty himself, irreconcilable with the well-acquired rights of third parties, in short nothing but the presumptuous attempt of an intriguing stranger against the Prince's fortune; that Cosmar be removed from everything, and be held to the strictest rendering of account.

The advance of 112,000 Rthl can, for which I too, if it be permitted, will stand surety with a security of 60,000 Rthl, in the shortest time indeed be discharged in various ways.

Were a payment on account, as such is set out under B, not to be accepted at 25,000 Rthl yearly, then a part of the 100,000 which Prince August is to receive as a "Praedikat" could for the present be applied to it. Only above all the settlement of 1811, so dishonouring even to the Prince, must be annulled, and an honest, expert administrator be appointed in the name of His Royal Majesty, since that Most High Person is interested therein on account of the guarantee. Then nothing stands in the way of the discharge of the advance of 112/m. I further remark most submissively that Cosmar holds behind him over 43,000 Rthl of the moneys which the Prince is to draw for his maintenance, and which the Prince assigned to the general creditors in the settlement — but which Cosmar absolutely will not pay — a further 60,000 Rthl or so of manufactured goods are in stock in the warehouses — these, however, are to be regarded as a working capital — meanwhile these are speaking proofs of the richness of the estates, from which a chicaner draws everything, keeps it behind him for the carrying out of his plan, and in the meanwhile lets the good Prince and those about him go hungry — I must further adduce here that I have included for amortisation the 35,000 Rthl formerly advanced to the Prince out of the B[text lost] treasury. From the benevolence constantly shown by Your Most Serene Highness toward the Prince of Hohenlohe I hope for the granting of my most submissive request, whereby I most obediently implore that a report be required of the Higher Provincial Court at Brzeg, to which His Excellency the Minister of Justice has referred the Prince himself, upon the situation of the Prince, upon all his sufferings which he must endure through the said Cosmar, and indeed that all here is truly and certainly depicted; from which more will be brought to light and the pressing distress of the Prince will be proved.

In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Vienna, the 20th of October

1814

faithful most humble servant

von Triebenfeld
