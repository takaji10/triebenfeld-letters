--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Zastrow kündigt ein mit heutiger Post abgehendes Schreiben des Königs an: Der König will den wahren Wert der südpreußischen Güter des Fürsten sowie von Krotoszyn und Polajewo untersuchen lassen, um sie gegeneinander abzuwägen. Stehen dem beabsichtigten Tausch keine wichtigen Hindernisse entgegen, werde der König den Wunsch gern erfüllen. Zastrow dankt für das Schreiben vom 7. des Monats. Die Sache gehöre nicht in sein Fach, er hoffe aber auf einen befriedigenden Abschluss.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Zastrow gives notice of a letter from the King going out with today's post: the King wishes to have the true value of the Prince's South Prussian estates and of Krotoszyn and Polajewo investigated, in order to weigh them against one another. If no important obstacles stand in the way of the intended exchange, he says the King will gladly grant the wish. Zastrow thanks him for the letter of the 7th of the month. He says the matter does not belong to his department, but that he hopes for a satisfactory conclusion.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
94

Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

From the letter of His Majesty the King going out by today's post, Your Most Serene Highness will be pleased to perceive more fully how His Majesty is minded to have the true value of Your Serene Highness's South Prussian estates, and that of Krotoszyn and Polajewo, more closely examined, in order then to be able duly to balance these possessions one against the other; and I can assuredly assure Your Serene Highness that, if no weighty obstacles stand in the way of the intended exchange, it will afford His Majesty the King pleasure to have complied with Your Serene Highness's wish in this matter.

For the gracious confidence which Your Most Serene Highness is pleased to show me by Your Serene Highness's gracious letter of the 7th of this month, I offer Your Serene Highness [uncertain: my] most deeply obliged thanks; and although the matter itself does not pass through my hands, inasmuch as it does not fall within my

department, I shall nonetheless be no less glad to see it brought to Your Most Serene Highness's complete satisfaction, inasmuch as the heartfelt veneration I bear for Your Serene Highness assuredly makes me take the warmest part therein. Nothing in the world could only hereafter make happier than to be able to lay before Your Most Serene Highness the proofs of that true reverence with which I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's Berlin,

the 18th of December

1798.

entirely most humble, faithful

and most obedient

Zastrow
