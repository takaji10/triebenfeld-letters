--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld kann Kontop nicht verlassen, da Coste nicht gekommen ist; Kontop ist verpachtet, Świniary noch nicht. Hawich bezichtigt Honrichs des Betrugs; Triebenfeld hat beide vorgeladen und will einen Actuarius nach Zagórów senden, um zu ermitteln, wer von Untertanen und Pächtern Geld genommen hat. Hawich, Honrichs und Hecker hätten sich widerrechtlich Hufen angeeignet. Über 12 Deputationen der Gemeinden baten um Zahlungsfrist. Er bittet um die Fracht-, Zoll- und Wagezettel für das Eisen.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld cannot leave Konotop because Coste has not come; Konotop has been leased out, Świniary not yet. Hawich accuses Honrichs of fraud; Triebenfeld has summoned both and wants to send an actuary to Zagórów to find out who has taken money from subjects and tenants. He says Hawich, Honrichs and Hecker have unlawfully appropriated Hufen for themselves. More than 12 deputations from the village communities asked for time to pay. He asks for the freight, customs and weighing notes for the iron.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

Your Serene Highness's most gracious letter of the 24th I received today, just as I was on the point of writing to Your Most High Self. I wished only to report most humbly to Your Most Serene Highness that I cannot yet depart from here, inasmuch as Coste has not come. I have indeed leased out Konotop, but not yet Świniary, and as regards the Berlin affair I am not clear in my own mind, for this is a wound struck with a blunt iron which keeps breaking open again, and which will yet cost me my life — Meanwhile God must help, let it last as long as it will.

Next to this, the Polish affair now holds me fettered here. Honrichs was here, as I lately reported to Your Most Serene Highness; what he proposed I have most obediently sent in to Your Most High Self — After his departure I received a letter from Hawich, who informs me

that Honrichs is an arch-swindler — Although this must first be proved, I have nevertheless at once summoned Honrichs and Hawich here, in order to investigate the matter

to investigate; and I also intend, when the [uncertain: L:] are here, to send the actuary from here, who [text lost: der Teutsch ein pohleisch Bau], to Zagórów, so that he may there call together all the people and enquire precisely what Honrichs, or anyone else, has taken from the subjects and the tenants. Honrichs and every man must then show where the monies have been applied, and there the fraud must come to light — I cannot believe it, with full right, since the sequestration has been lifted according to the enclosed decrees and only an overseer has been appointed, so that no money may be taken away there. Under these dispositions Honrichs surely may take nothing; it must surely come to light who has drawn monies and where he has applied them. Your Serene Highness has received nothing, and neither have I.

Whatever is in the matter shall and must come out; still, I do not believe Hawich's reports, for more than 12 deputations from the communities have been here and have repeatedly begged for time to pay. From these people I have after all always heard it myself, that they could not pay at present. Be it, however, as it may, the matter must come to light — Moreover, this much is quite certain, that neither Hawich nor anyone else has acted [uncertain: trom] the instructions and powers of attorney which I always gave. Honrichs alone has candidly protested against the conduct of the Dąbska woman and of the [uncertain: Geisteren] there, so that no matter could ever acquire legal force whereby Your Serene Highness's rights were secured. As I believe, the gentlemen have all defrauded, for Hawich, Honrichs and Hecker have appropriated Hufen to themselves and built upon them

but fortunately this is unlawful and [uncertain: ein Approbirt] by me; here one may chastise and let go. The places, moreover, are so handsomely laid out that each is worth several thousand Rthl — Had the gentlemen defrauded, there is something to hold on to.

When this is finished, and I am clear here with the Widows' Fund concerning my own affairs, then I shall come to lay myself at Your Serene Highness's feet.

Grevenitz writes just now from Berlin that General von Köckritz has said much that is [uncertain: Mühliches] and good of Your Serene Highness — God grant that in this year now begun everything may go according to Your Serene Highness's wish, where Your Most High Self will be compelled to learn to forget all Your sufferings.

I was written that Lieutenant General von Köckritz would retire and that General Kleist would [uncertain: eine hinnen] his place with the King; I have now asked him myself about it, but have as yet received no answer.

That the false Count Götz goes ad patriam is good; I have learned wondrous things of the man — Of the new organisations some hope much — I nothing; the money business will yet cause great terror — Grevenitz writes that the general moratorium had been extended to Midsummer 1810, and that it is said it is to last, by a newer resolution, until Midsummer 1812 — This too I do not believe; against it will contend [text lost: B — und Lecht Meyer Wolff].

May Your Most Serene Highness deign most graciously to send me as soon as possible the freight, toll and weigh slips etc. for the iron, sent to You in May, so that I may convince the boatman that I should otherwise have to pay here a second time; otherwise I shall not get the iron out. In deepest respect I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Just at the

Konotop

1 January 1810. humble and obedient servant

v Triebenfeld

moment at which I was about to send off this letter, I receive the enclosed letter of Honrichs by an express courier, which Your Most Serene Highness will deign to return to me together with the 3 resolutions.

My messenger sent to Zagórów has not yet returned — Hawich and Hecker must answer, or rather must come — if they stay away, then I am convinced that they are [uncertain: Lube]. I shall seek to find out exactly what is in the matter; in advance I am convinced that Hawich and Hecker are rascals, for how can the [uncertain: Liebe] say that the money has been carried out of the country? I have not seen a Groschen; nor can Honrichs take anything, because the people are forbidden to pay monies to Honrichs, and that this is welcome to the rent-paying people one may believe without an oath. Honrichs at least does his duty, in that he lets the Polish dogs take nothing. I am already content that he lives up to my orders and confronts that rabble there without fear, lets nothing be taken, and courageously protests against everything.

I shall learn everything from Brzechffa [uncertain: um] Garczynski; if Hecker and Hawich have made false reports, they shall be chastised. Hecker shall never see anything again of his paper caution. With the spirits and iron much might be done; since nothing can be sold from Your Serene Highness's foundry, I shall no doubt obtain iron elsewhere — I must make the trial, and see what comes out of this [text lost: Barotto Hamel].
