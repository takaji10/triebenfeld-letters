--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld klagt über die hinhaltenden Leixner und Meyer Bernhard. Sie versprechen nun, 76/m Rthl und etwa 20/m Rthl in Banco Obligation an Bernhardi zu schicken, halten aber 30/m Rthl zurück, weil der Fürst Betsche zurückgekauft habe. Für Trąbczyn nahm er einige Tausend Rthl auf Wechsel. Durch den Groß Kanzler und Köckritz erreichte er, dass die Deposition der Broniewskischen Gelder angeordnet wird. Köckritz stellt Güter in Erbpacht in Aussicht. Fürstin Sacken kündigte durch Cosmar das Capital von 30/m Rthl auf Koschentin.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld complains about Leixner and Meyer Bernhard, who keep putting him off. He says they now promise to send 76/m Rthl and about 20/m Rthl in bank bonds to Bernhardi, but are holding back 30/m Rthl because the Prince has bought Pszczew back. For Trąbczyn he raised a few thousand Rthl on a bill of exchange. Through the Grand Chancellor and Köckritz he obtained an order for the deposit of the Broniewski money. Köckritz holds out the prospect of estates on hereditary lease. Princess Sacken, through Cosmar, has called in the capital of 30/m Rthl on Koszęcin.

Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord!

Mr Leixner, Meyer Bernhard and their partners are wretched fellows. First they promised to pay everything in Berlin — in Berlin it was said again that Friedländer had gone away. They would settle everything in Frankfurt an der Oder. Now that I arrive here today, they assure me that they mean to send the 76/m Rthl, and likewise a further 20/m Rthl or so, by express courier in Bank Obligations, directly from Bernhardi. To the question why they are holding back 30/m Rthl — answer: we are already paying too much, for Your Serene Highness has bought Pszczew back In[illegible].

I am still doing battle with the [illegible], and just now Meyer Bernhard promises to add a further 10/m Rthl, but with 20 the rascal stays in arrears.

In Berlin, since Wunster had drawn up the obligation faultily, I was still able to obtain the money; I have merely taken a few thousand rt on bills of exchange

so as to be able to present a face in Trąbczyn in every way, and so that Your Serene Highness may find money there.

The deposition of the Broniewski monies will nevertheless without doubt be ordered; I have pushed it through by means of the Grand Chancellor and General von Köckritz. This is honourable for Your Serene Highness, and certainly some seventy thousand rt will be gained thereby. Had I acted sooner, on my own and with such boldness, everything would already have been disposed of. The answer from the King concerning the indemnification is quite obliging and seems to betoken good will. Meanwhile General von Köckritz has charged me to convey his most cordial greetings to Your Serene Highness, and to tell you most highly that you should most certainly receive estates, and indeed right good ones, in hereditary lease, from which something might be gained; for the King would not be in a position to give money, and since Schlabrendorff wished to return, this would follow of

itself. Meanwhile the hereditary lease should be negotiated advantageously. For the rest, the Princess Sacken has, out of particular grace and mildness, deigned to have the capital of 30/m Rthl standing [illegible] Koszęcin called in through Mr Cosmar, as soon as she heard that Your Serene Highness had bought the estates. Oh the shame of it. General von Köckritz despises her [uncertain: Breenhau], and so does every honest man.

May God bring Your Serene Highness back in good health — everything will nevertheless go well, and you will in every way yet stand as the victor.

In deepest respect I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's Frankfurt an der Oder, the 21st of June 1804.

most faithful servant

v Triebenfeld
