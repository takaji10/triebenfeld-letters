--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Berichtet, Russland verlange die Prosna als Grenze, wodurch Trąbczyn und Triebenfelds Blizanower Güter unter fremde Hoheit kämen und Trąbczyn schwer zurückzubekommen wäre. Der Fürst Kanzler widersetze sich. Erhalte Russland die Distrikte, müsse die Sache mit Kaiser Alexander abgemacht werden, da Dąbska oder Miączyńska bei Lanskoy in Gnaden stehe. Erwartet den Bericht des Brieger Oberlandesgerichts. Bittet um Blanquets, Vollmachten, den Brief an den Kanzler und den Revers für Barbe, da Pourtalès wohl eintrete.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
He reports that Russia is demanding the Prosna as the border, which would place Trąbczyn and Triebenfeld's Blizanów estates under foreign sovereignty and make Trąbczyn hard to recover. He says the Prince Chancellor is opposing this. Should Russia obtain the districts, he says the matter would have to be settled with Emperor Alexander, since Dąbska or Miączyńska stands in favour with Lanskoy. He awaits the report of the High Court of Appeal at Brzeg. He asks for blank forms, powers of attorney, the letter to the Chancellor, and the written undertaking for Barbe, since Pourtalès will probably come in.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

For some days past there have been great debates here concerning Poland, when on the other side they depart from the first treaty and demand absolutely the Prosna as the border, that is to say from Toruń onward as far as the fall of the Prosna into the Wartha and then down the Prosna as far as Ostroszew — If this comes to pass, then the Trąbczyn estates and even my own Blizanów estates come under [uncertain: Waste] sovereignty — And then it will be a hot business to get Trąbczyn back again — The upright Prince Chancellor sets himself alone vehemently against all this and appeals to the word given by the Emperor of Russia — the honest man had vexed himself quite feverish, and had to keep to his bed for 3 days — His Majesty the King seem also very [uncertain: pleased], for 6 days now the gentleman has dined quite alone off the napkin and sees no one — this gives occasion here to many rumours — All Vienna is a [uncertain: Gesammste] of great wars impending — indeed even from Berlin such reports come in — I must now be all the more attentive and watch which way the scale will incline, for if Russia obtains the districts in which the estates lie, then the Prince Chancellor alone will [uncertain: umfürehr] settle the matter with the Emperor Alexander, since he did not allow that I might present Your Serene Highness's letter to the Emperor — for the Dąbska or Miączyńska stands in high favour with Lanskoy and therefore everything can only be done with the Emperor alone — For the rest, every friend and admirer of Your Serene Highness here awaits eagerly the report of the Brzeg Superior Provincial Court; [text lost] indeed that I may receive the same speedily here and that it turns out conformable to the first memorial — then all will end according to our wish — Your letters to me may it please you now to have sent by way of Wrocław and Dresden under the address of the Royal Prussian Post Director Balke at Dresden —

1. To me nothing further than to the said von Triebenfeld, and take care to make it up with a wafer seal, then have 2 a [uncertain: courier] over it made out to Barbe; the address is — to the Royal Prussian Court Councillor and [uncertain: Maß] Knight Mr Barbe, Right Honourable [uncertain: Hochwol], at Vienna

3. Over this a cover is made out to the Royal Prussian Post Director Mr Balke, Your Honour, at Dresden — only in this manner do we receive the letters securely and [uncertain: geſcheinnde], for since the 17th of December

I have not had a syllable from Your Serene Highness — Full of longing, I look for the blanks, the powers of attorney and everything, and especially useful would be the letter to His Serene Highness the Chancellor. Dreadfully prejudicial may these delays prove in the end — The written undertaking to Barbe is also most necessary, for Pourtalès / not Portalis / will answer very quickly, and will certainly enter into it — He has a fortune of more than 1 1/2 million Rthl in ready money and follows blindly his friend from youth and schoolfellow B[arbe]: as for how I live here in other respects, that is hand to mouth, I have nothing on my back, and often nothing in my belly — Already today I am 104 ducats in debt to B; [uncertain: Auf] he looks a little dark when I wish to borrow. What is to become of it I do not know, for if the King should once break up suddenly, what is then to become of me? I would and do act for you, most gracious Lord, on as an honest fellow. The matter is of the highest importance for you — peace, honour and happiness for you are at stake, this I am to win, and you let me all but perish in want and distress, indeed you let me go hungry — even B[arbe] already shakes his head — B[arbe] has friends in the Russian cabinet; these too will have to be courted in the end, and with an empty hand that is impossible — If only the 100 ducats could first be here, that I might satisfy B — but then I must absolutely, at the very soonest, have 200 ducats more, so that I may pay everything and at last do well by the good people for their faithful assistance — Concerning your letters sent to me, be pleased, most gracious Lord, to have tracers sent after them, so that I may receive the letters and the money —

Most respectfully I remain, until death,

Your Serene Highness's

My poor children, do not

forget them either —

J. Mother: they say she is to be removed.

Vienna, the 10th of January

1815.

most faithful and humble and obedient servant

von Triebenfeld
