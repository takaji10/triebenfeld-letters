Vienne le Mai 1815
A.S.A.S Msgr. le Prince
Frederic Louis de Hohenlohe
à Slawenzitz
L.E. Mons. le Baron de Stein m’a communiqué la lettre que Votre Altesse lui a écrite le 12 du en[?]ire passé.
Mon Prince,
Je connois la reclamation qu’elle forme sur les terres de Kaemen et de Trąbczyn, pour les faire valoir, le réglement définitif des affaires de la Pologne. Ce moment ne parait pas, éloigné, et je vous prie mon Prince d’être persuadé que je ferai alors tout ce qui dépendra de moi pour appuyer votre demande auprès du Roi, dont les principes de justice doivent vous inspirer une entière confiance.
Je prie Votre Altesse S., d’agréer l’expression de ma haute considération.
Vienne le Mai 1815

--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Entwurf einer französischen Antwort Hardenbergs an den Fürsten: Stein habe ihm dessen Schreiben vom 12. mitgeteilt. Der Fürst möge mit seiner Forderung auf Kamionna und Trąbczyn die endgültige Regelung der polnischen Angelegenheiten abwarten, die nicht fern scheine. Hardenberg werde dann alles tun, um das Gesuch beim König zu unterstützen.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Draft of a French reply from Hardenberg to the Prince: he says Stein has passed on to him the Prince's letter of the 12th. The Prince should wait, with his claim to Kamionna and Trąbczyn, for the final settlement of Polish affairs, which he says does not seem far off. Hardenberg says he will then do everything he can to support the request with the King.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Vienna, the    of May 1815

To His Serene Highness Monseigneur the Prince

Friedrich Ludwig of Hohenlohe

at Sławięcice

His Excellency Baron vom Stein has communicated to me the letter which Your Highness wrote to him on the 12th of [uncertain: en ire] last.

My Prince,

I am acquainted with the claim which you advance upon the estates of Kamionna and Trąbczyn, in order to press them, the definitive settlement of the affairs of Poland. That moment does not appear to be distant, and I beg you, my Prince, to be assured that I shall then do everything that lies within my power to support your request with the King, whose principles of justice must inspire in you an entire confidence.

I beg Your Serene Highness to accept the expression of my high consideration.

Vienna, the    of May 1815
