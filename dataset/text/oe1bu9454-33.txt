--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Rät dringend zur Vorsicht mit Generalvollmachten: Laut Ober Inspector Hecker hat Honrichs kraft seiner Vollmacht den Preis der zu verkaufenden Hufen von 600 auf 300 Rthl gesenkt. Schlägt vor, mit einem Kapital von 150/m Rthl Oppenheimer, Haugt, Ephraim und Eysenhardt abzufinden und der Invaliden Casse 50000 Rthl in Courant zu zahlen. Bei französischen Behörden erwirkte er einen Befehl betreffend die Reste in Südpreußen mit militärischer Assistenz.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
He urges great caution with general powers of attorney: according to Chief Inspector Hecker, Honrichs used his power of attorney to lower the price of the Hufen to be sold from 600 to 300 Rthl. He proposes using a capital of 150/m Rthl to settle with Oppenheimer, Haugt, Ephraim and Eysenhardt, and to pay the Invalids' Fund 50,000 Rthl in Courant. He says he obtained from the French authorities an order concerning the arrears in South Prussia, with military assistance.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord, Your Serene Highness's glorious letter of the 28th of last month I have just received, and I do not fail (although very [uncertain: ailing] and weak) to effect it.

The [uncertain: clerk] Kopey has not yet returned, and for this reason I had also laid aside the letter enclosed for Amelang — only thus have I never dispatched the same; for that I have Your Serene Highness to thank. There is a second brother which most highly wounds your credit and mine to the wall. I hope to obtain the money on my own plain face. Only I beg Your Serene Highness to demand the bank Acarnisung back from Eysenhardt at once; otherwise I should again be held to account by the bank, and then I should be totally ruined.

cash Serene Highness, and my business must now be to get the money together and to pay all these people — I had prospects for that, that I should pledge the mortgage of 250/m Rthl against it; but have Your Serene Highness it there, so much the better. — As soon as a capital of 150/m Rthl is procured, Your Serene Highness can buy 250/m in treasury notes for it, and if at once pay Oppenheimer, Haugt, Ephraim, Eysenhardt and God knows whom, and the Invalids' Fund still receives the 50,000 Rthl in Courant, for this one must be satisfied in ringing coin; she has not deceived you. The other rogues, however, it does them no harm, and supposing it so, Madame Schlabrendorff doubts it too; in this manner you would be rid of your whole debts for half the sum, and nobody can reproach you over it — it is dangerous. In everything I refer to my letter dispatched 3 days ago; there you will find the yield and the debts to be paid, so far as I know them. I refer Your Serene Highness once more to the table which I gave you 2 years ago

back; through me, or with my knowledge, the matter there has not altered in anything, unless it be that your newly created and manifold general attorneys have made a chancellery of it — Ah, God, most gracious Lord, do be cautious with the granting of general powers of attorney and blank forms — Do not scold me over this, and do not imagine that I wished here to play the guardian; the thought will remain forever foreign to my heart. Only let this be permitted, that I advise you as a friend and faithful adherent. But the Chief Inspector Hecker writes to me that Honrichs, on the ground of the power of attorney given him, has reduced the price for the Hufen to be sold from 600 to 300 Rthl. Is that not enough to drive one mad and frantic on the spot? Will you then by force give your property into the hands of scoundrels? O my most gracious Prince, that is not good — I have let it cost me blood and sweat to bring matters so far; I have staked rest, health and everything upon it, having had in the scoundrels' thoughts not a penny to gain thereby. It was merely the pride of having rescued you out of all your [uncertain: wretched] situations, and especially out of the claws of the Weihnrer. O, and now that is shattered again. I repeat it once more, that in quiet I accomplish 10 times more than the whole host of your general attorneys — already I have obtained from the French authorities the order that in South Prussia the arrears are to be collected for Your Serene Highness with military assistance — Something more, and far greater, I hope to effect from another quarter (for which God will soon give fortune and occasion). My wish is to be well again soon, so as to be able to get away from here. I wish this the more ardently as the Minister von Stein does not seem to be my great patron, and that [uncertain: persons] here are minded to vex me in your business (for otherwise they cannot get at me).

That peace of mind is wanting to you, my most gracious Prince and Lord, I readily believe. But this lot now befalls every honest man. Only S. rejoice — everything here on earth has indeed its beginning and its end. I know religion is not strange to you. Things have too often dazzling outsides; the ignorant man will throw out the brilliant among the heap [uncertain: which] nature makes only for a short time, thus casting it away; but one warming sunbeam annihilates their existence, and the brilliant remains. — Our misfortune is the want of unity, and the all-great goodness of heart of the King; the M. and the other hangers-on do what they please, they hate one another; in this alone are they united: to harm the honest man, to push him back, to make him suspect in the eyes of the world, to drive him out into the last corner of existence, and even though the state should go to ruin over it.

To speak with Your Serene Highness I too wish most ardently — much would soon become clear to you. If you could really obtain there at least 150/m Rthl only, I would come in haste with the original mortgage and the original mortgage certificates — would cede the mortgage, and the business would be settled quick as lightning without any dispute. But upon a mere chance I cannot travel, for the misfortune that has struck me here has consumed my hitherto [uncertain: peasant] store, and I am blood-poor — from my estates I have nothing — there my poor little worms have scarcely enough to live on, and I remain faithful to my resolve: in this cursed [uncertain: council] to take nothing from the King nor from anybody whatever, nor to beg for it. Be pleased also to write very soon on this account; the answer I will still await. If you can obtain the money there in that manner, then I am there quickly — and cede, as aforesaid, the mortgage of 250/m Rthl. The money will then be assigned here or procured hither; every [illegible] paid must give a receipt at once, and the new credit has a clean, secure and all[illegible] mortgage, and so much must remain over that the [uncertain: legal] costs are paid. — perhaps more too, if you receive more than 150/m Rthl, so that your treasury may also be propped up. Now for weakness I can go no further — but eternally at Your Serene Highness's the old Prince S — is to be as well as [illegible] wished to live the [illegible].

Berlin, the 7th of April 1808.

most faithful [uncertain: sustenance] [illegible]

and humble and obedient servant

v. Triebenfeld
