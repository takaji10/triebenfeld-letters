--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld schickt das königliche Schreiben auf die letzte Eingabe. Minister v Struensee musste die Akten dem König vorlegen. Obrist v. Köckritz, der eine Pro Memoria Triebenfelds dem König vorlegte, verspricht einen guten Ausgang. Darin bietet Triebenfeld an, falls Polajewo im Wege stehe, die Güter 30 Jahre zu pachten, jährlich 6 Bauerngüter zu bauen, wüste Gründe zu roden und 20000 Rthl Pacht zu zahlen. Wegen der 7000 Rthl will er die Niedernhallsche Sache beenden.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld sends the royal reply to the last petition. Minister v Struensee had to lay the files before the King. Colonel v. Köckritz, who presented a memorandum by Triebenfeld to the King, promises a good outcome. In it Triebenfeld offers, should Polajewo stand in the way, to lease the estates for 30 years, to build 6 peasant farms each year, to clear waste ground and to pay 20,000 Rthl in rent. Because of the 7,000 Rthl he wants to bring the Niedernhall matter to an end.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
93

Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord!

To Your Most Serene Highness I transmit herewith the All-Highest letter which has followed upon the last petition. I hope that it will have answered Your Highness's wishes. This very day the Minister von Struensee had to lay all the files before His Majesty; it now remains to be seen whether everything will be properly discussed.

The truly upright Colonel von K[öckritz] — who most respectfully commends himself to Your Serene Highness — promises the most desirable outcome of the matter.

Mr C[abinet] C[ouncillor] Beyme is indeed mightily preoccupied; however, he is too honest and open a man, and therefore I have nothing to fear on his side.

The honest councillor of war von Cöln has set the matter out quite well, as Your Serene Highness will gather from the enclosure.

I gave this letter (which remains merely a plan, inasmuch as I received it too late), together with the P[ro] M[emoria], to Colonel von Köck[ritz], and he has had the kindness to lay it before His Majesty today. In this Pro Memoria, however, I have made the offer, on my own part, that if Polajewo should stand in the way of the matter, I would take these estates in lease for 30 years — and at the same time build 6 peasant holdings every year out of my own means — clear all waste grounds at my own cost — pay 20,000 Rthl. rent yearly — and, at the expiry of the lease years, make good whatever of the yield of 20,000 Rthl should not be shown. This heavy battery, it is thought, would at one stroke dismantle all the friendly pieces, and since one already stood upon the enemy's flank, having pushed him away from the main April, the victory would infallibly remain with Your Serene Highness. For the present I mean only to work a few days on account of the 7,000 Rthl, so that the Niedernhall matter may be happily concluded.

The Minister has departed; he has received a snuff box

worth several thousand [uncertain: gulden], with a very fine letter enclosed.

For the rest, neither Your Serene Highness nor I owe him any thanks — the good man — has done nothing for anyone but — yet let Your Serene Highness have the grace to meet him handsomely, to believe everything — but tell him straight out that you see that people mean to vex you and secretly [illegible] you — that you would no longer endure this, but from now on would say straight out what you thought, and would open the King's eyes to his face. I have suffered much these days; my openness and straightforwardness [illegible] many a villain does shrink from it after all — Colonel von Köckritz is a worthy, upright man; he alone has disposed the truly good King in Your Serene Highness's favour — and has explained matters to the honest Mr Cabinet Councillor B[eyme] more uprightly than all the others —

In deepest [uncertain: esteem] I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Berlin, 18th December 1798.

faithful and most humble and obedient [uncertain: servant]

v Triebenfeld
