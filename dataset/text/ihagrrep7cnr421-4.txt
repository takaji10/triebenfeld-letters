--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Der Kriminalrat Mondro als Bevollmächtigter Chełmskis an den König, Kalisch, 7. Dezember 1799: Er bittet um Vorbescheidung auf Chełmskis Beschwerde gegen die Regierung zu Kalisch wegen der Abweisung seiner Klage gegen den Fiskus. Chełmski habe die Beschwerde, betreffend eine Forderung von 12.000 Dukaten, am 9. Oktober eingereicht und keine Vorbescheidung erhalten. Die Dekrete der Regierung verwechselten die Klage, die Chełmski anfangs gegen den Fürsten von Hohenlohe als nunmehrigen Eigentümer der Prusimski eingezogenen Güter Trąmpczyn angestellt hatte, mit der jetzigen, die allein gegen den Fiskus gerichtet sei; dass die Forderung nicht gegen den Fürsten eingeklagt werden könne, sehe Chełmski selbst, doch hindere ihn das Hofreskript vom 17. September 1796 nicht, das Kapital nach der Kabinettsordre vom 16. desselben Monats vom Fiskus zu fordern, und selbst das Reskript verlange Instruktion und förmliches Erkenntnis, wenn eine Partei sich bei der Abweisung durch Dekret nicht beruhige. Er legt eine Abschrift der Klage mit dem Schuldschein bei, die Chełmski bei der Posener Regierung angebracht hatte, zuerst gegen Prusimski, ehe das Konfiskationserkenntnis erging; bei der Wiederaufnahme gegen den Fiskus sei nur die Person geändert worden. Er bittet um schleunige Vorbescheidung. Unterzeichnet Mondro, Kriminalrat. Am Rand eine Notiz, die mit „Anteriora“ beginnt (der Rest nicht gelesen). Dazu der Zettel der Behörde vom 23. Dezember 1799, in der Akte später eingebunden: Eine Abschrift der seinem Prinzipal erteilten Resolution vom 21. Oktober sei mitzuteilen (der Titel des Empfängers unsicher gelesen); mit einem nicht gelesenen Namen gezeichnet.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
The criminal councillor Mondro, as Chełmski's attorney, to the King, Kalisz, 7 December 1799: he asks for a preliminary ruling on Chełmski's complaint against the Government at Kalisz over the dismissal of his suit against the Fiscus. Chełmski had submitted the complaint, concerning a claim of 12,000 ducats, on 9 October and had had no preliminary ruling. The Government's decrees, Mondro writes, confuse the suit Chełmski first brought against the Prince of Hohenlohe, as present owner of the Trąbczyn estates confiscated from Prusimski, with the present one, which is directed against the Fiscus alone; Chełmski accepts that the claim cannot be sued for against the Prince, but the court rescript of 17 September 1796 does not prevent him from claiming the capital from the Fiscus under the cabinet order of the 16th of that month, and the rescript itself requires the taking of evidence and a formal judgment where a party does not rest content with a dismissal by decree. He encloses a copy of the suit with the bond, which Chełmski had brought before the Government at Poznań, first against Prusimski, before the judgment of confiscation was passed; when it was resumed against the Fiscus only the person was changed. He asks for a ruling without delay. Signed Mondro, criminal councillor. A note in the margin begins "Anteriora" (the rest not read). With it, the office's slip of 23 December 1799, bound later in the file: a copy of the resolution of 21 October given to his principal is to be communicated (the addressee's title is doubtfully read); signed with a name not read.

[r?]. [1?]5 Dec. 99. Most Serene, Most Mighty, Most Gracious King and Lord! Kalisz, 7 December 1799.

The criminal-court councillor Mondro asks, in the name of v Chełmski of Łukom, for a preliminary ruling on the complaint against the Government at Kalisz in his matter ./.

the Fiscus, over the dismissal of the suit.

Anteriora M[?]. with the same [?] Krb[?] 19. de[?]

As early as 9 October this year my client, the landowner von Chełmski, submitted to Your Royal Majesty a complaint against the Government here over the improper dismissal[?] of a suit brought against the Fiscus concerning a claim of

12,000 ducats,

and most humbly asked for a hearing at law. As no preliminary ruling has followed yet, I make bold to petition Your Most High Majesty again on this matter. The decrees of the Government plainly rest on a misunderstanding, in that they confuse the suit that von Chełmski first brought against the Prince of Hohenlohe, as present owner of the Trąbczyn estates confiscated from Prusimski, with the one that is directed against the Fiscus alone.

That the claim of 12,000 ducats cannot be sued for against the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, my client understands of himself. On the other hand, the Court Rescript of 17 September 1796 in no way prevents him from claiming the said capital, under the clear provision of the Most High[?] Cabinet Order of the 16th of the same month and year,

from the Fiscus.

If, moreover, the Government follows the letter of the Court Rescript, which is not binding in itself, it nonetheless cannot refuse von Chełmski a hearing at law in this matter, since this very Rescript expressly lays down that if the party does not rest content with a dismissal by decree, the taking of evidence is to be opened and the matter formally adjudged.

This, in my opinion, must happen in every legal matter where the party cannot be convinced that the dismissal was lawful. I enclose a copy of the suit with the bond ex quo, which my client had already brought before the Government at Poznań; only I must remark that he had first sued Prusimski before the judgment of confiscation

had been passed.

On the resumption of the proceedings against the Fiscus nothing was changed but the person. So that the matter may not be delayed any longer and the plaintiff may at last be satisfied, I most humbly ask Your Royal Majesty

to have a preliminary ruling given him without delay. I who remain, until death, in deepest reverence Your Royal Majesty's most humble Mondro
