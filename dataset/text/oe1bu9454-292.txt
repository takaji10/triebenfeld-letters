--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Meldet, die Entschädigungssache liege dem Kayser vor; Minister v. Stein habe Hilfe versprochen, Hardenberg werde schriftlich zusagen. Im Streit mit Cosmar verteidige Stägemann diesen; Triebenfeld verlangte vor dem Fürst Kanzler die Hinterlegung der Summen aus der Urkunde von 1799 und dem Transact von 1811 beim Oberlandesgericht Brzeg. Fürst August sollen per Cabinets Ordre 100000 Rthl sofort gezahlt werden. Cosmar soll wegen unterschlagener Familiengelder denunziert sein. Schlägt vor, den Fürst Kanzler in Nysa zu treffen.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
He reports that the compensation case is before the Emperor; he says Minister v. Stein has promised help and that Hardenberg will give his assurance in writing. In the dispute with Cosmar, he says Stägemann is defending him; before the Prince Chancellor, Triebenfeld demanded that the sums from the deed of 1799 and the settlement of 1811 be deposited with the High Court of Appeal at Brzeg. He says Prince August is to be paid 100,000 Rthl at once by cabinet order, and that Cosmar has reportedly been denounced for embezzling family money. He proposes meeting the Prince Chancellor in Nysa.

Most Serene Prince!

etc.

The compensation matter is recognised and lies before the Emperor. From the Russian side His Excellency the Minister von Stein has promised to do everything possible. From the Prince of Hardenberg I shall also receive the assurance in writing. In the Courland matter little more will happen; the result is that the King and the Prince Chancellor have promised to pay the S. ladies, and then one shall indeed see what the gentlemen will do — every thinking man says that Your Serene Highness must now come out of the obligation (ex nexu) — Mr Stägemann acts as man of business for the Prince Pingatilli — It is enough to drive one mad — The order concerning the deposit of the monies on Cosmar's part has been sent to the Minister of Justice with the reminder to hold him to order — Since in the meantime, however, complaints have come in from Kuh, from Sobottendorff, from Hahn, Mr Stägemann has

proposed to the Chancellor not [uncertain: Wesben nebst zu stürben], but to refer everything away from himself and to the Minister of Justice. Mr Stägemann now further plays the defender of Cosmar and snorts fury and vengeance that anyone should wish to make so ill of the upright man.

In a short conference held 3 days ago he flew up in a rage. Your Serene Highness can concern yourself no further with the matter, it all belongs before the Minister of Justice — the querulous complaining of the old Prince bears the stamp of untruth — the old Prince is a nullity in the matter, he has nothing to seek in the estates, he has been settled with — Prince August is here and he knows Cosmar as an honourable man, he has himself named him as his 9th authorised representative. Mr von Triebenfeld acts most indiscreetly in [illegible] running to His Serene Highness in this way. I replied that I was far from wishing to be burdensome to His Serene Highness. I demand for myself that my princely client be protected from starvation

[continues]

death, together with those about him; both the Prince and I have only pleaded, and that with the greatest right, that Cosmar should deposit at the Brzeg High Court of the Province the sums fixed in the deed of 1799 and in the settlement of 1811, in which matter that court has admonished him so many times; this is never done on Cosmar's part. He always came out defiantly, and the creditors with whom the Prince had come to agreement [uncertain: theirs] and implored the Prince for what was theirs — the sums mounted up from year to year, Cosmar was not solvent for them, and the question arose who was one day to make good these sums.

The Prince Chancellor was moved and said, the old Prince must after all live, and where then do the revenues go?

Stägemann wept that this would come to light readily enough, if the matter were closely investigated. Certainly the Prince would have received everything, and Cosmar too would doubtless have had to pay much to the creditors — after several turns of phrase the matter was broken off — and I still await the first line of an answer.

By more recent letters I hear that Cosmar is really said to have been denounced by a man from abroad for embezzling family monies, still from Courland — and that this matter is now to be investigated first in Berlin. On Prince Adolph's affair the matter is said to have great influence — Stägemann lately wished to defend him here too.

Prince August's matter is now so firmly concluded by Cabinet Order that the 100/m thaler are to be paid him absolutely and at once — Cosmar wished at the last to save himself with the excuse that the old Prince owed the Landgrave of Hesse a sum of 300/m Rthl. and that the Sławięcice estates stood liable in subsidium; but the privy councillor Philippsborn reported to the King and moved that the King must surely value his subjects sooner than

foreigners, and I proved that the debt attaches solely to the principalities of Öhringen, Neuenstein and Ingelfingen, and thereupon the King commanded the Chancellor that the monies should be paid; the matter nevertheless lay another 14 days, and about 6 days ago a fulminating letter went out to Minister Kircheisen — that these 100,000 Rthl. should be paid at once and without any contradiction.

Ernst August has thanked the Prince of Hardenberg handsomely and at the same time expressed the wish that Your Serene Highness might no longer be so continually pressed by Cosmar. I believe he will now, since Hahn is coming there, quickly withdraw the power of attorney — make common cause with Your Serene Highness, and then and only then is Cosmar's grave prepared.

The Prince Chancellor will probably not depart from here before the 5th of June — He goes by way of Nysa? How would it be if Your Serene Highness were to approach him there in person? It is necessary to speak with him about your military matter — I would hasten ahead with courier horses — The cook Murat has received his wages — vengeance has swiftly overtaken this gentleman.

I remain, in deepest reverence,

Your Serene Highness's

The King departed last night from the Countess Zichy.

Vienna, the 27th of May.

1815

most faithful and obedient servant

von Triebenfeld
