--- SUMMARY (German) ---
v. Triebenfeld verbürgt sich, die Sache des Fürsten werde in ein paar Monaten ehrenvoll und vorteilhaft beendet. Man wolle eine Taxe der fürstlichen Güter verhindern und alles als Gnadensache erscheinen lassen. Über den Tausch entscheide das Gleichgewicht der Güter. Graf Schlabrendorff habe er überredet, für die Zinsen einen Wechsel auf ein Jahr zu nehmen. Etwa am 16. April reist er bis Ende Mai nach Berlin. Die an Prinz George verkauften Güter seien nun über 700/m Rthl taxiert.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
v. Triebenfeld guarantees that the Prince's affair will be brought to an honourable and advantageous end within a couple of months. He says the intention is to prevent a valuation of the princely estates and to make everything appear as a matter of grace. The exchange, he says, will be decided by the balance between the estates. He says he has persuaded Count Schlabrendorff to accept a bill of exchange for one year for the interest. Around 16 April he will travel to Berlin until the end of May. The estates sold to Prince George, he says, are now valued at over 700/m Rthl.

Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord!

Gladly do I endure all the backbiting of fools, if only I may see Your Most Serene Highness's affair brought to the wished-for end, and that this will happen within a couple of months I pledge to Your Serene Highness upon my honour and my property. That [uncertain: grass speaker] will meanwhile do the least about it, for I believe and regard the matter as settled. A man quite insignificant and unknown will decide the matter. This I cannot entrust to paper — Meanwhile I have had the grace to convince Her Serene Highness the Princess of the truth. The whole affair will resolve itself and will be brought to a conclusion for Your Most Serene Highness as honourably as it will be advantageous — that there is a wish to prevent a valuation of Your Serene Highness's estates arises from the source most

known to Your Highness, and from that very quarter it is wished that it should be called a mere matter of favour — My sense of honour, however, always rebels against everything that carries so much as the appearance of begging, and with this in view I go on quite quietly and [uncertain: Bider] along the straight road marked out for me, and I am certain that, for all the idle chatter, I shall reach the goal of my wishes — The Monarch means well by Your Most Serene Highness, and the exchange has been [uncertain: approved] by the King, provided the estates hold the balance one against the other — the balance, then, decides the matter — and the King's friendship does only this much in the business, that for estates lying far off nearer ones are given — and to thank the Monarch for that is no disgrace — For the equality of the estates provision has however been made — and in this manner everything will resolve itself. Count Schlabrendorff I have persuaded so far that he takes from me, for the interest payable to him, a bill of exchange payable a year hence. I go to him on Sunday to settle this business.

Everything else I shall attend to in the best manner. For the rest, I humbly note further that Prittwitz

is a pitiful liar — the man said no further word to me, and that in the presence of the said [uncertain: Wildeganstes], than: "My God, Your Serene Highness torments me, I am to take the matter up — I have already told the gentleman that I cannot do this — and I shall write it to Your Serene Highness once more —" Since I now knew everything better, I let the fool talk and sought to advance the matter for myself. My sense of honour would never permit speeches of this kind either, and the discourse could not have ended so humiliatingly on my side as [uncertain: b.] would boast.
