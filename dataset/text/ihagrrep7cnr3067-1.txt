Nach den Gesetzen würden von den Urhebern und Theilnehmern der Süd-Preußi¬
schen Rebellion viele den Tod, die übrigen aber Confiscation und lebens wie¬
rige Festungs-Strafe verwürkt haben.
Allein diese gesetzliche Strenge ist durch Specielle Allerhöchste Verfügungen aus¬
nehmend gemildert worden.
Mehrere Pardonbriefe, und wiederholte Amnestie Patente, besonders die, vom
24ten Septbr. und 10ten December 1794, haben der Gerechtigkeit den größten Theil
ihrer Opfer gantz entzogen.
Vornehmlich aber hat die Immediat-Instruction vom 3ten April 1795 fest¬
gesetzt:
1, daß nur 6 oder 8 der Haupträdels führer nach der Strenge der Gesetze,

--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Die Promemoria des Großkanzlers Goldbeck, Berlin, 16. Februar 1797, an König Friedrich Wilhelm II. über den Ausgang der Verfahren gegen die Teilnehmer der südpreußischen Rebellion und darüber, die Gnade des Königs auf sie auszudehnen. Nach den Gesetzen hätten viele den Tod, die übrigen die Konfiskation und lebenslange Festungsstrafe verwirkt; doch Pardonbriefe, die Amnestiepatente vom 24. September und 10. Dezember 1794 und die Immediat-Instruktion vom 3. April 1795 hatten dies gemildert: Nur 6 oder 8 Haupträdelsführer sollten nach der Strenge der Gesetze, aber nicht mit dem Tode bestraft werden, die übrigen Geldbußen zahlen, die eine Finanzkommission in Posen festsetzte, und nur wer sich nicht unterwarf oder nicht zahlen konnte, sollte zu zeitiger Festungsstrafe verurteilt werden. Goldbeck vermerkt, dass die Justiz an diesen Verordnungen keinen Anteil hatte und sie nur zur Befolgung erhielt. Die Haupträdelsführer, Niemoiewski, Wybicki, Xaver Dombrowski, Mniewski, Prudzinski (Antoni Prusimski, der Besitzer von Trąbczyn), Krupecki, Jetzierski (Xaver Jezierski, der Domherr von Włocławek), Rokittnicki und andere, waren geflohen, so dass nur ihr zurückgelassenes Vermögen eingezogen werden konnte; andere Beförderer der Rebellion hatten sich mit der Kommission durch Geldbußen abgefunden. Von 387 Insurgenten hatten 307 nur Geldbußen gezahlt, 49 waren zu Festung bis zu 3 Jahren verurteilt worden, 26 zu langer, 2 zu lebenslanger Festungsstrafe und 3 zum Tode, von denen einer unter dem Galgen begnadigt wurde und einer sich durch die Flucht rettete. Sollte der König seine Gnade ausdehnen wollen, so rät Goldbeck: die Konfiskation gegen die außer Landes gegangenen Rebellen bestehen zu lassen, da über die eingezogenen Güter größtenteils schon verfügt sei; die Geldstrafen, auch rückständige, dennoch einzuziehen, weil die notwendigen Ausgaben der Provinz damit rechneten; den kurzzeitig Verurteilten, die ihre Strafe größtenteils schon verbüßt hatten, könne die übrige Haftzeit erlassen werden, soweit sie noch einsäßen; den auf der beiliegenden Liste zu langer Zeit Verurteilten etwa die Hälfte zu erlassen, ausgenommen Pratzmowski (Nr. 7), Ryczewski (Nr. 12) und Maruczewski (Nr. 13), die ihre Strafe hauptsächlich als Straßenräuber verwirkt hatten; und von den lebenslang Verurteilten Nowicki (Nr. 2), dem die Todesstrafe schon erlassen war, und Sobolewski (Nr. 3), hauptsächlich wegen Mordtaten und Straßenräubereien verurteilt, bei der Strafe zu belassen, während Stockowski (Nr. 1), der mehr aus Einfalt und Eitelkeit als aus bösem Vorsatz gesündigt zu haben schien, vielleicht bis auf 10 Jahre begnadigt werden könne, mit einer Pension oder Aussteuer aus den konfiszierten Gütern für seine unmündige Tochter, die kein eigenes Vermögen hatte. Einige noch schwebende Untersuchungen gegen Insurgenten, die sich erst kürzlich auf die Ediktalzitationen gemeldet hatten, seien nach den bisherigen Vorschriften zu entscheiden, die neuen Grundsätze aber bei Vorlage der Konfirmationsreskripte zu berücksichtigen. Stimme der König zu, so seien ein Publicandum über die Amnestie zu den Punkten 3 und 4 und die Ordres an den Etatsminister Grafen von Hoym und an den Großkanzler zur Vollziehung vorzulegen. Unterzeichnet Goldbeck. Darunter schrieb König Friedrich Wilhelm II. eigenhändig seine Entscheidung, datiert vom 16. Februar 1797 und unterzeichnet Fr. Wilhelm: Er findet keinen der Verurteilten einer Minderung der Strafe würdig; die zu Geldstrafen Verurteilten seien vielmehr zu deren Zahlung anzuhalten und, wer nicht zahlen kann, in verhältnismäßigen Arrest zu bringen. Mehrere Wörter der Hand des Königs sind unsichere Lesungen. Es folgt die Liste, die der Promemoria beilag: die südpreußischen Rebellen, gegen die auf Todesstrafe, lebenslange oder langjährige Festungsstrafe erkannt worden war, jeder mit seinem Verbrechen und, bei den langen Strafen, der Dauer der Strafe und dem Monat ihres Beginns. Zum Tode verurteilt: Franz von Wittwicki und Andreas Retznowski, die beim Überfall der Stadt Włocławek die Bedienten der Domherren gegen das Militär anführten und einen Offizier und einen Akzisebeamten ermordeten. Lebenslang: Joseph von Stockowski, General der Insurgenten von Wieluń, der sie gegen die königlichen Truppen anführte, Magazine beraubte und Kassen plünderte; Joseph Nowicki, wegen seiner Beteiligung an der Wegnahme des Artillerietransports in Włocławek zum Galgen verurteilt und vom König begnadigt; und Joseph Sobolewski, der sich nach der Auflösung der Insurrektion einer Räuberbande anschloss und bei Mordtaten an Soldaten und Reisenden half. Zu langer Festung 26 Männer, für 4 bis 15 Jahre, die Strafen beginnend zwischen Februar 1795 und November 1796: neun davon wegen vorzüglich tätiger Teilnahme an der Insurrektion; darunter Johann Thiele, Sekretär bei der Ordnungs- und Kriegskommission der Insurgenten; der Propst von Chicomski und Cajetan von Smolinski wegen Missbrauchs ihres geistlichen Amtes; Lorenz von Masowiecki und Dominic v. Dziewanowski, weil sie dem Überfall der Insurgenten auf die Stadt Rypin beigewohnt hatten; Komornick von Wardenski, der Vorwissenschaft von der Insurrektion sehr verdächtig; Felix von Krettkowski, der, obwohl nicht ganz freiwillig, General der Insurgenten von Łęczyca geworden war; Paul von Skorzewski, General der Insurgenten von Kalisz; Samuel Czimay und Szekus Ruba wegen tätiger Teilnahme am Überfall der Besatzung zu Włocławek; Friedrich Jacob v. Psarski, Präsident der Konföderation von Wieluń und Ostrzewo; und Constantin v. Mielewski, auch wegen des Verdachts der Ermordung eines Juden. Die längsten Strafen, 15 Jahre, haben Casimir von Pratzmowski, auch wegen Straßenraubs, und von Ryczewski, der sich einer Räuberbande angeschlossen hatte.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
The promemoria of the Grand Chancellor Goldbeck, Berlin, 16 February 1797, to King Friedrich Wilhelm II, on what had become of the participants in the South Prussian rebellion and on extending the King's mercy to them. By the laws many would have incurred death and the rest confiscation and fortress imprisonment for life; but letters of pardon, the amnesty patents of 24 September and 10 December 1794 and the Immediate Instruction of 3 April 1795 had softened this: only 6 or 8 chief ringleaders were to be punished with the rigour of the laws, though not with death, the rest to pay fines assessed by a finance commission in Poznań, and only those who would not submit or could not pay were to be sentenced to the fortress for a time. Goldbeck notes that the justice had no share in these ordinances and was only sent them to follow. The chief ringleaders, Niemoiewski, Wybicki, Xaver Dombrowski, Mniewski, Prudzinski (Antoni Prusimski, the owner of Trąbczyn), Krupecki, Jetzierski (Xaver Jezierski, the canon of Włocławek), Rokittnicki and others, had fled, so that only the property they left behind could be confiscated; other promoters of the rebellion had settled with the commission by paying fines. Of 387 insurgents, 307 paid fines only, 49 were sentenced to the fortress for up to 3 years, 26 for long terms, 2 for life and 3 to death, of whom one was pardoned under the gallows and one escaped by flight. Should the King wish to extend his grace, Goldbeck advises that the confiscation imposed on the rebels who had left the country stand, since the confiscated estates had for the most part already been disposed of; that the fines still be collected, even those in arrears, because the province's necessary expenses counted on them; that those sentenced to short terms, most of whom had already served them, could be remitted the rest if still held; that those on the enclosed list sentenced to long terms be remitted about half, except Pratzmowski (no. 7), Ryczewski (no. 12) and Maruczewski (no. 13), who had incurred their sentences chiefly as highway robbers; and that of those sentenced for life Nowicki (no. 2), already spared the death sentence, and Sobolewski (no. 3), condemned chiefly for murders and highway robberies, stay as sentenced, while Stockowski (no. 1), who seemed to have sinned from simplicity and vanity rather than evil intent, might be pardoned down to 10 years, with a pension or dowry out of the confiscated estates for his under-age daughter, who had no property of her own. A few investigations still pending against insurgents who had lately answered the public summonses are to be judged under the old rules, the new principles to be considered when the rescripts of confirmation are submitted. If the King agrees, a public notice of the amnesty under points 3 and 4 and the orders to the Minister of State Count von Hoym and to the Grand Chancellor are to be laid before him for signature. Signed Goldbeck. Beneath it King Friedrich Wilhelm II wrote his decision in his own hand, dated 16 February 1797 and signed Fr. Wilhelm: he finds none of the condemned worthy of a reduction of their punishment; those condemned to money fines are rather to be held to pay them and, those who cannot pay, to be put under arrest in proportion. Several words of the King's hand are doubtful readings. The list the promemoria encloses follows: the South Prussian rebels against whom sentence of death, of fortress imprisonment for life or of long fortress imprisonment had been pronounced, each with his offence and, for the long terms, the length of the sentence and the month it began. Sentenced to death: Franz von Wittwicki and Andreas Retznowski, who at the attack on the town of Włocławek led the canons' servants against the military and murdered an officer and an excise official. For life: Joseph von Stockowski, general of the insurgents of Wieluń, who led them against the royal troops, robbed magazines and plundered treasuries; Joseph Nowicki, sentenced to the gallows for his part in seizing the artillery transport at Włocławek and pardoned by the King; and Joseph Sobolewski, who after the insurrection was dissolved joined a band of robbers and helped in murders of soldiers and travellers. For long terms, 26 men, for 4 to 15 years, the sentences beginning between February 1795 and November 1796: nine of them for especially active participation in the insurrection; among them Johann Thiele, secretary at the insurgents' commission of order and war; the provost von Chicomski and Cajetan von Smolinski, for abusing their spiritual office; Lorenz von Masowiecki and Dominic v. Dziewanowski, for having been present at the insurgents' attack on the town of Rypin; Komornick von Wardenski, strongly suspected of having known of the insurrection beforehand; Felix von Krettkowski, who had become general of the insurgents of Łęczyca, though not entirely of his own free will; Paul von Skorzewski, general of the insurgents of Kalisz; Samuel Czimay and Szekus Ruba, for taking an active part in the attack on the garrison at Włocławek; Friedrich Jacob v. Psarski, president of the confederation of Wieluń and Ostrzewo; and Constantin v. Mielewski, also on suspicion of the murder of a Jew. The longest terms, 15 years, are those of Casimir von Pratzmowski, also for highway robbery, and von Ryczewski, who had joined a band of robbers.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
16 Feb. 1797

Promemoria

By the laws, many of the authors of and participants in the South Prussian rebellion would have incurred death, and the rest confiscation and lifelong imprisonment in a fortress.

But this legal severity has been exceptionally mitigated by special most-high dispositions.

Several letters of pardon, and repeated amnesty patents, especially those of 24 September and 10 December 1794, have withdrawn the greatest part of its victims from justice altogether.

Above all, however, the Immediate Instruction of 3 April 1795 laid down:

1, that only 6 or 8 of the chief ringleaders were to be punished with the rigour of the laws, but even these not with death;

2, that the rest were to be subjected only to certain fines, to be assessed by a finance commission in Poznań; and

3, that only against those who would not submit to the decrees of the commission, or could not raise the fines imposed, were temporary sentences of fortress imprisonment to be pronounced.

The justice had no share at all in any of these ordinances; they were merely sent to it to be complied with.

The chief ringleaders, Niemoiewski, Wybicki, Xaver Dombrowski, Mniewski, Prudzinski [Prusimski], Krupecki, Jetzierski [Jezierski], Rokittnicki etc., have saved themselves by flight. One has therefore had to be content with confiscating the property they left behind, and no example of punishment could be made on their persons.

Other chief promoters of the rebellion hastened to settle with the finance commission at Poznań by paying fines, and thereby to free themselves from all further claims of the justice.

Hence it has come about that of 387 insurgents who have been freed

307 merely paid fines,

49 were sentenced to shorter fortress imprisonment, not exceeding 3 years,

26 to long,

2 to lifelong fortress imprisonment, and only 3 to death; of whom, however, one received a pardon under the gallows, and a second saved himself by flight. = 387.

Should His Royal Majesty be inclined to extend His most high grace still further towards these leniently punished participants in the insurrection, this could nevertheless be advised only in the following measure.

1, the confiscation imposed on the rebels who have left the country should simply be allowed to stand, since in particular over the

confiscated estates disposition has for the most part already been made.

2, the fines imposed would nevertheless have to be collected, even if they were still in arrears, because these monies are already counted on for various necessary expenses of the province.

3, The insurgents condemned to the fortress for only a shorter time have for the most part already served their sentence. Those who are still sitting could be remitted the rest of their time of arrest entirely.

4, those who are condemned for a longer time, according to the enclosed list, might be remitted about half of the sentence pronounced.

But von Pratzmowski, sub num. 7, von Ryczewski, sub num. 12, and von Maruczewski, sub num. 13, would have to be excepted from this, because they incurred the sentence pronounced not merely as insurgents but chiefly as highway robbers.

5, As regards the insurgents condemned for life according to this same list, the sentence pronounced should simply be left as it stands in the case of Nowicki, sub num. 2, because the death sentence already pronounced on him has been remitted, and in the case of Sobolewski, sub num. 3, because he has been condemned chiefly as a participant in various murders and highway robberies. On the other hand, in the case of von Stockowski, sub num. 1, who seems to have sinned more from simplicity and vanity than from evil intent, a pardon down to 10 years might perhaps take place, and the settlement of a pension or dowry out of the confiscated estates for his daughter, who is under age and stripped of all property of her own.

A few investigations are still pending against insurgents who have only recently presented themselves in answer to the public summonses issued.

Against these, judgment would have to be given under the rules hitherto in force, but when the rescripts of confirmation are submitted

regard would be had to the principles now laid down.

Should His Royal Majesty most graciously deign to accept the above proposals, then

1, a public notice concerning the amnesty granted under 3 and 4,

2, the necessary order to the Minister of State Count von Hoym and to the Grand Chancellor would have to be laid before His Majesty for most high signature.

Berlin, 16 February 1797.

Goldbeck
