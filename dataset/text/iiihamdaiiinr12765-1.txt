Die Ansprüche des Herrn Fürsten von Hohenlohe auf die ihm im Jahre 1796, von des Hochseeligen
Königes Majestät geschenkten Sudpreußischen Güter, Trąbczyn und Kaemen werden nur erst

--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld erinnert Hardenberg daran, dass er ihm am 6. Mai in Paris ein Schreiben des Fürsten Hohenlohe an den russischen Kaiser übergeben hat. Er legt die darauf erteilte Resolution vom 14. Mai in Abschrift bei und bittet, die für den Fürsten wichtige Sache im jetzt entscheidenden Zeitpunkt wieder in Erinnerung bringen zu dürfen. Die Kanzlei vermerkt, das Gesuch sei am 20. November 1814 an Staatsrat Stägemann abgegeben worden. Unter dem Brief steht der Entwurf der Antwort vom 25. November 1814: Die Angelegenheit des Fürsten Hohenlohe wegen der Ansprüche auf Trąbczyn und Kamionna werde sich von selbst ausgleichen, sobald die allgemeine Auseinandersetzung und Übereinkunft wegen des Herzogtums Warschau zur Ausführung komme. Die beigelegte Abschrift ist Hardenbergs Schreiben an Triebenfeld vom 14. Mai 1814: Die Ansprüche des Fürsten von Hohenlohe auf die ihm 1796 geschenkten südpreußischen Güter Trąbczyn und Kamionna könnten erst mit Aussicht auf Erfolg angeregt werden, wenn über die Warschauer Angelegenheiten definitive Bestimmungen erfolgt sind. Das sei noch nicht der Fall. Hardenberg hält deshalb das Schreiben des Fürsten an Kaiser Alexander zurück und bittet, dies dem Fürsten zu eröffnen.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld reminds Hardenberg that on 6 May in Paris he handed him a letter from the Prince of Hohenlohe to the Russian Emperor. He encloses a Copy of the resolution issued on it on 14 May and asks to be allowed to bring the matter, which is important to the Prince, back to mind at what is now the decisive moment. The chancery notes that the petition was handed over to Councillor of State Stägemann on 20 November 1814. Beneath the letter stands the draft of the reply of 25 November 1814: the matter of the Prince of Hohenlohe concerning the claims to Trąbczyn and Kamionna will, it is said, settle itself as soon as the general settlement and agreement regarding the Duchy of Warsaw is carried out. The enclosed copy is Hardenberg's letter to Triebenfeld of 14 May 1814: he says the claims of the Prince of Hohenlohe to the South Prussian estates of Trąbczyn and Kamionna, given to him in 1796, could only be raised with any prospect of success once definitive arrangements have been made about the Warsaw affairs. That, he says, is not yet the case. Hardenberg is therefore holding back the Prince's letter to Emperor Alexander and asks that this be made known to the Prince.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince, Most gracious Prince and Lord! On the 6th of May of this year, in Paris, I presented to Your Most Serene Highness a letter from the Prince of Hohenlohe to His Majesty the Emperor of Russia. The resolution most graciously granted me thereupon on the 14th of May I enclose here in copy, and beg most obediently for most gracious indulgence that I venture to bring this matter, so important to the Prince, once more to Your Most Serene Highness's recollection at the present so decisive moment, and to implore Your Most Serene Highness's assistance. In boundless reverence I remain, until death, Your Most Serene Highness's most faithful and obedient servant

von Triebenfeld

Vienna, the 25th of October 1814.

the Councillor of War and Forests von Triebenfeld, Your Right Honourable, here. In reply to your petition of the 25th of last month I inform you that the affair of the Prince of Hohenlohe with respect to the claims upon Trąbczyn and Kamionna will settle itself, as soon as the general settlement and agreement concerning the Duchy of Warsaw, now in [uncertain: word / progress], comes to be carried out. W., Nov. 14.

Stägemann.

Hardenberg.

Copy. The claims of the Prince of Hohenlohe to the South Prussian estates of Trąbczyn and Kamionna, bestowed upon him in the year 1796 by His Majesty the late King of blessed memory, will only be capable of being brought forward with any presumptive prospect of success once definitive determinations have been reached concerning the Warsaw affairs generally. At present this is not yet the case, and I have therefore also thought it best to hold back, until that point in time arrives, the Prince's letter to His Majesty the Emperor Alexander which Your Right Honourable delivered to me with your communication of the 6th of this month; and I request you to disclose this to him. Paris, the 14th of May 1814.

Hardenberg

To

the Royal Councillor of War

Mr von Triebenfeld

Your Honour
