--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Ein neuer Befehl des General Finanz Departements verbietet den Kammern Revisionen gerichtlicher Taxen ohne dessen Vorwissen. Triebenfeld führt ihn auf Prinz George und Krieges Rath Korn zurück. Er hofft auf die Erlaubnis, die Präsident v Goldbeck beantragt hat und die Amelang in Berlin betreiben soll. Er will die Dismembrationspläne einreichen. Der Fürst soll für Goldbeck drei Büsten und ein Lavoir kaufen und schicken. Die Olesnicer Sache ist durch Hussarzewski an das Patrimonialgericht zurückgegangen.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
A new order from the General Finance Department forbids the chambers to carry out revisions of court valuations without its prior knowledge. Triebenfeld traces it back to Prince George and Councillor of War Korn. He hopes for the permission that President v Goldbeck has applied for and that Amelang is to pursue in Berlin. He intends to submit the dismemberment plans. The Prince is asked to buy and send three busts and a washbasin for Goldbeck. The Oleśnica matter has gone back to the patrimonial court through Hussarzewski.

Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord!

An order of the General Finance Department, issued a fortnight ago to all the Chambers here, hinders the course of my business extraordinarily, and it may be long before I reach my goal with the revision of the valuations, if indeed it is permitted at all. The cause of these new difficulties is Prince George together with the councillor of war Korn here. The former, some time ago, petitioned the Kalisz Chamber for Korn as reviser of his valuations of the estates situated in this, the Poznań, and other departments; and the Chamber granted this. But since, unhappily, the judicial valuations which were to be revised had been drawn up by Korn himself, and were contested in the [uncertain: Abord], this had to be laid before the head of the Department; and, indignant at this swindling — that a member of the Chamber should revise the valuations which he himself had made, and against which so many objections are raised, and make the Chamber answerable for them — the Minister issued the order

that no Chamber should any longer be permitted, without the prior knowledge of the General Finance Department, to order the revision of a judicial valuation; whereby the revision of Prince George's affairs was at once forbidden for ever. His Excellency the President von Goldbeck has, however, written at once to the Department and sought permission. I have sent this to Berlin by express courier, and at the same time have asked Amelang and Langens[illegible] to help procure the permission and to see to it that the same be dispatched here with all speed. It now remains to be seen whether the spiteful Voss will concede the revision.

Meanwhile I must lie here at anchor and await the answer. In the interim I shall submit the dismemberment plans for approbation, and level everything in this matter. Mr von Goldbeck was at first very [uncertain: Bau], and at the last he reproached me that I had not helped him to the Starostey Strehlitz, which afterwards Dolfs obtained, and which the Minister had promised him so firmly. The proof that I had done everything for him was not difficult for me to furnish,

and we were reconciled again. He wishes very much to have, as soon as possible, three bronzed busts, which he means to set upon his bookcase. Likewise he has asked me to procure him a washstand as soon as may be, so as to be able to wash his ailing lower abdomen daily. May Your Serene Highness have the grace to let three busts of that kind, and a washstand also, be bought, and send me these hither by the first post. The washstand is to be had in the joiners' warehouses.

I should dearly like to surprise the good man with it quite promptly. The Oleśnica matter has, through the friendly mediation of Mr von Hussarzewski, been sent back to the patrimonial court for decision; this is for us a great satisfaction — I shall now bring about the swiftest prosecution of the matter, and in the interim calm Lichnowski, so as to be able to dismember at leisure. Heaven grant only that I obtain the permission for the revision of the valuations as soon as possible; the cursed swindles of Prince George have already caused much terror, and will yet arouse much misfortune and mistrust. He is a frightful man indeed — I cannot praise God enough that I am parted from the man. In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's — If only I might soon receive the 3 [uncertain: bronzed] busts and the washstand, it would give much joy. Kalisz, the 24th of February 1805.

faithful, humble and obedient servant

v Triebenfeld
