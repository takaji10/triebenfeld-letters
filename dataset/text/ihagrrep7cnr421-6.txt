--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Abschrift von Chełmskis Klage, an den König gerichtet, datiert Posen, 19. März 1799, gegen den Starosten Anton von Prusimski auf Trąmpczyn wegen 12.000 Dukaten aus einem Schuldschein, mit der polnischen Abschrift von Prusimskis Verschreibung. Am 16. Juni 1794 habe er Prusimski 12.000 Dukaten auf ein Jahr, bis Johanni 1795, vorgeschossen und dafür im Beisein des Jacob von Bieńkowski auf Mierzewo bei Gnesen und des Antonius v Przeradzki ein Blankett erhalten; vereinbart worden sei, dass er bei Nichtzahlung das Blankett als antichretischen Vertrag (die Einkünfte des Pfandes statt der Zinsen) ansehen und von den Gütern Pfandbesitz nehmen könne. Zum vereinbarten Termin sei nicht gezahlt worden, und er habe den Besitz ergriffen. Inzwischen habe der Fiskus Prusimski wegen Teilnahme an der Insurrektion von 1794 angeklagt; da er nicht zurückgekehrt sei, habe sein Vermögen im Mai beschlagnahmt werden sollen, dem Chełmski durch seine Besitzergreifung im Juni zuvorgekommen sei. Der Fiskus habe am 7. Dezember 1795 die Exmissionsklage angestrengt; im Termin vom 27. Februar sei ein Vergleich geschlossen worden: Chełmski solle die Güter sequestrieren, von der Einnahme zuerst die Verzugszinsen zu 7 Prozent abziehen, dann Rechnung legen und, wie es scheint, weitere Forderungen gerichtlich wahrnehmen (der Satzteil nur teilweise gelesen). Er bittet um einen Instruktionstermin und um Verurteilung Prusimskis zur Zahlung der 12.000 Dukaten und der Verzugszinsen zu 7 Prozent unter Erstattung der Kosten und beruft sich auf die Zeugen. Unterzeichnet v Chełmski. Die polnische Abschrift, Łukom, 16. Juni 1794: Antoni Prusimski gibt, um Stanisław Chełmski, Schatzmeister (Skarbnik) von Wschowa, die Zahlung der im Blankett genannten 12.000 holländischen Dukaten in Gold am Vorabend von Johannes dem Täufer 1795 ohne Zinsen zu sichern, die Verschreibung, dass Chełmski bei Nichtzahlung in seine Güter eintreten dürfe – die Dörfer Trąmpczyn, Nowa Wieś, Osiny, Zarobna (der Name nicht sicher gelesen; so heißt das Dorf in der Liste der Güter von 1830), Łazy und alle Holländer der Herrschaft, kein Dorf und keinen Wald ausgenommen, nur das Eisenwerk, ein Magazin und die Holzvorräte dafür – und sie statt der Zinsen ohne Abrechnung besitzen, bis die Summe zurückgezahlt sei; die Verschreibung solle die Kraft einer gerichtlichen haben. Unterzeichnet Antoni Prusimski; Zeugen Jakub Bieńkowski und Antoni Przeradzki.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Copy of Chełmski's suit, addressed to the King and dated Poznań, 19 March 1799, against the Starost Anton von Prusimski of Trąbczyn for 12,000 ducats on a note of hand, with the Polish copy of Prusimski's undertaking. On 16 June 1794, he writes, he advanced Prusimski 12,000 ducats for one year, to St John's Day 1795, and received a blank note for it in the presence of Jacob von Bieńkowski of Mierzewo near Gniezno and Antonius v Przeradzki; it was agreed that if payment was not made at the fixed date he might treat the note as a contract of antichresis (the pledge's income in place of interest) and take possession of the estates as a pledge. It was not paid on the agreed date, he writes, and he took possession. Meanwhile the Fiscus charged Prusimski with taking part in the insurrection of 1794; as he had not returned, his property was to be seized in May, which Chełmski forestalled by taking possession in June. The Fiscus brought a suit for eviction on 7 December 1795; at the hearing of 27 February a settlement was made: Chełmski was to sequestrate the estates, first deduct the interest for delay at 7 per cent from the income, then render account, and, it seems, still pursue further claims at law (the clause is only partly read). He asks for a hearing to take evidence and that Prusimski be condemned to pay the 12,000 ducats and interest for delay at 7 per cent, with costs, and refers to the witnesses. Signed v Chełmski. The Polish copy, Łukom, 16 June 1794: to secure to Stanisław Chełmski, Treasurer of Wschowa, payment on the eve of St John the Baptist 1795 of the 12,000 Dutch ducats in gold named in the blank note, without interest, Antoni Prusimski gives his undertaking that if he does not pay, Chełmski may enter his estates – the villages of Trąbczyn, Nowawies, Osiny, Zarobna (the name doubtfully read; so the village is called in the list of the estates of 1830), Łazy and all the Hollanders of the estate, excepting no village or wood, only the ironworks, a magazine and the stores of timber for it – and hold them in lieu of interest, without account, until the sum is repaid; the undertaking is to have the force of one made before a court. Signed Antoni Prusimski; witnesses Jakub Bieńkowski and Antoni Przeradzki.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Copy.

Most Serene etc. Poznań, 19 March 1799. Suit of Stanisl[aus] von Chełmski of Łukom near Pyzdry ./. the Starost Anton v Prusimski of Trąbczyn, concerning 12,000 ducats on the strength of a handwritten bond (ex chirographo).

On 16 June 1794 I had to advance to the Starost Anton von Prusimski of Trąbczyn, Nowawies, Osiny and the Trąbczyn Hollanders 12,000 ducats for one year, namely until St John's Day 1795, for which he handed me the blank note enclosed in copy, in the presence of Jacob von Bieńkowski of Mierzewo near Gniezno and of Antonius v Przeradzki. At the loan itself it was agreed between me and the defendant debtor, in the presence of those witnesses, that if the repayment of these 12,000 ducats did not follow on the date fixed for payment, I might treat the said

blank note as a contract of antichresis (contractus antichreticus: the creditor takes the income of the pledge in place of interest) and take possession of the estates listed as a pledge. Payment did not in fact follow on the date agreed, namely at St John's Day last year, and I therefore took possession of these estates at that time.

Meanwhile my debtor was charged by the Fiscus with taking part in the insurrection that broke out in this province in 1794, and because he has not even yet returned, his property was to be seized by the Fiscus in the month of May last year, which I had forestalled only by taking possession of the said estates in the month of June.

The Fiscus did then bring a suit for eviction before Your Royal Majesty on 7 December 1795, and a hearing on it was set for the 27th

27 February this year before Your Most High Government; but at it the following settlement was reached between me and the Fiscus: that I am to sequestrate the oft-mentioned estates, first deduct the interest for delay at 7 per cent from the income, but then render account, while still pursuing my claims at law.

Before Your Royal Majesty I therefore bring my suit against the Starost Anton von Prusimski for these 12,000 ducats, with the request that a hearing to take evidence be appointed, and that the defendant be not only summoned to it but also condemned both to pay the 12,000 ducats owed and to pay interest for delay at 7 per cent, with costs.

In proof of the correctness of the loan, and that he stipulated taking possession of the pledge in

case of non-payment on the fixed date, I refer also to the witnesses proposed above, whom I at the same time most humbly[?] ask to have examined, and that this my suit be deemed sufficiently substantiated. who am etc.

Your Royal Majesty

etc.

v Chełmski.
