--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Grevenitz schickt Triebenfeld die bei ihm eingegangene Post und zwei Original-Anlagen. Er meldet, dass Hoffrath Barby in Paris gestorben ist, und will bei der Witwe nachfragen. Der Staats Kanzler wurde erwartet und ist inzwischen angekommen. Philippischen soll gefährlich krank sein. Grevenitz bekundet Anteil an Triebenfelds Krankheit. Cosmar wurde in erster Instanz unredlichen Verfahrens überführt, hat aber appelliert; bleibt es dabei, verliert er die Vormundschaft.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Grevenitz sends Triebenfeld the post that has reached him together with two original enclosures. He reports that Court Councillor Barby has died in Paris and says he intends to make enquiries with the widow. The State Chancellor had been expected and has meanwhile arrived. Philippischen is said to be dangerously ill. Grevenitz expresses his sympathy over Triebenfeld's illness. Cosmar, he says, has been convicted of dishonest dealing in the first instance but has appealed; should that stand, he will lose the guardianship.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
296 Berlin, the 8th of December 1815. Most esteemed Friend, What has come in here to me I enclose herewith: the Court Councillor Barby has died in Paris; I mean to enquire further of the widow as well. The State Chancellor is expected this week. Philippischen is said to have fallen very ill at Stache[?], which touches me most deeply. Be assured also of my sympathy on account of your illness. I do indeed expect [you/it] very soon.

Cosmar

has at first instance[?]

been held convicted of a dishonest proceeding — but has appealed. If it stands so, he loses the guardianship.

Your faithful and obedient servant, Grevenitz

Yesterday, etc., the State Chancellor really arrived — this I report at once, with the 2 original enclosures appended. Philippischen is said now to be dangerously ill — my grief is as great as if it were my own Kohler[?]. Your

faithful and obedient,

Grevenitz

8 December 1815.
