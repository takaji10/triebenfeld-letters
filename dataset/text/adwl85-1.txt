--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Der Bericht über die Visitation der Pfarrei Trąbczyn im Dekanat Konin, auf Polnisch, von 1790 und 1791 (Blatt 23 Rückseite bis 25 Rückseite und 28 Rückseite bis 29 Rückseite des Bandes; die Blätter dazwischen sind nicht aufgenommen). Der Pfarrer Józef Zurkowski, 1740 in der Diözese Posen geboren und 1767 zum Priester geweiht, wurde 1785 auf die Präsentation Antoni Prusimskis, Starosten von Niszczewice, Erbherrn und Patrons von Trąbczyn, eingesetzt; für einen Vikar, einen Organisten und einen Schulmeister gibt es eine Stiftung, doch keiner von ihnen ist an der Kirche. Zur Pfarrei gehören Trąbczyn, Mariantów, Porąbki, ein Vorwerk, die neuen und alten Olędry, Łazy, Osiny und Nowawies, alle Erbgut des Starosten. Sie zählt 760 Seelen, 555 Katholiken und 205 Dissidenten, 189 davon in den Olendry; die Tabelle für 1791 gibt die Zahlen Dorf für Dorf, und für 1790 nennen die Kirchenbücher 28 Taufen, 10 Trauungen und 29 Begräbnisse. Die kreuzförmige Kirche des heiligen Martin mit zwei hölzernen Kapellen, zum Teil von den einstigen Erben Trąmpczyński erbaut und später von der Kammerherrin Prusimska, geborene Rozdrażewska, erweitert (wann, weiß man nicht), wurde 1546 geweiht; der Bericht gibt ihre Maße, ihre Ausstattung und was auszubessern ist, den Friedhof, den Glockenturm mit zwei Glocken und das Beinhaus. Das Patronat ist weltlich und liegt jetzt bei Antoni Prusimski; es gibt fünf Altäre. Die Bücher der Taufen und Trauungen beginnen 1597, das der Toten 1728; Bücher, die bei der Visitation des Geistlichen Dorposki vorhanden waren, sind durch einen Betrug und weil man nicht wusste, wem sie gehörten, den Gläubigern des verstorbenen Pfarrers Paprocki gegeben worden. Die Stiftungen und Urkunden reichen von 1636 bis 1697: Summen auf Trąbczyn, Kurów, Strzałkowo und Przespolew für Messen, die Zehnten, die das Konsistorium in Gnesen 1692 zusprach, der Krug, der 1694 bestätigt wurde, und das Land, das Andrzej Trąmpczyński 1697 der Kirche abtrat. Auf der letzten Seite beginnt die Beschreibung des Pfarrhauses.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
The record of the visitation of the parish of Trąbczyn in the Konin deanery, in Polish, of 1790 and 1791 (leaves 23 verso to 25 verso and 28 verso to 29 verso of the volume; the leaves between are not included). The parish priest, Józef Zurkowski, born in the diocese of Poznań in 1740 and ordained in 1767, was instituted in 1785 on the presentation of Antoni Prusimski, Starost of Niszczewice, heir and patron of Trąbczyn; there is a fund for a vicar, an organist and a schoolmaster, but none of them is at the church. The parish takes in Trąbczyn, Mariantów, Porąbki, a farm, the new and old Olędry, Łazy, Osiny and Nowawies, all the Starost's hereditary property. It counts 760 souls, 555 Catholics and 205 dissidents, 189 of them in the Olendry; the table for 1791 gives the figures village by village, and for 1790 the registers give 28 baptisms, 10 marriages and 29 burials. The cruciform church of St Martin, with two wooden chapels, built in part by the Trąmpczyński family, once heirs of the place, and later enlarged by the Chamberlain's wife Prusimska, née Rozdrażewska (when, it cannot be known), was consecrated in 1546. The record gives its measurements, its furnishings and the repairs it needs, the cemetery, the belfry with two bells and the ossuary. The patronage is lay and now Antoni Prusimski's; there are five altars. The registers of baptisms and marriages begin in 1597, that of the dead in 1728; books that were there at the visitation of the Reverend Dorposki went, through a trick and because no one knew whose they were, to the creditors of the late parish priest Paprocki. The foundations and documents run from 1636 to 1697: sums secured on Trąbczyn, Kurów, Strzałkowo and Przespolew for Masses, the tithes the Gniezno consistory awarded in 1692, the inn confirmed in 1694, and the land Andrzej Trąmpczyński resigned to the church in 1697. The description of the parsonage begins on the last page.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
The village of Trąbczyn,

in which there is a church. Its parish priest is the Reverend Józef Zurkowski, born in the diocese of Poznań, as his baptismal record shows, in the year 1740, on 9 July. He was ordained priest by the Most Illustrious and Reverend Ignacy Augustyn Sariusz [uncertain: Kozierog] Kozierowski, Bishop Adratyński, in the year 1767, on 24 February, having first had letters dimissory for receiving the orders of the diaconate and the priesthood from the Most Illustrious and Reverend Teodor Czartoryski, Count of Klewań and Żuków, Bishop of Poznań, given to him on 17 December 1766. He was instituted to this church in the Illustrious Consistory of Gniezno on 21 February 1785, on the presentation of the Most Illustrious Antoni Prusimski, Starost of Niszczewice, Knight of the Order of St Stanislaus, heir and patron of these estates. He was approved to hear confessions for one year at the time of the retreat, in its appointed place and term, in

Konin, held in the present year on 15 October. A fund for a vicar, an organist and a schoolmaster is found in the documents, but none of them is at the church.

The state of this church.

This church, in the village of Trąbczyn, the hereditary property of the Most Illustrious Antoni Prusimski, Starost of Niszczewice, Knight of the Order of St Stanislaus, lies in the voivodeship of Kalisz, in the district and deanery of Konin, in the archdeaconry and officialate of Gniezno. It is 6 miles from the metropolitan city of Gniezno, 21 miles from Łowicz, 5 miles from His Royal Majesty's city of Kalisz, and 2 miles from Konin, the district town. By parish right it takes in the said village of Trąbczyn, in which the church stands; Mariantów, Porąbki, the farm called [uncertain: Uła], the new and old Olędry, Łazy, Osiny, Nowawies. These villages lie from the church: towards the winter sunrise 8 furlongs; towards the forenoon likewise; towards the afternoon half a mile; towards the sunset a quarter of a mile; to the west 10 furlongs. All of them are the hereditary property of the Most Illustrious Starost.

The parish extends a good half mile. In the whole parish 760 souls are counted in all, that is 170 men, 191 women, 95 boys coming to communion for the first time, 99 girls; of dissidents 63 men, 60 women, 40 boys, 42 girls. In the past year 1790, according to the entries of the registers, children baptised

of the male sex 13, of the female sex 15; couples married 10; men died 10, women 15, children of both sexes 4; all of which may be seen in the following table.

This church is in the form of a cross, with two wooden chapels, and has a new roof. The ceiling is damaged in places by leaks of rain from earlier times; the floor of planks throughout the church needs relaying in the great choir. The church faces the sunrise. The great door, to the west, closes well. It has a high choir loft, on which stands a good positive organ with seven stops and three bellows. Beside it, on the south side, a door from the cemetery to the porch, closing well, from which one enters the chapel and from it the church. The sacristy is of wood and needs its windows repaired and a new floor;

in it a table with four large drawers for the vestments and smaller ones for the chalices, veils and corporals etc., closing well; there too an old tabernacle for keeping the Most Holy Sacrament, broken; a large chest with a good lock. From this sacristy a door to the west into the library, in which a large cupboard stands for the books. Opposite the sacristy, to the north, a treasury, whose door and lock are good; a large cupboard stands there with a good lock.

The length of this church in all is 47 ells, the width 33 ells; that is, the presbytery is 14 ells long and 9 ells wide; the great choir 33 ells long and 14 ells wide. The chapel of St Martin on the north side is 12 ells long and 8½ ells wide; in it 2 cupboards, locked, for the lights. On the south side the second chapel, of St Aloysius, 10½ ells long and 8¼ ells wide. The sacristy is 8 ells long and 5 ells wide; the library 5 ells long and 4 ells wide; the treasury 8½ ells long and 2¾ ells wide; the porch 4½ ells long and 5½ ells wide.

Windows: in the small choir 4, needing repair; in the great choir 4, good; in the chapels two each, these good; in the sacristy 2 small windows with iron grilles, somewhat damaged; in the treasury only an iron grille, without a window.

In this church are 4 good confessionals painted blue, with grilles; 20 good pews painted the same way; a railing with balusters

painted, covered with white cloths.

This church was built in part by the noble Trąmpczyński family, once heirs of the place, and later enlarged by the noble Lady Prusimska, née Rozdrażewska, the Chamberlain's wife, heiress (when, it cannot be known). Its titular patron is St Martin, Bishop. It was consecrated in the year 1546, on 26 September. The day of its consecration is kept every year on the first Sunday after the feast of St Matthew the Apostle. The cemetery has on the south, east and west side a small, recent fence, into which there is a good gate for entering from the east, and from the west a carriage gate and a gate, good, with a lock, but there are no grilles by them. There too, straight opposite the great door of the church, is the belfry, needing sill-beams and doors over half of it, as the planks have fallen off, and a roof before long; in it two bells, whether consecrated is not known; one of them is chipped. To the north of the church is an ossuary of wood, good, with a window onto the cemetery; between it and the belfry the fence leans, further on it is good.

The right of patronage, or collation, of this church is lay, and the church has always been governed by secular priests. Its patron now is the Most Illustrious Antoni Prusimski, Starost of Niszczewice, Knight of the Order of St Stanislaus. There are no mansionaries, prebends or altarages, nor any fund for them.

In this church there are 5 altars: the first, the high altar, in which a picture of the Mother of God under the title of the Scapular, with a confraternity, closed by a sliding cover, with a dress and crowns of silver

smaller 20. Cushions on the altars 5. Covers for the altars 6. Good small carpets 2. An old carpet 1.

Church books.

Description of the rights and documents found at this church.

1662. A resignation made in court in the books of the Konin land court, of a piece of peasant land called Pierzchalińska, for the pay of the organist and the needs of the church. 1636: the first foundation sum, 1,000 Polish złoty, secured on Trąbczyn; the second, 1,500 złoty, in the year 1637, which the noble Trąmpczyński, heir, recorded at Kalisz and at Pyzdry. From these there is now due 80½ złoty each year, with the obligation of singing 52 Holy Masses a year, reading 156, and of 208 litanies sung by the organist; from this income also something for the fabric of the church. 1668: a sum of 350 Polish złoty, secured on part of Kurów and recorded there too, from the noble Mr Stefan Wardencki; likewise 250 złoty placed by the noble Mr Bogusławski; from it 21 złoty is due for saying 52 Masses. 1670: 500 Polish złoty secured on Strzałkowo, recorded at Kalisz and Pyzdry, from the noble Mr Strzałkowski; from it 17½ złoty is due, for 12 Masses. There too a sum of 2,000 złoty, recorded under the same year and act by the noble Lady Golinska on the estate of Przespolew, for saying 104 Masses every year. 1692, 16 December: a decree of the Gniezno consistory, taken from its archive, by which the tithe from the farm of Dręzgi, and likewise from the farm called Nowina, is awarded. 1694: the inn, confirmed by a decree of the Most Illustrious Official of Gniezno, with the right to sell beer and spirits in it; in the same decree a house for the school is named, which

now has the name of the organist's house. Item, in the same decree, a piece of the parish land: a separate pine wood, by which the peasants, with the parish priests' leave, cleared fields and small meadows for themselves; this clearing has the name Porąbki, and three cottagers now live on it; likewise a large meadow beyond the wood and Porąbki, belonging to the parish. By the same decree the measured tithe from the lords' farms, and a meadow called Gralewska, given long ago by the heirs, are awarded. 1697: a resignation of half a piece of land and a field, made at the Kaptur court at Kalisz after the death of John III, King of Poland; likewise of new land for the schoolmaster, lying by the church wood on the north side. Under the same act the noble Andrzej Trąmpczyński, heir of part of Trąbczyn, makes a submission to bear all dues and taxes that may be imposed on the said lands, which he resigned to the church of Trąbczyn. Apart from these there are no other documents at the church.
