--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Abschrift einer Eingabe Triebenfelds im Auftrag des Fürsten von Hohenlohe. Er übersendet die Korrespondenz mit Kircheisen, die das Oberlandesgericht Brieg nicht zu Protokoll nehmen wollte. Cosmar habe die Forderung der Curischen Erben von 300/m Rthl blockiert und mit Arrest belegt. Die Erben seien mit 112000 Rthl zufrieden: 12/m Rthl bar und eine Anweisung auf 100000 Rthl. Triebenfeld bittet, dem Fürsten diese Summe vorzuschießen und die Majoratsgüter verwalten zu lassen. Dafür will er die Schuldinstrumente und die königliche Garantie ausliefern.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Copy of a submission by Triebenfeld on behalf of the Prince of Hohenlohe. He sends the correspondence with Kircheisen, which the High Court of Appeal at Brzeg would not take down in the record. He says Cosmar has blocked the claim of the Curische heirs for 300/m Rthl and placed it under seizure. He says the heirs are content with 112,000 Rthl: 12/m Rthl in cash and an order for 100,000 Rthl. Triebenfeld asks that this sum be advanced to the Prince and that the entailed estates be put under administration. In return he offers to hand over the debt instruments and the royal guarantee.

Most Serene Prince etc. To Your Most Serene Highness I am, by commission of the Prince of Hohenlohe, to present the accompanying file of documents [uncertain: allerzubeißen — corrupt, probably "most humbly"]. It contains the correspondence with His Excellency M. von Kircheisen, which the Upper Provincial Court at Brzeg would not take into the record drawn up, on the ground that it had not been charged to do so. Since, however, from the letter of the medical councillor Cosmar of 22 June 1810, under G, the most unmistakable chicaneries against the poor good Prince appear — particularly as Cosmar himself admits that, on account of the claim of the Cur[ish] heirs for 300/m Rthl, which the Prince alone undertook to contest, he has cut off and placed under arrest everything belonging to the Prince — I hasten most humbly to lay the file of documents at the feet of Your Most Serene Highness.

How exceedingly necessary it now is to satisfy the Curish heirs, Your [uncertain: W.] [illegible] will be pleased to gather from this. The heirs can quite certainly, according to the proofs in hand, be satisfied with 112,000 Rthl; indeed, only 12/m Rthl in cash is demanded at once, and for 100,000 Rthl a secure order to pay. — Should Your [uncertain: W.] Highness approve these requests, and allow the sum to be advanced to the upright old Prince, but have all the entailed estates forthwith administered for the treasury in advance of the moneys being given, then this one honest Prince is at once [uncertain: gewißen — probably "torn"] out of the claws of Cosmar, and saved by Your Serene Highness's grace, upon which alone he counts. — I for my part bind myself, on forfeit of my entire goods and of my liberty, to deliver the instruments of debt and above all the guarantee of His Majesty at once into Your Serene Highness's hands. The one thing that remains to be wished is that the Monarch, if today or tomorrow the C[urish] heirs should once more approach His Majesty for their satisfaction, should disclaim the matter, as has already been done, whereby all this is quickly achieved and the King's word remains sacred. — Cosmar lies exposed and trodden in the dust, if he is especially held to the most expeditious rendering of accounts, the more so as Prince August is now demanding back the power of attorney given to him.

Funds for the discharge of the 112,000 Rthl as well as of the 35,000 [uncertain: is] are abundantly present; indeed, even the 18,000 Rthl which Prince August is to draw yearly, and of which some 54,000 Rthl already lie in store, can be applied thereto out of hand, and thus the debt of 147/m be discharged in a very short time. — [uncertain: Er führen — probably "Should Your Most Serene Highness grant"] Your Most Serene Highness this most humble request, then the good Prince, who [uncertain: ſol] through no fault of his own has already for 9 years endured the [uncertain: granz anlaſaſten] sufferings, is saved by Your most high grace, and every creditor set at rest. — In [uncertain: unbegragter — probably "unbounded"] veneration I remain, until death,

Your [illegible] Highness's. Vienna, the 24th of January

1815
