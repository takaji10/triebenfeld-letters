--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld reicht ein per Estaffette eingegangenes Schreiben Kunckels ein und bittet um Verhaltungsbefehle. Er hält ein Abkommen mit Otocki für nötig; ein gütlicher Vergleich, bei dem Otocki mit 1500 oder 2000 Rthl zufrieden wäre, sei vorteilhaft. Ob Kunkel Vollmacht zum Vergleich erhalten soll, stellt er dem Fürsten anheim. Er fürchtet, dass Kunkel und Jentsch gegeneinander handeln, und rät, beide zu instruieren. Glenck kam nicht mehr, ließ aber die Rechnungen von Konotop zurück, die über alles Aufschluss geben.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld submits a letter from Kunkel that arrived by express courier and asks for instructions on how to proceed. He considers an arrangement with Otocki necessary; an amicable settlement, in which Otocki would be satisfied with 1500 or 2,000 Rthl, would be advantageous, he says. Whether Kunkel should be given a power of attorney to settle he leaves to the Prince. He fears that Kunkel and Jentsch are working against each other and advises instructing both. Glenck did not come again, but left behind the accounts from Konotop, which explain everything.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
123 Late yesterday evening I received by express courier the enclosed letter from Kunkel, which I herewith [uncertain: lezt] submit to Your Most Serene Highness, requesting Your instructions as to how I am to conduct myself. It is indeed necessary to come to an arrangement with von Otocki, and if this could be brought about by an amicable settlement, such that Otocki were content with 1500 or 2,000 Rthl., it would be an advantageous and desirable thing. I leave it to Your Most Serene Highness's supreme judgement — whether the power of attorney for the settlement is to be given to Kunkel.

That Kunkel and Jentsch, moreover, appear to be acting so much against one another arouses my great apprehension — I fear that Kunkel, by his vehement conduct, will do foolish things, and that Jentsch, out of malice, will make intrigues, which may prove prejudicial. My humble counsel is to instruct these men well and to keep them within their bounds.

Glenck did not come to me again yesterday, and has even left the Konotop accounts behind here, which gave full information upon everything in this matter.

Wrocław, the 23rd of April 1804

v Triebenfeld
