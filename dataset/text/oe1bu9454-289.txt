--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld bittet Albrecht, beim König einen kategorischen Bescheid zur Forderung der Curischen Erben zu erwirken. Die Schuld beträgt 300/m Rthl, mit Zinsen 426/m Rthl, und der verstorbene König hat sie als Selbstschuldner unterschrieben. Zahlt der König, soll die Cosmarische Administration der Majoratsgüter aufgehoben werden; der Fürst habe in vier Jahren statt 96/m nur 2500 Rthl erhalten. Sagt er sich los, will Triebenfeld mit den Erben vergleichen, die zuvor circa 112/m Rthl annehmen wollten, und bittet um einen königlichen Vorschuss dafür. Cosmar legt keine Rechnung.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld asks Albrecht to obtain a categorical decision from the King on the claim of the Curische heirs. He says the debt amounts to 300/m Rthl, 426/m Rthl with interest, and that the late King signed it as principal debtor. If the King pays, Cosmar's administration of the entailed estates should be lifted; the Prince, he says, has received only 2,500 Rthl in four years instead of 96/m. If he disclaims it, Triebenfeld wants to reach a settlement with the heirs, who earlier were willing to accept about 112/m Rthl, and he asks for a royal advance for that purpose. He says Cosmar submits no account.

Right Honourable Sir,

Most highly esteemed Privy Cabinet Councillor!

It will assuredly not be unknown to Your Right Honourable that the Courland heirs have a claim against the Prince of Hohenlohe of 300/m Rthl; the instrument of debt for it was made out by the Prince and his divorced consort and was signed by His Majesty the late King as principal debtor himself, and the interest on it was also paid out of the Royal treasuries down to 1806. Upon this the Courland heirs believed they might found their right to demand this sum, which today with the interest amounts to 426/m Rthl, from the treasury, and they commenced proceedings against it. His Majesty had thereupon, a year and a day since, declared "that He wished to have nothing to do with the matter, but rather disclaimed it entirely." This moved the Prince / although the sum had in the first instance been negotiated for His Majesty the late King / to enter into a settlement with the Courland heirs, under which the latter would be content with about 112/m Rthl and would give a receipt for the whole item of debt. During His Majesty's presence here, however, He gave, entirely contrary to that first declaration, the assurance to the heiresses that He would have the matter laid before Him on His most high return to Berlin, and would then declare Himself further. On the ground of this most high decision, the councillor of justice Heneberg in Berlin has intimated to the Courland mandatary Eberhard at Brzeg at once to break off all settlement negotiations, because the treasury would now make the whole payment and must make it. The Prince had sent me here to bring the matter to an end and to request an advance of 112/m Rthl in accordance with the settlement, which was to be reimbursed again out of the estates in 4 years. But since now the Courland heirs have withdrawn their word upon the hope given them, I have been put out of a position to bring the matter to the desired goal and to preserve for His Majesty a capital of 426/m Rthl. I should also fall reverently silent, were my princely client not entreating so dreadfully, for on the ground of this debt the medical councillor Cosmar administers all the entailed estates and for 4 years now gives no man little or nothing of it, renders no account, and nobody knows where the revenues of these considerable estates remain. I must therefore, in the name of my client, beg a most high and most gracious categorical decision.
