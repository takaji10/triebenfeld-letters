--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Der Fürst schreibt Hardenberg, der Distrikt seiner Trąbczyner Güter falle unter russische Hoheit; da beide Souveräne die Handlungen der Kommission bestätigt hätten, seien sie für ihn verloren, und den Donataren solle eine Entschädigung durch andere Güter gegeben werden. Sein Verlust betrage über 261000 Rthl. Er wäre mit dem kleinen Amt Sokolnik zufrieden, das 8 bis 10000 Rthl Pacht gibt, und bittet Hardenberg, sich beim russischen Kaiser dafür zu verwenden. Auf dem Bogen des Briefes stehen zwei Entwürfe vom 6. Juni 1815. Der erste ist Hardenbergs Antwort an den Fürsten auf das Schreiben vom 12. des Vormonats wegen der Trąbczyner Güter: Ein Minister habe ihm auch das an ihn gerichtete Schreiben wegen Kamionna und Trąbczyn mitgeteilt; Hardenberg habe Zerboni ersucht, ihn über beide Güter in vollständige Kenntnis zu setzen, und behalte sich vor, dem Fürsten von seinen weiteren Schritten Nachricht zu geben. Der zweite ist ein Schreiben an Zerboni in Poznań: Hardenberg übersendet urschriftlich zwei Schreiben des Fürsten, an ihn und an Stein, über die Ansprüche wegen der entzogenen Güter Kamionna und Trąbczyn und bittet um Mitteilung, welche Schritte zugunsten des Fürsten noch getan werden können. Kamionna habe der Fürst an Leixner verkauft; die 55000 Rthl und Zinsen habe die Bank zu fordern. Er erwartet Zerbonis Bericht so bald als möglich.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
The Prince writes to Hardenberg that the district of his Trąbczyn estates falls under Russian sovereignty; since both sovereigns have confirmed the commission's acts, he says the estates are lost to him, and that the donees should be compensated with other estates. He says his loss amounts to more than 261,000 Rthl. He would be satisfied with the small domain of Sokolnik, which yields 8 to 10,000 Rthl in rent, and he asks Hardenberg to intercede for this with the Russian Emperor. On the letter's sheet stand two drafts of 6 June 1815. The first is Hardenberg's reply to the Prince concerning his letter of the 12th of the previous month about the Trąbczyn estates: a minister has also passed on to him the letter addressed to that minister regarding Kamionna and Trąbczyn; he has asked Zerboni to inform him fully about both estates, and he reserves the right to notify the Prince of his further steps. The second is a letter to Zerboni in Poznań: Hardenberg sends, in the original, two letters of the Prince, one to himself and one to Stein, concerning the claims over the confiscated estates of Kamionna and Trąbczyn, and asks to be told what steps can still be taken in the Prince's favour. He says the Prince sold Kamionna to Leixner, and that the 55,000 Rthl and interest are owed to the bank. He expects Zerboni's report as soon as possible.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
Most Serene Prince!

Poland's fate is now decided, and the boundaries determined. The district in which the Trąbczyn estates lie that were taken from me by the Polish administrative commission falls under Russian sovereignty. Since now both sovereigns have confirmed the acts of the Prussian commission, the estates taken from me remain lost to me for ever, and it is now my sole comfort that, according to the love of justice of both monarchs, the donees are to be given a compensation by means of other estates.

According to the highest probability I should have to await my indemnification from His Majesty the Emperor of Russia, on which account I have also already applied to the Minister Baron von Stein. But whether this matter depends on Baron von Stein alone, I do not know. — This, however, I do know, that through Your Serene Highness's word with His Majesty the Emperor of Russia I should at once be helped.

My loss amounts to over 261,000 Rthl. I might well with every right hope that estates of equal value would be conferred on me: in the meantime I should gladly be content with the small district office of Sokolnik situated not far from Boleslawice, which yields some 8 to 10,000 Rthl in rent. I most obediently beg Your Serene Highness to intercede for me, as your old and faithful admirer, with His Majesty the Emperor of Russia, and to so direct the matter that the Sokolnik district office be conferred on me as compensation, the more so as I am the only one from whom the estates have been taken and who must be compensated.

May Your Serene Highness accept the assurance of the high reverence with which I persist.

Your Serene Highness's

most obedient servant, F[riedrich]. L[udwig]. P[rince]. o[f]. Hohenlohe.

Sławięcice, the 12 May 1815.
