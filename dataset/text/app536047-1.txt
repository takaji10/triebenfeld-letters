--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Dekret des Kalischer Landgerichts in Konin von 1783, Eintrag Nr. 26 seiner Sitzung, zwischen Stanisław Chełmski, Schatzmeister des Landes Wschowa, als Kläger und Antoni Prusimski von Kolno, Starost von Niszczewice, als Geladenem. Chełmski hält Prusimski eine Verurteilung entgegen, die er 1780 beim Ortstermin auf dem Gut Grab wegen Verstoßes gegen ein Tribunalsdekret gegen ihn erwirkt hat. Prusimski weist sein Recht, gehört zu werden, nicht nach und unterwirft sich dem Ermessen des Gerichts; es legt ihm die halbe Kämmererbuße auf, halb an die widersprechende Partei, halb an das Gericht, sofort zu zahlen, und lässt ihn zur Sache zu und ordnet Verhöre beeideter Zeugen an, weil es um Gewalttaten geht. Aus den Verhören ergibt sich: Leute vom Hof Prusimskis aus Trąbczyn kamen, vom Weg abgeirrt, zur Wüstung Smoleniec, die zu Chełmskis Erbgut Łukom gehört, ließen sich den Weg zeigen und fragten den Olęder Jan Kłonica, wem das Dorf gehöre. Als Kłonica sagte, diese Olęder gehörten Chełmski, schalt ihn Prusimskis Unterkutscher einen Lügner und schlug ihn mit der Peitsche; der Kutscher schlug ihn mit einem Kiefernstock, der dabei zerbrach, und der Unterkutscher schrie, man solle ihn totschlagen; Kłonica floh in ein Erlengehölz. Die Leute wandten sich darauf zum Haus des Olęders, holten dort gewaltsam einen Knecht, schlugen ihn und schlugen ihn weiter auf dem Weg zum Krug von Tomice; er kam erst am folgenden Tag nach Hause. Ein Zeuge beschwerte sich noch am selben Tag bei Prusimski, der denselben Weg kam; Prusimski versprach, die Sache zu Hause zu bereinigen. Die Taten geschahen auf Chełmskis eigenem Grund; ob mit Wissen oder auf Befehl Prusimskis, klären die Verhöre nicht, und Kutscher und Unterkutscher sind in Trąbczyn nicht zu finden. Beschuldigt sind nach den Verhören nur der Kutscher Józef und der Unterkutscher Walenty; Prusimski erklärt, er könne sie nicht stellen, und bietet einen Reinigungseid an. Das Gericht legt ihm auf, binnen sechs Wochen vor dem Burgamt Konin zu beschwören, dass der von Chełmski erwirkte Arrest die beiden nicht erreicht hat und er sie nicht stellen kann, bei Strafe der Verbannung. Die Verhörprotokolle bleiben in der Kanzlei und werden den Parteien gegen Quittung herausgegeben.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
In October 1783 the land court of Kalisz sitting at Konin heard Stanisław Chełmski, Treasurer of the land of Wschowa, against Antoni Prusimski of Kolno, Starost of Niszczewice. Chełmski raised against Prusimski a condemnation that he had obtained against him in 1780, at the sitting on the ground at the estate of Grab, for breach of a Tribunal decree. Prusimski did not show his right to be heard and submitted to the court's discretion; the court laid on him half the capitaneal penalty, half to the objecting party and half to the court, payable at once, admitted him to the cause, and, because the cause concerned acts of violence, ordered sworn witnesses examined. They showed that men of Prusimski's household from Trąbczyn, having strayed from their road, came to the deserted settlement of Smoleniec, which belongs to Chełmski's hereditary estate of Łukom, had the way shown to them, and asked the Olęder settler Jan Kłonica whose village it was. When Kłonica said that these settlers belonged to Chełmski, Prusimski's under-coachman called him a liar and struck him with a whip; the coachman beat him with a pine stick, which broke, and the under-coachman shouted that he should be struck dead; Kłonica fled into an alder grove. The men then turned to the settler's house, took a servant from it by force, beat him, and went on beating him on the way to the tavern of Tomice; he came home only the next day. A witness complained the same day to Prusimski, who came along the same road; Prusimski promised to put the matter right at home. The acts were done on Chełmski's own ground; whether with Prusimski's knowledge or at his order is not shown, and the coachman and under-coachman are not to be found at Trąbczyn. Only the coachman Józef and the under-coachman Walenty are accused; Prusimski declared that he cannot produce them and offered an oath to clear himself. The court ordered him to swear within six weeks before the municipal court office at Konin that the arrest Chełmski obtained (an order to keep named subjects ready to be produced in court) did not reach the two men and that he cannot produce them, on pain of banishment.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
No. 26: Between the Right Honourable Stanisław Chełmski, Treasurer of the Land of Wschowa, Plaintiff, [represented] by the Honourable Józef Stablewski, [and] the Illustrious Right Honourable Antoni of Kolno Prusimski, Starost of Niszczewice, Defendant, [represented] by the Honourable Wojciech Żeromski — regarding the objected condemnation at the estate of the village of Grab, on Monday after the Feast of the Nativity of the Blessed Virgin Mary in the year 1780 — on the part of the Right Honourable Chełmski, Treasurer of Wschowa, against the Illustrious Right Honourable Prusimski, to his advantage, for contravention of the Tribunal's Decree, obtained and published: the Court holds that a proxy is to be produced on the part of the objecting party; and since he produces one, it therefore finds the Illustrious Right Honourable Prusimski bound to show his right of standing; and since he does not show it, but rather submits himself to the moderation of its judgement, therefore the Court decrees that the Illustrious Right Honourable Prusimski, Starost of Niszczewice, upon the establishment of his right of standing, shall pay and reckon out: to the objecting party, half of the Starost's Court bail; but to the Court, two [marks], by half, forthwith, under penalty of banishment, to be published in the determination of their courts. And meanwhile the Court finds the same Illustrious Right Honourable Starost of Niszczewice to have the right of standing, and commits the matter to trial; in which trial, since the present cause, with respect to the objected acts of violence, comes to be adjudicated, the Court therefore decrees that the Parties shall conduct and dispatch Inquisitions concerning the contents of the Term of the Plaintiff Party and the manifests of both Parties, during the continuance of its court sessions, interrogatories having first been drawn up and communicated to each other, and the oath on the uncorrupted [state] of the witnesses having been taken — with a Court Summoner appointed for the pronouncing of the form of the Oath; the production of the accused comprehended within the Term of the Plaintiff Party

the Court suspends until the reading of the Inquisitions. The Right Honourable Chełmski, Treasurer of Wschowa, Plaintiff, and the Illustrious Right Honourable Prusimski, Starost of Niszczewice, judicially rendered the Oath concerning the uncorrupted testimony of the witnesses. Both Parties dispatched the Inquisitions and deposited the same. The Court therefore betook itself to the reading of the same, from which, having been read, it is deduced:

That men, serving the household of the Illustrious Right Honourable Prusimski, from the estate of Trąbczyn — having strayed from the road — arrived at the deserted tract of Smoleniec, pertaining to the more principal hereditary estates of Łukom of the Right Honourable Chełmski; and there ordered that the road by which they ought to depart be shown to them; and asked the Laboriosus Jan Kłonica, a Hollander, whose village this might be? And when the same Laboriosus Kłonica reported that these very Hollanders belonged to the Right Honourable Chełmski, the Under-coachman of the Illustrious Right Honourable Prusimski reproached the same Hollander for lying, and struck him with a whip; and that by the Coachman too, the same Hollander was beaten with a broken pine-wood stick; and moreover that the Under-coachman immediately shouted out that he should be struck and killed; that after these things had been carried out, the same Hollander fled into an alder grove; but that the aforementioned men of the Illustrious Right Honourable Prusimski turned themselves toward the house of the same Hollander, and there violently seized a servant from the hill, beat him soundly, and ordered him to be led to the Tavern of Tomice; and that on the road the same servant was continually beaten by them; and that only now, on the following day, the aforesaid servant returned home; and that on this very day, to the Illustrious Right Honourable Prusimski, travelling along the same road, one witness complained of the above-described violence; and that the Illustrious Right Honourable Prusimski promised, at home, to facilitate this matter; and that these acts, moreover, carried out on the Right Honourable Chełmski's own land, had turned out to have been done violently — whether, however, they had been carried out with the knowledge or order of the Illustrious Right Honourable Prusimski?

Continuation of No. 26.

The Inquisitions explained this not at all; and likewise the same Inquisitions reported that the Coachman and the Under-coachman, the perpetrators of the above-written acts, are not found within the estate of Trąbczyn. Wherefore the Court, having taken note of the foregoing findings — since only the Laboriosi Józef the Coachman and Walenty the Under-coachman are accused, by the Inquisitions, of the violent act, but now, since the Illustrious Right Honourable Starost of Niszczewice is in no way able to produce the aforesaid Coachman and Under-coachman, and declares before his own Court that the same were not apprehended by arrest — and on that account, a Manifest having been lodged before the Records of the Konin Municipal Court, during the sitting of the present courts, with an offer of rendering a sworn clearance, he defends himself — therefore the Court decrees: that the same Illustrious Right Honourable Starost of Niszczewice, from now and from the present act, within six weeks, before the Records of the Konin Municipal Court, shall clear himself in that manner: that the arrest on the part of the Right Honourable Chełmski did not reach the Laboriosi Józef the Coachman and Walenty the Under-coachman, nor has he the means of producing the same — and this is to be published under penalty of banishment, in case of contravention, regarding the Manifest to be lodged. But the Inquisitions now conducted the Court holds and declares are to remain in the Chancellery of this place, and to be handed over to the Parties upon prior Acquittance, [by force of the present decree].
