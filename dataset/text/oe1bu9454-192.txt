--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Der Schreiber berichtet krank aus dem Bett: In Berlin seien 360000 Rthl zugesagt. Er habe die aufgehetzten Bauern beruhigt und die Pächter verjagt. Glenck wiegle mit den Olesnicaer Leuten die Menschen auf, weise Käufer ab und verbreite ein Pasquill unter kaufwilligen Kolonisten. Der Fürst habe bereits 10000 Rthl von Swięcia, 10000 Rthl von Olesnica und 12000 Rthl von Szetlewek erhalten. Das Urteil zur Olesnicaer Erbverpachtung verbietet die Exmittierung; der Schreiber will nun den Konsens beantragen.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
The writer reports from his sickbed: he says 360,000 Rthl have been promised in Berlin. He says he has calmed the peasants, who had been stirred up, and driven off the tenants. He says Glenck, together with the people of Olesnica, is inciting the inhabitants, turning buyers away and circulating a lampoon among colonists willing to buy. He says the Prince has already received 10,000 Rthl from Swięcia, 10,000 Rthl from Olesnica and 12,000 Rthl from Szetlewek. The judgment on the hereditary lease at Olesnica forbids eviction; the writer now intends to apply for consent.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
192

My dear friend, there are 360,000 Rthl lying in Berlin which have been promised to me. Only I lie here and have been keeping to my bed into the fifth week; I have vexed myself here quite to ruin. Meanwhile I have now put everything here in order and entirely calmed the peasants who had been stirred up by arch-rogues, and have also driven the tenants to the devil. When God or devils and hell-brands of men play me crosswise tricks, so that I must perish of [uncertain: Argus schien] — then all glory is at an end — Glenck is here, has joined together with the Oleśnica hell-brands and has engaged for the purpose a certain Kramplitz, Bergemann and Ribbeck, the [text lost] gang of hell stirs the people up and turns the buyers away; it is too mad how it goes with me. If meanwhile my constitution recovers and I am well again, then the devil take all the rascals. The tenants are, as said before, all gone to the devil, and from Christmas and Midsummer onward everything must be so understood that only monies may be raised, and that nothing more may come up for expenditure. Mr Weigel will be paid, for which I had it, since I receive the money from the Justice Fund for Weigel and Lichnowski; these must cede their mortgages and receive their money. I have already said this to the witnesses Mr Weigel also; but while I lie here in misery I cannot act in Berlin; as soon as I recover somewhat I shall come to Wrocław and go at once with my daughters to Berlin

From here I can as yet draw nothing, but from Michaelmas onward much money should come in by degrees. It would have been easy to obtain money here if I had been willing to give away the plots of land for a [uncertain: third part] of the price, but from that God preserve me — Now the Prince will still receive 190,000 Rthl in cash and 40,000 Rthl in yearly revenues in perpetuity. The Prince has already received 10,000 Rthl from Swięcia, 10,000 Rthl from Oleśnica, 12,000 Rthl from Szetlewek, and 9,000 Rthl have been given for grounds, all of which has gone on the costs and interest and also on old debts. The peasants' revolution and the Glenck knaveries have had their use — I have indeed had to give a good deal back, but now it has turned out all the better and the more abundant for it. Enough; everything will go well — if only I am well again first — writing comes very hard to me. So only this further, that I am unceasingly.

Yours. On the whole Koszęcin affair I must suspend my judgement, for I do not know it — As it seems to me, what is wanting is a man who knows the locality thoroughly, and rather a deal of money — the [uncertain: Stös:] praise is suspect to me — Well evaded — the Pro Memoria of the Koszęcin officials give sufficient[illegible]

guidance.

The enclosed lampoon Glenck has made upon me and had distributed among the colonists who wish to buy; the original I have sent to His Serene Highness — It was drafted and dealt out by Glenck's own high hand. Trąbczyn

12th August

1806

Humble friend and servant

v Triebenfeld

To the Oleśnica [uncertain: Schluzbuben] he has, to be sure, given certificates and helped to support their cause, and for that they have promised him to sell him the bailiwick. Yesterday the judgement came down upon the fellows:

1. [uncertain: Fenſe] may not evict the fellows

2. he is to discharge the debts encumbering Oleśnica

3. he is to apply for the consent.

4. When that is given, to have the hereditary lease entered in the mortgage book,

6. but Giese is dismissed, in that the Prince may not hand over the estates to him before the consent has hitherto been issued. That is a sentence at which the dogs might howl. I shall now at once apply to the authority for the consent; then the resolution will be that such scoundrels may not possess noble estates, and the Chamber will throw them out; then all the feud will be at an end.
