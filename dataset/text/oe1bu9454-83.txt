--- SUMMARY (German) ---
v. Triebenfeld meldet, Krotoszyn werde dem Fürsten sicher überlassen. Bei Polajewo gebe es noch Bedenken, doch Geh. Finanzrat Schulze versichere, auch dies sei zu erhalten; dort seien 100/m Rthl zu gewinnen, trotz Widerstand der Kammern. Er bittet, Prinz George, falls dieser früher eintreffe, aufzuhalten, bis er selbst kommt. Hoym sei nach Pommern gereist, Voss komme Donnerstag zurück. Triebenfeld hat an Voss, Schulze, Beyme und den Obersten geschrieben. Dazu Gerüchte: Repnin habe einen Ukas erhalten, die Franzosen wollten Hannover nehmen.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
v. Triebenfeld reports that Krotoszyn will certainly be made over to the Prince. In the case of Polajewo there are still doubts, but he says Privy Finance Councillor Schulze assures him that this too can be obtained; there, he says, 100/m Rthl are to be gained, despite the resistance of the chambers. He asks that Prince George be held back, should he arrive earlier, until he himself comes. He says Hoym has travelled to Pomerania and Voss will return on Thursday. Triebenfeld has written to Voss, Schulze, Beyme and the colonel. Added to this are rumours: he says Repnin has received a ukase and the French want to take Hanover.

Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Lord!

The letter graciously issued to me by Your Most Serene Highness I have duly received, together with all the enclosures, by the hand of Mr Michaelis.

As to the matter itself, I can say with the most joyful heart that everything goes as we could wish — Krotoszyn they are quite certainly giving to Your Most Serene Highness in return for the Suprenstis possessions. Only with regard to Polajewo do they seem still to raise some hesitation — but according to the assurance of the Privy Councillor of Finance Schulze, Your Most Serene Highness may obtain this one also. The chief aim of my most ardent wish and of my honest zeal for Your Serene Highness's peace and contentment is as good as attained, for Krotoszyn is and remains an inestimable [uncertain: acquisition] — with Polajewo too there is something like 100/m Rthl to be gained, only the obstacles with the Chambers will cost me many a struggle — yet this too should be capable of being overcome.

My one and only care is that His Serene Highness Prince George should not arrive there before I am in Wrocław — for here I would rather not depart

until I am sure of the matter.

Should the Prince arrive at the Forte before me, then let Your Serene Highness by all means hold him there until I come, for otherwise I shall be made a liar and the good Prince will be led astray. It is, before God, no boasting — but I can certainly assure you that no one among all our officials knows the province, still less the interior of the business, save he alone; and in Prince George's affair it is required that a man who has knowledge of the matter and understands the language should come forward, and then much may be gained. The minister Hoym set out today for Pomerania for ten days — the Monarch received him very graciously. On Thursday the minister von Voss returns, and then the matter will soon be clear — yesterday I sent another obliging letter to him, one likewise to Schulze, one to Mr Cabinet Councillor Beyme, and one to the Colonel — the effect has throughout been very good, for I referred myself in every instance to the cabinet letter as well as to that from Mr Cabinet Councillor

In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Berlin, the 15th of October

1798.

most faithful and humble servant

v. Triebenfeld

The good Monarch is said to be not at all in a good humour. That truly grieves me — the Prince Repnin is said, so people tell, to have received a ukase on the way not to show himself in Petersburg again — who knows whether this is not perhaps a masque?

The good Privy Cabinet Councillor Mencke is said to be in activity again, since his health has notably improved.

Among certain gentlemen it is said that discord prevails; it would be a pity, for they are a pair of honest men — Her Serene Highness the Princess Sacken is in good health. That Count Hoym has now gone to Pomerania I much censure — the X Spinne was just here and says he knows it for certain that the French mean to take Hanover; that this is why the King is so ill-humoured. But I do not believe a word of what he says; he has already lied too much —
