--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Von Sanitz fragt, ob das erste Tribunal über die Trąbczyner Güter des Fürsten schon entschieden hat, und bittet möglichst um Abschriften des Friedensrichter-Urteils und der Tribunalsentenz. Er ist nach Berlin gezogen, um Reste seines verlorenen Vermögens zu retten. Sein früherer Posener Sachwalter Weltzer hat wenig Hoffnung. Vom sächsischen Hof hört er, Polnische Commissarien hätten auf eine Bittschrift von Dąbska befohlen, ihr die Güter zurückzugeben. Man vermute einen baldigen Krieg im Norden.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Von Sanitz asks whether the first tribunal has already decided on the Prince's Trąbczyn estates, and asks if possible for copies of the justice of the peace's judgment and of the tribunal's sentence. He has moved to Berlin to save what remains of his lost fortune. He says his former agent in Poznań, Weltzer, holds out little hope. From the Saxon court he hears that Polish Commissioners, acting on a petition from Dąbska, have ordered the estates to be returned to her. He says war in the north is expected soon.

Right Honourable Sir, most particularly Highly Honoured Sir Lieutenant! Fine [uncertain: Schönen] it must appear of me, to have left Your Right Honourable's kind letter so long unanswered with due thanks; did I not count upon your kind indulgence, I should not now venture anew to burden you with my present one.

That I have had to leave Silesia and Wrocław, wherein I lived nearly 49 years, mostly happy and glad, and have chosen [uncertain: bei in] for my residence, in order better to pursue here my most pressing affairs — the loss of my fortune — and if possible still to save something, will perhaps not be entirely unknown to you. But the prospects in this are as yet not very clear, and even my old former lawyer in Poznań, Mr Weltzer, who is now likewise staying here, and who believes he knows the Polish justice, has little hope. The foreign department has [uncertain: verraut] itself on my behalf to the Dresden [uncertain: Huffe], but this one too, though it will indeed have it that the previously paid

debts might be sought after, yet as concerns the estates themselves, he seeks — [text lost] no official order of the Emperor being at hand for this — to excuse the Polish judges of that time, and to make the Tilsit peace articles, however much they be in favour and herein, not applicable to it.

The French envoy here, with whom formerly [uncertain: voriges] there was much during my being here, gives me hope in this manner: that if no express Imperial order exists for this, and I could give him the official one, he would take my part. Finally I have obtained the same from the Saxon court itself, which says that Dąbska in [uncertain: desden] petitioned the Emperor and begged for the restitution of the estates — that His Imperial Majesty merely forwarded her petition to the then Polish [uncertain: Pluischen] government. Thereupon the Polish commissioners ordered that the estates be given back to Dąbska. At present I am occupied in having this [uncertain: Übersehen] left aside, and shall see how far

the French envoy here will take up these matters; nor have I failed [uncertain: verfohlen] to give Your Right Honourable notice of it at once, so that His Most Serene Highness — to whose grace I beg to be commended — might perhaps likewise make use of it.

How shall I forget the benefactions I received through this Prince; my attachment can and never will die.

Has then still no sentence come out of the first Tribunal concerning the Prince's Trąbczyn estates? Would Your Right Honourable have the goodness to let me know, 1) if possible in copy — what the justice of the peace [uncertain: Friedens Ruhte] adjudged, and 2nd what the sentence with the tribunal has stated?

News I would gladly [uncertain: mit fielen] give you, but what is given out here for certain on one day is doubted the next, and on the third a second, often contradictory, one displaces

the first. So much, however, does seem to shine out of it all, that an early outbreak of the war in the North is presumed. Were it clear in the evening [uncertain: im Abend], then it would burn in the North; that, I believe, is the conductor. Awaiting a [uncertain: zütigen] reply and the pardon of my negligence, I remain in the most perfect esteem, lifelong, Your Right Honourable's
