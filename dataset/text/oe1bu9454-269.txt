Staats Kanzlers Durchlaucht, bey überreichung der v Kircheisenschen Correspondence
geschrieben und in Erinnerung gebracht habe —
Der Hoffrath B[arbe] hat es den Fürsten (weil ich aus traurigen Gründen nicht ausgehen kan) übergeben,
Fr Durchlaucht haben es gnädig aufgenommen, und in wohlwollendesten Tone alles Gute
zugeführt. Jezt liegt es in den Händen des Geheimen Staats Raths Stägemann —
B[arbe]: hält Cosmars Sturz, und Zeitwurf die Bewilligung des Darlehns für abgemacht —
wegen Trąbczyn fängt mir an ſehr bange zu werden, den fält der Kreis vorm
die Güter liegen würklich Rußland anheim, so sieht es böse aus, den dorten
vermögen die Schürzen, welche schon hier würken, alles — der Kayser soll
sich bereits geäußert haben — die pohl p Commission habe damals ihre Sache
Klüger gemacht, als die Preuß. Regierungen — Was hat man da zu

blüffen — der sagt, solte man den Fürsten die Güter nicht wiedergeben wollen.

damit durch einen etwan schnell ausbrechenden Krieg die Sachen nicht wieder liegen bleiben.

17ten Febr geht der [Kü?]stl. Kayser auch / vermuthlich über Warschau / auch nach Hauß

Ich werde / sobald ich im andern Zustand versetzt bin / die ante chambres sehr sorgfältig

--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld übersendet sein Schreiben an den Staatskanzler, das Hoffrath Barbe mit der Kircheisenschen Korrespondenz überreicht hat. Die Sache liegt nun bei Stägemann. Um Trąbczyn fürchtet er, falls der Kreis an Russland fällt. Hardenberg sage, der Fürst müsse sonst entschädigt werden. Eine Erlaubnis, sich wegen Trąbczyn an russische Behörden zu wenden, hat er noch nicht. Er will Cosmars Sturz und die Administration der Majoratsgüter betreiben und erwartet die Antwort des Grafen Pourtalès.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld sends his letter to the State Chancellor, which Court Councillor Barbe handed over together with the Kircheisen correspondence. The matter now lies with Stägemann. He fears for Trąbczyn should the district fall to Russia. He says Hardenberg states that the Prince must otherwise be compensated. He does not yet have permission to approach the Russian authorities about Trąbczyn. He intends to pursue Cosmar's removal and the administration of the entailed estates, and awaits the reply of Count Pourtalès.

Most Serene Prince

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

From the enclosure Your Serene Highness will graciously be pleased to gather what I have written to His Serene Highness the Prince State Chancellor, on presenting the von Kircheisen correspondence, and what I have brought to his remembrance. — Court Councillor B[arbe] handed it to the Prince (since for sad reasons I cannot go out); Fr[?] Serene Highness received it graciously, and in the most well-meaning tone promised everything good. Now it lies in the hands of the Privy State Councillor Stägemann. — B[arbe] holds Cosmar's fall, and [uncertain: Zeitwurf] the granting of the loan, to be settled. — As regards Trąbczyn I am beginning to grow very anxious, for if the district in which the estates actually lie falls to Russia, then things look bad, for there the petticoats, which are already at work here, can do anything. — The Emperor is said to have already declared himself: the Polish commission had at that time managed its business more cleverly than the Prussian governments. — What is there to be expected in that case? Meanwhile one counts on Prince Hardenberg — who [uncertain: Last / lets himself] not be disconcerted — who says that if they should be unwilling to give the estates back to the Prince, then he must be compensated. — For the rest, I still have no permission from the Prince to apply to the Russian authorities concerning Trąbczyn — the Prince, however, now sees further than all the swindlers. — Minister Wilhelm von Humboldt and Privy State Councillor Beguelin would [text lost] for me as truly upright men. — For the rest, Constantin rages frightfully in Poland; the country is being sucked entirely dry, and every male creature that can so much as bear a weapon is put into the ranks. — To this comes his proclamation that the Poles should hold together and assert their independence. — In what mood this puts our King, Your Serene Highness may easily decipher. — Everyone here speaks of war — may God prevent it. — I wish only that

Your Serene Highness's affairs might first of all be brought to an end here, so that the business does not again lie idle through a war that may break out suddenly. Already one after another is beginning to disperse. Tomorrow the Queen of Bavaria and the Grand Duke of Baden depart, and in a few days the King of Bavaria follows; on the 17th of February the [uncertain: Küstl.] Emperor also goes home / presumably by way of Warsaw / and then, if not sooner, the eternal Congress will surely be at an end. — I shall / as soon as I am put into another condition / keep watch on the antechambers most carefully, so that I may only see Cosmar overthrown and the entailed estates placed under administration — the rest will then all find itself, and that will quite certainly happen. For Count Pourtalès's answer I wait, together with B[arbe], most longingly. — This too [uncertain: auß / must] be brought into the clear and settled before my [uncertain: Lerm] arises. — The cabals of Prince Metternich and of the Duke of Benevento (Talleyrand) are boundless, and it will be a miracle of God if everything ends happily and quietly. —

With boundless veneration I am

Your Most Serene Highness's

Vienna, the 30th of January

1815.

faithful and most obedient servant

von Triebenfeld
