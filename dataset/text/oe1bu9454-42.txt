--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Triebenfeld berichtet, er sei als Bevollmächtigter des Fürsten von französischer Gendarmerie verhaftet worden, um rückständige Zinsen von 3521 Rthl an die Invaliden Casse zu zahlen. Da van der Lahr ihn wegen noch 3000 Rthl verklagt und seine Abreise verhindert, bittet er dringend um 2068 Rthl nach Konotop, das seine Gläubiger sequestrieren. Das französische Generalgouvernement habe die Aufhebung der Sequestration wegen der Invaliden Casse angeordnet. Gegen die Sequestration von Zagórów habe er protestiert und gegen Oppenheimer einen Prozess eingeleitet.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Triebenfeld reports that he was arrested by French gendarmes as the Prince's authorised representative, so that arrears of interest of 3,521 Rthl would be paid to the Invalids' Fund. Since van der Lahr is suing him for a further 3,000 Rthl and is preventing his departure, he urgently asks for 2,068 Rthl to be sent to Konotop, which his creditors are placing under sequestration. He says the French General Government has ordered the lifting of the sequestration on account of the Invalids' Fund. He says he has protested against the sequestration of Zagórów and has begun proceedings against Oppenheimer.

Most Serene Prince,

Most gracious Prince and Lord!

If ever a human creature has been pressed down by need and tribulation and whipped through every class of suffering, I am he now. It is now over a year since I was sent hither, in order to effect one thing and another here, and then to return from here by way of Vienna to Königsberg.

Just as I was on the point of departing hence, I was seized by French gendarmerie on the order of the General Government and for that reason placed under strict arrest, namely that I, as Your Serene Highness's authorised representative, was to pay at once to the Invalids' Fund the arrears of interest amounting to 3,521 Rthl. The affair excited a terror by which my honour and credit suffered — Against surety of the councillor of the Chamber Court von Grevenitz and the innkeeper Mathieu I was released from my arrest for 4 weeks, with the observation that within that time I should procure the 3,521 and pay the same, together with costs, in cash in hard Courant to the French Intendant Maluce[uncertain reading], failing which I, together with my sureties, should be taken into arrest anew.

During these 4 weeks, then, the money had to be procured

To obtain this sum in cash was a work of impossibility; I had to take papers at their nominal value, and in exchange promise to pay cash Courant again after 3 months. The honest Minister von Zastrow gave a pledge and no joint bill of exchange, which Grevenitz and I co-signed. The time ran out quickly and the merchant van der Lahr demanded 5,000 Rthl together with interest. For the sake of Mr von Zastrow and Grevenitz I had to find counsel quickly — I therefore took from the dyer Coste 9,000 Rthl in state papers at 6 per cent interest, which were deposited as cover, and with them the Zastrow pledge and the bill of exchange were redeemed — Coste had given me the state papers on the condition that I should return the same to him in precisely such papers. The papers stood very low at that time; I could not sell them, but pledged them, and that again cost heavy percentages and [uncertain: charges].

Meanwhile the 3,000 Rthl from Your Most Serene Highness came in; of this I paid 2,153 Rthl at once in cash to Lahr and bought papers with the remainder. Hecker gave 4,000 Rthl in paper as security; this I pledged again for 1,000 Rthl and paid interest and costs. In order, however, to profit from the speculation as all bank

bankers do, and to get out of the affair, I bought and sold papers to advantage, so that I now have the whole 9,000 Rthl for Coste together again, and nothing further is wanting but the cash interest, amounting to 340 Rthl. Once I have this, this entire sum is discharged and fully settled. Meanwhile Lahr has sued me for the 3,000 Rthl still coming to him, and is impeding my departure.

I have deposited papers for it, but I may not leave until the matter is smoothed out.

By the common law he must now be content with papers. Meanwhile I must pay the difference, and this amounts, first, upon the Coete papers, as said above, to 340 Rthl; second, interest, court and other costs, 568 Rthl.

3. For two bonds on which I still owe the whole bill of exchange, 360 Rthl.

4. For 2 bonds lying in deposit, 800 Rthl; sum total 2,068 Rthl. These 2,068 Rthl must be procured and paid; thereupon this exceedingly fatal business is lifted and settled, and I can travel where I will.

If it is in any way possible for Your Serene Highness in the world, do drive

the same together there and send them in all haste to Konotop; otherwise I cannot stir from the spot — and yet it would be very good if I were permitted to wait upon Your Serene Highness in person — for I myself do not know how to help myself — through this business of my being arrested here I lost credit and health — my creditors awoke on all sides, and to this very day are sequestrating Konotop upon me. I cannot go there myself, for I am under arrest here on Your Serene Highness's account — what pains me most is that Mathieu will in the end still hold back the remainder, since I still owe him 1,500 Rthl Münzer; 2,083 Rthl I have already paid the B —, and 1,500 Rthl still come to him, and on that account the [T?] will not let me travel.

For nearly 6 months I lay, over and above all the vexation, most wretchedly ill, and this business too costs me here some 300 Rthl, which I had to pay the doctor for all of it, and 170 Rthl which I had to pay at the apothecary's. Never has misfortune pursued me as it has this year here. Meanwhile this too will have been got through, if only I receive the 2,068 Rthl so as to be able to bring the Lahr matter entirely into the clear.

For this do find counsel, Your Serene Highness; I beg you heartily and urgently for it, otherwise I cannot get

away from here, and must sit here until help comes. From my estates I now have nothing; the taxes are too oppressive — I do not yet know how I am to be rid of the sequestrator in Konotop, and the longer I sit here the greater is my loss!

The damage I suffer is appalling. Your Serene Highness knows that I never complain, but now the water is up to my throat. I beg you, most gracious Lord, have mercy and see to it that I receive the 2,068 Rthl soon now, so that I may come into freedom. From the French General Government the order has been sent to the Ministry of Justice at Warsaw that the sequestration on account of the Invalids' Fund is to be lifted, and also that the interest extorted from one is to be repaid out of the estates — these Your [uncertain: Serene Highness] can then recover yourself against the original dispositions, and in that way you lose nothing, and may well indemnify me for the covering of the costs I have had here. As to the sequestration otherwise imposed in Zagórów, I have had the proper protestations lodged, and against the Jew Oppenheimer, who [text lost] the Bau

ceded his claim, I have here framed a suit in which I am having him proceeded against by the public prosecutor — for he reckons up to himself [uncertain: prore]netica and God knows what else — He pretends to have 105/m Rthl to claim, which is at bottom a lie — He has 72/m Rthl to claim of me, and this, once the suit is decided, will be deposited here in Berlin in papers, and then all will be in order there.

With the Invalids' Fund and all the debts, 122,000 Rthl would come out.

I have here a merchant who will take iron and other products too, and will give money. If one were in a position to raise 60,000 Rthl, one would have 120,000 Rthl, as the papers now stand, and could face everything down — Were the papers to fall again to 30 per cent, one could settle everything for 40,000 Rthl at the most — the plan has long been drawn up by me; were I only out of here first, then all would soon come into the clear.

Lay my petition to heart, Your Serene Highness, and see to it that I am rescued and set at liberty. I will willingly endure every hardship, travel day and night; only of my

liberty I will not and cannot be deprived. I am paralysed in everything; the very thought beats down all my courage again and makes me incapable of anything.

From Königsberg I have had letters of the 8th of this month; they write something consoling with regard to Your Serene Highness, and in particular General von Köckritz is said to have taken up Your Serene Highness's affairs very zealously. After the return of the post from Petersburg a good issue is promised — Whether Constantine will indeed effect any good remains to be seen. He promised much — Only I no longer put much trust nowadays in such gentlemen's promises. I have been too greatly and too often deceived; I have also suffered too much physically and morally, so that I still fear I shall become the worst of misanthropes. The Princess Sacken is said to be quite well; they have quartered General Dercke upon her, with which she is very discontented. Help speedily, Your Serene Highness, that I may get free from here, for then I shall fly to lay myself at the same's feet. In deepest reverence I remain, until death,

Your Most Serene Highness's

Berlin, the 16th of January

1809.

faithful and most obedient servant

v Triebenfeld
