beherzigen Sie dies und suchen den würdigen guten Man den Cammer

--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Der Schreiber reist unverrichteter Dinge nach Wrocław zurück, weil er vergeblich auf Glenck gewartet hat. Er klagt, bei Pszczew hätten andere 30000 Rthl am Fürsten gewonnen. Der Freund soll Director Stubenrauch zum Kauf von Kamionna bewegen, damit dieser das Geld bis Johanni hergibt. Der Schreiber will für 10000 Rthl jährliche Revenue haften. Der Fürst wäre mit 180000 Rthl zufrieden. Ohne Einstandsgeld zahlten die Unterthanen 50 Rthl pro Hufe jährlich. Er mahnt zur Vorsicht beim Cabinets Rath.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
The writer is travelling back to Wrocław having achieved nothing, since he waited in vain for Glenck. He complains that in the case of Pszczew others made 30,000 Rthl at the Prince's expense. His friend is to persuade Director Stubenrauch to buy Kamionna, so that he hands over the money by Midsummer. The writer says he will stand surety for an annual revenue of 10,000 Rthl. The Prince would be satisfied with 180,000 Rthl. Without an entry payment the subjects paid 50 Rthl per Hufe yearly. He urges caution with the Cabinet Councillor.

--- ENGLISH TRANSLATION (draft) ---
132

My dearest Friend, I am just now on the point of stepping into the carriage and returning to Wrocław. It has been enough to drive me mad, to sit here so long [uncertain: to sit] waiting upon Mr Glenck, and in the end to accomplish nothing. The result is always that people fish in troubled waters and want to profit barbarously at the Prince's expense. At Pszczew the people have gained thirty thousand Rthl in so short a time, without having to put themselves to the least trouble. The subjects merely buy themselves free and give 30,000 Rthl more than the purchasers gave. Meanwhile the latter pocket this sum. Spoiled by that transaction, at Kamionna still more than this was to be gained at once, and yet God knows what screws were then to be applied to the Prince — into this I cannot intervene, and so I depart with nothing accomplished — Now, my dear Friend, see to it that [uncertain: the gentleman] Director Stubenrauch buys the estates and furnishes the money. — I will stand surety to the man for a clear and perpetual revenue, if I am permitted to dismember the estates, of = 10,000 Rthl yearly — this would amount, reckoned at 5 per cent, to a capital of 200/m Rthl. But since Mr Glenck has been assured that the Order's capital is content if

[uncertain: it] has 4 per cent clear interest from its capitals, then the value of the estates might be taken at 250,000 Rthl. Now, however, the Prince, since he needs money, would be very well content with 180,000 Rthl, consequently [uncertain: the chapter would gain] 70,000 Rthl in cash by this purchase. You might further urge this, that the chapter or the Order's Chamber, if still more were to be got out of it, would have to give a bonus proportionate to the matter.

In case the Lord Director Stubenrauch will agree to this, have the kindness to send me an express courier; I [uncertain: hasten] then straight back to Berlin, conclude the matter and provide the warranty of title.

Had I known the business thus sooner, everything would already be settled, and much might have been gained by it. As it is, one always learns everything too late.

Mr Glenck has all along reckoned on a purchase money of 500 Rthl per Hufe, which the people were to pay — With this in view he [would] assume a light hereditary quit-rent of 10 to 12 Rthl per Hufe, and the value of the estates was thereby diminished; but if I now take no entry money, the subject willingly pays 50 Rthl per Hufe yearly. By this means an enormously high value now comes out, so that I am still able, out of old rents and for the beer and brandy excise, to bring out a perpetually

subsisting [uncertain: revenue] of 12,000 Rthl, and consequently a value at 4 per cent of 300,000 Rthl; on which footing the Order's Chamber would profit to the extent of 120,000.

Take this to heart, and seek to move that worthy good man the Chamber Director Stubenrauch to make the purchase and to furnish the moneys for it by Midsummer of this year. In the matter of the Koltzig estates, or in the business of compensation, seek to bring about the best that may be, but do go cautiously with the Lord Cabinet Councillor, for at present I fear that man extraordinarily.

With the most faithful heart and the greatest attachment I am unceasingly

Your

Frankfurt an der Oder

23 May 1804.

most faithfully devoted servant

v Triebenfeld
