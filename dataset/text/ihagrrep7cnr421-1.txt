--- SUMMARY (German) ---
Stanisław von Chełmski an den König, Kalisch, 8. Oktober 1799, mit dem Vermerk „conc Mondo“. Er hat dem Starosten von Prusimski 1794 12.000 Dukaten in Gold gegen einen Revers geliehen, zahlbar zu Johanni 1795; vorher wurde Prusimski flüchtig, und durch das Konfiskationsurteil wurde sein Vermögen vom Fiskus beschlagnahmt. Chełmski setzte sich nach Rücksprache mit ihm in den Besitz von Prusimskis Gütern Trąmpczyn und Nowa Wieś, musste sie aber nach dem Revisionserkenntnis dem Fürsten von Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen räumen, dem der König sie geschenkt hatte, und wurde mit seiner Forderung an den Fiskus verwiesen. Auf seine Klage gegen den Fiskus vom 6. September beschied ihn die Regierung zu Kalisch am 10. September, er müsse zuerst die Realqualität der Forderung nachweisen, und als er unter Berufung auf die Kabinettsordre vom 14. September 1796 auf einen Instruktionstermin drang, wies sie die Klage am 27. ab. Er führt aus: Nach dieser Kabinettsordre gehe der Fiskus wegen des Schadensersatzes allen persönlichen Gläubigern eines Insurgenten vor, stehe ihnen aber mit der Konfiskationsstrafe nach, müsse also eine persönliche Forderung, deren Richtigkeit anders als durch seinen bloßen Revers nachgewiesen werde, aus dem eingezogenen Vermögen befriedigen; das deklaratorische Hofreskript vom 17. September 1796 lege die Kabinettsordre ausdehnend und gegen ihren klaren Inhalt aus, was allein dem König und nicht dem Chef der Justiz zustehe; und selbst dieses Reskript lasse Personalklagen gegen den Fiskus zu, wobei der Fiskus dem Kläger eine beglaubigte Aufstellung der Insurrektionsschäden vorlegen dürfe, die zeige, dass das eingezogene Vermögen sie nicht decke. Dieses Verfahren nimmt er hin, behält sich aber seine Einwände gegen eine solche Schadensliquidation vor, und bittet um rechtliches Gehör und darum, die Regierung zu Kalisch anzuweisen, die Instruktion seiner Klage gegen den Fiskus einzuleiten. Unterzeichnet Stanisl v. Chełmski. Am Rand der ersten Seite die Notiz eines Beamten vom 18. Oktober, gezeichnet Massow: man möge ihm die Akten beilegen, in denen sich die in der Eingabe rot unterstrichenen Stücke befinden, die er in der ihm von Goldbeck zugestellten Sammlung nicht finde, und sie nötigenfalls aus dem Archiv erbitten (die Notiz nur zum Teil gelesen); und der Beschluss, Berlin, 21. Oktober 1799: Es bleibe bei der Resolution der Regierung vom 10. September, da jeder Gläubiger mit persönlichen Ansprüchen an das Vermögen eines Insurgenten sich selbst bescheiden werde, dass die Kriegskosten die ganze Masse eines solchen Insurgenten weit übersteigen; gezeichnet Willenz.

--- SUMMARY (English) ---
Stanisław von Chełmski to the King, Kalisz, 8 October 1799, with the note "conc Mondo". In 1794 he lent the Starost von Prusimski 12,000 ducats in gold against a written undertaking, repayable at St John's Day 1795; before then Prusimski became a fugitive, and by the sentence of confiscation his property was seized by the Fiscus. After consulting him, Chełmski took possession of Prusimski's estates of Trąbczyn and Nowawies, but after the judgment of revision had to give them up to the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, to whom the King had given them, and was referred with his claim to the Fiscus. To his suit against the Fiscus of 6 September the Government at Kalisz answered on 10 September that he must first prove the claim to be a real one, and when he pressed for a hearing to take evidence, citing the cabinet order of 14 September 1796, it dismissed the suit on the 27th. He argues that under that cabinet order the Fiscus takes precedence over all the personal creditors of an insurgent for compensation but ranks after them with the penalty of confiscation, and so must satisfy from the confiscated property a personal claim whose correctness is shown other than by his mere written undertaking; that the declaratory court rescript of 17 September 1796 extends the cabinet order and interprets it against its clear content, which belongs to the King alone and not to the Chief of Justice; and that even that rescript admits personal suits against the Fiscus, which may lay before the plaintiff a certified statement of the damage of the insurrection showing that the confiscated property does not cover it. He accepts that procedure, reserving his objections to such a liquidation of damages, and asks for a hearing at law and that the Government at Kalisz be instructed to open the taking of evidence on his suit against the Fiscus. Signed Stanisl v. Chełmski. In the margin of the first page are an official's note of 18 October, signed Massow, asking for the files that hold the pieces underlined in red in the petition, which he does not find in the collection Goldbeck sent him, to be attached, and if need be requested from the archive (the note only partly read); and the decision, Berlin, 21 October 1799: the Government's resolution of 10 September stands, since every creditor with personal claims on an insurgent's property will understand of himself that the costs of the war far exceed the whole estate of such an insurgent; signed Willenz.

Most Serene, Most Mighty, Most Gracious King and Lord! Kalisz, 8 October 1799.

In the year 1794 I gave the Starost von Prusimski a loan of 12,000 ducats in gold against a written undertaking, which he promised to repay at St John's Day 1795. Before the date of payment came, however, von Prusimski became a fugitive, and through the sentence of confiscation passed against him his property was seized by the Fiscus.

After consulting with him, von Prusimski, I did take possession of the estates of Trąbczyn and Nowawies belonging to him, but I had to give them up to the Prince of Hohenlohe-Ingelfingen, to whom these estates had been given by Your Royal

Majesty's own Most High Person, upon the opening of the judgment of revision, and was referred with my said claim to the Fiscus.

I then sued the Fiscus for payment of it on 6 September this year, but by the decree of the 10th of the same month, which I enclose in copy, I was told that before my suit could be admitted the real character of the said claim must first be proved.

Since by the judgment of revision mentioned above the contrary was already established, and since moreover I demand my satisfaction not from the

Prince of Hohenlohe but from the Fiscus as possessor of the whole of von Prusimski's property, the suit in this matter was not inadmissible on the ground that the claim in question is merely personal. Referring to the Most High Cabinet Order of 14 September 1796, I pressed repeatedly for a hearing to take evidence to be appointed; against my expectation, however, I was once more dismissed with the suit by the decree of the 27th of the same month, which I likewise present in copy.

As I cannot rest content with these adverse rulings,

I see myself compelled, for the following reasons, to have recourse to Your Majesty's Most High court. By the Cabinet Order just mentioned, No. 2, it is indeed ordained that the Fiscus, in respect of compensation for damage, takes precedence over all personal creditors of an insurgent, and that it shall then rank after them with the penalty of confiscation. It is thus clear that the Fiscus must give satisfaction from the confiscated property for a personal claim on the insurgent, if its correctness is proved from elsewhere (aliunde), otherwise than by my mere written undertaking; and that there is therefore no sufficient ground in the present case for dismissing the suit because

the claim is merely personal.

The declaratory Court Rescript of 17 September 1796 can find no application here, since on the one hand it interprets the Cabinet Order, the Most High will of Your Royal Majesty Yourself, extensively and against its clear content, which belongs solely to Your Most High[?] Person and not to the Chief of Justice; and on the other hand it touches on matters of substance.

But even the said Rescript lays down that personal suits against the Fiscus are to be admitted without question at the parties' request, but that a certified

statement of the damage done by the insurrection, for which by legal principles the insurgents are liable jointly and severally (in solidum), might be laid before such a plaintiff by the Fiscus, from which it should appear that the confiscated property of the insurgents is far from sufficient to cover these damages.

I will gladly accept that procedure, but reserve my objections to such a liquidation of damages, and most humbly ask Your Royal Majesty

to grant me a hearing at law, and to instruct the Government at Kalisz to open the taking of evidence on the suit I have brought against the Fiscus.

I who remain, until death, in deepest reverence Your Royal Majesty's most humble Stanisl v. Chełmski, in his own hand
